Subtopic note

One Consistent Formula per Forecast Row

Scope: One consistent formula across each forecast row; historical columns kept as hardcoded actuals

Why it matters

When every forecast cell in a row carries the same formula, auditing one cell audits the row. When FY27E is =L40*(1+M38) but FY28E is =M40*1.12 and FY29E is a typed 21,450, the row has three different logics and the reviewer has to inspect each cell — and the typed cell will not move when the scenario switch flips. Row consistency is the most heavily tested integrity rule in Big 4 model reviews and modelling assessments, including this week's Saturday audit. It goes hand in hand with a clean boundary: historical columns contain reported actuals (hardcoded or linked from Historicals), forecast columns contain one formula.

Core notes

The rule

For every calculation row \( r \) and forecast columns \( j = f, f+1, \ldots, N \):

\[ \text{Formula}_{r,j} \equiv \text{Formula}_{r,f} \quad \text{(identical in R1C1 notation)} \]

The A1 formula changes as it copies (=L40*(1+M38), =M40*(1+N38)), but its relative structure is the same — which is exactly what R1C1 notation shows (=R[0]C[-1]*(1+R[-2]C); see c01).

The historical/forecast boundary

Two acceptable designs:

Design Historical columns Forecast columns Pros Cons
A. Split row Hardcoded actuals (blue) or green links to Historicals One consistent formula (black) Simple; boundary visible; standard in banks and ER Two formulas per row (one per region); roll-forward requires copying the forecast formula one column left into the new actual year and replacing with actuals
B. Flagged single formula Same formula as forecast, using flags: =M$4*Hist!M40+M$5*(L40*(1+M38)) Same Truly one formula per row; roll-forward automatic Longer formulas; reviewer must understand flags

Design A is the norm in Indian equity research models — the roadmap phrase "historical columns kept as hardcoded actuals" describes it. FAST purists prefer design B (topic concept c06). Either way, the forecast region must be internally consistent, and the boundary must be marked (a vertical border or shaded actual columns, and the A/E label from the master timeline).

What "hardcoded actuals" should mean in practice

Historical numbers should be typed or pasted once, on the Historicals tab (blue, with source references), and linked into calc sheets (green). On calc sheets, historical driver rows (e.g., FY21A–FY25A DSO) are calculated from the linked actuals (black), so the historical ratios that justify forecast assumptions are formula-driven and transparent:

Row Label FY24A FY25A FY26E FY27E
40 Revenue (₹ crore) =Hist!L12 =Hist!M12 =Rev!N50 =Rev!O50
41 Receivables (₹ crore) =Hist!L60 =Hist!M60 =N40*N42/365 =O40*O42/365
42 DSO (days) =L41/L40*365 =M41/M40*365 =Inputs!N70 =Inputs!O70

Rows 41 and 42 switch logic at the boundary — the historical DSO is derived from actual receivables; the forecast receivables are derived from forecast DSO. This "inversion" is legitimate in design A and is why flags (design B) are sometimes preferred.

Typical row-consistency breaches

  1. First-forecast-year special formula — e.g., FY26E uses FY25A closing differently. Fix with a pre-timeline column or a first-forecast flag (First_Fcst_Flag).
  2. Manual override — a typed number in one forecast year. Fix with an explicit override row on Inputs plus a flag (pitfall in topic.md).
  3. Phase changes — e.g., capex ramp formula for FY26E–FY27E, maintenance capex afterwards. Fix with an input row by year or a flag row (=--(M$3>=Phase2_Start)).
  4. Stray edits — a reviewer "tests" a value and forgets to undo. Detect with Go To Special → Row differences (s07) or Inquire.
  5. Partial copy-across — formula updated in FY26E–FY28E only after a logic change. Detect the same way.

Illustration

Pidilite B2B revenue row (₹ crore):

FY25A FY26E FY27E FY28E FY29E FY30E
2,350 =M40*(1+N38) =N40*(1+O38) 2,960 (typed) =P40*1.11 =Q40*(1+R38)

FY28E typed and FY29E with an embedded 11% are breaches. If the Bull case raises growth by 2 points, FY26E, FY27E and FY30E respond but FY28E and FY29E do not — FY29E still grows 11% from a frozen FY28E base. Reconstructing: with growth 10% each year in Base, FY26E = 2,585.0, FY27E = 2,843.5, FY28E should be 3,127.9 (not 2,960).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Genuinely different logic by year (e.g., a known contract ending FY27): drive by a flag or an input schedule, not by different formulas.
  • Stub periods: a half-year FY26E stub should use a stub-fraction row, not a modified FY26E formula.
  • Totals and checks columns outside the timeline (e.g., column F five-year sum) are not part of the row-consistency test but must be visually separated.
  • Historicals in calc rows typed directly: acceptable in small models if blue; still prefer linking from a single Historicals tab to avoid multiple copies of actuals.
  • Rolling forward design A: when FY26 actuals arrive, the FY26E formula is replaced by a link to actuals; check the next column (FY27E) still refers correctly to FY26 as the base.

Key takeaways

  • Every forecast cell in a row carries the same formula (identical in R1C1).
  • Historical columns hold actuals — typed once on Historicals and linked into calc sheets.
  • Mark the actual/forecast boundary visibly; choose split-row or flagged design and apply it consistently.
  • Handle first-year, phase and override logic through flags and input rows, not cell-by-cell formulas.
  • Row-difference scans and R1C1 view detect breaches quickly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Spot the breaches [L1]

A Trent employee cost row reads: FY26E =M60*M61/100, FY27E =N60*N61/100, FY28E =O60*O61/100*1.05, FY29E 1,940, FY30E =Q60*Q61/100. Identify each breach and state its consequence for scenario analysis.

Q2. Remove the first-year exception [L2]

A Debt tab uses =Hist!M85 for FY26E opening debt and =M90 (prior closing) for FY27E onward in the same row. Redesign the row so that a single formula works from FY26E to FY30E.

💡 Hint: A pre-timeline column or a first-forecast flag can carry the FY25A closing balance into the same formula pattern.

Q3. Phase-based capex [L2]

Pidilite guides growth capex of ₹800 crore in each of FY26E and FY27E and maintenance capex of 3% of revenue thereafter. Design the input rows and one consistent capex formula for FY26E–FY30E, given revenue forecasts of ₹14,200, ₹15,700, ₹17,300, ₹19,000 and ₹20,900 crore.

Q4. Choosing the boundary design [L3]

Your team is standardising on either design A (split row) or design B (flagged single formula) for all models. Write a recommendation memo weighing auditability, roll-forward effort, formula length, reviewer familiarity and Power BI integration.
