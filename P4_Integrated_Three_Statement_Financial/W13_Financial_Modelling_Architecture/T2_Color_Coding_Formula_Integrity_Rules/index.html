Roadmap topic: Color Coding & Formula Integrity Rules: Blue for hardcoded inputs, Black for same-sheet formulas, Green for cross-sheet links; one consistent formula across each row

Overview

Topic t1 fixed where things go in a model; this topic fixes how each cell is built and how its nature is signalled. Colour coding tells a reader what each cell is — a typed assumption, a local calculation, a link from another tab, or a dependency on another file — without clicking it. Formula integrity rules make sure each row of the forecast is driven by a single, transparent formula whose every number traces back to a documented input. Together they make a model auditable at speed, and they are what this week's Saturday assessment checks: zero hardcoded numbers inside forecast formulas, and consistent formulas and timelines across every tab.

For the target roles, these are not cosmetic preferences. Equity research and investment banking teams in India apply the blue/black/green convention almost universally; Big 4 model-assurance and transaction-services teams test models against FAST, ICAEW and SSRB checklists in which row consistency, absence of embedded constants, error handling and volatile functions are standard findings; and FP&A teams rely on the same disciplines so that budgets can be re-run by people who did not build them. In a modelling interview, a candidate who navigates with Ctrl+[ and verifies rows in R1C1 view signals experience before saying a word about valuation.

The subtopics form a sequence. Start with the font colour convention (s01) and implement it robustly with input fills and Cell Styles (s02). Next come the two structural rules: one consistent formula per forecast row, with historicals as actuals (s03), made possible by correct absolute and mixed referencing (s04); and no constants inside formulas, with every number traced to Inputs (s05). Then learn the auditing tools that verify these rules cell by cell (s06) and the bulk scanning tools — Go To Special and Inquire — that verify them sheet by sheet and version by version (s07). Finally, keep models fast and traceable by avoiding volatile functions and whole-column references (s08). The concepts distil the operational tests: R1C1 identity (c01), the Constants census (c02), targeted error guards (c03), link-once importing (c04), replacing nested IFs (c05) and timeline flags (c06).

This topic is the practical enforcement layer for the architecture of topic t1, and it directly shortens the debugging work of Week 15, where an unbalanced balance sheet is traced through the schedules.

Related concepts to study

Phase 3 Excel skills

→ Phase 3 (W9–12): Excel auditing shortcuts, LET, XLOOKUP, conditional formatting

Phase 3 introduced the mechanics this topic turns into rules. The auditing shortcuts (Ctrl+[, Ctrl+], F5 return, Evaluate Formula, Watch Window) become the review routine of s06. XLOOKUP, with its if_not_found and match_mode arguments, is the replacement for nested IFs and for IFERROR-wrapped lookups (c03, c05). LET helps make a moderately long formula readable, though in core schedules helper rows remain preferable because intermediates stay visible. Conditional formatting, learned for dashboards, is used here as a self-policing layer: ISFORMULA rules that light up constants in calc areas and formulas on the Inputs tab (s02), and red fills on check rows. Revisit those Phase 3 exercises with the integrity lens of this week.

FAST and ICAEW transparency rules

FAST and ICAEW Code transparency and consistency rules

The FAST Standard and the ICAEW Financial Modelling Code (topic t1 c01, c02) are the external authorities behind this topic. FAST's rules are explicit: one formula per row, consistent columns, no embedded constants, short formulas, link-then-calculate, and avoidance of OFFSET/INDIRECT, merged cells and hidden content. The ICAEW Code states them as principles — transparency, consistency, separation of inputs, built-in checks — and leaves house style to the organisation, provided it is applied consistently. Both treat colour coding as a means rather than an end: the convention matters because it makes the nature of each cell visible. When a reviewer or client asks what standard your model follows, cite one of these and demonstrate compliance with the tests in s07.

Professional model review practice

Model review/audit practices (Operis, Big 4 model assurance)

Independent model review (often called model audit or model assurance) is commissioned by lenders in project finance, by acquirers and PE funds in transactions, and by companies for critical models such as Ind AS 36 impairment or Ind AS 113 fair value. Specialist firms such as Operis (with its OAK analysis tool) and the Big 4 model-assurance teams run a standard sequence: structural map, unique-formula analysis (reducing thousands of cells to the few hundred distinct R1C1 formulas), constants and hardcode scans, error and volatility scans, logic review of each unique formula, and scenario/sensitivity re-performance. Findings are graded by severity. Building to pass that sequence — consistent rows mean fewer unique formulas to review — directly reduces review cost and time.

Week 15 debugging dependency

→ Week 15 (w15_t4): integrity discipline shortens Balance Sheet debugging

When a fully linked 3-statement model does not balance, the debugging routine is: find the first period where the check fails, determine whether the difference is constant or growing, then trace each BS line's movement against its CFS line. Integrity discipline shortens every step. With one formula per row, an R1C1 scan instantly shows if the failing year has a different formula; with no embedded constants, there is no hidden override to hunt; with targeted error guards, a broken link appears as #REF! instead of a silent zero; with link-once import blocks, tracing from the BS back to a schedule is a single hop. Models built without these rules routinely take hours to debug; models built with them take minutes.

Common pitfalls

Stale colour after a cell's nature changes

Formula cells left blue after an input was replaced with a calculation, or vice versa

The mistake: an input cell is later replaced by a formula but stays blue (so reviewers think it is an assumption), or a formula is pasted as a value but stays black (so reviewers think it is calculated). Why it happens: colours are applied manually and not refreshed after edits; paste-values is used to "freeze" a number. Correct treatment: colour by content, not memory — use Cell Styles and re-run a Go To Special colouring pass (Constants → blue, Formulas → black, "!" → green) before every issue; add ISFORMULA conditional formatting that overrides the display when content and style disagree. Detection: Go To Special → Constants on calc sheets (anything selected that is not blue is a pasted value); Go To Special → Formulas on Inputs (anything blue selected is a stale colour).

Unflagged one-year overrides

One-year hardcoded overrides inside a forecast row with no flag or comment

The mistake: typing a management number (e.g., FY27E A&P ₹620 crore) directly into one forecast cell of a formula row. Why it happens: last-minute requests before a board meeting; the analyst intends to "fix it later". Correct treatment: create an override row on Inputs (value by year) and an override flag; the calc row becomes =IF(Override_Flag_t=1,Override_t,Calculated_t) or uses multiplication by the flag, applied consistently across the row; show an "Overrides active" indicator on the Cover. Detection: Go To Special → Row differences on forecast columns; R1C1 scan; flex test (the overridden year does not respond to driver changes); Inquire Spreadsheet Compare between versions.

IFERROR masking broken links

IFERROR wrapping that silently hides #REF! from broken links

The mistake: wrapping links and calculations in IFERROR(…,0) so the model "looks clean". Why it happens: early in a build, rows are linked to blank or not-yet-built schedules and generate errors; IFERROR silences them and is never removed. Correct treatment: use targeted guards (IF(Denominator=0,0,…), IFNA for lookups) and let all other errors propagate to the master check (c03); never wrap cross-sheet links or check rows in IFERROR. Detection: Find "IFERROR" with Look in: Formulas; Inquire Workbook Analysis; temporarily replace IFERROR with a pass-through and run Go To Special → Formulas → Errors to see what was being hidden.

Practice Questions

Q1. Integrity review of a revenue and opex build [L3]

A Trent model's Opex tab (FY26E–FY30E in columns M–Q) has these features: (i) employee cost row =M60*M61/100 in M, N and P, =O60*O61/100*1.04 in O and a typed 2,180 in Q; (ii) all Inputs references written inline as Inputs!$F$40 inside 220 formulas; (iii) rent row =IFERROR(Leases!M22*M70,0); (iv) a store-opex row whose values are blue but contain formulas; (v) DSO pulled via =OFFSET(Inputs!$E$70,0,M$7); (vi) freight =IF(M40<5000,M40*2.5%,IF(M40<10000,M40*2.2%,M40*2%)). (a) Classify each defect against s01–s08 and c01–c06. (b) Rewrite each formula correctly, stating any Inputs rows needed. (c) State the test that would have found each defect. (d) Prioritise the fixes by risk.

Q2. Building a self-auditing template [L3]

Design a calc-sheet template for a Pidilite model that enforces this topic's rules as far as possible without VBA: Cell Styles (with protection), conditional formatting rules for constants and stale colours, an import block, flags in rows 4–8, a constant column, and a row-level consistency indicator in column F. Specify each element precisely (formulas, ranges, formats) and explain which rules it cannot enforce automatically.

💡 Hint: For the row-consistency indicator, think about comparing FORMULATEXT of each forecast cell after converting to a relative form, or accept that it must be a manual R1C1 check.

Q3. Saturday audit dry run [L2]

Your 12-tab Week 13 model must pass the Saturday audit. Write a 90-minute audit script covering: Go To Special scans (Constants, Formulas → Errors, Row differences) per tab; R1C1 review of all forecast rows; embedded-constant search; IFERROR and volatile-function search; external-link check; timeline alignment check against the master; and a flex test of three drivers. For each step state the expected "clean" result and how you would document it.

Q4. Override governance case [L2]

The CFO of an NSE-listed company requires FY27E revenue to equal the board-approved budget of ₹18,450 crore, whereas the driver-based build gives ₹18,120 crore. (a) Design an override mechanism that preserves one formula per row and keeps the driver build visible. (b) Show how the ₹330 crore gap should be presented on the Outputs tab and Cover. (c) Explain how the override interacts with the Base/Bull/Bear switch. (d) Write the change-log entry.

Q5. Interview rapid-fire [L1]

Answer briefly, as in an interview: (a) What does green font mean in your models? (b) Why not IFERROR? (c) How do you find hardcodes in formulas? (d) Why avoid OFFSET? (e) What is the fastest way to check row consistency? (f) What does Ctrl+[ do when the precedent is on another sheet?
