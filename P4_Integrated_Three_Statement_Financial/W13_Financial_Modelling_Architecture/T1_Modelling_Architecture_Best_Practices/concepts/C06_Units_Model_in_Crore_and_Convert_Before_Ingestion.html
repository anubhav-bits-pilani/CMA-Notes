Key concept

Units: Model in ₹ Crore and Convert Before Ingestion

Concept: Units: model in ₹ crore; 1 crore = 10 million = 100 lakh; convert all sources before ingestion

Statement

All monetary values in the model are held in ₹ crore. The conversion identities:

\[ 1\ \text{crore} = 100\ \text{lakh} = 10\ \text{million} = 10^{7}\ \text{rupees} \]

\[ 1\ \text{lakh} = 10^{5}\ \text{rupees} = 0.1\ \text{million}, \qquad 1\ \text{billion} = 100\ \text{crore} \]

Hence:

\[ \text{₹ crore} = \frac{\text{₹ lakh}}{100} = \frac{\text{₹ million}}{10} = \frac{\text{₹}}{10^{7}} = \text{₹ billion} \times 100 \]

Every source (annual report, XBRL, investor presentation, industry report) is converted to ₹ crore at the point of ingestion — on the Historicals or a raw-data tab — before any calculation uses it.

Explanation

Why ₹ crore. Large Indian listed companies (Nifty 500 constituents) report in ₹ crore; it gives readable magnitudes (Trent revenue in the tens of thousands of crore, not billions of rupees), and it matches how Indian investors and media quote numbers. Mid-caps and SMEs often report in ₹ lakh; MNC subsidiaries and some IT companies report in ₹ million; XBRL instance documents typically carry absolute rupees or the reported scale. Schedule III permits rounding to the nearest hundreds, thousands, lakhs, millions or crores depending on turnover (Schedule III, General Instructions), so units genuinely differ across companies and even across a company's history.

Convert before ingestion. Converting inside calculation formulas (=H40/100) scatters unit logic; if one link misses the divisor, a number is off by 100×. Instead:

  1. Raw-data tab holds the source exactly as published, with a units cell (e.g., "₹ lakh").
  2. A conversion factor cell (named, e.g., Src_to_Cr = 0.01 for lakh) converts the whole block onto Historicals.
  3. Historicals and all downstream tabs are in ₹ crore only.

Non-monetary units. Volumes (tonnes, mn sq ft, # stores), per-unit values (₹ per sq ft, ₹ per kg) and per-share data (₹) are kept in their natural units, stated in the units column. The conversion to ₹ crore happens at the revenue line, with an explicit divisor, e.g., mn sq ft × ₹/sq ft = ₹ mn → ÷ 10 → ₹ crore.

Shares. If shares outstanding are in crore, EPS = PAT (₹ crore) ÷ shares (crore) is in ₹. If shares are in million, EPS = PAT × 10 ÷ shares (mn). Declare on the Cover.

Foreign currency. For a USD-reporting peer in Comps, convert at a documented rate: USD mn × ₹/USD ÷ 10 = ₹ crore.

Illustration

A mid-cap peer reports revenue ₹4,52,318.45 lakh; another reports ₹45,231.8 million; a third (XBRL) reports ₹45,23,18,45,000.

  • ₹4,52,318.45 lakh ÷ 100 = ₹4,523.18 crore.
  • ₹45,231.8 million ÷ 10 = ₹4,523.18 crore.
  • ₹45,23,18,45,000 ÷ 10⁷ = ₹4,523.18 crore.

All three are the same company in different units. Trent's Zudio: 9.8 mn sq ft × ₹9,500 per sq ft = ₹93,100 mn ÷ 10 = ₹9,310 crore.

Note the Indian digit grouping (4,52,318.45) versus international (452,318.45); when importing text via Power Query, set the locale (English – India) so separators parse correctly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Convert the sources [L1]

Convert to ₹ crore: (a) ₹87,456.30 lakh; (b) ₹12,340 million; (c) USD 415 million at ₹84.20/USD; (d) ₹2.35 billion; (e) 1,85,00,00,000 rupees.

Q2. Units-safe revenue row [L2]

Pidilite sells 6.8 lakh tonnes of adhesives at an average realisation of ₹1,62,000 per tonne. Write the revenue formula in ₹ crore with all unit conversions explicit (as separate constants or named factors) and state the units column entry for each row.

💡 Hint: Convert lakh tonnes to tonnes and the rupee product to crore in visibly separate steps.

Q3. Mixed-unit history [L2]

A company reported in ₹ lakh up to FY22 and in ₹ crore from FY23 (after crossing the turnover threshold for rounding). Design the raw-data and Historicals layout and the checks that guarantee a consistent ₹ crore series for FY21A–FY25A.
