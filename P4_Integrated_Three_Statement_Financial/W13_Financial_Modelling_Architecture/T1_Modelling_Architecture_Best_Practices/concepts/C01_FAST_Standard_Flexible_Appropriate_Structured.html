Key concept

FAST Standard: Flexible, Appropriate, Structured, Transparent

Concept: FAST Standard: Flexible, Appropriate, Structured, Transparent; one formula per row, no mixed units in a row, formulas "no longer than your thumb"

Statement

The FAST Standard (maintained by the FAST Standard Organisation) is a set of design rules for spreadsheet models built around four qualities:

Letter Quality Meaning in practice
F Flexible Easy to change, extend and roll forward without rebuilding
A Appropriate Detail and complexity proportionate to the decision; no more
S Structured Consistent layout, separation of inputs/calcs/outputs, logical flow
T Transparent Any competent reader can follow every calculation

Three of its best-known rules: one formula per row (every period cell in a calculation row carries the same formula); no mixed units in a row (one row, one unit, one meaning across all periods); and formulas "no longer than your thumb" (short enough to read at a glance — split long logic into helper rows).

Explanation

FAST is deliberately prescriptive. Its premise is that most model risk comes not from complex finance but from opaque construction. Its rules include:

  • Row consistency. If FY26E and FY27E revenue use different formulas, a reviewer must audit each cell. With one formula per row, auditing the first forecast cell audits the row. Where logic genuinely differs (actual vs forecast), FAST uses flags rather than different formulas (topic t2, c06).
  • Units. A row labelled "Revenue, ₹ crore" never contains a percentage in some years. Mixed units are a common outcome of ad hoc overrides.
  • Short formulas. The "thumb" test is a heuristic: if the formula bar overflows, the formula is too long. Long formulas hide errors; helper rows expose intermediate values that can be checked.
  • Calculation blocks. Each block starts by pulling in its precedents (links), then calculates, so the reader sees all inputs to a calculation together.
  • Avoid multiple-sheet chains, OFFSET/INDIRECT, merged cells, hidden rows, and complex nested IFs.

FAST is stricter than many bank house styles (it discourages mixing inputs on calculation sheets at all), but its core rules are now almost universal in Indian corporate finance and Big 4 modelling teams.

Illustration

A non-FAST formula for FY27E employee cost (₹ crore):

=IF(M4=1,Hist!M30,(L50+(M48-L48)/2)*Inputs!$E$22*(1+Inputs!M23)^(M7)/10^7*IF(M7>2,1.02,1))

Problems: actual/forecast logic inside one formula, a hardcoded 1.02 and threshold 2, a unit conversion hidden in 10^7, and a period-counter exponent that compounds the increment from a fixed base (wrong if increments vary by year). FAST rebuild:

Row Label Units Formula (col M)
48 Closing headcount # link to Inputs
49 Average headcount # =(L48+M48)/2
50 CTC per employee ₹ lakh =L50*(1+M51)
51 Increment % link to Inputs
52 Employee cost (forecast) ₹ crore =M49*M50/100
53 Employee cost (live) ₹ crore =M4*Hist!M30+M5*M52

With average headcount 24,000 and CTC ₹4.2 lakh, cost = 24,000 × 4.2 / 100 = ₹1,008 crore. Every row has one unit and one short formula.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the FAST tests [L1]

State which FAST rule each of the following breaks: (a) FY26E revenue cell uses =L40*1.15, FY27E uses =M38*M39; (b) row 22 shows DSO in days for FY21A–FY25A and receivables in ₹ crore for forecasts; (c) a 210-character formula computing tax with MAT credit utilisation.

Q2. Thumb-length refactor [L2]

Refactor =IF(M5=1,MAX(0,MIN(Inputs!$E$40,L60+M58-M59)),0)*(1+IF(M7>3,Inputs!$E$41,0)) (a capped revolver with a step-up after year 3) into helper rows, stating label, units and formula for each row.

💡 Hint: Separate the cap, the pre-cap balance and the step-up condition into distinct rows before combining.
