Key concept

Column Consistency: Same Column, Same Period, Every Sheet

Concept: Column consistency: same column = same period on every sheet so formulas copy across and links audit cleanly

Statement

In a column-consistent model, for every calculation sheet \( s \) and every timeline column \( j \):

\[ \text{PeriodEnd}_{s,j} = \text{PeriodEnd}_{\text{master},j} \]

where \( \text{PeriodEnd}_{\text{master},j} \) is the period end in column \( j \) of the master timeline. Consequently any cross-sheet reference to a same-period value is written to the same column letter as the cell containing it (a formula in column M references column M on other sheets), and a formula written in the first timeline column can be copied unchanged across the row.

Explanation

Column consistency is what makes three other rules possible:

  1. One formula per row. If the Revenue sheet's FY26E is in column M but the WC sheet's FY26E is in column L, the WC formula must refer to Revenue!M40 from column L — the column offset is fixed, so the formula still copies, but every reader must mentally remember the offset.
  2. Clean audit trails. A reviewer checking =Revenue!M40*M12/365 in column M sees immediately that periods match. An offset formula (Revenue!N40 from column M) could be an intentional lead or an error — indistinguishable without investigation.
  3. Safe structural edits. Inserting a year across all sheets keeps alignment; inserting it on one sheet breaks every offset link without any error message.

Exceptions to the "same column" rule are explicit time-shift references, such as the prior-year value (L40 from column M) in a growth formula or a corkscrew opening balance. These are left-looking by design and are fine because they refer to the prior column on purpose and consistently across the row.

Enforcement: build every sheet from a template, link timeline rows from the master, and add an alignment check per sheet: =SUMPRODUCT(--(H2:Q2<>Timeline!H2:Q2)) must be 0.

Illustration

Sheet Column H Column M Column Q
Timeline (master) FY21A FY26E FY30E
Revenue FY21A FY26E FY30E
WC (misaligned) FY22A FY27E (blank)

With WC misaligned by one column, the FY27E receivables formula in WC!M sits in column M. A builder who writes =Revenue!M40*M12/365 (natural instinct) pulls FY26E revenue of ₹16,800 crore instead of FY27E ₹19,600 crore. At 6 days DSO, receivables are understated by (19,600 − 16,800) × 6 / 365 = ₹46.0 crore. The model still balances — the error flows consistently through BS and CFS — so the balance check will not catch it. Only the alignment check will.

Connections

The rule is implemented in s03 (master timeline) and s04 (identical column structure). It is a precondition for topic t2's one-row-one-formula rule (c01) and absolute/mixed referencing (s04), and for Week 15 integrated linking, where the 3-statement tab pulls from many schedules. It also underpins the Saturday assessment test of "consistent left-to-right column timelines across all tabs".

Practice Questions

Q1. Alignment check formula [L1]

Write an alignment check for each of eight calc sheets against a master timeline in columns H:Q and the formula that rolls them into a single check on the Checks sheet.

Q2. Silent error quantification [L2]

On a PPE tab misaligned by one column (one year later than master), FY28E depreciation is computed as =Inputs!O20/Useful_Life where Inputs!O20 holds FY28E capex of ₹850 crore and FY29E capex is ₹950 crore; useful life is 10 years, straight-line, full-year convention. Quantify the depreciation error in the affected year and explain why the balance check stays at zero.

💡 Hint: Work out which year's capex the misaligned column actually corresponds to on the PPE tab.

Q3. Interview probe [L2]

An interviewer asks: "If your model balances, why would you care whether columns are aligned?" Prepare a structured answer using a concrete example.
