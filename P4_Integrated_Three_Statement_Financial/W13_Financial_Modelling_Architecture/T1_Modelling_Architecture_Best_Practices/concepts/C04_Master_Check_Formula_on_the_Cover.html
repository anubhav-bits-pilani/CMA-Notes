Key concept

Master Check Formula on the Cover

Concept: Master check: =--(SUMPRODUCT(ABS(Check_Rows))>0.001) displayed on Cover with conditional formatting

Statement

The master check aggregates every individual check into one binary flag:

=--(SUMPRODUCT(ABS(Check_Rows))>0.001)

\[ \text{Master} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \sum_{i}\sum_{t} \left\lvert d_{i,t} \right\rvert > \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

where \( d_{i,t} \) is the difference reported by check \( i \) in period \( t \), \( \tau \) is the tolerance (0.001 in model units, i.e., ₹0.001 crore = ₹10,000), and Check_Rows is a named range covering all check rows across the full timeline on the Checks sheet. The double unary -- converts TRUE/FALSE to 1/0. The result is displayed on the Cover with conditional formatting (green for 0, red for 1).

Explanation

Why ABS. Differences of opposite sign could cancel: a +₹12 crore balance error in FY27E and −₹12 crore in FY28E would sum to zero. Taking absolute values first prevents cancellation.

Why SUMPRODUCT. SUM(ABS(range)) only works as an array formula in older Excel (Ctrl+Shift+Enter); SUMPRODUCT evaluates arrays natively in every version. In Excel 365, =--(SUM(ABS(Check_Rows))>Tolerance) also works because of dynamic arrays, but SUMPRODUCT is version-safe.

Why a tolerance. Floating-point arithmetic leaves residues like 2.27E-13 after many additions. A zero test would fail spuriously; a large tolerance (e.g., 1) would hide real errors. 0.001 in ₹ crore is a common choice. Prefer a named Tolerance input over the literal 0.001.

Check rows design. Each row in Check_Rows must hold a difference (not a flag), otherwise ABS is pointless; or, if rows hold flags (0/1), the SUMPRODUCT simply counts failures. Do not mix the two in one range. Non-numeric cells (text "N/A") inside the range cause #VALUE! in ABS — use 0 with a comment for non-applicable checks, or exclude the row.

Display. On the Cover:

=IF(Master_Check=0,"ALL CHECKS OK","CHECK ERRORS — see Checks tab")

with a conditional formatting rule: formula =Master_Check=1 → red fill, white bold font; =Master_Check=0 → green fill. Many builders also echo the master flag in cell A1 of each sheet.

Error values. If any check row contains #REF! or #DIV/0!, SUMPRODUCT returns the error, and the master cell shows an error — this is desirable (fail loudly). Do not wrap the master check in IFERROR (topic t2 pitfall).

Illustration

Checks sheet (₹ crore), named range Check_Rows = Checks!H10:Q13:

Check FY26E FY27E FY28E FY29E FY30E
Balance 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000
Cash 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000
Revolver breach 0.0 0.0 0.0 4.5 0.0
Alignment 0 0 0 0 0

\( \sum \lvert d \rvert = 4.5 > 0.001 \), so Master = 1 and the Cover shows red, even though the statements balance — the FY29E revolver requirement exceeds the sanctioned limit by ₹4.5 crore.

Practice Questions

Q1. Why not SUM? [L1]

Explain why =--(SUM(Check_Rows)>0.001) is inadequate and give a numerical example where it shows green despite errors.

Q2. Mixed check rows [L2]

A Checks range contains balance differences (₹ crore), a circularity status row with text "ON", and a revolver breach flag (0/1). Explain what the master formula returns, and redesign the Checks sheet so that the master check works and remains informative.

💡 Hint: Separate difference rows from status displays and decide how flags should be weighted.

Q3. Scenario-wide master check [L3]

Design a mechanism (without VBA) that shows on the Cover whether the master check passes in all three scenarios (Base, Bull, Bear) simultaneously, noting the sheet-placement constraint of Excel data tables.
