Key concept

Sign Convention Declared Once and Applied Everywhere

Concept: Sign convention declared once (e.g., costs positive and subtracted, or costs negative and summed) and applied everywhere

Statement

A model must adopt one of two sign conventions and state it on the Cover:

Convention Costs stored as Totals computed by Example: EBITDA
A. "Positive and subtract" Positive numbers Explicit subtraction =Revenue - COGS - Employee - Other
B. "Signed and sum" Negative numbers (outflows, expenses) SUM of the block =SUM(Revenue:Other_Exp)

The chosen convention must apply to every tab — inputs, schedules, statements and outputs — with any presentation-only sign flip (e.g., showing CFS outflows in brackets) handled by number formatting or a clearly labelled output row, not by changing the stored sign.

Explanation

Convention A mirrors Schedule III presentation: expenses appear as positive amounts under "Expenses", and the reader knows to subtract. It is common in Indian equity research and corporate FP&A. Its weakness is the CFS, where the natural presentation shows outflows as negatives; the builder must flip signs consciously (=-Capex).

Convention B (often used in banking and FAST-style models) stores every flow with its economic sign: revenue +, costs −, capex −, debt drawdown +, repayment −. Subtotals are simple SUMs, and the CFS falls out naturally. Its weakness is readability for accountants and the risk of double negatives (=-(-450)).

What goes wrong when conventions mix:

  • A capex input stored negative in Inputs, added in the PPE roll-forward (=Opening + Capex) reduces gross block.
  • A "Less: discounts" row stored negative and then subtracted increases revenue.
  • Variance analysis (FP&A) where "favourable" is positive for revenue but negative for costs becomes confused unless the convention is fixed.

Balance sheet. Assets and liabilities are normally stored positive under both conventions, with the balance check as Assets − (Equity + Liabilities). Contra items (accumulated depreciation, treasury shares, provision for doubtful debts) must be declared: stored positive and subtracted, or stored negative and summed.

Changes in working capital. Under either convention the CFS line is \( -(\text{NWC}_t - \text{NWC}_{t-1}) \); an increase in receivables is a cash outflow. Sign errors here are the commonest cause of cash check failures.

Formatting. Display negatives in brackets with a custom number format such as #,##0.0;(#,##0.0);–, so the presentation reads like an annual report without changing stored values.

Illustration

FY26E (₹ crore), Convention A:

Line Stored Formula
Revenue 16,800 link
COGS 9,240 link
Employee 1,350 link
Other opex 2,690 link
EBITDA 3,520 =16,800-9,240-1,350-2,690
Capex (Inputs) 950 positive
CFS: Purchase of PPE (950) =-PPE!M12

If the Inputs tab had stored capex as –950 and the CFS still used =-PPE!M12, the CFS would show +950 (an inflow) while the PPE roll-forward, adding –950, would reduce gross block by 950. Gross block would be ₹1,900 crore lower than intended relative to the correct roll-forward, and cash would be overstated by ₹1,900 crore relative to the correct model — yet the BS may still balance, because the two errors offset within assets.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choose and justify [L1]

For an FP&A model that will feed a Power BI variance dashboard, choose a sign convention and justify it, stating how favourable/adverse variances will be computed for revenue and for costs.

Q2. Find the sign errors [L2]

A model stores: dividends paid 320 (positive) on Inputs; equity roll-forward =Opening + PAT + Dividends; CFS financing =-Inputs!M50. Repayment of term loan is stored –200; Debt closing =Opening + Drawdown - Repayment. Identify each error and its effect on equity, debt, cash and the balance check.

💡 Hint: Track each item through both the balance sheet roll-forward and the CFS separately before comparing.

Q3. XBRL ingestion signs [L2]

An XBRL export of a company's P&L shows expenses with negative signs and "Changes in inventories" as +65 (meaning inventory decreased). Your model uses Convention A. Write the transformation rules you would apply in Power Query or Excel when loading these lines into Historicals.
