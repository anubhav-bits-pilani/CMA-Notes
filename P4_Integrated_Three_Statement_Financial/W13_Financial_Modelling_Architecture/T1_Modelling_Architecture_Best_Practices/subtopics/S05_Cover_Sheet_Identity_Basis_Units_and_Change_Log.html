Subtopic note

Cover Sheet: Identity, Basis, Units and Change Log

Scope: Cover sheet: company, entity basis (consolidated/standalone), units (₹ crore), sign convention, version, author, change log

Why it matters

The Cover sheet is the model's contract with its reader. Before anyone looks at a number, they need to know which entity it describes, whether the figures are consolidated or standalone, what unit they are in, which sign convention applies, which version they are holding and who built it. Many real disputes in Indian deal and audit work trace back to exactly these questions: a lender's model built on standalone numbers compared to an equity research model on consolidated numbers; a valuation that mixed ₹ lakh and ₹ crore; a CFO presenting version 7 while the board pack quoted version 5. A disciplined Cover prevents these.

Entity basis: consolidated vs standalone

For valuing an Indian listed group, consolidated statements are normally the right basis because they capture subsidiaries (for Trent: its subsidiaries, plus Trent Hypermarket Pvt Ltd — the Star grocery business, a joint venture with Tesco — accounted under the equity method, so Star revenue never appears in consolidated revenue), NCI and associates. Standalone statements matter for dividend capacity (Sec 123 distributable profits are measured at entity level), lender covenants (often on the borrower entity) and tax. The Cover must state one basis, and the Historicals tab must load only that basis for every year and every statement (topic t3 pitfall).

Units

Model in ₹ crore (1 crore = 10 million = 100 lakh; c06). Annual reports of large companies report in ₹ crore; many mid-caps report in ₹ lakh or ₹ million; XBRL filings may be in ₹ (absolute) or ₹ lakh. State the unit on the Cover and again in each sheet header. Per-share values (EPS, DPS, target price) are in ₹; shares outstanding in crore or million — state which, because EPS = PAT (₹ crore) ÷ shares (crore) gives ₹ directly.

Sign convention

Declare it once: for example, "All P&L costs, capex and dividends are entered as positive numbers and subtracted in totals; balance sheet amounts positive; CFS outflows shown negative." Then apply it everywhere. Mixed conventions produce sign errors that the balance check may not catch (e.g., in the CFS a positive capex added to cash).

Version control and change log

A change log table on the Cover:

Version Date Author Change Tabs affected Check status
v1.0 05-Nov-2025 AK Initial build, FY21A–FY25A loaded Historicals OK
v1.1 12-Nov-2025 AK Zudio store build added; Q2FY26 concall drivers Inputs, Revenue OK
v1.2 18-Nov-2025 RS (review) Fixed DSO link to wrong year; lease schedule reworked WC, Debt OK
v1.3 21-Nov-2025 AK Updated CMP and WACC DCF OK

Version on the Cover should be a formula-free input that matches the filename (s08). Some teams add =CELL("filename",A1) to display the actual file path — useful but volatile; display it on the Cover only.

Building the Cover

  • Keep it one screen; no scrolling.
  • Inputs on the Cover (version, author, date, status, basis text) are blue; the master check and scenario label are formula outputs.
  • Conditional formatting on the master check cell: green fill when 0, red when 1.
  • A short "How to use" box: where inputs live, how to change scenario, how to toggle circularity.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Both bases needed. A lender model may need standalone for covenants and consolidated for group leverage. Build two clearly labelled historical blocks or two models; never mix within one statement set.
  • Division III companies. An NBFC model (e.g., a housing finance subsidiary) follows Schedule III Division III — different line items, no current/non-current split. State the Division on the Cover.
  • Restated comparatives. If FY24 numbers were restated in the FY25 report (topic t3, s05), note on the Cover which report each year was taken from.
  • Confidential data. Management-case models with MNPI must carry a confidentiality legend and the SEBI PIT Regulations restrictions on circulation.
  • Currency of foreign subsidiaries. Consolidated figures are already in ₹; if you build a segment in USD, declare the conversion rate source on the Cover.

Key takeaways

  • The Cover states entity, basis, framework, FY convention, units, sign convention, version, author and status.
  • Choose consolidated or standalone once and load only that basis everywhere.
  • Display the master check and active scenario on the Cover with conditional formatting.
  • Maintain a change log that records what changed, where and the check status after the change.
  • Keep the Cover to one screen and free of logic other than status displays.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draft a Cover [L1]

Draft the complete Cover sheet content (as a table) for a Pidilite Industries consolidated model with FY21A–FY25A historicals and FY26E–FY30E forecasts, price date 21-Nov-2025, version v0.9 draft, built by you and to be reviewed by your team lead.

Q2. Basis conflict [L2]

Your colleague's model loads FY21–FY23 from consolidated statements and FY24–FY25 from standalone statements because the standalone annual report was easier to download. Explain three specific distortions this creates in revenue growth, EBITDA margin and net debt, and how the Cover and Historicals tab should prevent it.

💡 Hint: Think about subsidiaries' revenue, equity-accounted JVs and cash held in subsidiaries.

Q3. Sign convention failure [L2]

A model's Inputs tab records capex as negative numbers (–450, –520, –600 ₹ crore) while the PPE schedule adds capex to opening gross block and the CFS shows "Capex" as =-PPE!H12. Trace the effect on gross block, CFS investing cash flow and the balance check, and propose a single declared convention.

Q4. Change log as audit evidence [L3]

An internal audit team asks you to demonstrate that the board-approved budget model (v2.4) was not altered after approval. Describe what the Cover, change log, file naming and protection settings should show, and which additional evidence (e.g., Inquire comparison) you would provide.
