Key concept

Internal Consistency of Scenarios: Growth Needs Capex, Stress Needs Funding

Concept: Internal consistency: Bull growth carries higher capex and NWC; Bear carries deleverage/revolver use

Statement

A scenario is internally consistent when its operating, investment and financing drivers describe the same world:

\[ \Delta \text{Revenue}^{(s)} > 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta \text{Capex}^{(s)} \ge 0 \text{ and } \Delta \text{NWC}^{(s)} \ge 0 \quad (\text{Bull}) \]

\[ \Delta \text{FCF}^{(s)} < 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{Revolver draw}^{(s)} > 0 \text{ or } \text{Cash}^{(s)} \downarrow \text{ or deleveraging actions} \quad (\text{Bear}) \]

where \(\Delta\) denotes the difference versus Base for scenario \(s\), capex includes growth and maintenance capex, NWC is operating net working capital, and FCF is free cash flow. Growth must be paid for; shortfalls must be funded.

Explanation

Bull growth carries higher capex and NWC. Revenue growth in a retailer comes from more stores (fit-out capex, leases, inventory) and in a manufacturer from more capacity (expansion capex) and higher receivables and inventory. A Bull case with higher revenue but Base capex and NWC implies impossibly high asset turnover and overstates FCF and valuation. Because the drivers link physically (stores → capex; revenue and COGS × days → NWC), a driver-based model produces this automatically if the Bull case flexes store adds or capacity rather than a revenue growth rate. Note that Bull FCF can be lower than Base in early years.

Bear carries deleverage or revolver use. In a Bear case, EBITDA falls and working capital often worsens (longer DSO, higher DIO). Cash falls; if minimum cash is breached, the revolver draws (Week 15), raising interest cost and reducing covenant headroom. Management's plausible response — deferring growth capex, cutting dividends, slowing store openings, selling non-core assets — should be part of the Bear narrative (s05). Equally, if the company has net cash (as Trent or Pidilite historically), the Bear case shows that cushion eroding rather than a revolver draw. A Bear case in which net debt is unchanged from Base signals that the cash consequences were not modelled.

Consistency checks to include in the model:

  • Capex intensity: capex ÷ incremental revenue, by case, within a sensible band of history.
  • Asset turnover: revenue ÷ net fixed assets by case — Bull should not show a jump without a reason.
  • NWC days: Bear days should not be better than Base without a narrative.
  • Funding: revolver balance, minimum cash, net debt ÷ EBITDA and DSCR by case on the scenario summary (c03).
  • Margin direction: Bear EBITDA margin should fall because of operating leverage (Topic 5) unless offsets are argued.

Illustration

FY27E, illustrative retailer (₹ crore):

Line Bear Base Bull
Revenue 21,500 24,000 26,800
EBITDA 3,100 3,900 4,500
Store additions 120 180 230
Capex 1,050 1,450 1,850
Increase in NWC 850 400 560
Pre-financing FCF (EBITDA − tax − capex − ΔNWC − lease payments) (100) 600 520
Dividends 300 300 300
Cash flow after dividends (400) 300 220
Revolver draw (after using ₹100 crore of cash above minimum) 300 0 0
Net debt ÷ EBITDA 0.4x (0.2)x (0.2)x

Bull has higher EBITDA but lower FCF than Base (capex and NWC absorb growth). Bear's NWC increase is the largest despite lower revenue because inventory days rise; it draws the revolver. These relationships come from the drivers, not from typed outputs.

Connections

The coherence principle of s05 applied to cash and funding, using the drivers of s04 (store adds, capex, DSO/DIO/DPO). It anticipates Week 14 (capex and NWC schedules), Week 15 (revolver, minimum cash, stress tests through this switch) and the topic pitfall of a Bear case that cuts revenue but leaves costs, capex and NWC unchanged. Scenario summaries (c03) should display the credit metrics that reveal consistency.

Practice Questions

Q1. Spot the inconsistency [L1]

A Bull case shows revenue +15% vs Base, capex unchanged, NWC unchanged and FCF +30%. Explain why this is inconsistent and which drivers should change.

Q2. Bear funding [L2]

In the Bear case, FY27 pre-financing FCF is ₹(420) crore, opening cash ₹350 crore, minimum cash ₹200 crore and the revolver limit ₹500 crore at 9.5% interest (on average balance, ignore circularity for this question). Compute the revolver draw and approximate interest, and describe two management actions that could be modelled as Bear-case responses.

💡 Hint: Cash available above the minimum funds part of the shortfall before the revolver.

Q3. Consistency checks [L2]

Design four checks for the Checks sheet that flag internally inconsistent scenarios (one each for capex, NWC, margins and funding), stating the formula logic and whether each should be a hard error or a warning.
