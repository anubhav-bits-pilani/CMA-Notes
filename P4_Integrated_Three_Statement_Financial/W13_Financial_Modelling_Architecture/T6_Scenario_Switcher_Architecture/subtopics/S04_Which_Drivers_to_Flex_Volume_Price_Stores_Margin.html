Subtopic note

Which Drivers to Flex: Volume, Price, Stores, Margin, Wages, Capex, NWC

Scope: Drivers to flex: volume growth, pricing, store adds, gross margin, wage inflation, capex, DSO/DIO/DPO

Why it matters

A scenario is only as good as the drivers it moves. Flex too few (revenue growth alone) and the Bear case is unrealistically benign — costs, capex and working capital stay at Base levels while revenue falls. Flex too many, independently, and the cases become incoherent noise that no one can explain. The drivers listed in the scope are the standard set for an Indian consumer or retail model because together they cover the full chain from demand to cash: volume and price determine revenue; store additions determine footprint and investment; gross margin and wage inflation determine profitability; capex and working-capital days determine cash conversion. Each has a clear source of uncertainty that can be evidenced from history, concall commentary or industry data.

Core notes

The driver set and what it affects

Wage inflation (increment %) Employee cost Salary surveys (~8–10%) ±1–2 pp

Volume growth

The primary demand driver. For Pidilite, UVG in C&B; for Trent, LFL (which combines footfall and ticket size) and new-store productivity. Base anchors to guidance and category growth; Bull reflects stronger rural/urban demand or share gain; Bear reflects a demand slowdown. Volume moves variable costs automatically if the cost build is driver-based (Topic 5); if it does not, the cost model needs fixing before scenarios mean anything.

Pricing

Price realisation is flexed separately from volume because their profit impact differs: price drops almost entirely to gross profit (unless it is passing through costs). In an input-cost spike scenario, price and gross margin should move together through the pass-through mechanism, not independently.

Store additions

The key growth investment for retailers. Bull: faster rollout (more stores, more cities); Bear: slower openings, higher closures. Because store adds feed revenue and capex, leases, staff and opex, they must be flexed only in the driver block — every downstream schedule then follows. Timing matters: Bear cases often delay openings rather than cancel them.

Gross margin

Flex either gross margin directly (simpler) or its drivers — input cost index, pass-through ratio, mix (better). The driver approach ensures that a VAM spike in the Bear case reduces gross margin through the model's logic rather than a separately typed margin assumption that might contradict the input-cost row.

Wage inflation

Increment % applied to CTC. India-specific risk: tight labour markets, minimum wage revisions and labour-code changes can push increments up; Bear cases may combine higher increments with lower revenue, compressing margins twice.

Capex

Flex unit costs (capex per sq ft, per tonne), project timing and maintenance capex. Note the counter-intuitive directions: Bull growth needs more capex (more stores/capacity); Bear may cut growth capex but maintenance capex is sticky; cost overruns are a Bear feature in project-heavy businesses.

DSO / DIO / DPO

Working-capital days convert P&L into cash. In a Bear case, receivable days often lengthen (customers and dealers pay slower), inventory days rise (slower sell-through, markdown risk for apparel), and payable days may shorten (suppliers tighten credit; MSME payment rules under Sec 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections) require payment to micro and small suppliers within the MSMED Act limit (15 days, or up to 45 days by written agreement) for deductibility). In a Bull case, inventory investment rises with growth even if days are constant.

Note the deliberate asymmetries: Bull capex per sq ft is higher than Base (faster rollout, premium locations), and Bear has higher increments — wage pressure does not ease just because demand does.

Selecting which drivers to flex

Prioritise by impact × uncertainty. A quick method: run a tornado-style sensitivity (Week 16) of EV or FY30 EBITDA to a ±1 standard deviation move in each candidate driver; flex those at the top of the tornado in scenarios and leave the rest at Base. For Trent, store adds, LFL and gross margin typically dominate; for Pidilite, UVG, VAM-linked gross margin and A&P intensity.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Non-flexed drivers. Tax rate, depreciation rates and dividend policy usually stay at Base; if changed, document why (e.g., a Bear case with lower payout to preserve cash).
  • Macro drivers. CPI and FX can be flexed at the macro level and flow into price, wage and import cost drivers — more coherent than flexing each separately (s05).
  • Interdependence. Volume and price can be inversely related (price cuts to defend volume); express that through the narrative rather than independent tweaks.
  • Days vs absolute. Flex days, not rupee balances; balances then move with both days and activity.
  • Segment-specific flex. Bear for B2B may differ from Bear for C&B; segment-level blocks allow this.
  • Over-precision. Spreads should reflect genuine uncertainty; a Bear case only 1% below Base adds no information.

Key takeaways

  • Flex the full chain: volume, price, store adds, gross margin, wages, capex and NWC days.
  • Every flexed driver must be one that the cost, capex and NWC schedules actually respond to.
  • Flex gross margin through input-cost and pass-through drivers where possible.
  • Expect asymmetries: Bull needs more capex; Bear can carry higher wage inflation and worse NWC days.
  • Prioritise drivers by impact × uncertainty using a tornado sensitivity.
  • Leave genuinely non-scenario drivers on single input rows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Driver flex table for Pidilite [L2]

Construct Base, Bull and Bear values for FY27E for C&B UVG, price realisation, VAM landed cost, pass-through ratio, A&P % of revenue, wage increment, maintenance capex and DSO/DIO/DPO for a Pidilite-style company. Justify each spread with a source type (guidance, history, commodity outlook).

Q2. Working-capital flex [L2]

Revenue is ₹14,000 crore and COGS ₹7,000 crore in FY27E Base, with DSO 40, DIO 70, DPO 55. The Bear case has revenue −8%, COGS −6%, DSO 48, DIO 82, DPO 48. Compute operating NWC (receivables + inventory − payables) in each case and the cash impact versus Base.

💡 Hint: Receivables use revenue; inventory and payables use COGS; compute balances before differences.

Q3. Asymmetry judgement [L2]

Explain why a Bull case for a retailer might show lower free cash flow than Base in the first two years, and why this is not an error. Identify the drivers responsible.

Q4. Prioritising drivers [L3]

You have 22 candidate drivers in a Trent model but want to flex no more than eight. Describe how you would use a tornado analysis on FY30 EBITDA and DCF equity value to choose them, and how you would handle drivers that are highly correlated with each other.
