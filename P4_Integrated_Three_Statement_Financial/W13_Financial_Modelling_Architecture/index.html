How this week fits

Week 13 opens Phase 4 (Integrated Three-Statement Financial Modelling & Valuation). Phases 1–3 gave you the ingredients: Ind AS presentation and measurement (Phase 1), financial-statement analysis, ratios and forensic flags (Phase 2), and Excel, Power Query and Power BI automation (Phase 3). This week assembles them into the first layer of an institutional model for an NSE-listed company: the architecture (tab flow, timeline, column layout, Cover, Checks), the formatting and integrity rules (colour coding, one formula per row, no hardcodes), the normalised five-year history, and the operating forecast — driver-based revenue, cost structure and a scenario switch.

The six topics run in build order. Topics t1–t3 (architecture, integrity rules, historical ingestion) produce the skeleton and the Historicals tab; topics t4–t6 (revenue, costs, scenarios) produce the operating schedules that drive the P&L. Week 14 adds the balance-sheet schedules (working capital, PPE and capex, leases, debt, tax, equity) that consume this week's store additions, capacity and revenue drivers. Week 15 integrates the three statements, resolves circularity and debugs; Week 16 values the company (DCF, WACC, comps, sensitivities). Phase 5 then reuses the same drivers for budgets and rolling forecasts. Any shortcut taken this week — a typed timeline, a hardcoded growth rate, a plug in historicals — becomes a rebuild in Week 15, so the emphasis is on discipline over speed.

Prerequisites: confidence with Schedule III Division II statements, the main Ind AS effects on EBITDA and cash flow, Excel referencing and lookup functions, and CMA cost-behaviour concepts. The refresh items below summarise them.

Refresh before starting

Schedule III Division II statement structure

Schedule III Division II P&L, Balance Sheet and notes structure

  • Applicability: Division II for Ind AS companies other than NBFCs (Division III for NBFCs; Division I for Indian GAAP companies).
  • P&L (by nature): Revenue from operations; Other income; Total income; Expenses — cost of materials consumed, purchases of stock-in-trade, changes in inventories of FG/WIP/stock-in-trade, employee benefits expense, finance costs, depreciation and amortisation, other expenses; Profit before exceptional items and tax; Exceptional items; PBT; Tax (current, deferred); Profit from continuing operations; Profit from discontinued operations (post-tax); Profit for the period; OCI (items that will/will not be reclassified to P&L); Total comprehensive income; EPS. Consolidated: share of profit of associates/JVs, and allocation of profit and OCI to owners and NCI.
  • Balance sheet: non-current and current assets (PPE, CWIP, investment property, goodwill, other intangibles, ROU assets, financial assets, DTA, inventories, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents, bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents, loans, other assets); equity (share capital, other equity, NCI); non-current and current liabilities (borrowings, lease liabilities, trade payables split MSME/others, other financial liabilities, provisions, DTL, other liabilities, current tax liabilities).
  • 2021 amendments (effective FY2021-22): current maturities of long-term borrowings shown within current borrowings; lease liabilities separately; ageing schedules for trade receivables, payables and CWIP; promoter shareholding; key ratios with explanations for variances above 25%; title deeds, benami property, wilful defaulter, crypto and other additional disclosures.
  • Notes: the detail behind every caption — your mapping source (topic t3, s02).

Ind AS 115, 116, 7 and 19 effects

Ind AS 115, 116, 7 and 19 effects on reported revenue, EBITDA and cash flows

  • Ind AS 115: revenue at the transaction price allocated to performance obligations; net of GST (collected on behalf of government), trade discounts, volume rebates, returns and other variable consideration (constrained estimates); principal vs agent determines gross vs net (e.g., concessionaire sales in retail). Effect: revenue and gross margin depend on scheme accounting; dealer incentives reduce revenue rather than sitting in A&P.
  • Ind AS 116: lessee recognises ROU asset and lease liability; P&L shows ROU depreciation and interest instead of rent, except short-term, low-value and variable (non-index) payments such as revenue-share rent. Effect: EBITDA up (often 600–1,000 bps for retailers), EBIT slightly up, early-year PBT slightly down; CFO up and CFF down by lease principal (and interest, for non-financial entities under Ind AS 7.33).
  • Ind AS 7: cash and cash equivalents ≤ 3-month original maturity, overdrafts repayable on demand included (7.8); interest paid financing and interest/dividends received investing for non-financial entities (7.33); reconciliation to BS (7.45); changes in liabilities from financing activities disclosure (7.44A–E).
  • Ind AS 19: short-term benefits expensed as service is rendered; defined contribution (PF) expensed; defined benefit (gratuity) via projected unit credit — service cost and net interest in P&L, remeasurements in OCI; compensated absences (leave encashment) as other long-term benefits with remeasurements in P&L. Effect: employee cost includes actuarial service costs; OCI remeasurements bypass EBITDA.

Excel referencing, lookups and validation

Excel: relative/absolute referencing, INDEX/MATCH, CHOOSE, data validation, named ranges

  • References: A1 relative; $A1 column fixed (constant-column drivers); A$1 row fixed (timeline flags); $A$1 fully fixed (scalars — prefer names). F4 cycles.
  • INDEX/MATCH: =INDEX(Return_Row,MATCH(Lookup_Value,Lookup_Row,0)); exact match with 0, approximate with 1 on sorted data; non-volatile; two-way lookup with INDEX(Range,MATCH(r),MATCH(c)). XLOOKUP adds if_not_found and match modes.
  • CHOOSE: =CHOOSE(Index,Value1,Value2,Value3) — the canonical scenario selector.
  • Data validation: Data → Data Validation → List (e.g., 1,2,3 or a named list), whole number/decimal ranges, input messages and error alerts — used on the scenario cell and input ranges.
  • Named ranges: Name Box or Formulas → Define Name; workbook scope; Name Manager to audit; names for scalars (Tax_Rate, Scenario, Circ), direct references for time series (topic t1, s07).

CMA cost behaviour and contribution

CMA cost behaviour: fixed, variable, semi-variable and step costs; contribution

  • Variable costs change in proportion to activity (materials, freight per tonne, packing, revenue-share royalty); constant per unit.
  • Fixed costs do not change with activity within the relevant range (corporate salaries, insurance, fixed rent — now largely ROU depreciation and interest under Ind AS 116); fixed in total, falling per unit.
  • Semi-variable (mixed) costs have fixed and variable elements (power with fixed demand charges, telephone, maintenance); separate using high-low or regression.
  • Step (step-fixed) costs are fixed within a band and jump at thresholds (an area manager per 15 stores, a new distribution centre per 250 stores).
  • Contribution = sales − variable costs; contribution margin ratio = contribution ÷ sales; break-even = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio; degree of operating leverage = contribution ÷ EBIT.
  • Modelling relevance: forecasting all costs as % of revenue removes operating leverage (topic t5 pitfall).

Cross-cutting concepts

Driver hierarchy from macro to financial lines

Driver hierarchy: macro (GDP, CPI) → industry → company operating KPIs → financial lines

A credible forecast links each financial line to an operating driver, and each operating driver to its industry and macro context. At the top sit macro variables — real GDP growth (India ~6–7%), CPI inflation (RBI's target 4% ± 2%), WPI for input costs, private consumption, housing starts, the INR/USD rate. They set the envelope for industry growth: organised apparel retail growing faster than total apparel consumption as formalisation proceeds; adhesives and construction chemicals tracking real-estate and furniture activity. Industry variables then constrain company KPIs: Trent's store additions, area per store and revenue per sq ft; Pidilite's underlying volume growth, price increases and VAM-linked gross margin. Finally, KPIs drive financial lines: revenue, COGS, employee cost, opex, capex and working capital.

The hierarchy works in both directions. Top-down, it provides sanity checks: if the model implies Trent's revenue grows 30% a year for five years, what market share does that imply in organised apparel? Bottom-up, it forces granularity: you cannot forecast revenue from a single growth rate if you must explain it with stores × area × throughput. In the model, macro and industry assumptions sit at the top of the Inputs tab with sources, and the revenue build cross-checks against them (topic t4, s07).

Timeline flags and constant columns

Timeline flags and constant columns keep one formula per row across actual/forecast boundary

The one-formula-per-row rule collides with an obvious fact: historical years and forecast years are computed differently. Two structural devices resolve it. Timeline flags — 0/1 rows on the master timeline, such as =--(Year>Last_Actual_Year) for the forecast flag, first-forecast flags, phase flags and stub fractions — let a single formula choose its logic by multiplication or IF: =(1-M$5)*Hist!M12+M$5*L50*(1+M48). Constant columns — a fixed column (e.g., E) on every calc sheet holding row-specific scalars such as useful life, a cap or an inflation rate — let a formula refer to $E31 and copy across unchanged.

Together they make every row copyable from the first to the last column, which in turn makes R1C1 audits, Go To Special → Row differences and roll-forward work. When FY26 results arrive, changing Last_Actual_End to 31-Mar-2026 moves every flag; formulas switch automatically, and FY27E grows from the new actual. Without flags, roll-forward means editing hundreds of formulas — and introducing the errors the Saturday audit is designed to catch. Anchoring matters: flags are referenced with a fixed row (M$5), constants with a fixed column ($E31) (topic t2, s04 and c06; topic t1, s03).

Exercise drivers for Trent or Pidilite

Exercise drivers: Trent (stores, area, revenue/sq ft by format) or Pidilite (UVG, price, VAM-linked gross margin)

The exercise company determines the driver set. Trent Ltd is a store-rollout story: for each format (Westside, Zudio; Star is an equity-accounted JV and stays outside consolidated revenue), forecast opening stores + additions − closures = closing stores; average stores (or timing-weighted additions); average area per store; revenue per sq ft growing with like-for-like (LFL) growth; and a maturity ramp for new stores. Revenue = average area × revenue per sq ft. Costs follow store economics: store staff by store, rent (mostly ROU under Ind AS 116, with revenue-share elements in opex), power per sq ft, step-fixed regional and DC costs.

Pidilite Industries is a volume-price-mix story: segments (Consumer & Bazaar, B2B; domestic vs international) with underlying volume growth (UVG), price/mix, and gross margin linked to vinyl acetate monomer (VAM) prices with a pass-through lag. Costs: A&P as % of revenue (brand-led), freight per tonne or % of revenue, employee cost from headcount × CTC, and fixed overheads with inflation. Choosing one company means choosing its KPI disclosures — check investor presentations and concall transcripts first, because drivers must be sourced.

Assumption log with sources and rationale

Assumption log: each driver with source (annual report, concall guidance, industry report) and rationale

Every forecast driver must be documented: what it is, its units, its values by year and scenario, where it came from and why it is reasonable. The assumption log is a section of the Inputs tab (or an adjacent "Assumptions log" tab) with columns: driver, units, historical range (from the ratio block, topic t3 s07), Base/Bull/Bear values, source (document, page or timestamp, date), rationale, owner and last updated. Sources rank by reliability: audited annual report data and notes; Reg 33 results; management guidance in concall transcripts (quote speaker and date); investor presentations; industry reports (CRISIL, Redseer, Technopak, CMIE, company DRHPs of peers); and analyst judgement (labelled as such).

The log serves four users. Reviewers test reasonableness quickly; the next modeller can update drivers after the next concall; the investment committee sees where the model departs from history or guidance; and you defend each number in an interview. A good rationale is specific: "Zudio revenue/sq ft LFL +5% FY26E (FY23–FY25 average 8%; management indicated cannibalisation in dense clusters, Q2FY26 call)". The ICAEW Code's "documented assumptions" principle is satisfied by this log (topic t1, c02).

Indian FY labels and ₹ crore units

Indian FY (April–March) labelling and ₹ crore units consistent across all tabs

Indian companies report for financial years ending 31 March (Sec 2(41) of the Companies Act 2013). Label years by the period end: FY26 = 1-Apr-2025 to 31-Mar-2026, written FY26E (forecast) or FY26A (actual); state the convention on the Cover. Generate labels from the master timeline (="FY"&RIGHT(YEAR(Period_End),2)&IF(Actual_Flag=1,"A","E")) and link them to every sheet — never type them. Quarterly labels follow SEBI results conventions: Q1 = April–June, so 30-Jun-2025 is Q1FY26. When calendarising comps with December-year-end peers, convert explicitly.

Units: hold all monetary values in ₹ crore (1 crore = 100 lakh = 10 million). Convert every source at ingestion — ₹ lakh ÷ 100, ₹ million ÷ 10, absolute ₹ ÷ 10⁷ — on the raw-data tab, with a named conversion factor. Non-monetary drivers keep natural units (stores, mn sq ft, tonnes, ₹ per sq ft, ₹ per kg) with an explicit conversion at the revenue line. Per-share data are in ₹ with shares in crore. Every sheet header states "₹ crore unless stated", and every row has a units column. Mixed units in a row, or one tab in ₹ lakh, is an assessment failure.

Q1. Self-audit: hardcodes in forecast formulas (25 marks) [L3]
