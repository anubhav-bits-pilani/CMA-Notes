Key concept

Ind AS 115 Revenue Basis: Net of GST, Discounts and Variable Consideration

Concept: Ind AS 115 revenue is net of GST, trade discounts, returns and variable consideration

Statement

Under Ind AS 115, revenue is measured at the transaction price — the amount of consideration to which an entity expects to be entitled in exchange for transferring promised goods or services, excluding amounts collected on behalf of third parties such as GST (Ind AS 115.47). For forecasting:

\[ \text{Revenue} = \text{Gross invoiced value} - \text{GST} - \text{Trade discounts} - \text{Expected returns} - \text{Rebates, schemes and other variable consideration} - \text{Consideration payable to customers} \]

Variable consideration (volume rebates, schemes, price concessions, incentives, penalties, rights of return) is estimated using the expected value or most likely amount method and included only to the extent it is highly probable that a significant reversal will not occur (the constraint, Ind AS 115.50–56).

Explanation

Realisation drivers in the revenue build (price per unit, ARPU, revenue per sq ft) must be on the same net basis as reported "Revenue from operations", or the historical calibration and the forecast will disagree.

  • GST is collected on behalf of the government and is excluded. Pre-2017 excise duty was included in gross revenue and shown as an expense; models spanning FY17–FY18 need re-basing to compare growth.
  • Trade discounts and cash discounts offered at invoicing reduce revenue. Dealer schemes, quantity discounts and year-end rebates in Indian distribution (very common for Pidilite, paints, FMCG) are variable consideration, accrued as a reduction of revenue (refund liability) — not as an A&P or selling expense.
  • Returns — sales with a right of return (apparel retail, e-commerce) are recognised net of expected returns, with a refund liability and a right-to-recover-goods asset.
  • Consideration payable to customers (slotting fees, cooperative advertising paid to retailers, cashbacks) reduces revenue unless it buys a distinct good or service at fair value (Ind AS 115.70–72).
  • Principal vs agent — whether revenue is gross (principal controls the good before transfer) or net (agent, commission only) changes realisation and margin structure; consignment and concessionaire arrangements in retail need this analysis.
  • Loyalty points — a material right; part of the transaction price is deferred.

Schedule III Division II presents "Revenue from operations" with a note splitting sale of products, sale of services and other operating revenues (scrap sales, export incentives); model other operating revenue separately because it has different drivers.

Forecasting implication: if management guides a 4% list-price increase but plans heavier dealer schemes, net realisation growth is below 4%. Model list price and scheme intensity (as % of gross sales) separately when the data permit.

Illustration

An adhesives company invoices dealers ₹1,180 crore including 18% GST in a quarter. Trade discounts at invoice are ₹40 crore (on the pre-GST value), and a quarterly volume rebate scheme is expected to cost 3% of net-of-discount sales; expected returns are 1%.

Step ₹ crore
Gross invoice value 1,180.0
Less GST (1,180 × 18/118) (180.0)
Pre-GST value 1,000.0
Less trade discounts (40.0)
Net of discounts 960.0
Less volume rebates (3%) (28.8)
Less expected returns (1%) (9.6)
Revenue from operations 921.6

Net realisation is 92.2% of the pre-GST invoice value; a model using list price would overstate revenue by about 8.5%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Net revenue from invoice data [L1]

A retailer's POS sales in a month are ₹590 crore including 18% GST on half the sales and 5% GST on the other half (by pre-GST value, equal split). Expected returns are 2.5% of pre-GST sales and loyalty points worth ₹4 crore of standalone selling price are earned. Compute revenue recognised for the month, stating your treatment of the points.

Q2. Schemes vs A&P [L2]

A company records ₹120 crore of dealer volume rebates under "advertisement and sales promotion". Explain the correct Ind AS 115 treatment, the effect on revenue, gross margin and A&P/sales ratios, and how you would adjust the historicals before forecasting.

💡 Hint: Ask whether the payment buys a distinct service from the dealer at fair value.

Q3. Gross vs net for concessions [L3]

A department store hosts third-party brand concessions where the brand owns inventory and sets prices, and the store earns 28% of sales. Using the Ind AS 115 control indicators, explain whether the store should recognise gross sales or commission, and how this affects revenue per sq ft comparisons with peers.
