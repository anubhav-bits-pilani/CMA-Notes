Key concept

Capacity Revenue: Capacity × Utilisation × Price with a Practical Cap

Concept: Capacity revenue = Capacity × Utilisation × Price; cap utilisation at a practical maximum (~85–90%)

Statement

\[ R_t = K^{\text{eff}}_t \times U_t \times P_t \]

\[ U_t \le U^{\max}, \quad U^{\max} \approx 85\%\text{–}90\% \]

where \(R_t\) is revenue, \(K^{\text{eff}}_t\) is effective (time-weighted) installed capacity in units per year, \(U_t\) is capacity utilisation (production ÷ effective capacity), \(P_t\) is net realisation per unit and \(U^{\max}\) is the practical maximum utilisation. When volume is demand-driven, the model enforces the cap:

\[ Q_t = \min\left(Q^{\text{demand}}_t,\; K^{\text{eff}}_t \times U^{\max}\right) \]

Explanation

Nameplate capacity is a theoretical output at continuous operation. Real plants lose output to planned maintenance shutdowns (kiln relining in cement, catalyst changes in chemicals), unplanned breakdowns, grade or product changeovers, raw-material and power interruptions, and logistics constraints (rakes, trucks). Sustained utilisation above ~90% is rare for continuous process plants; multi-product batch plants often top out at 70–80%. Indian cement's industry utilisation has typically been in the 65–75% range, with efficient regional leaders in the 80s.

The cap is therefore not a modelling nicety but a physical constraint. A model that grows volume at 12% for five years on fixed capacity silently drives utilisation above 100% — revenue the company cannot produce. With the cap in place, demand above practical capacity shows up as a shortfall, which is the trigger to schedule expansion capex (linked through a project table to the PPE schedule).

Conditions:

  • Use effective capacity: new capacity commissioned mid-year counts pro rata and usually ramps up (first-year utilisation 50–70%).
  • Constrain at the bottleneck stage (clinker vs grinding in cement; reactor vs finishing in chemicals).
  • Calibrate \(U^{\max}\) to the company's best historical year and peer data; justify any figure above 90%.
  • Contract manufacturing volume sits outside the owned-capacity cap.
  • Realisation is per unit sold; if sales and production diverge materially, model finished-goods inventory.

Illustration

A specialty chemicals plant has 100 KTPA of capacity. Demand-driven volume forecasts are 82, 90 and 98 KT for FY26–FY28; \(U^{\max}\) = 88%; realisation ₹1.8 lakh per tonne (₹18 crore per KT).

Year Demand (KT) Cap (KT) Volume (KT) Utilisation Shortfall (KT) Revenue (₹ crore)
FY26 82 88 82 82% 0 1,476
FY27 90 88 88 88% 2 1,584
FY28 98 88 88 88% 10 1,584

Without the cap, FY28 revenue would be ₹1,764 crore at 98% utilisation — ₹180 crore of revenue with no plant to make it.

Volume_t    = MIN(Demand_t, Eff_Capacity_t*Max_Util)
Shortfall_t = MAX(0, Demand_t - Eff_Capacity_t*Max_Util)
Util_t      = IF(Eff_Capacity_t=0, 0, Volume_t/Eff_Capacity_t)

Connections

Core formula of the capacity-led build (s04). It is the guardrail against the topic pitfall "revenue growth beyond capacity without matching capex", and it links forward to Week 14, where capacity projects drive capex, CWIP and depreciation. In Topic 5, fixed manufacturing overheads are spread over utilised capacity — the source of operating leverage in manufacturing. In scenarios (Topic 6), Bull cases must add capacity, not just utilisation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Capped volume and revenue [L1]

A cement plant has 12 MTPA effective capacity, practical maximum utilisation of 85%, demand of 10.9 mn tonnes and realisation of ₹5,400 per tonne. Compute constrained volume, utilisation, shortfall and revenue in ₹ crore.

Q2. Calibrating the practical maximum [L2]

A company's historical utilisation was 71%, 79%, 92% and 86% in FY22–FY25. The FY24 figure included a temporary shift of maintenance shutdowns into FY25. Recommend a practical maximum for the model and justify it.

💡 Hint: Normalise the FY24–FY25 pair for the shifted shutdown before choosing the cap.

Q3. Review finding [L2]

A model shows Pidilite-style plant utilisation of 104% in FY29 with no expansion capex. List the possible causes (including contract manufacturing and nameplate re-rating), and the corrections you would recommend for each.
