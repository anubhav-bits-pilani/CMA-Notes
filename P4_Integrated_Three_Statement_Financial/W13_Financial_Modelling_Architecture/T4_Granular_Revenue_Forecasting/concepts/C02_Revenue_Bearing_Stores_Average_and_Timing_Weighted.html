Key concept

Revenue-Bearing Stores: Average and Timing-Weighted Counts

Concept: Revenue-bearing stores = (Opening + Closing) ÷ 2, or timing-weighted additions, never year-end count

Statement

Simple average:

\[ S^{\text{rev}}_t = \frac{S^{\text{open}}_t + S^{\text{close}}_t}{2} \]

Timing-weighted:

\[ S^{\text{rev}}_t = S^{\text{open}}_t + \sum_{k} A_k \times \frac{m_k}{12} - \sum_{j} C_j \times \frac{12 - n_j}{12} \]

where \(S^{\text{open}}_t\) and \(S^{\text{close}}_t\) are store counts at the start and end of year \(t\), \(A_k\) is the number of stores opened in month (or batch) \(k\), \(m_k\) is months those stores operate in the year, \(C_j\) is the number closed in batch \(j\) and \(n_j\) is months they operated before closure. The same formulas apply to retail area, capacity and active customers.

Explanation

Revenue is a flow earned over the year; the store count is a stock measured at a point in time. Multiplying a flow driver (revenue per store per year) by a closing stock implicitly assumes every store opened during the year traded for twelve months. For a chain adding 30–40% to its store base each year, that overstates revenue by roughly half the year's additions × revenue per store.

The simple average is exact when openings and closures are spread evenly through the year. Indian retail openings are rarely even: chains push openings before the festive season (September–October) and in Q4 to meet annual targets. When management discloses quarterly store additions (most retailers do, in quarterly presentations), use the timing-weighted version — assume each quarter's openings occur mid-quarter, so Q1 adds operate 10.5 months, Q2 7.5, Q3 4.5 and Q4 1.5.

Closures work in reverse: a store closed in September contributed five or six months of revenue. Many models simply net closures into the average; for a chain with high churn, timing-weight both.

Keep the closing count for things that are genuinely stocks: closing area feeds next year's opening, the closing lease liability, fixtures in gross block, and per-store inventory at the balance-sheet date.

Exceptions: a business with a single large opening at the start of the year (e.g., a new plant commissioned 1 April) needs no averaging; the effective capacity is the full year.

Illustration

A QSR chain opens FY26 with 600 stores and adds 120: 15 in Q1, 25 in Q2, 30 in Q3 and 50 in Q4. No closures. Revenue per store per year is ₹2.5 crore.

Method Revenue-bearing stores Revenue (₹ crore)
Closing count (wrong) 720 1,800.0
Simple average (600 + 720) ÷ 2 = 660 1,650.0
Timing-weighted 600 + 15 × 10.5/12 + 25 × 7.5/12 + 30 × 4.5/12 + 50 × 1.5/12 = 646.25 1,615.6

The closing-count method overstates revenue by about ₹184 crore (11%) versus the timing-weighted answer. The simple average still overstates by about ₹34 crore because openings are back-ended.

Rev_Stores_t = Open_Stores_t + SUMPRODUCT(Qtr_Adds_t, Qtr_Months)/12

where Qtr_Months is a constant input row {10.5, 7.5, 4.5, 1.5}.

Connections

Used in the store rollout build (s02), the vintage cohort weight in the opening year (s03, the 0.5 factor), the capacity build (s04, effective capacity) and active customer averages (s05). It is the direct remedy for the topic pitfall "full-year revenue from stores opened at year-end". In Topic 5, the same averaging applies to headcount for employee cost; in Week 14, closing counts drive balance-sheet items such as inventory and lease liabilities.

Practice Questions

Q1. Average vs timing-weighted [L1]

A retailer has 250 stores at 1 April 2025, opens 60 (10 in H1, 50 in H2) and closes 8 (all in Q1) during FY26. Revenue per store per year is ₹18 crore. Compute revenue-bearing stores and revenue under the closing-count, simple-average and half-yearly timing-weighted methods.

Q2. Quarterly additions formula [L2]

Write an Excel formula that computes revenue-bearing stores for a forecast year from opening stores, a row of four quarterly additions and a row of four quarterly closures, using a constant row of operating months. Explain which references should be absolute and why.

💡 Hint: Use SUMPRODUCT against a fixed months row for both additions and closures.

Q3. Review comment [L2]

Reviewing a model, you find FY27 revenue = closing stores × revenue per store, while lease liabilities use the average store count. Write the review comment, explaining both errors and the corrected treatment.
