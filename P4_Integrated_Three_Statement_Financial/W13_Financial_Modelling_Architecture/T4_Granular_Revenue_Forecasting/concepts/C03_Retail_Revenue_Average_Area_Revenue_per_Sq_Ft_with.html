Key concept

Retail Revenue: Average Area × Revenue per Sq Ft with LFL

Concept: Retail revenue = Average retail area (mn sq ft) × Revenue per sq ft; Revenue per sq ft_t = prior × (1 + LFL)

Statement

\[ R_t = \bar{A}_t \times D_t \div 10 \]

\[ D_t = D_{t-1} \times (1 + \text{LFL}_t) \]

where \(R_t\) is retail revenue in ₹ crore, \(\bar{A}_t = (A^{\text{open}}_t + A^{\text{close}}_t) \div 2\) is average retail (trading) area in million sq ft, \(D_t\) is revenue per sq ft per year in ₹ (trading density), and \(\text{LFL}_t\) is like-for-like (same-store) sales growth. The division by 10 converts ₹ million to ₹ crore (mn sq ft × ₹ per sq ft = ₹ million; 1 crore = 10 million).

Explanation

Store counts are an imperfect volume measure because stores differ in size; area normalises them. Revenue per sq ft is the productivity measure retailers, mall developers and analysts use to compare formats and peers — DMart's very high revenue per sq ft versus department stores reflects its grocery-led, fast-turn model.

Rolling density forward at LFL embeds a specific assumption: that the whole average area trades at the mature density of comparable stores. That is right for a steady-state chain but wrong for a fast-expanding one, where new stores are still ramping (s03). Two fixes: (a) apply the formula to mature area only and model new vintages with a maturity curve; or (b) apply a blended density that is LFL-grown mature density × a fleet maturity factor.

Other conditions:

  • Retail area, not gross area. Use the company's disclosed metric consistently; some disclose "area under operation" including back-of-house.
  • Revenue basis. Density must be computed on the same revenue definition you forecast — net of GST and returns, excluding royalty or franchise income and online sales without an area driver.
  • LFL definition. The company's LFL may cover only stores older than 12 or 24 months and may exclude refurbished stores; it measures the mature base, which is exactly what \(D_t\) should represent.
  • LFL composition. LFL = (1 + footfall growth) × (1 + conversion change) × (1 + average bill value growth) − 1; where data allow, forecast its components for scenario flexing.

Illustration

A value-fashion format has 4.40 mn sq ft at 31 March 2025 and adds a net 1.20 mn sq ft in FY26. FY25 mature density was ₹14,000 per sq ft; FY26 LFL is 6%.

\[ \bar{A}_{FY26} = (4.40 + 5.60) \div 2 = 5.00 \text{ mn sq ft} \]

\[ D_{FY26} = 14{,}000 \times 1.06 = ₹14{,}840 \text{ per sq ft} \]

\[ R_{FY26} = 5.00 \times 14{,}840 \div 10 = ₹7{,}420 \text{ crore} \]

If one-third of the average area is in stores under two years old trading at an average 80% of mature density, the blended revenue is \(5.00 \times (2/3 + 1/3 \times 0.80) \times 14{,}840 \div 10 = ₹6{,}925\) crore — about 7% lower.

Avg_Area_t = (Open_Area_t + Close_Area_t)/2
Density_t  = Density_{t-1}*(1+LFL_t)
Revenue_t  = Avg_Area_t*Density_t/10

Connections

This is the core formula of the store rollout build (s02) and the Trent segment build (s06), refined by the vintage approach (s03). It depends on c02 (average, not closing, area) and on unit discipline from w13 t1 c06 (₹ crore, mn sq ft). In Phase 2 it is the peer-benchmarking metric for retailers; in Topic 5, store opex and rent per sq ft use the same area driver, so revenue and cost scale together.

Practice Questions

Q1. Density roll-forward [L1]

A department-store format has 3.20 mn sq ft at 1 April 2025 and 3.56 mn sq ft at 31 March 2026. FY25 density was ₹11,500 per sq ft and FY26 LFL is 8%. Compute FY26 revenue in ₹ crore.

Q2. Deriving historical density [L2]

From an annual report you have: FY24 closing area 2.9 mn sq ft, FY25 closing area 3.7 mn sq ft, FY25 format revenue ₹4,620 crore including ₹180 crore of online sales. Compute FY25 revenue per sq ft on an area-consistent basis and list the adjustments you made.

💡 Hint: Remove revenue that has no area driver before dividing by average area.

Q3. When does LFL × average area overstate revenue? [L2]

Explain why applying LFL-grown density to the entire average area overstates revenue for a retailer growing area at 35% a year, and describe two ways to correct the model.
