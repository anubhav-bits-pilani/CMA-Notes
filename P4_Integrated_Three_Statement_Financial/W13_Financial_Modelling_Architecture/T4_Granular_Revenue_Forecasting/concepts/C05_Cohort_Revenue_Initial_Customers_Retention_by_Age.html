Key concept

Cohort Revenue: Initial Customers × Retention by Age × ARPU by Age

Concept: Cohort revenue_t = Σ cohorts (Initial customers × Retention_age × ARPU_age)

Statement

\[ R_t = \sum_{c \le t} N_c \times r_{a} \times \text{ARPU}_{a} \times w_{a}, \qquad a = t - c \]

where \(R_t\) is revenue in year \(t\), \(c\) indexes acquisition cohorts (the year customers were acquired), \(N_c\) is initial (gross) customers acquired in cohort \(c\), \(a\) is cohort age in years, \(r_a\) is cumulative retention at age \(a\) (\(r_0 = 1\), non-increasing), \(\text{ARPU}_a\) is annual revenue per active customer at age \(a\) (optionally × a calendar price index), and \(w_a\) is a time weight (typically 0.5 at age 0 to reflect mid-year acquisition, 1 thereafter).

Explanation

A single churn rate applied to the whole customer base treats a customer acquired last month and a five-year loyal customer as equally likely to leave. In practice churn is front-loaded: many customers try a product and drop off quickly, while survivors are sticky. ARPU usually also rises with tenure — more products, higher limits, upgrades. A cohort model captures both shapes: the retention curve and the ARPU-by-age curve.

The model also separates two growth engines:

  • Acquisition — the size of each new cohort, linked to marketing spend and CAC.
  • Base dynamics — how earlier cohorts decay and expand.

In a maturing business, revenue from existing cohorts becomes the majority, so revenue is more predictable; in an early-stage business, revenue depends heavily on the next cohort, so a fall in acquisition (a regulatory change, a marketing cut) hits revenue immediately.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Retention curves should be non-increasing; reactivation is modelled separately if material.
  • Calendar effects (tariff hikes, price increases) apply to all cohorts in a year — use a year index, not the age curve.
  • ARPU must be on the Ind AS 115 revenue basis (net of incentives and cashbacks; gross vs net per principal-vs-agent).
  • A net revenue retention (NRR) above 100% means expansion outweighs churn for a cohort's revenue, even though customer count falls.

Illustration

Cohorts of 10, 14 and 18 lakh customers acquired in FY25, FY26 and FY27. Retention 100% / 62% / 50% at age 0/1/2; ARPU ₹1,200 / ₹1,500 / ₹1,650; age-0 weight 0.5. Revenue in FY27 (₹ crore = lakh × ₹ ÷ 100):

Cohort Age in FY27 Active (lakh) ARPU (₹) Revenue (₹ crore)
FY25 2 10 × 50% = 5.00 1,650 82.5
FY26 1 14 × 62% = 8.68 1,500 130.2
FY27 0 18 × 100% × 0.5 = 9.00 1,200 108.0
Total 22.68 320.7

One-third of FY27 revenue depends on the current year's acquisition.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cohort revenue for one year [L1]

Compute FY26 revenue in ₹ crore for cohorts of 6 lakh (FY24), 9 lakh (FY25) and 12 lakh (FY26) customers with retention 100% / 58% / 46% at age 0/1/2, ARPU ₹2,000 / ₹2,300 / ₹2,500 and an age-0 weight of 0.5.

Q2. Net revenue retention [L2]

A B2B SaaS cohort of 400 customers has ARPU of ₹6 lakh at age 0 (full year), retention of 88% at age 1 and ARPU of ₹7.4 lakh at age 1. Compute the cohort's net revenue retention and explain what it implies for the shape of the cohort revenue curve.

💡 Hint: Compare the cohort's age-1 revenue with its age-0 revenue.

Q3. Cohort vs single churn [L2]

Explain, with a numerical sketch of your choosing, why a single blended churn rate applied to the total customer base will misforecast revenue when acquisition slows sharply.
