Key concept

Revenue Identity: Volume × Realisation and Compounded Growth

Concept: Revenue = Volume × Realisation; (1 + g_revenue) = (1 + g_volume) × (1 + g_price/mix)

Statement

\[ R_t = Q_t \times P_t \]

\[ (1 + g_R) = (1 + g_Q) \times (1 + g_{P/M}) \]

where \(R_t\) is revenue from operations in year \(t\) (Ind AS 115 basis), \(Q_t\) is volume sold (units, tonnes, litres, or a volume index), \(P_t\) is average net realisation per unit (\(R_t \div Q_t\)), \(g_R\) is revenue growth, \(g_Q\) is volume growth and \(g_{P/M}\) is the combined growth in realisation from price changes and mix shift. Rearranged, the realisation growth implied by any pair of revenue and volume growth rates is:

\[ g_{P/M} = \frac{1 + g_R}{1 + g_Q} - 1 \]

Explanation

Revenue is a product of two quantities, so growth rates multiply, they do not add. The additive shortcut (\(g_R \approx g_Q + g_{P/M}\)) ignores the cross-term \(g_Q \times g_{P/M}\), which is small at low growth but material at Indian growth levels: 15% volume and 10% price give 26.5% revenue growth, not 25%. Over a five-year forecast the difference compounds into a visible error in revenue and, through margins, in EBITDA.

The identity matters in modelling because the two components drive different things:

  • Volume drives physical costs — raw materials, packing, freight, power — and the need for capacity, stores, people and working capital.
  • Realisation drives revenue without proportional physical cost, so price/mix changes flow disproportionately to gross margin (unless they are themselves pass-through of input costs).

Splitting \(g_{P/M}\) further into pure price and mix (s01) requires product-level data. When only aggregate revenue and a volume growth figure (e.g., Pidilite's UVG) are disclosed, the combined price/mix term is derived as a residual using the rearranged formula above — never as revenue growth minus volume growth.

Conditions and cautions: volume must be measured in a consistent unit across years; for multi-product companies use a volume index if units are not additive (litres of paint and kg of putty cannot be summed meaningfully). Realisation must be net of GST, discounts and schemes so that it ties to reported revenue (c06).

Illustration

A construction-chemicals business reported FY25 revenue of ₹4,000 crore. Management guides FY26 volume growth of 10% and price/mix growth of 3%.

\[ R_{FY26} = 4{,}000 \times 1.10 \times 1.03 = ₹4{,}532 \text{ crore} \]

Revenue growth is 13.3%, not 13%. Conversely, if a company reports 9% value growth and 11% UVG, implied price/mix is \(1.09 \div 1.11 - 1 = -1.8\%\) — consistent with price cuts passed on after a fall in input costs, a common pattern when VAM or crude-linked inputs deflate.

In Excel, with the volume index in row 20 and realisation in row 21:

Volume_t       = G20*(1+Inputs!H$30)
Realisation_t  = G21*(1+Inputs!H$31)
Revenue_t      = H20*H21
Implied_PM_t   = (1+Rev_Growth_t)/(1+Vol_Growth_t)-1

Connections

This identity is the base of every granular revenue build in this topic: store models (area × density), capacity models (capacity × utilisation × realisation) and cohort models (customers × ARPU) are all volume × price structures. It links to Topic 5 (variable COGS scale with volume, not with realisation), to the scenario driver blocks in Topic 6 (volume and price are flexed separately), and to price-volume-mix variance analysis in Phase 5 FP&A. The pitfall "double-counting price inflation inside volume growth" is the failure to respect this identity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Implied price/mix from disclosures [L1]

A paints company reports value growth of 4.5% and volume growth of 10.8% in a quarter. Compute the implied price/mix growth and suggest two business explanations for the sign of the result.

Q2. Additive vs multiplicative error over five years [L2]

Revenue is ₹2,000 crore in FY25. Volume grows 12% a year and price/mix 6% a year for five years. Compute FY30 revenue using the multiplicative identity and using the additive shortcut (18% a year), and the difference in ₹ crore.

Q3. Why separate volume from price? [L2]

A CFO asks why the FP&A model separates volume and price when the budget is approved in rupees. Explain, with one cost line and one capex line as examples, what decisions would be distorted by a value-only forecast.

💡 Hint: Think about which costs scale with physical units and which assets are sized on volume.
