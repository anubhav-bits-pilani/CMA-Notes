Subtopic note

Store Rollout Revenue Build: Stores, Area, Revenue per Sq Ft, LFL

Scope: Store rollout: opening stores + additions − closures, average area per store, revenue per sq ft, LFL/SSSG

Why it matters

For organised retail — Trent, Avenue Supermarts (DMart), V-Mart, Aditya Birla Fashion, Titan's jewellery and eyewear chains, quick-service restaurants such as Jubilant FoodWorks or Devyani — revenue growth is overwhelmingly explained by two things: how much retail space the company operates and how productive that space is. Sell-side models for these names are built store-by-store or format-by-format, and buy-side analysts test management's rollout guidance ("we will add 200 Zudio stores") against funding capacity, real-estate availability and new-store productivity. A store rollout build also drives the rest of the model: store adds drive capex and fit-out cost (Week 14), store opex and staff cost (Topic 5), lease additions under Ind AS 116 and inventory per store. If the revenue build is a flat growth rate, none of those links can be made.

Core notes

The store roll-forward

The store count is a stock that rolls forward each year:

\[ \text{Closing stores}_t = \text{Opening stores}_t + \text{Additions}_t - \text{Closures}_t \]

with Opening\(_t\) = Closing\(_{t-1}\). Additions are an input by format and year, sourced from management guidance and recent run-rates; closures are often 2–5% of the opening base for apparel and QSR (relocations, underperforming malls). Model gross additions and closures separately — net additions hide churn, and closures carry their own costs (impairment of ROU assets and fit-outs, lease termination).

Revenue does not come from the closing count. A store opened in February earns two months of revenue in that financial year. Use the average:

\[ \text{Revenue-bearing stores}_t = \frac{\text{Opening}_t + \text{Closing}_t}{2} \]

or, if openings are back-ended (common in Indian retail, where openings cluster around the festive season and in Q4), a timing-weighted version — opening stores plus additions × fraction of year operated (see c02).

Average area per store

Indian retail productivity is usually disclosed per square foot of retail (selling) area, not gross leasable area. Trent discloses total store area and store counts by format; DMart discloses total retail business area. Model area as:

\[ \text{Closing area}_t = \text{Opening area}_t + \text{Additions}_t \times \text{Avg area of new store} - \text{Closures}_t \times \text{Avg area of closed store} \]

New stores may be larger or smaller than the existing average — Westside's newer stores are larger than legacy ones; Zudio stores are typically 7,000–10,000 sq ft. Track the average area of new additions as a separate input so the fleet average drifts realistically. Revenue uses average area, \((\text{Opening area} + \text{Closing area}) \div 2\), for the same reason as store counts.

Revenue per sq ft

Revenue per sq ft (sometimes "trading density" or "throughput") is the productivity driver:

\[ \text{Revenue}_t = \text{Average retail area}_t \times \text{Revenue per sq ft}_t \]

Compute history from reported numbers: standalone revenue from the format ÷ average area. Use the same revenue definition you forecast (net of GST, returns and discounts; excluding franchise/royalty income). Revenue per sq ft is reported in ₹ per sq ft per year; with area in million sq ft (mn sq ft), ₹ per sq ft × mn sq ft ÷ 10 gives ₹ crore (because 1 crore = 10 million).

LFL / SSSG

Like-for-like (LFL) growth, also called same-store sales growth (SSSG), is the revenue growth of stores open for the whole of both comparison periods — typically stores operating for at least 12 (sometimes 18 or 24) months. It strips out new-store revenue and isolates productivity: footfall, conversion, average transaction value and price. Revenue per sq ft of the mature base rolls forward with LFL:

\[ \text{Rev per sq ft}_t = \text{Rev per sq ft}_{t-1} \times (1 + \text{LFL}_t) \]

LFL itself decomposes into transactions growth × average bill value growth, and average bill value into units per bill × average selling price — useful when management discloses footfall data. Indian apparel LFL has ranged from negative (FY21) to 15–20% (FY23 rebound); a steady-state assumption of 5–8% (mostly inflation plus modest volume) is common.

Worked illustration — one format

A value-fashion format (₹, mn sq ft):

Line FY25A FY26E FY27E
Opening stores 400 545 705
Additions 160 180 190
Closures 15 20 25
Closing stores 545 705 870
Avg area per new store ('000 sq ft) — 8.0 8.0
Closing area (mn sq ft) 4.40 5.68 7.00
Average area (mn sq ft) 3.70 5.04 6.34
Revenue per sq ft (₹) 15,000 15,750 16,380
Revenue (₹ crore) 5,550 7,938 10,385

FY26E closing area = 4.40 + 180 × 0.008 − 20 × 0.008 = 5.68 (assuming closed stores also average 8,000 sq ft). Revenue per sq ft grows 5% LFL in FY26 and 4% in FY27. FY26E revenue = 5.04 × 15,750 ÷ 10 = ₹7,938 crore.

A refinement: blended revenue per sq ft should be lower than mature density because new stores are still ramping (s03). Applying the mature density to the average area overstates revenue when the fleet is growing fast.

Model layout

On a Revenue sheet, one block per format (Westside, Zudio, Star): store roll-forward rows, area roll-forward rows, productivity rows, revenue row. Inputs (additions, closures, new-store size, LFL) are blue on Assumptions and linked in green. Add checks: closing stores ≥ 0; closures ≤ opening stores; format revenue sum = total revenue; revenue per sq ft growth within a sanity band (flag LFL outside −10% to +20%).

Avg_Area_t   = (Open_Area_t + Close_Area_t) / 2
Revenue_t    = Avg_Area_t * Rev_psf_t / 10          ' mn sq ft x Rs/sq ft -> Rs crore
Rev_psf_t    = Rev_psf_{t-1} * (1 + LFL_t)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Franchise vs owned stores. Franchise stores generate royalty or wholesale revenue, not retail revenue. Keep them in a separate roll-forward with their own revenue per store.
  • Shop-in-shops and online. Trent's online and Tata CLiQ-linked sales and Star's hypermarket formats need separate treatment; e-commerce revenue has no area driver — use GMV × take-rate or orders × AOV.
  • Store expansions vs new stores. A refit that adds 3,000 sq ft to an existing store increases area without a store addition; LFL definitions usually exclude expanded stores for a year.
  • LFL definition drift. Companies change the LFL base (12 vs 24 months, inclusion of relocated stores). Document the definition used in each year and avoid splicing inconsistent series.
  • Calendar effects. Festive timing (Diwali in October vs November) shifts revenue between quarters, not years, but a 53-week effect or a leap year can distort annual LFL marginally.
  • Ind AS 116 linkage. Each new store is a lease addition; the store count must reconcile to the lease schedule to avoid opex/depreciation mismatches in Topic 5.

Key takeaways

  • Closing stores = opening + additions − closures; model gross additions and closures separately.
  • Revenue uses average stores and average area, never year-end counts.
  • Track new-store size separately so fleet average area drifts with format strategy.
  • Revenue = average area × revenue per sq ft; mn sq ft × ₹ per sq ft ÷ 10 = ₹ crore.
  • LFL/SSSG isolates productivity of mature stores; mature density rolls forward at LFL.
  • Store adds must feed capex, opex, staff cost and lease schedules.

Practice Questions

Q1. Roll-forward and revenue for a jewellery chain [L1]

A jewellery chain has 310 stores at 31 March 2025 with average area 3,800 sq ft. It plans 45 additions and 5 closures in FY26, new stores averaging 4,200 sq ft and closed stores 3,000 sq ft. Revenue per sq ft in FY25 was ₹1,05,000 and LFL is expected at 9%. Compute FY26 closing stores, closing area, average area and revenue in ₹ crore.

Q2. Back-ended openings [L2]

A QSR chain opens 120 stores in FY26, of which 70 open in January–March 2026. Mature revenue per store is ₹2.4 crore a year. Compare FY26 new-store revenue using (a) the simple average count and (b) a quarterly timing-weighted approach assuming openings are evenly spread within each quarter and 50 openings are spread over April–December. Explain which you would use and why.

💡 Hint: Weight each quarter's openings by the average months operated in the year.

Q3. Reconciling disclosed data [L2]

Trent's annual report gives closing store counts and total area by format, but not revenue by format. Describe how you would derive historical revenue per sq ft by format, what assumptions you must make, and how you would test them against management commentary.

Q4. Build specification — multi-format revenue block [L3]

Specify the rows, inputs, formulas and checks for a five-year revenue build covering three formats with different store sizes, LFL and closure rates. Include how the block will feed the capex and lease schedules in Week 14.

Q5. LFL vs total growth [L1]

A retailer reports total revenue growth of 32% and LFL of 4%. Explain what this tells you about the business, and two risks this combination raises for the forecast.
