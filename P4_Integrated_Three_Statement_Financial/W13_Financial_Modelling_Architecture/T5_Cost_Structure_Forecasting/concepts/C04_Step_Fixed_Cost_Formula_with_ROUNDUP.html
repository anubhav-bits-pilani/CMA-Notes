Key concept

Step-Fixed Cost Formula with ROUNDUP

Concept: Step-fixed: =ROUNDUP(Stores/Stores_per_Manager,0)*Cost_per_Manager

Statement

\[ \text{Step cost}_t = \left\lceil \frac{D_t}{k} \right\rceil \times c_t \]

where \(D_t\) is the activity driver in year \(t\) (stores, area, plants, states, volume), \(k\) is the capacity of one step (e.g., stores per manager), \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) rounds up to the next whole number (Excel ROUNDUP(x,0); CEILING.MATH(x) is equivalent for positive numbers) and \(c_t\) is the annual cost of one step, itself inflating: \(c_t = c_{t-1} \times (1 + \pi_t)\). In Excel:

=ROUNDUP(Stores/Stores_per_Manager,0)*Cost_per_Manager

with Stores pointing to the relevant store row for the period, and Stores_per_Manager and Cost_per_Manager as named inputs (the cost as a time-series row if it inflates).

Explanation

A step-fixed (stepped) cost is constant over a range of activity and jumps when the range is exceeded — one area manager for up to 15 stores, two for 16–30, and so on. ROUNDUP captures this because the 16th store requires a second manager even though only one store's worth of the second manager's capacity is used.

Design choices:

  • Which driver period? Average stores for costs incurred as stores open (managers); next year's closing stores for costs that must lead (DCs); states entered for regional overheads.
  • Ratchet. =MAX(ROUNDUP(...), Prior_Steps) prevents the count from falling when the driver dips temporarily, reflecting that organisations rarely dismantle capacity quickly.
  • Floating-point guard. =ROUNDUP(ROUND(Stores/Stores_per_Manager,6),0) avoids an extra step from tiny binary errors (e.g., 45.0000000001).
  • Whole vs smooth. For many small steps (hundreds of managers) the unrounded ratio is a good approximation and avoids artificial volatility; for few large steps (DCs, plants), keep ROUNDUP.
  • Zero driver. ROUNDUP(0,0) = 0; a minimum fixed level (at least one regional office) needs MAX(1, …).

Step costs are a model expression of the CMA "relevant range": outside a range, "fixed" costs change.

Illustration

Stores (average) 118, 152, 190 for FY26–FY28; 15 stores per area manager; cost ₹28 lakh per manager in FY26 rising 9% a year.

Year Stores ÷ 15 Managers (ROUNDUP) Cost per manager (₹ lakh) Cost (₹ crore)
FY26 7.87 8 28.0 2.24
FY27 10.13 11 30.5 3.36
FY28 12.67 13 33.3 4.33

FY27 shows a 50% jump in cost on a 29% rise in stores: the step is visible in the margin bridge. The smoothed approximation (unrounded ratio) would give ₹2.20, ₹3.09 and ₹4.21 crore.

Practice Questions

Q1. ROUNDUP step [L1]

A chain requires one cluster head per 25 stores at ₹42 lakh a year (FY26), inflating 8%. Average stores are 310, 395 and 480 in FY26–FY28. Compute cluster heads and cost each year in ₹ crore.

Q2. Formula robustness [L2]

Rewrite =ROUNDUP(Stores/Stores_per_Manager,0)*Cost_per_Manager so that it (a) never decreases year to year, (b) is protected against floating-point artefacts, (c) has a minimum of two managers, and (d) handles a zero value in Stores_per_Manager without an error. Explain each modification.

💡 Hint: Nest the guards: a divide-by-zero test outside, then MAX for the floor and the ratchet.

Q3. Whole vs smoothed judgement [L2]

For each of (a) 900 store managers, (b) 6 distribution centres, (c) 40 regional sales offices, state whether you would use whole steps or a smoothed ratio, with reasons.
