Key concept

Employee Cost Formula: Average Headcount × CTC with Increments

Concept: Employee cost = Average headcount × CTC_t; CTC_t = CTC_t−1 × (1 + increment %), India wage inflation ~8–10%

Statement

\[ E_t = \overline{HC}_t \times \text{CTC}_t \]

\[ \overline{HC}_t = \frac{HC_{t-1} + HC_t}{2}, \qquad \text{CTC}_t = \text{CTC}_{t-1} \times (1 + i_t) \]

where \(E_t\) is employee benefits expense (excluding ESOP charges, which are forecast separately), \(HC_{t-1}\) and \(HC_t\) are opening and closing headcount, \(\text{CTC}_t\) is average annual cost to company per employee (salary, allowances, employer PF/ESI, gratuity and leave accruals, bonus, insurance), and \(i_t\) is the average annual increment. In Indian corporate salary surveys, average increments have been roughly 8–10% a year; front-line retail and manufacturing roles are often lower, technology and financial services higher.

Explanation

Average headcount converts a stock (people employed at a date) into a flow (person-years paid during the year). Using closing headcount overstates cost in a hiring year; using opening understates it.

The increment rate is the forecast's wage-inflation driver. Average CTC can, however, move differently from the increment because of mix: rapid hiring of new store staff at entry-level wages pulls average CTC down even while every continuing employee gets 9%. Two treatments:

  1. Group-level build — separate store, plant, sales and corporate groups, each with its own CTC and increment. Mix effects then emerge from differing headcount growth. Preferred.
  2. Mix adjustment — a single CTC with an explicit "mix effect" input. Simpler but harder to justify.

Timing: if increments are effective from July or October rather than April, the FY average CTC growth is a blend of two increment cycles:

\[ g^{\text{CTC}}_{FY} = \left(1 + i_{t-1}\right)^{\frac{m}{12}} \left(1 + i_t\right)^{\frac{12-m}{12}} - 1 \approx \frac{m}{12} i_{t-1} + \frac{12 - m}{12} i_t \]

where \(m\) is the number of months before the effective date (3 for July).

Calibration: CTC = historical employee benefits expense (ex-ESOP) ÷ average permanent headcount. If contract workers are in "other expenses", exclude them from both numerator and denominator. Large deviations between implied CTC growth and disclosed increments usually point to mix, capitalised salaries or one-offs (VRS, bonuses).

Illustration

Headcount 8,400 at 31 March 2025 and 9,600 at 31 March 2026; FY25 CTC ₹5.5 lakh; increment 9% effective 1 April 2025.

\[ \overline{HC}_{FY26} = (8{,}400 + 9{,}600) \div 2 = 9{,}000 \]

\[ \text{CTC}_{FY26} = 5.5 \times 1.09 = ₹5.995 \text{ lakh} \]

\[ E_{FY26} = 9{,}000 \times 5.995 \div 100 = ₹539.6 \text{ crore} \]

Using closing headcount would give ₹575.5 crore — ₹36 crore (6.7%) too high.

Avg_HC_t  = AVERAGE(HC_{t-1}, HC_t)
CTC_t     = CTC_{t-1}*(1+Increment_t)
Emp_Cost_t= Avg_HC_t*CTC_t/100

Connections

The formula for s03 (employee cost build), with store headcount linked to store counts from t4 s02 and corporate headcount stepped as in s06/c04. It mirrors the averaging logic of revenue-bearing stores (t4 c02). Employee cost growing at increments while revenue grows faster is a source of operating leverage (c03, s07). In Phase 5, FP&A headcount budgets use the same structure monthly; in Phase 1, Ind AS 19 governs the gratuity and leave components.

Practice Questions

Q1. Average headcount cost [L1]

Corporate headcount rises from 1,150 to 1,240 during FY26; FY25 CTC is ₹16.8 lakh and the increment is 10% effective 1 April 2025. Compute FY26 corporate employee cost in ₹ crore.

Q2. Mix effect [L2]

A retailer's average CTC fell 2% in FY25 although increments averaged 8%. Store staff (CTC ₹2.4 lakh) grew from 9,000 to 14,000 while corporate staff (CTC ₹15 lakh) stayed at 1,000. Explain the result numerically and recommend a model structure that handles it.

💡 Hint: Compute the weighted average CTC under old and new headcount mixes at constant group CTCs.

Q3. October increments [L2]

A company gives 9% increments effective 1 October each year. Compute the effective FY average CTC growth, and explain how a change to 1 April effective dates would affect the FY of transition.
