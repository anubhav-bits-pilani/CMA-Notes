Key concept

Ind AS 116 Effect on Opex and EBITDA: Rent to Depreciation + Interest

Concept: Post-Ind AS 116: fixed lease rent leaves opex → ROU depreciation + lease interest; EBITDA rises

Statement

For a lessee under Ind AS 116 (applicable to annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2019), fixed and in-substance fixed lease payments are capitalised:

\[ \text{Opex}^{\text{post}} = \text{Opex}^{\text{pre}} - L^{\text{fixed}}_t \]

\[ \text{EBITDA}^{\text{post}} = \text{EBITDA}^{\text{pre}} + L^{\text{fixed}}_t \]

\[ \text{P\&L charge}^{\text{post}}_t = \text{Dep}^{\text{ROU}}_t + r \times \text{LL}^{\text{open}}_t \;(\text{approx.}) \]

where \(L^{\text{fixed}}_t\) is fixed lease rent that would have been expensed pre-Ind AS 116, \(\text{Dep}^{\text{ROU}}_t\) is depreciation of right-of-use assets (in D&A), \(r\) is the discount rate (implicit rate or incremental borrowing rate) and \(\text{LL}^{\text{open}}_t\) is the opening lease liability (interest in finance costs). Short-term, low-value and variable (non-index) payments stay in opex.

Explanation

Ind AS 116 removed the operating/finance lease distinction for lessees. Economically nothing changed — the same rent is paid — but presentation moved:

  • EBITDA rises by the fixed rent removed. For a leased-store retailer, EBITDA margin can rise by 7–10 percentage points.
  • EBIT rises slightly (by the interest component of rent, since depreciation replaces the principal-like portion).
  • PBT changes little across a mature portfolio but is lower in the early years of each lease because interest is front-loaded (higher liability early); a fast-growing chain with many young leases shows lower PBT than under the old standard.
  • Operating cash flow rises, financing outflow rises (lease principal and, depending on policy, interest); free cash flow definitions must deduct lease payments to stay comparable.
  • Net debt increases by the lease liability if leases are included — state the convention.

Consequences for modelling:

  1. Remove fixed rent from the opex build; forecast only short-term, low-value and variable rent there.
  2. Forecast ROU assets and lease liabilities in a lease schedule (Week 14), with additions driven by store openings.
  3. Decide valuation consistency: if EBITDA is post-116, either include lease liabilities in net debt (EV/EBITDA) or deduct lease payments in FCF (DCF) — never mix (e.g., post-116 EBITDA with net debt excluding leases overstates value).
  4. Show a pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA memo (EBITDA − lease payments) for covenants and comparisons with pre-2019 history.

Illustration

A retailer pays fixed rent of ₹500 crore a year. Opening lease liability is ₹2,800 crore at 9%; ROU asset ₹2,400 crore over a remaining 7 years. Pre-116 EBITDA would be ₹900 crore.

Line (₹ crore) Pre-Ind AS 116 Post-Ind AS 116
EBITDA 900 1,400
D&A (ROU) — 342.9
EBIT 900 1,057.1
Finance cost (lease interest) — 252.0
Pre-tax contribution of these lines 900 805.1

EBITDA rises ₹500 crore; PBT is lower by about ₹95 crore in this year because the portfolio's interest plus depreciation exceeds rent (consistent with a relatively young lease book).

Practice Questions

Q1. EBITDA uplift [L1]

A QSR chain's pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA is ₹620 crore on revenue of ₹5,200 crore with fixed rent of ₹480 crore and revenue-share rent of ₹60 crore. Compute post-116 EBITDA and margin, stating which rent remains in opex.

Q2. Valuation consistency [L2]

An analyst values a retailer at 25x post-116 EBITDA but subtracts only bank debt to reach equity value. Explain the error, its direction, and two consistent alternatives.

💡 Hint: Match the earnings measure with the claims deducted in the EV-to-equity bridge.

Q3. Young vs mature lease book [L2]

Explain why a retailer opening 200 stores a year reports lower PBT under Ind AS 116 than it would have under the old operating-lease accounting, and when this effect reverses.
