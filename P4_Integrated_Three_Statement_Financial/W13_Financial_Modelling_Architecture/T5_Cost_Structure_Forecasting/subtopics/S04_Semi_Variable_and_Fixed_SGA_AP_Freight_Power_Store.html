Subtopic note

Semi-Variable and Fixed SG&A: A&P, Freight, Power, Store Opex, Overheads

Scope: Semi-variable and fixed SG&A: A&P as % of revenue, freight, power, store opex, corporate overheads with inflation

Why it matters

Schedule III lumps most operating costs into "Other expenses", with a note listing power and fuel, rent, repairs, advertisement and sales promotion (A&P), freight and forwarding, travel, legal and professional, CSR and miscellaneous items. For Pidilite, other expenses run around 15–18% of revenue; for a retailer, 10–15%. Treating the whole line as one % of revenue throws away the cost behaviour that CMA training makes second nature: some costs scale with revenue, some with volume, some with stores, and some are fixed and rise only with inflation. Getting this split right is what gives the model operating leverage (s07, c03) and makes scenarios behave sensibly — in a Bear case, revenue falls but rent, corporate overheads and much of store opex do not.

Core notes

Classifying each line by driver

Cost line Behaviour Driver Typical forecast formula
A&P (advertising, promotion) Discretionary, managed as % of revenue Revenue Revenue × A&P %
Freight and forwarding (outward) Variable with volume and distance Volume (tonnes) × freight per tonne Volume × rate × (1 + diesel/freight inflation)
Power and fuel (plant) Semi-variable: fixed base + variable with production Production volume, power tariff Fixed base × (1 + inflation) + Volume × kWh per unit × tariff
Store opex (electricity, maintenance, housekeeping, security, CAM) Fixed per store, semi-variable with area Stores or area Average area × opex per sq ft × (1 + inflation)
Packing (if not in COGS) Variable Volume Volume × unit cost
Corporate overheads (IT, legal, audit, travel, insurance) Fixed with step-ups Inflation, scale steps Prior × (1 + CPI) + step-ups
Royalty, commissions Variable Revenue Revenue × rate
CSR Rule-based 2% of average net profit (Sec 135) 2% × average Sec 198 net profit (broadly PBT with adjustments) of the preceding 3 years

A&P as % of revenue

A&P is the classic discretionary cost managed to a % of revenue (Pidilite and paint companies typically spend 3–5%; Trent spends relatively little because private-label value retail relies on stores and word of mouth). It is legitimately modelled as % of revenue, with the % flexed in scenarios — companies often increase A&P intensity during input-cost deflation (margin headroom) and cut it in cost spikes. Remember Ind AS 115: dealer schemes and consideration payable to customers are revenue deductions, not A&P (t4 c06).

Freight

Outward freight depends on tonnes shipped, distance (lead distance from plant to market) and diesel prices. For cement, freight is 20–25% of revenue and the single biggest cost; for adhesives it is smaller but material. Use volume from the revenue build:

\[ \text{Freight}_t = Q_t \times f_{t-1} \times (1 + \text{freight inflation}_t) \]

Mode shift (rail vs road), new plants closer to markets (reduced lead distance) and diesel price changes are the main freight drivers.

Power and fuel

A semi-variable cost: a fixed component (lighting, utilities, minimum demand charges) and a variable component per unit produced. Use the high-low or regression method from cost accounting to split historical power cost:

\[ \text{Variable rate} = \frac{\text{Cost}_{\text{high}} - \text{Cost}_{\text{low}}}{\text{Volume}_{\text{high}} - \text{Volume}_{\text{low}}} \]

Then forecast fixed × (1 + inflation) + volume × variable rate × tariff index. Captive solar or wind (common in Indian manufacturing) lowers the tariff index.

Store opex

Store running costs (electricity, CAM charges, maintenance, housekeeping, security, consumables) are best driven by average area or average stores:

\[ \text{Store opex}_t = \bar{A}_t \times \text{opex per sq ft}_{t-1} \times (1 + \text{inflation}_t) \]

This makes store opex move with the rollout and creates operating leverage on LFL growth: an LFL-driven revenue increase raises revenue per sq ft without raising opex per sq ft beyond inflation.

Corporate overheads with inflation

Head-office costs (IT, audit, legal and professional, insurance, travel, office rent if short-term) rise with inflation and occasional step-ups (a new ERP, a new regional office):

\[ \text{Overheads}_t = \text{Overheads}_{t-1} \times (1 + \text{CPI}_t) + \text{Step-up}_t \]

India CPI around 4–5% is the default inflation input (RBI's target is 4% ± 2%); use WPI or specific indices for industrial costs. Keep one inflation row on the Assumptions sheet that every fixed cost references, so the scenario switch can flex it in one place.

Worked illustration

A consumer business has FY25 revenue ₹10,000 crore growing 12% to ₹11,200 crore in FY26 (volume +8%). FY25 other expenses:

Line (₹ crore) FY25A Driver FY26E
A&P 420 (4.2% of revenue) 4.2% of revenue 470.4
Freight 380 Volume +8%, freight rate +4% 426.8
Power and fuel 150 (fixed 50, variable 100) Fixed +5%; variable volume +8%, tariff +3% 52.5 + 111.2 = 163.7
Corporate overheads 300 CPI 5% + step-up ₹12 crore 327.0
Total 1,250 (12.5%) 1,387.9 (12.4%)

A % of revenue approach would give ₹1,400 crore (12.5%); the driver build is ₹12 crore lower because fixed costs grow slower than revenue. With revenue falling 5%, the gap reverses and widens — which is the point.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Reclassification across years. Companies move items between "other expenses" and COGS or employee cost (e.g., contract labour). Re-cast history before computing ratios.
  • CSR under Sec 135 is 2% of average net profits of the preceding three years (computed under Sec 198) for qualifying companies; it is a P&L expense, but unspent amounts may sit in a separate account.
  • Forex and provisions in other expenses. Exchange losses, ECL provisions and impairment often appear in other expenses; exclude them from the opex driver base and forecast separately (or as zero).
  • Rent. Only short-term, low-value and variable rent remains in opex under Ind AS 116 (s05).
  • Inflation vs efficiency. Fixed costs do not always rise with inflation — cost-saving programmes and scale efficiencies matter; document any below-inflation assumption.
  • Volume-driven freight in retail. For retailers, inward freight is usually in inventory cost (COGS); outward e-commerce delivery cost is variable with orders.

Key takeaways

  • Split "Other expenses" by behaviour: variable, semi-variable, fixed and discretionary.
  • A&P is legitimately % of revenue; freight and packing scale with volume; store opex with area.
  • Split semi-variable costs (power) into fixed and variable using high-low or regression.
  • Fixed overheads rise with one central inflation input plus explicit step-ups.
  • Exclude forex, provisions and impairments from the opex driver base.
  • The split creates operating leverage in growth and downside protection analysis in Bear cases.

Practice Questions

Q1. High-low split of power cost [L1]

A plant's power cost was ₹62 crore at 140 KT production and ₹78 crore at 180 KT. Split the cost into fixed and variable components and forecast FY26 power cost at 200 KT with a 4% tariff increase and 5% inflation on the fixed part.

Q2. Store opex per sq ft [L2]

A retailer's FY25 store opex was ₹540 crore on average area of 9.0 mn sq ft. FY26 average area is 11.2 mn sq ft and opex inflation is 6%. FY26 revenue rises 30%. Compute FY26 store opex and its % of revenue if FY25 revenue was ₹9,000 crore.

Q3. Mapping the other-expenses note [L2]

You are given a Schedule III other-expenses note with 14 lines including rent, CSR, exchange loss, provision for doubtful debts, freight, power, A&P, repairs, legal and professional, travel, insurance, rates and taxes, commission to directors and miscellaneous. Classify each by behaviour and driver, and identify which lines you would exclude from the opex driver base.

💡 Hint: Separate operating cost behaviour from accounting items such as provisions and forex.

Q4. Bear case behaviour [L3]

In a Bear case revenue falls 8% (volume −10%, price +2%). Using the classification in these notes, explain how each cost category should behave, and estimate the direction of EBITDA margin change versus a model with all costs at a constant % of revenue.
