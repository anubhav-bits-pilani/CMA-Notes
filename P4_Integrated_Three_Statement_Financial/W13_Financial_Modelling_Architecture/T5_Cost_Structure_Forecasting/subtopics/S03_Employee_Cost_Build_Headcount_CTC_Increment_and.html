Subtopic note

Employee Cost Build: Headcount × CTC × Increment and Ind AS 19

Scope: Employee cost: headcount by function/store × average CTC × annual increment; Ind AS 19 gratuity/leave

Why it matters

Employee benefits expense is typically the second-largest operating cost for Indian retailers, IT services, pharma and consumer companies — around 6–9% of revenue for apparel retail, 10–12% for Pidilite, 50%+ for IT services. It is also the cost line with the strongest inflation and the most structural fixity: salaries rise every April whether or not revenue grows, and headcount is added ahead of new stores and plants. Modelling employee cost as a % of revenue makes margins static and hides two of the most common margin stories — operating leverage in a growth year and wage-inflation squeeze in a slowdown. A headcount × CTC build also links directly to the store rollout (staff per store) and to Ind AS 19 obligations that affect both the P&L and other comprehensive income.

Core notes

Structure of the build

Split headcount into groups with different drivers:

Group Driver Example
Store/front-line staff Stores × staff per store (or area × staff per '000 sq ft) Zudio store staff
Plant/factory staff Capacity or production shifts Pidilite plants
Sales force Distribution reach, dealers, territories C&B field sales
Corporate/support Step-fixed with scale; grows slowly Finance, HR, IT, design

For each group:

\[ \text{Employee cost}_{g,t} = \overline{HC}_{g,t} \times \text{CTC}_{g,t} \]

\[ \overline{HC}_{g,t} = \frac{HC_{g,t-1} + HC_{g,t}}{2}, \qquad \text{CTC}_{g,t} = \text{CTC}_{g,t-1} \times (1 + i_{g,t}) \]

where \(HC\) is headcount, CTC is average annual cost to company per employee and \(i\) is the annual increment %. Using average headcount matters for the same reason as average stores: hires made in March cost one month.

What CTC includes

Indian CTC typically includes basic salary, HRA, allowances, employer PF contribution (12% of basic, capped wage ceiling where applicable), employer ESI for eligible employees, gratuity accrual, bonus (statutory bonus under the Payment of Bonus Act for eligible wage levels), performance pay and insurance. Schedule III's "Employee benefits expense" includes salaries and wages, contribution to provident and other funds, share-based payment expense (Ind AS 102) and staff welfare. For the model:

  • Calibrate average CTC = historical employee benefits expense ÷ average headcount (from the annual report's BRSR or Director's report employee count).
  • Exclude ESOP (Ind AS 102) charge from the CTC base and forecast it separately — it is non-cash and often adjusted out of EBITDA (w13 t3 s04).
  • Note the Code on Wages (with the other labour codes notified as effective from 21 November 2025) redefines "wages" so that excluded allowances above 50% of remuneration are added back; this can increase PF and gratuity costs. Treat its forecast impact as a stated assumption.

Increment assumptions

Indian salary increments have averaged roughly 8–10% in recent surveys, varying by sector (higher in tech and financial services, lower in front-line retail). Increments usually take effect in April (start of FY) — some companies in July or October, in which case the FY increment is time-weighted. Separate:

  • Merit/inflation increment for continuing employees.
  • Mix effects — hiring at lower grades (new-store staff) reduces average CTC; promotions raise it.
  • Minimum wage revisions — state-level revisions twice a year (variable dearness allowance) for front-line staff.

Ind AS 19 — gratuity and leave

  • Gratuity (Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972: 15 days' wages per completed year after five years' service, subject to a ceiling of ₹20 lakh) is a defined benefit plan. Under Ind AS 19, service cost and net interest are in P&L (employee benefits expense and finance cost respectively — many Indian companies show net interest in employee cost; check the note); remeasurement gains/losses (actuarial changes, return on plan assets) go to OCI and are never recycled.
  • Compensated absences (leave encashment) are usually "other long-term employee benefits"; under Ind AS 19 their remeasurements go to P&L, not OCI.
  • Provident fund is generally a defined contribution plan (expense = contribution), except exempt trusts with guaranteed returns, which may be defined benefit.

Forecasting approach: model gratuity and leave expense as a % of salary cost calibrated from history (service cost ÷ salaries); do not attempt actuarial projections. Keep OCI remeasurements out of the P&L forecast (assume zero). The balance-sheet provisions grow with salary cost; cash payments are smaller than the expense while the workforce is young.

Worked illustration — retailer staff cost

A format has 545 stores at 31 March 2025, 705 at 31 March 2026. Each store has 22 staff; corporate headcount is 900 rising to 960. Store CTC is ₹2.6 lakh in FY25, corporate ₹14.0 lakh; increments are 8% (store) and 10% (corporate). Gratuity and leave add 4% of salaries.

Line FY26E
Average store headcount (545 + 705) ÷ 2 × 22 = 13,750
Store CTC 2.6 × 1.08 = ₹2.808 lakh
Store staff cost 13,750 × 2.808 ÷ 100 = ₹386.1 crore
Average corporate headcount (900 + 960) ÷ 2 = 930
Corporate CTC 14.0 × 1.10 = ₹15.4 lakh
Corporate staff cost 930 × 15.4 ÷ 100 = ₹143.2 crore
Gratuity and leave (4%) ₹21.2 crore
Employee benefits expense (ex-ESOP) ₹550.5 crore

Lakh × headcount ÷ 100 converts to ₹ crore. Note that store staff cost grows with store count; corporate cost grows mainly with increments — which is the source of operating leverage.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Contract/outsourced staff. Security, housekeeping and some store staff are outsourced; their cost sits in "other expenses" (contract labour). Map consistently and forecast with the same driver.
  • Variable pay. Sales incentives and bonuses vary with performance; model a variable component as % of revenue or EBITDA growth.
  • Capitalised employee cost. Staff cost on projects (software, plant construction) may be capitalised under Ind AS 16/38; reported expense is net of it.
  • Attrition. High front-line attrition (40–60% a year in retail) affects hiring cost and average CTC, but headcount is still the driver.
  • One-offs. Voluntary retirement schemes (VRS) and labour-code transition costs are exceptional items.
  • Headcount data quality. Annual reports give permanent employee counts at year-end (BRSR); calibrate average CTC accordingly and document the definition.

Key takeaways

  • Build employee cost by group: store/plant staff with operating drivers, corporate as step-fixed.
  • Cost = average headcount × CTC; CTC rolls forward with increments (~8–10% in India).
  • Calibrate CTC from historical expense ÷ average headcount, excluding ESOP charges.
  • Gratuity is a defined benefit plan: service cost in P&L, remeasurements in OCI; leave remeasurements in P&L.
  • Model gratuity/leave as % of salaries; do not forecast OCI remeasurements.
  • Link front-line headcount to stores or capacity so cost moves with the business.

Practice Questions

Q1. Headcount × CTC build [L1]

A plant workforce is 1,240 at 31 March 2025 and 1,380 at 31 March 2026; average CTC in FY25 is ₹6.2 lakh with a 9% increment from 1 April 2025. Corporate staff of 310 (constant) have CTC of ₹18 lakh with an 11% increment. Compute FY26 employee cost in ₹ crore.

Q2. Mid-year increments [L2]

A company gives increments of 10% effective 1 July each year. Average CTC for FY25 (April 2024 – March 2025) was ₹9.0 lakh, with the FY25 increment also effective 1 July 2024. Compute the FY26 average CTC and explain why it is not ₹9.9 lakh.

💡 Hint: Work out the monthly CTC before and after each July increment and weight by months.

Q3. Ind AS 19 presentation [L2]

A company's actuarial valuation shows gratuity current service cost ₹14 crore, net interest ₹3 crore, actuarial loss from change in discount rate ₹9 crore, and leave encashment actuarial loss ₹2 crore. State where each item appears in the financial statements and which ones you would include in forecast EBITDA.

Q4. Labour codes scenario [L3]

Explain how the redefinition of "wages" under the Code on Wages could affect PF and gratuity cost for a retailer whose basic pay is 35% of CTC. Design a scenario input that captures the impact and state how you would source it.
