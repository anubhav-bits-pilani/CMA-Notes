Rent Forecasting Under Ind AS 116: What Stays in Opex

Scope: Rent under Ind AS 116: only short-term, low-value and variable (revenue-share) rent stays in opex

Why it matters

For retailers, QSR chains, hospitals, warehousing and any business operating from leased premises, rent is one of the largest cash costs — 8–12% of revenue for apparel and QSR. Since Ind AS 116 became effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2019, most of that rent no longer appears in operating expenses: a lessee recognises a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a lease liability, and the P&L shows depreciation and interest instead. A model that forecasts "rent" as an opex line and builds an ROU/lease schedule double counts the cost; a model that removes all rent from opex understates EBITDA costs for leases that stay off balance sheet. Trent's leased store network makes this central to the Week 13 exercise, and the choice of pre- or post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA drives multiples and covenant comparisons.

Core notes

The lessee model in brief

At commencement, the lessee recognises:

\[ \text{Lease liability}_0 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{L_k}{(1 + r)^k}, \qquad \text{ROU asset}_0 = \text{Lease liability}_0 + \text{Initial direct costs} + \text{Prepaid rent} - \text{Incentives} + \text{Restoration cost} \]

where \(L_k\) are lease payments included in the liability (fixed payments, in-substance fixed payments, index-linked payments at the current index, residual value guarantees, purchase and termination options if reasonably certain) and \(r\) is the interest rate implicit in the lease or, usually for Indian retailers, the incremental borrowing rate. The P&L thereafter shows:

  • Depreciation of ROU asset (straight-line over lease term) — in D&A, below EBITDA.
  • Interest on lease liability (effective interest) — in finance costs, below EBITDA.

The cash flow statement shows principal repayment and interest on lease liabilities in financing activities (interest classification per policy under Ind AS 7), so operating cash flow also rises.

What stays in opex

Three categories of lease cost remain in "Other expenses" (rent):

  1. Short-term leases — lease term of 12 months or less at commencement, with no purchase option, if the lessee elects the exemption (by class of underlying asset). Examples: seasonal pop-up stores, temporary warehouses, short-term guest houses.
  2. Low-value assets — leases of assets of low value when new (applied lease by lease), such as laptops, small office equipment and furniture. A store premises is never low-value.
  3. Variable lease payments not based on an index or rate — notably revenue-share rent in malls (e.g., the higher of minimum guarantee and 10–12% of store sales, with the minimum guarantee capitalised and only the excess expensed), and usage-based payments. Payments linked to CPI or a fixed escalation (e.g., 15% every three years) are not variable in this sense — they are included in the liability.

Also typically in opex: common area maintenance (CAM) charges and other non-lease components, if the lessee has not elected to combine lease and non-lease components.

Forecasting approach

Component Forecast driver P&L line
New store leases Store additions × average area × rent per sq ft → PV → ROU addition D&A and finance cost
Existing leases Run-off of current liability and ROU asset D&A and finance cost
Revenue-share excess rent Store revenue × revenue-share % − minimum guarantee (if positive) Other expenses (rent)
Short-term and low-value Small base × inflation, or % of revenue Other expenses (rent)
CAM and maintenance Area × CAM per sq ft Other expenses

The lease schedule itself belongs in Week 14 (ROU asset and lease liability roll-forwards), but the cost classification is decided here. In the opex build, the "rent" line must include only the residual items.

Worked illustration — retailer rent split

A retailer's total rent economics for FY26 (₹ crore): fixed rent on long-term store leases ₹620; revenue-share rent in excess of minimum guarantees ₹45; short-term pop-up stores ₹18; low-value IT equipment ₹4; CAM charges ₹70. Lease liability opening ₹3,100 at an average incremental borrowing rate of 9%; ROU asset opening ₹2,700 with average remaining term 8 years (ignore additions for simplicity).

Line Pre-Ind AS 116 view Post-Ind AS 116 view
Rent in other expenses 620 + 45 + 18 + 4 = 687 45 + 18 + 4 = 67
CAM in other expenses 70 70
ROU depreciation — 2,700 ÷ 8 = 337.5
Interest on lease liability — 3,100 × 9% = 279 (approx.)
EBITDA impact vs pre-116 — +620
PBT impact (year) — 620 − 337.5 − 279 = +3.5

EBITDA rises by the fixed rent removed; PBT is close to neutral over the portfolio but front-loaded expense (higher interest early in each lease) means individual young leases reduce PBT in early years.

Model design rules

  • Keep a single Rent row in opex that is explicitly "variable, short-term and low-value rent", sourced from the lease note (Ind AS 116 disclosures of expenses relating to short-term leases, low-value leases and variable payments).
  • Calibrate historical residual rent as a % of revenue (revenue share) or a fixed amount (short-term and low-value).
  • Build fixed rent only inside the lease schedule; reference store additions and rent per sq ft from the same Assumptions block.
  • Provide a memo line for "EBITDA pre-Ind AS 116" = reported EBITDA − fixed lease payments (principal + interest), for comparisons with pre-2019 history, covenants and peers.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Lease term judgement. Indian store leases often have lock-in periods of three to five years with longer tenures and renewal options; the lease term includes renewal periods if reasonably certain. A shorter term means lower liability and higher depreciation per year.
  • Rent concessions. COVID-era concessions used a practical expedient; renegotiations are lease modifications requiring remeasurement.
  • Escalations. Fixed escalations (e.g., 5% a year or 15% every three years) are in the liability; don't add them to opex.
  • Minimum guarantee plus revenue share. Capitalise the minimum guarantee; only the excess over it is variable opex.
  • Sale and leaseback, subleases. Rare in retail models; treat separately if present.
  • Tax. For income tax, actual rent paid is deductible; ROU depreciation and lease interest are not — creating deferred tax (Ind AS 12) that the tax schedule must reflect.

Key takeaways

  • Post-Ind AS 116, fixed lease rent leaves opex and reappears as ROU depreciation plus lease interest.
  • Only short-term (≤ 12 months), low-value and variable (non-index) lease payments stay in opex rent.
  • Revenue-share rent above the minimum guarantee is variable opex; the minimum guarantee is capitalised.
  • Model fixed rent only in the lease schedule to avoid double counting.
  • Provide a pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA memo line for comparability.
  • Index-linked and fixed escalations are part of the lease liability, not variable rent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying lease payments [L1]

Classify each payment as capitalised or expensed under Ind AS 116 for a retailer: (a) store rent ₹40 lakh a year with 5% annual escalation for nine years; (b) mall store rent of the higher of ₹30 lakh or 10% of sales; (c) a 10-month warehouse lease; (d) leased laptops worth ₹70,000 each; (e) CAM charges billed separately; (f) rent revised annually by CPI.

Q2. Revenue-share rent forecast [L2]

A mall store has a minimum guarantee of ₹1.2 crore a year or 11% of sales, whichever is higher. FY26 sales are forecast at ₹13.5 crore. Compute the amount expensed as variable rent and explain where the minimum guarantee appears.

💡 Hint: Only the excess over the minimum guarantee is variable.

Q3. Double-count review [L2]

A model forecasts rent at 9% of revenue in other expenses and also builds ROU depreciation and lease interest from a lease schedule that includes all stores. Quantify the double count if revenue is ₹8,000 crore and 85% of rent is fixed, and describe the correction and checks you would add.

Q4. Pre- vs post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA [L3]

For a QSR chain with reported EBITDA of ₹1,100 crore, lease principal repayments of ₹310 crore and lease interest of ₹190 crore, compute pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA and explain which measure you would use for (a) EV/EBITDA comparison with a peer that owns its stores, (b) a bank covenant drafted in 2017, and (c) internal store-level profitability.
