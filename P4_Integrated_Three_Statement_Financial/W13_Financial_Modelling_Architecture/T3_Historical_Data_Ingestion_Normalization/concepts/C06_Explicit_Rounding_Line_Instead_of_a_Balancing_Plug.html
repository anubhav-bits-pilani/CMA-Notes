Key concept

Explicit Rounding Line Instead of a Balancing Plug

Concept: Rounding differences shown on an explicit, small "rounding" line, never absorbed into a balancing plug

Statement

When published statements are rounded (e.g., to ₹ crore with one or two decimals) and their components do not sum exactly to reported totals, the difference is recorded on a dedicated line:

\[ R_t = \text{Reported total}_t - \sum_i \text{Mapped components}_{i,t} \]

with a validation rule:

\[ \lvert R_t \rvert \le \varepsilon \]

where \( \varepsilon \) is a small materiality threshold related to the reporting precision (e.g., ₹0.05 crore when statements are in ₹ crore with two decimals, or a few units of the last reported digit). Any difference larger than \( \varepsilon \) is a mapping error to be fixed, not a rounding item. The rounding line is labelled, visible and flagged by a check; it never sits inside an operating line such as "other liabilities".

Explanation

Why rounding differences arise. Companies round each line independently; totals are rounded from unrounded sums. Statements in ₹ crore with two decimals commonly show 0.01–0.03 differences; those rounded to whole crore can show 1–2. XBRL values may carry more precision than the PDF.

Why a plug is dangerous. A balancing plug — forcing assets to equal liabilities by adjusting "other current liabilities" — hides mapping errors of any size (topic pitfall). A missed ₹140 crore borrowing line would be absorbed into "other liabilities", flowing into NWC, so forecast FCFF and net debt are both wrong, and the model still balances.

Design:

Element Implementation
Rounding row Separate line at the bottom of the asset or liability side on Historicals, e.g., "Rounding difference (reported vs mapped)"
Formula Reported total (blue, from source) − sum of mapped lines
Check =--(ABS(Rounding_t)>Rounding_Tolerance) rolled into the master check
Tolerance Named input (e.g., 0.05), justified by reporting precision
Forecast Rounding line is zero in forecast years (or carried as a constant last-actual value only if it is in opening balances — preferably absorbed into equity/other reserves at the boundary with a note)

Where to place it. Either side of the BS (one line), or per statement for the P&L and CFS. Some modellers place the BS rounding within "other equity" for the last actual year so that forecast opening balances are clean; whichever is chosen, it must be visible and small.

Distinguish from genuine reclassification. If a reported subtotal combines items (e.g., "other current liabilities" includes current lease liabilities in an older format), reclassify explicitly with a labelled adjustment row — that is not rounding.

Illustration

FY24 consolidated BS (₹ crore, reported to two decimals): reported total assets 12,846.37; sum of mapped asset lines 12,846.35 → R = 0.02, within ε = 0.05 ✓. Reported total equity and liabilities 12,846.37; mapped sum 12,706.37 → R = 140.00 ✗ — an error, not rounding. Investigation finds "Lease liabilities — current" (₹140.00 crore) shown on the face of the BS but not in the mapping table. Fix the mapping; R becomes 0.00.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rounding or error? [L1]

For statements reported in ₹ crore with two decimals and a tolerance of 0.05, classify each difference (reported − mapped) as rounding or error: (a) 0.01; (b) –0.03; (c) 0.09; (d) 2.40; (e) –0.05.

Q2. Remove a plug [L2]

A model's FY21–FY25 historicals balance because "Other current liabilities" is computed as total liabilities minus all other mapped liability lines. The plugged amounts are ₹212, ₹228, ₹590, ₹251 and ₹266 crore while the notes show other current liabilities of ₹210, ₹229, ₹245, ₹250 and ₹267 crore. Explain what the plug is hiding in FY23, how you would find it, and redesign the rows.

💡 Hint: Compare plugged values with note values year by year; the outlier year points to a missing line.

Q3. Forecast boundary [L2]

Your FY25A rounding difference on the BS is ₹0.03 crore. Explain two acceptable ways to handle it at the actual/forecast boundary so that the forecast balance check is exactly zero, and which you prefer.
