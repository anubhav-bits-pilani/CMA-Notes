Key concept

Adjusted EBITDA Bridge from PBT

Concept: Adjusted EBITDA = PBT + Finance costs + D&A − Other income ± Exceptional items; state pre/post Ind AS 116

Statement

\[ \text{Adj. EBITDA}_t = \text{PBT}_t + FC_t + DA_t - OI_t + EX_t \]

where \( \text{PBT}_t \) is profit before tax (continuing operations, after exceptional items, and — in consolidated statements — excluding the share of profit of associates and JVs, i.e., subtract that share if PBT includes it), \( FC_t \) finance costs, \( DA_t \) depreciation and amortisation, \( OI_t \) other income, and \( EX_t \) exceptional items with sign such that an exceptional loss is added back and an exceptional gain is deducted. The basis must be stated:

\[ \text{Adj. EBITDA}^{\text{pre-116}}_t = \text{Adj. EBITDA}^{\text{post-116}}_t - LP_t \]

where \( LP_t \) is cash lease payments for leases capitalised under Ind AS 116 (principal plus interest), or approximately ROU depreciation plus lease interest.

Explanation

Why build from PBT rather than from revenue minus costs. The PBT route ties to an audited subtotal and makes each adjustment explicit; the top-down route (revenue − COGS − employee − other expenses) gives the same answer if the P&L is fully mapped, and the two should be reconciled as a check.

Each term:

  • Finance costs include interest on borrowings, lease interest and exchange differences regarded as borrowing costs (Schedule III); all added back.
  • D&A includes ROU depreciation; added back.
  • Other income is deducted entirely unless specific items are genuinely operating and recurring (then reclassify to other operating revenue explicitly).
  • Exceptional items: the sign convention trips people — Schedule III shows exceptional items as a deduction from profit when they are expenses. Add back losses; deduct gains.
  • Share of associates/JVs: in Schedule III Division II consolidated P&L it typically appears before PBT; remove it — it is post-tax income from non-consolidated entities.

Pre/post Ind AS 116. Post-116 EBITDA excludes fixed lease rent; pre-116 deducts it. Valuation and multiples must match: EV/EBITDA on post-116 EBITDA requires lease liabilities in net debt; pre-116 EBITDA pairs with EV excluding leases. Always label the row (e.g., "Adj. EBITDA (post-Ind AS 116)").

Other agreed adjustments (ESOP, operating vs financing forex, unlabelled one-offs — s04) are separate, labelled rows after the core bridge.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both bases [L1]

FY25 (₹ crore): PBT 960 (after exceptional loss of 40, including share of associate profit 25); finance costs 110 (lease interest 70); D&A 300 (ROU 160); other income 85; lease cash outflow 245. Compute adjusted EBITDA post- and pre-Ind AS 116.

Q2. Reconcile to management EBITDA [L2]

Management's presentation reports FY25 EBITDA of ₹1,375 crore for the company in Q1. List the definitional differences that could explain the gap to your adjusted figure and design the reconciliation table you would place on the Historicals tab.

💡 Hint: Other income, exceptional items, associates and ESOP charges are the usual suspects.

Q3. Multiple consistency [L2]

A peer trades at 32x EV/EBITDA on post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA where EV includes lease liabilities. Your target's EBITDA in the comps sheet is pre-Ind AS 116. Explain the valuation error from applying 32x directly and how to correct it.
