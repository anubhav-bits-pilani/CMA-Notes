Key concept

CFS Closing Cash to Balance Sheet Cash Reconciliation

Concept: Cash reconciliation: CFS closing cash = BS cash & cash equivalents (excl. other bank balances) − bank overdrafts repayable on demand (Ind AS 7.8)

Statement

For every year \( t \):

\[ C^{\text{CFS}}_t = C^{\text{BS}}_t - OD_t \]

where \( C^{\text{CFS}}_t \) is closing cash and cash equivalents per the statement of cash flows, \( C^{\text{BS}}_t \) is the balance-sheet line "Cash and cash equivalents" (cash on hand, demand deposits and deposits with original maturity of three months or less — excluding "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents"), and \( OD_t \) is bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form an integral part of the entity's cash management, which Ind AS 7.8 includes as a component of cash and cash equivalents (presented within current borrowings on the BS).

Explanation

The Ind AS 7 definitions. Cash equivalents are held to meet short-term cash commitments, are readily convertible to known amounts of cash and are subject to insignificant risk of changes in value; an investment normally qualifies only when it has a short maturity of, say, three months or less from the date of acquisition (Ind AS 7.6–7.7). Equity investments are excluded. Overdrafts are included only if they are repayable on demand and the balance often fluctuates between positive and overdrawn (Ind AS 7.8); a term working-capital loan is financing, not a cash component.

What "other bank balances" are. Schedule III Division II requires "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" as a separate current-asset line: fixed deposits with original maturity over three months and up to twelve months, earmarked balances (unpaid dividend accounts), margin money and deposits under lien. They are excluded from CFS cash; their placement and maturity appear in investing activities (Ind AS 7.16).

Why the reconciliation matters in modelling. In the forecast, the CFS computes closing cash and the BS links to it (Week 15). If historical "cash" in the model is defined differently from the CFS definition, the FY25A opening cash for the forecast CFS will not equal the FY25A BS cash, and the forecast cash check will fail from the first forecast year. Defining it correctly in historicals prevents this.

Disclosure support. Ind AS 7.45 requires the components of cash and cash equivalents and a reconciliation of CFS amounts to the BS; the cash flow statement footnote usually gives it — replicate it.

Separate treasury definition. For net debt and the EV bridge, analysts often include other bank balances and liquid investments as cash-like. That is a valuation definition, kept on a separate row; it must not replace the CFS definition in the reconciliation.

Illustration

FY25 (₹ crore): BS cash and cash equivalents 340 (cash 2, current accounts 158, deposits ≤ 3 months 180); other bank balances 520 (FDs 4–12 months 505, unpaid dividend 15); cash credit repayable on demand used for daily cash management 60; WCDL (90-day tenor, not repayable on demand) 200; CFS closing cash reported 280.

Reconciliation: 340 − 60 = 280 ✓. WCDL is excluded (financing). Treasury cash for the bridge could be 340 + 505 = ₹845 crore (excluding the earmarked 15) — a different, labelled row.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and reconcile [L1]

Classify each as part of CFS cash or not, and compute CFS cash: cash on hand ₹1 crore; current account balances ₹96 crore; FD with original maturity 91 days (₹50 crore); FD with original maturity 80 days (₹70 crore); liquid MF ₹300 crore; overdraft repayable on demand ₹25 crore (integral to cash management); margin money deposits ₹12 crore.

Q2. Failure pattern [L2]

Your historical cash check fails every year by exactly the "bank overdraft" balance disclosed in the borrowings note. Explain the likely cause and the correction, and state how the correction affects the forecast debt schedule.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the overdraft is being treated as a cash component in the CFS but as debt in the model.

Q3. Policy judgement [L2]

A company's cash flow note includes overnight mutual fund units in cash equivalents, while a peer classifies them as current investments. Explain how you would handle each in historicals and in the net debt definition so that the two models are comparable.
