Key concept

Effective Tax Rate with Current and Deferred Split

Concept: Effective tax rate = Total tax expense ÷ PBT, with current and deferred tax split from the tax note

Statement

\[ \text{ETR}_t = \frac{T^{\text{cur}}_t + T^{\text{def}}_t}{\text{PBT}_t} \]

where \( T^{\text{cur}}_t \) is current tax expense (including any adjustments for earlier years, shown separately), \( T^{\text{def}}_t \) is deferred tax expense or credit (from the movement in deferred tax assets and liabilities recognised in P&L), and \( \text{PBT}_t \) is profit before tax from continuing operations — in consolidated statements, excluding the share of profit of associates and joint ventures, which is already post-tax. The split is taken from the income-tax note required by Ind AS 12.79–81, including the reconciliation of tax expense to accounting profit at the applicable rate (Ind AS 12.81(c)).

Explanation

The statutory benchmark. Domestic companies opting for Sec 115BAA of the Income-tax Act pay 22% base tax + 10% surcharge + 4% health and education cess = 25.168%, without MAT and without most incentives. Companies on the old regime face 30% (or 25% for smaller companies by turnover) plus surcharge (7% or 12% depending on income) and cess — e.g., 34.944% at the 12% surcharge — with MAT under Sec 115JB and MAT credit carry-forward. Many large companies have moved to 115BAA; some delayed the switch to use MAT credits or incentives (e.g., Sec 80-IC/80-IE units). New manufacturing companies under Sec 115BAB (15% base, 17.16% effective) had a commencement deadline of 31-Mar-2024. Always read the accounting policy and tax note for the regime.

Why the ETR differs from the statutory rate. The Ind AS 12.81(c) reconciliation explains differences: non-deductible expenses (CSR under Sec 135 is not deductible, Sec 37 Explanation 2), exempt or lower-taxed income (dividends from certain entities, LTCG on investments at concessional rates), subsidiaries in other jurisdictions, tax-rate changes on deferred tax (the one-time remeasurement on switching to 115BAA), prior-year adjustments, unrecognised deferred tax assets on losses.

Current vs deferred split. Deferred tax reflects timing differences — principally depreciation (tax WDV vs book), Ind AS 116 (ROU and lease liability), provisions for employee benefits and doubtful debts. For modelling:

  • Cash tax ≈ current tax (adjusted for advance tax and refunds timing).
  • Deferred tax affects the P&L but not cash (Week 14 tax schedule).
  • A persistently large deferred charge signals accelerated tax depreciation — cash tax below book tax.

Forecast use. Forecast ETR as statutory rate (25.168%) plus a stable structural wedge (e.g., CSR non-deductibility of ~0.3–0.5 pp), excluding one-offs. Do not average years containing a regime-switch remeasurement.

Illustration

FY25 consolidated (₹ crore): PBT 3,250 including share of JV profit 50; current tax 790 (of which earlier-year adjustment −12); deferred tax 26.

  • PBT excluding JV = 3,200.
  • Total tax = 816; ETR = 816 / 3,200 = 25.5% (on reported PBT including JV: 816 / 3,250 = 25.1% — understated).
  • Current tax ex-prior-year = 802 → 25.1%; deferred 0.8%.
  • Versus 25.168%: wedge ≈ +0.3 pp, consistent with CSR of about ₹40 crore (₹40 × 25.168% ≈ ₹10 crore of extra tax ≈ 0.3 pp).

Connections

Uses s03 (exclude equity-method share), s07 (ratio set); the tax schedule in Week 14 (current/deferred, MAT credit); WACC and DCF tax rate in Week 16 (marginal vs effective rate); Phase 1 Ind AS 12. FY figures depend on regime choice — date-sensitive.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the ETR [L1]

FY25 (₹ crore): PBT 1,420 including share of associate profit 60; current tax 340 (including +8 relating to earlier years); deferred tax credit (15). Compute the ETR on the correct base and split into current (ex-prior-year), prior-year and deferred components.

Q2. Regime switch distortion [L2]

In FY20 a company switched to Sec 115BAA and remeasured its net DTL of ₹420 crore from 34.944% to 25.168%, booking a deferred tax credit in that year. PBT was ₹2,300 crore and current tax ₹600 crore. Estimate the reported ETR, the normalised ETR, and explain why FY20 should be excluded from forecast averages.

💡 Hint: The remeasurement credit is the DTL scaled by the change in rate relative to the old rate.

Q3. Choosing the forecast rate [L2]

A Pidilite-type company reports ETRs of 25.9%, 25.4%, 26.1%, 25.6% and 25.7% for FY21–FY25 under Sec 115BAA. Explain how you would set the FY26E–FY30E ETR and which items in the Ind AS 12.81(c) reconciliation you would examine before deciding.
