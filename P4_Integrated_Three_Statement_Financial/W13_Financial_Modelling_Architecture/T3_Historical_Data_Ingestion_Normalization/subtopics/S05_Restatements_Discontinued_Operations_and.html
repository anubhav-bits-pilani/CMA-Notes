Subtopic note

Restatements, Discontinued Operations and Acquisitions

Scope: Restatements and reclassifications: use latest comparatives; Ind AS 105 discontinued operations; acquisitions

Why it matters

A five-year history assembled naively from five annual reports is not a consistent series. Companies regroup lines, correct errors, change policies, discontinue businesses, and acquire or merge entities — and each event changes how earlier years should look. Growth rates and margins computed across a structural break mislead forecasts: a business sold in FY24 inflates FY21–FY23 revenue; an acquisition inflates FY25 growth; a regrouping moves 150 bps between gross margin and other expenses. The modeller's job is to build the most comparable series available, and to document every judgement.

Core notes

Rule 1: use the latest comparatives

Each annual report presents the current year and the prior year (and, for some restatements, an opening balance sheet). The prior-year column in the latest report reflects:

  • Reclassifications/regroupings (Schedule III requires comparatives to be regrouped to match the current year; Ind AS 1.41 requires disclosure of the nature, amount and reason).
  • Retrospective restatements for errors and policy changes (Ind AS 8.22, 8.42) — with a third balance sheet at the start of the preceding period when material (Ind AS 1.40A).
  • Re-presentation for discontinued operations (Ind AS 105.34).
  • Common-control business combinations restated as if combined from the beginning of the earliest period presented (Ind AS 103 Appendix C).

Practice: for a five-year history FY21–FY25, take FY25 and FY24 from the FY25 report, FY23 from the FY24 report (FY23 comparatives), FY22 from the FY23 report, and FY21 from the FY22 report. Each year is then "as last reported". Where a later restatement affects older years not shown in later reports, adjust older years using disclosed amounts or flag a break.

Document in a "Source & restatement" row on Historicals: report used for each year and any adjustments.

Rule 2: discontinued operations under Ind AS 105

A discontinued operation is a component that has been disposed of or is classified as held for sale and represents a separate major line of business or geographical area (or is part of a plan to dispose of one, or is a subsidiary acquired exclusively for resale) (Ind AS 105.32).

Presentation:

Statement Treatment Comparatives
P&L Single amount: post-tax profit or loss of discontinued operations plus post-tax gain/loss on remeasurement/disposal (105.33) Re-presented for all prior periods shown (105.34)
CFS Net cash flows from operating, investing and financing of discontinued operations disclosed (in notes or on the face) Re-presented
BS Assets and liabilities of disposal group held for sale shown separately (105.38) Not re-presented (105.40)
EPS Basic and diluted EPS from continuing and discontinued operations separately (Ind AS 33.68) Re-presented

Model implications:

  • Forecast continuing operations only; history must be continuing-only for growth and margin analysis. The latest report gives the prior year re-presented; for older years, use the discontinued-operations note (it often gives revenue and expense breakdowns) or segment data to strip out the business.
  • Proceeds from disposal and remaining held-for-sale assets go into the EV → equity bridge or cash forecast.
  • BS ratios (DSO, DIO) in the year of classification: exclude assets held for sale from NWC.

Rule 3: acquisitions

For acquisitions under Ind AS 103 (acquisition method), the acquiree is consolidated from the acquisition date; comparatives are not restated. This creates inorganic growth:

Need Approach
Organic growth Exclude acquired revenue in the year of acquisition and its annualised effect in the following year (use pro forma revenue disclosed under Ind AS 103.B64(q): revenue and profit of the acquiree since acquisition date, and of the combined entity as if acquired at the start of the year)
Forecast base Use full-year (pro forma) revenue of the combined entity as the base for the next year's growth
Margins Check margin dilution/accretion from the acquiree
BS Goodwill and intangibles appear; amortisation of acquired intangibles affects D&A — note for EBITA metrics
Deferred/contingent consideration Debt-like item in the EV bridge

Common-control combinations (e.g., a group company merging a sister entity under an NCLT-approved scheme) use pooling of interests under Appendix C: prior periods are restated as if the combination had occurred from the beginning of the preceding period presented (or from the date control was established, if later) — so the latest report's comparatives are already combined, but older reports are not.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Year Reported revenue Of which discontinued segment Acquired entity (consolidated from 1-Oct-FY24) Comparable continuing organic
FY22 5,200 600 – 4,600
FY23 5,900 650 – 5,250
FY24 5,750 (re-presented, continuing only) – 180 (6 months) 5,570
FY25 6,700 – 400 6,300

FY24 organic growth = 5,570 / 5,250 − 1 = 6.1%; FY25 organic growth vs FY24 organic = 6,300 / 5,570 − 1 = 13.1%; reported FY25 growth = 6,700 / 5,750 − 1 = 16.5%. Using reported FY22–FY23 (including the discontinued segment) would have mis-stated the base by ₹600–650 crore. Using pro forma FY24 (with the acquiree's full-year revenue, say ₹360 crore) as the base: (6,700) / (5,570 + 360) − 1 = 13.0%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ind AS transition: FY2016-17/FY2017-18 comparatives were restated from Indian GAAP; pre-transition years are not comparable.
  • Schedule III amendments (FY2021-22): reclassification of current maturities of long-term debt into current borrowings and new captions; comparatives restated in FY22 reports.
  • Demerger of a business into a separately listed company: treat as discontinued; shareholders hold both — value the remaining company on continuing operations.
  • Errors discovered: restated comparatives prevail; examine the restatement for forensic signals (Phase 2).
  • Quarterly data: re-presentation of quarterly comparatives in later results may differ from original filings — always take the latest.

Key takeaways

  • Build history from the latest available comparatives, documenting the report used for each year.
  • Under Ind AS 105, P&L and CFS comparatives are re-presented for discontinued operations; BS comparatives are not.
  • Forecast continuing operations on a continuing-only history; handle disposal proceeds in the bridge.
  • Acquisitions are not restated: separate organic and inorganic growth using pro forma disclosures.
  • Common-control mergers restate comparatives under Ind AS 103 Appendix C.

Practice Questions

Q1. Which report for which year [L1]

You are building FY21–FY25 historicals in December 2025. State which annual report you would take each year from, and what you would do if the FY24 report re-presented FY23 for a discontinued operation but the FY23 report's FY22 column was not re-presented.

Q2. Strip a discontinued segment [L2]

A company classified its packaging division as discontinued in FY25. The FY25 report re-presents FY24. The FY24 report's segment note shows packaging revenue of ₹410 crore (FY23) and EBIT of ₹38 crore; FY23 consolidated revenue was ₹6,250 crore and EBIT ₹780 crore. Build continuing-operations revenue and EBIT for FY23 and explain the limitations of using segment EBIT for this purpose.

💡 Hint: Segment results may exclude unallocated corporate costs that partly relate to the discontinued division.

Q3. Organic growth with an acquisition [L2]

Pidilite-type company acquires a brand on 1-Jul-2024 (FY25). FY24 revenue ₹12,400 crore; FY25 revenue ₹13,900 crore including ₹520 crore from the acquiree since acquisition; Ind AS 103 pro forma disclosure states combined FY25 revenue would have been ₹14,080 crore had the acquisition occurred on 1-Apr-2024. Compute reported and organic FY25 growth and state the base you would use for FY26E growth.

Q4. Common-control merger case [L3]

In FY25, a listed company merges an unlisted sister company under an NCLT scheme with appointed date 1-Apr-2023. Explain how the FY25 annual report presents FY24 comparatives under Ind AS 103 Appendix C, what this means for your FY21–FY23 history, and how you would present and label a consistent five-year series in the model.
