Subtopic note

EBITDA Normalisation: Exceptional, Other Income, Leases, ESOPs, Forex

Scope: EBITDA normalisation: exceptional items, other income, Ind AS 116 lease effects, ESOP charges, forex gains/losses

Why it matters

Reported EBITDA is not a defined Ind AS measure. Companies, brokers and data vendors compute it differently — with or without other income, before or after exceptional items, post- or pre-Ind AS 116, with or without ESOP charges and forex. A forecast built on an unnormalised base extrapolates one-offs; a valuation multiple applied to an inconsistently defined EBITDA misprices the company; and a peer comparison mixing lease bases is meaningless (Ind AS 116 can move a retailer's EBITDA margin by 600–1,000 bps). Normalisation produces a clean, repeatable operating EBITDA for each historical year, with a reconciliation to reported figures.

Core notes

The normalisation bridge

Start from reported numbers and adjust explicitly (c03):

\[ \text{Adj. EBITDA} = \text{PBT} + \text{Finance costs} + \text{D\&A} - \text{Other income} \pm \text{Exceptional items} \pm \text{other agreed adjustments} \]

Then present a reconciliation table each year:

₹ crore (illustrative retailer, FY25) Amount
PBT (reported) 1,820
+ Finance costs (of which lease interest 310) 395
+ D&A (of which ROU depreciation 520) 870
− Other income (240)
+ Exceptional loss (impairment) 60
= Adjusted EBITDA, post-Ind AS 116 2,905
− Lease payments (principal + interest, per CFS/lease note) (830)
= Adjusted EBITDA, pre-Ind AS 116 ("rent-adjusted") 2,075

1. Exceptional items

Schedule III Division II shows "Exceptional items" as a separate P&L line before tax. Ind AS 1.97–98 requires separate disclosure of material items (write-downs, restructuring, disposals, litigation settlements). Normalisation:

  • Exclude from EBITDA if non-recurring and non-operating.
  • Scrutinise items that recur ("exceptional" every year), and items that are operating in nature but labelled exceptional (inventory write-downs, VRS for store closures).
  • Also look for unlabelled one-offs inside other expenses (e.g., a large provision for doubtful debts, a litigation charge) — concall transcripts and notes help.

2. Other income

Other income includes interest on deposits, dividends, fair-value gains on mutual funds (FVTPL), profit on sale of PPE, rental income and sometimes government grants or insurance claims. It is not operating EBITDA (topic pitfall): it depends on treasury size, and the treasury assets are added separately in the EV bridge (as cash/investments). Exclude all treasury income; consider keeping genuinely operating items (e.g., scrap sales mislabelled, recurring export incentives) only if they are operating and recurring — usually those belong in "other operating revenue".

3. Ind AS 116 lease effects

Under Ind AS 116, lessees recognise ROU assets and lease liabilities for most leases; P&L shows ROU depreciation and lease interest instead of rent. Exceptions remaining in opex: short-term leases (≤ 12 months), low-value assets, and variable lease payments not based on an index or rate (e.g., revenue-share rent) (Ind AS 116.5–8, 116.38).

Normalisation choices:

Basis Definition Use
Post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA As reported (rent excluded) Valuation with lease liabilities treated as debt; FCFF excludes lease payments from operating costs; WACC/multiples consistent
Pre-Ind AS 116 (rent-adjusted) EBITDA Post-116 EBITDA − lease payments (or − ROU depreciation − lease interest as an approximation) Peer comparison with franchise-heavy peers; credit analysis; management KPIs

State the basis explicitly (c03). For retailers like Trent, rent-adjusted EBITDA is what most analysts compare; for capital-light peers lease effects are small.

Lease payments: CFS financing shows "Payment of lease liabilities — principal" and "— interest" (Ind AS 116.50 applies Ind AS 7's rules for interest paid; under Ind AS 7.33 a non-financial entity classifies interest paid as financing, so both components normally sit in financing; short-term, low-value and variable lease payments remain operating). Use the total cash outflow for leases disclosed in the lease note (Ind AS 116.53(g)).

4. ESOP charges

Share-based payment expense (Ind AS 102) sits within employee benefits expense. It is non-cash but economically real (dilution). Practice:

  • Many Indian companies present "EBITDA before ESOP" in investor presentations (especially new-age companies).
  • For valuation: if EBITDA excludes ESOP costs, you must account for dilution (use fully diluted shares incl. future grants) — otherwise you overvalue. Conservative practice keeps ESOP cost within EBITDA.
  • Always show it as a separate line so either definition can be computed.

5. Forex gains and losses

Ind AS 21 exchange differences arise on monetary items (receivables, payables, foreign-currency borrowings). Schedule III requires exchange differences "regarded as an adjustment to borrowing costs" to be shown within finance costs; others are in other income or other expenses (net). Normalisation:

Source of forex difference Treatment in adjusted EBITDA
Trade receivables/payables (export/import operations) Operating — keep in EBITDA (it is part of realisation/cost), or normalise if volatile and hedged elsewhere
Foreign-currency borrowings (ECB) Financing — exclude from EBITDA
MTM on derivatives hedging borrowings Financing — exclude
Translation of foreign operations (FCTR in OCI) Not in P&L — no adjustment

Disclose the policy; volatile forex in one year (e.g., a sharp INR depreciation) can swing margins by tens of bps.

Excel implementation

Historicals tab rows: reported EBITDA (computed), each adjustment as a separate signed row (blue inputs with source note references), adjusted EBITDA, and both lease bases. Forecast EBITDA is built from drivers on the same definition as adjusted historical EBITDA.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capitalised costs: employee costs or borrowing costs capitalised to CWIP (Ind AS 16, 23) lift EBITDA; a forensic flag (Phase 2).
  • Government grants and PLI incentives: if recognised in other operating revenue and linked to production, arguably operating; if in other income, judge.
  • Gain on sale of PPE inside other income: exclude; loss on sale inside other expenses: exclude too for symmetry.
  • CSR expense: a statutory, recurring cost (Sec 135) — keep in EBITDA.
  • Discontinued operations: already excluded by Ind AS 105 presentation (s05).
  • Management-adjusted EBITDA: reconcile; don't adopt it without scrutiny.

Key takeaways

  • Build adjusted EBITDA via an explicit bridge from PBT and show both post- and pre-Ind AS 116 versions.
  • Exclude other income (treasury) and genuine exceptional items; hunt for unlabelled one-offs.
  • Under Ind AS 116 only short-term, low-value and variable non-index rent remains in opex.
  • Keep ESOP cost as a separate line; if excluded from EBITDA, reflect dilution.
  • Classify forex by source: operating forex stays, financing forex goes.
  • Forecast on the same definition as the normalised history.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the bridge [L1]

From the following FY25 data (₹ crore) compute reported and adjusted EBITDA (post- and pre-Ind AS 116): PBT 2,450; finance costs 180 (of which lease interest 95, forex loss on ECB 12); D&A 640 (ROU depreciation 210); other income 310 (interest 190, MF gains 85, profit on sale of land 35); exceptional items: VRS cost 75; total lease cash outflow 290; ESOP expense in employee cost 40.

💡 Hint: Decide where the ₹12 crore forex loss sits before adding back finance costs.

Q2. Exceptional or operating? [L2]

Classify and justify: (a) inventory write-down of ₹48 crore on discontinued SKUs shown as exceptional; (b) impairment of a subsidiary's goodwill; (c) store-closure costs recurring in each of the last four years; (d) insurance claim received for a fire at a warehouse, in other income; (e) one-time GST refund of earlier years in other operating revenue.

Q3. Peer comparability under Ind AS 116 [L2]

Retailer A owns 20% of stores and leases 80% on fixed rent; Retailer B leases all stores on revenue-share rent (8% of store sales). Both report EBITDA margin of 15%. Explain why their margins are not comparable and set out the adjustment you would make to each to compare them on a rent-adjusted basis.

Q4. ESOP policy memo [L3]

A new-age consumer company reports "Adjusted EBITDA (pre-ESOP)" of ₹420 crore versus reported EBITDA of ₹310 crore. Write a short memo for your IC on whether to value it on pre- or post-ESOP EBITDA, how each choice must be paired with the share count, and what disclosures from the Ind AS 102 note you would use.
