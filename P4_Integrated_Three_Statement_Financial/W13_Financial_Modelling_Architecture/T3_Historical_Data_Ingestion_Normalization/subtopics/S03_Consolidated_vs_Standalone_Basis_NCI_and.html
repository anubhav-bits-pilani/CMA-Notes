Subtopic note

Consolidated vs Standalone Basis, NCI and Associates

Scope: Consolidated vs standalone selection; NCI and associate (equity method) presentation

Why it matters

Every Indian listed company with subsidiaries publishes two complete sets of financial statements — standalone and consolidated — and both appear in Reg 33 results and the annual report. Choosing the wrong one, or mixing them, distorts growth, margins, leverage and valuation. Even once the consolidated basis is chosen, the model must handle non-controlling interests (NCI) and equity-accounted associates and joint ventures correctly: they affect which profit belongs to shareholders, which EBITDA is "operating", and how enterprise value bridges to equity value. These are routine interview questions and routine errors in student models.

Core notes

Standalone vs consolidated: what each represents

Aspect Standalone Consolidated (Ind AS 110)
Entity Legal entity only Parent + subsidiaries (line-by-line), associates/JVs (equity method, Ind AS 28)
Investments in subsidiaries At cost (or per Ind AS 27 policy) Eliminated; replaced by underlying assets, liabilities, goodwill
Intra-group sales, balances Included Eliminated
NCI Not applicable Presented within equity; profit and OCI split between owners and NCI
Use Dividend capacity (Sec 123), entity-level tax, lender covenants on the borrower, regulatory (e.g., Sec 135 CSR computation is entity-level) Valuation of the group, peer comparison, group leverage

Default for equity valuation: consolidated. It captures all businesses the shareholder owns through the group. Standalone is right when subsidiaries are immaterial (and the standalone history is cleaner) or when the analysis is entity-specific (credit, dividend). Whichever is chosen, use it for all years and all statements (topic pitfall), and declare it on the Cover.

Check materiality: compare consolidated and standalone revenue, EBITDA, net debt for the latest year. Pidilite's subsidiaries (domestic and international) add a modest share of revenue; Trent's consolidated statements include subsidiaries and the equity-accounted Star JV (Trent Hypermarket), so consolidated revenue excludes Star sales but consolidated PAT includes Trent's share of Star's profit or loss.

Non-controlling interests (NCI)

Under Ind AS 110, NCI is the equity in a subsidiary not attributable to the parent. Presentation:

  • BS: NCI shown within total equity, separately from equity attributable to owners of the parent.
  • P&L: total profit is allocated between "owners of the parent" and "non-controlling interests" below PAT (Ind AS 1.81B).
  • CFS: dividends paid to NCI are financing outflows.

Model implications:

Item Treatment
EPS Based on PAT attributable to owners (Ind AS 33)
EBITDA, FCFF Include 100% of subsidiaries (consolidated line-by-line)
EV → equity bridge Subtract NCI (at book or, better, market/fair value) because FCFF includes the NCI's share
NCI forecast NCI share of subsidiary PAT (a % of consolidated PAT or a subsidiary-level build) less dividends to NCI
Balance check Total equity includes NCI

Common error: using total PAT for EPS or P/E, or forgetting to deduct NCI in the EV bridge — both overvalue owners' equity.

Associates and joint ventures (equity method)

Under Ind AS 28, investments in associates (significant influence, presumed at 20%+) and joint ventures (joint control under Ind AS 111) are accounted for using the equity method in consolidated statements:

  • BS: one line "Investments accounted for using the equity method" — cost plus share of post-acquisition profit/OCI less dividends received.
  • P&L: "Share of profit/(loss) of associates and joint ventures" — Schedule III Division II presents it as a separate line, generally after exceptional items and before tax (it is already post-tax at the investee level).
  • CFS: share of profit is a non-cash adjustment in operating activities (indirect method); dividends received are investing cash flows for a non-financial entity (Ind AS 7.33, which — unlike IAS 7 — removes the operating-classification option for entities other than financial institutions).

Model implications:

Item Treatment
Revenue, EBITDA Exclude associates — none of their revenue is consolidated
EBIT for ROCE Exclude share of associates; also exclude the investment from capital employed
PBT/PAT Share of profit included (it is post-tax; don't apply tax again)
ETR Compute on PBT excluding share of associates (c04)
Valuation Add equity-method investments in the EV → equity bridge (at book, market value if listed, or a separate valuation), since FCFF excludes them
Forecast Share of profit via a growth assumption or the associate's own mini-model; investment balance rolls forward: opening + share of profit − dividends

Illustration (₹ crore, FY25, consolidated)

Item Amount
EBITDA (100% of subsidiaries) 3,200
PAT (total) 1,900
of which NCI 60
PAT attributable to owners 1,840
Share of profit of JV included in PAT 45
Investment in JV (equity method) 520
NCI in equity (book) 410

EV from DCF = ₹62,000 crore; net debt ₹1,200 crore. Equity value = 62,000 − 1,200 − 410 (NCI) + 520 (JV) = ₹60,910 crore. EPS uses ₹1,840 crore. ETR uses PBT excluding the ₹45 crore JV share.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Change in group structure (a subsidiary acquired mid-history): consolidated growth includes inorganic revenue — split organic vs acquired (s05).
  • Loss of control leading to an associate: revenue disappears from consolidation from that date; restate or segment.
  • Negative NCI (loss-making subsidiary with minority partners): still deducted (adds back) in the bridge; consider guarantees.
  • Listed associates: use market value (with a holding-company discount if appropriate) rather than book value.
  • Standalone-only history for older years (small companies without subsidiaries earlier): acceptable if documented and subsidiaries were immaterial.
  • Proportionate KPIs: management may present JV store counts (e.g., Star) — keep them out of consolidated revenue builds.

Key takeaways

  • Choose consolidated for group valuation; standalone for entity-level credit, dividend or tax analysis; never mix.
  • NCI sits within equity; EPS uses profit attributable to owners; deduct NCI in the EV bridge.
  • Associates/JVs are equity-accounted: excluded from revenue and EBITDA, included in PAT as a post-tax share, added in the EV bridge.
  • Compute ETR and ROCE excluding equity-method items.
  • Check materiality of subsidiaries by comparing standalone and consolidated metrics.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choose the basis [L1]

Which basis (standalone or consolidated) would you use for: (a) a DCF of Trent; (b) assessing Trent's capacity to pay dividends under Sec 123; (c) testing a term-loan covenant on net debt/EBITDA of the borrowing entity; (d) a P/E comparison with Avenue Supermarts; (e) Sec 135 CSR obligation?

Q2. EV-to-equity bridge [L2]

A consolidated group has DCF EV of ₹45,000 crore, gross debt ₹3,800 crore, lease liabilities ₹2,100 crore (treated as debt because FCFF is post-Ind AS 116), cash and liquid investments ₹2,600 crore, NCI book value ₹650 crore (subsidiary PAT multiple suggests fair value ₹1,400 crore), and a listed associate (book ₹900 crore, market value of stake ₹2,300 crore). Compute equity value on book and fair-value bases and recommend one.

💡 Hint: Be consistent: if FCFF excludes lease cash flows from operating costs, lease liabilities must be in the bridge.

Q3. ETR distortion [L2]

FY25 consolidated PBT (including share of JV profit of ₹120 crore) is ₹1,480 crore; total tax expense ₹342 crore. Compute the ETR with and without the JV share and explain which you would use to forecast tax.

Q4. Mixed-basis repair [L3]

A colleague's five-year history mixes standalone (FY21–FY22) and consolidated (FY23–FY25). Consolidated revenue in FY23 was 6% higher than standalone, and the subsidiary carried ₹300 crore of debt. Explain the distortions in revenue CAGR, margin trend, net debt and ROCE, and describe how you would rebuild a consistent consolidated history, including where to find FY21–FY22 consolidated figures.
