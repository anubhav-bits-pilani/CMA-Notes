Subtopic note

Deriving Historical Ratios as Forecast Anchors

Scope: Historical ratio derivation: growth, margins, DSO/DIO/DPO, capex/sales, D&A/opening gross block, effective tax rate

Why it matters

Forecast drivers are claims about the future; historical ratios are the evidence. Every assumption on the Inputs tab — growth, gross margin, working-capital days, capex intensity, depreciation rate, tax rate — should sit next to its historical range so that a reviewer can see whether the forecast is continuity, improvement or deterioration, and why. Ratios derived from mapped, normalised, checked historicals (s02–s06) also feed Phase 2-style peer benchmarking and forensic review. Getting definitions right matters: DIO on revenue rather than COGS, or depreciation on closing rather than opening gross block, produces drivers that do not reproduce history when plugged back into the forecast logic.

Core notes

Principle: compute ratios with the same formula the forecast will invert

If forecast receivables will be Revenue × DSO / 365, historical DSO must be Receivables / Revenue × 365 with the same revenue definition (revenue from operations net of GST, as reported) and the same balance (closing or average). Back-testing — applying the historical ratio to historical drivers — should reproduce the historical balance.

Ratio definitions

Ratio Formula Notes
Revenue growth \( g_t = \frac{R_t}{R_{t-1}} - 1 \) Use continuing, organic, normalised revenue (s05); also CAGR
CAGR \( \left(\frac{R_T}{R_0}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \) \( n \) = number of years
Gross margin \( \frac{R - \text{COGS}}{R} \) COGS = materials + purchases + Δ inventories
EBITDA margin \( \frac{\text{Adj. EBITDA}}{R} \) State Ind AS 116 basis (s04)
Line-item % of revenue Employee, A&P, freight, other opex Inputs for variable/semi-variable costs (topic t5)
DSO \( \frac{\text{Trade receivables}}{R} \times 365 \) Closing balance; average if seasonality or growth is large
DIO \( \frac{\text{Inventories}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365 \) On COGS, not revenue; retail may use inventory/revenue for simplicity — be consistent
DPO \( \frac{\text{Trade payables}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365 \) or on purchases Purchases = COGS + closing inventory − opening inventory
Cash conversion cycle DSO + DIO − DPO
Capex / sales \( \frac{\text{Purchase of PPE and intangibles (CFS)}}{R} \) Use CFS capex (cash), net of disposals separately
D&A / opening gross block \( \frac{\text{D\&A}_t}{\text{Gross block}_{t-1}} \) Owned assets; ROU depreciation separately on opening ROU
Effective tax rate \( \frac{\text{Total tax expense}}{\text{PBT}} \) Split current/deferred; exclude equity-method share from PBT (c04)
Other: ROCE, asset turnover, NWC % revenue For reasonableness checks

Why D&A on opening gross block

Assets added during the year depreciate only partly; using opening gross block approximates the asset base available for the whole year, and in the forecast, \( \text{D\&A}_t = r \times \text{Opening gross block}_t + \frac{r}{2} \times \text{Additions}_t \) is a common refinement (Week 14). Using closing gross block understates the rate in high-capex years. Separate owned PPE, intangibles and ROU assets — their lives and patterns differ (ROU depreciation follows lease terms).

Days conventions

  • 365 days (most common), 360 (some banks) — name it (Days_in_Year).
  • Closing vs average balance: average smooths growth effects but needs an opening balance (lose one year of history). For a fast-growing retailer like Trent (revenue growth > 40% in some years), closing-balance DIO looks low relative to average — pick one and forecast consistently.
  • Retail cash sales: DSO near zero; inventory days and payables dominate NWC.

Illustration (₹ crore, FY25)

Revenue 10,000; COGS 5,600; receivables 820; inventories 1,380; payables 1,050; opening inventory 1,250; CFS capex 520; D&A (owned) 290; opening gross block (owned) 4,150; PBT (excl. associates) 1,950; tax expense 498 (current 470, deferred 28).

  • Gross margin = 4,400 / 10,000 = 44.0%.
  • DSO = 820 / 10,000 × 365 = 29.9 days.
  • DIO = 1,380 / 5,600 × 365 = 89.9 days.
  • Purchases = 5,600 + 1,380 − 1,250 = 5,730; DPO on purchases = 1,050 / 5,730 × 365 = 66.9 days (on COGS: 68.4 days).
  • Capex / sales = 5.2%.
  • D&A / opening gross block = 290 / 4,150 = 7.0% (≈ 14-year average life).
  • ETR = 498 / 1,950 = 25.5% (current 24.1%, deferred 1.4%), close to the Sec 115BAA rate of 25.168%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • COVID year FY2020-21 distorts growth, margins and days — consider excluding from averages, or show both.
  • Acquisition years: closing balances include the acquiree but revenue only part-year — inflates days; use pro forma or average.
  • Factoring/bill discounting: derecognised receivables lower DSO; check notes.
  • Supplier finance (reverse factoring) arrangements can inflate DPO; the IASB's 2023 amendments to IAS 7 and IFRS 7 added specific disclosures — check whether and from when MCA has notified the equivalent Ind AS 7/Ind AS 107 amendments before relying on them.
  • Capex in CFS vs PPE additions: differ by capital creditors and CWIP movements — be clear which you forecast.
  • ETR anomalies: losses, MAT, deferred tax remeasurement on regime change, tax on dividends — explain rather than average.

Key takeaways

  • Define each ratio exactly as the forecast will invert it, and back-test.
  • DIO and DPO on COGS (or purchases); DSO on revenue; state closing vs average.
  • D&A rate on opening gross block, split owned PPE, intangibles and ROU.
  • ETR = total tax ÷ PBT (excluding equity-method share), split current/deferred.
  • Present ranges (3-year, 5-year, min, max) beside each forecast driver and flag abnormal years.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the ratios [L1]

FY24 and FY25 (₹ crore): revenue 8,600 / 9,700; COGS 4,900 / 5,450; receivables 690 / 760; inventories 1,120 / 1,240; payables 820 / 905; CFS capex 410 / 480; owned D&A 245 / 272; gross block (closing) FY23 3,500, FY24 3,880, FY25 4,330; PBT 1,610 / 1,830; tax 410 / 468. Compute FY25 growth, gross margin, DSO, DIO, DPO (on COGS, closing balances), capex/sales, D&A/opening gross block and ETR.

Q2. Average vs closing days [L2]

For a retailer growing revenue 45% in FY25 (FY24 revenue ₹12,400 crore), inventory rose from ₹1,700 crore to ₹2,350 crore and COGS was ₹10,100 crore in FY25. Compute DIO on closing and on average inventory, explain the difference, and recommend which to use for a forecast where growth slows to 25%.

💡 Hint: Consider which convention reproduces the forecast closing inventory you actually need for the balance sheet.

Q3. D&A driver choice [L2]

Explain why D&A divided by closing gross block understates the depreciation rate in a heavy capex year, illustrating with: opening gross block ₹2,000 crore, additions ₹1,000 crore on 1 October, straight-line life 10 years, no disposals.

Q4. Driver justification table [L3]

For a Pidilite-type model, build the Inputs-tab layout showing for each driver (UVG, price/mix, gross margin, A&P %, employee cost growth, DSO, DIO, DPO, capex/sales, D&A rate, ETR) the FY21–FY25 historical values, 3- and 5-year averages, the FY26E–FY30E assumption, and a rationale column; write the rationale for three drivers where you would deliberately depart from history.
