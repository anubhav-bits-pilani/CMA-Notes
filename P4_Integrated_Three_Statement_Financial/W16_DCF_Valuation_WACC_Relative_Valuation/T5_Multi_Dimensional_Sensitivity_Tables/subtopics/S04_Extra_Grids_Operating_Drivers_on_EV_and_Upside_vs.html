Subtopic note

Extra Grids: Operating Drivers on EV and Upside vs CMP

Scope: Extra grids: revenue growth vs EBITDA margin on EV; upside % vs CMP

Why it matters

Grids 1 and 2 flex the valuation inputs. Investment committees, boards and portfolio managers usually care more about the operating drivers, because those are what management controls and what quarterly results reveal. Typical questions are "what if growth is 4 points lower?" and "what if margins don't expand as planned?". A revenue-growth × EBITDA-margin grid on EV answers them directly. An upside % grid turns any valuation grid into the investment signal that matters to a fund manager or a promoter weighing an offer. These grids are also the natural bridge into FP&A sensitivity work (Phase 5), where the same structure flexes budget EBITDA or covenant headroom.

Core notes

Grid 3: revenue growth vs EBITDA margin on EV

Design problem. Revenue growth and margin are not single cells. They are rows of yearly assumptions on the Project 4 assumptions sheet, often scenario-dependent. A data table needs one input cell per axis, on the same sheet as the table (concept c03).

Solution: uniform delta drivers.

  1. On the DCF sheet, create D8 = revenue growth delta (pp, base 0.0%) and D9 = EBITDA margin delta (bp, base 0.0%).
  2. On the assumptions sheet, change the live forecast rows to Growth_used_t = Growth_scenario_t + DCF!$D$8 and Margin_used_t = Margin_scenario_t + DCF!$D$9. A cross-sheet reference from the assumptions sheet to the DCF sheet is allowed. The rule only requires the input cell to be on the table's sheet.
  3. Decide how the deltas propagate: - All forecast years uniformly. This is the simplest approach and the most common. - Ramp-in. The delta applies fully from year 2. - Explicit years only. Terminal-year margin reverts to the base. This is useful if the terminal assumption is anchored separately.
  4. Ensure the dependent schedules respond. NWC (days-based), capex (as % of revenue or capacity-driven), D&A and tax must all move with revenue. Otherwise the grid overstates the value of growth, because growth appears costless.
  5. Output: EV, not price. EV isolates the operating effect from bridge items. A second identical grid on price is optional.

Illustrative layout. A retailer has a base FY26–FY31 revenue CAGR of 18% and a post-116 EBITDA margin of 14%.

Margin Δ ↓ / Growth Δ → −4 pp −2 pp 0 +2 pp +4 pp

Use a display row showing the absolute CAGR (14%, 16%, 18%, 20%, 22%) and a display column showing the margins (12.5% … 15.5%) for readability.

Interpreting. If growth adds little value at a constant margin, the business is earning close to its WACC on incremental capital (RONIC ≈ WACC). That is an important insight, and it ties to the value-driver formula in topic t3, s06. Where growth destroys value (RONIC < WACC), the grid shows EV falling as growth rises.

Terminal-value consistency. If margins flex in all years, terminal FCFF changes. Make sure terminal normalisation (topic t3, s05) references the flexed terminal-year margin, not a hardcoded value.

Grid 4: upside % vs CMP

Upside at each cell is:

\[ U_{ij} = \frac{P_{ij}}{\text{CMP}} - 1 \]

There are two ways to build it:

  • Separate data table with the corner formula =Upside (that is, =Implied_Price/CMP-1). The same WACC and g inputs give an upside grid directly.
  • Derived presentation grid (cheaper): =I21/CMP-1, referencing each cell of Grid 1. There is no extra data table, so recalculation is faster, and it is always consistent with Grid 1.

The derived approach is preferred in Project 5 because it halves recalculation time.

Use a diverging colour scale with zero as the midpoint (red below, green above), so readers see instantly where the investment call flips (s05).

Rating overlay. Use conditional-format text or a mapped grid with =IF(U>15%,"Buy",IF(U<-5%,"Sell","Hold")) to show the rating boundaries.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Scenario interaction. If the scenario switch changes growth rows, the delta grid flexes around the active scenario. Label it.
  • Non-linear drivers. Margin deltas applied to a margin that is already capped (for example by a gross-margin ceiling formula) may be partially absorbed. Check that the grid's corners actually reflect the intended delta.
  • Upside grids and dilution. Implied price per cell should use the per-cell TSM. The closed form avoids circularity.
  • CMP date. Upside grids go stale as soon as CMP moves. Link CMP to a dated input cell, and show the date in the grid title.
  • EV vs equity value for operating grids. For highly levered companies, equity value can be more informative, because small EV changes create large equity swings. Show both if leverage is high.

Key takeaways

  • Flex operating drivers through uniform delta cells on the DCF sheet that feed the assumption rows.
  • Ensure NWC, capex and D&A respond, or growth appears free.
  • Use EV as the output of the operating grid. A derived upside grid from Grid 1 is cheap and consistent.
  • Diverging colour scales centred on zero show where the call flips.
  • The same structure extends to FP&A grids: covenant headroom, volume × price, capex × ROIC.

Practice Questions

Q1. Delta-driver design [L2]

The assumptions sheet holds FY27–FY31 revenue growth of 20%, 19%, 18%, 16%, 14% and EBITDA margins of 13.0%, 13.5%, 14.0%, 14.5%, 15.0% for the base scenario. Design the delta drivers on the DCF sheet and the modified assumption formulas. Describe how you would make the margin delta ramp in linearly over the first three years.

Q2. Growth that destroys value [L3]

A Grid 3 for a capital-goods company shows EV falling as revenue growth rises at a constant margin. Explain the economic reason, with reference to RONIC, WACC and reinvestment. Describe how you would verify that the result is not a modelling error. Explain what management would take from the grid.

💡 Hint: Check whether capex and NWC scale with revenue at an intensity that implies a return below WACC.

Q3. Upside grid from Grid 1 [L1]

Using the Shakti Grid 1 values (WACC 11%–13% across, g 4.5%–6.5% down; for example a centre of ₹ 550, a top-left of ₹ 568 and a bottom-right of ₹ 534) and a CMP of ₹ 405, describe the derived upside grid formula, compute upside for the centre and for the four corners, and propose a colour-scale specification.

Q4. Covenant headroom grid for FP&A [L2]

A company has a covenant of net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0×. FY27 budget EBITDA is ₹ 600 crore and net debt is ₹ 1,500 crore. Of the debt, ₹ 1,200 crore is floating rate. Design a grid of interest-rate change (0 to +200 bp in 50 bp steps) × EBITDA shortfall (0% to −20% in 5% steps). Output covenant headroom, and specify the cash-flow linkages that must flex.
