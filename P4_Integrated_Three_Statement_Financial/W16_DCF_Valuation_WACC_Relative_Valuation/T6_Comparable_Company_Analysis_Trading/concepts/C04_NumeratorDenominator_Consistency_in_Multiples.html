Key concept

Numerator–Denominator Consistency in Multiples

Concept: Numerator–denominator consistency: EV ↔ EBITDA/EBIT/Sales; equity value ↔ PAT/book value

Statement

A valuation multiple \(M = V \div X\) is meaningful only if the value \(V\) represents the claims of exactly those capital providers who are paid from the metric \(X\):

Numerator \(V\) Claimants Valid denominators \(X\)
Enterprise value Debt, leases (post-116), preference, NCI, equity Revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, EBITA, unlevered FCF, capacity measures (tonnes, beds)
Equity value (parent) Parent's ordinary shareholders PAT attributable to owners, EPS, owners' book value, dividends, FCFE

Additional consistency conditions:

  • the same Ind AS 116 basis in \(V\) and \(X\)
  • the same perimeter: consolidated with consolidated, and associates treated the same way on both sides
  • the same period convention across peers (LTM, FY+1)
  • the same date for prices and share counts

Explanation

EBITDA, EBIT and sales are generated before any payment to lenders or minority holders, so they belong to everyone. Divide them by EV. PAT and book value are what remains after interest and NCI, so they belong to the parent's equity. Divide them by equity value.

Mismatches create systematic biases:

Mismatch Effect
Market cap ÷ EBITDA Levered companies look cheap, because the debt claim is ignored
EV ÷ PAT Levered companies look expensive, because the debt claim appears in the numerator but interest has reduced the denominator
Peer P/E applied to the target's EBIT Overvalues the target, because EBIT exceeds PAT. Subtracting debt afterwards does not repair this, because the P/E already prices equity only, so the result mixes two bases
Post-116 EV ÷ pre-116 EBITDA Inflates the multiple (leases in EV, rent deducted in EBITDA)
Pre-116 EV ÷ post-116 EBITDA Deflates the multiple
Consolidated EV ÷ standalone EBITDA Inflates the multiple when subsidiaries are profitable
EV excluding associates ÷ EBITDA plus share of associates' profit Inflates the denominator

NCI subtlety. Consolidated EBITDA includes 100% of subsidiaries, so EV must include NCI. Consolidated PAT is split between owners and NCI, so P/E must use PAT attributable to owners.

Associates subtlety. If the share of associates' profit is excluded from EBITDA (the usual case), subtract the associate investment from EV. If an analyst includes the share of profit in "EBITDA" (non-standard), the associates must stay in EV.

When applying multiples to the target, the same pairing holds. An EV multiple gives target EV, after which you run the bridge to equity. An equity multiple gives target equity value directly. Never apply P/E and then subtract debt.

Illustration

Two peers, both with EBITDA of ₹ 1,000 crore and market caps of ₹ 10,000 crore:

Item Peer L (levered) Peer U (unlevered)
Net debt 4,000 0
EV 14,000 10,000
Interest (pre-tax) 360 0
PAT (25.17% tax, D&A 200) 329 599
Market cap ÷ EBITDA (wrong) 10.0× 10.0×
EV/EBITDA (right) 14.0× 10.0×
P/E (right) 30.4× 16.7×
EV ÷ PAT (wrong) 42.6× 16.7×

The PAT figures are: Peer L, (1,000 − 200 − 360) × 0.7483 = 329. Peer U, 800 × 0.7483 = 599.

The incorrect market cap/EBITDA shows the two peers as equally valued. The correct multiples show that Peer L is pricier relative to its operating earnings, and that its equity is levered: a higher P/E for the same business.

Practice Questions

Q1. Spot the mismatches [L1]

For each of the following, state whether it is consistent and, if not, the direction of the bias:

  • (a) market cap ÷ revenue for a heavily indebted telecom company
  • (b) EV (with leases) ÷ EBITDA after rent for a multiplex chain
  • (c) market cap ÷ consolidated PAT including NCI share
  • (d) EV ÷ EBIT for a cash-rich IT company with treasury income included in EBIT
  • (e) EV excluding associates ÷ EBITDA

Q2. Applying the wrong multiple [L2]

A target has EBIT of ₹ 500 crore, PAT attributable of ₹ 300 crore, net claims of ₹ 1,200 crore and 10 crore diluted shares. The peer P/E is 25× and the peer EV/EBIT is 18×. A colleague computes the value as 25 × 500 − 1,200. Compute the colleague's price, the correct P/E-based price and the correct EV/EBIT-based price. Explain each error.

💡 Hint: Decide which multiples produce EV and which produce equity value before adjusting for claims.

Q3. Interview: why EV/EBITDA for leverage comparisons? [L1]

An interviewer asks why EV/EBITDA is preferred over P/E when peers have very different capital structures. Answer using the Peer L / Peer U logic in under one minute.
