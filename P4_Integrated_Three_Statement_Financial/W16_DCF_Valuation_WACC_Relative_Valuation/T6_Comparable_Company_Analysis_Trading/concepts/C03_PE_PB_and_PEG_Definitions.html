Key concept

P/E, P/B and PEG Definitions

Concept: P/E = Price ÷ Diluted EPS; P/B = Market cap ÷ Owners' equity; PEG = P/E ÷ EPS growth (%)

Statement

\[ P/E = \frac{\text{CMP}}{EPS_{\text{dil}}} = \frac{MC_{\text{dil}}}{PAT_{\text{owners}}} \]

\[ P/B = \frac{MC_{\text{dil}}}{BV_{\text{owners}}} \]

\[ PEG = \frac{P/E}{g_{EPS} \times 100} \]

where:

  • \(EPS_{\text{dil}}\) is diluted EPS for the stated period (LTM, FY+1, FY+2), consolidated, attributable to owners of the parent, before exceptional items (net of tax)
  • \(MC_{\text{dil}}\) is diluted market capitalisation
  • \(PAT_{\text{owners}}\) is profit attributable to owners of the parent, after NCI share
  • \(BV_{\text{owners}}\) is equity attributable to owners: total equity less NCI, from the latest balance sheet
  • \(g_{EPS}\) is the expected annual EPS growth rate (a decimal, e.g. 0.18), usually the 2–3 year forward CAGR. Multiplying by 100 expresses it in percentage points, as the PEG convention requires

Explanation

These are equity-level multiples. The numerator is the value belonging to the parent's shareholders, so the denominator must be the earnings or book value belonging to them, after interest, tax and minority interests (concept c04).

P/E considerations:

  • Ind AS 33 diluted EPS uses the period-average share count and average market price for the TSM. For comps, computing \(MC_{\text{dil}} \div PAT_{\text{owners}}\) at the comps date avoids mismatches after QIPs or buybacks.
  • Strip exceptional items net of tax. Decide whether to strip treasury other income (ex-cash P/E) for cash-rich peers.
  • Tax regime differences affect PAT: Sec 115BAA at 25.17% versus the old regime with deductions and MAT. P/E comparisons across regimes need care.
  • Negative EPS or very low EPS means the multiple is n.m.

P/B considerations:

  • Book value reflects historical cost, accumulated profits, buybacks and goodwill. It is most meaningful where assets are carried near fair value: banks, NBFCs and insurers (for insurers, embedded value is the better measure).
  • The justified P/B is \((ROE - g) \div (K_e - g)\), so P/B and ROE must be read together.
  • For lenders, P/ABV (adjusted book value, net of NPAs) is common in Indian bank research.

PEG considerations:

  • PEG normalises P/E for growth. A PEG of 1.0 is a rough "fair" benchmark from US practice. Indian quality franchises often trade at 2–4× PEG because of their long growth runways and low risk.
  • The growth horizon must match across peers, for example the FY27–FY29 EPS CAGR. It must not be distorted by a low base year, such as recovery from a loss.
  • PEG ignores payout and risk. It cannot compare a 20%-growth cyclical with a 20%-growth staple.

Illustration

A paints peer (illustrative), comps date 14 November 2026:

Item Value
CMP ₹ 2,850
Diluted shares 96.0 crore
Diluted market cap ₹ 2,73,600 crore
PAT attributable to owners, FY27E (pre-exceptional) ₹ 5,600 crore
Equity attributable to owners ₹ 21,000 crore
EPS CAGR FY27–FY29E 12%
Multiple Calculation Result
P/E FY27E 2,73,600 ÷ 5,600 48.9×
P/B 2,73,600 ÷ 21,000 13.0×
PEG 48.9 ÷ 12 4.1

The PEG of 4.1 signals that growth alone does not explain the P/E. The market is paying for brand durability, high ROE (about 27% here) and pricing power. These drivers belong in the qualitative peer discussion (s01).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the three multiples [L1]

A consumer-durables company has CMP ₹ 1,640 and diluted shares of 32.0 crore. FY27E consolidated PAT is ₹ 1,050 crore, including a ₹ 40 crore post-tax exceptional gain, and the NCI share of PAT is ₹ 30 crore. Total equity is ₹ 7,400 crore, including NCI of ₹ 220 crore. The FY27–FY29 EPS CAGR is 20%. Compute P/E (normalised), P/B and PEG.

Q2. Base-year distortion in PEG [L2]

A cyclical chemicals company's EPS was ₹ 12 in FY26 (a trough) and is forecast at ₹ 30 in FY28, which implies a 58% CAGR. Its FY27E P/E is 28×. Compute its PEG. Explain why it is misleading, and propose a better growth measure.

💡 Hint: Consider normalised mid-cycle EPS as the base.

Q3. P/ABV for a bank [L2]

A private bank has book value of ₹ 1,20,000 crore, net NPAs of ₹ 4,500 crore and a market cap of ₹ 2,64,000 crore. Compute P/B and P/ABV (adjusting book value for net NPAs, ignoring tax). Explain why Indian bank analysts prefer P/ABV.
