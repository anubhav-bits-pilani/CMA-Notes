Key concept

Enterprise Value Definition for Trading Comps

Concept: EV = Diluted market cap + Debt + Lease liabilities (if EBITDA post-116) + Preference + NCI − Cash & liquid investments − Non-operating investments

Statement

\[ EV = MC_{\text{dil}} + D + L \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\text{post-116}} + PS + NCI - C - NOI \]

where:

  • \(MC_{\text{dil}}\) is CMP × diluted shares (TSM at CMP, if-converted)
  • \(D\) is total borrowings, including current maturities and accrued interest
  • \(L\) is lease liabilities. The indicator \(\mathbb{1}_{\text{post-116}}\) = 1 if the EBITDA used is post-Ind AS 116, and 0 if it is pre-116 (rent-adjusted)
  • \(PS\) is preference capital not counted in diluted shares
  • \(NCI\) is non-controlling interests: book value, or market value if the subsidiary is listed
  • \(C\) is unrestricted cash, bank balances and liquid investments
  • \(NOI\) is non-operating investments: associates and JVs (if their income is excluded from EBITDA), investment property, strategic stakes

All balances come from the latest consolidated balance sheet, adjusted for material subsequent events. Price and shares are taken at the comps date.

Explanation

EV measures the market value of the operating business attributable to all capital providers. It is the numerator for every firm-level multiple (EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT), so each component must match what the denominator covers.

  • Debt and preference are added because operating earnings (EBITDA, EBIT) are available to service them.
  • Leases are added only when the EBITDA excludes rent (post-116), because those rent cash flows then sit "below" EBITDA, as for debt.
  • NCI is added because consolidated EBITDA includes 100% of subsidiaries.
  • Cash and liquid investments are subtracted because their income (other income) is excluded from EBITDA.
  • Associates and other non-operating investments are subtracted for the same reason: their earnings are not in EBITDA.

This is the DCF equity bridge (topic t4, c01) run in reverse. Use identical definitions in both directions. Otherwise the implied DCF multiples and the comps multiples are not comparable, and the exit multiple sourced from comps (topic t3) would be inconsistent with the DCF bridge.

Practical simplifications for peers, where detail is limited:

  • book NCI is standard
  • restricted cash may not be separately disclosed; apply the obvious exclusions, such as the unpaid dividend account
  • debt-like items (gratuity deficits, contingencies) are usually omitted for peers unless they are material. If they are included for the target, be aware of the asymmetry.

Illustration

Peer Y (illustrative, consolidated, post-116, ₹ crore, comps date 14 November 2026):

Component Amount
Diluted market cap (₹ 240 × 50.0 crore) 12,000
+ Borrowings 2,700
+ Lease liabilities (EBITDA is post-116) 650
+ Preference capital 0
+ NCI (book) 300
− Cash & liquid investments (250)
− Investment in associates (share of profit below EBITDA) (180)
EV 15,220

If the analyst also wanted a pre-116 EV/EBITDA, the lease liabilities (650) would be removed, giving an EV of 14,570, to be divided by EBITDA after cash rent.

Practice Questions

Q1. EV build with basis choice [L1]

A peer has:

  • CMP ₹ 1,480 and basic shares 6.5 crore; options 0.12 crore at ₹ 900
  • borrowings ₹ 820 crore and lease liabilities ₹ 1,240 crore
  • CCPS converting into 0.20 crore shares
  • book NCI ₹ 40 crore
  • cash ₹ 310 crore and liquid MFs ₹ 190 crore
  • associates ₹ 70 crore

Compute EV on both post-116 and pre-116 bases.

Q2. Asymmetric treatment risk [L2]

Your DCF bridge for the target deducts a ₹ 90 crore gratuity deficit and ₹ 60 crore of probable tax disputes. Your comps EVs for peers exclude such items. Explain the direction of the bias in implied multiples. Describe how you would either correct it or disclose it.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the target's implied EV/EBITDA is computed before or after these items.

Q3. Listed subsidiary NCI [L2]

A holding-company peer owns 55% of a listed subsidiary with a market cap of ₹ 20,000 crore. Book NCI is ₹ 2,400 crore. Explain which NCI value to add in EV and why. Quantify the difference in EV and its effect on the peer's EV/EBITDA if consolidated EBITDA is ₹ 3,000 crore and the rest of EV is ₹ 25,000 crore.
