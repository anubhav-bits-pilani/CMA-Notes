Subtopic note

Normalising Peer Metrics: Ind AS 116, Exceptionals, Other Income

Scope: Normalisation: Ind AS 116 basis, exceptional items, other income, consolidated basis

Why it matters

Reported Ind AS numbers are not directly comparable across peers. One retailer's EBITDA excludes ₹ 500 crore of rent while another's owned-store model has little rent at all. One company books a ₹ 120 crore exceptional gain on land sale. Another earns 25% of its PBT as treasury income. Some data providers quote standalone numbers. Normalisation strips these differences so that each multiple compares like with like. It is also where the Phase 1–2 technical accounting skills pay off. Knowing exactly where Ind AS and Schedule III put each item lets you adjust confidently and explain the adjustments to reviewers.

Core notes

1. Ind AS 116 basis

Under Ind AS 116, lessees recognise ROU assets and lease liabilities. Rent is replaced by depreciation (above EBIT) and interest (below EBIT). The effects are:

  • EBITDA rises by roughly the cash lease payments.
  • EBIT rises by lease payments less ROU depreciation, which is approximately the interest component.
  • PAT changes slightly in the early years of a lease, because front-loaded interest lowers it, and is roughly neutral over the lease life.
  • EV rises when lease liabilities are included.

Choose one basis for the whole comps set, matching the DCF.

Basis EV includes leases? EBITDA EBIT
Post-116 (as reported) Yes Reported EBITDA Reported EBIT
Pre-116 (rent-adjusted) No Reported EBITDA − cash lease payments (principal + interest from the cash-flow statement) Reported EBIT − lease interest (an approximation)

Data sources:

  • the lease liability note: maturity analysis, additions, interest
  • the cash-flow statement: lease principal and interest in financing activities
  • the "short-term and low-value lease" expense under Ind AS 116 para 6, which stays in other expenses on either basis

Which basis? Post-116 is easier and auditable. Pre-116 is closer to how operators think about store economics, and is less sensitive to lease-term judgements. For lease-heavy sectors, many analysts present both.

P/E is basis-neutral for EV purposes, but the PAT timing effect means early-life lease portfolios show slightly lower PAT under Ind AS 116.

2. Exceptional items

Schedule III (Division II) provides an "Exceptional items" line before PBT. Ind AS 1.97–98 requires separate disclosure of material items, such as:

  • impairments
  • restructuring costs
  • litigation settlements
  • profit on sale of property or investments
  • one-time employee costs, such as code-on-wages related gratuity adjustments

The normalisation steps are:

  • Exclude exceptional items from EBITDA, EBIT and PAT, net of tax for PAT.
  • Look beyond the "exceptional" label. Some companies bury one-offs in other expenses or other operating revenue, such as export incentive true-ups or insurance claims. Read the MD&A and the notes.
  • Avoid the reverse error of treating recurring "one-offs" (annual restructuring) as exceptional.

3. Other income

"Other income" (Schedule III) includes interest on deposits, dividends, MF gains, rental income, foreign-exchange gains and sundry items.

  • EBITDA and EBIT should exclude treasury-type other income, consistent with subtracting cash and investments from EV.
  • Operating other income (scrap sales, export incentives, operating FX gains on receivables) is sometimes reclassified into EBITDA if it is recurring and business-linked. Document the treatment.
  • P/E includes other income in PAT. For cash-rich peers, also compute an ex-cash P/E (s04).

4. Consolidated basis

  • Use consolidated financials. Market cap values the whole group, including subsidiaries.
  • Use PAT attributable to owners for P/E and owners' equity for P/B.
  • EV includes NCI. EBITDA includes 100% of subsidiaries.
  • Associates' and JVs' share of profit is below EBITDA. Subtract associate investments from EV, consistently with the DCF bridge (topic t4, s03).
  • Some data aggregators default to standalone figures. Check against the consolidated results filing.

The pre-116 multiple is much higher, because removing rent cuts EBITDA by a larger proportion than removing leases cuts EV. A peer set mixing the two bases could show a 15-turn spread from accounting alone.

Excel implementation

Add basis-flag columns (Lease_Basis, Consolidated, Exceptionals_Removed) and an adjustments block per peer. Then add a check:

=IF(AND(Lease_Basis="Pre-116", Lease_Liab_in_EV<>0),"Basis error","OK")

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Owned vs leased models (DMart vs Trent and V-Mart). Pre-116 comparison arguably penalises lessees less arbitrarily, while post-116 makes owned-store companies look relatively cheaper. Present both bases.
  • Variable lease payments linked to sales (common in Indian malls) are expensed and not capitalised. They stay in EBITDA on both bases, so check how material they are.
  • Ind AS 115 or 116 transition effects in LTM. Use restated comparatives.
  • Capitalised R&D or borrowing costs. Policies differ across pharma and infrastructure peers. EV/EBITDA is less affected than P/E.
  • Hyperinflationary or FX-heavy subsidiaries. Strip translation effects if material.
  • Government grants and PLI incentives (Production Linked Incentive schemes). Decide whether they are recurring operating income, and apply the decision consistently across peers.

Key takeaways

  • Align the Ind AS 116 basis across EV and EBITDA for every peer. Present both bases in lease-heavy sectors.
  • Strip exceptional items, labelled or hidden, from all earnings metrics.
  • Exclude treasury other income from EBITDA and EBIT, consistent with cash being subtracted from EV.
  • Use consolidated figures, owners' PAT and owners' equity. Treat associates consistently.
  • Show a per-peer normalisation schedule with basis flags and checks.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pre- vs post-116 restatement [L2]

A QSR peer reports:

  • post-116 EBITDA of ₹ 640 crore
  • ROU depreciation of ₹ 210 crore and lease interest of ₹ 95 crore
  • cash lease payments of ₹ 285 crore (principal ₹ 190 crore, interest ₹ 95 crore)
  • lease liabilities of ₹ 1,150 crore
  • market cap ₹ 14,500 crore, borrowings ₹ 300 crore and cash ₹ 450 crore

Compute EV/EBITDA and EV/EBIT on both bases.

Q2. Hidden exceptional items [L2]

A chemical company's FY26 notes show the following:

  • other operating revenue includes a ₹ 48 crore insurance claim for a FY24 fire
  • other expenses include ₹ 22 crore of one-time VRS costs
  • the exceptional items line shows a ₹ 15 crore impairment
  • reported EBITDA (including other operating revenue, excluding other income) is ₹ 910 crore

Compute normalised EBITDA. Explain what evidence you would cite for each adjustment.

💡 Hint: Decide for each item whether it recurs, and where it sits relative to EBITDA.

Q3. Other income and ex-cash P/E [L2]

An IT services peer has a market cap of ₹ 1,20,000 crore, cash and investments of ₹ 18,000 crore, PAT of ₹ 4,600 crore, and other income (treasury) of ₹ 1,100 crore taxed at 25.17%. Compute the reported P/E and the ex-cash P/E. Explain which is more comparable to a debt-funded peer.

Q4. Standalone vs consolidated trap [L1]

A database lists a peer's P/E as 18× using standalone PAT of ₹ 900 crore. Consolidated PAT attributable to owners is ₹ 1,250 crore, because the peer has profitable overseas subsidiaries. Compute the consolidated P/E. Explain why the standalone figure is inconsistent with market cap.
