Subtopic note

Peer Selection by Business Model, Size, Growth and Margins

Scope: Peer selection by business model, size, growth and margins (e.g., Trent vs DMart, ABFRL, V-Mart, Shoppers Stop; Pidilite vs Asian Paints, Berger, Astral)

Why it matters

A multiple is only as meaningful as the peer set behind it. Choose peers that grow faster, earn higher margins or carry different risk, and the median multiple will mislead, whatever the arithmetic. In India, the problem is acute. Many sectors have only a handful of listed companies. Leaders often trade at large premiums: consumer franchises at 50×+ P/E. Conglomerate structures blur segment comparisons. Peer selection is also the most debated judgement in IPO "basis for issue price" sections, fairness opinions and research initiations. Reviewers will ask "why this peer, and why not that one?"

Core notes

Selection criteria

Dimension What to compare Why it matters for multiples
Business model Products, customers, channels, asset intensity, owned vs leased assets, B2B vs B2C, revenue model Drives margins, capital needs and risk
End-market and geography Domestic vs export mix, urban vs tier-2/3, cyclicality Drives growth and volatility
Size Revenue, EBITDA, market cap; Nifty 50 / Next 50 / midcap / smallcap Liquidity, index inclusion and scale advantages command premiums
Growth Revenue and EPS CAGR (historical 3–5 years, consensus 2–3 years) Higher growth justifies higher multiples (PEG logic)
Profitability and returns EBITDA margin, ROCE, ROE Value creation per unit of growth
Balance sheet Net debt/EBITDA, working-capital intensity Risk and the EV-vs-equity multiple choice
Accounting basis Ind AS consolidated, FY end, Ind AS 116 exposure Comparability of metrics
Liquidity and free float Average daily traded value, promoter holding Illiquid stocks have unreliable prices

A robust process starts with a long list: industry classification (NSE/BSE sector indices, AMFI market-cap categories), competitors named in the target's DRHP or annual report, and database screens. It then filters to a short list of 5–8 core peers, perhaps with a secondary "wider" set. Document why each peer was included or excluded.

Example 1: Trent and Indian apparel/value retail

Trent operates Westside (premium-value fashion), Zudio (value fashion, rapid store expansion) and Star (food and grocery, through a JV structure). Its candidate peers differ on every dimension.

Candidate Business model vs Trent Selection view
Avenue Supermarts (DMart) Grocery-led value retail, largely owned stores, very low margin and high asset turns, general merchandise and apparel a minority of sales A growth and "quality value retail" peer, but a different category mix and lease profile. Include it with caution, and compare on a pre-116 or consistent basis
ABFRL Fashion brands, ethnic and premium. The Madura lifestyle-brands business was demerged into a separately listed company in 2025, so the perimeter has changed. History of losses and leverage Brand-led apparel peer. Check the post-demerger perimeter and normalise the losses (P/E is likely n.m.)
V-Mart Retail Value fashion in tier-2/3 towns, leased stores Closest to Zudio's positioning, but smaller and slower growth
Shoppers Stop Department stores, premium, leased Same category, but a different format, lower growth and high lease intensity

The judgement: no peer matches Trent's mix of growth and returns. Many analysts present the peer multiples alongside each peer's growth and ROCE, and justify Trent's premium explicitly rather than applying a median. For Trent, a DCF with comps as a cross-check is more defensible than comps alone.

Example 2: Pidilite and building materials/consumer chemicals

Pidilite is a consumer and industrial adhesives franchise (the Fevicol and Dr. Fixit brands), with dominant market share, high margins and pricing power.

Candidate Business model vs Pidilite Selection view
Asian Paints Decorative paints leader. Similar distribution through dealers and contractors, similar brand power and premium valuation, home-improvement end-market Strong "consumer-chemicals franchise" peer. The new entrants into paints (since 2024) affect its growth and margin outlook
Berger Paints Number two in decorative paints, plus industrial coatings A peer for the paints sub-segment, with lower margins than Asian Paints
Astral Plastic pipes (CPVC, PVC) plus an adhesives business (Resinova and others) Overlaps only in adhesives. The pipes business has a different margin and capex profile. Use a segmental view, or keep it in the wider set

Pidilite's closest listed "pure" adhesive peers are limited. The peer set therefore mixes the paints franchises (shared channel economics) with partial overlaps. Statistics should be weighted by judgement, with a written rationale.

Quantitative screens

A useful screen table sits next to the multiples:

Peer Revenue (₹ cr) 3-yr revenue CAGR EBITDA margin ROCE Net debt/EBITDA Mkt cap (₹ cr) Include?

Practitioners sometimes regress EV/EBITDA on expected growth and ROCE across the peers. This "value map" indicates whether the target's premium or discount is explained by fundamentals. With only five peers, treat the regression as indicative only.

Global peers

When there are few domestic peers, global companies can be included, for example in specialty chemicals, auto components or IT services. Their multiples reflect different risk-free rates, ERPs and growth, so present them as a separate block. Do not merge them into the Indian median without adjustment.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Conglomerates and holding companies. Use segment data, or exclude them. Their multiples blend businesses.
  • Recently listed companies. Post-IPO prices may be volatile, and lock-in expiries (SEBI ICDR) affect supply. Note their listing date.
  • Turnaround or loss-making peers. Keep them for EV/Sales. Exclude them from P/E as n.m.
  • Promoter-held, illiquid peers (free float below 10–15%). Prices may not reflect fundamentals, so flag or exclude them.
  • Subsidiaries of MNCs (listed Indian arms). These have different payout policies and royalty arrangements, and royalty outflows depress margins. Normalise them, or show them separately.
  • Corporate actions (demergers, mergers). Use the post-transaction perimeter and restated financials.

Key takeaways

  • Select peers on business model first, then on size, growth, margins and returns. Document every inclusion and exclusion.
  • Few Indian sectors offer perfect peers. Combine a core set with a wider reference set.
  • Show the operating benchmarks next to the multiples, so that premiums and discounts can be explained.
  • Trent and Pidilite illustrate franchise premiums that a median cannot capture. Use comps as a cross-check to intrinsic value.
  • Watch for perimeter changes (demergers), illiquidity and loss-making peers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Peer screen table [L2]

Using illustrative data for five candidate peers of a mid-sized NSE-listed value retailer (target revenue ₹ 3,500 crore, CAGR 22%, EBITDA margin 11% post-116), construct a screen table with the columns shown in the notes. Apply explicit inclusion rules (for example revenue between 0.3× and 3× the target's, CAGR within ±10 pp, positive EBITDA). Justify the final peer set of four.

Q2. Trent premium justification [L3]

Write a 250-word note to an investment committee explaining why Trent trades at a premium to the median of DMart, ABFRL, V-Mart and Shoppers Stop. Use qualitative business-model arguments and the quantitative evidence you would show (growth, ROCE, store economics). State how you would use the peer set in a valuation given this premium.

💡 Hint: Separate what the peers tell you about sector multiples from what they cannot tell you about Trent's growth runway.

Q3. Astral inclusion debate [L2]

For a Pidilite comps set, argue for and against including Astral. Describe a segmental approach that would let you use Astral's adhesives business as a data point, and state its limitations.

Q4. Global peers block [L1]

A specialty chemicals target has only two close Indian peers. Explain how you would present three global peers alongside them. Describe the adjustments or caveats needed, and why you would not simply merge all five into one median.
