Subtopic note

LTM and Forward Metrics, Calendarisation and Consensus

Scope: LTM and forward (FY+1, FY+2) metrics; calendarisation; consensus estimates

Why it matters

A multiple compares today's price with a metric for a period. If peers' metrics cover different periods, the multiples are not comparable. This happens when one peer's "FY26" ends in March and another's in December, or when one peer's LTM includes a strong recent quarter and another's does not. Markets price future earnings, so forward multiples (FY+1, FY+2) based on consensus estimates are usually the most relevant for growth companies. LTM multiples are the auditable anchor. For Indian peers, most of which have March year-ends and publish quarterly results under SEBI LODR, the mechanics are straightforward, but they must be applied consistently.

Core notes

LTM (last twelve months)

\[ LTM = FY_{\text{last}} + YTD_{\text{current}} - YTD_{\text{prior}} \]

For example, at a November 2026 comps date, after the Q2 FY27 results:

\[ LTM_{\text{Sep-26}} = FY26 + H1\,FY27 - H1\,FY26 \]

Sources:

  • quarterly results under LODR Regulation 33, filed within 45 days of each quarter-end (60 days for the annual audited results)
  • investor presentations, for segment and operating KPIs

Use consolidated results. Strip exceptional items and other income consistently (s05).

Balance-sheet items are not "LTM'd". For P/B and net debt, use the latest point-in-time balance sheet.

Forward metrics

  • FY+1, FY+2. The next and following fiscal years. For a March-year-end company in November 2026, FY+1 = FY27 (ending March 2027) and FY+2 = FY28.
  • Rolling NTM (next twelve months). A time-weighted blend that keeps the horizon constant:

\[ NTM = w \times FY_{+1} + (1 - w) \times FY_{+2}, \quad w = \frac{\text{months remaining in FY+1}}{12} \]

In mid-November 2026, about 4.5 months of FY27 remain, so \(w \approx 0.375\).

Forward multiples matter most where growth is high. A company growing EPS at 25% looks expensive on LTM P/E and less so on FY+2.

Calendarisation

When peers have different fiscal year-ends, convert their metrics to a common period, usually the target's fiscal year. To map a December-year-end (CY) peer to a March-ending FY, start from the months covered. FY27 runs April 2026 – March 2027, which is the last 9 months of CY2026 plus the first 3 months of CY2027. A common error is to weight CY2026 by 3/12, which wrongly attaches January–March 2026 (already in FY26) to FY27. The correct mapping is:

\[ FY27_{\text{equiv}} = \tfrac{9}{12} \times CY2026 + \tfrac{3}{12} \times CY2027 \]

Going the other way, converting a March-FY company to calendar years:

\[ CY2026_{\text{equiv}} = \tfrac{3}{12} \times FY26 + \tfrac{9}{12} \times FY27 \]

This is because Jan–Mar 2026 sits in FY26 and Apr–Dec 2026 sits in FY27.

If quarterly data is available, sum the actual quarters instead of using time-weights. This is more accurate for seasonal businesses such as apparel retail (festive quarter) or agrochemicals (kharif quarter).

Consensus estimates

  • Sources. Bloomberg (BEst), LSEG/Refinitiv I/B/E/S, FactSet, S&P Capital IQ, and Indian aggregators. Free sources (company websites, brokers' published reports) are partial.
  • Metrics. Mean or median revenue, EBITDA, EPS and DPS for FY+1 and FY+2. Check the number of estimates (n) and the dispersion (high/low). A consensus from only 2 analysts is fragile.
  • Basis. Confirm that consensus EBITDA is on the same Ind AS 116 and consolidation basis as your reported LTM. Brokers sometimes publish pre-116 EBITDA for retailers.
  • Staleness. Estimates lag results. After a quarterly result, wait for revisions or note the "post-result consensus" date.
  • Your own forecast vs consensus. For the target, show both. The difference is your variant view (topic t4, s07).

Illustration

Peer B (March FY), comps date 14 November 2026, ₹ crore:

Metric FY26 (A) H1 FY26 H1 FY27 LTM Sep-26 FY27E (cons.) FY28E (cons.) NTM
Revenue 9,800 4,700 5,350 10,450 11,000 12,400 11,875
EBITDA 1,720 810 960 1,870 1,900 2,250 2,119

The NTM uses \(w = 0.375\).

A December-year-end peer has CY2026E EBITDA of ₹ 640 crore and CY2027E of ₹ 720 crore. On a FY27-equivalent basis: \(0.75 \times 640 + 0.25 \times 720\) = ₹ 660 crore.

Excel

LTM:          =FY_Last + YTD_Curr - YTD_Prior
NTM weight:   =(EOMONTH(FY1_End,0) - Comps_Date) / 365
NTM:          =w*FY1 + (1-w)*FY2
Calendarise:  =Months_in_CY_A/12*CY_A + (1-Months_in_CY_A/12)*CY_B

Keep a period-label row on the comps sheet ("LTM Sep-26", "FY27E", "FY28E") and a basis-check column.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Acquisitions within the LTM. Pro-forma the LTM for the acquired business (add the pre-acquisition months) if EV includes its debt and value. Otherwise the multiple is overstated.
  • Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105). Exclude them from LTM and from EV.
  • Changes in fiscal year (a company moving from a December to a March year-end, which leaves a 15-month transition period). Rebuild the LTM from quarters.
  • Seasonality. Use quarter sums, not time-weights.
  • Missing consensus for small-caps. Use your own forecasts, flag them clearly, and treat forward multiples as less reliable.
  • Restated comparatives. Use restated prior-year YTD figures in the LTM formula, not originally reported ones.

Key takeaways

  • LTM = last FY + current YTD − prior YTD, built from LODR quarterly results on a consolidated, normalised basis.
  • Forward multiples on consensus FY+1 and FY+2 (or NTM) are key for growth sectors. Check the n, dispersion and basis.
  • Calendarise peers with different year-ends by month-weighting, or preferably by summing quarters.
  • Balance-sheet metrics are point-in-time. Only P&L metrics are annualised.
  • Label every column with its period and basis.

Practice Questions

Q1. LTM build [L1]

A peer reports FY26 EBITDA of ₹ 2,340 crore, Q1+Q2 FY26 EBITDA of ₹ 1,050 crore and Q1+Q2 FY27 EBITDA of ₹ 1,220 crore. FY26 included a ₹ 60 crore one-off gain in other operating income in Q4, and there were no one-offs in H1. Compute the reported and normalised LTM EBITDA to September 2026.

Q2. Calendarisation [L2]

A December-year-end MNC subsidiary listed on NSE reports CY2025 EBITDA of ₹ 820 crore. Consensus is CY2026E ₹ 900 crore and CY2027E ₹ 1,000 crore. Your target has a March year-end. Compute the FY27E- and FY28E-equivalent EBITDA by time-weighting. Explain when you would use quarterly data instead.

💡 Hint: List which calendar months fall into each fiscal year before weighting.

Q3. NTM multiple at two dates [L2]

A peer trades at an EV of ₹ 18,000 crore on both 15 May 2026 and 15 February 2027. Its FY27E EBITDA is ₹ 1,200 crore and FY28E is ₹ 1,450 crore. Compute the NTM EV/EBITDA on each date. Explain why the NTM multiple changes even though EV is unchanged, and why this matters when comparing multiples over time.

Q4. Consensus quality review [L3]

For a five-peer comps set, the consensus FY+1 EPS for one peer has n = 2 estimates with a range of ₹ 18–34. Another peer's consensus EBITDA appears to be pre-116. Write a review note on how to handle each issue. Explain whether the peer stays in the forward-multiple statistics.
