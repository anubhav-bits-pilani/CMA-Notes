Subtopic note

Precedent Transactions, Control Premium and SEBI SAST Pricing

Scope: Precedent transactions: deal EV/EBITDA, control premium, SEBI SAST pricing

Why it matters

Trading comps price small, non-controlling stakes. When a buyer acquires control, it usually pays more. The extra reflects synergies, the right to direct strategy and cash flows, and competitive bidding. Precedent-transaction analysis collects past M&A deals in the sector, computes the multiples paid, and applies them to the target. It provides a "control value" bar on the football field. In India, listed-company acquisitions are shaped by the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (SAST). These set mandatory open-offer triggers and minimum pricing, so observed premiums partly reflect regulation, not just negotiation. Corporate development, transaction advisory and fairness-opinion roles use this analysis constantly.

Core notes

Building a precedents table

Selecting precedents:

  • same sector and business model (as for comps)
  • recent: the last 3–7 years, since older deals reflect different rate and market regimes
  • comparable size and deal type (control vs minority, strategic vs financial buyer)
  • Indian and, where few exist, regional or global deals, presented as separate blocks

Unlisted-target deals often have limited disclosure. Use MCA filings, acquirers' presentations and press estimates. Grade the data quality.

Control premium

\[ \text{Control premium} = \frac{\text{Offer price}}{\text{Unaffected price}} - 1 \]

The unaffected price is the market price before news or rumours of the deal affected it. Use, for example, the closing price or VWAP 1 day, 1 week or 1 month before the earliest leak or announcement. Choose the window carefully: a run-up from leaks inflates the "unaffected" price and understates the premium.

Premiums vary widely by deal. They depend on synergies, competition, the free float and the regulatory floor price. Present the median and range of the precedent premiums rather than a rule-of-thumb figure.

Use the premium as a cross-check. Trading-comps value × (1 + typical premium) should be broadly consistent with the precedent-multiple value.

SEBI SAST: triggers and pricing

Triggers (Regulations 3–5):

  • Reg 3(1). Acquiring shares or voting rights that take the acquirer and persons acting in concert (PACs) to 25% or more triggers an open offer.
  • Reg 3(2). An acquirer already holding 25% or more, but less than the maximum permissible non-public shareholding (75%, given the 25% minimum public shareholding requirement), triggers an open offer by acquiring more than 5% in a financial year (creeping acquisition).
  • Reg 4. Acquiring control, irrespective of shareholding, triggers an open offer.
  • Reg 5. Indirect acquisitions (control of a parent) can trigger offers for listed subsidiaries.

Offer size (Reg 7): at least 26% of the total shares of the target. In effect, a 25% trigger leads to an acquirer potentially holding 51% or more.

Minimum offer price (Reg 8(2), direct acquisitions of frequently traded shares). The offer price must be at least the highest of:

  1. the highest negotiated price per share under the agreement that triggered the offer
  2. the volume-weighted average price paid or payable by the acquirer or PACs in the 52 weeks before the public announcement
  3. the highest price paid or payable by the acquirer or PACs in the 26 weeks before the public announcement
  4. the volume-weighted average market price for 60 trading days before the public announcement, on the exchange with the highest volume, if the shares are frequently traded

For infrequently traded shares, the price is determined using valuation parameters (book value, comparable trading multiples and similar), supported by a valuation. Further rules cover indirect acquisitions and adjustments for delays. Check the regulation text and the latest amendments for the specific case, because SEBI periodically revises the SAST framework.

Implication for precedents. Because of the 60-day VWAP floor and the negotiated-price floor, the open-offer price for the public is at least the promoter's negotiated price. Indian deal premiums therefore often apply to the whole target equity, which makes them more comparable across deals. Note that the negotiated price itself may embed a "control premium" paid to the promoter, which the floor rule extends to public shareholders.

Illustration

A strategic acquirer agrees to buy a 40% promoter stake in an NSE-listed paints-and-coatings company at ₹ 1,560 per share.

Item Value
Negotiated price ₹ 1,560
60-trading-day VWAP before announcement ₹ 1,290
Acquirer's purchases in prior 52 / 26 weeks None
Minimum open-offer price ₹ 1,560 (the highest of the floor parameters)
Unaffected price (1-month VWAP before rumours) ₹ 1,240
Control premium \(1{,}560 / 1{,}240 - 1\) = 25.8%
Diluted shares 8.0 crore → equity value (100%) ₹ 12,480 crore
Net debt + leases ₹ 220 crore
Deal EV ₹ 12,700 crore
LTM EBITDA (normalised) ₹ 610 crore
Deal EV/EBITDA 20.8×

If peer trading comps show a median of 16.5×, the deal multiple implies a premium of about 26% at the EV level. This is consistent with the price premium, as expected when net debt is small.

Applying precedents to the target

\[ EV_{\text{target}} = \text{Median deal } EV/EBITDA \times LTM\,EBITDA_{\text{target}} \]

Then run the standard bridge. The resulting value is a control value. Use it for M&A pricing or a fairness opinion, and show it on the football field. Do not use it to rate a minority stake in a listed company without caveats.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Stale precedents. A 2021 deal at peak-liquidity multiples is not comparable to a 2026 rate environment. Show the deal dates and consider a time-based exclusion.
  • Distressed deals (IBC resolution plans under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016). Multiples reflect distress and haircuts. Exclude them, or show them separately.
  • Minority stake deals (PE investments at 10–20%). These have no control premium. Some include special rights. Classify them separately.
  • Stock-for-stock deals and schemes of arrangement. These are value-for-value swaps that the acquirer's own valuation can inflate or deflate. Use the swap ratio and the acquirer's undisturbed price.
  • Synergy-heavy strategic deals. High multiples reflect buyer-specific synergies. A financial buyer would not pay them.
  • SAST exemptions (Reg 10: inter-se promoter transfers, schemes). These deals may carry no open offer and no premium.

Key takeaways

  • Precedents price control. Compute deal EV/EBITDA on the target's pre-deal LTM, normalised, with consistent EV.
  • Control premium is offer ÷ unaffected price − 1. Pick the unaffected date before any leak.
  • Under SAST, the 25% trigger, control and creeping acquisitions lead to an open offer for at least 26%, priced at the highest of the Reg 8(2) parameters.
  • Present precedents with dates, consideration type and context. Exclude distressed and minority deals.
  • Show precedent values as a separate, usually higher, football-field bar, and explain the gap to trading comps.

Practice Questions

Q1. SAST minimum offer price [L2]

An acquirer buys 27% of a frequently traded NSE company from its promoters at ₹ 820 per share. In the prior 26 weeks, it bought 2% on the market at prices up to ₹ 790. Its 52-week VWAP of purchases is ₹ 760, and the 60-trading-day VWAP before the announcement is ₹ 805. Determine whether an open offer is triggered, its minimum size and its minimum price, citing the relevant regulations.

Q2. Precedents table build [L2]

Using four illustrative Indian deals in specialty chemicals (assume the announcement dates, stakes, prices, net debt and LTM EBITDA), build a precedents table with deal EV/EBITDA and control premiums. State your exclusion rules. Compute the median and range, and apply them to a target with LTM EBITDA of ₹ 380 crore, net debt of ₹ 150 crore and 6.5 crore diluted shares.

💡 Hint: Gross up each stake to a 100% equity value before adding net debt.

Q3. Unaffected price choice [L2]

A target's stock rose from ₹ 400 to ₹ 470 in the four weeks before a deal announcement at ₹ 540, amid media reports of talks. Compute the premium using the 1-day-prior price and the 1-month-prior price. Recommend which to present and why.

Q4. Interview: why are precedents above comps? [L1]

Explain in under 90 seconds why precedent-transaction multiples typically exceed trading-comps multiples, and name two situations in which they might not.
