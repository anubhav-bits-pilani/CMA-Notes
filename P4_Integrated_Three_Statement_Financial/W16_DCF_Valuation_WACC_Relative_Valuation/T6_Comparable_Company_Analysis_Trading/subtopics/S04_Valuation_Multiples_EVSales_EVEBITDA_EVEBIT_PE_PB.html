Subtopic note

Valuation Multiples: EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, P/E, P/B, PEG

Scope: Multiples: EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, P/E, P/B, PEG

Why it matters

Each multiple answers a slightly different question and suits different businesses. EV/EBITDA is the workhorse for Indian manufacturing, consumer and infrastructure companies. P/E dominates retail-investor and broker discussion. P/B is the anchor for banks and NBFCs. EV/Sales is used for loss-making or early-stage companies. PEG adjusts P/E for growth. Knowing which multiple to use, its limitations, and how to compute it on a consistent basis is core interview material for valuation and FP&A roles. In practice you present several multiples, explain which you weight, and say why.

Core notes

Definitions and when to use each

Multiple Formula Best for Limitations
EV/Sales EV ÷ Revenue Loss-making, early-stage, high-growth (new-age platforms); businesses with stable margins Ignores profitability. Only valid across peers with similar margin potential
EV/EBITDA EV ÷ EBITDA Capital-intensive and leveraged businesses; cross-capital-structure comparisons; most Indian corporates Ignores capex and D&A intensity, and tax differences. Ind AS 116 basis sensitivity
EV/EBIT EV ÷ EBIT Where D&A intensity differs (asset-heavy vs asset-light peers); closer to cash earnings Affected by depreciation policies and acquisition amortisation
P/E Price ÷ Diluted EPS (or Market cap ÷ PAT attributable to owners) Stable, profitable companies with similar leverage; consumer, IT services Affected by leverage, tax, other income and one-offs. Meaningless for losses
P/B Market cap ÷ Owners' equity Banks, NBFCs, insurers, asset-heavy cyclicals Book value distorted by buybacks, intangibles and goodwill. Weak for asset-light franchises
PEG P/E ÷ EPS growth (%) Growth-adjusted comparison across consumer and tech names Sensitive to the growth horizon. Ignores risk and payout

The fundamental drivers

Each multiple can be linked to DCF fundamentals. This is how you justify premiums.

  • EV/EBITDA, stable growth:

\[ \frac{EV}{EBITDA} = \frac{(1-t)\left(1 - \frac{g}{RONIC}\right) \times \frac{EBIT}{EBITDA}}{WACC - g} \]

Higher returns on new capital, higher growth (when RONIC > WACC), lower WACC and lower D&A intensity all raise the multiple.

  • P/E, stable growth:

\[ \frac{P}{E} = \frac{\text{Payout} \times (1 + g)}{K_e - g}, \quad \text{Payout} = 1 - \frac{g}{ROE} \]

This is the trailing P/E form.

  • P/B:

\[ \frac{P}{B} = \frac{ROE - g}{K_e - g} \]

A P/B above 1 is justified only when ROE exceeds Ke.

  • PEG:

\[ PEG = \frac{P/E}{g_{EPS} \times 100} \]

A PEG near 1 is a rule-of-thumb "fair" level. In India, where many quality franchises trade at PEGs of 2–4, the rule of thumb is unreliable.

Computation conventions

  • EV multiples. Use the EV from the c01 recipe and the denominator on the same Ind AS 116 basis and consolidated perimeter.
  • P/E. Use diluted EPS, consolidated, attributable to owners, before exceptional items, from consistent periods (LTM, FY+1, FY+2). Compute it as market cap ÷ PAT where share counts are reliable, which avoids EPS rounding.
  • P/B. Use owners' equity: total equity less NCI, from the latest balance sheet. Some analysts exclude goodwill and intangibles (P/TBV) for financials.
  • PEG. Use FY+1 P/E with a 2–3 year forward EPS CAGR, and state the horizon.
  • Negative or tiny denominators. Mark the result n.m. A P/E above a set cap (for example 100×) is also often marked n.m. (s06).

Applying multiples to the target

\[ EV_{\text{target}} = M_{\text{peer}} \times \text{Metric}_{\text{target}}, \qquad P_{\text{target}} = \frac{EV_{\text{target}} - \text{Net claims}}{N_{\text{diluted}}} \]

For equity multiples:

\[ P_{\text{target}} = (P/E)_{\text{peer}} \times EPS_{\text{target}} \]

Use the same bridge for net claims as in the DCF (topic t4).

Sector conventions in India

Sector Primary multiple(s) Notes
FMCG, paints, consumer durables P/E, EV/EBITDA Premium franchises at 40–60× P/E
IT services P/E, EV/EBIT Cash-rich, so the EV and equity values are close
Cement, steel, metals EV/EBITDA, EV/tonne Cyclicality: use mid-cycle EBITDA
Banks, NBFCs P/B, P/E (adjusted for ROE) EV is not meaningful for lenders
Retail, QSR EV/EBITDA (pre- or post-116, chosen consistently), EV/Sales Lease intensity
Telecom, infrastructure EV/EBITDA High leverage
New-age platforms EV/Sales, EV/Gross order value Losses make P/E n.m.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cash-rich companies. P/E includes treasury income and can appear cheap or dear relative to EV/EBITDA. An ex-cash P/E is \((\text{Market cap} - \text{Cash}) \div (PAT - \text{post-tax other income})\).
  • Different tax regimes. Peers under the old regime with MAT credits, versus Sec 115BAA at 25.17%, distort P/E but not EV/EBITDA.
  • Minority-heavy groups. P/E uses PAT attributable to owners. EV/EBITDA uses consolidated EBITDA with NCI in EV.
  • Goodwill-heavy acquirers. EV/EBIT is depressed by amortisation of acquired intangibles. Consider EV/EBITA.
  • PEG with negative or very low growth is meaningless.

Key takeaways

  • Use EV multiples for firm-level metrics and equity multiples for equity-level metrics. Pick the primary multiple by sector convention.
  • Each multiple has a fundamental driver: growth, RONIC or ROE, and risk. Use these to explain premiums.
  • Compute P/E on diluted, consolidated, owners' PAT before exceptional items. Compute P/B on owners' equity.
  • Mark negative or extreme multiples as n.m. and use several multiples, not one.
  • Apply peer multiples to the target's metrics and run the same bridge to get to price.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute a full multiple set [L1]

A peer has:

  • CMP ₹ 2,450 and diluted shares 4.2 crore
  • borrowings ₹ 1,300 crore, lease liabilities ₹ 220 crore, NCI ₹ 60 crore
  • cash and liquid investments ₹ 900 crore
  • FY27E: revenue ₹ 7,800 crore, EBITDA ₹ 1,250 crore, EBIT ₹ 980 crore, PAT owners ₹ 610 crore
  • owners' equity ₹ 4,400 crore and EPS CAGR of 15%

Compute EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, P/E, P/B and PEG.

Q2. Justified P/B for an NBFC [L2]

An NBFC has ROE of 17%, Ke of 14% and long-run growth of 10%, and trades at a P/B of 2.6×. Compute the justified P/B. Explain the gap, and state what ROE the market is implicitly pricing.

💡 Hint: Rearrange the P/B formula for ROE.

Q3. Which multiple, and why? [L2]

For each of the following, recommend the primary and secondary multiples and justify your choice:

  • a cement company at the bottom of the cycle
  • an IT services company with ₹ 8,000 crore of cash
  • a QSR chain with large lease liabilities
  • a loss-making quick-commerce company
  • a private-sector bank

Q4. Interview: why does Asian Paints trade at 50× P/E? [L3]

Using the P/E fundamental formula, explain what combination of ROE, growth and Ke could justify a 50× trailing P/E for a paints franchise. Discuss what new competition would do to each input, and how comps alone would miss this.
