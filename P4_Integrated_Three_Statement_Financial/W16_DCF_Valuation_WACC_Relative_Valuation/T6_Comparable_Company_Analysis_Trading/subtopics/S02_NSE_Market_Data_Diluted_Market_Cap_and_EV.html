Subtopic note

NSE Market Data, Diluted Market Cap and EV Components

Scope: NSE market data: CMP, diluted shares, market cap; EV components from latest balance sheet

Why it matters

Every comps multiple has a market value in its numerator: market cap for equity multiples, EV for firm multiples. If the price is from a different date, the share count is pre-QIP or pre-bonus, or the debt figure comes from last March, the multiple is wrong before any metric is chosen. In India, data hygiene is complicated by several factors: frequent bonuses and splits, ESOP-heavy new-age companies, QIPs and preferential issues, and a half-yearly (not quarterly) balance-sheet cycle under SEBI LODR. The numerator build is mechanical, but it must be done exactly the same way for every peer and for the target.

Core notes

Price (CMP)

  • Source. The NSE closing price on the comps date: from the NSE website quote, the end-of-day bhavcopy file, or a data terminal. Use one date for all peers and record it.
  • Volatility. For thinly traded or volatile names, consider a 5- or 10-day VWAP and disclose it. The same price date should be used for the CMP line in the football field.
  • Dual listing. Use the NSE price (usually the more liquid exchange). Check that BSE prices are similar.
  • Corporate actions. If a stock has gone ex-bonus or ex-split, the price and share count must both be post-action.

Shares outstanding

Source Timing Notes
Shareholding pattern (SEBI LODR Reg 31) Quarterly, within 21 days of quarter-end Total shares, promoter and public split
Exchange "listed capital" / corporate announcements Event-driven Allotments (ESOP, QIP, preferential, conversion)
Annual report: Ind AS 102 note, SBEB disclosures Annual Options outstanding, weighted-average exercise prices, ranges
Results filings (Ind AS 33 note) Quarterly Weighted-average basic and diluted shares (period-average, not point-in-time)

Diluted shares = basic shares at the comps date − treasury shares (ESOP trust) + TSM shares at CMP + if-converted shares (topic t4, s06). For most old-economy Indian peers dilution is negligible. For new-age companies (with ESOP pools of 5–10%) it is material.

Market capitalisation

\[ \text{Diluted market cap} = \text{CMP} \times N_{\text{diluted}} \]

Exchange-reported market cap uses basic shares. Rebuild it using diluted shares for consistency.

EV components from the latest balance sheet

Use the latest available consolidated balance sheet. For listed companies this is the half-yearly statement of assets and liabilities filed under LODR Reg 33 (30 September) or the annual balance sheet (31 March). Update it for material subsequent events: QIP proceeds, acquisitions, large debt repayments and dividends paid.

EV component Schedule III source Treatment
Borrowings (non-current + current, incl. current maturities) Financial liabilities Add
Lease liabilities (current + non-current) Separately presented Add if using post-116 EBITDA
Preference capital (liability or equity) Borrowings or equity Add (unless CCPS counted in diluted shares)
Non-controlling interests Equity section Add (book, unless a market value is available)
Cash and cash equivalents; unrestricted other bank balances Financial assets Subtract
Current investments (liquid MFs) and treasury non-current investments Financial assets Subtract
Investments in associates/JVs Equity-accounted Subtract (if their income is excluded from EBITDA)
Other non-operating assets (investment property, surplus land) Various Subtract if material and identifiable

This mirrors the DCF bridge (topic t4, c01), with signs reversed.

NCI in comps. Book NCI is the usual practical choice for peers, since fair values are rarely available. Be aware that book understates the claim for profitable subsidiaries. If a peer's subsidiary is separately listed (for example a listed subsidiary of a holding company), use NCI % × the subsidiary's market cap.

Excel structure

Lay the comps sheet out with one row per peer and column blocks for the following:

  1. Identifiers: NSE symbol, price date, FY end, basis flags (consolidated, Ind AS 116 basis).
  2. Market data: CMP, basic shares, options and strike, TSM shares, diluted shares, market cap.
  3. EV build: debt, leases, preference, NCI, cash, investments, associates, EV.
  4. Metrics: LTM, FY+1, FY+2.
  5. Multiples.

Pull CMPs with XLOOKUP from a Power Query staging table of the bhavcopy file:

=XLOOKUP([@Symbol], Bhav[SYMBOL], Bhav[CLOSE], "missing")

Add a data-freshness check:

=IF(MAX(Comps[Price_Date])-MIN(Comps[Price_Date])>0,"Price dates differ","OK")

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Partly paid shares (from rights issues). Count them on a fully paid-up equivalent basis, or add the unpaid calls to cash.
  • Different share classes (DVRs, rare in India). Value each class at its own price.
  • Treasury shares held by subsidiaries or trusts. Exclude them from the basic count.
  • Interim balance-sheet gap. Q1 or Q3 comps dates rely on a balance sheet that is 1–3 months older. Adjust for known large events, and note it.
  • Holding-company peers. The EV may be dominated by listed investments. Subtracting them at market value is essential.
  • Unrestricted vs restricted cash follows the same rules as the bridge (topic t4, s02). Apply them consistently, even with less detail available for peers.

Key takeaways

  • Use one price date for all peers. Use NSE closing prices or VWAP, and record the date.
  • Rebuild diluted shares (TSM at CMP, if-converted) rather than taking the exchange's basic count.
  • Build EV from the latest consolidated balance sheet, updated for material subsequent events.
  • Use the same EV recipe as the DCF bridge, with signs reversed, including the Ind AS 116 basis flag.
  • Automate price pulls with Power Query and add freshness checks.

Practice Questions

Q1. Diluted market cap and EV [L1]

Peer data:

  • CMP ₹ 845; basic shares 24.0 crore, of which 0.30 crore are held by an ESOP trust
  • options 0.80 crore at ₹ 420 and 0.20 crore at ₹ 900
  • borrowings ₹ 1,150 crore; lease liabilities ₹ 430 crore (post-116 basis)
  • book NCI ₹ 75 crore
  • cash ₹ 260 crore, of which ₹ 12 crore is in an unpaid dividend account
  • liquid MFs ₹ 340 crore
  • associate investment ₹ 95 crore, with share of profit below EBITDA

Compute diluted shares, diluted market cap and EV.

Q2. Subsequent events adjustment [L2]

A peer's 30 September 2026 balance sheet shows net debt of ₹ 2,400 crore. On 20 October 2026 it raised ₹ 1,500 crore through a QIP at ₹ 610, issuing 2.46 crore shares, and used ₹ 800 crore to prepay a term loan on 5 November. Your comps date is 14 November 2026. Restate the share count and EV components. Explain what happens to EV and market cap if you ignore these events.

💡 Hint: Track the cash leg and the share leg of the QIP separately.

Q3. Power Query price pipeline [L2]

Describe a Power Query process that imports the NSE bhavcopy for a chosen date, filters to the EQ series, and joins it to a peer list by symbol. The output table should feed the comps sheet. Include how you would parameterise the date and what checks you would add for missing symbols.

Q4. Holding-company peer [L3]

One candidate peer holds listed investments worth ₹ 6,000 crore against a market cap of ₹ 14,000 crore and EBITDA of ₹ 900 crore. Its operating business is comparable to the target. Explain how you would compute a meaningful EV/EBITDA for the operating business. Cover holding discount and tax considerations.
