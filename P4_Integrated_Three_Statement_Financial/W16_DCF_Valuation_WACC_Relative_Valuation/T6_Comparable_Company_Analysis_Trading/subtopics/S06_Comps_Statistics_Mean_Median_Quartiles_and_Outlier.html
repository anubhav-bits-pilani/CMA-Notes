Subtopic note

Comps Statistics: Mean, Median, Quartiles and Outlier Exclusion

Scope: Statistics: mean, median, quartiles; outlier exclusion

Why it matters

With five Indian peers, one outlier can move the average by several turns. That might be a stock at 120× P/E on depressed earnings, or a turnaround with a negative EBITDA. The summary statistics, and the rules used to exclude data points, determine the implied valuation range. They are often more consequential than the choice of peers. Reviewers and clients expect to see the mean, median and quartiles, an explicit outlier policy, and an explanation of why the selected range (usually the interquartile range around the median) is appropriate for the target.

Core notes

The statistics

Statistic Excel Use
Mean =AVERAGE(range) Reference only. Sensitive to outliers
Median =MEDIAN(range) Primary point estimate, robust to outliers
First quartile (Q1, 25th percentile) =QUARTILE.INC(range,1) Low end of the range
Third quartile (Q3, 75th percentile) =QUARTILE.INC(range,3) High end of the range
Min / Max =MIN(), =MAX() Show the full spread
Harmonic mean =HARMEAN(range) Theoretically appropriate for averaging ratios with price in the numerator, because it equal-weights the earnings yield. Lower than the arithmetic mean
Count =COUNT(range) Number of valid data points after exclusions

QUARTILE.INC and QUARTILE.EXC (and PERCENTILE.INC/.EXC) give different results on small samples. Use .INC (the same as the legacy QUARTILE) consistently, and state it.

Ignoring text and errors. These functions ignore text cells such as "n.m.". To ignore error values as well, use AGGREGATE:

Median ignoring errors:  =AGGREGATE(12,6,range)
Q1 ignoring errors:      =AGGREGATE(17,6,range,1)
Q3 ignoring errors:      =AGGREGATE(17,6,range,3)

Function 12 is MEDIAN and function 17 is QUARTILE.INC. Option 6 ignores errors.

Outlier policy

Define rules before looking at the results, and apply them to all peers:

  1. Not meaningful (n.m.): - negative denominators (losses, negative EBITDA) - P/E above a cap, for example 100× (or 75×) - EV/EBITDA above, for example, 60× - EV/Sales above 20×

Display "n.m." and exclude from the statistics. 2. Statistical fences (for larger peer sets of 8 or more). Exclude points outside \([Q1 - 1.5 \times IQR,\; Q3 + 1.5 \times IQR]\), where \(IQR = Q3 - Q1\). 3. Judgemental exclusion. A peer whose current metric is distorted (a strike, a plant fire, a one-off pricing windfall) may be excluded for that period only, for example kept for FY+2 but dropped from LTM. Disclose this in a footnote.

Do not remove a data point just because it is inconvenient. If the target is genuinely closer to the "outlier" peer, the policy must say so.

Suppose instead peer P4 had depressed EBITDA and showed 46.0×. The mean would become 19.1×, while the median would still be 13.9×. This is exactly why the median and quartiles are preferred.

From statistics to an implied range

For Shakti Auto Components (FY27E EBITDA ₹ 1,150 crore, net claims ₹ 1,110 crore, 22.36 crore diluted shares):

Point Multiple Implied EV Equity value Price
Q1 11.3× 12,995 11,885 ₹ 532
Median 13.9× 15,985 14,875 ₹ 665
Q3 14.1× 16,215 15,105 ₹ 676

The narrow Q1–Q3 band here reflects the small sample. In the football field, many practitioners show the Q1–Q3 range, or the median ±1×, and footnote the min and max.

Weighting and regression alternatives

  • Weighted statistics. Weight closer peers more heavily, by judgement or by similarity scores. This is less common and must be documented.
  • Regression (value map). Regress EV/EBITDA on expected EBITDA growth or ROCE across peers, then read the target's implied multiple from its own fundamentals. This is useful when the target differs systematically from the median peer. It is unreliable with five points, so treat it as a cross-check.

Excel layout

Show the statistics rows directly under the peer rows. Add a count row and an exclusions note:

Mean–median gap flag:  =IF(ABS(AVERAGE(r)/MEDIAN(r)-1)>15%,"Mean distorted – use median","OK")
n.m. rule:             =IF(OR(EBITDA<=0, EV/EBITDA>Cap_EVEBITDA),"n.m.",EV/EBITDA)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Very small sets (three peers). The quartiles are nearly meaningless. Present the individual peer values and a judgemental range.
  • Bimodal sets (for example premium franchises at 40× and commodity players at 12×). The median falls in a gap that no peer occupies. Split the set into tiers.
  • Negative EV (cash greater than market cap). The multiple is meaningless, so mark it n.m.
  • Survivorship and time-series. Also show the peer median over 3–5 years, to see whether current multiples are elevated relative to history. This is especially relevant for Indian consumer names.
  • Consistency across periods. The same peers should be valid for LTM, FY+1 and FY+2, or you should explain why the count differs.

Key takeaways

  • Use the median and the IQR as the primary statistics. Show the mean for reference, with a distortion flag.
  • Set the n.m. rules and outlier policy before computing, and apply them uniformly.
  • Use AGGREGATE to compute statistics that ignore errors. Use QUARTILE.INC consistently.
  • Translate Q1, median and Q3 into implied prices through the same bridge as the DCF.
  • Split bimodal peer sets, and show historical median multiples for context.

Practice Questions

Q1. Statistics with an outlier [L1]

The P/E FY27E values for six peers are 22×, 26×, 24×, 31×, 118× and a loss-making peer. Apply a 100× cap and an n.m. rule for losses. Compute the mean, median, Q1 and Q3 (QUARTILE.INC) before and after the exclusions. Comment on the difference.

Q2. IQR fences on a larger set [L2]

For ten EV/EBITDA values (8.5, 9.2, 10.1, 10.8, 11.5, 12.0, 12.6, 13.4, 14.0, 24.5), compute Q1, Q3, the IQR and the 1.5×IQR fences. Identify any outliers. Explain whether you would exclude the high value if that peer is the only other company with the target's niche product.

💡 Hint: The statistical rule and the business judgement can point in different directions. Your answer should address both.

Q3. Implied price range [L2]

Using an EV/EBITDA median of 12.4× and an IQR of 10.9×–13.8×, a target FY27E EBITDA of ₹ 860 crore, net claims of ₹ 1,450 crore and 18.0 crore diluted shares, compute the implied price at Q1, the median and Q3. Write the AGGREGATE formulas you would use on a peer column that contains "n.m." text and #DIV/0! errors.

Q4. Bimodal peer set [L3]

A consumer-durables target's peers split into three premium brands at 45–55× P/E and three value players at 18–24×. The target has premium-brand margins but value-player growth. Explain how you would present the statistics. Decide which tier to anchor on, and describe what supporting analysis you would add.
