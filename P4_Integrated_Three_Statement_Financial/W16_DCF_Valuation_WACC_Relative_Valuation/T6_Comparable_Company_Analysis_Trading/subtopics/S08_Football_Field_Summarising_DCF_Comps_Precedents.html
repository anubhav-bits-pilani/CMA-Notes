Subtopic note

Football Field: Summarising DCF, Comps, Precedents and 52-Week Range

Scope: Football field: DCF ranges, trading comps, precedents, 52-week high/low vs CMP

Why it matters

The football field is the one-page answer to "what is this company worth?". It is a horizontal bar chart in which each bar shows the per-share value range from one methodology. The bars cover DCF (Gordon and exit), trading comps, precedent transactions and the 52-week trading range, with a vertical line at CMP or the offer price. It appears in almost every Indian fairness opinion, board presentation for M&A, IPO pricing discussion and equity research initiation. It forces triangulation: where methods overlap, confidence rises, and where they diverge, the analyst must explain why. It is also the Project 5 deliverable that pulls together every tab you have built.

Core notes

Bars and how to derive each range

Bar Low High Source in the model
DCF: perpetuity growth Min of the Grid 1 inner range (e.g. WACC ±50 bp × g ±50 bp) or the full masked grid Max of the same Topic t5, s02 and c04
DCF: exit multiple Min of the Grid 2 range Max of Grid 2 Topic t5, s03
Trading comps: EV/EBITDA FY+1 Q1 multiple × target EBITDA → price Q3 multiple → price s06
Trading comps: P/E FY+1 Q1 P/E × target EPS Q3 P/E × EPS s04, s06
Precedent transactions Q1 deal EV/EBITDA → price Q3 → price s07
52-week trading range 52-week low (NSE) 52-week high Market data
Analyst target prices (optional) Lowest broker TP Highest TP Consensus
Reference line CMP (dated); offer price, if any

Range width discipline. Ranges should reflect reasonable disagreement, not the extreme corners of every grid. A common convention uses the inner 3×3 of each sensitivity grid, or ±1× multiple steps. State the convention in a footnote.

Consistency. All bars must be in ₹ per diluted share at the same valuation date, use the same bridge (topic t4) and the same Ind AS 116 basis, and be labelled with the metric period (LTM, FY+1).

Interpretation

Typical Indian patterns, and what they suggest:

Pattern Possible explanation
DCF above comps Your forecasts are above consensus; the market discounts governance or execution risk; or the terminal growth or WACC choice is generous
Comps above DCF (consumer franchises) Market multiples embed a long competitive-advantage period that the DCF fades faster; or the DCF uses a conservative ERP
Precedents above comps Control premium and synergies (expected)
CMP below all intrinsic bars Potential undervaluation, or a risk the models omit (litigation, promoter pledge)
52-week range very wide Volatility; event-driven moves; recent re-rating

The analyst then selects a valuation range, usually the overlap zone or a weighted range, and explains the weighting. That might be 50% DCF, 30% comps and 20% precedents for a control-oriented fairness opinion, or DCF-led for an intrinsic research call.

Chart build mechanics

The chart is a stacked bar chart (concept c05).

  1. Create a data table with columns: Method label | Low | High − Low (the "Spread") | High (for labels).
  2. Insert → Bar → Stacked Bar, using the Low and Spread columns.
  3. Format the Low series with no fill and no border, so it becomes invisible and offsets the visible bar to start at Low.
  4. Format the Spread series with a fill colour per method, or one colour with labels.
  5. Reverse the category order on the vertical axis, so the first method appears at the top.
  6. Add data labels. For the Low, show the value at "Inside Base" on the invisible series. For the High, add a label on the Spread series showing the High value via "Value From Cells".
  7. Add the CMP line: - Add a scatter series with X = CMP and Y = 0 and 1, on secondary axes. - Set the secondary Y-axis to 0–1 and hide it. - Format the series as a line with no markers, and label it "CMP ₹ 405 (30-Sep-26)". - Alternatively, use error bars on a single-point scatter.
  8. Set the horizontal axis minimum and maximum (for example ₹ 300–800) as fixed values, or link them via a small VBA routine or by careful rounding. Excel chart axes cannot reference cells natively.

Reading. The intrinsic methods overlap at ₹ 530–600. CMP sits below every intrinsic bar and near the middle of the 52-week range. The suggested valuation range is ₹ 530–600, with a point estimate around ₹ 550 (the DCF base). That implies 31–48% upside. The large gap to CMP needs explaining. Possible causes are the market pricing an EV-transition risk to engine-component revenue, or a consensus EBITDA below the model's. The precedents bar sits highest, consistent with a control premium.

Live linking for Project 5

All range cells should be formulas linked to the grids and the comps statistics, for example =MIN(DCF!J32:L34) on the masked presentation grid. That way the chart updates with the scenario switch after F9 (topic t5, s06). Add the scenario name and valuation date to the chart title via a linked text box (="Valuation summary – "&Scenario_Name).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Too many bars. More than 7–8 bars clutters the chart. Group the secondary methods.
  • Extreme ranges (for example a precedent at 40×). Truncate with an axis break marker, or footnote it rather than letting it compress the chart.
  • Offer context. For a fairness opinion, the offer price line matters more than CMP. Show both.
  • 52-week range distortion from a bonus or split. Adjust the historical prices.
  • Blended point estimate. Show it as a marker, not as another bar.
  • Colour meaning. Use one colour family for intrinsic methods and another for market-based methods, to aid reading.

Key takeaways

  • Each bar is a ₹-per-share range from one method, on the same date, bridge and basis.
  • Derive ranges from inner sensitivity grids and peer quartiles. Avoid extreme corners.
  • Build the chart as a stacked bar with an invisible Low series, a visible Spread series and a scatter-based CMP line.
  • Interpret overlaps and divergences explicitly, and select a range with a stated weighting.
  • Link all range cells live to the grids and comps so the chart follows the scenario switch.

Practice Questions

Q1. Chart data block [L1]

Given the method ranges DCF Gordon ₹ 610–820, DCF exit ₹ 640–760, comps EV/EBITDA ₹ 580–700, comps P/E ₹ 560–720, precedents ₹ 700–880 and 52-week ₹ 515–690, with CMP ₹ 655, build the chart data block (Low, Spread, High) and list the formatting steps for each series.

Q2. Triangulation memo [L3]

For a consumer-staples company:

  • DCF ₹ 1,850–2,300
  • trading comps (P/E FY+1 Q1–Q3) ₹ 2,600–3,200
  • precedents ₹ 2,900–3,500
  • 52-week range ₹ 2,450–3,050
  • CMP ₹ 2,900

Write a 250-word memo explaining the divergences and recommending a valuation range for a research report. Explain what additional analysis (reverse DCF, competitive advantage period) you would present.

💡 Hint: Consider what the market's multiple implies about the length of excess-return periods compared with your DCF fade.

Q3. Range-width convention [L2]

A junior analyst uses the full 5×5 Grid 1 (WACC ±100 bp × g ±100 bp) for the DCF bar, which produces ₹ 423–790. Propose two alternative conventions: one that flexes WACC ±100 bp at base g only, and one that flexes g ±50 bp at base WACC only. Compute each range from the Shakti Grid 1 values in topic t5, s02. Compare both with the inner 3×3 range used above, and recommend one.

Q4. Live chart and scenario switch [L2]

Describe how you would ensure the football field updates correctly when the Project 4 scenario switch moves from Base to Bear. Cover the range-cell formulas, the calculation mode, the chart title, axis limits and a check that the DCF bar contains the base-case price.
