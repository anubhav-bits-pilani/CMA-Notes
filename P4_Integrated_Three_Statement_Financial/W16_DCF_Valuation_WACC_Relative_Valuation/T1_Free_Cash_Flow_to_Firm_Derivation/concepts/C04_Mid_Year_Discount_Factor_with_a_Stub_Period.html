Key concept

Mid-Year Discount Factor with a Stub Period

Concept: Mid-year discount factor = 1 ÷ (1 + WACC)^(t − 0.5); with stub fraction s: exponents s/2, s + 0.5, s + 1.5…

Statement

Without a stub (valuation date = fiscal year-end), the discount factor for year \( t = 1, 2, \dots, n \) is:

\[ \text{DF}_t = \frac{1}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{t - 0.5}} \]

With a stub fraction \( s \) (time from the valuation date to the next 31 March, in years), the stub cash flow and subsequent full-year flows are discounted at:

\[ \text{DF}_{\text{stub}} = \frac{1}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{s/2}}, \quad \text{DF}_{k} = \frac{1}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{s + k - 0.5}}, \quad k = 1, 2, \dots \]

giving exponents \( s/2,\; s+0.5,\; s+1.5,\; s+2.5, \dots \)

Explanation

The mid-year convention assumes cash flows accrue evenly through each period, so their average timing is the midpoint. For a full year that is half a year before year-end; for a stub of length \( s \), it is \( s/2 \) after the valuation date. Each subsequent full year's midpoint is \( s \) (to the first 31 March) plus the elapsed full years less half a year.

Why it matters quantitatively: the mid-year convention raises PV of explicit flows by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} - 1 \), about 5.4% at 11% WACC and 5.8% at 12%. For a company where explicit flows are 35% of EV, the impact on EV is about 2%; the larger effect comes from terminal value treatment (t3/s07).

Conditions and exceptions:

  • Use mid-year for operating businesses with even cash generation; use year-end for lumpy annual flows (annual dividends, concession fees, bullet receipts).
  • For heavily seasonal businesses, use quarterly flows with quarter-mid exponents or at least actual-based stubs.
  • The stub cash flow must be only the portion arising after the valuation date (pro-rated or actual-based; see t1/s05).
  • The exponent is time in years, so \( s \) must be expressed as days ÷ 365 (or YEARFRAC), not months ÷ 12 rounded.

Illustration

Valuation date 31 December 2025: \( s = 90/365 = 0.2466 \). WACC 12%.

Period Exponent DF
FY26 stub (Jan–Mar 2026) 0.1233 0.9861
FY27 0.7466 0.9189
FY28 1.7466 0.8204
FY29 2.7466 0.7325
FY30 3.7466 0.6540

Check the FY27 factor: \( 1.12^{0.7466} = e^{0.7466 \times 0.11333} = e^{0.08461} = 1.0883 \); \( 1/1.0883 = 0.9189 \).

A common error is to discount FY27 at 1.5 (mid-year counted from 31 March 2025) — this understates its PV by \( 1.12^{0.7534} - 1 \approx 8.9\% \).

Connections

  • t3/s07 and t3/c05: terminal value discounting under mid-year — the TV exponent is not simply the last-year exponent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Exponent table [L1]

Valuation date 30 June 2025, WACC 11%. Compute the stub fraction (actual days/365) and mid-year discount factors for FY26 (stub) through FY30.

Q2. Convention impact [L2]

Explicit FCFF (₹ crore) FY26–FY30: 300, 340, 385, 430, 470; valuation date 31 March 2025; WACC 12.5%. Compute the PV of explicit flows under year-end and mid-year conventions and the percentage difference.

💡 Hint: The ratio of the two PVs should be close to a single closed-form factor; check your answer against it.

Q3. Debug the exponent [L2]

A model uses =1/(1+WACC)^(COLUMN()-5-0.5) for discount factors with a 30 September valuation date. Explain what goes wrong for each column and rewrite the formula using dates.
