Key concept

Reinvestment Rate, ROIC and Fundamental Growth

Concept: Reinvestment rate = (Capex − D&A + ΔNWC) ÷ NOPAT; Growth = Reinvestment rate × ROIC

Statement

\[ \text{RR} = \frac{\text{Capex} - \text{D\&A} + \Delta\text{NWC}}{\text{NOPAT}} \]

\[ g = \text{RR} \times \text{ROIC} \]

where RR is the reinvestment rate (share of NOPAT ploughed back into operating capital), Capex − D&A is net capital expenditure, ΔNWC the increase in operating working capital, NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − t), \( g \) the growth rate of NOPAT (and, in steady state, of FCFF), and ROIC the return on invested capital earned on the new investment (strictly RONIC; equal to ROIC when returns are stable).

Corollary: \( \text{FCFF} = \text{NOPAT} \times (1 - \text{RR}) = \text{NOPAT} \times \left(1 - \frac{g}{\text{ROIC}}\right) \).

Explanation

Growth is not free. A business grows its operating profit only by investing in more capacity, stores, inventory or receivables. If each rupee of net investment earns ROIC, then reinvesting RR × NOPAT this year adds RR × NOPAT × ROIC to next year's NOPAT — a growth rate of RR × ROIC.

This identity is the discipline check for every DCF:

  • Explicit period: compute implied RR and ROIC each year. If revenue grows 15% while RR is 10% and ROIC 18%, implied growth is only 1.8% — the forecast is under-investing and growth is unfunded.
  • Terminal year: pick g and RONIC, and the required RR follows: \( \text{RR} = g / \text{RONIC} \). At g = 5.5% and RONIC = 12%, RR must be 45.8%. Leaving RR at 10% while assuming 5.5% growth implies RONIC of 55% forever — an implausible moat.
  • Value creation: growth adds value only if ROIC > WACC. When RONIC = WACC, the terminal value equals \( \text{NOPAT}_{n+1} / \text{WACC} \) regardless of g (t3/c02).

Measurement notes:

  • Invested capital = net PPE + CWIP + intangibles (+ ROU assets post-116) + operating NWC; exclude cash, investments and goodwill if measuring organic returns (include goodwill to measure returns on acquisitions).
  • Use opening or average invested capital consistently.
  • Lumpy capex makes annual RR volatile; evaluate multi-year averages.
  • Post-116, ROU additions belong in reinvestment and ROU assets in invested capital.

Illustration

FY30E (₹ crore): NOPAT 620; capex 410; D&A 250; ΔNWC 55. Invested capital at 31-Mar-29: 4,100.

\[ \text{RR} = \frac{410 - 250 + 55}{620} = \frac{215}{620} = 34.7\% \]

\[ \text{ROIC} = \frac{620}{4{,}100} = 15.1\%; \quad g = 34.7\% \times 15.1\% = 5.2\% \]

If the same forecast shows FY31 revenue growth of 12%, the model is inconsistent: either reinvestment must rise (to ~79% of NOPAT at 15.1% ROIC) or the assumed growth must fall.

Connections

  • t1/c01: the numerator terms are the same lines as in FCFF.
  • t1/s04: fade period — RR and ROIC converge toward terminal levels.
  • t3/s05 and t3/s06: terminal normalisation and the value-driver formula use this identity.
  • Phase 2 (W5–8): ROIC, DuPont and competitive advantage period analysis.
  • FP&A: capex budgeting committees use the same logic — "what growth does this capex buy, at what return?"

Practice Questions

Q1. Implied growth [L1]

NOPAT ₹1,200 crore; capex ₹780 crore; D&A ₹460 crore; ΔNWC ₹100 crore; opening invested capital ₹8,000 crore. Compute RR, ROIC and implied growth.

Q2. Terminal consistency [L2]

Terminal g = 6%, WACC = 11.5%, RONIC options: 11.5%, 15% and 25%. Terminal NOPAT (year n+1) = ₹900 crore. For each RONIC, compute the required RR and terminal FCFF, and comment on how value changes with RONIC.

💡 Hint: FCFF = NOPAT × (1 − g ÷ RONIC).

Q3. Model review [L3]

A DCF for a listed QSR chain forecasts 18% revenue growth through FY35, while capex equals D&A from FY28 onwards and NWC is negative and constant. Using the reinvestment identity, explain the inconsistency, estimate what ROIC the forecast implicitly assumes, and propose a fix.
