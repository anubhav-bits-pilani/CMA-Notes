Key concept

FCFF Cross-Check from PAT

Concept: Cross-check: FCFF = PAT + Interest × (1 − t) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC (+ other non-cash items)

Statement

\[ \text{FCFF} = \text{PAT} + \text{Interest} \times (1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC} \; (+\text{ other non-cash items}) \; (- \text{ non-operating income} \times (1-t)) \]

where PAT is profit after tax attributable to the entity being valued, Interest is finance cost (incl. lease interest in a post-116 model), \( t \) the marginal rate, and "other non-cash items" are charges within PAT that do not consume cash and are not separately reinvested (e.g. non-cash provisions, unrealised operating forex losses). The last bracket removes after-tax non-operating income when the related assets are added in the bridge.

Explanation

The FCFF from EBIT (c01) is a top-down build. This cross-check is bottom-up: start from what the model already prints as PAT and undo financing and non-cash effects. Adding back \( \text{Interest} \times (1 - t) \) — not gross interest — reverses both the interest charge and the tax saving it generated, putting the cash flow on an unlevered basis.

Why the cross-check matters in a three-statement model:

  • It tests the plumbing. If the FCFF from EBIT and from PAT differ, something in the P&L (other income, exceptional items, tax computation, associate share) has not been handled consistently.
  • It reconciles to the CFS. A close relative is \( \text{FCFF} \approx \text{CFO} + \text{Interest paid} \times (1 - t) - \text{Capex} \), adjusted for items classified differently (interest received, dividends received, taxes on non-operating income).
  • It forces explicit choices about non-cash items. Ind AS 102 ESOP expense is the classic case: it reduces PAT but involves no cash. If you add it back, the dilution must be captured in the diluted share count (t4); if you do not, the expense is treated as a cash-equivalent cost. Analysts must not add it back and ignore dilution.

Reconciling items between the two routes when tax is computed on PBT in the model:

Item Effect
Other income (treasury) In PAT, not in operating EBIT → subtract after tax
Exceptional items In PAT, excluded from EBIT → reverse after tax
Share of associate profit In consolidated PAT, excluded → subtract
Deferred tax movements P&L tax ≠ marginal tax on EBIT → difference
Tax rate difference (ETR vs 25.17%) Creates a gap unless both routes use the same tax basis

The two routes agree exactly only when tax in PAT equals \( t \times \text{PBT} \) and the non-operating items are removed after tax.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): EBIT 612; other income 48; interest 40; PBT = 620; tax at 25.17% = 156.1; PAT = 463.9; D&A 144; capex 400; ΔNWC 66.

\[ \text{FCFF} = 463.9 + 40 \times 0.7483 - 48 \times 0.7483 + 144 - 400 - 66 \]

\[ = 463.9 + 29.9 - 35.9 + 144 - 400 - 66 = 136.0 \]

This matches c01's FCFF of ₹136.0 crore. Had the analyst added back gross interest (40) instead of after-tax (29.9) and kept other income, FCFF would be ₹182.0 crore — 34% too high.

Connections

  • c01 is the primary build; this concept is the check row on the DCF sheet (Checks! tab).
  • t1/s01 (other income exclusion) and t1/s06 (unlevered framing) explain why interest is added after tax and non-operating income removed.
  • t4 bridge: ESOP add-back must be matched by treasury-stock-method dilution; associates added at value.

Practice Questions

Q1. PAT-route FCFF [L1]

FY28E (₹ crore): PAT 820; finance cost 210 (of which lease interest 60); other income 70 (interest on FDs); tax rate 25.17%; D&A 400 (incl. ROU 120); capex 550; ROU additions 140; ΔNWC 85. The model is post-116. Compute FCFF via the PAT route.

Q2. Reconciliation break [L2]

Your FCFF from EBIT is ₹1,040 crore but the PAT-route check gives ₹1,112 crore. The P&L shows an exceptional gain of ₹60 crore (taxed at 25.17%), a deferred tax credit of ₹27 crore, and ESOP expense of ₹30 crore. Identify which items explain the break and whether the residual difference is acceptable.

💡 Hint: Reverse each item on an after-tax basis and see what remains unexplained.

Q3. ESOP judgement [L3]

A new-age platform company has ESOP expense of 9% of revenue. Analyst A adds it back to FCFF and uses basic shares; Analyst B does not add it back and uses diluted shares including all outstanding options. Evaluate both approaches and recommend a consistent treatment.
