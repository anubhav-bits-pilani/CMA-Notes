Key concept

Post-Ind AS 116 FCFF: Lease Payments, ROU Additions and the Bridge

Concept: Post-116: FCFF excludes lease payments, new ROU additions treated as reinvestment, lease liabilities deducted in bridge

Statement

On a post-Ind AS 116 basis, leases are treated as debt-financed asset acquisitions:

\[ \text{FCFF}_{\text{post}} = \text{EBIT}_{\text{post}}(1-t) + \text{D\&A}_{\text{PPE}} + \text{Dep}_{\text{ROU}} - \text{Capex} - \text{ROU additions} - \Delta\text{NWC} \]

\[ \text{Equity value} = \text{EV}_{\text{post}} + \text{Cash \& non-operating assets} - \text{Borrowings} - \text{Lease liabilities} - \text{Other claims} \]

where \( \text{EBIT}_{\text{post}} \) is after ROU depreciation and before lease interest; lease payments (principal and interest) are financing flows excluded from FCFF; ROU additions are the ROU assets recognised on new leases and remeasurements in the year; lease liabilities are measured at the valuation date (current + non-current).

Explanation

Three linked rules make post-116 FCFF internally consistent:

  1. Exclude lease payments. Under Ind AS 116 the lessee's cash rent splits into interest (finance cost) and principal (reduction of liability). Neither is an operating cash flow for FCFF purposes, exactly like interest and repayment of a term loan.
  2. Treat new ROU additions as reinvestment. Signing a 9-year lease for a new store is economically equivalent to borrowing the PV of rent and buying the right to use the store. The asset purchase must be deducted as reinvestment; otherwise the model captures the store's revenue and EBITDA without any cost of acquiring it.
  3. Deduct lease liabilities in the bridge (and include them in D for WACC). Existing leases' future payments are claims on the operating cash flows, just like debt.

If any rule is omitted, value is overstated. Omitting rule 2 is the most common error in Indian retail coverage because ROU additions are a non-cash item on the CFS (disclosed only in the Ind AS 116 note as "additions to right-of-use assets").

Consistency with WACC: with leases in D, WACC typically falls slightly (lease debt is cheaper than equity), and the after-tax cost of lease debt is the incremental borrowing rate × (1 − t). Comps (t6) must use post-116 EV (including leases) with post-116 EBITDA.

Terminal year: ROU additions should be at least ROU depreciation (adjusted for growth and inflation), mirroring capex ≥ D&A — stores must be renewed.

Illustration

A value retailer FY28E (₹ crore): EBITDA post-116 = 820; PPE D&A 90; ROU depreciation 150; capex 140; ROU additions on 45 new stores and renewals 260; ΔNWC 25; tax 25.17%. Lease liabilities at valuation date ₹1,150 crore; borrowings ₹200 crore; cash ₹300 crore.

\[ \text{EBIT} = 820 - 90 - 150 = 580; \quad \text{NOPAT} = 580 \times 0.7483 = 434.0 \]

\[ \text{FCFF} = 434.0 + 90 + 150 - 140 - 260 - 25 = 249.0 \]

Without the ROU additions FCFF would be ₹509.0 crore — more than double. In the bridge, deducting ₹1,150 crore of leases with ₹200 crore of borrowings means net debt-like claims of ₹1,050 crore, not the ₹100 crore net cash shown by a pre-116 reading of the balance sheet.

Practice Questions

Q1. Post-116 FCFF [L1]

FY27E (₹ crore): EBITDA (post-116) 1,640; PPE D&A 210; ROU depreciation 330; capex 260; ROU additions 410; lease payments 420; ΔNWC 30; tax 25.17%. Compute FCFF and state which item(s) must not be deducted.

Q2. Bridge error quantification [L2]

An analyst's post-116 DCF of a QSR chain uses FCFF that deducts lease payments of ₹380 crore per year and deducts lease liabilities of ₹2,200 crore in the bridge. Explain the double count and estimate the effect on equity value if lease payments are expected to grow at 8% per year and WACC is 11.5% over a 10-year horizon plus terminal value at 6% growth.

💡 Hint: The lease payments stream already reflects both existing and future leases; consider what each deduction represents.

Q3. Sourcing ROU additions [L1]

Explain where in an Indian listed company's annual report you would find historical ROU additions and lease cash outflows, and how you would forecast ROU additions from a store roll-out plan.
