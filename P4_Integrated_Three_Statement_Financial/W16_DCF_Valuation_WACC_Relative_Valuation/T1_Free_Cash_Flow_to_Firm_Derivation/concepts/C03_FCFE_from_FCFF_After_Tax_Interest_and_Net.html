Key concept

FCFE from FCFF: After-Tax Interest and Net Borrowing

Concept: FCFE = FCFF − Interest × (1 − t) + Net borrowing

Statement

\[ \text{FCFE}_t = \text{FCFF}_t - \text{Interest}_t \times (1 - t) + (\text{New debt}_t - \text{Debt repaid}_t) \]

where \( \text{Interest}_t \) is finance cost on borrowings (and on lease liabilities in a post-116 model, in which case lease principal repayments are part of "debt repaid" and ROU additions are part of "new debt"), \( t \) is the marginal tax rate, and net borrowing is the change in debt principal. For FCFE to ordinary shareholders, also deduct preference dividends and preference redemptions, and add fresh preference issues.

Explanation

FCFF belongs to all capital providers. To isolate the equity holders' share, remove what lenders take and add what lenders give:

  • Interest × (1 − t): lenders receive interest; the company saves tax on it, so the net cost to equity is after-tax interest. In FCFF, the tax shield was excluded; FCFE brings it back into the cash flow — which is why FCFE is discounted at Ke, not WACC (no shield in the rate).
  • Net borrowing: new debt is cash in for equity; repayments are cash out. A company that borrows to fund capex shows higher FCFE in that year; one that deleverages shows lower FCFE.

An equivalent bottom-up form:

\[ \text{FCFE} = \text{PAT} + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC} + \text{Net borrowing} \]

(valid when PAT includes only operating items plus interest; adjust for non-operating income and non-cash items as in c02).

Key conditions:

  • Debt plan dependence. FCFE is only as good as the debt schedule. Unrealistic borrowing inflates FCFE; a valuation can be manipulated by assuming more debt each year. Test leverage ratios (net debt/EBITDA, ICR) in each forecast year.
  • Ke must match leverage. If the debt plan changes D/E materially, Ke should change via relevered beta.
  • Terminal FCFE. In steady state, net borrowing = \( g \times \text{Debt}_n \) if leverage is held constant — not zero and not the explicit-period average.
  • Financial institutions. For banks/NBFCs, FCFE is reframed as PAT minus the equity needed to support asset growth at the target capital ratio.

Illustration

Continuing c01 (₹ crore): FCFF FY27E = 136.0; interest 40; new term loan 250; repayments 90; tax 25.17%.

\[ \text{FCFE} = 136.0 - 40 \times 0.7483 + (250 - 90) = 136.0 - 29.9 + 160 = 266.1 \]

FCFE exceeds FCFF because the company is borrowing ₹160 crore net to fund expansion. In FY30, when repayments of ₹220 crore occur without new borrowing, FCFE will be lower than FCFF. This volatility is why FCFE valuations of industrial companies need careful debt-plan review.

Practice Questions

Q1. FCFE computation with preference shares [L1]

FY27E (₹ crore): FCFF 520; interest on borrowings 95; tax 25.17%; new borrowings 180; repayments 260; preference dividend 18 (not tax-deductible); preference redemption 50. Compute FCFE to ordinary shareholders.

Q2. Terminal FCFE [L2]

In the terminal year, FCFF is ₹900 crore, debt is ₹3,000 crore at an 8.5% pre-tax cost, the company maintains a constant D/E, and perpetual growth is 5.5%. Compute terminal-year FCFE and explain why net borrowing is not zero.

💡 Hint: Constant leverage means debt grows at the same rate as the firm.

Q3. Manipulation test [L3]

An analyst's FCFE valuation of a cement company rises by 12% when the debt schedule is changed to assume net debt/EBITDA climbs from 1.5× to 3.0× over five years, while Ke is held at 14%. Explain why the value increases, why this is a flawed result, and the two corrections required.
