Subtopic note

Ind AS 116 Consistency: Post-116 vs Pre-116 FCFF

Scope: Ind AS 116 consistency: post-116 (lease liability as debt) vs pre-116 (deduct lease rentals) FCFF

Why it matters

Since 1 April 2019, Ind AS 116 has moved almost all lessee leases onto the balance sheet: a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a lease liability replace the old operating-lease rent line. For lease-heavy sectors — apparel and grocery retail (Trent, DMart, ABFRL, V-Mart, Shoppers Stop), quick-service restaurants (Jubilant FoodWorks, Devyani, Sapphire), multiplexes (PVR INOX), hospitals, airlines (InterGlobe) and warehousing — the effect on EBITDA is large: rent of 8–15% of revenue disappears from opex and reappears as depreciation and interest. A DCF can be built on either basis, and both should give approximately the same equity value. Mixing them — post-116 EBITDA with pre-116 debt, or vice versa — routinely produces 20–40% valuation errors for such companies. The choice also has to be mirrored in WACC weights (t2), the EV bridge (t4) and trading comps (t6).

Core notes

What Ind AS 116 changes in the P&L and CFS

For a lessee (Ind AS 116, lessee model; short-term ≤ 12 months and low-value leases may be expensed under the recognition exemptions):

Item Pre-116 (Ind AS 17 operating lease) Post-116
Rent expense in other expenses Full rent Only short-term/low-value/variable rent
EBITDA Lower Higher by fixed rent
Depreciation PPE only PPE + ROU depreciation
Finance cost Borrowings only + interest on lease liability
CFO After rent Before rent (interest may sit in CFO or CFF by policy; principal in CFF)
Debt Borrowings Borrowings + lease liabilities (current and non-current, Schedule III requires separate presentation)

Over the life of a lease, total P&L charge is the same, but post-116 is front-loaded (interest is higher early).

Approach A — post-116 FCFF (lease liability as debt)

Treat leases as a financing of the ROU asset. Then:

  1. EBIT is after ROU depreciation and before lease interest. Lease interest is a financing cost, so it is excluded like any other interest.
  2. D&A add-back includes ROU depreciation.
  3. Capex must include ROU additions — new leases signed (store openings, renewals, remeasurements). This is the step most often missed. A lease is economically a debt-financed asset purchase; if the "purchase" is not deducted as reinvestment, the model enjoys the growth from new stores without paying for them.
  4. Lease payments are not deducted in FCFF (both principal and interest are financing flows).
  5. Bridge: lease liabilities at the valuation date are deducted from EV as debt-like claims.
  6. WACC: lease liabilities are included in D, and the cost of lease debt (incremental borrowing rate) enters \( K_d \).

\[ \text{FCFF}_{\text{post-116}} = \text{EBIT}_{\text{post}} (1-t) + \text{D\&A (incl. ROU)} - \text{Capex} - \text{ROU additions} - \Delta\text{NWC} \]

Approach B — pre-116 FCFF (deduct lease rentals)

Reverse Ind AS 116 and treat rent as an operating expense:

  1. EBITDA pre-116 = reported EBITDA − fixed lease payments (principal + interest, i.e. the cash rent).
  2. D&A excludes ROU depreciation; EBIT pre-116 = EBITDA pre-116 − PPE D&A.
  3. No ROU additions as capex — new stores' rent is captured in future rent expense.
  4. Bridge: lease liabilities are not deducted.
  5. WACC: D excludes leases.

\[ \text{FCFF}_{\text{pre-116}} = (\text{EBITDA}_{\text{post}} - \text{Lease rentals} - \text{D\&A}_{\text{PPE}})(1-t) + \text{D\&A}_{\text{PPE}} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC} \]

Which to choose

Criterion Post-116 Pre-116
Matches reported numbers and consensus EBITDA Yes Needs adjustment
Data needed ROU additions forecast, lease liability at valuation date Lease rental forecast (from Ind AS 116 maturity analysis and cash-flow note)
Comparability with global peers (IFRS 16) Good Good with US GAAP operating-lease presentation
Sensitivity to lease discount rate Yes (liability and WACC) No
Practitioner preference in Indian retail coverage Common in sell-side for EV/EBITDA Many analysts show "pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA" for operating comparison

Either is acceptable if applied consistently through FCFF, WACC, bridge and comps. Many valuers prefer pre-116 for its simplicity and because leases are typically renewed perpetually (rent is a recurring operating cost), and present post-116 as a cross-check.

Why the two approaches don't match exactly

They converge if (i) the lease discount rate equals the pre-tax cost of debt implicit in WACC, (ii) ROU additions in post-116 equal the PV of new leases, and (iii) the tax treatment is consistent. Differences arise because: lease interest rates (incremental borrowing rate, IBR) differ from WACC's Kd; adding leases to D changes the WACC weights; and the tax deduction under the Income-tax Act is on the rent paid (Indian tax law does not follow Ind AS 116), creating deferred tax. A gap of 2–5% is normal; larger gaps indicate an error.

Worked illustration

Apparel retailer FY27E (₹ crore): reported (post-116) EBITDA 1,200; PPE D&A 180; ROU depreciation 260; lease interest 110; fixed lease payments 340; new ROU additions 420; capex 300; ΔNWC 60; tax 25.17%.

Post-116:

\[ \text{EBIT} = 1{,}200 - 180 - 260 = 760;\quad \text{FCFF} = 760 \times 0.7483 + 440 - 300 - 420 - 60 = 228.7 \]

Pre-116:

\[ \text{EBIT} = 1{,}200 - 340 - 180 = 680;\quad \text{FCFF} = 680 \times 0.7483 + 180 - 300 - 60 = 328.8 \]

Pre-116 FCFF is higher in this year because ROU additions (420) exceed the rent (340) — the company is expanding its store base. But post-116 EV will be compared against a bridge that deducts lease liabilities, and its WACC will be slightly lower. The omission of the ₹420 crore ROU additions would have overstated post-116 FCFF to ₹648.7 crore — almost double.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Variable rent (revenue-share leases common in malls) is not capitalised under Ind AS 116; it stays in opex under both approaches.
  • Short-term and low-value leases stay in opex; ensure the pre-116 adjustment deducts only payments relating to capitalised leases.
  • Lease modifications and remeasurement (rent escalations, extensions) increase ROU assets without cash — treat as ROU additions in post-116 forecasts.
  • Lessor side. For lessors (e.g. REITs, warehousing owners), lease income is operating revenue — do not apply this adjustment.
  • Sale-and-leaseback transactions generate a cash inflow and a lease liability; treat as financing in post-116 FCFF.
  • Terminal year: under post-116, ROU additions in perpetuity should approximate ROU depreciation × (1 + growth adjustment) — the lease analogue of capex ≥ D&A.

Key takeaways

  • Post-116: exclude lease payments, add back ROU depreciation, deduct ROU additions as reinvestment, deduct lease liabilities in the bridge and include them in WACC debt.
  • Pre-116: deduct full lease rentals from EBITDA, exclude ROU depreciation and additions, do not deduct lease liabilities, exclude leases from WACC.
  • Both approaches should converge within a few percent; large gaps signal inconsistency.
  • Forgetting ROU additions is the costliest post-116 error for expanding retailers.
  • Document the basis on the DCF cover and mirror it in comps EV and EBITDA.

Practice Questions

Q1. Converting reported EBITDA [L1]

A QSR chain reports FY25 EBITDA of ₹1,050 crore, depreciation of ₹520 crore (of which ROU ₹310 crore), finance cost of ₹230 crore (of which lease interest ₹180 crore). Cash outflow for lease liabilities (principal + interest) is ₹470 crore. Compute pre-116 EBITDA and pre-116 EBIT, and state which bridge items change between the two bases.

Q2. Post-116 FCFF with store expansion [L2]

A grocery retailer plans 60 new stores per year, each with a lease liability at inception of ₹18 crore, and capex of ₹12 crore per store. FY27E post-116 EBITDA is ₹2,400 crore, PPE D&A ₹300 crore, ROU depreciation ₹380 crore, ΔNWC ₹40 crore, tax 25.17%, existing-store maintenance capex ₹150 crore. Compute post-116 FCFF and explain the effect of omitting ROU additions.

💡 Hint: ROU additions = number of new stores × lease liability at inception (ignore initial direct costs).

Q3. Reconciling the two approaches [L3]

Your post-116 DCF of a multiplex operator gives equity value ₹14,200 crore and your pre-116 DCF gives ₹11,600 crore. List the possible causes of a gap of this size, the checks you would perform in order, and which result you would present to an investment committee pending resolution.

Q4. Interview: why not just use EBITDA? [L1]

An interviewer asks: "Ind AS 116 increased Trent's EBITDA — did it increase Trent's value?" Give a structured two-minute answer covering FCFF, bridge and multiples.

Q5. Tax treatment mismatch [L2]

Under the Income-tax Act, a lessee deducts rent paid, not ROU depreciation and lease interest. For a lease with annual rent ₹100 crore, ROU depreciation ₹80 crore and interest ₹35 crore in year 1, explain the deferred tax consequence and how you would compute operating taxes in a post-116 FCFF for that year.
