Subtopic note

D&A Add-Back, Capex Split and ΔNWC Linked from Schedules

Scope: D&A add-back, maintenance + growth capex, ΔNWC linked from schedules

Why it matters

NOPAT is an accounting profit; FCFF is cash. The three reinvestment lines — D&A add-back, capital expenditure and the change in net working capital — convert one into the other. In most Indian manufacturing valuations these lines together absorb 30–70% of NOPAT, so they drive value as much as margins do. They are also where integrated models most often break: D&A typed into the DCF sheet instead of linked from the fixed-asset schedule, capex that does not match the PPE corkscrew, or ΔNWC with the wrong sign. A reviewer will test that every one of these lines is a same-column link to the Project 4 schedules (Weeks 14–15) and that the FCFF reconciles to the cash flow statement.

Core notes

D&A add-back

Depreciation (Ind AS 16), amortisation of intangibles (Ind AS 38) and — post-Ind AS 116 — depreciation of right-of-use assets are deducted to arrive at EBIT but involve no cash outflow in the period. They are added back:

\[ \text{FCFF} = \text{NOPAT} + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC} \]

Points of care:

  • Link, don't recompute. D&A on the DCF sheet must equal the D&A row on the P&L, which must equal the depreciation row in the PPE/intangibles corkscrew. A three-way identity check (=ABS(DCF!F14-IS!F17)<0.01) belongs on the checks sheet.
  • Amortisation of acquired intangibles (customer relationships, brands recognised in a PPA under Ind AS 103) is non-cash and added back; but the tax effect depends on whether it is tax-deductible. Goodwill amortisation is not tax-deductible post Finance Act 2021 amendments to Sec 32.
  • Impairment losses (Ind AS 36) are non-cash too but are usually treated as exceptional and excluded from EBIT, so no add-back is needed in the forecast.
  • Other non-cash items inside EBIT (ESOP expense, unrealised forex on operating items, provisions not utilised) are discussed in c02; in the core FCFF build they remain inside NOPAT unless the analyst explicitly adds them back with a corresponding dilution/ liability treatment.

Capex: maintenance vs growth

Capital expenditure is additions to PPE, CWIP and intangibles (including intangible assets under development) in the year — on the CFS, "Purchase of property, plant and equipment" net of proceeds from disposals if disposals are recurring.

Splitting capex into two components is essential for forecasting discipline and terminal-year logic:

Component Purpose Typical driver Steady-state level
Maintenance capex Keep existing capacity productive (replacements, overhauls, IT refresh) % of opening gross block, or ≈ D&A adjusted for inflation ≥ D&A in nominal terms because replacement cost exceeds historical cost
Growth (expansion) capex Add capacity: new lines, plants, stores, brownfield/greenfield Capacity plan × capex per unit (₹ crore per MTPA, ₹ lakh per store, ₹ per sq ft) Linked to terminal growth via reinvestment rate

Indian disclosures rarely split capex; sources include management commentary in earnings calls, DRHP/investor presentations (e.g. "₹1,200 crore greenfield, commissioning Q3 FY27"), and industry benchmarks such as cement capex of roughly ₹700–1,000 crore per MTPA for greenfield integrated plants (date-sensitive estimate). A common proxy: maintenance capex ≈ D&A × (1 + inflation adjustment) in mature years; growth capex = Δcapacity × cost per unit.

CWIP timing. Capex is a cash outflow when spent, not when capitalised. If the fixed-asset schedule models CWIP and transfers to gross block on commissioning, FCFF must pick up the cash spend (additions to CWIP + direct additions), not the capitalisation. Depreciation starts only after capitalisation, so a big CWIP build compresses FCFF before EBIT benefits.

Capital creditors and advances. Capex on the CFS is a cash number — it is adjusted for movements in capital creditors and capital advances. If the model's PPE corkscrew uses accrual additions, either model capital creditors separately or accept the approximation and note it.

ΔNWC linked from the working-capital schedule

Net (operating) working capital is operating current assets less operating current liabilities:

\[ \text{NWC} = \text{Trade receivables} + \text{Inventories} + \text{Other operating CA} - \text{Trade payables} - \text{Other operating CL} \]

Exclude cash, current investments, current maturities of borrowings, current lease liabilities, interest accrued, dividend payable and income-tax balances (unless modelling operating taxes on a cash basis). The working-capital schedule from Week 14 forecasts each line from days drivers:

\[ \text{Receivables} = \text{Revenue} \times \frac{\text{DSO}}{365}, \quad \text{Inventory} = \text{COGS} \times \frac{\text{DIO}}{365}, \quad \text{Payables} = \text{Purchases or COGS} \times \frac{\text{DPO}}{365} \]

Then:

\[ \Delta\text{NWC}_t = \text{NWC}_t - \text{NWC}_{t-1} \]

An increase in NWC consumes cash and is deducted. Sign errors are the classic bug: on the CFS, an increase in receivables appears as a negative number; if the DCF row "Less: ΔNWC" is linked to that CFS line and then subtracted again, the effect is reversed. Choose one convention — e.g. the DCF row shows ΔNWC as a positive number for an increase and the FCFF formula subtracts it — and label it.

Negative working capital businesses (DMart's quick-turn grocery is near-neutral; FMCG majors such as HUL and Nestlé India run negative NWC; quick-service restaurants and e-commerce marketplaces often too) generate cash as they grow — ΔNWC is negative, and FCFF exceeds NOPAT + D&A − Capex. That is genuine value, but it must be bounded: payable days cannot stretch forever, and MSMED Act Sec 43B(h) (effective FY24) disallows deductions for MSME dues unpaid beyond 45 days, pressuring DPO.

Worked illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): NOPAT 431; D&A 205 from PPE schedule; maintenance capex 230, growth capex 320; NWC 31-Mar-26 = 640, 31-Mar-27 = 712.

\[ \text{FCFF} = 431 + 205 - (230 + 320) - (712 - 640) = 14 \]

Despite NOPAT of ₹431 crore, a heavy expansion year leaves only ₹14 crore of free cash. That is normal in a capex cycle and not a valuation problem — the expansion should show up in higher NOPAT in FY28–FY30.

Excel implementation

DCF!F14  =IS!F17                     ' D&A (links to PPE!F30 via P&L)
DCF!F15  =PPE!F12                    ' Maintenance capex
DCF!F16  =PPE!F13                    ' Growth capex incl. CWIP spend
DCF!F17  =WC!F40-WC!E40              ' Increase in NWC (positive = cash outflow)

Checks!F5 =ABS(DCF!F18-CFS!F60)<0.5  ' FCFF vs CFO - capex + after-tax interest reconciliation

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Asset-light vs asset-heavy. For software or platform businesses, capitalised development cost (Ind AS 38) is capex — do not treat it as an expense add-back without also deducting it.
  • Acquisitions. Planned bolt-on acquisitions are capex-like reinvestment; include them only if the related EBITDA is also forecast.
  • Government grants (PLI, capital subsidies) reduce net capex if deducted from asset cost or are deferred income if presented gross (Ind AS 20); ensure the cash is counted once.
  • Disposals. One-off land sales are non-operating; recurring disposals of fleet vehicles (logistics) can net against capex.
  • Working-capital seasonality. A 31-March balance sheet may understate NWC for companies with festive-season build-ups (Diwali inventory peaks in Q2–Q3); use average or normalised days for the terminal year.
  • ΔNWC in the stub year. If the valuation date is mid-year, ΔNWC for the stub must use the roll-forward NWC at the valuation date, not a pro-rated annual change.

Key takeaways

  • D&A (including ROU depreciation post-116) is added back because it is non-cash; link it from the fixed-asset schedule.
  • Split capex into maintenance (≥ D&A in steady state) and growth (capacity-driven); count CWIP spend when incurred.
  • ΔNWC is the change in operating NWC only — exclude cash, debt, lease and tax balances.
  • An increase in NWC reduces FCFF; fix one sign convention and test it against the CFS.
  • Negative NWC is a genuine value source but must be bounded by realistic days and regulatory limits such as Sec 43B(h).

Practice Questions

Q1. FCFF from schedule data [L1]

For FY28E (₹ crore): EBIT 820, marginal tax 25.17%, depreciation 260, amortisation of software 18, maintenance capex 290, growth capex 410, opening NWC 1,050, closing NWC 1,135. Compute FCFF and state which schedule each input should be linked from.

Q2. Maintenance capex estimation [L2]

A cement company has 20 MTPA capacity, gross block of ₹14,000 crore, D&A of ₹720 crore and guides to adding 4 MTPA by FY28 at ₹850 crore per MTPA. Historical total capex averaged ₹1,100 crore over five years during which capacity grew from 16 to 20 MTPA. Estimate maintenance capex using two methods, explain the difference, and design the capex rows of the PPE schedule for FY26–FY30.

💡 Hint: Back out growth capex from historical capacity additions; compare the residual with D&A and with a % of gross block.

Q3. NWC sign trap [L2]

A model's CFS shows "(Increase)/decrease in trade receivables: (85)", "(Increase)/decrease in inventories: (40)", "Increase/(decrease) in trade payables: 52". The DCF sheet has =CFS!F22+CFS!F23+CFS!F24 in the row "Less: Increase in NWC" and the FCFF formula subtracts this row. Identify the bug, compute the misstatement of FCFF, and write the corrected formula.

Q4. Negative working capital retailer [L3]

A quick-commerce company runs NWC at −12% of revenue because payable days (60) exceed inventory days (15) and customers pay upfront. Revenue is forecast to grow from ₹8,000 crore to ₹20,000 crore over five years. Discuss how much of the resulting NWC release you would accept in the DCF, what supplier-side and regulatory constraints apply, and how the terminal-year NWC assumption should be set.

Q5. CWIP timing [L2]

A plant costing ₹1,500 crore is spent 40% in FY26, 50% in FY27 and 10% in FY28 and is commissioned on 1 April 2028 with a 25-year life (SLM). Show the capex and D&A rows for FY26–FY29 and explain why linking FCFF capex to "capitalisation into gross block" would misstate value.
