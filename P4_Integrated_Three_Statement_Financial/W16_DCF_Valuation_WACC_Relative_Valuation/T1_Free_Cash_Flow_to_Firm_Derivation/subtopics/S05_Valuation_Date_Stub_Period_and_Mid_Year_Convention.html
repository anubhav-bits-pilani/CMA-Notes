Subtopic note

Valuation Date, Stub Period and Mid-Year Convention

Scope: Valuation date, stub period from last 31-March balance sheet, mid-year convention

Why it matters

Indian companies close their books on 31 March, but valuations are performed on any date — a fairness opinion on 15 November, a Rule 11UA report on the date of share allotment, an equity research note on the day of Q2 results. The DCF must discount cash flows from the valuation date, not from the last balance sheet date, and must count only cash flows that occur after the valuation date. Getting this wrong mis-times every flow: valuing as at 31 March 2025 while presenting the result as "value on 20 November 2025" understates value by roughly 7–8 months of WACC (about 7% at a 12% WACC) and ignores cash generated in the interim. The mid-year convention adds a second refinement: cash flows arrive through the year, not on 31 March. Together, the stub and mid-year conventions are a standard interview and assessment test.

Core notes

Valuation date

The valuation date is the date at which value is expressed. It determines:

  • The discounting origin (t = 0).
  • The balance-sheet items in the bridge (cash, debt, leases, NCI) — these must be at or rolled forward to the valuation date (see t4).
  • Market inputs: G-Sec yield, market prices for peer betas and comps, share count.

ICAI Valuation Standards 2018 define the valuation date (VS 101) and require it to be stated in the report (VS 202, Reporting and Documentation); Rule 11UA of the Income-tax Rules ties it to the date of issue/transfer for tax purposes. Market inputs must be as at that date, while financial information is the latest available (the valuer must consider events up to the valuation date).

Stub period

If the last audited balance sheet is 31 March 2025 and the valuation date is 30 September 2025, the first forecast year FY26 (ending 31 March 2026) is only partly in the future. The stub fraction is:

\[ s = \frac{\text{Days from valuation date to next 31 March}}{365} \]

For 30 September 2025: 182 days to 31 March 2026, \( s = 182/365 = 0.4986 \approx 0.5 \).

Two treatments of the FY26 cash flow:

  1. Stub cash flow = FCFF_FY26 × s. Simple pro-rating; assumes even generation through the year. Accept when no half-year data exist.
  2. Stub cash flow = FCFF_FY26 − FCFF_H1 actual. Uses SEBI LODR half-yearly results (Reg 33 requires a half-yearly balance sheet and cash flow statement) to remove cash already generated. More accurate, especially with seasonality.

Under either, the balance sheet used in the bridge must be the one consistent with the stub: if H1 cash flows are excluded from the DCF, the bridge must use 30 September cash and debt (which already reflect H1 cash).

Mid-year convention

Year-end discounting treats FCFF of year \( t \) as received on the last day of the year, discounting at \( (1 + \text{WACC})^t \). Cash actually accrues across the year, so on average flows arrive at mid-year. The mid-year convention discounts year \( t \) at \( t - 0.5 \):

\[ \text{DF}_t = \frac{1}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{t - 0.5}} \]

The uplift is roughly \( (1+\text{WACC})^{0.5} \): at 12% WACC, about 5.8% more value on the explicit flows. Most sell-side, transaction and Ind AS 36 practitioners use mid-year for operating businesses; year-end is used for flows that genuinely arrive annually (dividends, annual concession payments) or when the client's methodology mandates it.

Combining stub and mid-year

With stub fraction \( s \), the stub flow arrives on average at \( s/2 \), the first full year at \( s + 0.5 \), the second at \( s + 1.5 \), and so on:

Period FY26 stub FY27 FY28 FY29 FY30
Year-end exponent \( s \) \( s+1 \) \( s+2 \) \( s+3 \) \( s+4 \)
Mid-year exponent \( s/2 \) \( s+0.5 \) \( s+1.5 \) \( s+2.5 \) \( s+3.5 \)

Worked illustration

Valuation date 30 September 2025, \( s = 0.5 \), WACC 12%. FCFF FY26 full year ₹400 crore (H1 actual ₹180 crore), FY27 ₹460 crore.

Stub FCFF (using actuals) = 400 − 180 = 220.

\[ \text{PV}_{\text{stub}} = \frac{220}{1.12^{0.25}} = \frac{220}{1.0287} = 213.9 \]

\[ \text{PV}_{\text{FY27}} = \frac{460}{1.12^{1.0}} = 410.7 \]

Note the FY27 exponent is \( s + 0.5 = 1.0 \) — not 1.5 (the year-end count from 31 March 2025) and not 0.5 (ignoring the stub).

Excel implementation

Use dates, not hard-coded fractions:

DCF!C4   =DATE(2025,9,30)                               ' Valuation_Date
DCF!F5   =DATE(2026,3,31)                               ' Period end dates across row 5
DCF!F6   =(F5-MAX(E5,Valuation_Date))/365               ' Period fraction (stub in F, 1.0 thereafter)
DCF!F7   =YEARFRAC(Valuation_Date,F5,1)                 ' Year-end exponent
DCF!F8   =IF(MidYear=1,F7-F6/2,F7)                      ' Mid-year exponent toggle
DCF!F9   =1/(1+WACC)^F8                                 ' Discount factor
DCF!F10  =FCFF_Full*F6                                  ' Stub FCFF (pro-rata variant)

The F7−F6/2 construction automatically yields \( s/2 \) for the stub and \( t - 0.5 \) for later full years. A toggle (MidYear) lets reviewers flip conventions. YEARFRAC with basis 1 (actual/actual) or simple /365 are both acceptable; be consistent.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Valuation date = 31 March. No stub; mid-year exponents are 0.5, 1.5, 2.5…
  • Valuation date just after year-end, accounts not yet audited. Use provisional/limited-review numbers; disclose the basis.
  • Seasonal businesses (fertiliser in kharif/rabi, ACs in summer, apparel in festive Q3): pro-rata stub misstates — use actual H1 and forecast H2.
  • Terminal value under mid-year — see t3/s07; the TV exponent differs from the final-year FCFF exponent depending on method.
  • Dividends paid during the stub reduce cash in the bridge; if the valuation is per share cum-dividend vs ex-dividend, align the share price comparison.
  • Inconsistent bridge. Using 31 March cash with a stub that excludes H1 cash flows double-counts or loses H1 cash — the balance-sheet date must match the cash-flow cut-off.

Key takeaways

  • Discount from the valuation date; include only cash flows after it.
  • Stub fraction = days from valuation date to next 31 March ÷ 365; stub cash flow is pro-rated or computed from half-year actuals.
  • Mid-year exponents with stub: \( s/2 \), \( s + 0.5 \), \( s + 1.5 \)…
  • Drive the schedule from dates and a mid-year toggle, not typed fractions.
  • The bridge balance-sheet date must match the cash-flow cut-off used for the stub.

Practice Questions

Q1. Exponent schedule [L1]

Valuation date 15 December 2025; FY ends 31 March. Compute the stub fraction (actual days/365) and the mid-year discount exponents for FY26 (stub) through FY30.

Q2. Stub PV computation [L2]

Valuation date 31 December 2025, WACC 11.5%. Full-year FCFF forecasts (₹ crore): FY26 600, FY27 680, FY28 750, FY29 810, FY30 860. Nine-month FY26 actual FCFF was ₹410 crore. Compute the PV of explicit-period FCFF under (a) pro-rata stub with mid-year convention and (b) actual-based stub with mid-year convention. Comment on the difference.

💡 Hint: The stub fraction is 90/365; under (a) the stub flow is FY26 × s, under (b) it is FY26 full-year less the nine-month actual.

Q3. Bridge consistency [L2]

An analyst values a company as at 30 September 2025 using an actual-based stub but deducts net debt from the 31 March 2025 balance sheet. During H1 the company generated FCFF of ₹250 crore, paid dividends of ₹90 crore and repaid debt of ₹100 crore. Quantify the error in equity value and explain the correction.

Q4. Year-end vs mid-year debate [L3]

A client's Ind AS 36 impairment model uses year-end discounting; your transaction valuation of the same CGU uses mid-year. The difference is ₹280 crore. Prepare talking points for the audit committee explaining the source of the difference and which convention better reflects the economics.

Q5. Model build: date-driven discounting [L2]

Write the Excel row formulas (period end dates, period fraction, year-end exponent, mid-year exponent with toggle, discount factor) for a DCF whose valuation date is an input, so that changing the valuation date from 30 September to 31 March automatically removes the stub.
