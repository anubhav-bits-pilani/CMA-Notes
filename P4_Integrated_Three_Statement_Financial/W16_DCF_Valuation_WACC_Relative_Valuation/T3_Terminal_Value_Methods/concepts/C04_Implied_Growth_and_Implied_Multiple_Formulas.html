Key concept

Implied Growth and Implied Multiple Formulas

Concept: Implied g = (TV × WACC − FCFF_n) ÷ (TV + FCFF_n); Implied multiple = TV_Gordon ÷ EBITDA_n

Statement

Implied perpetuity growth embedded in an exit-multiple terminal value:

\[ g_{\text{implied}} = \frac{\text{TV} \times \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_n}{\text{TV} + \text{FCFF}_n} \]

Implied EV/EBITDA multiple embedded in a Gordon terminal value:

\[ \left(\frac{\text{EV}}{\text{EBITDA}}\right)_{\text{implied}} = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}}{\text{EBITDA}_n} \]

where TV is the terminal value at the end of year \( n \), FCFF\(_n\) the normalised final explicit-year FCFF, WACC the discount rate and EBITDA\(_n\) the final explicit-year EBITDA (same basis as the comps). Both TVs must be measured at the same point in time.

Explanation

Derivation of implied g. Start from \( \text{TV} = \text{FCFF}_n (1 + g) / (\text{WACC} - g) \). Cross-multiply: \( \text{TV} \cdot \text{WACC} - \text{TV} \cdot g = \text{FCFF}_n + \text{FCFF}_n \cdot g \). Collect g terms: \( g(\text{TV} + \text{FCFF}_n) = \text{TV} \cdot \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_n \). Divide.

Purpose. Each TV method hides an assumption the other makes explicit. An exit multiple hides a growth rate; a Gordon growth rate hides a multiple. Computing the hidden number exposes inconsistency:

  • Implied g above long-run INR nominal growth (roughly 6–7% as an outer bound for most companies) or close to WACC → exit multiple too high for steady state.
  • Implied multiple far from mature-state peer multiples → revisit g, RONIC, WACC or the terminal margin.

Timing under mid-year convention. A Gordon TV built on mid-year flows is a value at \( n - 0.5 \); before comparing with an exit TV (a value at \( n \)), multiply it by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \). Equivalently, when solving implied g from an exit TV under mid-year, use \( \text{TV}_{\text{Exit}} / (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \) in place of TV.

Basis. EBITDA\(_n\) must match the multiple's basis (LTM vs forward; post- vs pre-116). For a forward multiple use EBITDA\(_{n+1}\).

Excel:

=(TV_Exit*WACC-FCFF_n)/(TV_Exit+FCFF_n)        ' implied g (year-end basis)
=TV_Gordon*(1+WACC)^(0.5*MidYear)/EBITDA_n      ' implied LTM multiple at end of year n

Illustration

Year n = 7: FCFF\(_7\) ₹780 crore; EBITDA\(_7\) ₹1,600 crore; WACC 12%; exit multiple 14× → TV = ₹22,400 crore.

\[ g_{\text{implied}} = \frac{22{,}400 \times 0.12 - 780}{22{,}400 + 780} = \frac{2{,}688 - 780}{23{,}180} = 8.23\% \]

An 8.2% perpetual INR growth rate is above a defensible steady state, so 14× is aggressive.

Gordon at g = 5.5%: TV = \( 780 \times 1.055 / 0.065 = 12{,}660 \); implied multiple (year-end basis) = 12,660 ÷ 1,600 = 7.9×; under mid-year, \( 12{,}660 \times 1.12^{0.5} / 1{,}600 = 8.4\times \).

Practice Questions

Q1. Implied g [L1]

TV from exit multiple ₹15,000 crore; FCFF\(_n\) ₹700 crore; WACC 11%. Compute implied g and judge whether it is reasonable in INR.

Q2. Both directions with mid-year [L2]

FCFF\(_5\) ₹520 crore; EBITDA\(_5\) ₹1,100 crore; WACC 12.5%; g 5%; exit multiple 10×; mid-year convention. Compute the Gordon TV, its implied multiple at the end of year 5, and the implied g of the exit TV using the mid-year adjustment.

💡 Hint: Put both TVs at the end of year 5 before comparing; for implied g, deflate the exit TV by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \).

Q3. Derivation [L1]

Derive the implied-growth formula from the Gordon equation and state the two conditions under which the result is meaningful.
