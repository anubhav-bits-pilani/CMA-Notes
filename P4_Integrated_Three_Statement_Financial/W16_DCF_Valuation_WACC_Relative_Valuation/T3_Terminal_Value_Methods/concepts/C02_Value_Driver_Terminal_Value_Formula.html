Key concept

Value-Driver Terminal Value Formula

Concept: Value driver: TV = NOPAT_n+1 × (1 − g ÷ RONIC) ÷ (WACC − g)

Statement

\[ \text{TV}_n = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_{n+1} \times \left(1 - \dfrac{g}{\text{RONIC}}\right)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

where \( \text{NOPAT}_{n+1} = \text{EBIT}_{n+1} \times (1 - t) \) is the first terminal-year operating profit after marginal tax, \( g \) the perpetual growth rate, RONIC the return on new invested capital in perpetuity, \( g / \text{RONIC} \) the required net reinvestment rate, and WACC the long-run cost of capital. Validity: WACC > g and RONIC > g (otherwise reinvestment exceeds NOPAT).

Explanation

The formula substitutes \( \text{FCFF}_{n+1} = \text{NOPAT}_{n+1} (1 - \text{RR}) \) and \( \text{RR} = g / \text{RONIC} \) into the Gordon equation. Its power is that growth is no longer free — every point of growth costs reinvestment, and its net effect on value depends on RONIC relative to WACC:

  • RONIC > WACC: higher g raises TV (value-creating growth).
  • RONIC = WACC: TV = NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) / WACC for any g — growth is value-neutral.
  • RONIC < WACC: higher g lowers TV (value-destroying growth).

Practitioner usage:

  • Competitive-equilibrium anchor: McKinsey and Damodaran suggest RONIC converging toward WACC for most firms in the long run; strong franchises can sustain a modest excess (e.g. RONIC 1.3–2× WACC).
  • Cross-check: compute the implied RONIC of the plain Gordon terminal column: \( \text{RONIC}_{\text{implied}} = g / \text{RR}_{\text{terminal}} \). Implausibly high values reveal under-investment (capex ≈ D&A while g > 0).
  • Distinction from ROIC: NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) already reflects high returns on existing capital (brands, distribution); RONIC applies only to new investment.
  • Lease basis: on post-116 basis NOPAT excludes rent and reinvestment includes net ROU additions; RONIC is measured on lease-inclusive capital.

Illustration

Paints company: EBIT\(_{n+1}\) ₹3,200 crore; tax 25.17% → NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) = ₹2,394.6 crore; WACC 11%; g 5.5%.

RONIC RR = g/RONIC FCFF\(_{n+1}\) TV (₹ crore) TV ÷ NOPAT\(_{n+1}\)
11% 50.0% 1,197 21,769 9.1×
18% 30.6% 1,663 30,234 12.6×
30% 18.3% 1,956 35,556 14.8×

At RONIC 11% (= WACC), TV = 2,394.6 ÷ 0.11 = 21,769 — identical to setting g = 0. The difference between the 11% and 30% cases (63%) shows that the moat assumption, not g alone, drives value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute TV [L1]

EBIT\(_{n+1}\) ₹850 crore; tax 25.17%; g 5%; RONIC 14%; WACC 11.5%. Compute NOPAT\(_{n+1}\), reinvestment, FCFF\(_{n+1}\) and TV.

Q2. Implied RONIC [L2]

A terminal column shows NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) ₹1,500 crore, capex ₹420 crore, D&A ₹400 crore, ΔNWC ₹30 crore, g 6%. Compute the implied reinvestment rate and RONIC, and the TV under the value-driver formula if RONIC is instead set to 15% (WACC 12%).

💡 Hint: Net reinvestment = capex − D&A + ΔNWC.

Q3. Value-destroying growth [L2]

Explain with numbers (NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) ₹1,000 crore, WACC 12%, RONIC 10%) why raising g from 4% to 6% lowers TV, and give an Indian sector example where this might be realistic.
