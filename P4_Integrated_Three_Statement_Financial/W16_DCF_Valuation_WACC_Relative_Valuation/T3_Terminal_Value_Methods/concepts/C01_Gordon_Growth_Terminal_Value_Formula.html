Key concept

Gordon Growth Terminal Value Formula

Concept: TV_Gordon = FCFF_n × (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g)

Statement

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_n \times (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

where \( \text{FCFF}_n \) is the normalised free cash flow to the firm of the final explicit forecast year \( n \), \( g \) is the constant perpetual growth rate of FCFF from year \( n+1 \) onward, and WACC is the long-run weighted average cost of capital. The result is the value, one period before FCFF\(_{n+1}\), of all cash flows from year \( n+1 \) to infinity. Validity requires \( \text{WACC} > g \) and a steady state from year \( n+1 \).

Explanation

The formula is the sum of a geometric series:

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\text{FCFF}_n (1+g)^k}{(1+\text{WACC})^k} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_n (1+g)}{\text{WACC} - g} \quad \text{for } g < \text{WACC} \]

Intuition: a perpetuity of ₹1 growing at \( g \) is worth \( 1 / (\text{WACC} - g) \). At WACC 11.5% and g 5.5%, each rupee of FCFF\(_{n+1}\) is worth ₹16.7 — so FCFF\(_{n+1}\) must be a true steady-state cash flow.

Conditions and cautions:

  • Normalisation: FCFF\(_n\) × (1 + g) presumes the final-year FCFF is in steady state. Where it is not (capex cycle, NWC swing, tax holiday, cyclical margin), build FCFF\(_{n+1}\) directly in a terminal column (t3/s01, s05).
  • Growth funding: g must be consistent with reinvestment: \( g = \text{RR} \times \text{RONIC} \) (see c02).
  • g selection: below long-run nominal GDP, Damodaran cap \( g \le R_f \), INR inflation plus real growth (t3/s02).
  • Sensitivity: \( \partial \text{TV} / \partial g \) rises sharply as \( g \to \text{WACC} \); guard formulas against \( g \ge \text{WACC} \).
  • Timing: under mid-year convention, the TV is discounted at \( n - 0.5 \) (c05, s07).
  • Consistency triad: FCFF with WACC gives EV; if valuing FCFE, use Ke and FCFE\(_n\) instead.

Illustration

FY30 (n = 5) normalised FCFF = ₹480 crore; g = 5%; WACC = 11.8%.

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} = \frac{480 \times 1.05}{0.118 - 0.05} = \frac{504}{0.068} = 7{,}412 \]

Sensitivity: at g = 6%, TV = \( 508.8 / 0.058 = 8{,}772 \) (+18%); at WACC 12.8% and g 5%, TV = \( 504 / 0.078 = 6{,}462 \) (−13%). The asymmetry illustrates why the WACC − g spread dominates the valuation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and flex [L1]

FCFF\(_n\) ₹1,250 crore; g 5.5%; WACC 12%. Compute TV, then TV at g ± 0.5% and WACC ± 0.5%, and present the five values.

Q2. Formula guard [L2]

In a sensitivity grid, WACC ranges from 9% to 13% and g from 4% to 9%. Identify which combinations make the Gordon formula invalid or produce implausible TVs (TV/FCFF\(_{n+1}\) > 40×), and explain how you would display them.

💡 Hint: TV ÷ FCFF\(_{n+1}\) = 1 ÷ (WACC − g).

Q3. FCFE variant [L2]

For an equity valuation, FCFE\(_n\) ₹300 crore, Ke 14%, g 6%. Compute the equity terminal value and explain why using WACC instead of Ke in this formula would be wrong.
