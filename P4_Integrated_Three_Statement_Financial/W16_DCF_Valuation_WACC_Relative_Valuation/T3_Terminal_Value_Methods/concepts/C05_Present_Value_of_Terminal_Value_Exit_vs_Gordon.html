Key concept

Present Value of Terminal Value: Exit vs Gordon Timing

Concept: PV(TV_Exit) = TV ÷ (1 + WACC)^n; Gordon TV of mid-year flows discounted at (n − 0.5)

Statement

\[ \text{PV}(\text{TV}_{\text{Exit}}) = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Exit}}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n}} \]

\[ \text{PV}(\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}) = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n - 0.5}} \quad \text{(mid-year convention)} \]

where \( n \) is the number of years from the valuation date to the end of the final explicit year, \( \text{TV}_{\text{Exit}} = \text{EBITDA}_n \times \text{multiple} \) is a point-in-time value at the end of year \( n \), and \( \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} = \text{FCFF}_{n+1} / (\text{WACC} - g) \) with FCFF\(_{n+1}\) assumed to arrive at \( n + 0.5 \). With a stub of length \( s \) and the stub counted as year 1, replace \( n \) by \( s + n - 1 \) and \( n - 0.5 \) by \( s + n - 1.5 \). Under year-end convention, both TVs are discounted at \( n \).

Explanation

The Gordon formula values a perpetuity one period before its first payment. If the first terminal flow arrives mid-way through year \( n+1 \) (at \( n + 0.5 \)), the formula's output sits at \( n - 0.5 \) — so it is discounted for \( n - 0.5 \) years. An equivalent and more transparent formulation:

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon, at } n} = \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} \times (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5}, \qquad \text{PV} = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon, at } n}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n}} \]

The exit multiple, by contrast, is calibrated on market EV ÷ LTM EBITDA at a point in time; applied to EBITDA\(_n\), it estimates a sale price on the last day of year \( n \), so no half-year shift applies.

Why it matters:

  • The half-year shift is worth \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} - 1 \approx 5.5\text{–}6\% \) of TV at Indian WACCs of 11–13%.
  • Applying \( n - 0.5 \) to the exit TV overstates value; applying \( n \) to a mid-year Gordon TV understates it (some templates do this deliberately as a conservative convention — disclose it).
  • When comparing the two TVs (implied g, implied multiple), move them to the same date first (c04).

Excel (date-driven rows from t1/c04):

=TV_Gordon/(1+WACC)^INDEX(Exp_MidYear,Last_Col)
=TV_Exit/(1+WACC)^INDEX(Exp_YearEnd,Last_Col)

Illustration

Valuation date 31 December 2025 (\( s = 0.2466 \)); explicit periods FY26 stub + FY27–FY30 (so the final year FY30 ends at \( s + 4 = 4.2466 \) years); WACC 11.5%.

TV Amount (₹ crore) Exponent PV factor PV
Gordon on mid-year flows 12,000 3.7466 0.6651 7,981
Exit multiple 12,800 4.2466 0.6299 8,062

Factors: \( 1.115^{-3.7466} = e^{-3.7466 \times 0.10885} = e^{-0.4078} = 0.6651 \); \( 1.115^{-4.2466} = e^{-0.4622} = 0.6299 \). Discounting the exit TV at 3.7466 instead would give PV ₹8,513 crore — 5.6% too high.

Practice Questions

Q1. PV both TVs [L1]

n = 5 (no stub), WACC 12%, Gordon TV ₹8,400 crore (mid-year flows), exit TV ₹9,100 crore. Compute both PVs and the PV of the Gordon TV if it were (incorrectly) discounted at 5 years.

Q2. Stub exponents [L2]

Valuation date 15 August 2025; final explicit year FY31; WACC 11%. Compute the stub fraction, the exponents for each TV method and the PV factors.

💡 Hint: Count the stub as the first period; the final year-end is s plus the number of full years after the stub.

Q3. Convention disclosure [L2]

A bank's template discounts Gordon TV at \( n \) while using mid-year explicit flows. Estimate the effect on EV for a company where PV(TV) is 75% of EV at a 12% WACC, and draft a one-line disclosure for the valuation report.
