Key concept

Exit Multiple Terminal Value Formula

Concept: TV_Exit = EBITDA_n × EV/EBITDA multiple

Statement

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Exit}} = \text{EBITDA}_n \times \left(\frac{\text{EV}}{\text{EBITDA}}\right)_{\text{exit}} \]

where \( \text{EBITDA}_n \) is EBITDA for the final explicit forecast year \( n \) (on the same Ind AS 116 basis, consolidation basis and treatment of exceptional items as the comps), and the exit multiple is an LTM EV/EBITDA appropriate to the company's expected profile at the end of year \( n \). The result is an enterprise value at the end of year \( n \). If a forward multiple is used, apply it to \( \text{EBITDA}_{n+1} \).

Explanation

The exit-multiple method assumes the business could be sold at the end of the forecast horizon at a market multiple. It is preferred in PE and M&A because it mirrors how exits are priced, and it links the DCF to observable data from trading comps (t6) or precedent transactions.

Critical conditions:

  • Basis consistency: EV in the peer multiple and EBITDA in the DCF must share the lease basis (post-116 EV includes lease liabilities and divides post-116 EBITDA), consolidation basis and normalisation (exceptional items, other income excluded).
  • Maturity matching: current peer multiples reflect current growth expectations. In year n the company should be in steady state; a mature-state multiple is usually lower than today's Indian consumer multiples (t3/s03).
  • Control vs minority: transaction multiples embed control premiums; use trading multiples for a minority (market) value.
  • Cross-check: compute the implied perpetuity growth (c04); if it exceeds long-run nominal GDP or approaches WACC, the multiple is too high.
  • Timing: the TV is a point-in-time value at the end of year n — discount at the full \( n \) (c05).

Alternative multiples: EV/EBIT for capital-intensive businesses (D&A differences), EV/Sales for early-stage businesses, P/E or P/B for banks and NBFCs (equity-based TV).

Illustration

Retailer, FY35 (n = 10): EBITDA (post-116) ₹7,500 crore. Peer median today 45×; mature global retailers 12–14×; Gordon-implied multiple 13×.

Multiple TV (₹ crore) Comment
45× 3,37,500 Perpetuates today's elevated Indian multiple
14× 1,05,000 Mature global retailer benchmark
13× 97,500 Consistent with Gordon at g = 5.5%

The 45× case implies the business still trades like a hyper-growth stock after ten years — a contradiction of the terminal-value premise.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute exit TV and PV [L1]

FY30 EBITDA ₹2,150 crore; exit multiple 12.5×; WACC 11.5%; valuation date 31 March 2025; n = 5. Compute TV and its PV.

Q2. Basis mismatch [L2]

Peers' EV/EBITDA of 20× is computed with EV including lease liabilities and post-116 EBITDA. The DCF's FY32 EBITDA is pre-116 (₹1,200 crore; post-116 equivalent ₹1,520 crore). An analyst applies 20× to ₹1,200 crore. Quantify the effect and state the correct pairing and bridge treatment.

💡 Hint: Decide on one basis and keep the EBITDA, multiple and lease deduction aligned.

Q3. Choosing the metric [L2]

For (a) a capital-intensive telecom tower company, (b) a loss-making quick-commerce platform, (c) a private bank, recommend the exit multiple metric and explain why EV/EBITDA might mislead.
