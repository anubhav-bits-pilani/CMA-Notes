Subtopic note

Cross-Checks: Implied Growth and Implied Multiple

Scope: Implied growth from exit-multiple TV; implied multiple from Gordon TV

Why it matters

The two terminal value methods answer different questions. Gordon Growth asks "what is a steady-state perpetuity worth at this WACC and growth?"; the exit multiple asks "what would the market pay for this EBITDA?". A robust valuation computes one and uses the other as a sanity check: the implied perpetuity growth inside an exit-multiple TV, and the implied EV/EBITDA multiple inside a Gordon TV. If a 25× exit multiple implies 9% perpetual growth in INR, or a 5% Gordon growth rate implies a 7× multiple for a high-ROIC consumer brand, something is inconsistent. Equity research templates, fairness opinions and investment committee papers in India routinely show both numbers side by side, and the Week 16 checklist requires you to reconcile them.

Core notes

Implied perpetuity growth from an exit-multiple TV

Set the exit-method TV equal to a Gordon TV and solve for \( g \):

\[ \text{TV} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_n (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

\[ \text{TV} \times \text{WACC} - \text{TV} \times g = \text{FCFF}_n + \text{FCFF}_n \times g \]

\[ g_{\text{implied}} = \frac{\text{TV} \times \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_n}{\text{TV} + \text{FCFF}_n} \]

where FCFF\(_n\) is the final explicit-year (normalised) FCFF and TV is the exit-multiple TV at the end of year \( n \). If the Gordon formula is written with FCFF\(_{n+1}\) directly, \( g = \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_{n+1}/\text{TV} \) — but then FCFF\(_{n+1}\) itself depends on g, so the FCFF\(_n\) form is the usual convention.

Implied multiple from a Gordon TV

\[ \left(\frac{\text{EV}}{\text{EBITDA}}\right)_{\text{implied}} = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}}{\text{EBITDA}_n} \]

(LTM basis). Use \( \text{EBITDA}_{n+1} \) for a forward multiple. Compare it with the comps' current and mature-state multiples (s03).

Interpreting the cross-checks

Result Interpretation Action
Implied g within 4–6% INR and close to your Gordon g Methods consistent Present both, maybe average
Implied g > 7% or ≥ WACC Exit multiple embeds growth beyond steady state Lower multiple to mature-state, or extend explicit horizon
Implied g < 2% for a quality franchise Exit multiple too low (perhaps cyclical trough) Revisit peer set, use through-cycle
Implied multiple far below peer current median Gordon assumptions conservative, or market overvalued Explain; check RONIC and reinvestment
Implied multiple far above peers Gordon g too high or WACC too low Revisit g and WACC

Divergence is not always an error. Indian consumer stocks trade at multiples that imply very high g because markets price in long growth runways; a DCF with a 10-year horizon will show a lower Gordon-implied multiple than today's trading multiple. The appropriate comparison for the terminal year is a mature-state multiple, not today's.

Worked illustration

FY35 (n = 10): EBITDA ₹2,400 crore; normalised FCFF ₹1,050 crore; WACC 11.5%.

Exit method: 16× EBITDA → TV = ₹38,400 crore.

\[ g_{\text{implied}} = \frac{38{,}400 \times 0.115 - 1{,}050}{38{,}400 + 1{,}050} = \frac{4{,}416 - 1{,}050}{39{,}450} = 8.53\% \]

Gordon method: g = 5.5% → TV = \( 1{,}050 \times 1.055 / 0.06 = 18{,}463 \).

\[ \text{Implied multiple} = \frac{18{,}463}{2{,}400} = 7.7\times \]

The exit multiple implies 8.5% perpetual growth — above long-run INR nominal GDP — so 16× is too high for a steady state. But 7.7× may also look low for a company with strong returns. Check the reinvestment assumption in the Gordon FCFF: if FCFF is 44% of EBITDA and RONIC is high, perhaps reinvestment is overstated. Using the value-driver formula (s06) with RONIC 20% instead of an implicit lower return may lift the Gordon TV materially. Reconciliation proceeds by adjusting assumptions with economic justification, not by averaging.

Excel implementation

DCF!P40  =TV_Exit                                           ' exit-multiple TV at end of year n
DCF!P41  =(P40*WACC-FCFF_n)/(P40+FCFF_n)                    ' implied perpetuity growth
DCF!P42  =TV_Gordon/EBITDA_n                                ' implied LTM EV/EBITDA
DCF!P43  =IF(P41>=WACC,"FLAG: implied g >= WACC",IF(P41>Max_g,"FLAG: implied g above cap","OK"))

Display both cross-checks in the DCF output box beside the two TVs; they are standard items in the football field footnotes.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mid-year convention: if Gordon TV is computed on mid-year-convention flows and discounted at \( n - 0.5 \) (s07), the comparison with an exit TV at \( n \) must adjust timing — compare values at the same point in time (multiply the Gordon TV by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \) to move it to n, or vice versa).
  • Negative or tiny FCFF\(_n\): implied g becomes extreme; the company is not in steady state — extend the horizon.
  • Lease basis: EBITDA and the exit multiple must be on the same Ind AS 116 basis; the implied multiple from a post-116 Gordon TV is a post-116 multiple.
  • EV/EBIT is a cleaner cross-check for capital-intensive firms whose D&A/EBITDA varies.

Key takeaways

  • Implied g = (TV × WACC − FCFF\(_n\)) ÷ (TV + FCFF\(_n\)); implied multiple = Gordon TV ÷ EBITDA\(_n\).
  • Use each method to test the other; large divergences reveal non-steady-state assumptions.
  • Compare implied multiples with mature-state, not current, peer multiples.
  • Reconcile by fixing assumptions (g, RONIC, reinvestment, horizon), not by averaging blindly.
  • Align timing and lease basis before comparing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Implied growth [L1]

Final-year FCFF ₹620 crore; EBITDA ₹1,300 crore; WACC 12%; exit multiple 12×. Compute the exit TV, implied g, and comment.

Q2. Two-way reconciliation [L2]

FY31 EBITDA ₹3,200 crore; normalised FCFF ₹1,280 crore; WACC 11%; Gordon g 5.5%; peer median EV/EBITDA 22× today; mature global peers 14×. Compute Gordon TV, implied multiple, exit TVs at 22× and 14×, and implied g for each. Recommend a TV with reasons.

💡 Hint: Compute all values at the end of FY31 before comparing.

Q3. Mid-year timing mismatch [L2]

An analyst computes Gordon TV on FCFF\(_{n+1}\), discounts it at \( n - 0.5 \), and compares its PV with the PV of an exit-multiple TV discounted at \( n \), concluding that the two methods agree. Explain why the comparison is not like-for-like and how to correct it.

Q4. Excel flagging [L1]

Write formulas that compute implied g and implied multiple, and flag (a) implied g ≥ WACC, (b) implied g above a user-defined cap, (c) implied multiple outside the comps' interquartile range.
