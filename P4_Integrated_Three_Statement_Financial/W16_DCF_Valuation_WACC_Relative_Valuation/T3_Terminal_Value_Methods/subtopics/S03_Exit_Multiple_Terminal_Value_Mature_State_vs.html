Exit Multiple Terminal Value: Mature-State vs Current Multiples

Scope: Exit multiple: terminal EBITDA × EV/EBITDA from comps, mature-state vs current multiple

Why it matters

The exit multiple method values the business at the end of the explicit period as if it were sold at a market multiple of its then-EBITDA. It is the dominant TV method in private equity, M&A and sell-side practice in India because it speaks the language of deal-making and links the DCF to observable market prices. But it imports the market's current mood into a valuation that is supposed to be intrinsic. Indian consumer, retail and specialty-chemical stocks trade at 40–70× EV/EBITDA today; applying such multiples to year-10 EBITDA implicitly assumes the company will still be in a high-growth phase in ten years — contradicting the steady-state premise of a terminal value. The skill is to choose a multiple appropriate for the company's mature state at the exit date, and to cross-check it against the Gordon method.

Core notes

The formula

\[ \text{TV}_n^{\text{Exit}} = \text{EBITDA}_n \times \left(\frac{\text{EV}}{\text{EBITDA}}\right)_{\text{exit}} \]

where \( \text{EBITDA}_n \) is the EBITDA of the final explicit year (trailing basis) or \( \text{EBITDA}_{n+1} \) (forward basis), and the multiple must be on the matching basis (LTM multiple × year-n EBITDA; forward multiple × year n+1 EBITDA). The result is a value at the end of year \( n \).

Variants: EV/EBIT (better for capital-intensive firms with differing D&A), EV/Sales (loss-making or early-stage businesses), and for financial companies P/B or P/E (equity-value TV).

Sourcing the multiple from comps

The exit multiple comes from the trading comps (t6) and, in M&A contexts, precedent transactions:

  1. Build the peer set on the same basis (consolidated, Ind AS 116 basis consistent with the DCF EBITDA, exceptional items stripped).
  2. Use the median (and quartiles) of LTM or forward EV/EBITDA.
  3. Adjust for maturity: today's peers are growing at today's rates. A multiple that reflects 15–20% growth is inappropriate for a company growing 5% in year n+1.

Current vs mature-state multiple

Choice When appropriate Risk
Current peer median Short horizon (5 years) where the company will still resemble peers today; PE exit assumption Embeds current growth expectations and sentiment; overvalues if today's multiples are elevated
Mature-state multiple Long horizon where the company is at steady state at exit Requires judgement; must be justified
Historical through-cycle average Cyclical sectors Long Indian histories show regime changes (pre-/post-GST, post-COVID re-rating)
Gordon-implied multiple (s04) Consistency anchor Circular only if used as sole justification

Ways to estimate a mature-state multiple:

  • Fundamental multiple: derive EV/EBITDA from the value-driver formula using terminal g, RONIC, WACC, tax and D&A/EBITDA (s06 and c02). This is the Gordon-implied multiple.
  • Mature-peer multiple: use multiples of companies in the same industry that are already mature — e.g. global peers (Sherwin-Williams, PPG for paints at roughly 15–20× EBITDA) or mature Indian incumbents in slower categories.
  • Multiple fade: start from today's multiple and fade it toward a long-run market multiple over the horizon.

The consistency problem

The exit multiple method is a relative valuation grafted onto an intrinsic one. If the explicit FCFFs are intrinsic and the TV is a market price, the DCF is a hybrid — acceptable if transparent, but the TV must be sanity-checked:

  • Compute the implied perpetuity growth (s04): if a 40× multiple implies g ≥ WACC or g > 8% in INR, the multiple is too high for a steady state.
  • Compare with the Gordon TV; a large gap must be explained (e.g. growth not yet at steady state → extend the horizon).

Worked illustration

Adhesives company: FY35 (year 10) EBITDA ₹6,000 crore; current peer median EV/EBITDA (LTM) 45×; mature global peers 18×; WACC 11.5%; Gordon TV (g = 5.5%) = ₹1,02,000 crore.

Approach Multiple TV_FY35 (₹ crore)
Current Indian peer median 45× 2,70,000
Mature-state (global peers) 18× 1,08,000
Gordon-implied 17× 1,02,000

The 45× exit implies a TV 2.6× the Gordon value — equivalent to assuming the company still deserves a growth-stock multiple after ten years. The mature-state 18× is consistent with Gordon at 5.5% g.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • EBITDA basis: post-116 vs pre-116 EBITDA must match the comps' EV basis; a post-116 exit multiple applied to pre-116 EBITDA understates TV (and vice versa).
  • Control vs minority: precedent transaction multiples include control premiums; for a minority-basis DCF, use trading multiples.
  • Cyclical peaks: apply a through-cycle multiple to mid-cycle EBITDA, not a trough multiple to peak EBITDA.
  • Different capex intensity: EV/EBITDA ignores D&A differences; for capital-heavy firms prefer EV/EBIT or check EV/(EBITDA − capex).
  • Short horizons in PE models (5-year exit) often use the entry multiple as the exit multiple ("no multiple expansion") — a conservative convention but not a steady-state argument.

Key takeaways

  • TV_Exit = EBITDA\(_n\) × exit EV/EBITDA, on matching LTM or forward basis, from comps on the same accounting basis.
  • Today's peer multiples reflect today's growth; terminal multiples should reflect the company's mature state.
  • Derive mature-state multiples fundamentally (Gordon-implied), from mature peers or by fading.
  • Always compute the implied perpetuity growth of the exit TV.
  • Keep the multiple as one driver feeding TV, sensitivity grids and the football field.

Practice Questions

Q1. Exit TV and PV [L1]

FY30 (year 5) EBITDA ₹1,450 crore; exit EV/EBITDA 11× (LTM); WACC 12%. Compute TV at end of FY30 and its PV under year-end discounting with a 31 March 2025 valuation date.

Q2. Basis matching [L2]

Peers' median EV/EBITDA is 14.5× on a post-116 basis (EV incl. leases) and 17.0× on a pre-116 basis. Your FY32 EBITDA is ₹900 crore post-116 and ₹760 crore pre-116. Compute the exit TV under each consistent pairing, explain why they differ, and state what must happen in the EV bridge under each.

💡 Hint: Under the post-116 pairing, lease liabilities at exit are part of EV; consider how the bridge at the valuation date handles leases.

Q3. Mature-state argument [L3]

An analyst values a listed QSR chain with a 10-year DCF and uses the current peer median EV/EBITDA of 38× as the exit multiple. FY35 revenue growth is forecast at 7%, WACC is 12%, RONIC 15%. Draft a review note proposing a mature-state multiple, explaining two methods to estimate it and the expected effect on value.

Q4. Cyclical exit [L2]

A cement company's FY30 EBITDA per tonne is ₹1,350 (peak), versus a through-cycle average of ₹1,000; capacity 40 MTPA, utilisation 80%. Peers' 10-year average EV/EBITDA is 12×; current is 16×. Propose the terminal EBITDA and multiple you would use and justify each.
