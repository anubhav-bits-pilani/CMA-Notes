Subtopic note

Terminal-Year Normalisation: Margins, Capex vs D&A and NWC

Scope: Terminal-year normalisation: steady margins, capex ≥ D&A, NWC growing with revenue

Why it matters

The terminal year is the template for every year to infinity. Whatever is in it — a peak margin, an unusually low capex, a working-capital release — is capitalised at 1 ÷ (WACC − g), typically a multiplier of 15–20×. A ₹50 crore distortion in terminal FCFF becomes a ₹750–1,000 crore distortion in EV. Normalisation is the step where an analyst converts the last explicit-period year into a defensible steady state. Reviewers of Indian DCFs — sell-side supervisory analysts, valuation peer reviewers, audit specialists testing Ind AS 36 models — focus on three tests: are margins sustainable, does capex at least cover D&A when the company is growing, and does working capital grow in line with revenue?

Core notes

1. Steady margins

The terminal EBITDA/EBIT margin should represent a sustainable competitive equilibrium, not the end of a margin-expansion story.

  • Cyclical businesses (cement, steel, chemicals, sugar, airlines): use mid-cycle margins — average across a full cycle (often 7–10 years), adjusted for structural change (e.g. consolidation in Indian cement improving pricing discipline).
  • Margin expansion stories: operating leverage and premiumisation can lift margins during the explicit period; the terminal margin should be at or slightly below the final year if competition is expected to erode excess returns.
  • Time-bound benefits: PLI incentives (typically 4–6 years), state capital subsidies, SEZ tax benefits, MAT credit — remove from terminal EBIT and tax.
  • Benchmarking: compare terminal margins with mature global peers and long-run Indian sector averages.

2. Capex ≥ D&A (when growing)

In steady state with positive nominal growth, the operating asset base must grow at \( g \). Since gross additions must cover both depreciation (replacing worn-out capacity) and growth:

\[ \text{Capex}_{n+1} = \text{D\&A}_{n+1} + \text{Net investment in fixed assets}, \quad \text{Net investment} \approx g \times \text{Net fixed assets}_n \]

Additionally, inflation means replacement capex exceeds historical-cost depreciation: a machine bought for ₹100 crore ten years ago costs perhaps ₹150 crore to replace. So even at zero real growth, nominal capex > D&A when inflation is positive.

A terminal year with capex = D&A while g = 5% implies the asset base does not grow while revenue grows forever — an infinite asset-turnover improvement, which overstates FCFF. Capex < D&A is worse: the asset base shrinks while revenue grows.

Practical rules:

  • Capex/D&A ratio in the terminal year typically 1.1–1.5× for Indian companies with g of 4–6%, depending on asset life and inflation.
  • Better: compute net reinvestment from the value-driver relationship (s06): \( \text{Net reinvestment} = \text{NOPAT}_{n+1} \times g / \text{RONIC} \); then capex = D&A + net reinvestment − ΔNWC.
  • Post-116: ROU additions ≥ ROU depreciation by the same logic.

3. NWC growing with revenue

If NWC is a stable percentage of revenue (steady-state days), then:

\[ \Delta\text{NWC}_{n+1} = \text{NWC\%} \times (\text{Revenue}_{n+1} - \text{Revenue}_n) = \text{NWC\%} \times g \times \text{Revenue}_n \]

Normalise the NWC % itself: avoid using a year-n balance sheet distorted by seasonal stock build-ups or a temporary stretch in payables. For negative-NWC businesses, ΔNWC is negative (a cash inflow) in perpetuity — legitimate if the business model sustains it, but the NWC % must be realistic (supplier terms under Sec 43B(h) MSME rules, e-commerce payment cycles).

4. Other normalisation items

  • Tax rate: marginal 25.17%; no holidays, no loss carry-forwards.
  • ESOP cost: keep in EBIT (recurring compensation).
  • Other income / treasury: excluded (assets added in bridge).
  • Exceptional items: none in terminal year.

Worked illustration

FY30 (year 5) explicit numbers vs normalised FY31 (g = 5%; WACC 12%; NWC 12% of revenue; RONIC 15%):

₹ crore FY30 explicit FY31 normalised Comment
Revenue 5,000 5,250 × 1.05
EBIT margin 19% (peak) 16% Mid-cycle
EBIT 950 840
NOPAT (25.17%) 711 (at 21% holiday tax: 750) 629 Marginal tax
D&A 220 231 Grows with asset base
Capex 180 371 D&A + net investment
ΔNWC (20) release 30 12% × 250
FCFF 810 (at 21% tax) 459

Net reinvestment in FY31 = \( 371 - 231 + 30 = 170 \), a reinvestment rate of 27% and an implied RONIC of \( 5\% / 27\% = 18.5\% \). The value-driver check at the stated RONIC of 15% requires net reinvestment of \( 629 \times 5\% / 15\% = 210 \). The analyst must choose: either accept RONIC 18.5% (justify) or raise capex to make net reinvestment ≈ 210. This is the kind of internal check a terminal column must survive.

Naive TV on FY30 FCFF: \( 810 \times 1.05 / 0.07 = 12{,}150 \). Normalised TV: \( 459 / 0.07 = 6{,}557 \) (or ₹5,986 crore with reinvestment of 210). The naive TV is overstated by more than 85%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Asset-light businesses (IT services, platforms): capex ≈ D&A may be close to correct because growth is funded by people and working capital; check reinvestment via sales-to-capital instead.
  • Long-life assets (hydro, ports): D&A on old assets is low relative to replacement cost; capex/D&A may be 2× or more.
  • Declining businesses: capex < D&A can be consistent with g < 0.
  • Land-heavy businesses: land is not depreciated; net investment in land still required for growth (new stores/plants).
  • Inflation accounting: where D&A is far below replacement cost, use normalised maintenance capex studies.

Key takeaways

  • The terminal year is capitalised at 1 ÷ (WACC − g); every distortion is magnified 15–20×.
  • Use sustainable, mid-cycle margins; strip time-bound incentives and holiday tax rates.
  • With positive g, capex must exceed D&A (and ROU additions exceed ROU depreciation post-116).
  • ΔNWC = NWC % × ΔRevenue, using a normalised NWC %.
  • Reconcile terminal reinvestment with g ÷ RONIC; justify any gap.

Practice Questions

Q1. Normalised terminal FCFF [L1]

Year-n revenue ₹3,000 crore; terminal g 5.5%; EBIT margin 14%; D&A ₹120 crore growing at g; net fixed assets ₹1,500 crore; NWC 18% of revenue; tax 25.17%. Compute terminal capex (D&A + g × net fixed assets), ΔNWC and FCFF\(_{n+1}\).

Q2. Capex = D&A trap [L2]

A model's terminal year has capex equal to D&A and ΔNWC of zero while g is 6%. NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) = ₹900 crore, WACC 12%. Compute the TV as modelled, the RONIC this implicitly assumes, and the TV if RONIC is 15%.

💡 Hint: If net reinvestment is zero, what return on new capital is required to deliver 6% growth?

Q3. Cyclical normalisation [L3]

A steel company's FY30E EBITDA margin is 23% with capacity fully utilised; its 12-year margin history ranges from 9% to 27% with a median of 16%. Industry consolidation and import duties are cited as reasons for structurally higher margins. Propose the terminal margin and the supporting analysis you would present, including how you would test the sensitivity of value to this choice.

Q4. Negative NWC in perpetuity [L2]

A D2C FMCG brand has NWC of −8% of revenue in FY30 because of extended supplier credit (90 days). Discuss whether to carry −8% into the terminal year, considering Sec 43B(h), supplier bargaining power and scale, and propose a normalised figure with reasoning.
