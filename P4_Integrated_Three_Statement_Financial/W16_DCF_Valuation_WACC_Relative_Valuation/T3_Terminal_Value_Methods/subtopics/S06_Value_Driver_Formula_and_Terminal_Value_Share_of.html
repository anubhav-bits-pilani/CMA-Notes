Subtopic note

Value-Driver Formula and Terminal Value Share of EV

Scope: Value-driver formula linking g, ROIC and reinvestment; TV share of EV

Why it matters

The plain Gordon formula treats growth as free: raise \( g \) and value rises. In reality growth must be paid for with reinvestment, and growth only creates value if the return on that new investment exceeds WACC. The value-driver formula (McKinsey's "key value driver" formula, also used by Damodaran) makes this explicit, linking \( g \), RONIC and reinvestment in one expression. It is the single most useful tool for testing whether a terminal value is economically coherent. Its companion diagnostic — the terminal value's share of total EV — tells reviewers how much of the valuation rests on post-forecast assumptions; in India TV shares of 70–85% are common and must be disclosed and stress-tested.

Core notes

The value-driver formula

\[ \text{TV}_n = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_{n+1} \left(1 - \dfrac{g}{\text{RONIC}}\right)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

where NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) is the first terminal-year NOPAT, \( g \) the perpetual growth rate, RONIC the return on new invested capital, and \( g / \text{RONIC} \) is the reinvestment rate required to deliver \( g \). This follows directly from \( \text{FCFF} = \text{NOPAT} \times (1 - \text{RR}) \) and \( g = \text{RR} \times \text{RONIC} \) (t1/c05).

Three regimes

RONIC vs WACC Effect of higher g on TV Interpretation
RONIC > WACC Increases TV Growth creates value (moat)
RONIC = WACC No effect: TV = NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) ÷ WACC Growth is value-neutral
RONIC < WACC Decreases TV Growth destroys value

The RONIC = WACC case is important: it is the competitive-equilibrium assumption Damodaran and McKinsey suggest for most companies in the very long run, because excess returns attract competition. Setting RONIC = WACC makes the TV insensitive to \( g \) — a useful conservative anchor. Strong franchises (Asian Paints, Pidilite, HUL) can justify RONIC > WACC in perpetuity, but even then usually below their current very high ROICs.

Note the distinction between RONIC and ROIC: existing capital can keep earning its historical high returns (e.g. a brand built long ago), while new capital earns RONIC. The formula applies RONIC only to the growth investment; NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) already reflects returns on existing capital.

Linking to reinvestment and capex

The formula's reinvestment \( \text{NOPAT}_{n+1} \times g / \text{RONIC} \) is total net reinvestment (capex − D&A + ΔNWC, plus ROU additions − ROU depreciation post-116). The terminal column (s05) should produce a net reinvestment equal to this amount; a check row should show the implied RONIC from the explicit terminal capex and ΔNWC.

TV share of EV

\[ \text{TV share} = \frac{\text{PV(TV)}}{\text{PV(Explicit FCFF)} + \text{PV(TV)}} \]

Typical ranges:

Company profile, horizon Typical TV share
Mature utility, 5-year horizon 55–70%
Industrial, 5–7 years 65–80%
High-growth consumer, 10 years 70–85%
Early-stage / loss-making > 90% (or > 100% if explicit FCFF negative)

A high TV share is not wrong in itself — it is mathematically inevitable for growing businesses. It signals where to focus: on g, RONIC, WACC and the terminal margin. A TV share above ~85% with a 5-year horizon suggests the explicit period is too short (the company is not in steady state). Disclose the share in the output box and football field footnotes.

Worked illustration

NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) = ₹1,000 crore; WACC 11.5%; g 5.5%.

RONIC Reinvestment rate g/RONIC FCFF\(_{n+1}\) TV
11.5% (= WACC) 47.8% 522 8,696
15% 36.7% 633 10,556
25% 22.0% 780 13,000
Plain Gordon with RR = 10% (implies RONIC 55%) 900 15,000

At RONIC = WACC, TV = 1,000 ÷ 0.115 = 8,696 regardless of g. The plain Gordon with a 10% reinvestment rate implicitly assumes RONIC of 55% forever.

TV share: if PV of explicit FCFF (10 years) is ₹3,100 crore and PV(TV at RONIC 15%) = 10,556 ÷ 1.115\(^{10}\) = 10,556 ÷ 2.970 = ₹3,554 crore (year-end discounting), TV share = 3,554 ÷ 6,654 = 53%.

Excel implementation

DCF!P50  =NOPAT_n1*(1-Term_g/Term_RONIC)/(WACC-Term_g)   ' value-driver TV
DCF!P51  =Term_g/((Capex_n1-DA_n1+dNWC_n1)/NOPAT_n1)      ' implied RONIC from terminal column
DCF!P52  =PV_TV/(PV_TV+SUM(PV_FCFF))                       ' TV share of EV

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • RONIC < g: reinvestment rate > 100%, FCFF negative in perpetuity — not a steady state.
  • Very high RONIC (asset-light, negative NWC): reinvestment small; ensure RONIC is defensible against competitive dynamics.
  • Post-116: invested capital includes ROU assets; RONIC should be measured on lease-inclusive capital.
  • Inflation and RONIC: nominal RONIC on new investment, consistent with nominal g and WACC.
  • Negative explicit FCFF makes TV share > 100% — present it but explain.

Key takeaways

  • TV = NOPAT\(_{n+1}\)(1 − g ÷ RONIC) ÷ (WACC − g): growth requires reinvestment at rate g ÷ RONIC.
  • Growth adds value only when RONIC > WACC; at RONIC = WACC, TV = NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) ÷ WACC.
  • Check that terminal capex and NWC imply the stated RONIC.
  • Report TV share of EV; very high shares on short horizons signal a non-steady-state exit.
  • A plain Gordon with low reinvestment silently assumes extreme RONIC.

Practice Questions

Q1. Value-driver TV [L1]

NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) ₹640 crore; WACC 12%; g 5%; RONIC 16%. Compute the reinvestment rate, FCFF\(_{n+1}\) and TV.

Q2. RONIC sensitivity [L2]

For NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) ₹2,000 crore, WACC 11%, compute TV for g ∈ {4%, 6%} × RONIC ∈ {11%, 14%, 20%}. Present the 2×3 grid and interpret the pattern.

💡 Hint: At RONIC equal to WACC the two g columns should give the same value.

Q3. TV share diagnosis [L2]

A 5-year DCF of a fast-growing NBFC-adjacent fintech shows PV of explicit FCFF of −₹450 crore and PV(TV) of ₹6,200 crore. Compute the TV share and discuss what it implies for the horizon, the method and the presentation to an investment committee.

Q4. Moat argument [L3]

A buy-side analyst argues that a leading Indian paints company deserves RONIC of 40% in perpetuity because its current ROIC is 45%. Critique this, distinguishing ROIC on existing capital from RONIC, and propose a defensible RONIC range with reasoning about competitive entry (new large-capacity entrants in Indian decorative paints).
