Subtopic note

Gordon Growth Terminal Value on Normalised FCFF

Scope: Gordon Growth on normalised terminal-year FCFF

Why it matters

In most Indian DCFs the terminal value (TV) is 55–80% of enterprise value; for high-growth consumer and specialty businesses it can exceed 85%. The Gordon Growth (perpetuity growth) method is the intrinsic way to compute it — it values all cash flows after the explicit period as a growing perpetuity, based only on the company's own economics and the discount rate. Its weakness is sensitivity: the denominator \( \text{WACC} - g \) is small, so the cash flow it capitalises must be right. The most common error in practice is not the formula but the input: applying Gordon to a final-year FCFF that is distorted by a capex peak, a working-capital swing, a tax holiday or a cyclical margin. "Normalised terminal-year FCFF" is the discipline that prevents this.

Core notes

The formula

\[ \text{TV}_n = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{n+1}}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_n^{\text{norm}} \times (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

where \( \text{TV}_n \) is the value at the end of year \( n \) (the last explicit year) of all FCFFs from year \( n+1 \) onward, \( g \) is the constant perpetual growth rate, and \( \text{FCFF}_n^{\text{norm}} \) is a normalised steady-state cash flow for year \( n \). Conditions: \( \text{WACC} > g \); the firm is in steady state from \( n+1 \); WACC is constant.

Derivation: the PV at time \( n \) of a cash flow stream \( C(1+g)^{k-1} \) received at \( n + k \) is a geometric series summing to \( C/(\text{WACC} - g) \) when \( g < \text{WACC} \).

Why normalise

The last explicit-year FCFF is rarely a steady-state number. Typical distortions:

Distortion in year n Effect if not normalised Normalisation
Growth capex for a plant commissioning in year n+1 FCFF too low → TV understated Capex = maintenance + growth capex consistent with g
Capex below D&A (end of a capex cycle) FCFF too high → TV overstated Capex ≥ D&A, reinvestment = g ÷ RONIC × NOPAT
Large NWC release (inventory destocking) FCFF too high ΔNWC = NWC% × ΔRevenue at g
Tax rate below 25.17% (holiday, MAT credit) FCFF too high Tax at marginal 25.17%
Cyclical peak/trough margin TV distorted Mid-cycle margin
PLI incentive in EBIT Perpetuates a time-bound subsidy Remove after scheme ends
Explicit-year growth ≠ g FCFF_n × (1 + g) mismatched Build a separate normalised "year n+1" column

Building the terminal-year column

Best practice is a dedicated "Terminal year (n+1)" column on the DCF sheet rather than multiplying the last explicit FCFF by \( (1 + g) \):

  1. Revenue\(_{n+1}\) = Revenue\(_n\) × (1 + g).
  2. EBIT margin = sustainable steady-state margin.
  3. Tax at the marginal rate (25.17%).
  4. Reinvestment = NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) × g ÷ RONIC (value-driver, s06), or capex and D&A set so capex ≥ D&A, with ΔNWC = NWC % of revenue × ΔRevenue.
  5. FCFF\(_{n+1}\) = NOPAT\(_{n+1}\) − net reinvestment.
  6. TV\(_n\) = FCFF\(_{n+1}\) ÷ (WACC − g).

This makes every steady-state assumption visible and auditable, and it allows the implied multiple (s04) and TV share of EV (s06) to be computed transparently.

Worked illustration

Specialty chemicals company, FY35 (year n = 10) explicit FCFF ₹520 crore, but FY35 includes ₹180 crore of growth capex for a plant that would only be justified by high growth. Terminal assumptions: g = 5.5%, WACC = 11.5%, RONIC = 14%.

FY36 normalised: revenue ₹8,440 crore (FY35 ₹8,000 crore × 1.055); EBIT margin 16% → EBIT ₹1,350 crore; NOPAT = 1,350 × 0.7483 = ₹1,010 crore.

\[ \text{Reinvestment} = 1{,}010 \times \frac{5.5\%}{14\%} = 397; \quad \text{FCFF}_{n+1} = 1{,}010 - 397 = 613 \]

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{FY35}} = \frac{613}{0.115 - 0.055} = 10{,}217 \]

Naively using \( 520 \times 1.055 = 548.6 \) would give TV of ₹9,143 crore — ₹1,074 crore lower, because a temporary capex bulge was capitalised in perpetuity. Conversely, if FY35 had capex below D&A, the naive TV would be overstated.

Excel implementation

DCF!P8   =O8*(1+Term_g)                         ' Revenue n+1
DCF!P10  =P8*Term_EBIT_Margin                   ' EBIT n+1
DCF!P12  =P10*(1-Tax_Marginal)                  ' NOPAT n+1
DCF!P16  =P12*Term_g/Term_RONIC                 ' Net reinvestment
DCF!P18  =P12-P16                               ' FCFF n+1
DCF!P20  =IF(WACC>Term_g,P18/(WACC-Term_g),NA()) ' TV at end of year n

The IF(... NA()) guard prevents a meaningless negative or infinite TV appearing in sensitivity tables (t5 masks these as "n.m.").

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative terminal FCFF (still investing heavily): the company is not in steady state; extend the horizon.
  • Cash-rich, low-reinvestment firms: if RONIC is very high (asset-light IT/FMCG), reinvestment is small and FCFF ≈ NOPAT; ensure the RONIC assumption is defensible over decades.
  • Finite-life businesses (concessions, mines): Gordon is inappropriate; use explicit flows to end of life plus salvage.
  • Changing WACC in perpetuity (de-leveraging): use the long-run target WACC in the TV denominator.
  • Lease basis: post-116 terminal FCFF must deduct steady-state ROU additions; pre-116 deducts rent growing at g.

Key takeaways

  • TV_Gordon = FCFF\(_{n+1}\) ÷ (WACC − g), valid only for steady state with WACC > g.
  • Capitalise a normalised year-n+1 cash flow, not a raw final-year number.
  • Build a dedicated terminal-year column: revenue × (1 + g), steady margin, marginal tax, reinvestment consistent with g and RONIC.
  • Normalise capex, NWC, tax, cyclical margins and time-bound incentives.
  • Guard against WACC ≤ g in formulas.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic Gordon TV [L1]

Final explicit-year FCFF ₹340 crore, already in steady state; g = 5%; WACC = 12%. Compute TV at the end of year n and its PV if n = 5 (year-end discounting).

Q2. Normalisation [L2]

FY30 (year 5) data, ₹ crore: revenue 4,000; EBIT margin 14% (cyclical peak; 10-year average 11%); D&A 160; capex 110; ΔNWC −40 (one-off destocking); tax 18% (last year of an incentive). Terminal g 5%, WACC 11.8%, NWC 15% of revenue. Construct a normalised FY31 FCFF (state each assumption) and compute TV. Compare with the naive TV on FY30 FCFF × 1.05.

💡 Hint: Set capex at least equal to D&A grown for inflation, compute ΔNWC from the revenue increase, use mid-cycle margin and the 25.17% rate.

Q3. Excel guard and audit [L2]

Write the Excel formula for a Gordon TV cell that returns an error flag if WACC ≤ g or if terminal FCFF is negative, and describe two checks you would add to the checks sheet linked to this cell.

Q4. Interview: why normalise? [L1]

In under 120 words, explain to an interviewer why "FCFF of the last forecast year × (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g)" can be wrong even when the formula is correct.
