Discounting Terminal Value under the Mid-Year Convention

Scope: TV discounting under mid-year convention

Why it matters

Once the explicit-period FCFFs are discounted using mid-year exponents (t1/s05, t1/c04), the terminal value must be discounted with an exponent that is consistent with what the TV represents. This is one of the most frequently botched mechanics in DCF models and assessment papers: some analysts discount a Gordon TV at \( n - 0.5 \) because "we're using mid-year", others discount an exit-multiple TV at \( n - 0.5 \) too, and others discount everything at \( n \). The difference is a factor of \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \) — about 5–6% of TV, or 3–5% of EV given TV shares of 60–85%. In a fairness opinion or a sell-side target price, that is material. The rule follows from timing logic, not convention.

Core notes

What each TV represents in time

Exit-multiple TV. An EV/EBITDA multiple applied to year-n EBITDA estimates what the business could be sold for on the last day of year n (a point-in-time transaction value). It is discounted at the full \( n \) years (or \( s + n - 1 \) with a stub — i.e. the year-end exponent of the final explicit year):

\[ \text{PV}(\text{TV}_{\text{Exit}}) = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Exit}}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n}} \]

Gordon TV — the subtle case. The Gordon formula \( \text{FCFF}_{n+1} / (\text{WACC} - g) \) gives the value, one period before the first cash flow, of a perpetuity of flows spaced one year apart. Under the mid-year convention, the first terminal flow FCFF\(_{n+1}\) is assumed to arrive at \( n + 0.5 \), the next at \( n + 1.5 \), and so on. One period before the first flow is \( n - 0.5 \). Therefore:

\[ \text{PV}(\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}) = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n - 0.5}} \]

This is the treatment stated in the brief and used by most practitioners (e.g. common bank and Big Four templates): the Gordon TV built on mid-year flows is discounted at \( n - 0.5 \), the same exponent as the final explicit-year FCFF.

Equivalent formulation. Some analysts discount the Gordon TV at \( n \) and multiply by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \):

\[ \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} \times (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n}} = \frac{\text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}}}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{n - 0.5}} \]

The two are algebraically identical. The second makes the logic explicit: \( \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} \times (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \) is the value at the end of year n of a perpetuity of mid-year flows.

Why not discount the exit TV at n − 0.5?

The exit multiple is calibrated on market EVs, which are values at a point in time (today's price ÷ today's LTM EBITDA). Applied at year n, it gives a value at the end of year n — no mid-year shift. Discounting it at \( n - 0.5 \) inflates PV by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \).

Comparing the two TVs consistently

To compare a Gordon TV and an exit TV (for implied g or implied multiple, s04), express both at the same date — the end of year n:

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}, \text{at } n} = \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} \times (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \]

Then implied multiple = \( \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}, \text{at } n} / \text{EBITDA}_n \) and implied g is solved from the exit TV against the mid-year-adjusted Gordon form. Many templates skip this adjustment; the error is small (≈ 5–6%) but is precisely the sort of inconsistency assessors look for.

With a stub period

With stub fraction \( s \), explicit mid-year exponents are \( s/2, s + 0.5, s + 1.5, \dots \); the final explicit year \( n \) (counting the stub as year 1) has mid-year exponent \( s + n - 1.5 \) and year-end exponent \( s + n - 1 \). Hence:

TV method Exponent
Exit multiple (point-in-time at end of final year) \( s + n - 1 \)
Gordon on mid-year flows \( s + n - 1.5 \)

The Excel date-based approach handles this automatically: use the final explicit column's year-end exponent for exit TV, and its mid-year exponent for Gordon TV.

Worked illustration

Valuation date 31 March 2025 (no stub); n = 5; WACC 12%; FCFF\(_6\) = ₹600 crore; g = 5.5%; EBITDA\(_5\) = ₹1,150 crore; exit multiple 9×.

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Gordon}} = \frac{600}{0.065} = 9{,}231; \quad \text{PV} = \frac{9{,}231}{1.12^{4.5}} = \frac{9{,}231}{1.6653} = 5{,}543 \]

\[ \text{TV}_{\text{Exit}} = 1{,}150 \times 9 = 10{,}350; \quad \text{PV} = \frac{10{,}350}{1.12^{5}} = \frac{10{,}350}{1.7623} = 5{,}873 \]

Gordon TV at end of year 5 (for comparison) = \( 9{,}231 \times 1.12^{0.5} = 9{,}769 \); implied multiple = 9,769 ÷ 1,150 = 8.5×, close to the 9× exit multiple. Had the exit TV been discounted at 4.5 years, its PV would be ₹6,215 crore — 5.8% too high.

Excel implementation

DCF!C70  =TV_Gordon/(1+WACC)^INDEX(MidYear_Exp_Row,n)     ' Gordon on mid-year flows
DCF!C71  =TV_Exit/(1+WACC)^INDEX(YearEnd_Exp_Row,n)        ' Exit multiple at year-end
DCF!C72  =TV_Gordon*(1+WACC)^0.5/EBITDA_n                  ' implied multiple at same date
DCF!C73  =IF(MidYear=1,C70,TV_Gordon/(1+WACC)^INDEX(YearEnd_Exp_Row,n))   ' toggle-aware

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Year-end convention throughout: both TVs discounted at \( n \); no adjustment.
  • Forward exit multiple (× EBITDA\(_{n+1}\)): still a point-in-time value at end of year n; discount at \( n \).
  • Some practitioners discount the Gordon TV at \( n \) even under mid-year (treating the perpetuity as year-end flows from n+1). This is conservative and internally inconsistent with mid-year explicit flows; if a client template does this, disclose it.
  • Quarterly models: the same logic applies with quarter-mid exponents.
  • Sensitivity grids (t5) must flex the same exponent row, so the TV exponent should be a formula, not a typed number.

Key takeaways

  • Exit-multiple TV is a point-in-time value at end of year n: discount at \( n \) (or \( s + n - 1 \) with a stub).
  • Gordon TV on mid-year flows is a value at \( n - 0.5 \): discount at \( n - 0.5 \), equivalently at \( n \) after multiplying by \( (1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5} \).
  • Compare the two TVs only after moving them to the same date.
  • Drive TV exponents from the same date-based rows as the explicit flows.
  • Misapplying the half-year shift misstates TV by about 5–6%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Exponents with a stub [L1]

Valuation date 30 September 2025; explicit forecast FY26 (stub) to FY30. Compute the stub fraction and the exponents used for (a) the FY30 FCFF, (b) a Gordon TV on mid-year flows, (c) an exit-multiple TV.

Q2. PV of both TVs [L2]

n = 5, no stub, WACC 11.5%, FCFF\(_6\) ₹420 crore, g 5%, EBITDA\(_5\) ₹900 crore, exit multiple 10×. Compute both TVs, their PVs under correct mid-year treatment, the Gordon-implied multiple at the end of year 5, and the implied g of the exit TV.

💡 Hint: Move the Gordon TV to the end of year 5 before computing the implied multiple; use FCFF\(_5\) = FCFF\(_6\) ÷ 1.05 in the implied-g formula.

Q3. Template critique [L2]

A client template discounts every TV at \( n - 0.5 \) when the mid-year toggle is on. Explain which TV is mis-discounted, the direction and approximate size of the error at a 12% WACC, and the formula change required.

Q4. Interview question [L1]

"Why is the exit-multiple TV discounted for a full n years when the explicit cash flows are discounted at mid-year?" Answer in under 100 words.
