Selecting the Perpetuity Growth Rate in INR

Scope: Perpetuity growth selection: below long-run nominal GDP; Damodaran cap g ≤ Rf; INR inflation-plus-real-growth logic

Why it matters

The perpetuity growth rate \( g \) is the most leveraged assumption in a DCF. At WACC 11.5%, moving \( g \) from 5% to 6% raises Gordon TV by about 18% and EV by 12–15%. Because India's nominal GDP has grown at 10–12% for decades, analysts are tempted to set \( g \) at 7–8% "because India is a growth economy". In perpetuity, that is indefensible for almost any company: no firm can grow faster than the economy forever, and long-run nominal GDP growth will itself decline as India's real growth converges and inflation stays near the RBI's 4% target. Reviewers expect a clear rationale linking \( g \) to inflation, real growth and the risk-free rate.

Core notes

Constraint 1 — below long-run nominal GDP growth

A company growing faster than the economy in perpetuity would eventually become larger than the economy. Hence:

\[ g \le g_{\text{nominal GDP, long run}} \]

and for most companies strictly below, because mature firms' growth tends to lag the economy as industries mature and new entrants take share. "Long run" matters: India's current nominal GDP growth (roughly 9–11% in FY24–FY26, depending on the deflator) is not the perpetuity rate. Over decades, real growth is expected to converge from 6–7% toward 3–4% as the economy matures and demographics shift, while inflation anchors near 4%. A long-run nominal path of roughly 7–8% averaged over the coming decades, declining further thereafter, suggests a perpetuity \( g \) for a typical company of 4–6% in INR.

Constraint 2 — Damodaran cap: g ≤ Rf

Damodaran argues that the risk-free rate is a reasonable proxy for long-run nominal growth because, in equilibrium, real interest rates ≈ real growth and both embed the same expected inflation. Thus:

\[ g \le R_f \]

In his INR framework, Rf is the G-Sec yield less the sovereign default spread (around 4.3–4.6% in 2025), which caps g at roughly 4.5%. Practitioners using the full G-Sec Rf (~6.5%) would cap g there. The cap is a ceiling, not a target — consumer companies with strong brands might sit near it; commodity or disrupted businesses below it.

Constraint 3 — INR inflation plus real growth

Build g bottom-up:

\[ 1 + g = (1 + g_{\text{real}}) \times (1 + \pi_{\text{INR}}) \]

with \( \pi_{\text{INR}} \) = long-run expected Indian CPI/WPI inflation for the company's prices (anchored by RBI's 4% CPI target; sector price inflation may differ — e.g. pharma under DPCO price controls, or technology products with deflationary prices), and \( g_{\text{real}} \) = sustainable real volume growth (0–2% for a mature company; up to 3% for under-penetrated categories with a long runway).

Example: \( \pi = 4.0\% \), \( g_{\text{real}} = 1.5\% \) → \( g = 1.04 \times 1.015 - 1 = 5.56\% \).

Consistency with other inputs

  • WACC − g spread: for an Indian WACC of 11–13%, a g of 4–6% gives a spread of 5–9%. Spreads below ~4% produce implausibly high implied multiples — check via s04.
  • Reinvestment: higher g requires higher reinvestment (s06). A g of 6% at RONIC 12% needs 50% of NOPAT reinvested; raising g without raising reinvestment is the commonest inconsistency.
  • Currency: a USD DCF of the same company uses a USD g (≈ 2–3%), lower by the inflation differential.
  • Lease and capex basis: g applies to the full FCFF, including ROU additions in post-116.

Selection grid (illustrative INR)

Company type Typical INR perpetuity g Rationale
Commodity / cyclical (steel, cement, chemicals intermediates) 3.5–5.0% Price-taking; inflation pass-through partial
Mature consumer staples, paints, adhesives 5.0–6.0% Brand pricing power; under-penetrated rural demand
Regulated utilities 3.5–5.0% Capped returns; growth tied to capacity additions
Technology services (INR reporting, USD revenue) 4.0–5.5% USD real growth + rupee depreciation
Disrupted / declining (legacy media, thermal) 0–3% Real decline offset by inflation

Worked illustration

Paints company, WACC 11.2%. Inputs: inflation 4.0%, real growth 1.8% → g = 5.87%. Damodaran Rf = 4.4% → cap suggests g ≤ 4.4%. Full G-Sec = 6.5%.

The analyst chooses 5.5%, justifying above the Damodaran cap because the WACC uses the local-market Rf (6.5%) convention and paints in India are under-penetrated (per-capita consumption well below global averages). The implied EV/EBITDA (s04) is then checked against long-run mature-market multiples. Had the analyst picked 8% ("India nominal GDP"), the spread \( 11.2\% - 8\% = 3.2\% \) would imply a TV multiple of 31× FCFF\(_{n+1}\).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Very high WACC companies (small caps, Ke 16%): a higher g in absolute terms may still be modest relative to WACC; focus on reinvestment consistency.
  • Negative g is legitimate for declining businesses (but TV formula still requires WACC > g).
  • Hyper-growth sector with long runway (e.g. organised retail at <20% penetration): extend the explicit/fade period rather than raising g.
  • Deflationary products (electronics, solar modules): use nominal price deflation in π for the company, not CPI.
  • Interaction with the Rf choice: use the same convention (stripped or full G-Sec) when applying the g ≤ Rf cap as was used in Ke.

Key takeaways

  • Perpetuity g must be below long-run nominal GDP growth, not today's 10–11%.
  • Damodaran's ceiling: g ≤ Rf (stripped INR Rf ≈ 4.4% in 2025) — a cap, not a target.
  • Build g as (1 + real growth) × (1 + INR inflation) − 1; typical INR range 4–6%.
  • Higher g must be funded by higher reinvestment at a stated RONIC.
  • Check the implied multiple; extend the horizon rather than inflating g.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build g [L1]

Long-run expected CPI inflation 4.2%; sustainable real volume growth 1.2%; Damodaran INR Rf 4.5%; full G-Sec 6.6%. Compute the bottom-up g and state whether it complies with the Damodaran cap under each Rf convention.

Q2. TV sensitivity [L2]

FCFF\(_{n+1}\) ₹800 crore; WACC 11.5%. Compute Gordon TV for g = 4%, 5%, 6%, 7% and 8%, and the percentage change between each step. Comment on the shape of the curve.

💡 Hint: TV = FCFF ÷ (WACC − g); the percentage changes rise as the spread narrows.

Q3. Defend g to an investment committee [L3]

A board member argues: "India will grow 10% nominal for the next 25 years; your 5.5% terminal growth undervalues our FMCG company." Prepare a structured response covering long-run GDP convergence, the meaning of perpetuity, the reinvestment cost of growth and the role of the explicit/fade period.

Q4. USD vs INR g [L2]

A US-based analyst values an Indian IT services company in USD using g = 3%. Its INR valuation uses g = 5.5%. With inflation of 4.5% (India) and 2.3% (US), check whether the two growth rates are consistent and explain any adjustment needed.
