Refresh before starting

Time value of money and perpetuities

Time value of money: discount factors, perpetuity and growing-perpetuity formulas

  • Discount factor: \( \text{DF}_t = 1 / (1 + r)^t \); with mid-year convention, exponent \( t - 0.5 \); with stub \( s \), exponents \( s/2, s + 0.5, s + 1.5, \dots \)
  • Level perpetuity (first payment in one period): \( \text{PV} = C / r \).
  • Growing perpetuity: \( \text{PV} = C_1 / (r - g) \), valid only if \( r > g \); value sits one period before \( C_1 \).
  • Annuity: \( \text{PV} = C \times [1 - (1 + r)^{-n}] / r \); growing annuity: \( \text{PV} = \frac{C_1}{r - g}\left[1 - \left(\frac{1 + g}{1 + r}\right)^n\right] \).
  • Effective annual rate from semi-annual yield: \( (1 + y/2)^2 - 1 \) (G-Sec quotes).
  • Fisher relation: \( (1 + i) = (1 + r_{\text{real}})(1 + \pi) \).

CAPM, beta and portfolio theory

CAPM, beta and portfolio theory (CMA Strategic Financial Management)

  • Systematic vs unsystematic risk; diversification eliminates only the latter.
  • CAPM: \( K_e = R_f + \beta (R_m - R_f) \); security market line; beta = Cov\((R_i, R_m)\) ÷ Var\((R_m)\).
  • Capital market line vs security market line; Sharpe ratio; efficient frontier.
  • Modigliani–Miller with taxes: levered firm value = unlevered value + PV of tax shields; equity beta rises with leverage (Hamada).
  • Limitations: single-factor model, unobservable market portfolio, unstable betas; arbitrage pricing theory and multi-factor models as alternatives.

Ind AS 116, 110, 28 and 33 effects on EV and share count

Ind AS 116, 110, 28 and 33 effects on EV and share count

  • Ind AS 116: lease liabilities (current + non-current) are debt-like on a post-116 basis; EBITDA excludes fixed rent; ROU depreciation and lease interest replace rent.
  • Ind AS 110: consolidation includes 100% of subsidiaries' EBITDA and debt; the NCI share of equity must be deducted in the bridge (and added to EV in comps).
  • Ind AS 28: associates/JVs are equity-accounted — their revenue/EBITDA are not in consolidated EBITDA, so their value is added in the bridge (and deducted from EV in comps).
  • Ind AS 33: diluted EPS uses treasury stock method for options/warrants and if-converted for convertibles; anti-dilutive instruments excluded. Valuation share counts use similar mechanics at the current or implied price.
  • Ind AS 102: ESOP expense in employee costs; outstanding options drive dilution.

Excel functions, data tables and charts

Excel SLOPE, COVARIANCE.S/VAR.S, data tables and stacked bar charts

  • =SLOPE(known_y's, known_x's) — stock returns first; =COVARIANCE.S(y,x)/VAR.S(x) as a check; RSQ, LINEST(...,TRUE,TRUE) for statistics.
  • =YIELD(...) for NCD YTM; =YEARFRAC(start,end,1) for stub fractions.
  • What-If Analysis → Data Table: row input cell for the variable across the top, column input cell for the variable down the side; both inputs on the same sheet as the table; {=TABLE(row,col)} cannot be edited cell-by-cell.
  • Calculation options: "Automatic except for data tables"; F9 to recalculate.
  • Stacked bar chart: an invisible first series (no fill) plus a visible second series creates floating bars; add a CMP line via a scatter series or error bar.

Cross-cutting concepts

The consistency triad

Consistency triad: FCFF ↔ WACC ↔ EV; FCFE ↔ Ke ↔ Equity value

Every DCF must match its cash flow to its discount rate and to the value it produces. FCFF is the cash available to all capital providers before financing flows; it is discounted at WACC, which blends the returns required by lenders (after tax) and shareholders, and it yields enterprise value — the value of the operating assets. To reach equity value, the bridge adds non-operating assets and subtracts every non-equity claim. FCFE is cash after interest, repayments and new borrowings; it belongs to shareholders alone and is discounted at Ke to give equity value directly. Mixing pairs is a fundamental error: FCFF at Ke undervalues (ignores cheaper debt financing); FCFE at WACC overvalues (applies a debt-blended rate to a residual equity stream); deducting debt from an FCFE-derived value double-counts it. The triad also governs tax shields: in FCFF they sit in WACC via \( K_d(1 - t) \); in FCFE they are in the cash flow. In this week's model, FCFF/WACC/EV is primary, with an FCFE memo row as a cross-check of the debt plan. Check every input sheet against the triad before presenting a value.

Currency and inflation consistency

Currency/inflation consistency: INR cash flows with INR Rf and India ERP

Discount rates embed expected inflation. INR cash flows — forecast with Indian price inflation of about 4–5% — must be discounted at an INR rate: Rf from the 10-year G-Sec (full yield, or stripped of the sovereign default spread under Damodaran's approach), an ERP consistent with that Rf, and INR cost of debt from Indian rating spreads. Using a US Treasury yield or a USD WACC with INR flows understates the discount rate by the inflation differential (≈ 2–2.5 points) and can inflate EV by a quarter or more. The same logic applies to terminal growth: an INR g of 5–6% corresponds to a USD g of about 3%. If a parent provides a USD WACC, convert it: \( (1 + \text{WACC}_{\text{INR}}) = (1 + \text{WACC}_{\text{USD}}) \times (1 + \pi_{\text{INR}}) / (1 + \pi_{\text{USD}}) \). For USD-earning Indian companies, either forecast in USD and discount in USD, or convert USD revenues using a parity-consistent forward rate path and discount in INR. Never mix a stripped Rf with an ERP built for the full G-Sec, or vice versa.

Ind AS 116 treatment aligned across the model

Ind AS 116 treatment aligned across FCFF, WACC weights, EV bridge and comps

Choose one lease basis and apply it in four places. Post-116: FCFF excludes lease payments but deducts new ROU additions as reinvestment; WACC includes lease liabilities in debt (peer unlevering and target weights); the bridge deducts lease liabilities at the valuation date; comps use EV including lease liabilities divided by post-116 EBITDA. Pre-116: FCFF deducts fixed lease rentals; WACC excludes leases; the bridge does not deduct lease liabilities; comps use EV excluding leases and pre-116 EBITDA (reported EBITDA less lease payments). A model that mixes bases — post-116 EBITDA in FCFF with no ROU additions, or lease liabilities deducted in the bridge while FCFF already deducts rent — misstates value by 10–40% for lease-heavy companies such as Trent, DMart, ABFRL, Jubilant FoodWorks or PVR INOX. Implement a single Post116 switch referenced by every affected formula and add a check row confirming all four components point to it.

Triangulation of value

Triangulation: DCF vs trading comps vs precedents vs 52-week range, with divergences explained

No single method is "the" value. The DCF gives an intrinsic range (from the WACC × g and WACC × exit multiple grids); trading comps give a market-relative range (interquartile EV/EBITDA and P/E applied to the subject's metrics); precedent transactions give a control-inclusive range (deal multiples, typically 20–40% above trading); the 52-week high/low shows where the market has actually priced the stock. The football field places these bars side by side with the CMP line. The analyst's job is to explain divergences, not hide them: a DCF below trading comps may mean the market prices a longer growth runway than the DCF's horizon (or the sector is overvalued); precedents above both reflect control premiums and synergies; a CMP outside the DCF range may indicate mispricing or a missed driver. State which method you weight and why — e.g. DCF primary for a stable industrial, comps primary for a peer-rich sector with volatile cash flows.

Exercise integration with Project 4

Exercise: DCF and WACC tabs, two 2D grids, 5-peer comps and football field, all live to Project 4 scenario switch

Project 5 is not a separate workbook: the new tabs sit inside the Project 4 model and must remain live to its scenario switch (Base/Bull/Bear). Every FCFF line links to the integrated statements in the same column; WACC, g and exit multiple are named inputs on the DCF sheet (data tables require their input cells on the same sheet); the two 2D grids (WACC × g; WACC × exit multiple) are built with What-If Data Tables whose centre cells equal the model output; the comps sheet holds five NSE peers with EV built identically to the DCF bridge; the football field chart pulls its bars from the DCF grids, comps quartiles, precedent range and 52-week high/low. Switching the scenario should change FCFF, EV, the implied share price, the grids (after F9) and the football field's DCF bar — with no manual steps except recalculation of the tables. A checks sheet confirms each link.

Saturday assessment paper

Assessment: Saturday 3-Hour Valuation Case: Compute WACC, 5-year FCFF, Terminal Value, and Implied Share Price for an Indian manufacturing company and defend terminal assumptions.

Instructions: Time 3 hours. Total 100 marks. Use Excel; submit the workbook and a written note (max 2 pages) for Q7. State all assumptions. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Tax 25.17% throughout.

Case company — Deccan Precision Components Ltd (DPCL): NSE-listed auto-component manufacturer. Valuation date 30 September 2025. CMP ₹1,240. Basic shares 12.0 crore.

FY25 actuals (31 March 2025): revenue 4,500; EBITDA 675; D&A 180; other income 40 (interest on FDs); finance cost 70 (incl. lease interest 10); NWC 720.

Forecast drivers (FY26–FY30): revenue growth 12%, 12%, 11%, 10%, 9%; EBITDA margin 15.0%, 15.5%, 16.0%, 16.0%, 16.0%; D&A 4% of revenue; capex 300, 320, 280, 260, 250; NWC 16% of revenue. H1 FY26 actual FCFF: 95.

Market data: 10-year G-Sec 6.50%; Damodaran India default spread 2.10%; mature ERP 4.33%; India CRP 2.92%. Peers (levered β, market D/E incl. leases, cash/firm value): A (1.10, 0.20, 0.03), B (0.95, 0.10, 0.08), C (1.25, 0.40, 0.02), D (1.00, 0.15, 0.05), E (1.40, 0.70, 0.01). DPCL target D/(D+E) 15%. Normalised EBIT/interest 6.0×; spread table: AA 1.40%, A+ 1.90%, A 2.60%.

Balance sheet at 30 September 2025: cash and liquid MFs 310 (of which unpaid dividend account 4, margin money 12); borrowings 520; lease liabilities 110; NCI 45; investment in associate (book) 60, market value of stake 140; unfunded gratuity 18. ESOPs: 0.30 crore options at ₹800 strike and 0.20 crore at ₹1,400 strike.

Q1. WACC (20 marks) [L2]

Compute the Damodaran-consistent Rf and India ERP; unlever, cash-adjust and take the median of peer betas; relever at the target structure; derive Kd by synthetic rating; compute WACC. Present a source/assumption column.

Q2. FCFF and stub (20 marks) [L2]

Build FY26–FY30 FCFF (EBIT excluding other income, NOPAT at 25.17%, D&A, capex, ΔNWC). Compute the actual-based stub FCFF for H2 FY26 and the mid-year discount exponents from the valuation date. Compute PV of explicit FCFF.

💡 Hint: The stub fraction is 182 days ÷ 365; FY27 is discounted at s + 0.5.

Q3. Terminal value (15 marks) [L2]

Compute (a) Gordon TV at g = 5% on a normalised FY31 FCFF (capex at least 1.2× D&A, ΔNWC on 16% NWC) and (b) exit TV at 11× FY30 EBITDA. Discount each with the correct exponent. Compute the implied g of the exit TV and the implied multiple of the Gordon TV at the same date.

Q4. EV to equity bridge and diluted price (15 marks) [L2]

Compute EV under each TV method; bridge to equity value (state which cash is excluded and why, and how the associate and gratuity are treated); compute diluted shares using the treasury stock method at the implied price (iterate once); compute implied share price and upside vs CMP.

Q5. Sensitivity (10 marks) [L2]

Build a WACC (base ± 100 bp, 50 bp steps) × g (4.0%–6.0%, 50 bp steps) data table on implied share price, with an n.m. mask and heat map. Show that the centre cell equals the model output.

Q6. Comps cross-check (10 marks) [L2]

Peers' LTM EV/EBITDA (post-116): 10.5×, 12.0×, 13.5×, 9.0×, 22.0×. Compute mean, median and interquartile range, justify any outlier exclusion, and compute DPCL's implied share price range using LTM EBITDA to 30 September 2025 (H1 FY26 EBITDA 360; H1 FY25 EBITDA 318).

Q7. Defend your terminal assumptions (10 marks) [L3]

In a written note, defend your choice of g and exit multiple against these challenges: (i) "India's nominal GDP grows at 10–11%; your 5% is too low"; (ii) "Listed auto-component leaders trade at 25–30× EBITDA; why 11×?"; (iii) "Your TV is over 70% of EV." Reference implied growth, implied multiples, the value-driver formula and the Damodaran cap.
