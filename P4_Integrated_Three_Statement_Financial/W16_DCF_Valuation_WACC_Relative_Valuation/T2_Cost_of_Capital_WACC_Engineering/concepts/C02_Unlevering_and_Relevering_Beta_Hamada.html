Key concept

Unlevering and Relevering Beta (Hamada)

Concept: βU = βL ÷ [1 + (1 − t) × D/E]; βL(target) = βU × [1 + (1 − t) × D/E(target)]

Statement

\[ \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t)\dfrac{D}{E}} \]

\[ \beta_{L,\text{target}} = \beta_U \times \left[1 + (1 - t)\frac{D}{E}_{\text{target}}\right] \]

where \( \beta_L \) is the observed levered (equity) beta, \( \beta_U \) the unlevered (asset) beta, \( t \) the marginal tax rate (25.17% for Indian domestic companies under 115BAA), \( D/E \) the market debt-to-equity ratio over the beta estimation window, and \( D/E_{\text{target}} \) the subject company's target ratio consistent with WACC weights.

Explanation

Financial leverage magnifies equity risk: with more fixed claims ahead of shareholders, a given swing in operating income produces a bigger swing in equity returns. Hamada's formula (derived from Modigliani–Miller with corporate taxes) assumes debt is risk-free (debt beta = 0) and the debt amount is fixed so tax shields are as risky as debt. The \( (1 - t) \) term reflects that the tax shield absorbs part of the leverage effect.

Why unlever and relever:

  • Peers have different leverage; their equity betas are not comparable until leverage is removed.
  • The subject's own regression beta reflects past leverage, not the target.
  • Unlisted targets have no regression beta at all.

Conditions and variants:

  • Debt beta not zero (high-yield borrowers): \( \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L + \beta_D(1 - t)D/E}{1 + (1 - t)D/E} \).
  • Harris–Pringle (debt rebalanced to constant D/V, tax shields as risky as operations): \( \beta_U = \beta_L / (1 + D/E) \).
  • Loss-making peers: \( t = 0 \).
  • Debt definition must match the FCFF basis (include leases if post-116; t2/s07).
  • Cash: adjust βU for cash/firm value (t2/s04).

Excel implementation (named ranges):

=Beta_L/(1+(1-Tax_Marginal)*DE_Peer)          ' unlever
=Beta_U_Median*(1+(1-Tax_Marginal)*DE_Target) ' relever

Illustration

Peer: βL 1.18, average market D/E 0.45, tax 25.17%.

\[ \beta_U = \frac{1.18}{1 + 0.7483 \times 0.45} = \frac{1.18}{1.3367} = 0.883 \]

Subject target D/E = 0.20:

\[ \beta_L = 0.883 \times (1 + 0.7483 \times 0.20) = 0.883 \times 1.1497 = 1.015 \]

Using the peer's raw 1.18 directly would overstate the subject's Ke by \( (1.18 - 1.015) \times 7.25\% = 1.20\% \) at a 7.25% ERP.

Practice Questions

Q1. Peer median relevered [L1]

Three peers: (βL 0.95, D/E 0.20), (βL 1.10, D/E 0.55), (βL 0.80, D/E 0.05); tax 25.17%. Compute each βU, the median βU, and relever at target D/E 0.30.

Q2. Debt beta variant [L2]

A highly leveraged infrastructure peer has βL 1.60, D/E 1.50 and an estimated debt beta of 0.25. Compute βU with and without the debt beta and discuss which you would include in the peer median.

💡 Hint: Use the debt-beta variant formula stated in the Explanation.

Q3. Tax rate choice [L2]

A peer is a loss-making new-age company with accumulated tax losses; another pays tax under the old regime at 34.94%. Explain which tax rate you would use to unlever each and why the subject should be relevered at 25.17%.
