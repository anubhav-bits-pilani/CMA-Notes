Key concept

The WACC Formula

Concept: WACC = E/(D + E) × Ke + D/(D + E) × Kd × (1 − t)

Statement

\[ \text{WACC} = \frac{E}{D + E} K_e + \frac{D}{D + E} K_d (1 - t) \]

where \( E \) is the market value of equity (diluted market capitalisation or target equity weight), \( D \) the market value of interest-bearing debt (including lease liabilities on a post-Ind AS 116 basis), \( K_e \) the cost of equity (CAPM), \( K_d \) the pre-tax marginal cost of debt, and \( t \) the marginal tax rate (25.17%). If preference capital \( P \) exists, add \( \frac{P}{D + P + E} K_p \) (no tax adjustment, since preference dividends are not deductible) and adjust the denominators.

Explanation

WACC is the blended required return of all capital providers to the firm's operating assets, and is the discount rate for FCFF. The tax shield on interest appears as \( K_d (1 - t) \) — which is why FCFF itself is computed without interest or its tax benefit (t1/s06). Key conditions:

  • Market-value target weights (t2/s06) — not book values, not momentary ratios if a different long-run target is intended.
  • Consistency of weights with beta: the D/E used to relever β must equal \( w_D / w_E \).
  • Marginal costs: Ke and Kd at today's market rates.
  • Same currency and inflation basis as the cash flows (INR flows → INR WACC; t2/c06).
  • Same debt definition as FCFF and the bridge (leases in or out).
  • Constant WACC assumes leverage stays at target; if the capital structure will change substantially, use period-specific WACC with cumulative discount factors or APV.

WACC is not a hurdle that the company "pays"; it is an opportunity cost. A low-debt company's WACC ≈ Ke. Adding debt lowers WACC only up to the point where rising Kd (lower rating) and rising βL offset the tax shield.

Indian manufacturing company, valuation date November 2025:

\[ \text{WACC} = 0.80 \times 12.16\% + 0.20 \times 6.38\% = 9.73\% + 1.28\% = 11.00\% \]

Practice Questions

Q1. WACC with preference capital [L1]

Market cap ₹8,000 crore; borrowings ₹2,500 crore; redeemable preference shares ₹500 crore with a 9.5% dividend; Ke 14.2%; Kd 9.8%; tax 25.17%. Compute WACC.

Q2. Consistency check [L2]

A WACC tab shows target D/(D+E) = 30% but relevers beta at D/E = 0.30. Recompute the correct D/E, the relevered beta (βU 0.85, tax 25.17%), Ke (Rf 4.4%, ERP 7.3%) and WACC (Kd 9.0%), and state the size of the original error.

💡 Hint: D/E = w_D ÷ w_E.

Q3. Optimal leverage interview question [L3]

"If debt is cheaper than equity, why don't companies take on 80% debt to minimise WACC?" Answer using the WACC formula, the relevering formula, rating-linked Kd and Indian practical constraints (covenants, RBI large-exposure norms for lenders).
