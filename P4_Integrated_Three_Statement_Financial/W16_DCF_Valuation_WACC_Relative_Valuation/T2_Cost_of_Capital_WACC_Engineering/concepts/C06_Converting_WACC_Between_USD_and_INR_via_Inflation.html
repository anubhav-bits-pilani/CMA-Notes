Key concept

Converting WACC Between USD and INR via Inflation Differentials

Concept: Currency conversion: (1 + WACC_INR) = (1 + WACC_USD) × (1 + π_INR) ÷ (1 + π_USD)

Statement

\[ 1 + \text{WACC}_{\text{INR}} = (1 + \text{WACC}_{\text{USD}}) \times \frac{1 + \pi_{\text{INR}}}{1 + \pi_{\text{USD}}} \]

where \( \text{WACC}_{\text{USD}} \) is the discount rate in US dollar terms, and \( \pi_{\text{INR}} \), \( \pi_{\text{USD}} \) are the expected long-run inflation rates in India and the US. The same relationship converts any rate (Rf, Ke, Kd) between currencies.

Explanation

Discount rates are currency-specific because they embed expected inflation. If Indian inflation runs above US inflation, INR nominal rates must be higher by the inflation differential (relative purchasing power parity / international Fisher effect). The multiplicative form is exact; the additive approximation (\( \text{WACC}_{\text{INR}} \approx \text{WACC}_{\text{USD}} + \pi_{\text{INR}} - \pi_{\text{USD}} \)) is close for small differentials.

When to use it:

  • A USD-based analysis (e.g. a global parent using a USD WACC from US Treasury + US ERP + Indian CRP) needs converting for INR cash flows.
  • Cross-checking an INR WACC built from G-Sec inputs.
  • Valuing USD-earning Indian companies (IT services, pharma exports): either forecast in USD and discount at USD WACC, then convert at spot; or forecast in INR (with rupee depreciation consistent with the inflation differential) and discount at INR WACC. Both must give the same answer if forex forecasts follow parity.

Inputs for India and the US:

  • π_INR: RBI's flexible inflation-targeting framework sets a CPI target of 4% (band 2–6%), reaffirmed for 2021–26; long-run expectations from RBI surveys and professional forecasters are about 4–5%.
  • π_USD: Fed target 2%; market breakevens (TIPS) about 2.2–2.5% (date-sensitive).

The key failure this rule prevents: discounting INR cash flows (which embed ~4–5% Indian inflation) at a USD rate (which embeds ~2–2.5% US inflation). That overstates value by roughly the capitalised inflation gap — a 2–2.5 percentage-point shortfall in the discount rate, which can inflate EV by 25–40% for a long-duration business.

Illustration

A US-listed parent's USD WACC for its Indian subsidiary: 9.20%. Expected inflation: India 4.5%, US 2.3%.

\[ 1 + \text{WACC}_{\text{INR}} = 1.0920 \times \frac{1.045}{1.023} = 1.0920 \times 1.02151 = 1.11549 \]

\[ \text{WACC}_{\text{INR}} = 11.55\% \]

Additive approximation: \( 9.20\% + 2.2\% = 11.40\% \) — 15 bp lower. Compare with the subsidiary's bottom-up INR WACC built from G-Sec inputs (say 11.3–11.8%) to check consistency.

The same logic gives implied INR depreciation: expected INR/USD rises by about \( 1.045/1.023 - 1 = 2.15\% \) per year. A USD-revenue forecast converted to INR at a flat exchange rate while discounting at INR WACC would understate value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Convert a USD Ke [L1]

USD Ke for an Indian pharma exporter is 10.4%; expected inflation India 4.2%, US 2.1%. Compute INR Ke using the exact and the additive formula.

Q2. Two-currency DCF consistency [L2]

An IT services company earns all revenue in USD. Year-1 to Year-3 FCFF forecasts are USD 120m, 132m, 145m; USD WACC 9.0%; spot ₹88.0/USD; inflation India 4.5%, US 2.3%. Set out the two equivalent valuation routes (USD DCF converted at spot; INR DCF with parity-consistent exchange-rate path and INR WACC) and explain why they converge.

💡 Hint: Project the INR/USD rate using the inflation differential and convert WACC using the stated formula.

Q3. Review finding [L2]

A valuation report for a domestic Indian FMCG company uses Rf = 4.1% (US 10-year Treasury), ERP = 7.3% (Damodaran India) and INR cash flows growing at 10% nominal. Identify the inconsistency, estimate the corrected Rf using an inflation differential of 2.3%, and state the direction of the valuation error.
