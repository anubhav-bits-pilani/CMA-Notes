Key concept

Raw Beta with SLOPE and the Blume Adjustment

Concept: Raw β: =SLOPE(Stock_Returns,Nifty_Returns); Blume β = 0.67 × raw β + 0.33

Statement

\[ \beta_{\text{raw}} = \frac{\text{Cov}(R_{\text{stock}}, R_{\text{Nifty}})}{\text{Var}(R_{\text{Nifty}})} \]

implemented in Excel as:

=SLOPE(Stock_Returns,Nifty_Returns)
=COVARIANCE.S(Stock_Returns,Nifty_Returns)/VAR.S(Nifty_Returns)   ' identical check

\[ \beta_{\text{Blume}} = 0.67 \times \beta_{\text{raw}} + 0.33 \]

where Stock_Returns and Nifty_Returns are aligned periodic (weekly or monthly) return ranges of equal length over the same dates; the weights 0.67 and 0.33 (more precisely 2/3 and 1/3) shrink the estimate toward the market beta of 1.0.

Explanation

SLOPE(known_y's, known_x's) returns the OLS slope of y on x — exactly the market-model beta when y is the stock's returns and x the index's. Argument order matters: SLOPE(Nifty, Stock) returns \( \text{Cov}/\text{Var}(\text{stock}) \), which is not beta. The COVARIANCE.S/VAR.S pair gives the same answer (sample vs population versions cancel provided both are sample or both population — mixing COVARIANCE.P with VAR.S introduces an \( (n-1)/n \) error).

Supporting functions:

  • RSQ(y, x) — proportion of variance explained (typical Indian large-caps: 0.2–0.5).
  • LINEST(y, x, TRUE, TRUE) — returns slope, intercept and their standard errors; INDEX(LINEST(...),2,1) gives SE(β).
  • INTERCEPT(y, x) — alpha (Jensen's alpha if returns are excess returns).

Blume's adjustment (1971) is based on the empirical tendency of betas to regress toward 1 in subsequent periods. Bloomberg's "Adjusted Beta" applies exactly this formula to a raw beta (by default 2-year weekly). Rules: apply it once; disclose whether the beta in Ke is raw or adjusted; apply it consistently across peers; consider not applying it when using a peer median (which already reduces estimation error) — but be consistent.

Data hygiene: use adjusted closing prices (splits, bonuses), align dates (Power Query merge on date), drop holidays from both series, and use the same weekday for weekly returns.

Illustration

Weekly returns for a cement company vs Nifty 500, 104 weeks:

Statistic Value
=SLOPE(D4:D107,E4:E107) 1.21
=COVARIANCE.S(...)/VAR.S(...) 1.21
=RSQ(...) 0.38
SE(β) from LINEST 0.15

\[ \beta_{\text{Blume}} = 0.67 \times 1.21 + 0.33 = 1.141 \]

At ERP 7.25%, Blume reduces Ke by \( (1.21 - 1.141) \times 7.25\% = 0.50\% \). With SE 0.15, the 95% confidence band on raw β is about 0.91–1.51 — a reminder to cross-check against the peer-based beta.

Practice Questions

Q1. Formula audit [L1]

A model has =SLOPE(E4:E107,D4:D107) where D holds stock returns and E holds Nifty returns, and =COVARIANCE.P(D4:D107,E4:E107)/VAR.S(E4:E107) as its check. Identify both errors and write the corrected formulas.

Q2. Blume impact [L2]

Raw betas for five peers are 0.62, 0.88, 1.05, 1.34 and 1.71. Compute Blume-adjusted betas, the median of raw and of adjusted betas, and comment on what shrinkage does to the dispersion of the peer set.

💡 Hint: Shrinkage moves every beta toward 1 by one-third of its distance from 1.

Q3. Standard-error interpretation [L2]

A stock's 2-year weekly raw β is 0.74 with SE 0.21 and R² 0.09. Explain what these statistics imply for using it in Ke, and describe the evidence you would gather before accepting it.
