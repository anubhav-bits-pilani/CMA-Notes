Key concept

Cost of Equity via CAPM and the Company-Specific Premium

Concept: Ke = Rf + βL × ERP (+ documented company-specific premium only if justified)

Statement

\[ K_e = R_f + \beta_L \times \text{ERP} \; (+\; \text{CSRP}) \]

where \( R_f \) is the INR risk-free rate (10-year G-Sec yield, or the yield less sovereign default spread under the Damodaran approach), \( \beta_L \) is the levered equity beta at the target capital structure, ERP is the equity risk premium consistent with the chosen Rf, and CSRP is an optional company-specific risk premium, added only when supported by documented, quantifiable risks not captured in beta or in the cash flows.

Explanation

CAPM says investors are compensated only for systematic (non-diversifiable) risk, measured by beta relative to the market portfolio. In Indian practice it is the dominant model for Ke across sell-side, transaction and Ind AS 36 work. Its components come from this topic: Rf (s01), ERP (s02), βL (s03–s04 relevered at the s06 target).

Company-specific risk premium. Practitioners — particularly in unlisted, small-company and Rule 11UA valuations — sometimes add 1–5% for key-person dependence, customer concentration, small size or forecast risk. Theory argues against it: diversifiable risks should not be priced, and forecast risk belongs in the cash flows (scenario weighting) rather than in the rate. ICAI Valuation Standards and auditors' specialists expect any CSRP to be:

  • Specific (identify the risk: e.g. 45% revenue from one customer whose contract expires in FY28);
  • Not double-counted (not already in haircut projections, or in a size-adjusted beta);
  • Quantified with reasoning (e.g. probability-weighted cash flow loss converted to rate equivalent, or a size premium study);
  • Disclosed in the report.

A CSRP should never be used as a plug to reach a desired value. For listed large-caps, a CSRP is rarely justified.

Size premium. Studies (Kroll/Duff & Phelps for the US) show small companies earned returns above CAPM; Indian evidence is less established. If used, cite the source and avoid stacking with a CSRP for the same risk.

Illustration

Unlisted precision-engineering company, valuation date November 2025:

Input Value Source
G-Sec 10-yr 6.52% FBIL
India default spread 2.10% Damodaran Jan update
Rf (Damodaran) 4.42% Derived
ERP (India total) 7.25% Damodaran Jan update
βU peer median (cash-adjusted) 0.92 6 listed peers
Target D/E 0.25 Peer median
βL \( 0.92 \times (1 + 0.7483 \times 0.25) = 1.092 \) Relevered
CSRP 1.5% 52% revenue from one OEM; contract renewal in FY27

\[ K_e = 4.42\% + 1.092 \times 7.25\% + 1.5\% = 4.42\% + 7.92\% + 1.5\% = 13.84\% \]

Without the CSRP Ke is 12.34%. The 1.5% must be defended in the report; an alternative is to keep Ke at 12.34% and model a probability-weighted downside scenario for the OEM contract.

Practice Questions

Q1. CAPM computation [L1]

G-Sec 6.85%; Damodaran India default spread 2.20%; India total ERP 7.40%; βU 0.78; target D/E 0.30; tax 25.17%. Compute the Damodaran-consistent Ke.

Q2. CSRP justification [L3]

A promoter-run unlisted textile exporter's draft valuation adds a 4% CSRP for "small size, key-person risk and forecast uncertainty". The projections already assume revenue growth of 22% p.a. versus a five-year historical 9%. Critique the CSRP, propose how each risk should be handled, and estimate the Ke without the CSRP given Rf 4.4%, ERP 7.3%, βL 1.15.

💡 Hint: Separate risks that belong in cash flows from those that may legitimately affect the rate.

Q3. Listed large-cap [L2]

An analyst adds 2% CSRP to Ke for a Nifty 50 company "because of regulatory risk in the sector". Explain whether this is appropriate under CAPM, and what alternative modelling approaches would capture regulatory risk.
