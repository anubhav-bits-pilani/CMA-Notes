Subtopic note

Cost of Debt: NCD Yields, Rating Spreads and Synthetic Ratings

Scope: Cost of debt: YTM on listed NCDs, rating-based spreads (CRISIL/ICRA/CARE) over G-Sec, synthetic rating from ICR

Why it matters

Kd is the rate at which the company could borrow today on a long-term basis — the marginal, forward-looking cost, not the historical coupon on its books. In WACC it carries a weight of 10–40% for typical Indian industrials (more for infrastructure and utilities), and its after-tax value \( K_d (1 - t) \) is where the interest tax shield enters the valuation. Errors are common: using the book "interest expense ÷ average debt" (which mixes old fixed-rate loans, subsidised PLI-linked loans, foreign-currency loans and capitalised interest), or ignoring that a BBB-rated company cannot borrow at the AAA rate. Indian practitioners have three market-based methods, in descending order of preference.

Core notes

Method 1 — YTM on the company's listed NCDs

If the company has traded, long-dated, senior unsecured (or similar) non-convertible debentures listed on NSE/BSE, their yield to maturity on the valuation date is the best estimate of marginal pre-tax Kd. Sources: NSE/BSE debt segment quotes, FIMMDA/FBIL corporate bond valuations, CCIL reported trades, and rating-agency bond-yield matrices.

YTM solves:

\[ P = \sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{C}{(1 + y)^k} + \frac{F}{(1 + y)^N} \]

where \( P \) is the clean/dirty price, \( C \) the coupon, \( F \) the face value and \( N \) periods to maturity. In Excel: =YIELD(settlement, maturity, rate, pr, redemption, frequency, basis).

Caveats: Indian corporate bond markets are thin beyond AAA/AA PSU and NBFC issuers; many NCDs are privately placed and rarely traded. A stale or single-trade yield is unreliable. Secured NCDs yield less than the company's unsecured marginal cost. Tenor should be close to 10 years to match Rf; adjust via the credit curve if not.

Method 2 — Rating-based spread over G-Sec

If the company is rated by CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings (Fitch group), Acuité or Brickwork, use its long-term rating and the market spread for that rating:

\[ K_d = R_{f,\text{G-Sec}} + \text{Spread}_{\text{rating, tenor}} \]

Spreads are available from FIMMDA/FBIL corporate bond valuation matrices and rating agencies' default and spread studies. Indicative ranges (highly date-sensitive; verify on the valuation date):

Rating Indicative spread over 10-yr G-Sec
AAA 50–90 bp
AA+ / AA 90–160 bp
AA− / A+ 160–250 bp
A / A− 250–400 bp
BBB 400–600 bp

Note that for INR Kd, the base is the full G-Sec yield, not the Damodaran-stripped Rf, because the corporate spread is measured over G-Secs. (In Damodaran's framework, Kd = Rf(stripped) + country default spread + company spread, which arrives at about the same number.)

Bank loans: many Indian mid-caps borrow from banks at MCLR- or EBLR (repo-linked) based rates. The marginal term-loan rate (e.g. 1-year MCLR + spread) is a useful corroboration, but bank rates are floating and short-reset; WACC needs a long-term rate.

Method 3 — Synthetic rating from the interest coverage ratio

For unrated companies (most unlisted targets), estimate a rating from the interest coverage ratio (ICR = EBIT ÷ interest expense) using a mapping table, then apply that rating's spread. Damodaran publishes a table (separate for large and small/risky firms); an illustrative large-firm mapping:

ICR (EBIT ÷ Interest) Synthetic rating
> 8.5 AAA
6.5 – 8.5 AA
5.5 – 6.5 A+
4.25 – 5.5 A
3.0 – 4.25 A−
2.5 – 3.0 BBB
2.0 – 2.5 BB+/BB
< 2.0 B and below

Indian rating agencies use a wider set of metrics (net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, scale, promoter support), so treat the synthetic rating as a cross-check. Use normalised EBIT (mid-cycle) and, in a post-116 model, include lease interest in the denominator and EBIT post-116 in the numerator.

Pre-tax vs post-tax

WACC uses \( K_d \times (1 - t) \) with the marginal tax rate (25.17%), provided the company is tax-paying in the relevant years. If interest is not deductible (losses, Sec 94B thin-capitalisation limits on interest paid to non-resident associated enterprises above 30% of EBITDA), the effective shield is lower.

Worked illustration

An unrated mid-cap auto component maker: normalised EBIT ₹420 crore, interest ₹95 crore (incl. lease interest ₹15 crore), ICR = 4.42 → synthetic rating A. 10-yr G-Sec = 6.52%; A-rated spread = 3.00%.

\[ K_d^{\text{pre}} = 6.52\% + 3.00\% = 9.52\%; \quad K_d^{\text{post}} = 9.52\% \times (1 - 0.2517) = 7.12\% \]

Its book cost (₹95 crore ÷ ₹1,150 crore average debt) is 8.26% — lower because older loans were contracted when rates were lower and include a PLI-linked subsidised loan.

Excel implementation

WACC!C15  =YIELD(Val_Date,DATE(2034,6,15),8.65%,101.2,100,1,1)   ' YTM on listed NCD (annual coupon)
WACC!C16  =VLOOKUP(EBIT/Interest,ICR_Table,2,TRUE)               ' synthetic rating (ascending ICR lower bounds)
WACC!C17  =Rf_GSec+VLOOKUP(C16,Spread_Table,2,FALSE)             ' rating-based Kd
WACC!C18  =C17*(1-Tax_Marginal)                                   ' after-tax Kd

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Foreign-currency borrowings (ECBs, USD bonds): convert to INR-equivalent cost using interest-rate parity (add the forward premium/ hedging cost); a 5% USD coupon is not a 5% INR cost.
  • Group support: a subsidiary of a AAA group may be rated higher than its standalone credit; if the support is expected to persist, the supported rating is the market cost.
  • Distressed companies: Kd from distressed yields includes expected default loss; the expected return on debt is lower than the promised yield.
  • Convertible debt / CCDs: split into debt and equity components; use straight-debt Kd.
  • Short-term working capital lines are part of debt, but their (lower) cost does not represent long-run Kd.
  • Capitalised interest (Ind AS 23) understates P&L interest; use total borrowing cost for ICR.

Key takeaways

  • Kd is the marginal long-term pre-tax borrowing rate on the valuation date, not the book coupon.
  • Best source: YTM on liquid long-dated listed NCDs; next: rating-based spread over G-Sec; for unrated firms: synthetic rating from ICR.
  • Rating spreads are measured over the full G-Sec yield.
  • After-tax Kd = Kd × (1 − 25.17%) only if interest is deductible and the company pays tax.
  • Treat FX loans, group support, convertibles and capitalised interest explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. YTM on an NCD [L1]

A listed AA-rated NCD: face ₹1,000, annual coupon 8.40%, maturity 7 years 3 months from the valuation date, clean price ₹1,012. Write the Excel YIELD formula and explain why you might prefer the rating-based method if this NCD traded only twice in the last quarter.

Q2. Synthetic rating [L2]

Unrated FMCG distributor: FY25 EBIT ₹96 crore, FY26E ₹118 crore; finance cost ₹28 crore (including lease interest ₹6 crore); G-Sec 6.45%. Using the ICR table in these notes and indicative spreads, estimate pre-tax and after-tax Kd using FY26E EBIT. Comment on whether you would use FY25 or FY26E EBIT.

💡 Hint: Map ICR to a rating, then add the rating's spread to the G-Sec yield.

Q3. Book vs market cost [L2]

A company's CFO argues that WACC should use its 7.6% average cost of borrowing (₹2,400 crore loans at fixed rates contracted in 2021) because "that's what we actually pay". The company's current CRISIL rating is A+ and A+ spreads are 210 bp over a 6.6% G-Sec. Draft your response, quantifying the difference in after-tax Kd.

Q4. ECB conversion [L3]

A company has a 5-year USD ECB at SOFR + 175 bp (all-in 6.10% USD) and plans similar future borrowing. Five-year USD/INR forward premium is about 2.4% per annum. Explain how you would derive an INR-equivalent Kd for WACC and why using 6.10% directly is inconsistent with INR cash flows.
