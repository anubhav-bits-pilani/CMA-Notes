Subtopic note

Lease Liabilities in the WACC Debt Weight (Post-Ind AS 116)

Scope: Lease liabilities in debt weight when FCFF is post-Ind AS 116

Why it matters

When FCFF is built on a post-Ind AS 116 basis (t1/s03, t1/c06), leases are treated as debt-financed acquisitions of right-of-use assets. The discount rate must then reflect the fact that part of the firm's capital is lease financing. For a lease-heavy Indian retailer or QSR operator, lease liabilities can be 5–30% of enterprise value — larger than borrowings — so including or excluding them changes the debt weight, the relevered beta and WACC materially. The rule is simple: the WACC capital structure must use the same definition of debt as the FCFF and the EV bridge. Reviewers check this triangle (FCFF basis ↔ WACC weights ↔ bridge deductions) on every lease-heavy valuation.

Core notes

The consistency logic

Post-116 FCFF excludes lease payments. The value of the operating assets therefore includes the value attributable to lessors, who are then paid out of EV via the lease-liability deduction in the bridge. Lessors are capital providers, so:

\[ \text{WACC}_{\text{post}} = w_E K_e + w_D K_d (1-t) + w_L K_L (1-t) \]

where \( w_L = L / (D + L + E) \), \( L \) is lease liabilities (current + non-current) and \( K_L \) is the pre-tax cost of lease financing. In practice, \( K_L \) is often set equal to \( K_d \) and leases are simply added to D.

Three linked adjustments

  1. Debt weight: \( D_{\text{post}} = \text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities} \). Use the target ratio of leases to total capital, typically benchmarked to peers on the same basis.
  2. Beta: unlever peers using D/E that includes their lease liabilities, then relever the subject at its target D/E including leases (t2/s04). Unlevering with borrowings only and relevering with borrowings + leases (or vice versa) creates a mismatch.
  3. Cost of lease debt: the incremental borrowing rate (IBR) disclosed in the Ind AS 116 note (Indian retailers commonly disclose weighted average IBRs of 8–10%) is a reasonable proxy for \( K_L \). It reflects secured-like financing of a specific asset; it is often close to the company's secured Kd.

Effect on WACC

Adding leases typically lowers WACC slightly because more of the capital is (cheaper, tax-deductible) debt-like financing. But relevered beta rises, offsetting part of the effect. The net movement is usually 20–80 bp for a lease-heavy retailer. Under the pre-116 basis, rent is an operating cost that already embeds the lessor's return, so leases are excluded from weights.

Element Post-116 basis Pre-116 basis
FCFF Excludes lease payments; deducts ROU additions Deducts rent
Peer unlevering D/E Borrowings + leases Borrowings only
Target D/(D+E) Including leases Excluding leases
Kd Blend of borrowing Kd and IBR Borrowing Kd
Bridge Deduct lease liabilities Do not deduct
Comps EV incl. leases ÷ post-116 EBITDA EV excl. leases ÷ pre-116 EBITDA

Worked illustration

Value retailer: market cap ₹60,000 crore; borrowings ₹800 crore; lease liabilities ₹4,200 crore; median peer unlevered β (lease-inclusive) 0.80, (lease-exclusive) 0.86; Rf (Damodaran) 4.40%; ERP 7.25%; Kd 8.8%; IBR 9.0%; tax 25.17%.

Post-116 (D incl. leases = 5,000; D/E = 0.0833):

\[ \beta_L = 0.80 \times (1 + 0.7483 \times 0.0833) = 0.850; \quad K_e = 4.40\% + 0.850 \times 7.25\% = 10.56\% \]

\[ w_D = \frac{5{,}000}{65{,}000} = 7.69\%; \quad K_d^{\text{blend}} = \frac{800 \times 8.8\% + 4{,}200 \times 9.0\%}{5{,}000} = 8.97\% \]

\[ \text{WACC} = 0.9231 \times 10.56\% + 0.0769 \times 8.97\% \times 0.7483 = 9.75\% + 0.52\% = 10.27\% \]

Pre-116 (D = 800; D/E = 0.0133): \( \beta_L = 0.86 \times 1.00997 = 0.869 \); \( K_e = 10.70\% \); \( w_D = 1.32\% \); WACC = \( 0.9868 \times 10.70\% + 0.0132 \times 8.8\% \times 0.7483 = 10.556\% + 0.087\% = 10.64\% \).

The post-116 WACC is about 37 bp lower — consistent with its FCFF being higher (no rent deducted) and the bridge deducting ₹4,200 crore of leases.

Implementation

On the WACC tab, add a "Lease basis" switch (Post116 = 1/0) driving: debt definition in the peer beta table, target weights, Kd blend, and the bridge deduction in the DCF sheet. A check row confirms that the same switch value is referenced in FCFF, WACC, bridge and comps.

WACC!C22  =Borrowings+IF(Post116=1,Lease_Liab,0)          ' debt for weights
Peers!G5  =(Borr5+IF(Post116=1,Lease5,0))/MktCap5          ' peer D/E on same basis
DCF!C60   =-IF(Post116=1,Lease_Liab_ValDate,0)             ' bridge deduction

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Short-term and variable leases are not in lease liabilities on either basis; their rent stays in FCFF.
  • Lease liability vs future lease commitments: post-116 liability covers only the non-cancellable period plus reasonably certain extensions; it excludes leases not yet commenced. Future stores' leases are captured via ROU additions in FCFF.
  • Peers reporting under US GAAP (operating leases also on balance sheet but expense remains a single straight-line cost): for comps, adjust EBITDA; for beta, liabilities can be included.
  • Market value of leases is approximated by book lease liability; if IBRs at inception were far below current rates, the book value overstates the market value of the lease obligation.
  • Lessors (REITs) do not apply this; their leases are assets.

Key takeaways

  • Post-116 FCFF requires lease liabilities in WACC debt, in peer unlevering and in the target structure.
  • Cost of lease debt ≈ incremental borrowing rate disclosed in the Ind AS 116 note.
  • Including leases usually lowers WACC modestly, partly offset by higher relevered beta.
  • FCFF basis, WACC weights, bridge deduction and comps must all use the same switch.
  • Pre-116: exclude leases everywhere and deduct rent in FCFF.

Practice Questions

Q1. Post-116 WACC [L1]

Market cap ₹12,000 crore; borrowings ₹1,500 crore; lease liabilities ₹2,300 crore; Ke (relevered on lease-inclusive basis) 13.2%; Kd 9.4%; IBR 9.8%; tax 25.17%. Compute the post-116 WACC treating leases as a separate capital component.

Q2. Mismatch diagnosis [L2]

A model unlevers peer betas using borrowings only, relevers the subject using borrowings + leases, uses pre-116 FCFF (rent deducted) and deducts lease liabilities in the bridge. Identify each inconsistency, state its direction of bias on value, and set out the corrected configuration for each of the two consistent bases.

💡 Hint: Tabulate FCFF, beta, weights and bridge under each basis, then compare to the model.

Q3. Interview: does Ind AS 116 change WACC? [L2]

Explain to an interviewer why WACC falls under the post-116 basis for a QSR chain, why equity value should nonetheless be roughly unchanged, and what residual differences you would expect.
