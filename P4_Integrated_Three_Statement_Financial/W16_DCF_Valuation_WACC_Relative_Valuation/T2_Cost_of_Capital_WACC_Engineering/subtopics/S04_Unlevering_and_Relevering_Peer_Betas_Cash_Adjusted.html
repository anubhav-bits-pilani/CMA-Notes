Subtopic note

Unlevering and Relevering Peer Betas; Cash-Adjusted Betas

Scope: Peer unlevering/relevering using market D/E and 25.17% tax; cash-adjusted betas

Why it matters

A single company's regression beta is noisy, reflects its current (not target) leverage, and is unavailable for unlisted targets, IPO candidates, divisions or illiquid stocks. The bottom-up (peer) beta solves all three: unlever each comparable company's beta to remove its financial leverage, take a median of the asset (unlevered) betas, adjust for cash, then relever at the subject company's target capital structure. This is standard practice in transaction valuations, Rule 11UA reports for unlisted companies, Ind AS 36 impairment tests (where a CGU has no market price) and sell-side initiations. A well-constructed peer beta table is one of the first things a reviewer examines on a WACC tab.

Core notes

The Hamada relationship

Assuming debt beta is zero and debt is fixed in amount (tax shields with the risk of debt), the levered equity beta relates to the unlevered (asset) beta by:

\[ \beta_L = \beta_U \times \left[1 + (1 - t) \times \frac{D}{E}\right] \]

Hence:

\[ \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t) \times D/E} \]

where \( D/E \) is the market debt-to-equity ratio and \( t \) the marginal tax rate. Alternatives: Harris–Pringle (\( \beta_L = \beta_U (1 + D/E) \)), appropriate when debt is rebalanced to a target ratio; and versions with a non-zero debt beta for high-leverage firms. Hamada is the market convention in India; state the formula used.

Inputs for each peer

  • Levered beta: regression beta per s03, same index, frequency and window across peers.
  • Market D/E: debt at book value (a proxy for market value of debt, acceptable for investment-grade issuers) ÷ market capitalisation averaged over the beta estimation window (beta reflects leverage during that window, not just today's). At minimum use the D/E at the end of the window and note it.
  • Debt definition: borrowings (current + non-current) plus lease liabilities if the WACC/FCFF basis is post-116 — the same definition used for the subject company.
  • Tax rate: marginal 25.17% for Indian domestic peers under 115BAA; peer-specific statutory rate for foreign peers.

Cash-adjusted (pure operating) betas

A company holding large cash has a beta that is a weighted average of a low-risk cash beta (~0) and its operating beta. Measured unlevered beta is therefore biased downward for cash-rich peers (typical in Indian IT, FMCG, pharma). Correct by:

\[ \beta_{U,\text{operating}} = \frac{\beta_U}{1 - \frac{\text{Cash}}{\text{Firm value}}} \]

where firm value = market cap + debt (or EV + cash). Equivalent shortcut: unlever using net debt in D/E — but this produces negative D/E for net-cash firms and is less transparent. Use the cash adjustment explicitly.

The bottom-up beta procedure

  1. Select 5–10 peers by business model and risk (see Phase 2 peer selection; t6/s01).
  2. Compute or obtain each peer's levered beta (raw or Blume, applied consistently).
  3. Compute each peer's average market D/E and cash/firm value over the window.
  4. Unlever each beta (Hamada, 25.17%) and cash-adjust.
  5. Take the median (robust to outliers); show mean and range.
  6. Relever at the subject's target D/E (s06) and marginal tax:

\[ \beta_{L,\text{target}} = \beta_{U,\text{median}} \times \left[1 + (1 - 0.2517) \times \frac{D}{E}_{\text{target}}\right] \]

  1. Cross-check against the subject's own regression beta.

Worked illustration

Pidilite-type adhesives/building-materials peer set (illustrative numbers, ₹ crore):

Peer Levered β Avg market cap Debt (incl. leases) Cash D/E βU (Hamada) Cash/FV Cash-adj βU
A 0.78 1,40,000 2,100 5,600 0.015 0.771 0.039 0.803
B 0.92 2,80,000 4,200 4,000 0.015 0.910 0.014 0.923
C 0.85 55,000 1,650 1,100 0.030 0.831 0.019 0.848
D 1.05 40,000 4,000 800 0.100 0.977 0.018 0.995

Peer A: \( \beta_U = 0.78 / (1 + 0.7483 \times 0.015) = 0.771 \); cash/FV = \( 5{,}600 / (1{,}40{,}000 + 2{,}100) = 0.039 \); cash-adjusted = \( 0.771 / 0.961 = 0.803 \).

Median cash-adjusted βU = average of C and B = (0.848 + 0.923) ÷ 2 = 0.886. Relever at target D/E of 0.10:

\[ \beta_L = 0.886 \times (1 + 0.7483 \times 0.10) = 0.952 \]

Excel implementation

Peers!H5  =C5/(1+(1-Tax_Marginal)*G5)          ' unlevered beta
Peers!I5  =F5/(D5+E5)                           ' cash / firm value
Peers!J5  =H5/(1-I5)                            ' cash-adjusted unlevered beta
Peers!J12 =MEDIAN(J5:J9)                        ' median asset beta
WACC!C10  =Peers!J12*(1+(1-Tax_Marginal)*Target_DE)   ' relevered beta

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Foreign peers: use their local tax rate in unlevering and beta measured against their local index — then accept the beta as a measure of relative risk; mixing indices is a known compromise.
  • Loss-making peers: tax shield is zero; unlever with \( t = 0 \).
  • Highly levered peers (D/E > 1): Hamada with zero debt beta overstates βU; consider a debt beta (e.g. 0.2–0.3) or exclude.
  • Conglomerate peers: contaminate the asset beta; exclude or use pure-play segments.
  • Peer D/E at a single date vs window average: if leverage moved a lot during the window (deleveraging after a QIP), a single-date D/E mis-unlevers.
  • Lease consistency: if peers' debt includes leases, the subject's target D/E must too (s07).

Key takeaways

  • Unlever with \( \beta_U = \beta_L \div [1 + (1 - t) D/E] \) using market D/E and 25.17%; relever at target D/E.
  • Use window-average market D/E and a debt definition consistent with the FCFF basis.
  • Cash-adjust: \( \beta_U \div (1 - \text{Cash/Firm value}) \) for cash-rich peers.
  • Take the median of peer asset betas; show range and mean.
  • Cross-check the relevered beta against the subject's own regression beta.

Practice Questions

Q1. Unlever, adjust and relever [L1]

Peer data: levered β 1.20, market cap ₹18,000 crore, debt ₹6,000 crore, cash ₹1,500 crore, tax 25.17%. Compute βU, cash-adjusted βU, and relevered β at a target D/E of 0.40.

Q2. Peer table for a retailer [L2]

For five retail peers (levered β, market cap, borrowings, lease liabilities, cash in ₹ crore): P1 (1.05, 2,40,000, 1,200, 3,800, 2,600); P2 (0.92, 2,60,000, 0, 1,900, 3,100); P3 (1.25, 30,000, 2,900, 4,100, 600); P4 (1.10, 7,500, 150, 1,050, 220); P5 (1.40, 4,000, 700, 1,200, 90). Compute each peer's βU on a post-116 basis (debt including leases) and on a pre-116 basis (borrowings only), the median of each, and explain why the two medians differ.

💡 Hint: Keep the debt definition identical across peers and the subject, and use 25.17% throughout.

Q3. Cash-adjustment debate [L2]

An analyst argues that cash-adjusting betas for Indian IT peers is unnecessary because "investors value the whole company, including cash". Respond, showing how failing to cash-adjust interacts with adding cash in the EV-to-equity bridge.

Q4. Unlisted target [L3]

You are valuing an unlisted packaging company for a PE investment. Five listed peers have relevered betas ranging from 0.6 to 1.6 and D/E from 0.05 to 1.30. Describe your full procedure to arrive at a defensible Ke, including peer screening, the treatment of the high-leverage peer, the choice between Hamada and Harris–Pringle, and how you would document the final beta.
