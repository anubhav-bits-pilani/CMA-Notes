Subtopic note

Equity Risk Premium for India: Damodaran and Implied ERP

Scope: ERP: Damodaran India total ERP = mature-market ERP + India CRP (latest January update); implied ERP alternatives

Why it matters

The equity risk premium is the single largest judgement in Ke. At a beta of 1.0, a 1 percentage point change in ERP moves Ke by 1 point and WACC by 0.7–0.9 points, which can move EV by 10–15% for a long-duration consumer business. Sell-side reports, fairness opinions and Ind AS 36 impairment models in India commonly cite Damodaran's January update as the ERP source because it is transparent, annually refreshed and widely accepted by auditors. Equally commonly, it is misapplied — added to an Rf that already contains country risk. Understanding how Damodaran builds the India number, and what the alternatives are, lets you defend the input to an audit partner or investment committee.

Core notes

What the ERP represents

The ERP is the extra return investors demand to hold a diversified equity portfolio over the risk-free asset. It is forward-looking and unobservable; it can be estimated from history, from surveys, or implied from current prices.

Damodaran's India total ERP

Professor Aswath Damodaran (NYU Stern) publishes country ERPs each January (with a mid-year update). The construction:

\[ \text{ERP}_{\text{India}} = \text{ERP}_{\text{mature}} + \text{CRP}_{\text{India}} \]

  1. Mature-market ERP — the implied ERP for the S&P 500 at the start of the year (an FCFE-based implied premium; see below). Recent values: about 4.6% (Jan 2024) and 4.33% (Jan 2025) — verify the current update.
  2. Country default spread — from the sovereign's Moody's local-currency rating (India Baa3) mapped to a default spread table, or from CDS spreads where available.
  3. CRP — default spread scaled by relative equity-to-bond volatility:

\[ \text{CRP} = \text{Default spread} \times \frac{\sigma_{\text{Equity, emerging}}}{\sigma_{\text{Bond, emerging}}} \]

Damodaran uses a global emerging-market ratio (around 1.2–1.5×). For India, CRP has been roughly 2.9–3.2% in recent updates, giving a total India ERP of approximately 7.2–7.6%. These numbers are date-sensitive — always cite the update used (e.g. "Damodaran, January 2025 update: India total ERP 7.26%").

Damodaran pairs this ERP with an INR risk-free rate stripped of the sovereign default spread (t2/c05). That pairing is what makes the method internally consistent.

"Latest January update" discipline

The January dataset is the reference most practitioners cite because it coincides with Damodaran's annual recalculation of the S&P 500 implied ERP and his ratings/default-spread tables. Rules:

  • Use the latest update available on the valuation date; do not cherry-pick an older year with a lower ERP.
  • If a mid-year update exists and the valuation date is after it, either use it or justify using January.
  • Record the file name and table (country risk premium dataset, "ctryprem").

Implied ERP alternatives for India

(a) Implied ERP from the Nifty 50 (Gordon/FCFE approach). Solve for the expected return that equates the index level to the PV of expected cash flows to equity holders:

\[ \text{Index level} = \frac{\text{Expected cash yield}_1 \times \text{Index}}{K_e - g} \;\Rightarrow\; K_e = \frac{D_1 + \text{Buybacks}_1}{P_0} + g; \quad \text{ERP}_{\text{implied}} = K_e - R_f \]

A more robust version uses a two-stage model: consensus earnings growth for 5 years, then convergence to long-run nominal growth. With the Nifty 50 trading at ~22× trailing earnings and a ~1.3% dividend yield, implied ERPs computed this way tend to be low (4–6% over the full G-Sec yield), reflecting high valuations.

(b) Historical ERP. Excess return of the Sensex/Nifty over G-Secs (or T-bills) since 1980/1990. Indian historical series are short, volatile and survivorship-affected; estimates range widely (often 5–9% arithmetic). Standard errors are large.

(c) Survey-based ERP. Fernández et al. annual survey of analysts and academics (India responses typically 7–8%); useful corroboration.

(d) Local market practice. Many Indian valuers use Rf = full G-Sec yield and an "Indian market return" of 12–14%, giving an implied ERP of roughly 6–7.5% over the full G-Sec yield. This is a local-market approach, consistent only with the full G-Sec Rf and no separate CRP.

Comparing approaches

Approach Rf paired with Strength Weakness
Damodaran total ERP G-Sec − default spread Transparent, annually updated, globally comparable Depends on rating-based spread and volatility ratio
Implied (Nifty) Full G-Sec Market-consistent at valuation date Sensitive to growth input; low when markets are expensive
Historical Full G-Sec (same tenor as history) Simple, data-based Short series, huge standard error, backward-looking
Survey/market return Full G-Sec Reflects practitioner consensus Subjective, potentially circular

Worked illustration

Damodaran (illustrative January data): mature ERP 4.33%; India default spread 2.18%; equity/bond volatility ratio 1.34 → CRP = 2.18% × 1.34 = 2.92%; total ERP = 7.25%. G-Sec 6.52% → Damodaran Rf = 4.34%. For β = 0.95:

\[ K_e = 4.34\% + 0.95 \times 7.25\% = 11.23\% \]

Local-market approach with full G-Sec and ERP of 5.0% (no CRP): \( K_e = 6.52\% + 0.95 \times 5.0\% = 11.27\% \) — nearly identical. The double-counted version (6.52% + 0.95 × 7.25% = 13.41%) is 218 bp higher.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Companies with large foreign revenue. Damodaran suggests weighting CRP by operating exposure (lambda approach): an Indian IT exporter with 90% US revenue arguably bears less India CRP. Document if used.
  • Rating changes. India's S&P upgrade (BBB, August 2025) does not change Damodaran's Moody's-based spread unless Moody's also moves; check which agency the dataset uses.
  • Crisis periods. Implied ERPs spike; spot-consistent valuation uses them, but some valuers normalise.
  • Size premium / company-specific risk premium is not part of ERP; it is a separate, contested add-on (t2/c01).

Key takeaways

  • Damodaran India ERP = mature-market (implied S&P 500) ERP + India CRP; CRP = default spread × equity/bond volatility ratio.
  • Pair Damodaran's total ERP with an INR Rf stripped of the default spread.
  • Use the latest update available on the valuation date and cite it.
  • Implied, historical and survey ERPs are alternatives or cross-checks; each must be paired with a consistent Rf.
  • The difference between consistent approaches is small; the double count is large.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the India ERP [L1]

Given a mature-market ERP of 4.60%, India default spread of 2.20% and an equity/bond volatility ratio of 1.40, compute India CRP and total ERP. With a 10-year G-Sec yield of 7.05%, compute the Damodaran-consistent Rf and Ke for β = 1.15.

Q2. Implied ERP from the Nifty 50 [L2]

Nifty 50 is at 25,800; expected dividends plus buybacks next year are 1.4% of the index; consensus EPS growth is 13% for five years, then 8% (nominal) in perpetuity; current earnings yield is 4.5% and payout is 35% through the high-growth phase, rising to a payout consistent with 8% growth and 12% ROE thereafter. G-Sec is 6.5%. Set up (but you need not solve) a two-stage implied ERP model in Excel, specifying the cells and the Goal Seek target.

💡 Hint: Goal Seek the discount rate such that the PV of cash flows equals the index level; ERP = that rate − Rf.

Q3. Defend the ERP to an auditor [L3]

Your Ind AS 36 impairment test for a cement CGU uses Rf = 6.5% (full G-Sec) and ERP = 7.3% (Damodaran India). The auditor's valuation specialist challenges the combination. Write a response either defending or revising your inputs, quantifying the change in Ke at β = 1.05, and explaining the effect on headroom.

Q4. Exporter CRP [L2]

An Indian IT services company earns 85% of revenue from North America and Europe. Discuss how you would adjust India CRP in its Ke, the arguments for and against, and how you would document the decision.
