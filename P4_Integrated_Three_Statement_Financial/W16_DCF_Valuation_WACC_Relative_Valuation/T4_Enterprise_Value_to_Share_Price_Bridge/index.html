Roadmap topic: Enterprise Value to Implied Share Price Bridge: EV + Cash & Liquid Investments – Total Debt – Minority Interest – Preferred Capital = Implied Equity Value ÷ Diluted Shares

Overview

A DCF built on Free Cash Flow to Firm values only the operating business: the cash that the operating assets generate for all capital providers, discounted at a WACC that blends the returns those providers require. That number, the enterprise value (EV), is not what a shareholder owns. To get from EV to a per-share value you must (a) add back everything of value the company owns that the FCFF did not capture, such as surplus cash, liquid mutual funds, associates and idle land, and (b) subtract every claim that ranks ahead of, or alongside, the ordinary shareholder and that the FCFF did not already pay for. That means borrowings, lease liabilities, preference capital, non-controlling interests and debt-like obligations. Only then do you divide by a share count that reflects all the instruments that can turn into equity.

The bridge is where valuation models most often go quietly wrong. The DCF mechanics are usually checked carefully. The bridge is assembled from balance-sheet notes, often from the wrong date, and with little thought about whether each item's cash flows are already inside FCFF. A ₹ 400 crore error in net debt on a company with 10 crore shares is ₹ 40 per share. That is easily the difference between a "Buy" and a "Hold". For an FP&A or valuation analyst in India, the bridge also ties directly to Ind AS reporting: NCI under Ind AS 110, associates under Ind AS 28, lease liabilities under Ind AS 116, defined-benefit obligations under Ind AS 19, provisions and contingencies under Ind AS 37, preference shares under Ind AS 32, and diluted shares under Ind AS 33 and Ind AS 102.

The learning sequence is:

  1. Assemble EV from the discounted explicit-period FCFF and the discounted terminal value.
  2. Add cash and non-operating assets.
  3. Add associates and JVs.
  4. Deduct debt and debt-like claims.
  5. Make sure every balance is measured at the valuation date, not at the last 31 March.
  6. Build the diluted share count.
  7. Sanity-check the answer against the current market price (CMP) and against the multiples it implies.

The unifying principle is the consistency rule in concept c05. An item belongs in the bridge only if its cash flows are outside FCFF.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 110, 28, 33 and 102 linkages

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 110 NCI, Ind AS 28 associates, Ind AS 33 dilution, Ind AS 102 ESOPs

Each bridge line comes from a Phase 1 standard. Under Ind AS 110 the parent consolidates 100% of a subsidiary's EBITDA and debt even when it owns only 60%. Consolidated FCFF therefore includes cash flows that belong to minority holders, and NCI must be deducted. Under Ind AS 28, associates and JVs are equity-accounted. Only the share of profit appears, below EBIT, so their value is missing from FCFF and must be added. Ind AS 33.45–46 sets out the treasury stock method for options. Valuation uses the same mechanics but substitutes the current or implied price for the period-average price. Ind AS 102 disclosures give you the number of options outstanding, weighted-average exercise prices and vesting conditions, which are the raw inputs for the dilution schedule.

Identical EV definition in comps

→ Week 16 (w16_t6): identical EV definition in comps

Trading comps run the bridge in reverse. You start from market capitalisation and add debt, leases, preference and NCI, then subtract cash and non-operating investments, to get the market's EV. If your DCF bridge deducts lease liabilities but your comps EV excludes them, the EV/EBITDA you apply in the exit-multiple terminal value and the implied multiples you compare against are on different bases. Build one EV definition, document it once (pre- or post-Ind AS 116, treatment of associates, which investments count as non-operating) and apply it in both directions. When implied DCF EV/EBITDA is compared with the peer median, both must come from the same recipe.

M&A bridges and SEBI SAST pricing

→ Phase 6 (W21–24): M&A bridges, SEBI SAST open-offer pricing

In an acquisition, the bridge becomes a negotiated "enterprise-to-equity" schedule in the share purchase agreement. Cash-free/debt-free pricing, a normalised working-capital peg, and explicit lists of debt-like items (gratuity shortfall, unpaid statutory dues, deferred consideration, customer advances) are debated line by line. For listed targets, the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 set a floor for the open-offer price under Regulation 8, based on negotiated price, prices paid by the acquirer and volume-weighted market prices. Your intrinsic per-share value from the bridge is then compared with this regulatory floor to judge whether the acquirer is overpaying or offering a control premium that the standalone DCF cannot support.

Common pitfalls

Stale net debt

Net debt taken from a stale year-end rather than valuation date

The mistake: discounting cash flows to, say, 15 November 2026 but deducting net debt from the 31 March 2026 balance sheet.

Why it happens: the audited annual report is the easiest source. Analysts forget that seven months of operating cash generation, capex, dividends and borrowing have happened since.

Correct treatment: use the latest available balance sheet. For listed companies this is the half-yearly statement of assets and liabilities filed under SEBI LODR Regulation 33, or the lender/MIS balance sheet for unlisted ones. Roll it forward to the valuation date. Only the cash flows after that date should be in the discounted FCFF.

Review test: check that stub-period FCFF plus net debt at the valuation date reconciles to FY-end net debt less full-year FCFF, adjusted for dividends and equity flows.

Lease double-count or omission

Lease liabilities deducted while FCFF already deducts lease payments (or vice versa)

The mistake: under Ind AS 116, EBITDA excludes lease rentals. If the FCFF deducts rent anyway (a pre-116 framing) and the bridge deducts the lease liability, the lease is charged twice and value is understated. The opposite error is post-116 FCFF that ignores rent, with no lease liability deducted. That overstates value, and the overstatement is severe for retailers, QSR chains and multiplexes.

Why it happens: model sheets are built at different times by different people.

Correct treatment: choose one framing. Post-116 means FCFF excludes lease payments, new ROU additions are treated as capex-like reinvestment, the lease liability is deducted in the bridge and it is included in WACC debt weights. Pre-116 means rent is deducted in FCFF and no lease liability appears in the bridge.

Review test: a flag cell Lease_Basis that drives FCFF, WACC and bridge together.

Restricted cash and basic share count

Adding restricted cash (unpaid dividend accounts, margin money) or using basic shares

The mistake: "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" under Schedule III often hold earmarked balances. These include the Unpaid Dividend Account under Sec 124, margin money against bank guarantees and LCs, and deposits under lien. None of these is available to shareholders. A related error is dividing equity value by basic shares when ESOPs, RSUs or CCDs are outstanding.

Why it happens: analysts lift the cash line totals and the share count from the cover page of the results.

Correct treatment: read the cash note. Exclude earmarked and lien-marked balances, or deduct the matching liability. Build a diluted share count using TSM for options and if-converted for convertibles.

Review test: reconcile cash added in the bridge to the Ind AS 7 cash note, line by line. Check that diluted shares are at least basic shares.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full bridge for a listed auto-component company [L2]

Shakti Auto Components Ltd (NSE-listed, FY ends 31 March) is valued as of 30 September 2026. Σ PV of FCFF for 1 October 2026 – 31 March 2031 (mid-year convention, WACC 12.0%) is ₹ 3,850 crore, and PV of terminal value is ₹ 7,940 crore. The 30 September 2026 consolidated balance sheet (filed under LODR Reg 33) shows the following:

  • cash and cash equivalents ₹ 310 crore
  • other bank balances ₹ 145 crore, of which unpaid dividend account ₹ 6 crore and margin money ₹ 39 crore
  • liquid mutual funds ₹ 520 crore
  • a 30% associate carried at ₹ 260 crore (associate is listed, and Shakti's stake has a market value of ₹ 410 crore)
  • borrowings ₹ 1,640 crore
  • lease liabilities ₹ 210 crore (FCFF is post-Ind AS 116)
  • 8% redeemable preference shares ₹ 100 crore
  • NCI ₹ 180 crore at book (subsidiary earns 9% of consolidated EBITDA, and an NCI fair value of ₹ 420 crore is estimated)
  • an unfunded gratuity obligation of ₹ 48 crore

Basic shares are 22.0 crore. There are 0.80 crore ESOPs outstanding at a weighted strike of ₹ 300. CMP is ₹ 405.

(a) Build the bridge to equity value, stating which measurement basis you choose for the associate and NCI. (b) Compute diluted shares using TSM on CMP, then iterate on the implied price. (c) Compute implied price and upside vs CMP. (d) Explain how each excluded item would mislead the conclusion if included.

💡 Hint: Tax-effect the gratuity at 25.17% because it is deductible on payment. Use the closed form \(P = (E + nK) \div (N + n)\) to check your iteration.

Q2. Valuation-date mismatch diagnosis [L2]

An analyst values Saffron Foods Ltd at 31 December 2026. They discount full-year FY27 FCFF of ₹ 240 crore at \(t = 0.5\) and deduct 31 March 2026 net debt of ₹ 900 crore. Actual net debt at 31 December 2026 was ₹ 760 crore. FCFF for April–December 2026 was ₹ 175 crore, and a ₹ 35 crore dividend was paid in August 2026. WACC is 11.5%. Identify every inconsistency. Restate the stub-period discounting and the net-debt figure that should be used. Quantify the directional impact on equity value.

💡 Hint: Separate cash already earned (now inside net debt) from cash still to be earned (inside the PV).

Q3. Lease-heavy retailer: two consistent framings [L3]

A fashion retailer has FY27E post-116 EBITDA of ₹ 1,200 crore and cash lease payments of ₹ 380 crore. Lease liabilities are ₹ 2,150 crore, and new ROU additions are expected at ₹ 300 crore a year. Borrowings are ₹ 400 crore and cash is ₹ 650 crore. Build a short memo and an Excel layout (row labels and the formulas you would use) that value the company on both a post-116 and a pre-116 basis. Show which lines change in FCFF, WACC weights and the bridge. Explain why the two equity values will not be identical in practice and what drives the residual gap.

Q4. Convertible and ESOP dilution with implied-price circularity [L3]

Equity value before considering instruments is ₹ 5,600 crore and basic shares are 12.0 crore. Outstanding instruments:

  • Tranche A: 0.30 crore options at strike ₹ 250
  • Tranche B: 0.20 crore options at strike ₹ 520
  • 0.05 crore RSUs at nil strike
  • ₹ 300 crore of CCDs convertible at ₹ 400 per share, carried as debt in the bridge

(a) Decide whether the CCDs should be treated as debt or as converted shares and justify your choice. (b) Set up an Excel iterative or algebraic solution for the implied price. (c) Describe the circularity controls (iteration toggle, convergence check) you would add.

Q5. Interview: walk me from EV to price [L1]

In an FP&A/valuation interview you are asked: "Your DCF gives EV of ₹ 18,000 crore. Walk me to a share price, and tell me three items that analysts most often get wrong in an Indian listed-company bridge." Prepare a structured two-minute answer that references the relevant Ind AS or Schedule III line for each item.
