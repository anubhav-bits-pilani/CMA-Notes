Subtopic note

Adding Cash, Liquid Investments and Non-Operating Assets to EV

Scope: Add cash, other bank balances, liquid mutual funds, non-operating assets at valuation date

Why it matters

Many Indian listed companies carry large treasury balances. Promoter-run FMCG, IT services, pharma and auto-ancillary firms often hold surplus cash, bank FDs and debt mutual funds worth 10–30% of market capitalisation. These assets earn "other income", which a correctly built FCFF excludes from EBIT. If they are not added back in the bridge, the DCF undervalues the equity. If they are added and their income stays in EBIT, the equity is overvalued. The analyst's job is to decide which balances are genuinely surplus and available, which are operating or restricted, and how to value non-operating assets such as idle land or strategic stakes. All of these must be measured at the valuation date.

Core notes

Where the balances sit in Schedule III

Schedule III line (Division II) Typical content Bridge treatment
Cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.6–7.7) Cash on hand, current accounts, deposits with original maturity ≤ 3 months Add, net of any minimum operating cash
Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents FDs with original maturity 3–12 months; earmarked balances (unpaid dividend), margin money, deposits under lien Add free FDs; exclude earmarked, lien-marked and margin balances
Current investments Liquid/overnight/ultra-short debt MFs, commercial paper, T-bills, typically at FVTPL under Ind AS 109 Add at fair value (NAV) at valuation date
Non-current investments Long-term bonds, tax-free bonds, equity of unrelated listed companies, InvITs/REITs Add if non-operating, at market value
Other non-current financial assets Bank deposits with maturity > 12 months Add if free and unencumbered
Investment property (Ind AS 40) Land/buildings held for rental or appreciation Add at fair value (Ind AS 40 requires fair-value disclosure)
Assets held for sale (Ind AS 105) Disposal groups Add expected net proceeds if their cash flows are excluded from FCFF

Cash: gross vs surplus

The bridge should add surplus cash, which is cash beyond what the operating business needs. Two approaches are used.

  1. Add all unrestricted cash, and treat minimum operating cash inside NWC. In this approach operating cash grows with revenue in the forecast, so ΔNWC captures it.
  2. Add only excess cash, being total unrestricted cash less a minimum cash balance (for example 1–2% of revenue, or one month of cash opex).

Either approach is acceptable if it is consistent. Never deduct minimum cash in the bridge while also including operating cash in NWC.

Restricted and earmarked balances

The following balances are not available to equity holders and must be excluded:

  • Unpaid Dividend Account (Sec 124, Companies Act 2013). Dividends unclaimed within 30 days are transferred to a special account within 7 days. After 7 years they move to IEPF under Sec 125. The balance is matched by an "unpaid dividend" liability. Either exclude both, or include both.
  • Margin money against bank guarantees, LCs and derivatives, and deposits under lien for borrowings. These are encumbered, so either exclude them or net them against the related facility.
  • Escrow and Sec 135 CSR unspent account balances. Unspent CSR on ongoing projects must go to a special bank account under Sec 135(6), so these are earmarked.
  • Cash trapped in jurisdictions with repatriation limits or withholding tax. Haircut these by the tax and friction cost.

Liquid mutual funds and treasury investments

Debt mutual funds are measured at FVTPL, so the carrying amount at the balance sheet date is close to NAV. Roll this forward to the valuation date at the latest NAV. Since the Finance Act 2023, gains on specified mutual funds (those with ≤ 35% in equity, acquired on or after 1 April 2023) are taxed as short-term capital gains at slab or corporate rates, regardless of holding period. When unrealised gains are large, deduct the embedded tax on redemption at the applicable rate. Tax rules are date-sensitive, so verify the regime for the investment vintage.

Non-operating assets

Non-operating assets are assets whose cash flows are excluded from EBIT and FCFF:

  • Idle or surplus land (for example a closed plant site). Value at fair value less selling costs and capital-gains tax. Use a registered valuer's report or circle rates as a floor.
  • Investment property (Ind AS 40). Use the disclosed fair value. Rental income must be excluded from EBIT if this asset is added.
  • Strategic minority stakes in unrelated listed companies (FVOCI equity under Ind AS 109). Use market value, and consider a holding-company discount and tax on gains.
  • Loans to group companies, inter-corporate deposits. Add at recoverable value only if they are genuinely collectible and their interest is excluded from EBIT. Haircut related-party ICDs that are unlikely to return.

The test throughout is the consistency rule: if the income or cash flow from an asset is in FCFF, the asset is not added.

Excel layout

Keep a "Bridge Inputs" block on the DCF sheet. Each line should be linked to the balance-sheet roll-forward and carry a Y/N "include" flag and a haircut %:

=SUMPRODUCT(Bridge_Balance, Bridge_Include_Flag, 1-Bridge_Haircut)

This makes the bridge auditable and lets reviewers toggle items.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative working capital businesses (DMart supplier credit, FMCG distributors) hold cash funded by creditors. Some of that cash is operating, so adding all of it can overstate equity. Test whether cash falls when the cycle normalises.
  • Customer advances and deposits (real estate, education, insurance distribution) fund cash balances. Treat the advances as debt-like or exclude the matching cash.
  • Cash in a partly owned subsidiary belongs partly to NCI. That is handled through NCI deduction at fair value, which already reflects the cash.
  • Promoter-pledged or group-parked funds (ICDs to promoter entities) carry governance risk, so haircut heavily.
  • Tax-free bonds and long-dated G-Secs. Use market value, not face value or amortised cost.
  • Treasury income in EBIT. If the company classifies MF gains in revenue (some NBFC-like holding companies do), remove them from EBIT before adding the investments.

Key takeaways

  • Add only unrestricted, surplus financial assets and non-operating assets, measured at the valuation date.
  • Read the Schedule III "other bank balances" note. Unpaid dividend and margin-money balances are not shareholder cash.
  • Value liquid MFs at NAV and consider tax on unrealised gains under the post-2023 debt-fund regime.
  • Non-operating land, investment property and strategic stakes are added at fair value, net of tax and costs.
  • Never add an asset whose income remains inside EBIT or FCFF.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cash note dissection [L1]

The 30 September 2026 cash notes of a pharma company show the following:

  • cash on hand ₹ 1 crore
  • current accounts ₹ 84 crore
  • FDs < 3 months ₹ 120 crore
  • FDs 3–12 months ₹ 260 crore, of which ₹ 45 crore is under lien for a term loan
  • unpaid dividend account ₹ 4 crore
  • margin money ₹ 22 crore
  • non-current FDs > 12 months ₹ 75 crore (free)
  • liquid MFs at NAV ₹ 410 crore, cost ₹ 370 crore, acquired after 1 April 2023

Compute the cash and liquid investments to add in the bridge at a 25.17% tax rate. State each exclusion.

Q2. Surplus land and investment property [L2]

A textile company owns a closed mill site with book value ₹ 8 crore. A registered valuer's fair value is ₹ 150 crore, selling costs are 2%, and long-term capital gains tax applies at an assumed effective 12.5% on the gain over indexed or actual cost (state your assumption). It also owns an office building leased to third parties, with Ind AS 40 fair value of ₹ 60 crore and rental income of ₹ 4.5 crore currently reported in revenue. Show the bridge additions and the EBIT adjustment required to keep FCFF consistent.

💡 Hint: Check whether the rental income is in EBIT before adding the building.

Q3. Negative working-capital cash judgement [L3]

A value-retailer reports cash of ₹ 1,800 crore and trade payables of ₹ 2,400 crore, against inventory of ₹ 1,900 crore and negligible receivables. Its forecast NWC is negative and becomes more negative as stores grow. Write a review note on how much of the cash should be added in the bridge, how the forecast ΔNWC interacts with this, and what alternative presentation would avoid double counting.

Q4. Excel bridge-input block [L2]

Design the "Bridge Inputs" block for a DCF sheet that lists eight candidate assets. It needs columns for balance at valuation date, include flag (1/0), haircut %, tax on gain and amount added. Write the formulas for the amount-added column and the total. Describe one data-validation rule and one check cell you would add.
