Subtopic note

Deducting Debt, Leases, Preference, NCI and Debt-Like Claims

Scope: Deduct borrowings, lease liabilities (post-116), preference capital, NCI, debt-like items (unfunded gratuity, probable contingencies)

Why it matters

EV belongs to every capital provider. Ordinary shareholders receive only what remains after lenders, lessors, preference holders and minority shareholders in subsidiaries have been satisfied, and after obligations that behave like debt have been settled. In Indian companies these claims are scattered across Schedule III notes: current maturities of long-term debt sit inside "short-term borrowings", lease liabilities appear as a separate financial liability, gratuity sits under provisions, and contingent liabilities are only disclosed. Missing any of them inflates the implied price. Over-deducting, for example deducting an operating provision that is already in NWC, understates it. Due-diligence teams and valuation reviewers check this block first.

Core notes

Borrowings

Deduct total borrowings at the valuation date:

  • non-current borrowings (term loans, NCDs, ECBs, inter-corporate borrowings)
  • current borrowings, which include current maturities of long-term debt under the 2021 Schedule III amendment
  • interest accrued but not due on borrowings (in other current financial liabilities)
  • bills discounted with recourse and supplier-finance or reverse-factoring arrangements that are economically borrowing. The IASB's 2023 supplier-finance disclosure amendments to IAS 7 and IFRS 7 help you identify these arrangements. Check the MCA notification status of the corresponding Ind AS 7 and Ind AS 107 changes for the reporting period.

Measurement. Book value (amortised cost under Ind AS 109) is the default. For fixed-rate NCDs whose market yields have moved materially, fair value is theoretically superior. Use it when the difference is material and the WACC Kd is also market-based.

FX debt. Translate at the valuation-date rate. If the debt is hedged, add the mark-to-market of the hedge derivative (asset or liability) so the bridge reflects the net economic obligation.

Lease liabilities (post-Ind AS 116)

If FCFF is built on post-116 EBITDA and does not deduct lease payments, the present value of the lease obligations is a financing claim and must be deducted. Take the current plus non-current lease liabilities at the valuation date. The same balance should enter the WACC debt weight (topic t2, s07), and the forecast must treat new ROU additions as reinvestment (topic t1, c06).

If FCFF is pre-116 and deducts rent, do not deduct the lease liability. For mixed portfolios (short-term or low-value leases expensed under the Ind AS 116.5 exemption), the expensed rentals stay in EBITDA either way.

Preference capital

Ind AS 32 classifies preference shares by substance:

Instrument Ind AS 32 classification Bridge treatment
Redeemable, cumulative preference shares Financial liability (in borrowings) Deduct at redemption value or PV, plus arrears of dividend
Compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS) at a fixed ratio Equity Do not deduct. Add conversion shares to the diluted count (if-converted)
Optionally convertible preference shares Compound or liability Deduct the debt value if conversion is out of the money; otherwise if-converted

Avoid double counting. A redeemable preference share already reported within borrowings must not be deducted again on a separate "preference capital" line.

Non-controlling interests (NCI)

Consolidated FCFF includes 100% of every subsidiary's cash flows (Ind AS 110). The minority's share of that value must be removed. The options are:

  1. Fair value of the NCI (preferred). NCI % × the subsidiary's equity value, derived from its own DCF or a multiple of its PAT or EBITDA.
  2. Market value, if the subsidiary is separately listed. NCI % × subsidiary market cap.
  3. Book value (the NCI line in equity). This is quick, but it usually understates the claim for profitable subsidiaries because book is below fair value.

A useful proxy is NCI share of profit × the group P/E, which roughly approximates fair value when the subsidiary has similar growth and risk to the group.

Debt-like items

These items are claims on the company that FCFF does not service. Deduct them net of any tax shield:

Item Source Treatment
Unfunded gratuity / leave encashment (Ind AS 19 net defined-benefit liability) Provisions note, actuarial report Deduct the deficit (DBO − plan assets) × (1 − t). The deduction is allowed on payment basis under Sec 43B/40A(7)
Probable contingencies (Ind AS 37) Provisions; contingent-liabilities note Deduct recognised provisions for disputes. For disclosed contingent liabilities, deduct a probability-weighted expected outflow for material tax/legal cases
Unpaid statutory dues (GST, PF, TDS overdue) Other current liabilities Deduct overdue amounts beyond the normal cycle
Deferred / contingent acquisition consideration Other financial liabilities (Ind AS 103) Deduct at fair value
Put options to NCI holders (written puts) Financial liability under Ind AS 32 Deduct; avoid also deducting the same NCI
Decommissioning / ARO provisions Ind AS 37 / 16 Deduct if future outflows are not in FCFF
Declared but unpaid dividends after valuation date Liability Deduct (cash will leave)
Customer advances in long-cycle businesses Contract liabilities (Ind AS 115) Debt-like if treated as financing; otherwise part of NWC

The rule: if the outflow already appears in forecast FCFF (for example normal annual gratuity cost inside employee expenses), deduct only the past-service deficit. Do not deduct future service cost again.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Overdraft and cash credit that are permanently drawn are debt, even though they are classified as current. Do not net them into NWC.
  • Seasonal debt. If debt at the valuation date is unusually high or low because of seasonal NWC (sugar, fertiliser), use an average, or adjust NWC consistently.
  • Income-tax demands and disputes. Deduct expected outflow, not the full disclosed amount, and document the probability with reference to precedent rulings.
  • Deferred tax liabilities are generally not deducted. Their reversal is reflected in the cash-tax forecast. Deduct only if the model uses book tax and the DTL has a near-term cash reversal.
  • Hybrid perpetual bonds classified as equity under Ind AS 32 are still senior claims economically, so deduct them.
  • NCI with losses. Book NCI can be negative. Fair value cannot be below zero for a limited-liability claim.

Key takeaways

  • Deduct every claim that FCFF does not service: debt, leases (if post-116), preference, NCI, and debt-like items.
  • Follow Ind AS 32 substance for preference shares. CCPS go to the share count, not the deductions.
  • Prefer the fair value of NCI over book NCI, especially for profitable listed subsidiaries.
  • Tax-effect deductible obligations such as gratuity, and probability-weight contingencies.
  • Never deduct an outflow that is also inside forecast FCFF or NWC.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying claims from Schedule III notes [L1]

From the following, compute total deductions for a post-116 DCF at a 25.17% tax rate:

  • non-current borrowings ₹ 900 crore
  • current borrowings ₹ 350 crore (including ₹ 120 crore current maturities)
  • interest accrued ₹ 14 crore
  • lease liabilities ₹ 260 crore
  • 9% cumulative redeemable preference shares ₹ 75 crore, already within non-current borrowings, with dividend arrears of ₹ 7 crore
  • CCPS ₹ 50 crore
  • book NCI ₹ 60 crore (NCI share of PAT ₹ 12 crore, group P/E 22×)
  • gratuity DBO ₹ 95 crore and plan assets ₹ 70 crore
  • disclosed contingent liabilities ₹ 140 crore, of which you estimate 20% expected outflow

Q2. Written put to minority holders [L2]

A parent holds 70% of a subsidiary and has written a put option to the 30% holders, exercisable at fair value in 2028. Under its accounting policy, the obligation is recognised as a financial liability of ₹ 480 crore and the NCI is derecognised. Explain how the bridge should treat the NCI and the put liability. Describe the double-count risk.

Q3. Gratuity: deficit vs service cost [L2]

Employee benefit expense in the forecast includes ₹ 12 crore a year of gratuity current service cost. The actuarial report shows a DBO of ₹ 150 crore and plan assets of ₹ 90 crore. A colleague proposes deducting the full ₹ 150 crore DBO in the bridge. Critique the proposal and state the correct deduction with justification.

💡 Hint: Separate past service already earned from future service already in FCFF.

Q4. Hybrid instruments judgement [L3]

A power company has ₹ 1,000 crore of perpetual, unsecured, non-cumulative bonds with an issuer call after five years, classified as equity under Ind AS 32. It also has ₹ 400 crore of OCPS (optionally convertible at ₹ 250 against a CMP of ₹ 180). Prepare a short memo recommending the bridge treatment for each instrument. Explain the effect on the WACC weights.
