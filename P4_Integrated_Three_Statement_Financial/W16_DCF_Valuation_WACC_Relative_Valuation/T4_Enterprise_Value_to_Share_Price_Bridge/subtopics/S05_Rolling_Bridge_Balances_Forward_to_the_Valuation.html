Subtopic note

Rolling Bridge Balances Forward to the Valuation Date

Scope: Roll-forward of balances to valuation date

Why it matters

The PV of FCFF is measured at the valuation date. It captures only cash flows that arise after that date. Every balance in the bridge (cash, investments, borrowings, leases, NCI, debt-like items) must therefore be measured at the same date. Indian companies close books on 31 March, but valuations happen throughout the year: fairness opinions, ESOP valuations, Rule 11UA reports, research initiations and acquisition bids. Using the 31 March balance sheet for a November valuation either double-counts cash already generated between April and November or ignores it. The same applies to debt repaid, dividends paid and capex spent. The roll-forward is the bridge between the last reported balance sheet and the valuation date.

Core notes

The timing identity

Let the last reported balance sheet date be \(B\), the valuation date be \(V\), and the first forecast year-end be \(F\). The DCF counts cash flows in \((V, F]\) and beyond. Cash flows in \((B, V]\) have already happened. They are reflected in the valuation-date balance sheet, not in the PV.

\[ \text{Net debt}_V = \text{Net debt}_B - FCFF_{(B,V]} + \text{Dividends}_{(B,V]} + \text{Buybacks}_{(B,V]} - \text{Equity raised}_{(B,V]} + \text{Acquisitions}_{(B,V]} + \text{New leases}_{(B,V]} \pm \text{FX and fair-value moves} \]

Net debt here means borrowings plus lease liabilities less cash and liquid investments.

The pairing must be consistent:

Approach Cash flows discounted Net debt used
A (correct) Stub FCFF for \((V, F]\) plus later years Net debt at \(V\)
B (correct, approximation) Full FY1 FCFF for \((B, F]\), discounted to \(B\). Equity value at \(B\) is then rolled to \(V\): \(E_V \approx E_B \times (1+K_e)^{s'} - \text{Dividends}_{(B,V]}\), where \(s'\) is the fraction of a year from \(B\) to \(V\) Net debt at \(B\)
C (wrong) Stub or full FY1 from \(V\) Net debt at \(B\)

Approach A is the market standard for listed companies.

Sources of valuation-date balances

  1. Latest published interim balance sheet. Listed entities publish a half-yearly statement of assets and liabilities with their 30 September results under SEBI LODR Regulation 33(3)(f), within 45 days of the half-year end. Q1 and Q3 results usually lack a balance sheet, but debt and cash are often disclosed in investor presentations.
  2. Management accounts / MIS for unlisted companies, or for a date close to \(V\). Request a trial balance and a debt schedule from lenders.
  3. Model roll-forward. If no balance sheet exists near \(V\), forecast \(B \to V\) using the three-statement model's monthly or quarterly split, and true it up to the latest data points (debt-ladder disclosures, cash in investor decks).

Net debt (borrowings + leases − cash − FDs − MFs) moved from ₹ 1,120 crore to ₹ 920 crore. The ₹ 200 crore reduction should reconcile approximately to H1 FCFF less the H1 dividend, adjusted for new leases and NAV gains. If it does not, find the gap before using the balances.

Valuation date after the half-year

For a valuation on 15 November 2026, the 30 September balance sheet is 46 days old. The options are:

  • treat 30 September as \(V\) (a practical convenience, disclosed), with stub \(s = 0.5\)
  • roll forward six more weeks using MIS, and set \(s = \text{YEARFRAC}(15\text{-Nov}, 31\text{-Mar})\)
  • in research practice, value at the balance-sheet date and roll the price target forward 12 months

The market price used for upside must be at the same date as \(V\), or the difference must be acknowledged.

Price-sensitive events between B and V

Adjust for material events even if they are not yet in any balance sheet:

  • dividends declared but not yet paid, which are deducted
  • QIP or preferential issue proceeds received, which are added to cash and to shares
  • an acquisition completed, which adds the target's EV contribution or is otherwise modelled
  • a large tax refund or demand that has crystallised
  • a buyback that has been completed, which reduces cash and shares

Excel implementation

Build a "BS Roll-Forward" tab with columns B, movement, V. Link movements to the quarterly split of the forecast or to actual interim figures, with a source tag per cell. The bridge on the DCF sheet references column V only. Add a check cell:

=ABS((NetDebt_B - NetDebt_V) - (FCFF_BtoV - Div_BtoV - NewLeases_BtoV + Equity_BtoV + Other_BtoV)) < Tolerance

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Seasonal businesses. A 30 September balance sheet for a festive-season retailer may show peak inventory and peak borrowing. Using it as \(V\) with full stub FCFF is still consistent, provided the stub FCFF includes the release of working capital from October to March.
  • Fiscal mismatch in subsidiaries with December year-ends. Align them to the group's reporting date before rolling forward.
  • FX-denominated balances. Re-translate at the valuation-date RBI reference or FBIL rate.
  • NCI and associate market values are measured at \(V\), so use the CMP on the valuation date, not at B.
  • Shares outstanding must also be at \(V\). ESOP exercises, allotments and buybacks after B change the count.
  • No interim data. Disclose the estimation basis and sensitivity. A model roll-forward is acceptable but carries estimation risk that reviewers will question.

Key takeaways

  • Bridge balances and share count must be measured at the same date as the PVs.
  • Pair stub-period FCFF with valuation-date net debt. Never pair it with year-end net debt.
  • Use the LODR half-yearly balance sheet, MIS or a reconciled model roll-forward.
  • Reconcile the net-debt movement to interim FCFF, dividends, leases and equity flows.
  • Capture price-sensitive events between the last balance sheet and the valuation date explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Roll-forward reconciliation [L2]

A company reports net debt of ₹ 640 crore at 31 March 2026. Between April and September 2026:

  • FCFF ₹ 210 crore
  • dividend paid ₹ 60 crore
  • new leases recognised ₹ 25 crore
  • QIP proceeds ₹ 300 crore
  • acquisition of a small brand for ₹ 90 crore cash
  • FX loss on ECB ₹ 8 crore

Compute the expected net debt at 30 September 2026. The H1 balance sheet shows ₹ 420 crore. Investigate the difference and list possible explanations.

Q2. Approach A vs approach C quantified [L2]

Valuation date is 30 September 2026. FY27 full-year FCFF is ₹ 300 crore, of which ₹ 140 crore was generated in H1. WACC is 12%. Net debt is ₹ 1,000 crore at 31 March 2026 and ₹ 900 crore at 30 September 2026 (after a ₹ 40 crore dividend). Compute the FY27 contribution to equity value under approach A and approach C, and the resulting error in equity value.

💡 Hint: In approach C, the H1 cash is counted twice in one direction or ignored in the other. Work out which.

Q3. Mid-November valuation design [L3]

You are preparing a fairness opinion dated 18 November 2026 for a scheme of amalgamation of two NSE-listed companies. The latest balance sheets are at 30 September 2026. Design the roll-forward approach, data requests to management, cut-off of share prices (consider SEBI's pricing guidance for schemes) and disclosures you would include. Explain how you would set the stub fraction.

Q4. Shares at valuation date [L1]

Between 31 March and 30 September 2026, a company allotted 0.4 crore shares on ESOP exercise, completed a buyback of 0.9 crore shares at ₹ 520, and issued 0.25 crore shares on CCD conversion. Basic shares at 31 March were 30.0 crore. Compute basic shares at the valuation date and explain how each event affects cash, debt and the dilution schedule.
