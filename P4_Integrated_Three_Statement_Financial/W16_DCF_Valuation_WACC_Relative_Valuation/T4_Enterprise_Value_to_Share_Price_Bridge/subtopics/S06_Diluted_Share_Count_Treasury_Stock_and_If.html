Subtopic note

Diluted Share Count: Treasury Stock and If-Converted Methods

Scope: Diluted share count: treasury stock method for ESOPs, if-converted for convertibles

Why it matters

Equity value belongs to everyone who holds, or can acquire, ordinary shares. Indian new-age and mid-cap companies often have ESOP pools of 3–8% of share capital, RSUs, CCDs, CCPS and warrants issued on preferential allotments. Dividing by basic shares overstates value per share. Dividing by every instrument at face value, ignoring strike proceeds, understates it. The treasury stock method (TSM) and the if-converted method are the two standard mechanics. They are the same mechanics Ind AS 33 uses for diluted EPS, but applied with the valuation price instead of the period-average market price.

Core notes

Building blocks

Instrument Method Shares added
ESOPs / stock options (vested and unvested, in-the-money) TSM \(n \times (1 - K/P)\)
RSUs / options with nil or nominal strike TSM (strike ≈ 0) ≈ \(n\)
Share warrants (preferential, with upfront 25% paid under SEBI ICDR) TSM on the unpaid balance \(n \times (1 - K_{\text{unpaid}}/P)\)
Compulsorily convertible debentures/preference shares (CCDs/CCPS) If-converted, always Conversion shares. Remove the instrument from debt deductions
Optionally convertible (OCDs, FCCBs, OCPS) If-converted only if conversion value > redemption value; otherwise treat as debt Conversion shares, or none
Partly paid shares Add the unpaid call as an asset, count full shares; or count shares pro-rata Consistent with the Ind AS 33 application guidance on partly paid shares

Here \(n\) is the number of options, \(K\) the exercise price and \(P\) the share price used.

Treasury stock method

The TSM assumes that all in-the-money (ITM) options are exercised and that the company uses the strike proceeds to buy back shares at price \(P\). The net new shares are:

\[ \Delta N = \sum_{i:\,K_i < P} n_i \left(1 - \frac{K_i}{P}\right) \]

Options with \(K_i \ge P\) are anti-dilutive and are excluded (Ind AS 33.41 and 33.46).

Which price? For a trading-comps market cap, use CMP. For a DCF implied price, use the implied price itself. This creates a circularity (see concept c03), which has a closed-form solution when all options are ITM:

\[ P = \frac{E + \sum n_i K_i}{N + \sum n_i} \]

where \(E\) is equity value and \(N\) basic shares. This is equivalent to adding the strike proceeds to equity and counting all options as shares.

Which options? Ind AS 33 treats unvested options with only service conditions as potential ordinary shares. In valuation, most practitioners include all outstanding ITM options, vested and unvested, because unvested options will largely vest. A more refined approach adjusts for expected forfeiture. Options with unmet performance conditions are included only if the conditions are expected to be met. Ind AS 102 disclosures provide the tranche data: numbers outstanding, weighted-average exercise prices and exercise-price ranges. The SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021 also require annual-report disclosures of grants, exercises and lapses.

Granular vs aggregated. Apply the TSM tranche by tranche using the exercise-price ranges in the notes. Aggregating at the weighted-average strike can wrongly include OTM tranches or exclude ITM ones.

If-converted method

For convertibles, assume conversion if it is beneficial to the holder:

  • add the conversion shares, which are the face value divided by the conversion price, or the number set by the fixed ratio
  • do not deduct the instrument as debt in the bridge, because it becomes equity
  • in a DCF the interest add-back is irrelevant (FCFF is pre-interest), but keep the WACC weights consistent. A convertible counted as equity in the share count should not be in the debt weight.

If the conversion is out of the money (the conversion price is well above the implied price for OCDs or FCCBs), treat the instrument as debt. If the outcome is close, a more precise approach values the conversion option separately (Black-Scholes or binomial). That is uncommon in practice for Indian mid-caps.

Worked illustration

Equity value is ₹ 7,000 crore and basic shares are 22.0 crore. Outstanding instruments:

Tranche Options (crore) Strike (₹)
ESOP 2021 0.50 180
ESOP 2023 0.30 450
RSUs 0.05 10
CCDs ₹ 200 crore at ₹ 320 0.625 shares n.a.

The CCDs are compulsorily convertible. Count 0.625 crore shares and do not deduct the ₹ 200 crore as debt, so equity becomes ₹ 7,000 crore with no CCD deduction. Basic plus CCD shares = 22.625 crore.

First pass at a price of 7,000 ÷ 22.625 = ₹ 309.4:

  • the ESOP 2021 tranche is ITM: \(0.50 \times (1 - 180/309.4)\) = 0.209
  • the ESOP 2023 tranche is OTM, so it is excluded
  • RSUs: \(0.05 \times (1 - 10/309.4)\) = 0.048

Diluted shares = 22.882 crore, giving price ₹ 305.9.

Iterating again changes the TSM shares only marginally. The closed form for the ITM set gives \(P = (7{,}000 + 0.50 \times 180 + 0.05 \times 10) \div (22.625 + 0.55)\) = ₹ 305.95. The ESOP 2023 tranche remains OTM at ₹ 306, so the solution is stable.

Excel implementation

TSM shares per tranche:  =IF(Strike<Price, Options*(1-Strike/Price), 0)
Diluted shares:          =Basic + Conv_Shares + SUM(TSM_Range)
Implied price:           =Equity_Value / Diluted_Shares

To avoid circular references, either iterate with File → Options → Formulas → Enable iterative calculation (keep a breaker toggle), or compute the closed form across the ITM set and then verify ITM status with a check cell.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • ESOP trust structures. Shares held by an employee welfare trust are already issued. Many Indian companies consolidate the trust and present those shares as treasury shares, deducted from equity. Remove trust-held shares from the basic count, then add them back through TSM as options are granted.
  • Cash-settled SARs are a liability under Ind AS 102. Deduct their fair value in the bridge rather than adding shares.
  • Warrants with 25% upfront (SEBI ICDR preferential issues). The upfront payment is already in cash, so only the balance 75% is the strike in the TSM.
  • Contingently issuable shares (earn-outs settled in shares). Include them if the conditions are expected to be met.
  • Buyback-authorised shares. Ignore them until executed.
  • Comps consistency. Use the same TSM approach at CMP for each peer's diluted market cap (topic t6, s02).

Key takeaways

  • Divide equity value by diluted, not basic, shares.
  • TSM: ITM options add \(n(1 - K/P)\). Anti-dilutive tranches are excluded. Work tranche by tranche.
  • Use the implied price in a DCF, which needs iteration or the closed form \(P = (E + \Sigma nK) \div (N + \Sigma n)\) for ITM options.
  • If-converted: add the conversion shares and remove the instrument from debt. Never do both or neither.
  • Use Ind AS 102 and SEBI SBEB disclosures for tranche data. Treat cash-settled awards as liabilities.

Practice Questions

Q1. Tranche-level TSM at CMP [L1]

CMP is ₹ 640 and basic shares are 48.0 crore. Option tranches (crore options, strike): 0.60 at ₹ 210; 0.45 at ₹ 520; 0.30 at ₹ 700; 0.10 RSUs at ₹ 5. Compute TSM dilution and diluted shares. Then compute them again using a single weighted-average strike, and explain the difference.

Q2. Implied-price iteration [L2]

Equity value is ₹ 12,800 crore and basic shares are 40.0 crore. Options: 1.2 crore at ₹ 150 and 0.8 crore at ₹ 330. Convertible: FCCB of ₹ 600 crore (USD-denominated, translated) convertible at ₹ 350, otherwise redeemable at par, currently deducted as debt. Determine the implied price, deciding whether to treat the FCCB as converted. Show how you test the stability of the solution.

💡 Hint: Test conversion against the resulting implied price, not CMP.

Q3. ESOP trust and treasury shares [L2]

A company's basic shares are 15.0 crore, including 0.40 crore held by its ESOP trust that are presented as treasury shares in consolidated equity. Options outstanding are 0.35 crore at ₹ 120, and the implied price is ₹ 410. Compute the correct diluted share count, explaining why the trust shares should not be counted twice.

Q4. Excel build without circularity [L3]

Design a dilution schedule for 6 option tranches, 1 CCPS series and 1 OCD series. It must compute the implied price without enabling iterative calculation, flag any tranche whose ITM status flips between CMP and implied price, and report dilution as % of basic shares. Specify the formulas and check cells.
