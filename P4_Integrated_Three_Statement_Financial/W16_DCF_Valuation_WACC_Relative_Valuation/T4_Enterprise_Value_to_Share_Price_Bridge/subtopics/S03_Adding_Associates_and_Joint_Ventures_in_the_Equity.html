Subtopic note

Adding Associates and Joint Ventures in the Equity Bridge

Scope: Add associates/JVs (market or book value) when their income is excluded from FCFF

Why it matters

Indian groups often hold value through equity-accounted stakes. Examples include automotive companies with JV engine plants, cement groups with captive power JVs, conglomerates with listed associates, and pharma companies with marketing JVs. Under Ind AS 28 the investor reports only a single line, "share of profit/(loss) of associates and joint ventures", below EBIT. It does not consolidate revenue, EBITDA, capex or debt. A DCF that starts from consolidated EBIT therefore captures none of the associate's value. Unless the stake is added in the bridge, equity is understated. The amounts can be large: in some holding-company structures the associate stakes exceed the value of the operating business.

Core notes

Why the add-back is needed

Under Ind AS 28.10 the equity method initially recognises the investment at cost. It is then adjusted for the investor's share of post-acquisition profit or loss and OCI, less dividends received. In the consolidated statement of profit and loss, the share of profit sits after finance costs and before tax in most Indian presentations. It is shown after "profit before share of profit of associates/JVs and exceptional items", so it is outside EBIT.

The flows behave as follows:

  • The associate's operating cash flows are not in consolidated FCFF.
  • Dividends received are in investing cash flows (or operating, by policy choice under Ind AS 7.31–7.33), but FCFF built from EBIT ignores them.
  • The associate's debt is not in consolidated borrowings.

The consistent treatment is therefore to add the value of the stake in the bridge. It is an equity-level asset, so no debt or NCI adjustment is needed for it.

Measurement choices

Basis When to use Caveats
Market value (listed associate) = shares held × CMP Listed, liquid associates Consider a holding-company discount (commonly 20–50% in Indian market practice) and tax on eventual sale
Own DCF or multiple of the associate Material unlisted associates/JVs with data available Use the associate's own WACC and risk profile, then value the stake as % × associate equity value
P/B or P/E of peers applied to share of book value or profit Medium-size unlisted stakes Choose a multiple consistent with the associate's sector
Book value (Ind AS 28 carrying amount) Immaterial stakes or no better information Carrying amount reflects historical cost plus retained share of profit, not fair value

Fair value is preferred whenever it is reliably available. Book value is a fallback and should be flagged as such. Ind AS 112 requires summarised financial information for material associates and JVs. Use it to check reasonableness, for example the implied P/B of the stake.

Jointly controlled operations vs JVs

Under Ind AS 111, a joint operation is accounted for line by line (the investor recognises its share of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses). Its cash flows are already in EBIT and FCFF, so there is no bridge addition. Only joint ventures under the equity method are added. Read the Ind AS 111 classification note before adding anything.

Tax on the stake

If the bridge assumes the stake could be monetised, consider capital-gains tax on the excess over cost. If the stake is strategic and will be held, many practitioners skip the tax but apply a holding discount. State the choice either way.

Dividends from Indian companies are taxable in the shareholder's hands under the post-2020 regime. Under Sec 80M, a domestic company receiving dividends may deduct them to the extent it distributes dividends onward, before the due date of return filing. This affects the net value of the cash stream to the parent.

Worked illustration

Shakti Auto Components holds 30% of Shakti Castings Ltd, which is listed.

Item Value
Shares held 1.50 crore
Associate CMP ₹ 273.3
Market value of stake ₹ 410 crore
Ind AS 28 carrying amount ₹ 260 crore
Holding discount applied 20%
Bridge addition (market basis) ₹ 328 crore
Bridge addition (book basis) ₹ 260 crore

The share of profit of ₹ 31 crore is below EBIT in Shakti's consolidated P&L. The DCF's EBIT excludes it, so the addition is consistent. Had the model taken EBIT after adding share of associate profit (some analysts pull "EBIT" from "profit before interest and tax including share of associates"), the associate would be double-counted if it were also added in the bridge.

Excel implementation

Associate_Value = IF(Assoc_Basis="Market", Shares_Held*Assoc_CMP*(1-Holdco_Disc), Assoc_Carrying)
EBIT check cell: =IF(ABS(EBIT_Model - (PBT + Finance_Cost - Other_Income - Share_Assoc - Exceptional))<0.5,"OK","CHECK")

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Loss-making associate with carrying value already zero (Ind AS 28.38–39 stops recognising losses below zero unless there are obligations). If the investor has guaranteed the associate's debt, the guarantee is a debt-like item to deduct, not an asset to add.
  • Associate that is a key supplier or customer. Transfer pricing may shift profit into or out of the operating business. Value the combined economics rather than double-count margins.
  • Step-up to subsidiary after the valuation date. Value the stake as an associate at the valuation date. Model the acquisition separately.
  • Subsidiary held for sale. If its results are presented as discontinued under Ind AS 105 and excluded from FCFF, add the expected net proceeds.
  • Cross-holdings in which the associate holds shares of the parent. Adjust for the circular holding, or treat those shares like treasury shares.
  • Comps consistency. If associates are added in the DCF bridge, they must be subtracted when computing peer EVs in trading comps, as non-operating investments (topic t6, c01).

Key takeaways

  • Equity-accounted associates and JVs sit outside EBIT, so their value must be added in the bridge.
  • Prefer market or intrinsic value. Use the Ind AS 28 carrying amount only as a documented fallback.
  • Joint operations under Ind AS 111 are already in EBIT, so do not add them.
  • Apply holding-company discounts and tax judgements explicitly and show them as separate cells.
  • Mirror the treatment in comps EV to keep multiples consistent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Market vs book basis [L1]

A cement company holds 26% of a listed power JV. It owns 4.2 crore shares at a CMP of ₹ 118, and the Ind AS 28 carrying amount is ₹ 310 crore. Its 49% stake in an unlisted packaging JV has carrying amount ₹ 95 crore, with the JV's PAT ₹ 40 crore and peer P/E 14×. Compute the bridge addition under (a) market/multiple basis with a 25% holding discount on the listed stake and (b) book basis. Discuss which you would present.

Q2. Detecting double counting [L2]

A model's "EBIT" row equals PBT plus finance costs. The company reports other income of ₹ 55 crore, share of profit of associates of ₹ 42 crore and an exceptional loss of ₹ 18 crore. The bridge adds associates at ₹ 520 crore and treasury investments at ₹ 700 crore. Identify the errors, restate EBIT, and explain the effect on EV and on equity value.

💡 Hint: List every item that sits between EBIT and PBT in a Schedule III P&L.

Q3. Guarantee for a loss-making JV [L3]

An auto OEM's 50:50 JV has negative net worth. The OEM's Ind AS 28 carrying amount is nil, and the OEM has guaranteed ₹ 200 crore of the JV's bank debt. The JV is expected to need ₹ 120 crore of further support over three years. Explain how this JV should appear in the bridge, which Ind AS paragraphs inform the treatment, and what disclosure you would include in the valuation report.

Q4. Joint operation classification [L2]

A refinery operator holds a 40% participating interest in an oil block, accounted for as a joint operation under Ind AS 111, and a 50% stake in a lubricants JV accounted for under the equity method. Explain which of the two enters the bridge, and why. Describe the evidence from the financial statements you would cite.
