Key concept

Bridge Consistency Rule: Assets Outside FCFF, Claims Not Serviced

Concept: Consistency rule: add assets whose income is excluded from FCFF; deduct claims not serviced within FCFF

Statement

For every balance-sheet item \(X\) at the valuation date:

\[ \text{Bridge treatment}(X) = \begin{cases} +\,FV(X) & \text{if } X \text{ is an asset and its cash flows are excluded from FCFF} \\ -\,FV(X) & \text{if } X \text{ is a claim and its servicing is excluded from FCFF} \\ 0 & \text{if } X\text{'s cash flows are already inside FCFF} \end{cases} \]

where \(FV(X)\) is fair value at the valuation date, net of tax and transaction frictions where relevant.

Explanation

FCFF is defined at the operating level, before interest and before any return to capital providers. EV therefore captures exactly what flows through EBIT, tax on EBIT, D&A, capex and ΔNWC. Anything else must be reconciled in the bridge, and only once.

Item Cash flow in FCFF? Bridge
Surplus cash, liquid MFs No. Treasury income is excluded from EBIT as other income Add
Operating cash in NWC Yes, through ΔNWC Do not add (or add all cash and exclude it from NWC; choose one)
Associates/JVs (Ind AS 28) No. Share of profit is below EBIT Add
Joint operations (Ind AS 111) Yes. Line-by-line in EBIT Do not add
Borrowings No. Interest is excluded Deduct
Lease liabilities, post-116 FCFF No. Rent is excluded from EBITDA Deduct
Lease liabilities, pre-116 FCFF (rent deducted) Yes Do not deduct
Unfunded gratuity: past-service deficit No. Only current service cost is in opex Deduct (net of tax)
Trade payables, provisions for warranties Yes, through NWC or opex Do not deduct
NCI Yes, 100% of the subsidiary's cash flows are in consolidated FCFF Deduct the minority's share
Rental income from investment property Depends on whether it is in EBIT Add the property only if rent is excluded

Diagnostic question for each line: "Where does this item's cash flow appear in my forecast?" If the answer is "in FCFF", leave it out of the bridge. If the answer is "nowhere", it belongs in the bridge.

The same rule applies symmetrically to:

  • WACC weights. Claims deducted in the bridge (debt, leases, preference) are the claims whose cost is blended into WACC.
  • Comps EV. Items added in the DCF bridge are subtracted in the comps EV, and items deducted in the bridge are added in the comps EV.

Illustration

A QSR chain reports the following:

  • post-116 EBITDA ₹ 620 crore and cash rent ₹ 210 crore
  • lease liabilities ₹ 1,400 crore
  • cash ₹ 380 crore, of which ₹ 60 crore is minimum operating cash already modelled in NWC
  • a 49% delivery-tech associate carried at ₹ 90 crore, with share of loss below EBIT
  • franchise deposits received ₹ 45 crore, modelled in NWC
Item Treatment under post-116 FCFF Bridge
Lease liabilities Rent excluded from FCFF; ROU additions in reinvestment −1,400
Cash 380 (of which 60 operating in NWC) Surplus portion only +320
Associate Excluded from EBIT + value (₹ 90 crore book, or fair value)
Franchise deposits Already in NWC 0

If the model instead deducted rent in FCFF (pre-116) and deducted the ₹ 1,400 crore lease liability, value would be understated by roughly the lease liability. That is the double-count pitfall.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify ten items [L1]

For a post-116 FCFF DCF, classify each item as add, deduct or ignore, with a one-line reason:

  1. FDs under lien for working-capital facilities
  2. a 26% JV in an Ind AS 111 joint operation
  3. customer advances in a capital-goods company where the forecast NWC includes them
  4. accrued interest on NCDs
  5. deferred consideration on an acquisition
  6. investment property whose rent is included in revenue
  7. unfunded leave encashment (past service)
  8. dividend declared after the valuation date
  9. deferred tax liability on accelerated depreciation
  10. CCPS

Q2. Rebuild an inconsistent bridge [L2]

A model's FCFF includes "other income" of ₹ 75 crore in EBIT. Its bridge adds liquid investments of ₹ 1,100 crore (yield 6.8%) and deducts lease liabilities of ₹ 500 crore, while FCFF also deducts cash rent of ₹ 110 crore. Restate FCFF and the bridge consistently, using either framing, and explain the directional effect on equity value.

💡 Hint: Fix one inconsistency at a time and decide the lease framing first.

Q3. Interview: the one rule for the bridge [L1]

An interviewer asks: "If you could give a junior analyst one rule for building the EV-to-equity bridge, what would it be?" State the rule. Illustrate it with one asset and one claim that juniors commonly mishandle in Indian companies.
