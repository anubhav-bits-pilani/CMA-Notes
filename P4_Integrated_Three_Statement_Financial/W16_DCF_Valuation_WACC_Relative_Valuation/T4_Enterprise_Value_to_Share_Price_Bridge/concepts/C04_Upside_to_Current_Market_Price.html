Key concept

Upside to Current Market Price

Concept: Upside = Implied price ÷ CMP − 1

Statement

\[ U = \frac{P_{\text{implied}}}{\text{CMP}} - 1 \]

where:

  • \(U\) is upside, or downside if negative, expressed as a percentage
  • \(P_{\text{implied}}\) is the DCF or blended implied price per diluted share at the valuation date
  • \(\text{CMP}\) is the current market price on the same date: the NSE closing price, or a short-window VWAP for volatile or illiquid stocks

For a 12-month target price \(TP\), the expected total shareholder return is:

\[ TSR_{12m} = \frac{TP - \text{CMP} + DPS_{12m}}{\text{CMP}} \]

Explanation

Upside is the headline output of an equity valuation. It translates intrinsic value into an investment signal. Three points of discipline apply.

  1. Date alignment. Implied price and CMP must refer to the same date. A DCF valued at 30 September with a CMP from 20 November mixes two information sets. Restate one of them, or disclose the gap.

  2. Upside is not return. Today's upside assumes the gap closes immediately. A 12-month target rolls value forward at Ke less dividends. The expected return, \(TSR_{12m}\), should be compared with Ke. A stock with 10% upside and a Ke of 13% is not attractive on a 12-month basis unless the gap is expected to close quickly.

  3. Ranges over points. Present upside across the sensitivity grid (topic t5, s04) rather than as a single number. A base-case upside of +20% that turns into −10% at +100 bp WACC is a much weaker call than one that stays positive across the grid.

For blended valuations, for example 70% DCF and 30% comps, compute upside on the blended price. Show each component's upside separately so readers can see what drives the call.

Under SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014, research reports must disclose the rating definitions and the basis of the target price. Upside is therefore a disclosed, auditable number, not a slogan.

Illustration

Item Value
Implied price (DCF), 30 Sep 2026 ₹ 550.4
CMP (NSE close), 30 Sep 2026 ₹ 405.0
Upside today 35.9%
Ke 13.2%
DPS next 12 months ₹ 6.0
12-month TP = 550.4 × 1.132 − 6.0 ₹ 617.1
Expected TSR (617.1 − 405 + 6) ÷ 405 53.9%
Upside range across WACC ±100 bp × g ±50 bp grid +10% to +77%

The call is robust: upside stays positive across the grid. A reviewer would still probe why the market discounts the stock so heavily (topic t4, s07).

Practice Questions

Q1. Upside vs TSR [L1]

Implied price is ₹ 2,340 and CMP is ₹ 2,150. Ke is 12.5% and DPS is ₹ 40. Compute today's upside, the 12-month TP using \(TP = P \times (1 + K_e) - DPS\), and the TSR. Comment on whether the stock is attractive relative to Ke.

Q2. Blended price upside [L2]

DCF implied price is ₹ 880, trading comps (median EV/EBITDA) give ₹ 760, and precedents give ₹ 950. Weights are 50/30/20. CMP is ₹ 810. Compute the blended price, the blended upside and each component's upside. Write a two-line explanation of what drives the result.

💡 Hint: Weighting prices and weighting upsides give the same answer only if CMP is common. Confirm this.

Q3. Stale CMP review point [L2]

A draft initiation report dated 25 November 2026 shows a DCF as of 30 September 2026 with upside computed against the 30 September CMP of ₹ 640. CMP on 24 November is ₹ 712 after strong Q2 results. Write the review comment and the two acceptable ways to fix the presentation.
