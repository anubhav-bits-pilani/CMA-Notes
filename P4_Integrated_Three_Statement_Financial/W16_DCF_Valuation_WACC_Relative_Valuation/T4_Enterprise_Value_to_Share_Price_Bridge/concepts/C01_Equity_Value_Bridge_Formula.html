Key concept

Equity Value Bridge Formula

Concept: Equity value = EV + Cash & liquid investments + Non-operating assets − Debt − Lease liabilities − NCI − Preference capital

Statement

\[ E = EV + C + NOA - D - L - NCI - PS - DL \]

where:

  • \(E\) is equity value attributable to the parent's ordinary shareholders
  • \(EV\) is enterprise value, the PV of FCFF plus PV of TV at the valuation date
  • \(C\) is unrestricted cash, bank balances and liquid investments (FVTPL MFs, T-bills) at fair value
  • \(NOA\) is non-operating assets: associates/JVs, investment property, surplus land, strategic stakes, assets held for sale
  • \(D\) is borrowings, including current maturities and accrued interest
  • \(L\) is lease liabilities (only when FCFF is post-Ind AS 116)
  • \(NCI\) is non-controlling interests, preferably at fair value
  • \(PS\) is preference capital classified as a claim (redeemable or cumulative), including arrears
  • \(DL\) is other debt-like items: unfunded employee benefits net of tax, probable contingencies, deferred consideration, declared unpaid dividends

The roadmap's version of the formula omits \(DL\). Practitioners include it explicitly, because gratuity deficits and tax disputes are material in Indian companies.

Explanation

The bridge converts the value of the operating business into the value of the parent's ordinary equity. It works from the asset side to the claims side:

  • Additions are assets whose economic benefits are not in FCFF, because their income sits outside EBIT as other income or share of associates' profit.
  • Deductions are claims ranking ahead of or alongside ordinary equity whose servicing is not in FCFF. Interest, lease payments (post-116), preference dividends and minority dividends are all excluded from FCFF.

Every term is measured at the valuation date (s05), using fair value where reliably determinable. The formula only works if each term is consistent with the FCFF definition (concept c05). This is the same identity used in reverse in comps: \(EV = \text{Market cap} + D + L + PS + NCI - C - NOA\) (topic t6, c01).

Several Ind AS standards feed the terms:

  • \(NCI\) comes from Ind AS 110 consolidation
  • \(NOA\) associates come from Ind AS 28
  • \(L\) comes from Ind AS 116
  • \(PS\) classification follows Ind AS 32
  • \(DL\) gratuity comes from Ind AS 19, and contingencies from Ind AS 37

Connections

  • This formula ties together subtopics s02 (additions), s03 (associates), s04 (deductions) and s05 (timing), and feeds c02 (implied price).
  • It mirrors the comps EV definition in topic t6 (c01) and the WACC weights in topic t2 (s06, s07), where lease and debt definitions must match.
  • In Phase 6 M&A work, the same terms become the enterprise-to-equity adjustments in a share purchase agreement.
  • In FP&A long-range plans, a "value bridge" uses this identity to show the effect of deleveraging or cash build on equity value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Bridge with mixed instruments [L1]

EV is ₹ 8,600 crore. Balances at the valuation date:

  • cash ₹ 240 crore, of which unpaid dividend ₹ 5 crore
  • liquid MFs ₹ 300 crore (no material gain)
  • investment property at fair value ₹ 80 crore (rental excluded from EBIT)
  • term loans ₹ 1,450 crore
  • lease liabilities ₹ 330 crore (post-116 FCFF)
  • CCPS ₹ 150 crore
  • 7% redeemable preference ₹ 60 crore (not within borrowings)
  • NCI fair value ₹ 210 crore
  • leave-encashment deficit ₹ 30 crore, tax rate 25.17%

Compute equity value and state the treatment of the CCPS.

Q2. Shortcut vs full bridge [L2]

A sell-side model computes equity value as EV − (borrowings − cash). Using a company with EV ₹ 5,000 crore, borrowings ₹ 900 crore, cash ₹ 400 crore, lease liabilities ₹ 600 crore (post-116), book NCI ₹ 90 crore (fair value ₹ 250 crore), and a listed associate stake with market value ₹ 300 crore, quantify the error in the shortcut. Explain which errors offset.

💡 Hint: Classify each omitted item as an asset or a claim before netting.

Q3. Debt-like item debate [L3]

A client argues that a disclosed contingent liability of ₹ 600 crore (an income-tax transfer-pricing demand under appeal before the ITAT) should be ignored because "it is not a provision under Ind AS 37". Write a short response explaining the valuation treatment of contingent liabilities, how you would estimate an expected outflow, and how the item should be disclosed in the valuation report.
