Key concept

Implied Share Price from Equity Value and Diluted Shares

Concept: Implied share price = Equity value ÷ Diluted shares

Statement

\[ P_{\text{implied}} = \frac{E}{N_{\text{diluted}}}, \qquad N_{\text{diluted}} = N_{\text{basic}} - N_{\text{treasury}} + \Delta N_{\text{TSM}} + N_{\text{conv}} \]

where:

  • \(E\) is equity value attributable to the parent's ordinary shareholders (concept c01)
  • \(N_{\text{basic}}\) is issued ordinary shares at the valuation date
  • \(N_{\text{treasury}}\) is shares held by the company's ESOP trust or by subsidiaries, presented as treasury shares
  • \(\Delta N_{\text{TSM}}\) is net incremental shares from in-the-money options, RSUs and warrants under the treasury stock method (concept c03)
  • \(N_{\text{conv}}\) is shares from convertibles treated as converted under the if-converted method

Explanation

The implied price is the per-share intrinsic value on a fully diluted basis. It is the number compared with CMP, the 52-week range and peer valuations.

Numerator and denominator must match. If a convertible's shares are counted in \(N_{\text{conv}}\), its carrying amount must not be deducted from \(E\). If CCPS are equity under Ind AS 32, they enter the share count, not the deductions. If an ESOP trust's shares are treated as treasury shares, they are removed from basic and re-enter only via TSM.

Same date. Shares outstanding must be at the valuation date, because allotments, ESOP exercises and buybacks change the count (s05). Use the latest shareholding pattern filed under SEBI LODR Regulation 31, or the stock exchange's "listed capital" data, and adjust for post-filing corporate actions.

Face value changes. Splits and bonus issues (common in India) change the share count but not the value. Restate CMP and historical per-share data consistently. Ind AS 33.64 requires retrospective adjustment of EPS, and comparable per-share figures in a valuation should be restated the same way.

Diluted, not Ind AS 33 diluted. The diluted EPS denominator uses average market price over the period. A valuation uses the implied or current price at a point in time. The two share counts will differ, and the valuation count is the one used here.

Illustration

Item Crore shares
Basic shares at 30 Sep 2026 22.00
Less: held by ESOP trust (treasury) 0.00
TSM: 0.80 crore ESOPs at ₹ 300, solved at implied price 0.36
Convertibles 0.00
Diluted shares 22.36

Equity value of ₹ 12,308 crore divided by 22.36 crore diluted shares gives an implied price of ₹ 550.4.

Had basic shares been used, the price would have been ₹ 559.5, an overstatement of ₹ 9.1 per share (1.7%). With an 8% ESOP pool at low strikes, the overstatement can exceed 5%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Share count reconciliation [L1]

Issued shares are 64.5 crore, of which 1.1 crore are held by the ESOP trust. Options outstanding are 2.0 crore at ₹ 95 and 0.6 crore at ₹ 410. CCPS convert into 0.9 crore shares. Equity value (with CCPS not deducted) is ₹ 26,000 crore. Compute diluted shares and the implied price, testing each tranche for ITM status at the implied price.

Q2. Bonus issue after the valuation date [L2]

A company announces a 1:1 bonus with a record date three weeks after your valuation date. CMP before the ex-date is ₹ 1,800 and your implied price is ₹ 2,050 on the pre-bonus count of 10.0 crore diluted shares. Explain how you would present the implied price and upside after the ex-date. Describe how the ESOP strikes in the dilution schedule adjust.

💡 Hint: Check the scheme's ESOP plan terms for the adjustment mechanism on corporate actions.

Q3. Interview: basic vs diluted in a DCF [L1]

Explain in under 90 seconds why a DCF must use diluted shares. Explain why the Ind AS 33 diluted share count from the annual report is not the right number to use, and give one example where using it would materially misstate the implied price.
