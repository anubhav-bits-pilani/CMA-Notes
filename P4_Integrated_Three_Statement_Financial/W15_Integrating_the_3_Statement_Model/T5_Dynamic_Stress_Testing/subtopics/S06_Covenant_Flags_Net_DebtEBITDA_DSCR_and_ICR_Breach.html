Covenant Flags: Net Debt/EBITDA, DSCR and ICR Breach Years

Scope: Covenant flags: net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, ICR breach years

Why it matters

Financial covenants are what lenders actually test, so they turn a stress case into consequences. A covenant breach can trigger an event of default, a step-up in interest, a cash sweep, a dividend block or, under Ind AS 1, reclassification of the whole loan as current. Indian term loans from banks, NCDs placed with mutual funds and ECBs typically carry two to four financial covenants. Listed debt issuers must also disclose ratios such as debt-equity, DSCR and ICR with their results (SEBI LODR Reg 52(4)). Schedule III (2021 amendment) requires disclosure of the DSCR and ten other ratios in the notes, with explanations for changes over 25%. A stress test that does not report covenant headroom and the first breach year has not answered the lender's or CFO's question. For FP&A, covenant headroom is a standing KPI in the monthly pack, and the "how far can revenue fall before we breach" analysis (c04) starts here.

Core notes

Definitions (always follow the facility agreement)

Covenant definitions are contractual. EBITDA may exclude exceptional items or include run-rate synergies. Debt may include or exclude lease liabilities, guarantees, preference shares or acceptances. Treat the definitions below as defaults and override them with the agreement's wording.

\[ \text{Net debt/EBITDA} = \frac{\text{Total borrowings (+ lease liabilities if included)} - \text{Cash and liquid investments}}{\text{EBITDA}} \]

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{D\&A} + \text{Finance costs} + \text{Other non-cash items}}{\text{Finance costs} + \text{Scheduled principal repayments (+ lease payments)}} \]

\[ \text{ICR} = \frac{\text{EBITDA (or EBIT)}}{\text{Finance costs}} \]

Indian bank practice often computes DSCR as (PAT + depreciation + interest on term loans) ÷ (interest on term loans + term loan instalments), excluding working capital interest and revolver repayments. The Schedule III ratio uses "earnings available for debt service" ÷ "debt service". The ICAI Guidance Note on Schedule III gives the suggested components. Typical thresholds:

Covenant Typical level Direction
Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5x–3.5x (≤ 4.0x for infrastructure) Breach if above
DSCR ≥ 1.20x–1.50x (average and minimum) Breach if below
ICR ≥ 2.0x–3.0x Breach if below
TOL/TNW, debt/equity ≤ 2.0x–3.0x Breach if above
FACR (fixed asset coverage) ≥ 1.25x–1.33x Breach if below

Building the flags

On a covenants block (typically on the debt or checks sheet):

ND_EBITDA      = IF(EBITDA <= 0, NA(), Net_Debt / EBITDA)
Flag_ND        = --OR(EBITDA <= 0, Net_Debt / MAX(EBITDA, 0.0001) > Cov_ND_Max)
DSCR           = CFADS / Debt_Service
Flag_DSCR      = --(DSCR < Cov_DSCR_Min)
ICR            = IF(Fin_Cost = 0, NA(), EBITDA / Fin_Cost)
Flag_ICR       = --AND(Fin_Cost > 0, EBITDA / Fin_Cost < Cov_ICR_Min)
Headroom_ND    = Cov_ND_Max - ND_EBITDA
First_Breach   = IFERROR(INDEX(Year_Row, MATCH(1, Flag_ND_Row, 0)), "None")

Points to note:

  • EBITDA ≤ 0: net debt/EBITDA is not meaningful. A negative ratio would appear to pass a "≤ 3.0x" test. Treat EBITDA ≤ 0 as an automatic breach.
  • Zero debt service: DSCR is undefined in years with no repayments or interest. Return "n.a." and do not flag.
  • Headroom in both ratio and absolute terms is more useful than the flag alone. For example, "EBITDA can fall by a further ₹85 crore before breach" is computed as EBITDA − Net debt ÷ Cov_ND_Max.
  • Testing basis: annual models test the year-end ratio on full-year EBITDA. Quarterly covenants on trailing twelve-month EBITDA need a quarterly model. Flag the limitation.

Breach-year summary

The checks dashboard should show for each covenant: the minimum headroom across the forecast, the first breach year, and the number of breach years, for the live scenario. A scenario table (s01) then shows these for Base/Bull/Bear side by side.

Covenant Threshold Base first breach Bull Bear Bear min headroom
Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0x None None FY27E −1.4x
DSCR ≥ 1.20x None None FY27E −0.35x
ICR ≥ 2.5x None None FY28E −0.6x

Accounting consequence of a breach (Ind AS 1)

If a covenant is breached on or before the reporting date and the loan becomes repayable on demand, Ind AS 1.74 requires current classification, because the entity lacks the right to defer settlement for 12 months. Ind AS carries an Indian-specific modification: if the lender agrees after the reporting period, but before approval of the financial statements, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach, the liability is not classified as current (unlike IAS 1, which requires the waiver by the reporting date). Check the current text of Ind AS 1 for any amendments on covenant-linked classification notified by MCA. In a model, an optional switch can reclassify the term loan to current borrowings in breach years. That is presentation only, with no cash flow (t3 c04), but it changes the current ratio and any working-capital-based covenant.

Illustration

Tapi Packaging Ltd, Bear case (₹ crore):

Row FY26E FY27E FY28E
EBITDA 320 180 240
Net debt 820 990 940
Net debt/EBITDA 2.56x 5.50x 3.92x
Flag (≤ 3.0x) 0 1 1
Finance costs 82 96 98
ICR (EBITDA basis) 3.90x 1.88x 2.45x
Flag (≥ 2.5x) 0 1 1

First breach year: FY27E for both covenants. EBITDA headroom in FY26E = 320 − 820 ÷ 3.0 = ₹46.7 crore, so a 15% EBITDA decline in FY26E alone would already breach the covenant.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Equity cure rights. Some agreements allow sponsors to inject equity to cure a breach, which is common in private credit. Model it as an optional input that adds to EBITDA or reduces net debt per the agreement.
  • Lease liabilities after Ind AS 116. Many pre-2019 Indian facility agreements define debt without leases ("frozen GAAP" clauses). Calculate both, and use the contractual one for the flag.
  • Revolver and cash netting. Some definitions deduct only "free" cash (excluding earmarked balances, margin money and unpaid dividend accounts).
  • DSCR in bullet years. A bullet repayment produces DSCR well below 1.0x by construction. Lenders usually test DSCR excluding refinanced bullets, or test "average DSCR over the loan life". State the basis.
  • Circularity. Covenants depend on interest, which depends on debt, which may depend on a cash sweep triggered by covenants. Keep the flags downstream (outputs only) unless a sweep or lock-up mechanism is explicitly required.
  • RBI framework. A covenant breach is distinct from default. Overdue classification (SMA-0/1/2, NPA after 90 days) depends on payment delays, not ratios. Do not conflate the two in commentary.

Key takeaways

  • Covenants are contractual. Model the facility agreement's definitions of EBITDA, debt and debt service, and keep standard definitions only as defaults.
  • Treat EBITDA ≤ 0 as an automatic net debt/EBITDA breach, and return "n.a." where debt service or interest is zero.
  • Report headroom, first breach year and number of breach years for each covenant and each scenario.
  • A breach at the reporting date can force current classification under Ind AS 1.74. Note the Indian modification for post-period waivers, and remember reclassification has no cash effect.
  • Schedule III and LODR Reg 52(4) ratio disclosures (DSCR, ICR, debt-equity) make these flags directly relevant to reporting as well as to lenders.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and flag [L1]

For FY27E, Wainganga Chemicals Ltd has EBITDA ₹210 crore, D&A ₹70 crore, finance costs ₹60 crore, PAT ₹60 crore, term loan repayment ₹90 crore, total borrowings ₹720 crore, lease liabilities ₹80 crore and cash ₹110 crore. Covenants: net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.25x (excluding leases), DSCR ≥ 1.25x (bank definition: PAT + D&A + finance costs over finance costs + repayments), ICR ≥ 3.0x. (a) Compute each ratio. (b) Flag any breach. (c) Compute EBITDA headroom on the leverage covenant.

Q2. Negative EBITDA trap [L2]

A model computes =Net_Debt/EBITDA and flags a breach if the result exceeds 3.0x. In FY27E Bear, EBITDA is −₹40 crore and net debt is ₹600 crore. (a) State what the formula returns and whether the flag fires. (b) Rewrite the ratio and flag formulas correctly. (c) Explain how you would present the ratio on the dashboard.

💡 Hint: A negative denominator makes a large debt load look like a pass.

Q3. First breach year and headroom [L2]

DSCR for FY26E–FY30E in a Bear case is 1.42x, 1.18x, 1.09x, 1.27x and 1.55x, against a covenant of ≥ 1.20x. (a) Write the Excel formulas returning the first breach year, the number of breach years and the minimum headroom. (b) Explain what additional information a lender would ask for beyond these three outputs.

Q4. Classification consequence [L3]

At 31 March 2027, Ken Infra Ltd breaches its net debt/EBITDA covenant on a ₹1,200 crore term loan (₹200 crore due within 12 months under the original schedule). The lender issues a waiver letter on 20 April 2027, and the board approves the financial statements on 15 May 2027. (a) Explain the classification under Ind AS 1 as applicable in India, contrasting it with IAS 1. (b) Explain how you would model the classification in the forecast BS and its effect on the CFS. (c) List the disclosures you would expect in the notes.
