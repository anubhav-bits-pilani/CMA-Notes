Subtopic note

Dividends Under Losses: Floored at Zero, Capped by Profits and Cash

Scope: Dividends under losses: floored at zero, capped by profits/cash

Why it matters

A payout-ratio dividend formula (=PAT × Payout) works in every profitable year and fails in the first loss year. A loss of ₹100 crore at a 30% payout gives a "dividend" of −₹30 crore, which the model treats as a cash inflow from shareholders that increases equity. The BS still balances, because the error is consistently linked, which is why the balance check cannot catch it. The output is nonsense: shareholders refunding dividends in a recession. Even with a zero floor, a model can pay dividends the company cannot afford in cash, or that company law would not permit. Indian listed companies must also publish a dividend distribution policy (SEBI LODR Reg 43A, for the top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation) that typically sets out financial parameters, including liquidity and profit conditions. The forecast should respect those rules. For FP&A and credit work, dividend capacity under stress is a direct output: can the company maintain its dividend through a downturn, and what does that do to leverage?

Core notes

The three constraints

  1. Floor at zero. Dividends cannot be negative.
  2. Profit constraint (Companies Act 2013, Sec 123). Dividends may be declared out of the current year's profits after depreciation, out of undistributed profits of previous years, or both. Before declaring out of current profits, previous years' losses and depreciation not provided must be set off against the current year's profits (Sec 123(1) proviso). Declaring out of reserves when current profits are inadequate or absent is governed by Rule 3 of the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014: - The rate cannot exceed the average of the rates declared in the three preceding years. - The amount drawn cannot exceed one-tenth of paid-up capital plus free reserves (per the latest audited financial statements). - The amount drawn must first be used to set off losses of the year in which dividend is declared, before any dividend is paid. - The balance of reserves after the withdrawal cannot fall below 15% of paid-up share capital. Interim dividends under Sec 123(3): if the company has incurred a loss during the current year up to the end of the quarter immediately preceding the declaration, the interim rate cannot exceed the average of the three preceding years.
  3. Cash constraint. The company must have cash, above its minimum operating balance, without drawing the revolver to pay dividends. Lenders often restrict this directly through covenants such as "no distributions while DSCR < 1.25x or in an event of default."

Formulas

Basic floored payout:

Dividend = MAX(0, PAT_to_Owners * Payout)

Capped by cash available:

Cash_Avail_Div = MAX(0, Cash_Pre_Div - Min_Cash)
Dividend = MIN(MAX(0, PAT_to_Owners * Payout), Cash_Avail_Div)

Capped by a reserve-based legal limit (simplified):

Div_Legal_Cap = MAX(0, PAT_to_Owners - Prior_Losses_Unabsorbed) + Free_Reserves_Drawable
Dividend = MIN(MAX(0, PAT_to_Owners * Payout), Cash_Avail_Div, Div_Legal_Cap)

Optional covenant lock-up:

Dividend = IF(DSCR < Div_Lockup_DSCR, 0, Dividend)

Use PAT attributable to owners, not consolidated PAT. NCI dividends are paid by subsidiaries to minority shareholders out of the subsidiary's own profits, and need their own floor.

Circularity caution

If Cash_Pre_Div includes interest on average cash, and dividends reduce closing cash, the dividend formula becomes part of the interest loop. Either route it through the breaker or compute cash available on opening cash plus pre-dividend flows excluding interest income. Many practitioners avoid the cash cap in the core model and instead flag "dividend exceeds free cash" on the dashboard. That keeps the model simpler while still surfacing the issue.

Timing: declared vs paid

Under Ind AS 10.12–13, a final dividend proposed after the reporting date is not recognised as a liability at that date. It is recognised in equity when approved by shareholders, usually at the AGM in the following financial year, and it is paid within 30 days (Sec 127). So the FY26 final dividend reduces FY27 equity and cash. For annual models, many practitioners simplify by deducting the dividend in the year to which the profit relates. Either is acceptable if applied consistently. If you base the dividend on the prior year's PAT (the more realistic timing), the floor must use that prior-year PAT. A loss in FY27E then removes the final dividend paid in FY28E, not the one paid in FY27E.

Illustration

Damodar Steels Ltd (₹ crore), payout 35% of PAT to owners, minimum cash 120. Dividend paid in the same year as the profit (simplification):

Row FY26E FY27E FY28E FY29E
PAT to owners 260 −140 40 310
Payout × PAT 91.0 −49.0 14.0 108.5
Floored 91.0 0 14.0 108.5
Cash before dividend 310 95 128 410
Cash available (above 120) 190 0 8 290
Dividend (floor and cash cap) 91.0 0 8.0 108.5

FY28E: the floored payout of 14.0 exceeds cash available of 8.0, so the dividend is capped at 8.0. FY28E also raises a Sec 123 issue: the FY27E loss of 140 must be set off against FY28E profit of 40 before declaring out of current profits, which leaves nothing. The FY28E dividend therefore has to come from undistributed profits of earlier years, subject to the company's reserves and its board's dividend policy. A careful model adds the legal cap and flags it.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Fixed dividend per share (some PSUs and MNC subsidiaries maintain a fixed DPS, and DIPAM guidelines for CPSEs set minimum payouts as a share of PAT or net worth). Model MAX(Min_DPS × Shares, Payout × PAT), then apply floors and caps.
  • Buybacks have analogous constraints: Sec 68 limits buybacks to 25% of paid-up capital plus free reserves (10% with board approval only), sets a post-buyback debt-to-equity limit of 2:1, and requires buybacks to be funded from free reserves, securities premium or proceeds of other securities. Indian buyback tax treatment has changed over time (from 1 October 2024 the payout is taxed as deemed dividend in shareholders' hands), so state the regime used.
  • Dividend from subsidiaries to parent in consolidated models eliminates on consolidation. Only NCI dividends leave the group.
  • TDS on dividends (Sec 194) is withheld from shareholders and remitted by the company. It does not change the total cash outflow.
  • Negative retained earnings. Even with positive current-year PAT, Sec 123(1) requires previous losses to be set off, so accumulated losses can block dividends for several years.
  • Do not floor PAT itself. Only the dividend is floored. Floor the wrong variable and equity becomes overstated.

Key takeaways

  • Dividends = MAX(0, PAT to owners × payout). Never let a loss create a negative dividend (a shareholder refund).
  • Cap dividends by cash available above minimum cash, and optionally by a covenant lock-up and the Sec 123 / Rule 3 legal limit.
  • Use PAT attributable to owners for the parent dividend, and model NCI dividends separately.
  • Be consistent on timing: dividends are declared and paid after the year end (Ind AS 10), or deducted in the profit year as a simplification.
  • The balance check cannot catch a negative-dividend error. Add a dashboard check that dividends are ≥ 0 and ≤ cash available.

Practice Questions

Q1. Dividend formula build [L1]

Write a single Excel formula for the FY27E parent dividend using PAT_Owners, Payout, Cash_Pre_Div, Min_Cash, DSCR and Lockup_DSCR. It should floor at zero, cap at cash above minimum, and set the dividend to zero if DSCR is below the lock-up level. Explain each nested function.

Q2. Loss year and Sec 123 [L2]

Pulicat Foods Ltd: paid-up capital ₹50 crore and free reserves ₹400 crore at 31 March 2027. Dividend rates for FY24–FY26 were 120%, 150% and 180% of face value (₹10 shares, 5 crore shares). FY27 shows a loss of ₹30 crore. The board wants to declare a dividend of 150% for FY27 out of reserves. (a) Apply each condition of Rule 3 of the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014. (b) State whether the proposed dividend is permissible. (c) Explain how you would model this constraint in a 5-year forecast.

💡 Hint: Compute the three-year average rate and the 10% cap on paid-up capital plus free reserves separately.

Q3. Timing lag effects [L2]

A model pays the final dividend in the year after the profit year, at 30% of prior-year PAT to owners. PAT to owners is ₹200 crore (FY26E), −₹90 crore (FY27E) and ₹150 crore (FY28E). FY25A PAT was ₹180 crore. (a) Compute dividends paid in FY26E–FY29E with a zero floor. (b) Explain what the floor should reference in each year. (c) Explain the error that arises if the floor references current-year PAT instead.

Q4. Dividend vs revolver [L3]

In a Bear case, Ganga Ceramics Ltd's model pays a ₹60 crore dividend in FY27E while the revolver draws ₹85 crore in the same year. As a reviewer, explain (a) why this combination is a red flag, (b) what lenders typically require in Indian term loan agreements regarding distributions, and (c) how you would change the model logic and dashboard to handle it.
