Key concept

Dividend Floor and Cash Cap Formula

Concept: Dividends = MAX(0, PAT × Payout), optionally MIN with cash available

Statement

\[ D_t = \min\left(\max(0,\; \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t \times p),\; \max(0,\; C^{\text{pre-div}}_t - C^{\min})\right) \]

where \( D_t \) is the dividend paid to the parent's shareholders in year \( t \), \( \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t \) is profit attributable to owners of the parent, \( p \) is the payout ratio, \( C^{\text{pre-div}}_t \) is closing cash before dividends, and \( C^{\min} \) is the minimum operating cash balance. The outer MIN is optional. Without it, the formula reduces to the floored payout \( \max(0, \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t \times p) \).

Explanation

  • The floor stops a loss from creating a negative dividend, which would be an implied capital injection by shareholders. A negative dividend raises cash and equity together, so the BS still balances, and the balance check cannot detect the error. Only logic or a sign check can.
  • The cash cap stops the model from paying dividends it cannot fund without breaching minimum cash or drawing the revolver. In practice boards cut dividends when liquidity is tight, and lenders often restrict distributions through covenants.
  • Owners' PAT is the right base in consolidated models. NCI dividends are paid by subsidiaries and modelled separately in the NCI corkscrew.
  • Legal limits (Sec 123 of the Companies Act 2013 and Rule 3 of the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014) sit on top. Previous losses must be set off against current profits, and declarations out of reserves are capped. Add them as a further MIN term when relevant (s05).
  • Timing: if dividends are paid in the year after the profit year (the AGM-approved final dividend, Ind AS 10.12–13), use prior-year PAT inside the floor and the current year's cash for the cap.
  • Circularity: if interest income depends on average cash, the cash cap enters the loop. Route it through the breaker, or compute available cash before interest income.

Excel:

Div_Floor = MAX(0, PAT_Owners * Payout)
Div_Cash  = MAX(0, Cash_Pre_Div - Min_Cash)
Dividend  = IF(Cash_Cap_On, MIN(Div_Floor, Div_Cash), Div_Floor)

Always enter dividends as a positive number in the equity schedule, with the CFS applying the minus sign (or the reverse, as long as you are consistent, per Topic 4 s05).

Illustration

Ganga Pharma Ltd (₹ crore), payout 40%, minimum cash 150, cash cap on:

Row FY26E FY27E FY28E
PAT to owners 180 −50 120
PAT × payout 72 −20 48
Floored 72 0 48
Cash before dividend 400 210 175
Cash available (above 150) 250 60 25
Dividend 72 0 25

Without the floor, FY27E would show a −20 "dividend" that adds ₹20 crore to cash and equity. Without the cash cap, FY28E would pay 48 and leave cash at 127, below the minimum, which would force a revolver draw to fund a dividend.

Connections

This concept is the formula behind s05 (dividends under losses) and feeds the equity corkscrew from t3 (other equity = opening + PAT to owners − dividends + other movements). It interacts with s03 (revolver), because a cash cap stops dividends from being revolver-funded, and with s06, because DSCR-based dividend lock-ups are common covenants. On the checks dashboard (s07) it pairs with the "negative dividends" and "dividends > free cash" checks. In Week 16, the FCFE and dividend discount approaches use the same capped dividend logic.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the formula [L1]

PAT to owners for FY26E–FY29E is ₹90, −₹40, ₹30 and ₹150 crore. Payout is 35%, minimum cash is ₹80 crore, and cash before dividends is ₹200, ₹110, ₹95 and ₹260 crore. Compute dividends with (a) the floor only, and (b) the floor and cash cap. Explain one consequence of each difference for the BS and CFS.

Q2. Prior-year PAT basis [L2]

A model pays dividends in the year after the profit year at 30% of prior-year PAT to owners. FY25A PAT is ₹220 crore, FY26E is −₹60 crore and FY27E is ₹140 crore. Minimum cash is ₹100 crore and cash before dividends is ₹160 crore in FY26E, ₹90 crore in FY27E and ₹210 crore in FY28E. Compute dividends paid in FY26E, FY27E and FY28E, and write the Excel formula for FY27E using named ranges.

💡 Hint: The floor references the prior year's PAT, but the cash cap references the current year's cash.

Q3. Why the balance check misses it [L2]

Explain, using a ₹100 crore loss at 30% payout, why a model with Dividend = PAT × Payout still balances, and design two dashboard checks that would detect the error. State which category (integrity error or business warning) each check belongs to.
