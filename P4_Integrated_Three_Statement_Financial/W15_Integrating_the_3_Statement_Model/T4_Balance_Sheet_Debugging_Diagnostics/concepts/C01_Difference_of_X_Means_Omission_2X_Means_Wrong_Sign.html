Key concept

Difference of X Means Omission; 2X Means Wrong Sign

Concept: Difference = X → an item's movement is omitted from CFS; Difference = 2X → item entered with wrong sign

Statement

Let \( X \) be the cash effect a BS movement should have in the CFS (negative for an asset increase, positive for a liability or equity increase). Let \( C_{\text{model}} \) be the cash effect actually recorded in the CFS. Because cash is linked from the CFS, the error shows up as a misstatement of cash, the balancing asset, and the balance check (Assets − Liabilities − Equity) becomes:

\[ \text{Check} = C_{\text{model}} - X \]

Two special cases cover most real errors:

\[ C_{\text{model}} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \lvert\text{Check}\rvert = \lvert X\rvert \quad \text{(omission)} \]

\[ C_{\text{model}} = -X \;\Rightarrow\; \lvert\text{Check}\rvert = 2\lvert X\rvert \quad \text{(wrong sign)} \]

Here \( X \) is the true cash effect, \( C_{\text{model}} \) is what the CFS shows, and the absolute value notation indicates size regardless of direction.

Explanation

The BS is internally complete: every non-cash line moves by its schedule amount whether or not the CFS is right. Cash is the only line whose closing value depends on the CFS. So any CFS error shows up as a cash misstatement of exactly the same size, and the check row exposes it.

  • Omission: the BS line moves by \( X \), but cash does not move at all. The imbalance equals \( X \).
  • Wrong sign: the BS line moves by \( X \) and cash moves by \( X \) in the wrong direction. The cash misstatement is the true effect missed plus the false effect added, which is \( 2X \).

The same arithmetic applies to errors on the BS side. If a schedule closing (for example DTL) is not linked to the BS while the CFS adds back ΔDTL, the BS is out by \( X \). If a schedule's closing formula adds a deduction instead of subtracting it, the BS line is out by \( 2X \).

This is why the halving test works: a difference \( D \) that matches no item may match an item of \( D/2 \) with the wrong sign.

Limits of the rule:

  • Partial errors. A wrong percentage, wrong column or wrong driver gives a difference that is neither \( X \) nor \( 2X \), for example the difference between this year's and last year's movement.
  • Multiple errors add up. Test pairs before concluding.
  • Circular models distort the clean multiple because the error changes interest, which changes PAT and cash. Switch the breaker off first.
  • Errors that do not unbalance. A P&L item with the wrong sign that flows consistently through PAT, tax, equity and cash does not affect the check. The model balances but is wrong. The rule only diagnoses linking errors.

Illustration

Kaveri Consumer Ltd, FY27E. True movements: inventory +24 (cash effect −24), ESOP charge 6 (add-back +6), lease principal paid 15 (cash effect −15).

Scenario CFS records Cash misstatement Check (A − L − E)
ESOP add-back omitted 0 instead of +6 Cash 6 too low −6 (= X)
Inventory with wrong sign +24 instead of −24 Cash 48 too high +48 (= 2X)
Lease principal omitted 0 instead of −15 Cash 15 too high +15 (= X)
Lease principal and ESOP both omitted Cash +15 − 6 +9 (sum)

A reviewer seeing +48 would first search for 48 (no match), then for 24 (Δ inventory), and would find the sign error immediately.

Connections

This is the arithmetic basis of s03 (halving test and exact-match search) and of the reconciliation block in s04, which computes the gap between \( X \) and \( C_{\text{model}} \) for every line. It combines with c02 (the pattern across years) to confirm a hypothesis, and with c03 (cash check) to decide whether the faulty leg is in the CFS or on the BS. It is the modelling version of the trial-balance halving test from Phase 1 double-entry. In interviews it is the standard answer to "my model is out by 40, what do you do?"

Practice Questions

Q1. Size the error [L1]

For each case, state the check (A − L − E) in the affected year, given the convention that cash is linked from the CFS. (a) Δ trade payables of +18 omitted from CFO. (b) Capex of 120 entered in CFI as +120. (c) ΔDTL of +5 added back twice. (d) Dividends paid of 40 entered as −40 in CFF and also deducted from PAT on the CFO start line.

Q2. Find the item from the difference [L2]

FY29E check = −27.60 (first imbalanced year). FY29E movements: Δ receivables +27.60, Δ inventory +13.80, Δ payables +9.20, ΔDTL +4.60, ESOP charge 13.80, ROU additions 18.40. (a) List every item that could explain the difference by omission, and every item that could explain it by sign error. (b) For each candidate, state whether the direction of the check is consistent with the error. (c) Name the additional evidence you would use to choose among the consistent candidates.

💡 Hint: A negative check means cash is too low, so check which errors would understate cash.

Q3. When the rule fails [L2]

Explain, with a numerical example of your own, why using the prior-year movement of receivables in the CFS (instead of the current-year movement) produces a difference that is neither X nor 2X. Show how the difference behaves across three years when receivables growth is accelerating.
