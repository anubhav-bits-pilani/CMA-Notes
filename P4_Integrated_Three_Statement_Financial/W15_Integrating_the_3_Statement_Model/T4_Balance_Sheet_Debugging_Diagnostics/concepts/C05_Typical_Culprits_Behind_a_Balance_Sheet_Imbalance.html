Key concept

Typical Culprits Behind a Balance Sheet Imbalance

Concept: Typical culprits: ΔNWC sign, ΔDTL, ESOP reserve, NCI profit/dividends, dividends payable, CWIP, lease additions

Statement

Most imbalances in Indian three-statement models come from seven items. Each one has a leg that is easy to drop or a sign that is easy to flip:

Culprit Typical error Size of imbalance
ΔNWC sign Asset increase added (or liability increase deducted) in CFO 2 × movement
ΔDTL Deferred tax not added back, or DTL not moved on the BS ΔDTL
ESOP reserve Charge not added back in CFO, or reserve not credited ESOP charge
NCI profit/dividends CFS starts at PAT to owners while NCI grows by its share; NCI dividends not in CFF NCI share of profit; NCI dividends
Dividends payable CFS deducts declared dividend while BS records payable (or vice versa) Declared − paid
CWIP Capex excludes CWIP additions, or capitalisation counted as a second capex CWIP additions, or transfer amount
Lease additions New ROU and lease liability treated as capex in CFI or debt drawdown in CFF Lease additions

Explanation

ΔNWC sign. The convention "increase in asset = cash outflow" is applied to a dozen lines, and a single row computed as closing − opening instead of opening − closing doubles the movement. It also changes direction when working capital releases in a downturn, which produces alternating differences.

ΔDTL. Tax expense = current + deferred tax. The CFS starting at PAT must add back the deferred portion, which is non-cash. Modellers who add back total tax (wrong) or nothing (also wrong) create imbalances. DTA increases are deducted, not added. Under the Ind AS 7 Indian format starting from PBT, income taxes paid are deducted directly, and ΔDTL is not a CFO line at all. Mixing formats causes double counting.

ESOP reserve. Under Ind AS 102 the charge is debited to employee benefits expense and credited to a share-based payment reserve in other equity. No cash moves until exercise, when the exercise price is received and the reserve is transferred to share capital and securities premium. Both legs (add-back and reserve) must be present.

NCI. In consolidated models, PAT includes the NCI share. If CFO starts at consolidated PAT, the NCI share is already in cash, and the NCI corkscrew (opening + share of profit − NCI dividends) explains the BS. Starting at PAT attributable to owners omits the NCI share. Dividends paid to NCI are a CFF outflow in the consolidated CFS (Ind AS 7.34 permits dividends paid to be classified as financing, and NCI dividends follow the same classification). They are often forgotten because the parent's own dividend line is already there.

Dividends payable. Under Ind AS 10.12–13, a final dividend proposed after the reporting date is not a liability. It is recognised when shareholders approve it at the AGM, usually in the next financial year. Interim dividends declared by the board are recognised when declared and are usually paid within 30 days (Sec 127 requires payment within 30 days of declaration). If a model reduces equity in the year of declaration but the CFS deducts cash in the year of payment, it needs a dividends-payable line to bridge the timing.

CWIP. Capex = additions to CWIP plus direct additions to PPE. Transfers from CWIP to PPE on capitalisation are internal and non-cash. Two classic errors: counting capitalisations as capex in CFI (double count), or leaving CWIP off the BS PPE total while capex includes it.

Lease additions. New leases under Ind AS 116 create an ROU asset and lease liability simultaneously, with no cash (a non-cash transaction, Ind AS 7.43). Cash moves only through lease payments (principal and interest in CFF for most Indian non-financial companies, Ind AS 7.17(e) and 7.31–33 choice for interest). Putting ROU additions into capex, or treating them as a debt drawdown, creates an error equal to the additions.

Illustration

Ken-Betwa Industries Ltd, FY27E, ₹ crore. The check is −4.40 and the cash check is 0. Candidate culprit sizes: Δreceivables +12.0, ΔDTL +2.2, ESOP 4.4, NCI share of profit 6.6, dividends declared 20 and paid 18 (payable +2), CWIP additions 15, lease additions 9.

The exact match of 4.4 points to the ESOP add-back. The halving test (2.2) points to the ΔDTL sign. Both are negative-check errors (cash too low): a missing ESOP add-back, or a ΔDTL deducted instead of added. The pattern decides. If the ESOP charge was introduced in FY27E and the check is −4.4 in FY27E, −8.8 in FY28E and so on, the ESOP add-back is missing. If ΔDTL varies by year and the check follows 2 × ΔDTL, it is a sign error.

Connections

These culprits are the first places to search after the size and pattern tests (s02, s03, c01, c02). Most of them involve a non-cash leg, so they are exactly what the triad (c04) is designed to catch. They map to t2 (CFS non-cash items, ΔDTL pitfall, ESOP pitfall), t3 (equity corkscrew, NCI, CWIP and ROU schedules) and Week 14 schedule sign conventions. In the Saturday assessment, expect at least four of the six planted errors to come from this list.

Practice Questions

Q1. Match the culprit [L1]

For each symptom (first imbalanced year shown, A − L − E convention), name the most likely culprit from the list and the fix. (a) Check −3.1 in FY26E, growing in line with the ESOP schedule. (b) Check +15.0 constant from FY28E, the year a ₹15 crore lease of a new warehouse begins. (c) Check equal to −NCI share of profit in every year. (d) Check +2.0 in FY27E only, where FY27E declared dividend is 22 and paid dividend is 20.

Q2. CWIP double count [L2]

Shipra Power Ltd forecasts capex of ₹400 crore in FY27E into CWIP, of which ₹250 crore is capitalised into PPE in the same year. The CFS shows "Purchase of PPE" of −₹650 crore (capex 400 plus capitalisation 250). (a) Compute the check impact. (b) Rewrite the PPE and CWIP corkscrews for FY27E with an opening CWIP of ₹120 crore and opening net PPE of ₹1,800 crore, depreciation ₹140 crore. (c) Identify the correct CFS line.

💡 Hint: Only the cash spent leaves the company. Capitalisation moves cost from one BS line to another.

Q3. Dividend timing [L2]

Satluj Consumer Ltd's board recommends a final dividend of ₹48 crore for FY26 on 15 May 2026. It is approved at the AGM on 10 August 2026 and paid on 5 September 2026. An interim dividend of ₹20 crore for FY27 is declared on 25 March 2027 and paid on 15 April 2027. (a) State the equity and cash effects in FY26E, FY27E and FY28E under Ind AS 10. (b) Design the dividends-payable corkscrew needed to keep the model balanced. (c) State what imbalance arises if the model deducts all dividends from equity and cash in the year declared.
