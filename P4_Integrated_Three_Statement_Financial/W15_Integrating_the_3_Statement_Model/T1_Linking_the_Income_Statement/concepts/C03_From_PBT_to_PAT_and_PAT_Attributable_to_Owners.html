Key concept

From PBT to PAT and PAT Attributable to Owners

Concept: PAT = PBT − (Current tax + Deferred tax); PAT to owners = PAT − NCI share

Statement

\[ \text{PAT} = \text{PBT} - (T_c + T_d) \;(+ A \text{ if associates are presented after tax}) \]

\[ \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}} = \text{PAT} - \text{NCI}_{\text{share}} \]

where \( T_c \) is current tax for the year (taxable income × statutory rate, after loss set-off, never negative), \( T_d \) is deferred tax charge (positive) or credit (negative) through P&L = change in net DTL recognised in P&L, \( A \) is share of associates/JVs, and \( \text{NCI}_{\text{share}} \) is the share of consolidated profit attributable to non-controlling interests in subsidiaries.

Explanation

Ind AS 12 requires the tax expense to include both current and deferred tax (Ind AS 12.58), with tax on OCI items going to OCI. In the model, both components come from the Week 14 tax schedule. The split matters for the cash flow statement: current tax approximates cash tax (subject to advance tax and payable timing), while deferred tax is entirely non-cash and is added back in CFO (or, equivalently, the change in DTL is a non-cash item). A model that books only "tax = PBT × rate" must keep DTL flat to stay consistent.

Ind AS 110 requires profit or loss and each component of OCI to be attributed to owners of the parent and to NCI (Ind AS 110.B94), even if this results in a deficit NCI balance. Schedule III Division II presents the split below profit for the year. PAT to owners is the figure that:

  • flows to retained earnings in the equity corkscrew,
  • is the numerator of basic EPS (Ind AS 33),
  • is the "earnings" in ROE and P/E.

The NCI share flows to the NCI corkscrew, which also takes dividends paid by subsidiaries to minority holders (a group CFF outflow). PAT (group) is the starting point for a PAT-start CFS because the cash belongs to the group, not just the parent's owners.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): PBT ex-associates 1,176; taxable income 1,216 (₹40 crore of provisions disallowed until paid under Sec 43B); rate 25.168%; JV share 60 after tax; 51%-owned subsidiary PAT 140.

\[ T_c = 1{,}216 \times 25.168\% = 306.0, \qquad T_d = -40 \times 25.168\% = -10.1 \]

\[ \text{PAT} = 1{,}176 - (306.0 - 10.1) + 60 = 940.1 \]

\[ \text{NCI}_{\text{share}} = 49\% \times 140 = 68.6, \qquad \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}} = 871.5 \]

The DTA of 10.1 increases on the BS, and in the PAT-start CFS the deferred tax credit is deducted as a non-cash item.

Practice Questions

Q1. PAT and attribution [L1]

PBT ₹2,450 crore; current tax ₹598 crore; deferred tax charge ₹31 crore; NCI share ₹142 crore. Compute PAT, PAT to owners and the ETR, and state where each figure goes in the equity corkscrew.

Q2. Deferred tax credit and CFO [L2]

Taxable income exceeds book PBT by ₹80 crore due to gratuity and leave provisions disallowed until paid. PBT ₹900 crore; rate 25.168%. Compute current tax, deferred tax and PAT, then show the tax-related lines in a PAT-start CFO, assuming current tax is paid in full during the year.

💡 Hint: A deferred tax credit is non-cash income and is deducted in the CFO bridge.

Q3. NCI deficit and dividends [L3]

A 70%-owned subsidiary reports a loss of ₹60 crore in FY27E and pays no dividend; in FY28E it earns ₹90 crore and pays a dividend of ₹30 crore. Opening NCI is ₹10 crore. Build the NCI corkscrew for both years, explain the Ind AS 110 treatment of the deficit, and state the CFS line for the FY28E dividend.
