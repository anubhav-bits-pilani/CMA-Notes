Key concept

EBITDA and EBIT Built from By-Nature Expense Lines

Concept: EBITDA = Revenue − COGS − Employee cost − Other opex; EBIT = EBITDA − D&A

Statement

\[ \text{EBITDA} = R - \text{COGS} - E - O \]

\[ \text{EBIT} = \text{EBITDA} - \text{D\&A} \]

where \( R \) is revenue from operations (net of GST, discounts and rebates, including other operating revenue), \( \text{COGS} \) is cost of materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories of finished goods, WIP and stock-in-trade, \( E \) is employee benefits expense (including Ind AS 102 share-based payment cost), \( O \) is other expenses (power, freight, advertising, short-term/low-value lease rent, CSR, ECL, etc.), and \( \text{D\&A} \) is depreciation of PPE and ROU assets plus amortisation of intangibles.

Explanation

The formula maps directly onto the Schedule III Division II by-nature expense lines: every expense above "Depreciation and amortisation expense" and "Finance costs" is operating, so EBITDA is revenue less all those lines. EBIT then removes D&A. Three exclusions make it an operating measure:

  • Other income is excluded (it is treasury or non-operating income; see c02 and c05).
  • Finance costs are excluded (capital-structure cost).
  • Exceptional items are excluded (non-recurring by nature).

Neither EBITDA nor EBIT is defined by Ind AS. The General Instructions to the Schedule III P&L permit companies to present EBITDA as a separate line item on the face of the statement, but do not oblige them to and leave the measurement to the company. In SEBI ICDR offer documents such figures are treated as non-GAAP measures that must be defined and reconciled to the statutory numbers. That is why the model must state its definition (c05).

Practical conditions:

  1. Revenue and expense lines are taken from operating schedules (s01), not computed on the P&L.
  2. Share-based payment cost stays inside employee cost — "adjusted EBITDA" excluding ESOP cost is a management metric, not the base.
  3. Under Ind AS 116, rent for leases capitalised as ROU assets is not in \( O \); it reappears as ROU depreciation (in D&A) and lease interest (in finance costs). EBITDA is therefore "post-Ind AS 116" by default.

EBIT is the base for NOPAT in DCF (c04), ROCE and interest coverage. If the company has large other income from operating activities (e.g., export incentives classified as other income by some companies), reclassify into revenue consistently in history and forecast.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): revenue 8,400; materials consumed 3,950; purchases of stock-in-trade 420; changes in inventories (30); employee cost 690 (including ESOP cost 22); other expenses 1,860; D&A 350; other income 256.5.

\[ \text{COGS} = 3{,}950 + 420 - 30 = 4{,}340 \]

\[ \text{EBITDA} = 8{,}400 - 4{,}340 - 690 - 1{,}860 = 1{,}510 \quad (18.0\%) \]

\[ \text{EBIT} = 1{,}510 - 350 = 1{,}160 \quad (13.8\%) \]

Other income of 256.5 is added only after EBIT, on the way to PBT. Including it in EBITDA would give 1,766.5 (21.0%) — a 300 bp overstatement of operating margin.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute EBITDA and EBIT from statutory lines [L1]

A listed auto-components company reports FY26A (₹ crore): revenue from operations 6,120; other income 85; cost of materials consumed 3,710; changes in inventories 25 (inventories fell); employee benefits 540; finance costs 96; D&A 240; other expenses 1,020; exceptional items (45). Compute COGS, EBITDA, EBIT and their margins.

Q2. Adjusted vs reported EBITDA [L2]

Management reports "adjusted EBITDA" of ₹1,210 crore which excludes ESOP cost of ₹64 crore and a ₹30 crore one-time IPO expense included in other expenses. Your statutory-basis EBITDA is ₹1,116 crore. Explain which figure your forecast base should use, how you treat the IPO expense in history, and how ESOP cost flows through the CFS.

💡 Hint: Separate recurring non-cash cost (ESOP) from genuinely non-recurring items.

Q3. Reclassifying operating other income [L2]

A company classifies ₹120 crore of export incentives (RoDTEP) under other income. Revenue is ₹4,800 crore and EBITDA (ex-other income) ₹610 crore. Discuss whether RoDTEP should be in EBITDA, show the margin under both treatments, and state how you would implement the choice consistently in the model.
