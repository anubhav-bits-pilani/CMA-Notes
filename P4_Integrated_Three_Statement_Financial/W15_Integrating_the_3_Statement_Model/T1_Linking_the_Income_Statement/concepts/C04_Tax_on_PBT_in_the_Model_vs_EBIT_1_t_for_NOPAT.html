Key concept

Tax on PBT in the Model vs EBIT × (1 − t) for NOPAT

Concept: Tax in the 3-statement model is on PBT; EBIT × (1 − t) is only for NOPAT in DCF

Statement

In the integrated three-statement model:

\[ T_c = t \times \max\left(0, \text{PBT}^{\text{ex-assoc}} + \Delta_{\text{book-tax}} - \text{Loss b/f utilised}\right) \]

In the DCF (Week 16) only:

\[ \text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) \]

where \( t \) is the statutory rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA), \( \text{PBT}^{\text{ex-assoc}} \) is PBT excluding post-tax associate share, \( \Delta_{\text{book-tax}} \) is the net of book-to-tax adjustments (depreciation difference, Sec 43B, disallowances), and NOPAT is the hypothetical after-tax operating profit of an unlevered firm.

Explanation

The three-statement model must reproduce what the company will actually record and pay. Tax law taxes total income — operating profit plus other income less deductible interest — so the P&L tax is on PBT. Interest is deductible (subject to Sec 94B thin-capitalisation limits for interest paid to non-resident associated enterprises above ₹1 crore — capped at 30% of EBITDA), so a leveraged company pays less tax than an unlevered one. This interest tax shield is real cash and belongs in the forecast cash flows and the BS.

NOPAT is a valuation construct. In FCFF-based DCF, cash flows are measured as if the firm had no debt, and the tax shield on interest is captured through the after-tax cost of debt in WACC:

\[ \text{WACC} = \frac{E}{D+E} k_e + \frac{D}{D+E} k_d (1 - t) \]

If NOPAT were computed using actual tax (on PBT), the shield would be counted twice — once in the cash flows and once in WACC. Conversely, if the three-statement P&L used EBIT × t as tax, PAT, retained earnings, cash and the tax payable would all be wrong, and the BS would still balance — which makes the error invisible to the check row.

Consequences:

  • The tax line on the IS links from the tax schedule; never =EBIT*t.
  • The DCF tab computes NOPAT separately, from EBIT on the IS × (1 − t), or uses "operating cash taxes" (tax on PBT + tax shield on interest − tax on other income).
  • Other income is excluded from NOPAT; the cash and investments that generate it are added in the EV-to-equity bridge.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): EBIT 1,160; other income 256.5; finance costs 240.5; PBT 1,176; no book-tax differences; t = 25.168%.

Three-statement P&L DCF NOPAT
Base PBT 1,176 EBIT 1,160
Tax 296.0 291.9
After-tax figure PAT 880.0 NOPAT 868.1

The tax difference (about 4.0) is the net of tax on other income (+64.6) and the interest shield (−60.5). Both are real for the company but handled elsewhere in valuation (cash in the bridge, shield in WACC).

Practice Questions

Q1. Spot the error [L1]

A model's IS tax row reads =IS!F19*Inputs!$C$8, where row 19 is EBIT and C8 holds 25.168%. PBT is ₹840 crore and EBIT ₹980 crore. Quantify the PAT error, explain why the check row still shows zero, and name two outputs on the dashboard that are wrong.

Q2. Reconcile tax to NOPAT tax [L2]

EBIT ₹3,200 crore, other income ₹410 crore, finance costs ₹620 crore, Sec 43B timing difference adding ₹50 crore to taxable income, t = 25.168%. Compute current tax in the three-statement model, NOPAT, and reconcile the two tax figures line by line.

💡 Hint: Separate the effect of other income, the interest shield and the timing difference.

Q3. Interview framing [L2]

In two minutes, explain to an interviewer why using EBIT × (1 − t) in the DCF while taxing PBT in the model is consistent rather than contradictory. Include what would go wrong if you used PAT + D&A − capex − ΔNWC as FCFF.
