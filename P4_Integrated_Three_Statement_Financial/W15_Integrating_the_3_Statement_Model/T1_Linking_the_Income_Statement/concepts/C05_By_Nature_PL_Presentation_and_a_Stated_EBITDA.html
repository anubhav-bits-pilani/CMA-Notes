Key concept

By-Nature P&L Presentation and a Stated EBITDA Definition

Concept: Schedule III Div II presents expenses by nature; EBITDA definition (pre/post Ind AS 116, ex other income) stated on sheet

Statement

Schedule III Division II requires the Statement of Profit and Loss to classify expenses by nature: cost of materials consumed; purchases of stock-in-trade; changes in inventories of finished goods, WIP and stock-in-trade; employee benefits expense; finance costs; depreciation and amortisation expense; other expenses. Because EBITDA is not an Ind AS measure, the model must display, on the IS sheet, a definition of the form:

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{model}} = R - \text{COGS} - E - O \quad \text{(post-Ind AS 116, excluding other income)} \]

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{pre-116}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{model}} - \text{Lease payments for capitalised leases} \]

with \( R, \text{COGS}, E, O \) as defined in c01.

Explanation

Ind AS 1.99 allows analysis of expenses by nature or by function; Schedule III Division II, which is mandatory for Ind AS companies under Sec 129, effectively mandates the by-nature face. Function-based figures (cost of sales, SG&A) appear only in some voluntary presentations; there is no statutory "gross profit" or "EBITDA" line. Every analyst therefore builds these subtotals and, because different users build them differently, disputes arise. Three definitional choices must be explicit:

  1. Other income in or out. Standard practice is out — EBITDA is operating. Some managements include it.
  2. Pre- or post-Ind AS 116. Post-116 EBITDA excludes rent for capitalised leases (because it is now ROU depreciation + lease interest). Pre-116 EBITDA deducts lease payments, comparable with older history and with covenants that exclude lease liabilities from debt. For lease-heavy businesses (retail, QSR, multiplexes, hospitals, airlines) the gap is 5–15 percentage points of margin.
  3. Treatment of ESOP cost, forex and exceptional items. Statutory-basis EBITDA includes ESOP cost and operating forex; excludes exceptional items.

The definition must match the ratio it feeds: net debt/EBITDA with lease liabilities in net debt uses post-116 EBITDA; with leases excluded, pre-116 EBITDA. EV/EBITDA must pair EV including lease liabilities with post-116 EBITDA. Mismatches distort valuation by the capitalised value of leases.

Implementation: a note cell above the EBITDA row — "EBITDA = Revenue from operations − COGS − Employee − Other expenses; post-Ind AS 116; excludes other income and exceptional items" — and a memo row for pre-116 EBITDA linked from the lease schedule (lease payments). The dashboard shows which basis is displayed.

Illustration

A QSR chain, FY27E (₹ crore): revenue 2,800; COGS 950; employee 420; other expenses (excluding capitalised leases) 860; lease payments 260 (ROU depreciation 190, lease interest 85, with the liability also falling by principal of 175); other income 30.

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{model}} = 2{,}800 - 950 - 420 - 860 = 570 \;(20.4\%) \]

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{pre-116}} = 570 - 260 = 310 \;(11.1\%) \]

Including other income would add 30 (1.1 pp). A broker quoting "11% margin" and management quoting "21%" may both be right — on different definitions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write the EBITDA definition note [L1]

Draft the one-line definition note you would place above the EBITDA row of a Project 4 model for a cement company with immaterial leases, no ESOPs and large treasury income. Explain each element of the note.

Q2. Covenant definition matching [L2]

A loan agreement defines leverage as (borrowings − cash) ÷ EBITDA, where EBITDA "shall be computed after deducting all rental and lease payments". FY27E: borrowings ₹1,800 crore; cash ₹300 crore; lease liabilities ₹900 crore; post-116 EBITDA ₹950 crore; lease payments ₹210 crore. Compute the covenant ratio correctly, and compute the ratio a careless analyst would show using post-116 EBITDA.

💡 Hint: Debt excludes leases, so EBITDA must too.

Q3. Function vs nature mapping [L3]

A CFO wants the board MIS to show a by-function P&L (cost of sales, selling, G&A) while the model is built by nature. Design a mapping table approach so that both presentations come from the same schedules, and identify two expense lines that are hardest to allocate and why.
