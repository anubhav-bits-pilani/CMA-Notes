Roadmap topic: Linking the Income Statement: Pulling Revenue, Opex, Depreciation, Interest Expense, and Tax Expense from supporting schedules down to Net Income

Overview

By Week 15 every building block of Project 4 exists: the Week 13 operating builds (revenue, COGS, employee cost, other expenses) and the Week 14 supporting schedules (working capital, PPE/intangibles/ROU corkscrews, debt and interest with the revolver, tax and deferred tax, equity and NCI). Linking the income statement is the step that turns those schedules into the Schedule III Division II Statement of Profit and Loss — revenue down to PAT, PAT attributable to owners and EPS — without performing a single new calculation on the statement itself. The P&L becomes a sheet of links and subtotals, plus a block of KPI rows that double as error detectors.

This matters for every target role. An equity research or investment banking analyst defends EPS and EBITDA forecasts line by line; an FP&A analyst uses the same P&L structure for budget-versus-actual and rolling forecasts; a credit analyst reads interest coverage straight off it. Interviewers test the flow-through of a single change (depreciation +₹10 → PAT −7.48 at a 25.17% tax rate), the tax-on-PBT versus NOPAT distinction, and the treatment of leases, associates and NCI. Each of these rests on the P&L pulling from the right schedule with the right sign.

The learning sequence follows the statement from top to bottom. Start with the operating block (s01) — revenue, COGS and opex from the Week 13 builds — and the EBITDA identity (c01) with its stated definition (c05). Then step below EBITDA: D&A from three asset schedules to reach EBIT (s03), other income on average treasury balances (s02) and finance costs combining debt interest, lease interest and Ind AS 23 capitalisation (s04). The EBIT-to-PBT bridge (c02) adds exceptional items, forecast at zero, and the equity-method share of associates (s05). The tax schedule produces current and deferred tax, the NCI schedule splits PAT, and the share schedule gives EPS (s06, c03). The conceptual distinction between tax on PBT in the model and EBIT × (1 − t) for NOPAT (c04) prepares Week 16. Finally, KPI rows (s07) summarise and police the forecast.

The interest lines (s02, s04) close the circularity loop built in Week 14; the circuit breaker must be able to zero them so the P&L can be computed and debugged without iteration. Tax, NCI and associates create the non-cash items and equity movements that topic t2 (CFS) and topic t3 (BS) will consume.

Related concepts to study

Week 13 operating builds and Week 14 schedules

→ Week 13 (w13_t4, w13_t5) operating builds; → Week 14 schedules

The P&L can only be as good as the schedules behind it. The Week 13 revenue build (w13_t4) and cost build (w13_t5) supply every line above EBITDA: segment or volume × price revenue, COGS ratios or per-unit material costs, headcount-driven employee cost and fixed/variable other expenses. Week 14 supplies everything below EBITDA: the PPE, intangibles and ROU corkscrews (D&A), the debt schedule with revolver and commitment fees (interest), the lease schedule (lease interest), the CWIP schedule (capitalised borrowing cost), the tax schedule (current and deferred tax) and the equity/NCI schedule. Before linking, re-check that each schedule's output row is in the same column layout as the IS, that sign conventions are documented, and that each schedule produces one clean total per year for the P&L to pull.

Ind AS 1/Schedule III presentation and Ind AS 28 equity method

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 1/Schedule III presentation, Ind AS 28 equity method

Phase 1 covered how Ind AS 1 and Schedule III Division II structure the P&L: by-nature expense classification, separate lines for other income, finance costs, D&A, exceptional items and tax (current, deferred, earlier years), profit split between owners and NCI, OCI in two categories (items that will and will not be reclassified) and EPS on the face. The model's historical columns must replicate this exactly so drivers are calibrated on reported numbers. Ind AS 28's equity method — investment at cost adjusted for share of post-acquisition profit and OCI, reduced by dividends, with losses stopping at zero carrying amount — directly determines how the associate line flows in s05 and how the investment moves on the BS.

Margin analysis and MIS vs statutory P&L formats

→ Phase 2 (W5–8): margin analysis; → Phase 5 (W17–20): MIS vs statutory P&L formats

Phase 2 margin analysis decomposed gross, EBITDA and PAT margins into price, mix, input cost and operating leverage effects — the same lenses used to judge whether forecast KPI rows (s07) are plausible. Revisit how lease accounting, ESOP costs and other income distort reported margins across peers. Phase 5 will reorganise the same numbers into management (MIS) formats: by-function or contribution-margin P&Ls, segment and channel views, and budget-versus-actual bridges. A well-linked model makes that possible because every statutory line is traceable to a driver; a mapping table from nature to function can then generate both views from one set of schedules.

Common pitfalls

Calculating D&A or interest on the P&L sheet

Calculating D&A or interest on the P&L sheet instead of linking schedules

The mistake: writing =Inputs!D&A_rate*Revenue or =9%*AVERAGE(BS!E30:F30) directly in the IS row. Why it happens: it is quick, and early in the build the schedules may not exist. Why it is wrong: the BS net block and the debt balance come from the schedules, so the P&L charge no longer matches the asset or liability movement; the CFS add-back may link to the schedule while the P&L uses its own number, creating an imbalance; the circuit breaker does not reach a P&L-level interest formula; and scenario or sensitivity changes on the schedule do not flow. Correct treatment: the P&L row is a single same-column link to the schedule total (e.g., =PPE!F35+Intang!F20+Leases!F22). Detection: use Go To Special → Formulas and inspect any IS forecast cell that contains operators or constants other than links and same-sheet subtotals; compare D&A on IS, CFS and the PPE schedule in a three-way check row.

Lease cost double-counted

Lease cost double-counted: rent in opex plus ROU depreciation and lease interest

The mistake: other expenses are forecast as a percentage of revenue using a historical ratio that still contains rent (pre-FY20 history, or a company with partially capitalised leases), while the lease schedule also charges ROU depreciation and lease interest. Why it happens: opex ratios are calibrated on a long history spanning the Ind AS 116 transition, or rent disclosures are not separated. Why it is wrong: lease cost is charged twice, understating EBITDA, EBIT and PAT; on the CFS, the lease payment may also be deducted twice (once inside CFO through opex, once in CFF). Correct treatment: restate history so other expenses exclude rent on capitalised leases; keep only short-term, low-value and variable lease payments (Ind AS 116.5, 116.38) in other expenses; forecast capitalised leases solely through the lease schedule. Detection: compute a "total lease cost" memo row (rent in opex + ROU depreciation + lease interest) and compare with disclosed lease payments; a ratio near 2× is the red flag.

Exceptional items forecast as historical average

Exceptional items forecast as historical average

The mistake: the exceptional items row is forecast as the three- or five-year historical average, e.g. −₹45 crore per year. Why it happens: a desire to be "conservative", or a mechanical fill-right of a driver formula across every P&L row. Why it is wrong: exceptional items are by nature non-recurring (Ind AS 1.97 disclosures); an average has no driver, no tax logic and no cash or BS mapping, so it distorts PAT, EPS and valuation every year and may unbalance the model if its cash effect is not mapped. Correct treatment: forecast zero via an explicit blue input row; enter only known, announced items with their tax, cash and BS consequences mapped. Detection: the exceptional row should be visibly zero in forecasts; reviewers should ask for the source of any non-zero value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Link a full forecast P&L for one year [L3]

You are given FY27E schedule outputs for an NSE-listed consumer company (₹ crore): revenue build total 9,200; materials consumed 4,250; purchases of stock-in-trade 480; changes in inventories (40); employee cost 760 (incl. ESOP 28); other expenses 2,010; PPE depreciation 280; intangible amortisation 35; ROU depreciation 62; interest on average cash 110; MF yield 85; other recurring income 12; term-loan and NCD interest 190; revolver interest and fees 9; lease interest 40; capitalised borrowing costs 22; exceptional items input 0; share of JV profit (after tax, presented after tax) 48; current tax from tax schedule 270; deferred tax charge 14; NCI share from NCI schedule 52; weighted-average shares 52.4 crore. (a) Lay out the Schedule III-ordered P&L with EBITDA and EBIT subtotals. (b) Compute PAT, PAT to owners and basic EPS. (c) Compute the KPI rows: gross margin, EBITDA margin, PAT margin and ETR. (d) State the EBITDA definition note for the sheet.

Q2. Interview walk-through: interest rate rise [L2]

The floating rate on a ₹2,000 crore term loan rises by 100 bp for FY28E (average balance ₹2,000 crore). Tax rate 25.168%; there is no capitalised interest. Walk through the effect on finance costs, PBT, tax, PAT, PAT to owners (NCI holds 10% of the borrowing subsidiary and the loan sits in that subsidiary), CFO/CFF under the Indian CFS format, cash and equity, quantifying each line. Then explain how the circularity changes the second-round effect.

💡 Hint: First-round effect is ₹20 crore pre-tax; the second round arises because lower cash raises the revolver or lowers interest income.

Q3. Review a junior's P&L [L3]

A junior's IS sheet contains: revenue =E8*(1+12%); other expenses =F8*Inputs!$D$20 where D20 was calibrated on FY18–FY26 including pre-Ind AS 116 rent; D&A =PPE!F35; finance costs =Debt!F80; exceptional items =AVERAGE(B24:E24); tax =F19*25.168% where row 19 is EBIT; NCI =F31*Hist_NCI_ratio. For each line identify the problem, its likely impact on P&L, CFS and BS (including whether the check row will catch it), and the corrected linking approach.

Q4. Associates, NCI and EPS for a holding structure [L3]

A listed parent owns 60% of a listed subsidiary (forecast PAT FY27E ₹500 crore, payout 30%) and 35% of an associate (forecast PAT ₹180 crore, payout 50%). Parent standalone PBT excluding dividends from these entities is ₹1,400 crore, tax 25.168%, no temporary differences. Parent shares: 80 crore, with a 2 crore-share QIP on 1 October 2026. (a) Compute consolidated PAT, NCI share, PAT to owners and basic EPS for FY27E (ignore intra-group eliminations other than dividends). (b) State the CFS lines for dividends paid to NCI and dividends received from the associate. (c) Roll forward the associate investment from an opening ₹900 crore.
