Subtopic note

D&A from PPE, Intangible and ROU Schedules to EBIT

Scope: D&A from PPE, intangible and ROU schedules; EBIT

Why it matters

Depreciation and amortisation is the single most important non-cash charge in a three-statement model. It reduces PBT and therefore tax, it is added back in CFO, and it reduces the net block on the Balance Sheet. The famous interview question — "depreciation goes up by ₹10, walk me through the statements" — tests exactly this triad. In an Indian Ind AS model D&A has three sources: the PPE corkscrew (Ind AS 16 / Schedule II), the intangible assets corkscrew (Ind AS 38: software, brands, licences, customer relationships from acquisitions) and, since FY20, the right-of-use asset corkscrew (Ind AS 116). Pulling all three from their schedules, and only from there, is what makes EBIT reliable and what lets the dashboard show EBITDA both pre- and post-Ind AS 116.

Core notes

Sources and the P&L line

Schedule III Division II presents a single expense line "Depreciation and amortisation expense", with a note splitting it by asset class. The model mirrors that:

\[ \text{D\&A}_t = \text{Dep}^{\text{PPE}}_t + \text{Amort}^{\text{Intangibles}}_t + \text{Dep}^{\text{ROU}}_t \]

Component Schedule (Week 14) Driver Ind AS / law
PPE depreciation PPE corkscrew: existing-asset run-off + new-capex vintage waterfall Gross block, useful life, SLM/WDV Ind AS 16; Schedule II useful lives
Intangible amortisation Intangibles corkscrew Cost ÷ useful life; indefinite-life intangibles not amortised Ind AS 38
ROU depreciation Leases schedule ROU asset ÷ shorter of lease term and useful life Ind AS 116
Investment property depreciation PPE or separate row Cost model only under Ind AS 40 in India Ind AS 40

Goodwill is not amortised under Ind AS 103/36; it is tested for impairment. Impairment losses, if any, are separately disclosed and in a forecast are normally zero (treat like exceptional items).

Linking pattern

IS!F18  =PPE!F35+Intang!F20+Leases!F22        ' D&A expense
IS!F19  =IS!F16-IS!F18                         ' EBIT = EBITDA − D&A

Keep the three components as memo rows beneath the P&L (or on the D&A line itself via a small block) because the dashboard and the CFS use them separately: ROU depreciation feeds the pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA reconciliation, and intangible amortisation from acquisitions is often excluded in "adjusted PAT".

EBIT and what it means in an Indian model

\[ \text{EBIT}_t = \text{EBITDA}_t - \text{D\&A}_t \]

EBIT here is operating EBIT — before other income and finance costs. It is the base for NOPAT in DCF (EBIT × (1 − t), see c04), for ROCE (EBIT ÷ capital employed) and for interest coverage (EBIT ÷ finance costs). Note that some Indian broker reports define "EBIT" including other income, and the statutory face shows only "Profit before exceptional items and tax"; define your EBIT on the sheet.

Post-Ind AS 116, EBIT includes ROU depreciation but excludes lease interest (which is in finance costs). Pre-Ind AS 116, the whole rent sat in opex above EBITDA. So Ind AS 116 raises EBITDA by roughly the full lease payment and raises EBIT by approximately the interest component of the lease (because rent is replaced by depreciation + interest, and only depreciation sits above EBIT). For retailers, QSR chains, multiplexes and airlines this is material.

Sign conventions

Decide once whether expenses are stored as positive numbers and subtracted, or as negatives and summed (Week 14 sign convention). A common Project 4 convention: schedules output D&A as a positive number; the P&L subtracts it; the CFS adds it back as a positive. If the PPE schedule stores depreciation as negative (because it reduces the net block), the P&L link must be =-PPE!F35. Mixing conventions produces an imbalance of exactly 2 × D&A — a textbook halving-test case.

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

Item Amount
EBITDA (from s01) 1,510
PPE depreciation (existing 210 + new capex 45) 255
Intangible amortisation (ERP software, acquired brand) 38
ROU depreciation (warehouses, offices) 57
D&A 350
EBIT 1,160
EBIT margin on revenue 8,400 13.8%

Pre-Ind AS 116 view: lease payments of ₹75 crore would have been rent. EBITDA pre-116 = 1,510 − 75 = 1,435; EBIT pre-116 ≈ 1,160 + 57 − 75 = 1,142. The difference (18) approximates lease interest.

Flow-through

A ₹10 crore increase in D&A (say, a shorter useful life input on the PPE sheet): EBIT −10; PBT −10; tax −2.52 at 25.168%; PAT −7.48; CFO: PAT −7.48 + D&A add-back +10 = +2.52 (lower cash tax); net block −10; cash +2.52; total assets −7.48; retained earnings −7.48. The model balances only if all three statements pull D&A from the same schedule cell.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Componentisation (Ind AS 16.43). Large assets (a blast furnace lining, aircraft engines) are depreciated by component; the schedule may need separate lives — the P&L still pulls one total.
  • Schedule II lives vs management estimates. Companies may use lives different from Schedule II with technical justification disclosed; use the company's disclosed policy, not Schedule II by default.
  • Change in depreciation method is a change in estimate (prospective, Ind AS 8), not a restatement — the forecast simply continues on the new basis.
  • Tax depreciation ≠ book depreciation. The P&L uses book D&A; the tax schedule uses income-tax WDV block rates; the difference drives DTL (s06, t3/s04). Never link tax depreciation to the P&L.
  • Impairment and accelerated depreciation on store closures or plant shutdowns belong in exceptional items in history; forecast at zero unless a known closure plan exists.
  • Short-term and low-value leases (Ind AS 116.5) stay in other expenses — they are not in the ROU schedule, so not in D&A.
  • Amortisation of acquired intangibles is often excluded in management's adjusted PAT; your statutory forecast keeps it in.

Key takeaways

  • D&A on the P&L = PPE depreciation + intangible amortisation + ROU depreciation, each from its own Week 14 schedule.
  • EBIT = EBITDA − D&A and is operating EBIT, before other income and finance costs.
  • Ind AS 116 shifts rent into ROU depreciation (above EBIT) and lease interest (below EBIT); state the basis of EBITDA and EBIT.
  • Fix a single sign convention; a sign flip on D&A produces a 2 × D&A imbalance.
  • Book D&A goes to the P&L; tax depreciation lives only on the tax schedule.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the D&A link [L1]

The PPE sheet outputs depreciation as a negative number in row 35, the Intangibles sheet outputs amortisation as a positive number in row 20, and the Leases sheet outputs ROU depreciation as a positive number in row 22. Write the FY27E P&L D&A formula (column F) so that D&A appears as a positive expense, and explain what imbalance would arise if you forgot the sign on the PPE term (PPE depreciation ₹255 crore).

Q2. Ind AS 116 and the EBITDA bridge [L2]

A multiplex chain's FY26A Ind AS P&L shows EBITDA ₹1,420 crore, ROU depreciation ₹520 crore and lease interest ₹480 crore; cash lease payments were ₹960 crore. Revenue was ₹5,600 crore. Compute pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA and margin, estimate the change in EBIT due to Ind AS 116, and state which EBITDA you would use for a net debt/EBITDA covenant that defines debt excluding lease liabilities.

💡 Hint: Match the EBITDA definition to the debt definition used in the ratio.

Q3. D&A flow-through walk [L2]

Useful life of new plant is revised from 20 to 15 years, increasing FY28E depreciation by ₹24 crore. Tax rate is 25.168%; tax depreciation is unchanged. Walk through every line affected on the P&L, CFS and BS (including deferred tax), quantifying each change.

Q4. Impairment in history, nothing in forecast [L3]

A consumer durables company reported a ₹140 crore impairment of an acquired brand in FY25A within "Depreciation, amortisation and impairment". Your intern has computed the historical D&A/revenue ratio of 4.9% including the impairment and applied it to forecast revenue. Explain the problem, how you would restate the history, and how D&A should instead be forecast for the intangible block.
