Subtopic note

Linking Revenue, COGS and Opex from Operating Schedules

Scope: Revenue, COGS and opex pulled from Week 13 operating schedules

Why it matters

The income statement in an integrated model is a reporting sheet, not a calculation sheet. Every number above EBITDA should already exist on an operating schedule built in Week 13 — the revenue build (volume × price, store × throughput, segment × growth), the cost build (material cost per unit, employee cost per head, other expenses as % of revenue or fixed + variable). The P&L simply pulls those results into the Schedule III order. When this discipline holds, a reviewer can audit the P&L in minutes because every forecast cell is a single, same-column link. When it breaks — a growth rate typed into the P&L, an opex line computed as "last year × 1.08" on the statement itself — scenario switches stop flowing, sensitivities miss lines and the Balance Sheet may still balance while the P&L is simply wrong. Analysts in equity research, PE portfolio teams and corporate FP&A all rely on this separation: management can challenge the driver, and the statement updates without anyone touching it.

Core notes

The architecture: drivers → schedules → statements

A standard Project 4 workbook separates three layers:

Layer Sheet(s) Contents Colour convention
Inputs Inputs, Scenarios Growth, price, volume, margin, days, capex drivers per scenario Blue font, input fill
Calculations Revenue, Costs, WC, PPE, Debt, Tax, Equity Corkscrews and driver arithmetic Black font
Outputs IS, BS, CFS, Dashboard Links only, plus subtotals Green font for cross-sheet links

The P&L lines above EBITDA come entirely from the Revenue and Costs schedules. The rule is simple: one forecast cell = one link or one subtotal. A subtotal (e.g. Total expenses) is a SUM of rows on the same sheet; anything else is a direct reference to a schedule row in the same column.

Revenue from operations

Revenue from operations on the P&L equals the total row of the revenue build. For an NSE-listed FMCG company the build might be segment-wise (Home care, Personal care, Foods) with volume growth and price/mix growth; for a cement company, capacity × utilisation × realisation per tonne; for a retailer, store count × revenue per sq ft × area. Whatever the build, the P&L shows the total, and ideally a memo of segment revenue below the statement for the dashboard.

Points to watch:

  • Revenue from operations vs other operating revenue. Schedule III requires "Revenue from operations" to include sale of products, sale of services and other operating revenue (export incentives, scrap sales). If the historical figure includes ₹40 crore of PLI incentives, either forecast them as a separate driver or exclude them consistently from the historical base before growing.
  • Net of GST. Ind AS 115 revenue excludes GST collected on behalf of government; the historical base must be net. Excise duty appears gross only in pre-GST years (FY17 and earlier), so long histories need restating.
  • Discounts and rebates are deducted under Ind AS 115 variable-consideration rules; model net realisation, not list price.

COGS block

Schedule III Division II classifies expenses by nature, so "COGS" is not a single line. It is assembled from:

\[ \text{COGS} = \text{Cost of materials consumed} + \text{Purchases of stock-in-trade} + \text{Changes in inventories of FG, WIP and stock-in-trade} \]

The cost schedule usually forecasts COGS as a percentage of revenue (or material cost per unit × volume) and then, if the P&L must replicate the statutory face, splits it into the three lines using historical proportions. The changes in inventories line deserves care: it is the opening minus closing finished goods/WIP/traded goods. In a model where inventory is forecast on the WC schedule from DIO, the change-in-inventory line can be linked to that schedule so the P&L and BS tell the same story. Many practitioners simply present a single "Cost of goods sold" row in the forecast and keep the nature split only in historicals; that is acceptable provided the EBITDA definition is stated (see c05).

Opex block

The remaining operating expenses by nature are:

  • Employee benefits expense — salaries, PF/ESI contributions, gratuity and leave (Ind AS 19 service cost), share-based payment expense (Ind AS 102). Forecast as headcount × cost per head with wage inflation, or as % of revenue with a floor for fixed staff. ESOP expense should be its own row on the cost schedule because it is non-cash and must be added back in the CFS.
  • Other expenses — power and fuel, freight, advertising, rent for short-term/low-value leases, royalty, repairs, CSR spend (Sec 135: 2% of average net profit of preceding three years for qualifying companies), legal and professional, ECL charge. Split fixed vs variable where the business has operating leverage.

Implementation pattern in Excel

IS!F8   =Revenue!F42                 ' Revenue from operations (total of build)
IS!F10  =Costs!F15                   ' Cost of materials consumed
IS!F11  =Costs!F16                   ' Purchases of stock-in-trade
IS!F12  =WC!F28-WC!G28               ' wrong: mixed columns — avoid
IS!F12  =Costs!F17                   ' Changes in inventories (computed on Costs sheet)
IS!F13  =Costs!F25                   ' Employee benefits expense
IS!F14  =Costs!F40                   ' Other expenses
IS!F15  =SUM(IS!F10:F14)             ' Total operating expenses
IS!F16  =IS!F8-IS!F15                ' EBITDA

Each link is to the same column (same year). Use named ranges or consistent sheet layouts (year columns aligned across all sheets — Week 13 formula-integrity rule) so a single formula can be filled right across all five forecast years.

Worked illustration

A consumer company's Week 13 schedules give, for FY27E (₹ crore): revenue 8,400 (volume +7%, price +4% on FY26E 7,480 → 7,480 × 1.07 × 1.04 ≈ 8,324; plus new-category revenue 76); materials 3,950; traded goods 420; change in inventories −30 (opening minus closing FG/WIP: closing exceeds opening by 30 because inventory is being built, so the line is negative and reduces the expense total); employee cost 690; other expenses 1,860. The P&L sums operating expenses to 3,950 + 420 − 30 + 690 + 1,860 = 6,890 and EBITDA = 8,400 − 6,890 = 1,510, an 18.0% margin. Not one of these figures is typed on the P&L.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Historical columns are hardcoded inputs, sourced from the annual report and restated for consistency; only forecast columns are links. Keep historicals on the P&L in blue or on a separate Hist sheet referenced by the P&L.
  • Reclassifications between years (a company moves freight from other expenses to COGS) must be restated in history before computing ratios, else driver percentages jump.
  • Segment builds that do not sum to consolidated revenue because of inter-segment eliminations need an explicit elimination row on the revenue schedule, not a plug on the P&L.
  • Ind AS 116 removes most rent from other expenses; if the historical base includes pre-FY20 years, rent appears in opex there and not after, distorting cost ratios (see c05 and the lease double-count pitfall).
  • Scenario flow: if the P&L links to the schedule, Bull/Bear changes on the input sheet propagate automatically; any typed number on the P&L will silently ignore the scenario.

Key takeaways

  • The P&L is a links-and-subtotals sheet; every forecast operating line comes from a Week 13 schedule in the same column.
  • COGS under Schedule III is materials + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories; decide whether the forecast replicates that split or presents one COGS row.
  • Keep ESOP expense, CSR and ECL identifiable on the cost schedule because they matter later (CFS add-backs, tax disallowances).
  • Restate historicals (GST, reclassifications, Ind AS 116) before computing the drivers the schedules use.
  • Same-column links and aligned year columns let one formula fill across all forecast years and all scenarios.

Practice Questions

Q1. Audit a P&L revenue row [L1]

A reviewer finds the FY28E revenue cell on the IS sheet reads =E8*(1+Inputs!$D$12) while FY27E reads =Revenue!F42. State two ways this inconsistency can break the model and the correct formula for FY28E assuming the revenue build total sits in row 42 of the Revenue sheet with FY28E in column G.

Q2. Assemble COGS from Schedule III lines [L2]

For FY26A a listed paints company reports (₹ crore): cost of materials consumed 5,120; purchases of stock-in-trade 610; changes in inventories of FG, WIP and stock-in-trade (+85) (i.e. inventories fell); revenue from operations 10,450. The FY27E cost schedule forecasts COGS at the FY26A ratio to revenue and revenue grows 11%. Compute FY26A gross margin, FY27E COGS and explain how you would split FY27E COGS into the three statutory lines.

Q3. Other operating revenue in the base [L2]

A company's FY26A revenue from operations of ₹2,400 crore includes ₹96 crore of PLI scheme incentives that end in FY28. The analyst grows the total ₹2,400 crore at 12% for five years. Explain the forecasting error, quantify the overstatement in FY29E assuming core revenue grows 12% and PLI income is ₹100 crore in FY27E and nil from FY28E, and describe how the revenue schedule should be restructured.

💡 Hint: Separate core revenue and incentives into two driver rows before growing either.

Q4. Designing the cost schedule for operating leverage [L3]

You are building Project 4 for a quick-service restaurant chain listed on NSE. Other expenses of ₹780 crore in FY26A comprise royalty (5% of revenue), delivery commissions (variable), power (semi-variable), advertising (policy: 4.5% of revenue) and corporate overheads (fixed, inflating at 6%). Design the cost-schedule rows and drivers so that the P&L pulls only totals, and explain how your design behaves in a Bear case where same-store sales fall 8%.

Q5. Interview: why not compute on the P&L? [L1]

An interviewer asks: "Why bother with separate schedules? It's faster to compute opex on the income statement." Give a structured answer covering scenario flow, audit trail and error risk.
