Subtopic note

KPI Rows Below the P&L: Growth, Margins and ETR

Scope: KPI rows below P&L: growth, gross/EBITDA/PAT margins, ETR

Why it matters

The first thing a reviewer — a portfolio manager, an investment committee, an interviewer — does with a forecast P&L is scan the ratio rows beneath it. Revenue growth jumping from 9% to 22% in one year, gross margin expanding 400 bp without a stated reason, an ETR of 31% for a 115BAA company or 12% with no MAT credit to explain it: these are spotted in seconds and they decide whether the rest of the model is trusted. KPI rows are therefore both an output (they summarise the forecast) and a control (they reveal broken links, sign errors and implausible assumptions). In FP&A, the same rows become the variance-analysis backbone of monthly MIS. Building them once, consistently, directly beneath the statement is cheap insurance.

Core notes

Placement and design

KPI rows sit in a block immediately below PAT/EPS on the IS sheet, separated by a blank row and a heading "Key ratios". Each ratio is calculated from rows on the same sheet (no cross-sheet references), formatted as % to one decimal (or bp for changes), and shown for both historical and forecast columns so the eye can follow the trend across the actual/forecast boundary. Use IFERROR(…, 0) sparingly — it hides genuine #DIV/0! in an empty column, which is fine, but it can also mask a missing link; prefer an explicit test such as =IF(E8=0,"n.a.",F8/E8-1).

The core rows

\[ \text{Revenue growth}_t = \frac{\text{Revenue}_t}{\text{Revenue}_{t-1}} - 1 \]

\[ \text{Gross margin}_t = \frac{\text{Revenue}_t - \text{COGS}_t}{\text{Revenue}_t} \]

\[ \text{EBITDA margin}_t = \frac{\text{EBITDA}_t}{\text{Revenue}_t}, \qquad \text{EBIT margin}_t = \frac{\text{EBIT}_t}{\text{Revenue}_t} \]

\[ \text{PAT margin}_t = \frac{\text{PAT}_t}{\text{Revenue}_t}, \qquad \text{ETR}_t = \frac{\text{Tax expense}_t}{\text{PBT}_t - \text{Share of associates}_t} \]

Definitions to fix on the sheet:

  • Revenue = revenue from operations (not total income). Including other income in the denominator flatters margins of cash-rich companies.
  • COGS = materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories (Schedule III by-nature lines). Some analysts include power/fuel and freight for manufacturing businesses ("contribution margin") — state the definition.
  • EBITDA = ex-other income; post-Ind AS 116 unless stated (c05).
  • PAT margin on PAT (group) or PAT to owners — be explicit. For companies with large NCI, show both.
  • ETR on PBT excluding associate share (since that share is post-tax). A forecast ETR should be near the statutory rate (25.168% under 115BAA) plus permanent disallowances; material deviations need a reason.

Additional rows worth adding

Row Formula Use
Volume growth / price growth From revenue build memo Decompose growth; Bull/Bear sanity
Employee cost % of revenue Employee cost ÷ revenue Operating leverage check
Other expenses % of revenue Other expenses ÷ revenue Cost discipline
D&A % of opening gross block D&A ÷ opening gross block Implied useful life sanity
Interest cost on average debt Debt interest ÷ average borrowings Implied rate sanity vs input rate
Interest coverage EBIT ÷ finance costs Covenant and rating context
EPS growth Current EPS ÷ prior-year EPS − 1 Valuation
Δ margin (bp) (Current margin − prior margin) × 10,000 Highlights jumps

The implied-rate rows are especially powerful controls: if the input rate is 9% and the implied rate on average debt is 13%, something (a fee, a revolver, a link to the wrong column) is wrong.

Excel implementation

IS!F40  =IF(E8=0,"n.a.",F8/E8-1)                       ' Revenue growth
IS!F41  =IF(F8=0,"n.a.",(F8-SUM(F10:F12))/F8)          ' Gross margin
IS!F42  =IF(F8=0,"n.a.",F16/F8)                        ' EBITDA margin
IS!F43  =IF(F8=0,"n.a.",F31/F8)                        ' PAT margin
IS!F44  =IF((F26-F25)<=0,"n.m.",F30/(F26-F25))         ' ETR on PBT ex-associates
IS!F45  =(F42-E42)*10000                               ' EBITDA margin change, bp

Conditional formatting can shade cells outside sanity bands (e.g., ETR outside 23–28%, EBITDA margin change > 300 bp) so the model flags itself. Note the ETR guard: when PBT is negative or near zero, ETR is not meaningful ("n.m."), which matters in Bear and stress cases.

Worked illustration (₹ crore)

FY26A FY27E
Revenue 7,480 8,400
COGS 4,010 4,340
EBITDA 1,320 1,510
PBT ex-associates 1,060 1,190
Tax 271 299.5
PAT 840 950.5

FY27E: growth 12.3%; gross margin (8,400 − 4,340) ÷ 8,400 = 48.3% vs FY26A 46.4% (+190 bp — needs a reason: price increases ahead of input cost? mix?); EBITDA margin 18.0% vs 17.6%; PAT margin 11.3% vs 11.2%; ETR 25.2% vs 25.6%.

Using KPIs as diagnostics

  • A revenue growth of exactly the same % in every scenario suggests the scenario switch does not reach the revenue build.
  • An EBITDA margin identical to the last actual to many decimals in every year suggests opex is modelled purely as % of revenue — fine as a base, but no operating leverage.
  • ETR swinging when PBT is stable often means deferred tax is linked to the wrong column.
  • PAT margin falling while EBITDA margin rises: check D&A (capex surge) or finance costs (revolver draws).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Growth from a negative or near-zero base (loss to profit) is not meaningful; display "n.m.".
  • Acquisitions in the forecast distort organic growth; show organic and inorganic growth separately.
  • Quarterly seasonality (Q4-heavy capital goods, festive-quarter consumer) does not affect annual ratios but matters when the same template is used for MIS.
  • Ind AS 116 breaks in history (FY19 vs FY20) need a footnote or restated pre-116 margins.
  • ETR with associate share in PBT understates the true rate; always strip it.
  • Margin expansion in Bull cases should be capped by peer bands — a KPI is only useful if someone compares it to something.

Key takeaways

  • Put KPI rows directly beneath the P&L, calculated from same-sheet rows, for historicals and forecasts.
  • Core rows: revenue growth, gross margin, EBITDA margin, (EBIT margin), PAT margin and ETR on PBT ex-associates.
  • Define each metric on the sheet; inconsistent definitions are the main source of disputes with brokers and management.
  • Implied-rate and bp-change rows turn ratios into error detectors.
  • Guard ratios against zero/negative denominators so stress cases display "n.m." rather than nonsense.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and interpret KPI rows [L1]

FY27E: revenue ₹12,600 crore (FY26A ₹11,200 crore), COGS ₹7,180 crore, EBITDA ₹2,270 crore, PBT ₹1,580 crore including ₹40 crore share of associates, tax ₹392 crore, PAT ₹1,188 crore. Compute revenue growth, gross margin, EBITDA margin, PAT margin and ETR, stating the ETR denominator.

Q2. Spot the broken link from ratios [L2]

A model shows an implied interest rate on average debt of 8.9%, 9.1%, 9.0%, 14.6%, 9.2% for FY27E–FY31E with constant input rates of 9%. EBITDA margin and ETR are smooth. List three possible causes of the FY30E spike and how you would test each.

💡 Hint: Look at what changes in FY30E on the debt schedule (a maturity, a revolver draw, a column misalignment).

Q3. Conditional-format sanity bands [L2]

Write formulas (or describe conditional-formatting rules) that flag: (a) ETR outside 23–28% where PBT is positive, (b) EBITDA margin change of more than 250 bp year on year, (c) revenue growth above 25% in any year. Explain why each band is sensible for a mature Indian FMCG company and how you would widen them for a high-growth new-age company.

Q4. Management vs model margins [L3]

Management guides to "EBITDA margin of 21–22% by FY28". Your Base case shows 19.4% in FY28E. Management's EBITDA includes other income and is pre-Ind AS 116. FY28E figures (₹ crore): revenue 9,600; your EBITDA 1,862; other income 140; lease payments 165; ROU depreciation 120; lease interest 52. Reconcile the two definitions and state whether your forecast is truly below guidance.
