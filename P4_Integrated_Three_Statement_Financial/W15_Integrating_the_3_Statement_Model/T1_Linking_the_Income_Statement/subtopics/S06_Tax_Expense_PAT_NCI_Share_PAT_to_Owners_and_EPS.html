Subtopic note

Tax Expense, PAT, NCI Share, PAT to Owners and EPS

Scope: Tax expense from tax schedule; PAT, NCI share, PAT to owners, EPS

Why it matters

The bottom of the P&L is where the model's valuation outputs are born. PAT attributable to owners drives retained earnings, dividends, ROE and EPS; EPS × P/E is how most Indian equity research arrives at a target price. Getting there requires three links that are easy to get wrong: tax expense must come from the Week 14 tax schedule (current tax + deferred tax, not PBT × a hardcoded rate), the NCI share must come from the equity/NCI schedule using each subsidiary's ownership, and EPS must use the weighted-average share count from the share schedule (with dilution for ESOPs). Each of these feeds the CFS and BS — deferred tax as a non-cash add-back, NCI through its own equity corkscrew, share count through capital and premium — so a shortcut here becomes a balance-sheet problem later.

Core notes

Tax expense from the tax schedule

Ind AS 12 tax expense is:

\[ \text{Tax expense}_t = \text{Current tax}_t + \text{Deferred tax}_t \]

The tax schedule (Week 14) computes:

  • Current tax = taxable income × statutory rate, where taxable income = PBT (ex-associate share) + book depreciation − tax depreciation ± Sec 43B items, disallowances (CSR spend under Sec 37 Explanation 2, penalties), Chapter VI-A deductions largely unavailable under 115BAA, exempt income, and less losses brought forward utilised. Under Sec 115BAA the rate is 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%; no MAT.
  • Deferred tax = change in net DTL (or DTA) through P&L, driven by temporary differences (WDV vs book block, gratuity/leave provisions, ECL, Ind AS 116 leases, unrealised FVTPL gains) × rate.
  • Adjustments for earlier years are zero in forecast.

The P&L links two rows:

IS!F28  =Tax!F40           ' Current tax
IS!F29  =Tax!F55           ' Deferred tax charge/(credit)
IS!F30  =IS!F28+IS!F29     ' Total tax expense
IS!F31  =IS!F26-IS!F30     ' PAT (Profit for the year)

Schedule III Division II shows current tax, deferred tax and (where applicable) tax relating to earlier years separately.

A simplified model may set tax = PBT × ETR, with deferred tax zero, if the company's book and tax bases are close. But then the CFS must not add back any ΔDTL, and the BS DTL must be held flat — consistent simplification. The full approach is preferred for Project 4.

PAT and the NCI split

Schedule III Division II requires profit for the year to be split into "attributable to owners of the parent" and "attributable to non-controlling interests"; the same split applies to OCI and total comprehensive income (Ind AS 110.B94, Ind AS 1.81B).

\[ \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t = \text{PAT}_t - \text{NCI share}_t \]

Computing the NCI share:

  1. Subsidiary-level approach (preferred where material). For each listed or significant subsidiary, NCI share = NCI % × subsidiary PAT. Example: a parent holds 51% of a listed subsidiary whose PAT is ₹400 crore → NCI share = 49% × 400 = 196.
  2. Ratio approach. NCI share = historical NCI share ÷ consolidated PAT × forecast PAT. Simple but wrong if subsidiary growth differs from group growth.

The NCI share feeds the NCI corkscrew on the equity schedule:

\[ \text{NCI}_t = \text{NCI}_{t-1} + \text{NCI share of PAT}_t + \text{NCI share of OCI}_t - \text{Dividends to NCI}_t \]

PAT to owners feeds retained earnings. Dividends paid by the subsidiary to NCI are a CFF outflow for the group (Ind AS 7.17 — payments to owners include NCI). Forgetting this is a frequent imbalance source.

EPS

Under Ind AS 33:

\[ \text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{PAT attributable to equity holders of parent}}{\text{Weighted-average equity shares outstanding}} \]

\[ \text{Diluted EPS} = \frac{\text{PAT}^{\text{owners}} + \text{after-tax effect of dilutive instruments}}{\text{WA shares} + \text{dilutive potential shares}} \]

  • Weighted average from the share-count schedule: shares issued under ESOPs or a QIP mid-year weighted by time outstanding; buyback shares removed from the buyback date (Ind AS 33.19–21).
  • Bonus issues and splits are applied retrospectively to all periods (Ind AS 33.64) — restate historical EPS when a split occurs (e.g., face value ₹10 → ₹2).
  • Dilution from ESOPs uses the treasury-stock method: options × (1 − exercise price ÷ average market price) (Ind AS 33.45–46). Anti-dilutive instruments are excluded.
  • Preference dividends on cumulative preference shares are deducted from the numerator.

EPS is presented in ₹ to two decimals on the face of the P&L (Schedule III). Forecast EPS is a key dashboard KPI and the bridge to the P/E cross-check in Week 16.

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

Line Amount
PBT (ex-associates) 1,190
Share of JV profit 60
Current tax (taxable income 1,230 × 25.168%) 309.6
Deferred tax credit (DTA increase on ₹40 crore of provisions disallowed until paid) (10.1)
Total tax 299.5
PAT 1,190 + 60 − 299.5 = 950.5
NCI share (49% × subsidiary PAT 140) 68.6
PAT to owners 881.9
WA shares (crore) 48.2
Basic EPS (₹) 18.30

ETR on PBT ex-associates = 299.5 ÷ 1,190 = 25.2%. Here taxable income is ₹40 crore above book PBT (e.g., provisions disallowed until paid under Sec 43B), creating a DTA increase of ₹10.1 crore, i.e. a deferred tax credit.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Companies still on the old regime (with MAT credit or Sec 80-IA/10AA deductions) may have ETRs well below 25%; model the regime choice explicitly and the eventual switch to 115BAA once MAT credit is exhausted.
  • Tax on OCI items (DB remeasurement, FVOCI) goes to OCI, not P&L tax expense.
  • Loss years: current tax cannot go negative — use MAX(0, …) and a loss carry-forward corkscrew (t5 stress testing).
  • Subsidiaries in other jurisdictions — use a blended rate or entity-wise tax if material.
  • NCI share of losses continues to be attributed even if NCI becomes a deficit balance (Ind AS 110.B94).
  • Buyback in the forecast: reduces WA shares from the buyback date; EPS accretion is a common management talking point — verify it is driven by the share schedule, not typed.

Key takeaways

  • Tax expense = current tax + deferred tax, both linked from the tax schedule; never PBT × a hardcoded rate in a full model.
  • PAT is split into owners and NCI; the NCI share is best computed subsidiary-wise and feeds the NCI corkscrew.
  • Dividends to NCI are a group CFF outflow; forgetting them unbalances the BS.
  • EPS uses weighted-average shares (and diluted shares for ESOPs) from the share schedule, restated for bonus/splits.
  • A simplified ETR approach is acceptable only if DTL is held flat and no ΔDTL is added back.

Practice Questions

Q1. Link tax and compute ETR [L1]

For FY28E the tax schedule shows current tax ₹342 crore and a deferred tax charge of ₹18 crore. PBT excluding a ₹25 crore associate share is ₹1,410 crore. Write the P&L rows for tax and PAT and compute the ETR, stating the denominator you use and why.

Q2. NCI share: ratio vs subsidiary approach [L2]

Group PAT FY26A is ₹2,000 crore with NCI share ₹180 crore, all from a 60%-owned listed subsidiary. Forecast FY27E: group PAT ₹2,300 crore; subsidiary PAT grows from ₹450 crore to ₹600 crore. Compute FY27E NCI share under both approaches and explain which is correct and why.

Q3. Weighted-average shares and diluted EPS [L2]

Opening shares 50 crore (FV ₹2). ESOP allotment of 0.6 crore shares on 1 October 2027. Buyback of 1.2 crore shares on 1 January 2028. FY28E PAT to owners ₹1,050 crore. Outstanding options at year-end: 1.5 crore with exercise price ₹320; average market price ₹480. Compute basic and diluted EPS for FY28E.

💡 Hint: Weight each change by months outstanding; treasury-stock method for options.

Q4. Simplified vs full tax approach [L3]

An analyst sets tax expense = PBT × 25.168% on the P&L, but leaves the tax schedule's ΔDTL row linked into the CFS and the DTL line on the BS linked to the tax schedule's closing DTL. Explain why the model will not balance, estimate the difference if the tax schedule's ΔDTL is ₹22 crore, and recommend a consistent fix under each approach.
