Subtopic note

Exceptional Items and Equity-Method Share of Associates/JVs

Scope: Exceptional items (zero in forecast), share of associates/JVs under equity method

Why it matters

Two lines on an Indian consolidated P&L routinely confuse modellers. Exceptional items — VRS payouts, impairment of a subsidiary, profit on sale of a business, labour-code transition costs, fire losses, regulatory settlements — swing reported PAT but are by nature non-recurring; forecasting them at a historical average contaminates every year of the forecast. Share of profit/(loss) of associates and joint ventures — Tata Motors' JV share, a cement company's stake in a grinding JV, an FMCG company's 49% associate — is a one-line equity-method income that has no cash counterpart except dividends received. Treat it as cash and the model's cash is overstated; omit the investment roll-forward and the BS does not balance. These two lines are small on the face but are among the most common sources of P&L–CFS–BS mismatches in forecast models.

Core notes

Exceptional items: what Schedule III and Ind AS require

Schedule III Division II includes a line "Exceptional items" between "Profit before exceptional items and tax" and "Profit before tax". Ind AS 1 does not use the word "exceptional"; it requires separate disclosure of the nature and amount of material items of income and expense (Ind AS 1.97–98), e.g. write-downs of inventories or PPE, restructuring costs, disposals of PPE or investments, discontinued operations, litigation settlements and reversals of provisions. Ind AS 1 prohibits presenting any item as extraordinary (Ind AS 1.87). In Indian practice, "exceptional items" is the Schedule III vehicle for the Ind AS 1.97 disclosures.

Why zero in forecast

Exceptional items, by definition, are not expected to recur in the normal course. Forecasting them at a historical average:

  • builds a permanent drag (or boost) into PBT, PAT, EPS and valuation;
  • has no driver — nobody can explain what the ₹50 crore per year represents;
  • distorts tax (is it deductible?) and the CFS (is it cash?).

The forecast row therefore contains zero, as an explicit blue input row (Inputs!F40:J40 = 0) so a user can model a known item — for example, announced restructuring costs of ₹120 crore in FY27E, or a contracted sale of a non-core division with expected profit. If a known item is entered, the user must map its cash and BS consequences (cash payout in CFO or proceeds in CFI; disposal of assets/liabilities), which is why a single input row with a comment "map cash effect in CFS row X" is preferable to a buried number.

Discontinued operations

If a business is classified as held for sale or discontinued (Ind AS 105), Schedule III presents "Profit/(loss) from discontinued operations (after tax)" separately. Forecasts normally exclude the discontinued business from operating schedules from the expected disposal date, show the disposal proceeds in CFI, and derecognise its assets and liabilities.

Share of associates and JVs: equity method mechanics

Under Ind AS 28, an investment in an associate (significant influence, typically 20–50%) or a joint venture (joint control, Ind AS 111) is initially recognised at cost and adjusted thereafter for the investor's share of the investee's profit or loss and OCI; dividends received reduce the carrying amount (Ind AS 28.10). In the consolidated P&L the share of profit is a single line.

Equity-method investment corkscrew:

\[ \text{Inv}_t = \text{Inv}_{t-1} + s \times \text{PAT}^{\text{assoc}}_t + s \times \text{OCI}^{\text{assoc}}_t - \text{Dividends received}_t + \text{Additional investment}_t \]

with \( s \) the investor's ownership share. Losses beyond the carrying amount are not recognised unless the investor has obligations (Ind AS 28.38–39): the investment floors at zero.

Where it sits in the P&L

Schedule III Division II does not mandate a single position. Companies present it either:

  • after PBT — "Profit before share of associates/JVs and tax", then "Share of profit of associates/JVs", then tax — or more commonly
  • between exceptional items and tax, with a note that the share is already net of the investee's tax, or
  • after tax expense, just before "Profit for the year".

Because the share is already post-tax for the investee, adding it before tax expense and then applying the group's tax rate on PBT would over-tax it. The model must compute tax on PBT excluding the share of associates, whatever the presentation. Hence the concept formula "± share of associates per presentation" (c02).

Cash flow and BS treatment

Statement Treatment
P&L Share of profit (post-tax) increases PAT
CFS (indirect) Deduct share of profit in CFO reconciliation (non-cash); dividends received from associates in CFI
BS Investment in associates increases by share of profit − dividends

Forecasting choices: (a) grow the associate's PAT at a stated rate and apply the payout ratio for dividends; (b) hold the share of profit at last actual; (c) for immaterial associates, hold both the P&L line and the investment flat (zero in forecast) — consistent, because nothing moves.

Worked illustration (₹ crore)

Investor holds 40% of a JV. JV PAT FY27E 150, dividend payout 50%.

  • Share of profit = 40% × 150 = 60 (P&L, post-tax line).
  • Dividend received = 40% × 75 = 30 (CFI inflow).
  • CFO: deduct 60 (non-cash income).
  • Investment: opening 520 + 60 − 30 = 550.
  • Net cash effect = +30; BS asset change +30 (cash) +30 (investment) = +60 = equity increase via PAT. Balanced.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Known exceptional items (announced VRS, signed asset sale): model explicitly with cash and BS effects; otherwise zero.
  • Tax on exceptional items: if entered, assess deductibility (impairment of goodwill is not deductible; VRS under Sec 35DDA is deductible over five years) — tax schedule adjustment needed.
  • Associate turning loss-making: stop recognising losses when the carrying amount reaches zero; the model should MAX(0, …) the investment and suspend the share line.
  • Joint operations (Ind AS 111) are not equity accounted; the share of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses is line-by-line.
  • Standalone vs consolidated: in standalone financials, associates are held at cost (or fair value) under Ind AS 27; share of profit does not appear — only dividends as other income. Ensure the Project 4 model is built on the consolidated set.
  • NCI interaction: share of associates held through a non-wholly-owned subsidiary flows partly to NCI via PAT allocation.

Key takeaways

  • Exceptional items are a Schedule III line used for Ind AS 1.97 disclosures; forecast at zero via an explicit input row.
  • Any known exceptional item entered must have its tax, cash and BS consequences mapped.
  • Share of associates/JVs is post-tax, non-cash income; deduct it in CFO, show dividends received in CFI, and roll the investment forward.
  • Compute group tax on PBT excluding the associate share regardless of presentation.
  • Hold immaterial associates flat on both P&L and BS — consistency beats precision.

Practice Questions

Q1. Restating history for exceptional items [L1]

A company's FY24A–FY26A PBT includes exceptional items of ₹−85 crore (impairment), ₹+210 crore (profit on sale of land) and ₹−40 crore (VRS). Explain how you would compute normalised PBT margins for driver calibration and what the forecast exceptional row should contain.

Q2. Equity-method corkscrew [L2]

A company owns 26% of an associate whose PAT is forecast at ₹200 crore in FY27E growing 10% p.a., with a 40% dividend payout. Opening investment on 1 April 2026 is ₹610 crore. Build the investment roll-forward for FY27E–FY29E, and list the CFO and CFI lines each year.

Q3. Associate share and tax [L2]

A model shows PBT including a ₹60 crore share of JV profit and computes tax at 25.168% on that PBT. PBT excluding the share is ₹1,200 crore. Quantify the error in tax expense and PAT, and propose a formula structure that is correct regardless of where the line is presented.

💡 Hint: Tax base = PBT − share of associates; present the share wherever the company does.

Q4. Announced restructuring [L3]

Management has announced a plant closure in FY27E: VRS cost ₹90 crore (paid in cash in FY27E, deductible under Sec 35DDA in five equal instalments), impairment of the plant's net block ₹140 crore, and sale of land in FY28E for ₹60 crore with carrying value ₹15 crore. Describe every entry you would make across the input row, P&L, tax schedule, CFS and BS for FY27E and FY28E.
