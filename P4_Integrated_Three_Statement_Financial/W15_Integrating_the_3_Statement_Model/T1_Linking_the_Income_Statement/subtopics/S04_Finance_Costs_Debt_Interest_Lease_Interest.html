Subtopic note

Finance Costs: Debt Interest, Lease Interest, Capitalised Interest

Scope: Finance costs: debt interest + lease interest − capitalised borrowing costs

Why it matters

Finance costs connect the capital structure to earnings. In a leveraged Indian company — infrastructure, power, cement, real estate, telecom, airlines — finance costs can consume half of EBIT, and their behaviour in a downside scenario drives covenant breaches and the revolver. In an integrated model the line is assembled from three schedules: the debt schedule (tranche-wise interest including the revolver and commitment fees), the lease schedule (Ind AS 116 interest unwinding on the lease liability) and the CWIP/borrowing-cost schedule (Ind AS 23 capitalisation, which reduces the P&L charge and increases the asset). Missing any one of these produces a P&L that cannot be reconciled to the company's note on finance costs, and an interest coverage ratio that misleads.

Core notes

Composition

Schedule III Division II "Finance costs" covers interest expense, other borrowing costs and the applicable net gain/loss on foreign-currency transactions treated as borrowing costs (Ind AS 23.6(e)). In the model:

\[ \text{Finance costs}_t = I^{\text{debt}}_t + I^{\text{lease}}_t + F_t - C_t \]

where \( I^{\text{debt}}_t \) is interest on all borrowings (term loans, NCDs, CP, cash-credit/revolver), \( I^{\text{lease}}_t \) is interest on lease liabilities, \( F_t \) is other borrowing costs (commitment fees on undrawn revolver, amortisation of upfront fees under the effective-interest method; interest on income-tax shortfalls is presented in finance costs by some companies and in other expenses by others — follow the company's disclosed policy), and \( C_t \) is borrowing cost capitalised into qualifying assets (CWIP).

Debt interest

From the Week 14 debt schedule, tranche by tranche:

\[ I^{\text{debt}}_t = \sum_{k} r_{k,t} \times \frac{D_{k,t-1} + D_{k,t}}{2} + \text{fee} \times (\text{Limit} - \overline{\text{Revolver}}_t) \]

with \( r_{k,t} \) the tranche rate (fixed coupon for NCDs; MCLR/EBLR/T-bill benchmark + spread for bank loans), \( D_{k} \) the closing balances, and the commitment fee on the undrawn revolver limit. Average-balance interest creates the debt circularity (interest → PAT → cash → revolver → interest) which runs through the circuit breaker exactly as interest income does (s02). With the breaker on, interest is zero or switched to opening balance.

Lease interest

The Ind AS 116 lease schedule unwinds the liability:

\[ L_t = L_{t-1} + \text{New leases}_t + I^{\text{lease}}_t - \text{Lease payments}_t, \qquad I^{\text{lease}}_t = i \times L_{t-1} \;(\text{or on average}) \]

where \( i \) is the incremental borrowing rate. The P&L links \( I^{\text{lease}}_t \) from that schedule. The CFS shows the whole lease payment (principal + interest) in financing (see t2). Lease interest belongs in finance costs, never in other expenses — putting it in opex inflates EBITDA confusion and double-counts if rent is also there (topic pitfall).

Capitalised borrowing costs (Ind AS 23)

Borrowing costs directly attributable to the acquisition, construction or production of a qualifying asset (one that necessarily takes a substantial period to get ready — a new plant, a real-estate project, a refinery expansion) are capitalised into the asset's cost (Ind AS 23.8). Capitalisation starts when expenditure, borrowing costs and activities are all under way, is suspended during extended periods of inactive development, and ceases when the asset is substantially ready (Ind AS 23.17, 20, 22). For general borrowings, the capitalisation rate is the weighted average rate on borrowings outstanding, applied to the expenditure on the asset (Ind AS 23.14).

Model mechanics (CWIP schedule):

CWIP corkscrew (₹ crore) FY27E
Opening CWIP 600
Add: capex spend on project 900
Add: capitalised borrowing cost 54
Less: transferred to PPE on commissioning (1,000)
Closing CWIP 554

Capitalised interest of ₹54 crore: total interest on debt is computed on the debt schedule; ₹54 crore is moved into CWIP and the P&L finance cost is reduced by ₹54 crore. The cash interest paid is still the full amount — in the CFS, the capitalised portion is a cash outflow. Ind AS 7.32 requires total interest paid to be disclosed whether expensed or capitalised; practice in India is to show capitalised interest within capex (CFI) or within interest paid (CFF) — choose one and apply consistently, keeping the BS change in CWIP equal to capex cash + capitalised interest − transfers.

Flow logic: capitalised interest raises CWIP, then PPE on commissioning, and comes back to the P&L as higher depreciation over the asset's life.

Linking pattern

Debt!F80  =SUM(Debt!F40:F44)                 ' tranche interest incl. revolver
Debt!F81  =Inputs!F25*(Inputs!F24-AVERAGE(Debt!E30,Debt!F30))   ' commitment fee on undrawn limit
Leases!F18                                    ' lease interest
CWIP!F12                                      ' borrowing cost capitalised
IS!F22    =Debt!F80+Debt!F81+Leases!F18-CWIP!F12

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

Term loan opening 2,000, closing 1,700 at 9.0%; NCD 1,000 flat at 8.2%; revolver opening 0, closing 150 at 9.5%, limit 500, commitment fee 0.25% on undrawn average; lease liability opening 420 at 9%; capitalised borrowing cost 54.

  • Term loan: 9.0% × 1,850 = 166.5
  • NCD: 8.2% × 1,000 = 82.0
  • Revolver: 9.5% × 75 = 7.1; fee 0.25% × (500 − 75) = 1.1
  • Lease interest: 9% × 420 = 37.8
  • Less capitalised: (54.0)

Finance costs = 166.5 + 82.0 + 7.1 + 1.1 + 37.8 − 54.0 = 240.5. Interest coverage on EBIT 1,160 = 4.8×.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unwinding of discount on provisions (decommissioning, Ind AS 37) is also presented within finance costs by many companies; forecast it from the provision schedule or hold at last actual if immaterial.
  • Interest on income-tax and GST delays — treat per the company's accounting policy; do not forecast.
  • Forex on foreign-currency borrowings: only the portion regarded as an adjustment to interest cost is a borrowing cost (Ind AS 23.6(e)); forecasts normally assume no forex movement.
  • Zero-coupon or deep-discount instruments accrue effective interest that is non-cash: the P&L charge exceeds cash interest paid, and the accrual increases the debt balance — CFS must add back the non-cash portion.
  • Capitalisation ceasing when a project commissions mid-year: the CWIP schedule should time the stop, otherwise finance costs jump a year late.
  • Revolver interest with circularity on — keep the breaker switch so the P&L can be computed without the loop when debugging.

Key takeaways

  • Finance costs = debt interest (incl. revolver and fees) + lease interest − Ind AS 23 capitalised borrowing costs.
  • Each component is computed on its schedule; the P&L only adds them.
  • Capitalised interest reduces the P&L charge but not cash paid; it builds CWIP and later depreciation.
  • Average-balance interest is circular; route it through the circuit breaker.
  • Classify lease interest and capitalised interest consistently between P&L, CFS and the net-debt reconciliation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Assemble finance costs [L1]

From the schedules for FY28E (₹ crore): term loan interest 142, NCD interest 82, revolver interest 11, commitment fee 1.4, lease interest 41, unwinding of decommissioning provision 6, borrowing costs capitalised 0 (project commissioned in FY27E). Compute finance costs and the interest coverage ratio if EBIT is ₹1,240 crore.

Q2. Ind AS 23 capitalisation rate [L2]

A cement company is building a 3 MTPA plant financed partly by a specific loan of ₹800 crore at 8.8% (drawn on 1 April FY27E, of which ₹200 crore was temporarily invested at 6.5% for the full year) and partly by general borrowings. General borrowings are ₹1,500 crore at 9.2% and ₹500 crore at 8.4% throughout the year. Expenditure on the plant financed from general borrowings averages ₹300 crore during FY27E. Compute the borrowing cost to be capitalised and the reduction in P&L finance costs.

💡 Hint: Specific borrowings: actual cost less investment income on temporary investment; general borrowings: weighted-average rate × expenditure.

Q3. Capitalised interest in the CFS [L2]

Total interest paid in cash is ₹310 crore, of which ₹54 crore is capitalised into CWIP. Capex cash spend excluding interest is ₹900 crore. Show two acceptable CFS presentations, and for each confirm that the CFS movement reconciles to the change in CWIP + PPE on the BS.

Q4. Recession scenario and finance costs [L3]

In the Bear case, EBITDA falls 35%, the revolver draws to ₹480 crore against a ₹500 crore limit, and the floating spread rises 150 bp under a ratings-downgrade step-up clause. Describe which schedule rows change, how the circularity affects convergence, and which dashboard flags you would expect to trip.
