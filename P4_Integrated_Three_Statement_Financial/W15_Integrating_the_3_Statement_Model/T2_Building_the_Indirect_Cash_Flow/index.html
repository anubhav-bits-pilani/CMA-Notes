Roadmap topic: Building the Indirect Cash Flow Statement: Net Income + D&A – ΔNWC (CFO) – Capex (CFI) + Net Debt Issuance/Repayment – Dividends (CFF) = Net Change in Cash

Overview

The indirect cash flow statement is the bridge between the income statement and the balance sheet. In an integrated model it is not forecast — it is derived from the other two: profit, adjusted for every non-cash item, plus or minus every balance sheet movement. That is why a correctly built CFS makes the balance sheet balance automatically: closing cash from the CFS is the only number that ties the BS together, and it is right only if every movement has been captured with the correct sign. The roadmap formula — Net income + D&A − ΔNWC (CFO), − Capex (CFI), ± net debt − dividends (CFF) = net change in cash — is the skeleton; this topic fills it in for an Indian Ind AS company.

For the target roles this is core craft. Equity analysts in India read cash conversion (CFO/EBITDA, FCF/PAT) from the Indian-format statement; credit analysts reconcile net debt through it; FP&A teams build monthly cash forecasts from the same blocks; and interviewers love questions such as "if depreciation rises by ₹10, what happens to cash?". Two formats coexist: the international PAT-start layout used by most training material and models, and the Indian PBT-start layout used by every Indian annual report. A practitioner must be able to build either and reconcile them exactly.

The learning sequence: start with the PAT-start CFO engine (s01, c01) — non-cash add-backs and working capital — then convert it to the Indian format that starts at PBT, reverses finance costs and interest income and deducts taxes paid (s02, c02). Move to investing flows — capex including CWIP, disposals, investments, interest and dividends received (s03, c03) — and financing flows — debt, revolver, equity, buybacks, dividends, interest and lease payments (s04, c04). Close with the cash roll-forward and the definition of cash and cash equivalents versus other bank balances (s05, c05), and use the Ind AS 7 financing-liability reconciliation as a cross-check (s06). Throughout, the sign rule (c06) is the organising principle: an increase in an asset is an outflow, an increase in a liability or equity is an inflow, and every CFS line maps to a BS movement or a P&L non-cash item.

The CFS draws on every Week 14 schedule — WC, PPE/CWIP, intangibles, leases, debt and revolver, tax, equity and NCI — and on the P&L from topic t1. Its closing cash is consumed by topic t3 (linking the BS) and its line-by-line mapping is the backbone of t4 debugging.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 7 classification for non-financial entities

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 7 classification (interest paid in financing for non-financial entities)

Ind AS 7.33 gives non-financial entities a choice: interest paid may be operating or financing, interest and dividends received operating or investing; Ind AS 7.34 allows dividends paid in financing or operating; Ind AS 7.35 puts taxes in operating unless specifically identifiable. Indian practice for non-financial companies is settled — interest paid in CFF, interest and dividends received in CFI, dividends paid in CFF, taxes in CFO — and the choice must be applied consistently across periods. Revisit how this differs for banks and NBFCs (interest in CFO), and how Ind AS 116 lease payments follow the interest-paid policy for the interest portion. The model's PBT-start CFO only works if these classifications are mirrored in the CFI and CFF sections.

CFO/EBITDA conversion and earnings quality

→ Phase 2 (W5–8): CFO/EBITDA conversion and earnings-quality analysis

Phase 2 used cash conversion to test earnings quality: CFO/EBITDA persistently below 60–70% for a mature company suggests aggressive revenue recognition, rising receivables, inventory build-up or capitalisation of operating costs; CFO/PAT well below 1 suggests accruals-driven profit. The forecast CFS should produce conversion ratios consistent with history unless the WC drivers deliberately change. Adding a CFO/EBITDA row beneath the CFS and comparing it with the last five years is a sanity check: a jump to 110% usually means a sign error on ΔNWC or a missing deduction such as taxes paid.

FCFF from the same blocks

→ Week 16 (w16_t1): FCFF built from the same blocks

Week 16's free cash flow to the firm uses the same components as this CFS, but rearranged on an unlevered basis: FCFF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC (± other non-cash). Equivalently, FCFF ≈ CFO (Indian format, before interest) − taxes on interest shield − capex. Interest paid, debt flows and dividends are excluded because they are claims of capital providers; interest received and treasury flows are excluded because cash is added in the EV bridge. Lease treatment must match the EV definition. Building the CFS cleanly now — with each line sourced from a schedule — means the DCF can link to the same rows rather than recomputing them.

Ind AS 116 lease payments in CFF

Ind AS 116 lease payments split: principal and interest both in CFF

Under Ind AS 116.50 a lessee classifies cash payments for the principal portion of the lease liability within financing, the interest portion according to its Ind AS 7 policy for interest paid, and short-term, low-value and variable payments not included in the liability within operating. Because Indian non-financial entities classify interest paid in financing, both principal and interest on capitalised leases sit in CFF. Consequences: CFO rises versus the pre-Ind AS 116 era (rent no longer deducted), CFF falls by the same amount, and FCF definitions must decide whether to deduct lease payments. New leases are non-cash (Ind AS 7.43): the ROU asset and the liability rise with no CFS line.

Common pitfalls

Adding back total tax expense instead of only deferred tax

Adding back total tax expense instead of only deferred tax

The mistake: in a PAT-start CFO, the modeller adds back the full tax expense (current + deferred) as a "non-cash" item, or adds it back and then forgets to deduct taxes paid. Why it happens: confusion between the two formats — the Indian PBT-start layout implicitly "adds back" all tax by starting before tax, then deducts taxes paid. Why it is wrong: current tax is (largely) cash; adding it back overstates CFO by current tax less any Δtax payable. Correct treatment: PAT-start — add back only the deferred tax charge (and Δ current tax liability as a separate line); PBT-start — no tax add-back, deduct income taxes paid from the tax schedule. Detection: the BS check shows a difference equal to current tax (or taxes paid) in every year — a large, growing difference roughly 25% of PBT is the giveaway.

Mixing PAT-start and PBT-start formats

Mixing PAT-start and PBT-start formats, so interest is double-counted or omitted

The mistake: the CFO starts at PAT (which is after finance costs and includes interest income) but the CFF also deducts interest paid and the CFI adds interest received — without the CFO reversing them; or the CFO starts at PBT and adds back finance costs, but CFF omits interest paid. Why it happens: copying a US-style PAT-start template while applying Indian classification for interest. Why it is wrong: interest expense is deducted twice (or never), and interest income counted twice (or never), so cash is misstated by the net interest amount. Correct treatment: pick one classification and enforce it: if interest paid is in CFF, CFO must add back finance costs; if interest received is in CFI, CFO must remove interest income. Detection: the BS difference equals finance costs, interest income or their net; compare CFO with the reported historical CFO in the same format.

Missing a non-cash item

Missing a non-cash item (ESOP expense, ΔDTL), creating an imbalance of exactly that amount

The mistake: the ESOP reserve on the BS increases with the Ind AS 102 charge, or the DTL increases with the deferred tax charge, but the CFS has no add-back line. Why it happens: the CFS template was built before the SBP or deferred tax schedule existed, or the item is small and assumed immaterial. Why it is wrong: the BS line moves without a cash counterpart, so cash is understated by exactly the item; the check row shows a difference of exactly the SBP charge or ΔDTL each year. Correct treatment: maintain a BS–CFS mapping table (c06) with every BS line tagged to a CFS line or "held constant"; add the missing non-cash line. Detection: search the schedules for a movement equal to the check-row difference (exact-match search, t4); a difference that matches the ESOP charge or ΔDTL to the rupee identifies it immediately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a full forecast CFS in Indian format [L3]

FY28E data for an NSE-listed consumer company (₹ crore): PBT 1,420 (associate share of 40 presented after tax); D&A 410 (incl. ROU 70); finance costs 260 (debt interest 210 incl. revolver; lease interest 50); capitalised interest 0; interest income 90 and MF income 60, all received in cash; SBP 25; receivables +110; inventories +60; payables +70; other current liabilities +15; current tax 360, current tax liability +10; deferred tax charge 18; capex spend into CWIP 700 plus direct additions 80; no disposals; liquid funds held flat; dividend from associate 15; term-loan repayment 400; NCD issue 500; revolver from 150 to 0; dividends paid to owners 320 and NCI 30; ESOP proceeds 12; interest paid 210; lease payments 130 (interest 50); opening cash and CE 812. (a) Prepare CFO (PBT start), CFI and CFF. (b) Compute closing cash. (c) Reconcile CFO to a PAT-start build.

Q2. Interview flow test [L2]

Depreciation increases by ₹10 crore; tax rate 25.168%; no change to tax depreciation. Walk through the effect on PAT, deferred tax, CFO (in both PAT-start and Indian formats), cash and the BS, explaining why the answer differs from the classic "PAT −7.48, cash +2.52" when tax depreciation is unchanged.

💡 Hint: If tax depreciation does not change, current tax does not change; the tax saving is deferred, not cash.

Q3. Diagnose three imbalances [L3]

A model's check row shows: FY27E +22.0; FY28E +24.5; FY29E +27.1 (assets exceed liabilities + equity), growing each year. Separately, in FY30E only, there is an additional +480. The ESOP charge is 22.0, 24.5, 27.1, 29.8, 32.8 across FY27E–FY31E, and CWIP rises by 480 in FY30E (from 300 to 780) before falling back in FY31E. (a) Identify the two errors and the CFS lines responsible. (b) Predict the FY31E difference before fixing. (c) Describe the corrected links.

Q4. Lease-heavy retailer cash flows [L3]

A retailer has opening lease liabilities of ₹2,400 crore, signs new leases of ₹600 crore on 1 April 2027 (IBR 9%), and pays ₹520 crore of lease payments at year-end. Variable rent (revenue-linked, not in the liability) is ₹80 crore. (a) Prepare the lease-liability corkscrew and the Ind AS 7.44 reconciliation. (b) Show where each cash flow appears in the CFS and the effect on CFO versus a pre-Ind AS 116 presentation. (c) Explain how you would define FCF for this company on the dashboard.
