Key concept

CFF Identity: Debt, Equity, Distributions, Interest, Leases

Concept: CFF = Debt raised − Debt repaid ± Revolver + Equity issued − Buyback − Dividends − Interest paid − Lease payments

Statement

\[ \text{CFF}_t = \text{DR}_t - \text{DP}_t \pm \Delta\text{Rev}_t + \text{EI}_t - \text{BB}_t - \text{Div}_t - \text{IP}_t - \text{LP}_t \]

where \( \text{DR}_t \) is proceeds from borrowings (term loans, NCDs, ECBs, net short-term facilities), \( \text{DP}_t \) repayments (mandatory amortisation, bullets, sweep), \( \Delta\text{Rev}_t = \text{Rev}_t - \text{Rev}_{t-1} \) the revolver draw (+) or repayment (−), \( \text{EI}_t \) equity issued net of issue costs (QIP, rights, ESOP exercise), \( \text{BB}_t \) buyback consideration, \( \text{Div}_t \) dividends paid to owners and to NCI, \( \text{IP}_t \) interest paid on borrowings (including capitalised interest if so presented), and \( \text{LP}_t \) total lease payments (principal + interest) on Ind AS 116 lease liabilities.

Explanation

Financing activities change the size and composition of equity and borrowings (Ind AS 7.17). Each term is a corkscrew movement:

Term Corkscrew
DR, DP Tranche debt schedule
ΔRev Revolver block (cash waterfall)
EI, BB Share capital, securities premium, CRR
Div Retained earnings (owners) and NCI corkscrew; adjusted for dividends payable
IP Finance cost on borrowings − Δinterest accrued (+ capitalised)
LP Lease liability corkscrew

For Indian non-financial companies, interest paid is financing (Ind AS 7.33) and both lease principal and lease interest are financing (Ind AS 7.17(e), Ind AS 116.50). Dividends paid may be financing or operating (Ind AS 7.34); financing is universal in practice. Gross presentation of borrowings is required except for quick-turnover items (Ind AS 7.21–22).

Exceptions: non-cash financing (conversion of debt to equity, new leases, bonus issues, CRR transfers) does not appear (Ind AS 7.43). Dividends declared after year-end are not liabilities (Ind AS 10), so CFF dividends in a year may relate to the prior year's profit.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): repay term loan 300; revolver from 0 to 150; ESOP proceeds 18; dividends paid to owners 260 and to NCI 25; interest paid 310.7; lease payments 95.

\[ \text{CFF} = 0 - 300 + 150 + 18 - 0 - (260 + 25) - 310.7 - 95 = -822.7 \]

Each term reconciles to a BS movement: borrowings −300 + 150; equity +18 (plus non-cash SBP transfer); retained earnings −260 (the FY26 final dividend, declared at the AGM held in FY27 and paid in FY27); NCI −25; interest accrued flat; lease liability −57.2 net of interest accrual.

Connections

Implements s04 and feeds c05. The revolver term links to the Week 14 cash waterfall and to t3/c05 (negative cash prevented by revolver draw). The lease term connects to the related item on Ind AS 116 lease payment split and to s06 (financing-liability reconciliation). Week 16 FCFE and FCFF differ precisely by the CFF debt and interest terms.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute CFF [L1]

NCD issue ₹1,000 crore; term loan repayment ₹450 crore; revolver from ₹220 crore to ₹60 crore; buyback ₹600 crore; dividends paid ₹350 crore; dividends to NCI ₹20 crore; interest paid ₹280 crore; lease principal ₹90 crore and lease interest ₹45 crore. Compute CFF.

Q2. Debt-to-equity conversion [L2]

Compulsorily convertible debentures of ₹500 crore convert into equity shares on 30 September 2027. Accrued interest of ₹15 crore on them is paid in cash at conversion. State the CFF lines (if any), the non-cash disclosure and the BS movements.

💡 Hint: Separate the cash element from the non-cash conversion.

Q3. Dividend declared vs paid [L2]

The equity schedule reduces retained earnings by a final dividend of ₹320 crore declared at the August 2027 AGM and paid in September 2027, and by an interim dividend of ₹150 crore declared in February 2028 and paid in April 2028. Determine dividends paid in FY28E CFF, any dividend payable at 31 March 2028, and the imbalance that would arise if CFF were linked to dividends declared.
