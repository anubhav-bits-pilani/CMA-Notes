Key concept

PAT-Start CFO Identity

Concept: CFO = PAT + D&A + Other non-cash items − ΔNWC

Statement

\[ \text{CFO}_t = \text{PAT}_t + \text{D\&A}_t + \text{NC}_t - \Delta\text{NWC}_t \]

where \( \text{PAT}_t \) is consolidated profit for the year (before NCI attribution), \( \text{D\&A}_t \) is depreciation of PPE and ROU assets plus amortisation of intangibles, \( \text{NC}_t \) is the net of all other non-cash items in PAT (deferred tax charge, share-based payment expense, impairment, unrealised FX and FV losses, less non-cash income such as share of associates, FV gains and gains on disposal), and \( \Delta\text{NWC}_t = \text{NWC}_t - \text{NWC}_{t-1} \) on operating working capital (receivables + inventories + other operating current assets − payables − other operating current liabilities). If the entity classifies interest paid in financing and interest received in investing, \( \text{NC}_t \) also includes + finance costs − interest/dividend income.

Explanation

PAT is an accrual number. To turn it into cash, the indirect method (Ind AS 7.20) reverses everything that was recognised without a matching cash flow in the year, and adds the cash effect of balances that changed without passing through the P&L. The logic is double-entry: each P&L item that is non-cash has a BS counterpart (accumulated depreciation, DTL, SBP reserve, investment in associate), and each BS working-capital movement has either a P&L or cash counterpart. So the formula is really "PAT ± (all non-cash P&L items) ± (all operating BS movements)".

Conditions and exceptions:

  • Use group PAT, not PAT to owners; NCI cash leakage is only via dividends to NCI in CFF.
  • The NWC definition must exclude cash, debt, current maturities, lease liabilities, tax balances, dividends payable and accrued interest on borrowings, each of which has its own CFS treatment.
  • Items already netted in WC balances (ECL on net receivables, inventory write-downs on net inventory) must not be added back again.
  • Gains/losses on disposals and investments are removed from CFO because their cash is in CFI.

This identity is the international-format engine; in India the presentation starts from PBT (c02), but both must give the same CFO for the same classification policy.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): PAT 940.1; D&A 350; deferred tax credit (10.1); SBP 22; share of JV (60); ΔNWC +58 (receivables +85, inventories +40, payables +55, other CL +12).

\[ \text{CFO} = 940.1 + 350 + (-10.1 + 22 - 60) - 58 = 1{,}184.0 \]

With interest classified in CFF/CFI: add finance costs 240.5, deduct interest and investment income 241.5 → CFO 1,183.0.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the identity [L1]

PAT ₹460 crore; D&A ₹180 crore; deferred tax charge ₹9 crore; impairment of goodwill ₹25 crore; FV gain on liquid funds ₹14 crore; NWC rises from ₹610 crore to ₹690 crore. Interest is classified in CFO. Compute CFO.

Q2. Spot double-counting [L2]

A model computes ΔNWC on net receivables (after ECL allowance) and also adds back the ECL charge of ₹18 crore as a non-cash item. Explain the effect on CFO, cash, and the BS check, and state the corrected treatment.

💡 Hint: Ask which BS line the ECL charge has already reduced.

Q3. NWC definition boundary [L2]

A junior includes current maturities of long-term debt (rising from ₹200 crore to ₹350 crore) and dividends payable (rising from ₹0 to ₹40 crore) in "other current liabilities" in the NWC schedule. Explain the effect on CFO and CFF and whether the BS still balances.
