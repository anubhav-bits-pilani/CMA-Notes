Key concept

CFS Sign Rule and BS-to-CFS Mapping

Concept: Sign rule: ↑asset = outflow; ↑liability or equity = inflow; every CFS line maps to a BS movement or P&L non-cash item

Statement

For any non-cash balance sheet line \( B \) with movement \( \Delta B_t = B_t - B_{t-1} \):

\[ \text{CFS effect}_t = \begin{cases} -\Delta B_t & \text{if } B \text{ is an asset} \\ +\Delta B_t & \text{if } B \text{ is a liability or equity} \end{cases} \]

and the CFS is complete if and only if

\[ \sum \text{CFS lines}_t = \sum_{\text{liab, eq}} \Delta B_t - \sum_{\text{non-cash assets}} \Delta B_t = \Delta \text{Cash}_t \]

where each CFS line is either (i) a BS movement (whole or partial), or (ii) a P&L item reversed because it is non-cash, whose BS counterpart absorbs the other side.

Explanation

The rule is the balance sheet equation rearranged: \( \text{Cash} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity} - \text{Non-cash assets} \). An increase in an asset (receivables, inventory, PPE through capex, investments) uses cash; an increase in a liability (payables, borrowings, lease liability net of non-cash additions) or equity (PAT, share issue) provides cash. P&L items that are non-cash appear in the CFS only because they changed a BS line without cash — D&A reduces net block, deferred tax increases DTL, SBP increases a reserve, share of associates increases an investment. Each must therefore appear once, with the sign that offsets its effect in PAT.

The practical tool is a BS–CFS mapping table (t3/c03): list every BS line, its movement, the CFS line(s) it maps to, and a difference column that must be zero. Lines held flat have zero movement and no CFS line. Split movements: PPE changes by capex (CFI) − depreciation (CFO add-back) − disposals (CFI proceeds, CFO gain); retained earnings by PAT (CFO start) − dividends (CFF); lease liability by new leases (non-cash) + interest (non-cash) − payments (CFF).

Common sign traps:

  • Writing \( \Delta \text{Receivables} \) as =F20-E20 and adding it in CFO (should be subtracted).
  • Treating an increase in DTA (an asset) as an add-back.
  • Subtracting the increase in provisions (a liability) instead of adding it.

FY27E movements (₹ crore), taken from the running Project 4 example: receivables +85; inventory +40; payables +55; DTA +10.1 (deferred tax credit through P&L); borrowings −150 (term-loan repayment −300 and revolver draw +150).

Each line's CFS effect equals minus the asset change or plus the liability change.

Connections

Underlies every subtopic of this topic and the t3 mapping table (t3/c03). In t4 debugging, a difference of X indicates an omitted movement and 2X a sign error — both violations of this rule. Phase 1 double-entry fundamentals: every BS movement has a cash or non-cash counterpart.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the sign rule [L1]

State the CFS effect and section for each FY28E movement (₹ crore): inventory −30; advances from customers +45; capital advances +60; DTL +12 (through P&L); current tax liability −8; dividends payable 0; securities premium +200 from a QIP; investment in associate +40 (share of profit 55, dividend 15).

Q2. Mapping table construction [L2]

Design the columns of a BS–CFS mapping table for Project 4 and populate it for PPE, CWIP, lease liability, retained earnings and NCI with hypothetical movements of your choice. Show how the difference column proves completeness.

💡 Hint: Split lines whose movement has several causes into sub-rows.

Q3. Sign error detection [L2]

A model's CFO line for payables is =-(WC!F30-WC!E30). Payables rise by ₹64 crore in FY28E. Predict the BS check difference, its sign (assets vs liabilities + equity), and explain why it does not appear in a year when payables are flat.
