Key concept

CFI Identity for Non-Financial Entities

Concept: CFI = −Capex + Disposal proceeds ± Investment flows + Interest/dividends received (non-financial entities)

Statement

\[ \text{CFI}_t = -\text{Capex}_t + \text{DP}_t \pm \text{IF}_t + \text{IR}_t + \text{DR}_t \]

where \( \text{Capex}_t \) is cash spent on PPE, CWIP, intangibles and capital advances (spend into CWIP plus direct additions, adjusted for capex creditors), \( \text{DP}_t \) is proceeds from disposal of PPE and intangibles, \( \text{IF}_t \) is net investment flows (purchases negative, sales/redemptions positive; placements/maturities of other bank balances; acquisitions net of cash acquired; loans given/repaid), \( \text{IR}_t \) is interest received and \( \text{DR}_t \) dividends received (including from associates and JVs). The last two are in CFI only for entities that classify them as investing under Ind AS 7.33 — standard for Indian non-financial companies.

Explanation

Investing activities are acquisitions and disposals of long-term assets and other investments not included in cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.6), restricted to expenditures that result in a recognised asset (Ind AS 7.16). Every line maps to a BS asset: capex to PPE + CWIP + intangibles; disposals to PPE derecognition; investments to the investments schedule; dividends from associates to the equity-method investment; interest received to the treasury income in the P&L (reversed in CFO in the Indian format).

Key conditions:

  • Capex is spend, not capitalisation. The CWIP corkscrew separates the two; CFI uses spend.
  • Non-cash additions are excluded (Ind AS 7.43): ROU assets from new leases, assets acquired by issuing shares, capitalised interest (if presented in CFF).
  • Financial entities (banks, NBFCs) classify interest and dividends in CFO.
  • Net vs gross investment flows: actuals are reported gross; forecasts typically model net change.

Illustration

FY28E (₹ crore): spend into CWIP 650; direct additions 90; capex creditors up 30; disposal proceeds 25 (NBV 18, gain 7); net purchase of liquid funds 200; placement of 6-month FDs 100; interest received 85; dividend from associate 22.

\[ \text{CFI} = -(650 + 90 - 30) + 25 - 200 - 100 + 85 + 22 = -878 \]

The gain of 7 is removed in CFO; the 25 is in CFI; the PPE schedule derecognises NBV 18.

Connections

Implements s03. Capex links to the Week 14 PPE/CWIP corkscrews; interest and dividends received to t1/s02 and t1/s05. The sign rule (c06) explains why asset increases are negative. Week 16 FCFF deducts capex but excludes treasury flows and interest received, which is why CFI is not the same as "investment in operations".

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute CFI [L1]

Capex spend into CWIP ₹1,200 crore; direct additions ₹50 crore; capital advances rise ₹80 crore; no disposals; redemption of bonds ₹300 crore; interest received ₹140 crore; dividend from JV ₹35 crore. Compute CFI.

Q2. Non-cash additions [L2]

During FY27E a company acquires ROU assets of ₹160 crore under new leases and a ₹90 crore office building in exchange for its own equity shares. An analyst includes both in capex. Explain the effect on CFI, CFF, closing cash and the BS check, and how Ind AS 7.43 requires them to be disclosed.

💡 Hint: Ask whether any cash moved and which BS lines changed.

Q3. Classification for an NBFC subsidiary [L3]

A manufacturing group acquires an NBFC subsidiary. Discuss how interest received and paid are classified in the NBFC's standalone CFS versus the group's consolidated CFS, and the implications for your consolidated CFI and CFO forecasts.
