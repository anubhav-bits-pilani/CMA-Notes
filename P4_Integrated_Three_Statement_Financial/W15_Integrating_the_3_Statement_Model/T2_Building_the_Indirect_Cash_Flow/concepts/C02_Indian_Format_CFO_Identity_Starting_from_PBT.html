Key concept

Indian-Format CFO Identity Starting from PBT

Concept: Indian-format CFO = PBT + D&A + Finance costs − Interest income ± Non-cash ± WC changes − Taxes paid

Statement

\[ \text{CFO}_t = \text{PBT}_t + \text{D\&A}_t + \text{FC}_t - \text{II}_t \pm \text{NC}_t \pm \Delta\text{WC}_t - \text{TP}_t \]

where \( \text{PBT}_t \) is statutory profit before tax, \( \text{FC}_t \) is finance costs expensed (debt and lease interest, net of capitalisation), \( \text{II}_t \) is interest income plus dividend income and investment gains whose cash is classified in CFI, \( \text{NC}_t \) is other non-cash items (SBP, impairment, provisions, unrealised FX, share of associates if inside PBT, gain/loss on PPE disposal), \( \Delta\text{WC}_t \) is the signed change in each operating working-capital line (increase in assets negative, increase in liabilities positive), and \( \text{TP}_t \) is income taxes paid net of refunds:

\[ \text{TP}_t = \text{Current tax}_t - \Delta\text{Current tax liability}_t + \Delta\text{Advance tax asset}_t \]

Explanation

This is the layout every Indian listed company uses (Ind AS 7.18–20 indirect method, with Indian practice starting from PBT). Finance costs are added back and interest income removed because, for non-financial entities, interest paid is presented in CFF and interest/dividends received in CFI (Ind AS 7.33). Tax is handled entirely by the cash line at the bottom (Ind AS 7.35), so deferred tax never appears. The subtotal after the adjustments — "operating profit before working capital changes" — is close to EBITDA plus non-cash operating charges, which is why Indian analysts read CFO/EBITDA directly off the statement.

The identity reconciles to the PAT-start version (c01):

\[ \text{PBT} - \text{TP} = \text{PAT} + T_c + T_d - \text{TP} = \text{PAT} + T_d + \Delta\text{CTL} - \Delta\text{ATA} \]

so the deferred tax add-back and the tax-payable movement in c01 are exactly what the "taxes paid" line absorbs here. If a model starts from PBT but also adds back deferred tax, or starts from PAT but also deducts taxes paid, tax is double-counted.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): PBT 1,176; D&A 350; FC 240.5; interest and investment income 241.5; SBP 22; ΔWC −58; current tax 306.0, CTL flat.

\[ \text{CFO} = 1{,}176 + 350 + 240.5 - 241.5 + 22 - 58 - 306.0 = 1{,}183.0 \]

The same figure as the PAT-start CFO after interest reclassification. (The JV share is excluded because it is presented after tax; had it been inside PBT, a −60 adjustment would be needed.)

Connections

Implements s02 and must agree with c01. Taxes paid come from the Week 14 tax schedule; finance costs and interest income from t1/s02 and t1/s04; ΔWC from the WC schedule. The topic pitfall on mixing PAT-start and PBT-start formats is the failure of this reconciliation. Phase 1 covered Ind AS 7 classification; Phase 2 uses the operating-profit subtotal for cash conversion analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute Indian-format CFO [L1]

PBT ₹1,520 crore; D&A ₹410 crore; finance costs ₹230 crore; interest income ₹70 crore; dividend income ₹15 crore; loss on sale of PPE ₹6 crore; SBP ₹18 crore; receivables up ₹90 crore; inventories up ₹60 crore; payables up ₹75 crore; current tax ₹375 crore; current tax liability rises ₹12 crore. Compute operating profit before WC changes, cash generated from operations and net CFO.

Q2. Reconcile two formats [L2]

Using the data in Q1 with a deferred tax charge of ₹21 crore, compute PAT and build the PAT-start CFO (with interest reclassified). Show line by line why the two formats agree.

💡 Hint: Map deferred tax and ΔCTL in the PAT-start build to the taxes-paid line.

Q3. Associate share inside PBT [L2]

A company presents share of JV profit of ₹45 crore above tax, so reported PBT is ₹980 crore. A model's CFS starts from reported PBT and has no adjustment for the JV share; dividends of ₹20 crore from the JV are shown in CFI. Quantify the cash and BS-check effect and give the corrected adjustment line.
