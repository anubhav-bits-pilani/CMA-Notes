Subtopic note

Net Change in Cash, Opening/Closing Cash and Other Bank Balances

Scope: Net change in cash, opening and closing cash; other bank balances vs cash equivalents

Why it matters

The bottom of the cash flow statement is the hinge of the entire three-statement model. Net change in cash plus opening cash gives closing cash, and that closing cash — and nothing else — is linked to the balance sheet. If the CFS is complete and every BS movement has a cash counterpart, the BS balances; if not, the check row shows the gap. Getting the definition of cash right is the subtle part in India: Schedule III splits "Cash and cash equivalents" from "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents", and Ind AS 7 reconciles only the former. Indian companies routinely hold large fixed deposits with 3–12 month maturities, earmarked balances for unpaid dividends and margin money for guarantees — none of which are cash equivalents. A model that lumps them together will reconcile to nothing in the annual report and may misstate net debt.

Core notes

The identity

\[ \text{Net change in cash}_t = \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}_t \]

\[ \text{Closing cash}_t = \text{Opening cash}_t + \text{Net change in cash}_t (+ \text{FX effect}_t) \]

\[ \text{Opening cash}_t = \text{Closing cash}_{t-1} \]

Opening cash in the first forecast year links to the last historical BS cash and cash equivalents (not to the historical CFS closing line, in case of presentation differences such as overdrafts). Each later year links to the prior forecast year's CFS closing cash. Ind AS 7.28 requires the effect of exchange rate changes on cash held in foreign currency to be shown separately; forecasts assume zero.

Cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.6–7.9)

  • Cash: cash on hand and demand deposits.
  • Cash equivalents: short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible to known amounts of cash and subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value — normally with an original maturity of three months or less from acquisition (Ind AS 7.7).
  • Bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form an integral part of cash management may be included as a component (Ind AS 7.8); Indian companies usually present cash credit within borrowings.

Liquid mutual funds are generally not cash equivalents under Indian practice (classified as current investments at FVTPL), although some companies treat overnight funds as such; follow the company's disclosed policy.

Other bank balances (Schedule III)

Schedule III Division II, under current financial assets, requires "Bank balances other than (iii) above" to be shown separately, covering:

  • fixed deposits with original maturity > 3 months and ≤ 12 months;
  • earmarked balances (unpaid dividend accounts under Sec 124, CSR unspent accounts under Sec 135(6));
  • margin money and deposits under lien for guarantees and LCs;
  • balances with banks held as security against borrowings.

Deposits with remaining maturity > 12 months are non-current "other financial assets". Movements in these balances are CFI flows (placement/maturity of deposits), and they are excluded from the Ind AS 7 cash reconciliation.

Modelling choices

Approach BS CFS Comment
Separate lines (preferred) Cash & CE linked to CFS closing; Other bank balances from a treasury schedule ΔOther bank balances in CFI Reconciles to annual report; net debt includes both
Hold other bank balances flat Flat line, zero CFS movement No CFI line Acceptable if immaterial; consistent
Combine into one "cash" line Single treasury line Historical CFS will not reconcile Avoid for listed-company models

For net debt (and the interest income calculation), include cash & CE, other bank balances and liquid current investments — but disclose the composition.

Excel pattern

CFS!F45  =CFS!F23+CFS!F33+CFS!F44          ' Net change = CFO + CFI + CFF
CFS!F46  =BS!E12                           ' Opening cash (FY27E only: last actual BS)
CFS!G46  =CFS!F47                          ' Opening cash later years = prior closing
CFS!F47  =CFS!F46+CFS!F45                  ' Closing cash
BS!F12   =CFS!F47                          ' BS cash & cash equivalents
Checks!F5 =ROUND(CFS!F47-BS!F12,3)         ' Cash check (should be 0)

A cash check (CFS closing − BS cash = 0) is trivially zero if BS cash links to CFS, but it becomes meaningful if anyone overwrites BS cash; it is also the first diagnostic in t4.

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

From s02–s04: CFO 1,183.0; CFI (748.5); CFF (822.7). Opening cash and CE 1,200.

\[ \text{Net change} = 1{,}183.0 - 748.5 - 822.7 = -388.2 \]

\[ \text{Closing cash} = 1{,}200 - 388.2 = 811.8 \]

If the company also places ₹100 crore in a 6-month FD at year-end, CFI includes (100), closing cash and CE falls to 711.8, and other bank balances rise by 100 — total treasury unchanged. The interest income calculation in t1/s02 should use whichever treasury base the yield was calibrated on.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Minimum cash in the waterfall (Week 14) should be compared with cash & CE, not total treasury, unless the policy says otherwise.
  • Restricted cash in subsidiaries (e.g., escrow) — disclose under Ind AS 7.48; keep it out of the revolver logic.
  • Negative closing cash without a revolver means a funding gap; the model must flag it (t3/c05).
  • First forecast year link: linking opening cash to the historical CFS closing rather than BS cash can carry an overdraft reclassification into the BS and break year 1.
  • Overdrafts netted in cash for some companies: if historicals net them, net them consistently in forecasts or restate.
  • Circularity: closing cash feeds average-cash interest income; the breaker must allow closing cash to be computed without the loop.

Key takeaways

  • Net change = CFO + CFI + CFF; closing = opening + net change; opening = prior closing (year 1 = last actual BS cash).
  • Only cash and cash equivalents (original maturity ≤ 3 months) are reconciled in the CFS; other bank balances move through CFI.
  • Keep cash & CE and other bank balances as separate BS lines so historicals reconcile to the annual report.
  • BS cash links to CFS closing cash; the cash check catches overwrites.
  • Net debt and interest income can use a wider treasury definition — state it.

Practice Questions

Q1. Roll forward cash [L1]

Opening cash and CE ₹540 crore. FY28E CFO ₹1,320 crore, CFI (₹980 crore), CFF (₹410 crore). Compute net change and closing cash, and state which cell the BS cash line should reference.

Q2. Classifying treasury balances [L2]

At 31 March 2027 a company holds: current account balances ₹120 crore; FDs with original maturity of 2 months ₹200 crore; FDs with original maturity 9 months ₹450 crore; FDs maturing in 18 months ₹300 crore; unpaid dividend account ₹6 crore; margin money for bank guarantees ₹40 crore; overnight liquid fund units ₹150 crore (company policy: current investments). Classify each per Schedule III and Ind AS 7, and compute cash & CE and gross treasury for net debt.

💡 Hint: Original maturity decides cash equivalent status; remaining maturity decides current vs non-current for deposits.

Q3. FD placement and the check row [L2]

A modeller adds a ₹500 crore placement of 6-month FDs in FY28E to the other bank balances line on the BS but forgets the CFI line. Predict the check-row difference, its sign, and how the cash check would behave if BS cash is linked to CFS closing cash.

Q4. First-year opening cash link [L3]

FY26A BS shows cash and CE ₹410 crore; the FY26A reported CFS shows closing cash and CE ₹380 crore because a ₹30 crore bank overdraft is netted in the CFS. Your forecast BS presents the overdraft in short-term borrowings. Explain which opening cash figure the FY27E CFS should use, and what happens to the FY27E check row if you use the other figure.
