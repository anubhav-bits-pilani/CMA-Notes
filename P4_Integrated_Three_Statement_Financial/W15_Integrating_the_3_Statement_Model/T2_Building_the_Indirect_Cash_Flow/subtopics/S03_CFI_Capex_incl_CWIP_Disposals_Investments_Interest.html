Subtopic note

CFI: Capex incl. CWIP, Disposals, Investments, Interest Received

Scope: CFI: capex incl. CWIP, disposal proceeds, investment purchases/sales, interest/dividends received

Why it matters

Investing cash flows decide how much of operating cash is reinvested and how much is left for lenders and shareholders. For capital-intensive Indian sectors — cement, steel, power, telecom, chemicals, airports — capex can exceed CFO for years, and the way capex is linked (cash spend, including CWIP, versus capitalisations into PPE) determines whether the BS balances and whether FCF is right. Treasury-rich companies (IT, FMCG, pharma) have the opposite issue: billions of rupees rotating between bank deposits, liquid funds and bonds, which drown the "real" investing flows. A clean CFI section ties every line to a Week 14 schedule: the PPE and CWIP corkscrews, the investments schedule and the interest/dividend income computed in t1.

Core notes

What belongs in CFI

Ind AS 7.16 limits investing activities to expenditures that result in a recognised asset — so exploration costs expensed, or R&D research costs, are operating. Typical lines in Indian reports:

Line Sign Schedule source
Purchase of PPE and intangibles (including CWIP and capital advances) − PPE, CWIP, Intangibles schedules: cash capex
Proceeds from sale of PPE + PPE disposal block
Purchase of investments (MFs, bonds, bank deposits > 3 months) − Investments schedule
Sale/redemption of investments + Investments schedule
Investment in subsidiaries/associates/JVs; acquisition of business (net of cash acquired) − Acquisition inputs
Loans given to / repaid by related parties ∓ Loans schedule
Interest received + Treasury income (t1/s02)
Dividends received (including from associates/JVs) + Associates schedule, investments
Movement in other bank balances (FDs 3–12 months, earmarked balances) ∓ Cash schedule (s05)

Capex including CWIP

The cash outflow is capex spend, not additions to PPE:

\[ \text{Cash capex}_t = \Delta\text{Gross block}_t + \Delta\text{CWIP}_t + \text{Disposals at cost}_t - \text{Capitalised interest}_t \pm \Delta\text{Capex creditors}_t \pm \Delta\text{Capital advances}_t \]

In model terms, the CWIP corkscrew receives capex spend and transfers capitalisations to the gross block:

CWIP corkscrew Gross block corkscrew
Opening CWIP Opening gross block
+ Capex spend (cash) + Transfers from CWIP (capitalisation)
+ Capitalised borrowing cost + Direct additions (not via CWIP)
− Transfers to gross block − Disposals at cost
= Closing CWIP = Closing gross block

CFI picks up capex spend + direct additions (and capitalised interest if that is the chosen presentation, see t1/s04). Linking CFI to "transfers to gross block" instead is a classic error: in a year with a large plant under construction, CWIP rises but cash capex is understated, producing an imbalance equal to ΔCWIP.

Capex creditors (payables for capital goods) and capital advances are excluded from operating NWC (Week 14) and adjust cash capex if modelled; most forecasts assume they are flat.

Disposals

Proceeds from sale of PPE = net book value of assets sold + gain (or − loss). The gain/loss sits in the P&L (other income/other expenses) and is removed from CFO (s01/s02); the full proceeds appear in CFI; the PPE schedule removes cost and accumulated depreciation. Forecasts usually assume zero disposals, but if modelled, all three statements must move.

Investment purchases and sales

A treasury company's investments schedule:

\[ \text{Inv}_t = \text{Inv}_{t-1} + \text{Purchases}_t - \text{Sales at carrying value}_t + \text{FV gains}_t \]

Purchases and sale proceeds are CFI; FV gains are non-cash (removed in CFO in the PBT format via "net gain on investments"). If investments are held flat, purchases = sales, FV gains = 0, and CFI investment lines are zero — consistent. If the yield on investments is earned as FV gain on growth MFs (no cash), the investment balance must grow by that gain. Indian reports often show gross purchases and sales of liquid funds in tens of thousands of crores — forecasting gross is pointless; forecast the net change.

Interest and dividends received

For non-financial entities in India, interest and dividends received are investing flows (Ind AS 7.33). In the model:

\[ \text{Interest received}_t = \text{Interest income}_t - \Delta\text{Interest accrued receivable}_t \]

Most forecasts set accrued interest flat, so interest received = P&L interest income. Dividends from associates/JVs come from the associate corkscrew (t1/s05) and reduce the investment carrying amount.

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

CWIP: opening 600, capex spend 900, capitalised interest 54 (presented within interest paid in CFF), transfers 1,000, closing 554. Direct additions (vehicles, IT) 120. No disposals. Liquid MF investments held flat. Interest received 97.5; MF income 144 (redeemed and reinvested — realised, cash); dividend from JV 30.

CFI line Amount
Purchase of PPE/intangibles incl. CWIP (900 + 120) (1,020.0)
Interest received 97.5
Income on investments received 144.0
Dividends received from JV 30.0
Net CFI (748.5)

Check: ΔCWIP + Δgross block (before depreciation) = (554 − 600) + (1,000 + 120) = 1,074 = cash capex 1,020 + capitalised interest 54. ✓

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capital advances (paid to suppliers for machinery) are non-current assets; their increase is capex cash. Exclude from operating NWC.
  • ROU additions are non-cash (Ind AS 7.43 non-cash transactions): never put new leases in capex. The cash flows are lease payments in CFF.
  • Acquisitions: consideration paid net of cash acquired is one CFI line; acquired assets and liabilities hit the BS without passing through CFO/WC lines.
  • Government grants for capex: if deducted from asset cost, the receipt may be shown in CFI; if presented as deferred income, it is often CFI or CFF — follow the company's policy.
  • Bank deposits > 3 months: not cash equivalents; their placement is a CFI outflow (s05).
  • Capex creditors rising at year-end (common at commissioning) means cash capex < accounting capex.

Key takeaways

  • CFI lines are capex spend (incl. CWIP), disposal proceeds, investment purchases/sales (net in forecasts), acquisitions, loans, and interest/dividends received.
  • Link capex cash to the CWIP corkscrew's spend line, not to capitalisations into gross block.
  • Disposals: proceeds in CFI, gain/loss reversed in CFO, cost and accumulated depreciation removed in the PPE schedule.
  • Hold-flat investments imply zero CFI investment flows; FV gains require the balance to grow.
  • New leases are non-cash; lease cash is CFF.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cash capex from corkscrews [L1]

FY28E: opening CWIP ₹320 crore, closing ₹780 crore; transfers to gross block ₹450 crore; direct additions ₹60 crore; capitalised borrowing cost ₹25 crore (presented in interest paid); capex creditors rise by ₹40 crore. Compute cash capex for CFI and explain each adjustment.

💡 Hint: Start from spend into CWIP, then adjust for non-cash and timing items.

Q2. Disposal across three statements [L2]

A plant with cost ₹200 crore and accumulated depreciation ₹150 crore is sold for ₹80 crore on 31 March 2028. Tax rate 25.168%; ignore tax depreciation effects. Show the P&L, CFO adjustment, CFI line and the PPE schedule entries, and confirm the BS balances.

Q3. Treasury company investments [L2]

An IT company's investments schedule shows opening ₹25,000 crore, net purchases ₹3,000 crore and FV gains (unrealised) ₹900 crore; interest on bonds ₹1,100 crore received in cash. Build the CFI lines and state the CFO adjustment for the FV gain in the Indian format.

Q4. Imbalance equal to ΔCWIP [L3]

A model's BS is out by ₹460 crore in FY28E only; it balances in all other years. CWIP rises from ₹320 crore to ₹780 crore that year and the CFS capex line is linked to "Transfers to gross block + Direct additions". Explain the error, why it appears only in one year, and the corrected link. Then explain what the error would look like in a year when CWIP falls.
