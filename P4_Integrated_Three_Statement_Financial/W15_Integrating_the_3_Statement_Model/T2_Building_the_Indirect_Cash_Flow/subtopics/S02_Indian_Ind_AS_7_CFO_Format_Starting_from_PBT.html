Subtopic note

Indian Ind AS 7 CFO Format Starting from PBT

Scope: Indian Ind AS 7 format: starts at PBT, adds finance cost, removes interest income, deducts income taxes paid

Why it matters

Open any Indian listed company's annual report and the cash flow statement starts not at PAT but at profit before tax. This is the format the auditor signs, the format your historical columns must be copied from, and the format analysts at Indian brokerages and rating agencies read. It differs from the international PAT-start layout in three ways — it begins before tax, it strips out finance costs and interest income (because those cash flows are classified in CFF and CFI), and it deducts income taxes paid as a single cash line at the bottom of CFO. If the forecast CFS is built in one format and the historicals are in the other, CFO trends become meaningless and interest is double-counted or omitted. Being fluent in both, and in reconciling them, is a basic expectation in Indian FP&A and equity research roles.

Core notes

Legal basis

Ind AS 7.18 permits the direct or indirect method; Indian companies almost universally use the indirect method (Ind AS 7.20). Ind AS 7 does not prescribe the starting line; Indian practice (carried over from AS 3 and reinforced by Schedule III's requirement to present the CFS per Ind AS 7) starts from profit before tax. Under Ind AS 7.33, interest paid and interest and dividends received may be classified as operating, investing or financing for non-financial entities; Ind AS 7.34 permits dividends paid in financing or operating. Indian non-financial companies overwhelmingly classify interest paid in CFF and interest/dividends received in CFI. Ind AS 7.35 requires taxes on income to be classified as operating unless specifically identifiable with investing or financing activities, and disclosed separately.

The layout

Line Source Note
Profit before tax IS Statutory PBT (include associate share only if presented above tax, then reverse it below)
Adjustments: Depreciation and amortisation IS / asset schedules Non-cash
Finance costs IS Added back because interest paid is shown in CFF
Interest income IS (other income note) Removed because interest received is in CFI
Dividend income IS Removed; dividends received in CFI
Net gain on investments (FVTPL, realised) IS Removed; proceeds/value in CFI
(Profit)/loss on sale of PPE IS Removed; proceeds in CFI
Share-based payment expense Cost schedule Non-cash
ECL / bad debts, provisions, unrealised forex IS / schedules Non-cash (if not already in net WC movements)
Share of profit of associates/JVs (if in PBT) IS Non-cash income: removed
Operating profit before working capital changes Subtotal ≈ EBITDA + non-cash − other operating income adjustments
Movements in trade receivables, inventories, other assets, trade payables, other liabilities, provisions WC schedule Line by line
Cash generated from operations Subtotal
Income taxes paid (net of refunds) Tax schedule Current tax − Δ current tax liability (+ Δ advance tax asset)
Net cash from operating activities Total

Note that deferred tax does not appear: the format starts before tax, so neither current nor deferred tax expense is in the starting line, and only cash tax paid is deducted.

Income taxes paid

From the Week 14 tax schedule:

\[ \text{Taxes paid}_t = \text{Current tax}_t - (\text{CTL}_t - \text{CTL}_{t-1}) + (\text{Advance tax asset}_t - \text{Advance tax asset}_{t-1}) \]

where CTL is the current tax liability (provision for tax net of advance tax). Under the advance-tax regime (Sec 207–211: 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December, 100% by 15 March), most of a year's tax is paid within the year; a small balance or excess sits on the BS at year-end, refunded or paid after assessment.

Why "operating profit before WC changes" ≈ EBITDA

\[ \text{PBT} + \text{D\&A} + \text{FC} - \text{Interest income} - \text{Other non-operating income} = \text{EBITDA} \]

plus non-cash items inside EBITDA (SBP, ECL, provisions). This makes the Indian format analytically convenient: the subtotal is directly comparable to EBITDA, and CFO/EBITDA conversion (Phase 2) is read straight off the statement.

Compare with the PAT-start build: 940.1 PAT + 350 − 10.1 + 22 − 60 − 58 = 1,184.0; then + 240.5 finance costs − 241.5 treasury income = 1,183.0. The two formats agree once interest is classified the same way — which is the point of s02 and the topic pitfall.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Associate share inside PBT: if the company presents it above tax, the starting PBT includes it and an adjustment line must remove it.
  • Exceptional items: in history, cash exceptional items (VRS paid) are often shown as a separate CFO line; non-cash ones (impairment) as adjustments.
  • Interest on lease liabilities is part of finance costs, so it is added back here and paid in CFF along with principal.
  • Capitalised interest is not in finance costs, so is not added back; its cash is in capex or interest paid (t1/s04).
  • Tax refunds with interest (Sec 244A): the interest portion is other income; the refund reduces taxes paid.
  • Financial entities (NBFCs, banks) classify interest in CFO — do not apply this layout to them.

Key takeaways

  • Indian CFS starts at PBT, adds D&A and finance costs, removes interest and dividend income and other non-operating gains, then applies WC changes and deducts income taxes paid.
  • The subtotal "operating profit before WC changes" approximates EBITDA plus non-cash items.
  • No deferred tax line appears; only cash taxes paid from the tax schedule.
  • Interest paid sits in CFF and interest/dividends received in CFI, consistent with Ind AS 7.33 choices for non-financial entities.
  • PAT-start and PBT-start CFO reconcile exactly once interest classification is aligned.

Q1. Convert a PAT-start CFO to Indian format [L2]

PAT ₹620 crore; current tax ₹190 crore; deferred tax charge ₹12 crore; D&A ₹240 crore; finance costs ₹95 crore; interest income ₹40 crore; SBP ₹10 crore; ΔNWC outflow ₹70 crore; current tax liability rises by ₹8 crore. Build the Indian-format CFO from PBT and confirm it equals the PAT-start CFO adjusted for interest reclassification.

💡 Hint: Rebuild PBT first; taxes paid = current tax − increase in CTL.

Q2. Taxes paid from the tax schedule [L1]

FY27E current tax ₹420 crore. Advance tax paid during the year ₹398 crore. Opening current tax liability (net) ₹15 crore, which is settled in FY27E. Compute income taxes paid and closing CTL.

Q3. Reading a reported CFS [L2]

A company's FY26A CFS shows: PBT 1,050; D&A 300; finance costs 140; interest income (55); profit on sale of investments (25); operating profit before WC changes 1,410; WC changes (180); cash generated 1,230; taxes paid (260); CFO 970. EBITDA per the P&L is 1,385. Compute CFO/EBITDA and cash generated/EBITDA, and explain the difference between EBITDA and the operating-profit subtotal.

Q4. Ind AS 7 classification choice [L3]

A newly listed holding company argues for classifying dividends received from subsidiaries and interest received in CFO because "investing is our business". Explain the Ind AS 7.33 position for non-financial entities, the impact on CFO, CFI and FCF in your model, and how you would document the choice to keep historical and forecast columns consistent.
