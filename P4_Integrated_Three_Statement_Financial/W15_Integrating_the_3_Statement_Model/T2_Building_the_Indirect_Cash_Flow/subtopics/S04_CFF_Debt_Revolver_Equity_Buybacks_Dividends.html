Subtopic note

CFF: Debt, Revolver, Equity, Buybacks, Dividends, Interest, Leases

Scope: CFF: debt drawdowns/repayments, revolver, equity issue, buyback, dividends, interest paid, lease principal and interest

Why it matters

Financing cash flows are where the model's capital structure decisions become cash: scheduled term-loan repayments, NCD maturities, revolver draws in a bad year, QIP or rights issues, buybacks, dividends to shareholders and to NCI, interest, and — since Ind AS 116 — every rupee of lease payments. In an integrated model, CFF is also where the revolver plugs the funding gap, which makes it the last step before closing cash. Because almost every CFF line comes from a corkscrew (debt, lease, equity, NCI), the section is mechanically simple but extremely sensitive to sign and timing errors: a dividend declared but unpaid, a buyback counted twice, lease interest left in CFO. These are among the most common causes of a non-zero check row.

Core notes

Definition

Ind AS 7.17: financing activities result in changes in the size and composition of contributed equity and borrowings. Typical lines and their sources:

CFF line Sign Schedule source
Proceeds from long-term borrowings (term loans, NCDs, ECBs) + Debt schedule: drawdowns
Repayment of long-term borrowings − Debt schedule: mandatory amortisation, bullets, sweep
Net proceeds/(repayment) of short-term borrowings (CP, WCDL, cash credit) ± Debt schedule
Revolver draw/(repayment) ± Revolver block of debt schedule
Proceeds from issue of equity (QIP, rights, ESOP exercise), net of issue costs + Equity schedule
Buyback of equity shares (including buyback tax where borne by company — pre-October 2024 regime) − Equity schedule
Dividends paid to owners − Equity schedule: dividends paid (not declared)
Dividends paid to NCI − NCI corkscrew
Interest paid on borrowings (including capitalised, if that presentation chosen) − Debt schedule
Payment of lease liabilities: principal − Lease schedule
Payment of lease liabilities: interest − Lease schedule
Upfront fees/transaction costs on borrowings − Debt schedule

Debt and revolver

From the Week 14 debt schedule, drawdowns and repayments are shown gross for term debt (Ind AS 7.21 requires gross presentation of major classes, with net presentation permitted for items with quick turnover, large amounts and short maturities under Ind AS 7.22 — cash credit and CP rollovers are typically net). The revolver line is the cash sweep's balancing item:

\[ \text{Revolver draw}_t = \max\left(0, \text{Min cash} - \text{Cash before revolver}_t\right) \text{ capped at limit} \]

and repayment uses surplus cash, first in the waterfall. The revolver line in CFF equals \( \text{Rev}_t - \text{Rev}_{t-1} \) on the BS.

Equity issue and buyback

ESOP exercise brings cash = exercise price × shares; the share capital and securities premium increase by that cash plus a transfer from the SBP reserve (non-cash). A QIP or rights issue brings cash net of issue expenses (issue expenses are debited to securities premium under Sec 52, so equity rises by the net amount). Buybacks reduce cash by the price paid. Under the regime effective 1 October 2024, buyback proceeds are taxed as deemed dividend in shareholders' hands, so the company no longer pays Sec 115QA buyback tax (check the latest Finance Act). CRR creation on buyback (Sec 69) is a non-cash reclass within equity.

Dividends

The CFF line is dividends paid, which in Indian practice is often the final dividend of the previous year, approved at the AGM (typically July–September) and paid within 30 days of declaration (Sec 127), plus interim dividends of the current year. Under Ind AS 10, a final dividend proposed after year-end is not a liability at the BS date. Model choices:

  • Simple: dividends paid in year t = payout × PAT of year t (assumes all in-year; no dividend payable).
  • Realistic: dividends paid in year t = interim dividend of t + final dividend of t − 1. Retained earnings fall when declared; cash falls when paid; any declared-but-unpaid amount is a liability (unclaimed dividend, dividend payable) — usually small.

Dividends to NCI (paid by subsidiaries) are a separate CFF outflow and reduce the NCI corkscrew.

Interest paid

\[ \text{Interest paid}_t = \text{Finance cost on borrowings}_t + \text{Capitalised interest}_t - \Delta\text{Interest accrued}_t \]

Lease interest is shown separately (or with lease principal). Non-cash interest (deep-discount accretion, amortisation of upfront fees under EIR) is removed. Ind AS 7.32 requires total interest paid to be disclosed whether expensed or capitalised.

Lease payments under Ind AS 116

Ind AS 7.17(e) and Ind AS 116.50: cash payments for the principal portion of the lease liability are financing; the interest portion follows the entity's policy for interest paid (financing for Indian non-financial entities); short-term, low-value and variable payments not in the liability are operating. So in Indian models the full lease payment — principal + interest — is in CFF. The lease schedule gives:

\[ \text{Lease payment}_t = \text{Principal}_t + \text{Interest}_t, \qquad L_t = L_{t-1} + \text{New leases}_t + \text{Interest}_t - \text{Payment}_t \]

New leases are non-cash (Ind AS 7.43) — they increase the ROU asset and lease liability without any CFS line.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Dividends declared vs paid: linking CFF to declared dividends when the equity schedule pays them next year creates an imbalance equal to the change in dividends payable.
  • Unpaid dividend account (Sec 124) balances are restricted "other bank balances", not cash equivalents.
  • Bank overdraft/cash credit repayable on demand may be included in cash and cash equivalents if it forms an integral part of cash management (Ind AS 7.8) — Indian companies usually present cash credit as borrowings instead; be consistent with the BS.
  • Upfront fees paid on drawdown reduce the carrying amount under EIR; cash outflow at drawdown, amortisation non-cash.
  • Interest capitalised: presenting it in capex (CFI) or interest paid (CFF) are both seen; do not do both.
  • Buyback of shares at a premium reduces securities premium/free reserves; the cash line is the full price.

Key takeaways

  • CFF lines come from the debt, revolver, lease, equity and NCI corkscrews; each equals a BS movement or a P&L cash cost.
  • Term debt drawdowns and repayments are shown gross; revolver and short-term facilities may be net.
  • Dividends in CFF are dividends paid (often prior-year final + current interim); NCI dividends are separate.
  • Indian non-financial entities show interest paid and full lease payments (principal and interest) in CFF.
  • New leases, CRR creation and SBP-to-premium transfers are non-cash and have no CFF line.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build CFF from corkscrews [L1]

FY28E (₹ crore): term loan drawdown 500; repayment 250; CP net repayment 100; revolver closing 0 vs opening 150; QIP proceeds 1,200 less issue costs 18; dividends paid 300; dividends to NCI 30; interest paid 220; lease principal 70, lease interest 40. Compute net CFF and list the BS line each item reconciles to.

Q2. Dividend timing [L2]

Payout policy is 30% of PAT, paid as a final dividend after the AGM in the following year. PAT: FY27E ₹900 crore, FY28E ₹1,050 crore; FY26A final dividend of ₹240 crore is paid in FY27E. Compute dividends paid in FY27E and FY28E, the retained earnings movement in each year, and explain why no dividend payable appears on the 31 March BS under Ind AS 10.

💡 Hint: Separate the declaration date (equity) from the payment date (cash).

Q3. Lease flows across statements [L2]

Opening lease liability ₹500 crore; new leases ₹120 crore on 1 April 2027; incremental borrowing rate 9% on opening balance plus new leases; annual payments ₹140 crore at year-end. Build the lease liability corkscrew for FY28E, and state each CFS line affected (including any non-cash disclosure).

Q4. Revolver as the funding plug [L3]

Cash before revolver in FY29E (Bear case) is −₹180 crore; minimum cash is ₹100 crore; revolver limit ₹250 crore; opening revolver ₹0. Compute the revolver draw, the closing cash, any shortfall, and explain how the CFF line and the BS revolver balance are linked so that the model still balances when the limit binds.
