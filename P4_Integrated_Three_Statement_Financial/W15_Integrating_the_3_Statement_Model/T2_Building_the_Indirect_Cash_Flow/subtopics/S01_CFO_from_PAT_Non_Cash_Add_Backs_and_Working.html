Subtopic note

CFO from PAT: Non-Cash Add-Backs and Working Capital

Scope: CFO from PAT: add D&A, ΔDTL, share-based payment, impairment; less ΔNWC

Why it matters

Cash flow from operations is where the income statement meets the balance sheet. In a three-statement model it is not forecast independently — it is derived: start from profit, reverse every item that did not move cash, and adjust for the cash tied up in or released from working capital. This "PAT-start" layout is the international modelling convention (US GAAP style, most training material, most interview questions), and it is the cleanest way to prove that each BS movement has a cash counterpart. Even when the final presentation follows the Indian PBT-start format (s02), many modellers build the PAT-start version first as the engine, because every line maps directly to a schedule row. Missing one non-cash item produces a BS imbalance of exactly that amount — so the add-back list is also a debugging checklist.

Core notes

The structure

\[ \text{CFO} = \text{PAT} + \text{D\&A} + \Delta\text{DTL} + \text{SBP} + \text{Impairment} \pm \text{Other non-cash} - \Delta\text{NWC} \]

Line Source Sign logic
PAT (group, before NCI) IS Starting point: all profit, including NCI's share, is group cash
D&A (PPE + intangibles + ROU) PPE, Intangibles, Leases schedules Expense with no cash: add back
Deferred tax charge / ΔDTL (net of ΔDTA through P&L) Tax schedule Charge reduces PAT, no cash: add back; credit: deduct
Share-based payment expense Cost schedule / Equity schedule (SBP reserve) Expense settled in equity: add back
Impairment, ECL charge (if allowance modelled separately), write-downs Exceptional / asset schedules Non-cash expense: add back
Share of associates/JVs IS Non-cash income: deduct (dividends go to CFI)
Unrealised FVTPL gains, unwinding of discount on provisions, non-cash interest Treasury / provision / debt schedules Reverse non-cash element
Gain/loss on disposal of PPE PPE schedule Remove gain (proceeds go to CFI)
(Increase)/decrease in operating NWC WC schedule Increase in asset = outflow; increase in liability = inflow

Why PAT and not PAT to owners

The CFS is for the consolidated entity. Cash generated by a 51% subsidiary is 100% in group cash; the NCI's claim is recognised in equity (NCI line) and its cash leaves only when dividends are paid to NCI (CFF). So CFO starts from total PAT (profit for the year), not PAT attributable to owners.

Deferred tax

Tax expense = current tax + deferred tax (t1/s06). Only current tax is (approximately) cash. Adding back the deferred tax charge — equivalently the increase in net DTL recognised through P&L — converts the tax line to current tax. Two subtleties:

  • Deferred tax recognised in OCI (on DB remeasurements or FVOCI) does not pass through PAT, so it is not added back in CFO; it changes the DTL and OCI reserve together.
  • If current tax payable changes (advance tax shortfall, refunds due), that is a working capital-like item from the tax schedule: \( \Delta \)current tax liability is an inflow. Many models show it as a separate line "Δ income tax payable/(receivable)" rather than inside NWC (Week 14 excluded tax balances from operating NWC).

Share-based payment

Under Ind AS 102 equity-settled ESOPs, the fair value of options is expensed over the vesting period with the credit to "share options outstanding account" / SBP reserve in other equity. There is no cash, so the expense is added back. On exercise, cash received (exercise price × shares) is a CFF inflow and the reserve is transferred to securities premium — itself a non-cash reclass within equity. Cash-settled SARs are different: the liability is remeasured and its increase is a non-cash item, but settlement is cash.

Working capital

\[ \Delta\text{NWC}_t = \text{NWC}_t - \text{NWC}_{t-1}, \quad \text{NWC} = \text{Receivables} + \text{Inventory} + \text{Other op. CA} - \text{Payables} - \text{Other op. CL} \]

An increase in NWC is a deduction from CFO. Present line by line in the model (Δ receivables, Δ inventories, Δ other current assets, Δ trade payables, Δ other current liabilities and provisions) so each row maps to a BS line. Exclude cash, borrowings, current maturities, lease liabilities, tax balances, dividends payable and interest accrued on borrowings — these belong to other sections or have their own CFS lines.

Excel pattern

CFS!F8   =IS!F31                       ' PAT (profit for the year)
CFS!F9   =IS!F18                       ' D&A
CFS!F10  =IS!F29                       ' Deferred tax charge
CFS!F11  =Costs!F27                    ' Share-based payment expense
CFS!F12  =-IS!F27                      ' Less share of associates
CFS!F13  =-(WC!F20-WC!E20)             ' (Increase)/decrease in receivables
CFS!F14  =-(WC!F21-WC!E21)             ' (Increase)/decrease in inventories
CFS!F15  =WC!F30-WC!E30                ' Increase/(decrease) in payables
CFS!F16  =Tax!F62-Tax!E62              ' Increase/(decrease) in current tax liability
CFS!F18  =SUM(CFS!F8:F16)              ' CFO

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

PAT 940.1; D&A 350; deferred tax credit (10.1); SBP 22; share of JV (60); receivables up 85; inventories up 40; payables up 55; other CL up 12; current tax payable unchanged.

\[ \text{CFO} = 940.1 + 350 - 10.1 + 22 - 60 - 85 - 40 + 55 + 12 = 1{,}184.0 \]

CFO/EBITDA = 1,184 ÷ 1,510 = 78% — a plausible cash conversion for a growing consumer business, but note this CFO is after interest effects embedded in PAT; if interest paid is presented in CFF, add back finance costs and remove interest income (see s02 and the pitfall on mixing formats).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Interest in a PAT-start CFO. PAT is after finance costs and includes interest income. If the Ind AS 7 classification puts interest paid in CFF and interest received in CFI (Indian non-financial practice), the PAT-start CFO must add back finance costs and deduct interest income, otherwise interest is double-counted. Decide the format once (pitfall in topic).
  • Lease interest is inside finance costs; under Ind AS 116 both principal and interest are CFF, so add it back with finance costs.
  • Impairment of receivables (ECL) when receivables are modelled net: the ECL charge is already absorbed in Δ net receivables — do not add it back separately, or it is counted twice.
  • Inventory write-downs similarly sit inside Δ inventories if inventory is modelled net.
  • Provisions for gratuity/leave (Ind AS 19): the service cost is in employee cost; the increase in the provision (non-current) is a non-cash item or NWC line — map it once.
  • Gain on sale of investments must be removed from CFO; proceeds go to CFI.

Key takeaways

  • PAT-start CFO = total PAT + D&A + deferred tax charge + SBP + impairment ± other non-cash − ΔNWC.
  • Start from group PAT (before NCI); NCI cash leakage is only via dividends in CFF.
  • Every add-back maps to a schedule row; every NWC line maps to a BS line — the list is also a debugging checklist.
  • Deferred tax through OCI, and ECL/inventory write-downs already netted in balances, must not be added back again.
  • If interest is classified in CFF/CFI, a PAT-start CFO must reverse finance costs and interest income.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a PAT-start CFO [L1]

FY28E (₹ crore): PAT 1,120; D&A 410; deferred tax charge 24; ESOP expense 30; gain on sale of PPE 8 (proceeds 20); receivables increase 70; inventories decrease 15; payables increase 40; current tax liability decreases 6. Compute CFO, listing each line with its sign, and state where the PPE sale proceeds appear.

Q2. Where does the imbalance come from? [L2]

A model balances in FY27E but in FY28E total assets exceed liabilities + equity by exactly ₹36 crore. The SBP reserve on the BS increases by ₹36 crore in FY28E (new grant). Explain the likely missing link, which statements show the error, and how a ±₹36 crore pattern would look if the line were entered with the wrong sign instead.

💡 Hint: An omission creates a difference equal to the item; a sign flip doubles it.

Q3. Deferred tax through OCI [L2]

An actuarial loss of ₹40 crore on the gratuity plan is recognised in OCI with deferred tax of ₹10.1 crore. P&L deferred tax charge is ₹15 crore. Describe the BS movements in DTL/DTA, other equity and the provision, and state the amount to add back in CFO for deferred tax.

Q4. Interest in a PAT-start CFO [L3]

PAT ₹800 crore includes finance costs of ₹180 crore (₹30 crore lease interest) and interest income of ₹60 crore. The company classifies interest paid in CFF and interest received in CFI. Show the corrected CFO bridge items, and explain the error that results if the CFF section also deducts interest paid of ₹180 crore while the CFO bridge does not add back finance costs.
