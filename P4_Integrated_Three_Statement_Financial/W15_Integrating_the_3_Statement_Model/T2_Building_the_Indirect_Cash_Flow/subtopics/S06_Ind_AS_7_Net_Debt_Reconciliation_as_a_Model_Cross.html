Subtopic note

Ind AS 7 Net-Debt Reconciliation as a Model Cross-Check

Scope: Ind AS 7 net-debt reconciliation disclosure as a cross-check

Why it matters

Since the 2017 amendment (notified in India through the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2017, effective FY2017-18), Ind AS 7 requires a reconciliation of changes in liabilities arising from financing activities, separating cash movements from non-cash movements. For analysts it is the single best source for understanding how debt and lease liabilities actually moved: how much was repaid in cash, how much new leasing added, how much FX or fair-value changes inflated foreign-currency borrowings. For modellers it is also a ready-made control: build the same table in the forecast, and every financing liability on the BS must reconcile to its CFF cash line plus explicit non-cash items. A difference points straight to a CFF or BS link error before it ever shows up as a mysterious check-row gap.

Core notes

The requirement

Ind AS 7.44A requires disclosures that enable users to evaluate changes in liabilities arising from financing activities, including both cash and non-cash changes. Ind AS 7.44B lists the changes to disclose, to the extent necessary:

  1. changes from financing cash flows;
  2. changes arising from obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries or other businesses;
  3. the effect of changes in foreign exchange rates;
  4. changes in fair values;
  5. other changes.

Liabilities arising from financing activities are those whose cash flows were, or will be, classified as financing (Ind AS 7.44C) — borrowings, lease liabilities, and in Indian practice often interest accrued (because interest paid is CFF) and sometimes dividends payable. Ind AS 7.44D suggests a reconciliation between opening and closing balances; 7.44E requires financial assets that hedge such liabilities to be included if their cash flows are financing.

Ind AS 7 does not require a "net debt" table including cash — that is a UK/analyst convention — but the financing liabilities reconciliation is mandatory and analysts extend it with cash to get net debt.

The row "Closing (BS)" links to the balance sheet; the difference row must be zero. Note how lease interest is both a non-cash accrual (increasing the liability) and a cash payment (CFF) — the net effect on the liability is principal only.

Extending to net debt

\[ \text{Net debt}_t = \text{Borrowings}_t + \text{Lease liabilities}_t - \text{Cash \& CE}_t - \text{Other bank balances}_t - \text{Liquid investments}_t \]

\[ \Delta\text{Net debt}_t = -(\text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t^{\text{ex treasury}} + \text{CFF}_t^{\text{equity, dividends}}) + \text{Non-cash debt changes}_t \]

The second identity is the analyst's free-cash-flow-to-net-debt bridge: net debt falls by FCF after interest, dividends and equity flows, and rises by non-cash additions (new leases, acquired debt, FX). Building it in the model gives a powerful check: if the change in BS net debt does not equal the bridge, a cash flow has no BS counterpart (or vice versa). Choose whether lease liabilities are in net debt and keep that consistent with the EBITDA definition (t1/c05).

Why it is an effective cross-check

The BS check (Assets − Liabilities − Equity) aggregates every error into one number. The financing-liability reconciliation isolates one family of lines:

  • If the lease liability does not reconcile, a lease addition was put in capex, or lease interest is in opex, or principal and interest were split inconsistently.
  • If borrowings do not reconcile, a drawdown or repayment on the debt schedule is not on the CFS, or the current-maturities reclassification has been treated as a cash flow (t3/c04).
  • If interest accrued does not reconcile, interest paid in CFF does not match finance cost less Δaccrual, or capitalised interest has been omitted from interest paid.

Excel implementation

NetDebt!F10  =BS!E40                       ' Opening borrowings (prior column)
NetDebt!F11  =CFS!F36+CFS!F37              ' Drawdowns + repayments (signed)
NetDebt!F12  =Debt!F92                     ' Non-cash: EIR amortisation
NetDebt!F13  =SUM(NetDebt!F10:F12)         ' Computed closing
NetDebt!F14  =BS!F40                       ' BS closing
NetDebt!F15  =ROUND(NetDebt!F13-NetDebt!F14,3)   ' Must be 0

Feed each difference row to the checks dashboard alongside the BS check and cash check.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Current maturities of long-term debt are a reclassification within borrowings; include current and non-current together in the reconciliation so the reclass nets to zero.
  • Acquisitions: debt assumed is a non-cash "obtaining control" change, not a CFF inflow.
  • Foreign-currency loans (ECBs): forex restatement is non-cash; forecasts usually hold FX constant.
  • Lease modifications and terminations change the liability without cash; show in "other changes".
  • Dividends payable may be included if declared-but-unpaid amounts are material.
  • Revolver draws and repayments are cash flows; the revolver limit is irrelevant to the reconciliation but relevant to the dashboard flags.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 7.44A–44E require reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities, split into cash and non-cash changes.
  • Rebuild the table in the model: opening (prior BS) + CFF cash + non-cash items = computed closing; compare with BS closing.
  • Lease liabilities reconcile via new leases (non-cash), interest accrual (non-cash) and payments (cash).
  • Extending to net debt gives an FCF-to-net-debt bridge that catches cash flows with no BS counterpart.
  • Feed every reconciliation difference to the checks dashboard.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a lease-liability reconciliation [L1]

Opening lease liability ₹650 crore; new leases ₹140 crore; lease terminations reducing the liability by ₹20 crore; interest accrued ₹58 crore; lease payments ₹170 crore. Prepare the reconciliation, separating cash and non-cash movements, and compute the closing balance.

Q2. Borrowings reconciliation with FX and acquisition [L2]

Opening borrowings ₹4,200 crore (including ₹800 crore ECB). FY28E: drawdowns ₹600 crore, repayments ₹900 crore, debt assumed on acquisition of a subsidiary ₹350 crore, ECB forex loss of ₹40 crore, EIR amortisation of upfront fees ₹6 crore. Prepare the Ind AS 7.44B reconciliation and compute the CFF borrowing lines.

💡 Hint: Only drawdowns and repayments are financing cash flows.

Q3. Diagnosing with the reconciliation [L2]

Your model's lease-liability reconciliation shows a difference of ₹75 crore in FY27E, and the BS check shows assets exceeding liabilities + equity by ₹75 crore. New leases of ₹75 crore were signed that year. List two possible link errors that would produce this pattern and how you would confirm which one occurred.

Q4. Net debt bridge [L3]

FY27E (₹ crore): CFO 1,183; capex (1,020); interest and investment income received 241.5; dividends from JV 30; dividends paid to owners (260) and NCI (25); ESOP proceeds 18; interest paid (310.7); lease payments (95); new leases 75. Opening net debt including leases ₹2,232 crore (gross debt 3,432 including interest accrued, less cash 1,200). Build the bridge to closing net debt and explain how you would confirm it against the BS.
