Roadmap topic: Finalizing Project 4: Complete 5-year integrated 3-statement financial model with executive summary dashboard

Overview

Project 4 is the first full deliverable of Phase 4: a 5-year integrated three-statement model of an Indian listed company, balancing in every year and scenario, with an executive summary that a CFO, investment committee or interviewer can read in two minutes. Topics 1–5 built and tested the engine. This topic turns it into a product. The difference between a working model and a deliverable model is the presentation layer (dashboard and charts), the evidence layer (assumption log, checks summary, model map) and the hygiene layer (formats, print setup, grouping, review). Employers in FP&A, equity research, credit and transaction advisory judge candidates' models on all three. A model that balances but has pasted numbers on the dashboard, an unexplained ROCE doubling, or a failing Bear check will be rejected in a case study round.

The financial content of the dashboard is the standard analyst set: revenue, EBITDA and margins, PAT and EPS, free cash flow, net debt, ROCE and ROE. You computed many of these as KPI rows in Topic 1 and in Phase 2 ratio analysis. The dashboard's job is to link them, never to recompute them (c04), and to show them against history and against sanity bands (c05) so that implausible forecasts stand out. Charts (s02) show the trajectory: growth with margin, cash generation, and the deleveraging path, which lenders and equity investors care about most. A scenario panel (s03) shows what drives Base vs Bull vs Bear, with a live label so no one misreads which case is on screen.

The evidence and hygiene layers make the model reviewable. The Cover (s04) carries the master check status and a map of every tab. The assumption log (s05) ties every key input to a source (annual report page, concall transcript, investor presentation slide) and a rationale, which is the modelling equivalent of audit working-paper referencing. The self-review and peer-review checklist (s06) applies Topic 4 diagnostics and Topic 5 stress tests one final time, then fixes print setup, number formats, grouping and freeze panes so the model is usable on screen and on paper. Recommended sequence: finish the dashboard (s01) and charts (s02), then the scenario panel (s03), then the Cover (s04) and assumption log (s05), and finally the review (s06). Ratios (c01–c03) and sanity bands (c05) are applied while building s01.

Related concepts to study

Ratio analysis and DuPont

→ Phase 2 (W5–8): ratio analysis and DuPont

Phase 2 taught you to compute and interpret margins, returns, turnover and leverage ratios on historical Indian financial statements, including the Schedule III ratio disclosures and the DuPont decomposition of ROE. In Project 4 the same ratios are computed on forecast statements and shown next to history. The interpretive skill transfers directly. A forecast ROE rising from 14% to 22% needs a DuPont explanation: is it margin, asset turnover or leverage? If the answer is "asset turnover doubles because capex stops", that is a modelling issue, not a business insight. Use the Phase 2 peer benchmarking work as the source of sanity bands (c05), and keep ratio definitions identical across historical and forecast columns, or trend comparisons are meaningless.

Dashboard design principles from Power BI

→ Phase 3 (W9–12): dashboard design principles from Power BI

Phase 3's Power BI work established principles that apply equally to an Excel dashboard. The most important message goes top-left. KPI cards come before detail. A consistent colour carries meaning (actuals vs forecast, pass vs fail). Each chart answers one question. Clutter is removed (no 3D, minimal gridlines, direct labels instead of legends where possible). Excel adds two constraints of its own: the dashboard must fit a printed A4 or A3 landscape page, and every number must be a live link. The Power BI idea of a single semantic model feeding all visuals corresponds to the Excel rule that the dashboard links to one output sheet and never recalculates (c04). If you later rebuild the Project 4 outputs in Power BI for an FP&A role, the Excel dashboard becomes your wireframe.

DCF and comps bolt-on

→ Week 16: DCF and comps bolt onto this model

Week 16 builds FCFF, WACC, terminal value and trading comparables on top of the Project 4 model, so the quality of this week's deliverable determines next week's valuation. FCFF uses EBIT, tax on EBIT, D&A, capex, ΔNWC and lease adjustments from the same schedules. Net debt from the forecast BS bridges enterprise value to equity value. Forecast EPS and EBITDA feed P/E and EV/EBITDA comps. A dashboard that already shows FCF, net debt and ROCE makes the valuation story coherent: ROCE above WACC justifies growth creating value. Build the dashboard with space for valuation outputs (implied share price, upside, EV/EBITDA) so Week 16 extends it rather than replacing it.

Capstone presentation and interview walkthrough

→ Phase 6 (W21–24): capstone presentation and interview model walkthrough

In Phase 6 you will present a capstone model and walk interviewers through it live. The Project 4 dashboard, Cover and assumption log are rehearsal material for that. Interviewers commonly ask you to open the model, explain the structure from the model map, change one input and trace its effect through all three statements, defend two or three assumptions with sources, and explain what happens in the Bear case. A well-organised Cover and assumption log turns these questions into a guided tour. Practise a three-minute walkthrough now (business, key drivers, outputs, checks, risks) and refine it after each later project.

Common pitfalls

Dashboard with hardcoded or pasted numbers

Dashboard with hardcoded or pasted numbers

The mistake: the dashboard shows values pasted from an earlier run, or typed headline numbers ("Revenue CAGR 14%") that no longer match the model. Why it happens: charts and formatted tables are fiddly, so builders paste values to "freeze" a clean layout, or type figures from a draft deck. Correct treatment: every dashboard cell is a direct link or a simple reference (=Outputs!H12) to an output sheet, and every chart series references live ranges (c04). Text commentary that cites numbers uses formulas (="Revenue CAGR "&TEXT(CAGR,"0.0%")). Detection in review: Go To Special → Constants on the dashboard; toggle the scenario and check that every number and chart moves; Inquire's "hardcoded values" report.

Forecast ratios drifting implausibly

Forecast ratios drifting implausibly (e.g., ROCE doubling) unchecked

The mistake: the forecast shows ROCE going from 18% to 38%, EBITDA margin expanding 900 bps, or capex/sales falling below depreciation/sales for five years, and nobody questions it. Why it happens: each driver looks reasonable on its own (modest margin gains, capex flat in rupees), but together they compound. Builders also focus on the BS check rather than economic plausibility. Correct treatment: show sanity bands (historical range and peer range) next to each key ratio and flag forecasts outside them (c05). Explain or fix each flag: a capex holiday that drives ROCE up must be reconciled with growth assumptions. Detection in review: read the ratio rows across the years, look at DuPont components, and compare capex/D&A and asset turnover with history.

Delivering with checks failing in Bull/Bear

Delivering with checks failing in Bull/Bear

The mistake: the model is submitted with the Base case balancing but the Bull or Bear check non-zero, or with a convergence warning, often because the last edit was tested only in Base. Why it happens: time pressure at the end, and a dashboard that shows only the live scenario's status. Correct treatment: re-run the scenario check table (Topic 5 s01) after the final edit, show it on the Cover, and refuse to deliver until all 15 checks (5 years × 3 scenarios) and extreme-input tests pass. Save the evidence (a dated snapshot) in the model. Detection in review: the reviewer toggles each scenario and inspects the Cover status, and the scenario check table must be live, not pasted.

Practice Questions

Q1. Project 4 final review case [L3]

You are reviewing a colleague's Project 4 model of Sahyadri Paints Ltd (NSE-listed). FY25A: revenue ₹8,200 crore, EBITDA margin 17.5%, PAT ₹890 crore, ROCE 24%, net cash ₹650 crore. The FY30E forecast shows revenue ₹15,900 crore, EBITDA margin 23.0%, PAT ₹2,450 crore, ROCE 46%, and capex/sales falling from 6.5% to 2.0%. Peers' EBITDA margins range from 15% to 21% and ROCE from 20% to 32%. The dashboard has three cells with constants, Bull checks are 0, and Bear checks are 0, 0, 0, 3.2, 6.4. (a) List every issue you would raise, classifying each as integrity, plausibility or presentation. (b) For the ROCE trajectory, explain the likely DuPont drivers and the questions you would ask. (c) Propose a fix order with timeboxes for a one-day turnaround.

💡 Hint: Separate what makes the model wrong from what makes it implausible.

Q2. Build the executive summary [L2]

Using the following FY26E–FY30E outputs for Kaveri Consumer Ltd (₹ crore), design the dashboard table and identify the formulas required. Revenue: 5,500, 6,160, 6,900, 7,660, 8,430. EBITDA: 880, 1,010, 1,150, 1,290, 1,430. D&A: 190, 210, 230, 250, 270. Finance costs: 60, 55, 50, 45, 40. Other income: 30, 32, 35, 38, 40. ETR 25.17%. Shares outstanding 40 crore. Capex: 300, 320, 340, 360, 380. ΔNWC: 60, 65, 70, 72, 75. Lease principal payments 25 each year. Closing net debt: 420, 330, 210, 60, −110. Average equity: 3,100, 3,500, 3,950, 4,450, 5,000. (a) Specify the KPI rows and their formulas (EBITDA margin, PAT, EPS, FCF, FCF conversion, net debt/EBITDA, ROE, ROCE on a stated definition). (b) State which rows must be links and which may be formulas on an outputs sheet. (c) Describe the three charts you would add and what each should show.

Q3. Assumption log and walkthrough [L2]

For a 5-year model of an Indian two-wheeler company, list eight key assumptions you would put in the assumption log (revenue drivers, margins, capex, working capital, financing, tax, dividends). For each, give the typical source document (annual report section, concall, investor presentation, industry body data) and how you would defend the value in an interview.

Q4. Delivery readiness [L2]

Write a delivery-readiness checklist of at least 12 items for Project 4, covering integrity (checks, scenarios, extreme inputs), plausibility (ratio bands), presentation (dashboard, charts, labels) and hygiene (formats, print, grouping, freeze panes, file naming, version log). For each item, state how a peer reviewer would verify it in under two minutes.
