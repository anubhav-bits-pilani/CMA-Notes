Subtopic note

Key Assumptions by Scenario and the Live Scenario Label

Scope: Key assumptions by scenario and live scenario label

Why it matters

A dashboard number means nothing without the assumptions behind it, and in a multi-scenario model it means less than nothing if the reader does not know which scenario is on screen. Two errors happen in real boardrooms and investment committees: a Bear-case page printed and circulated as Base because the switch was left on 3, and a Bull case presented without anyone realising it assumed 900 bps of margin expansion. The key-assumptions panel shows, side by side, the five to eight drivers that distinguish Base, Bull and Bear, and highlights the live one. The live label puts the scenario name on every output, every chart title and every printed page. Together they make the model's story explicit: "Bear assumes volume decline of 5% and gross margin down 250 bps, which takes ROCE from 24% to 13%."

Core notes

Choosing the key assumptions

Show only the drivers that (a) differ between scenarios and (b) materially move the outputs. For a typical Indian listed company:

Driver category Example (consumer company) Example (capital goods)
Volume / demand Volume growth % Order inflow growth %, book-to-bill
Price / mix Price-mix growth % Execution rate (% of opening order book)
Gross margin Gross margin % (RM inflation) Gross margin % (commodity pass-through)
Operating cost Ad spend % of sales, employee cost growth Employee cost growth, other expenses %
Capex Capex ₹ crore or % of sales Capex ₹ crore (capacity expansion timing)
Working capital Inventory days, receivable days Receivable days, contract assets % of revenue
Financing Interest rate, dividend payout Interest rate, mobilisation advances
Macro INR/USD for import-heavy RM Commodity index (steel, copper)

Show 5–8 rows. More than that turns the panel into a copy of the inputs sheet.

Building the panel

The panel links to the inputs sheet's three scenario columns and the live column:

Assumption (FY26E–FY30E avg) Base Bull Bear Live
Volume growth 8.0% 11.0% 2.0% =INDEX(…)
Price-mix growth 4.0% 5.0% 2.5%
Gross margin 44.0% 46.0% 41.5%
Ad spend % sales 8.0% 7.5% 9.0%
Capex (₹ crore p.a.) 320 380 220
Receivable days 25 22 32
Dividend payout 40% 45% 25%

Implementation points:

  • If inputs vary by year, show the 5-year average or the FY30E value and say which. Better still, show a small "FY26E / FY30E" pair.
  • Highlight the live column with conditional formatting: apply to the Base:Bear block with a formula such as =COLUMN()-COLUMN($B$5)+1=Scen_Switch and a bold border or fill.
  • Add a row of key outputs under each scenario (FY30E revenue, EBITDA margin, ROCE, net debt/EBITDA). This needs either a data table on the switch (Topic 5 s01) or a stored snapshot. If you use a snapshot, label it "Snapshot as at [date], refresh after any change" and add a check comparing the live scenario's snapshot to live outputs.

The live scenario label

One named cell, Scen_Label, derived from the switch:

Scen_Label = INDEX(Scen_Names, Scen_Switch)
Header     = Company_Name & " – " & Scen_Label & " case – ₹ crore"
Chart_Title= "Revenue and EBITDA margin – " & Scen_Label & " case"

Use it in:

  • The dashboard header and every output sheet header (with a large, coloured font).
  • Every chart title (linked title, s02).
  • The page header or footer for printing. Excel headers cannot link to cells directly, so place the label in a cell inside the print area or within print titles, or use a short VBA BeforePrint routine to write it into the header.
  • The file name convention when saving outputs (for example, Project4_XYZ_v12_Bear.pdf).

Add a stress-mode indicator: if the Topic 5 stress layer is on, the label should read, for example, "Bear + STRESS (growth −30%)" in red, so a stressed output is never mistaken for a scenario output.

Scen_Label_Full = Scen_Label & IF(Stress_On = 1, " + STRESS", "")

Telling the scenario story

Under the panel, a short linked commentary turns numbers into narrative:

="Bear assumes volume growth of "&TEXT(Vol_Bear,"0.0%")&" vs "&TEXT(Vol_Base,"0.0%")&" in Base and gross margin "&TEXT((GM_Bear-GM_Base)*10000,"0")&" bps lower; FY30E ROCE "&TEXT(ROCE_Bear,"0.0%")&"."

Keep it to one or two sentences per scenario. Formulas keep the text in sync when inputs change.

Illustration

Kaveri Consumer Ltd, live scenario Bear. Header: "Kaveri Consumer Ltd – Bear case – ₹ crore". Panel: the Bear column highlighted. The output row shows FY30E revenue ₹6,600 crore (Base ₹8,430 crore), EBITDA margin 13.1% (Base 17.0%), ROCE 15.2% (Base 27.4%) and net debt/EBITDA 1.6x (Base net cash). Commentary: "Bear assumes volume growth of 2.0% vs 8.0% in Base and gross margin 250 bps lower; FY30E ROCE 15.2%." A board member reading a printed page knows immediately which case it is and why it differs.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Scenario names vs numbers. Users toggle by name through a dropdown with data validation on a list of names. The model converts to a number with MATCH. Never let users type numbers outside 1–3.
  • More than three cases (Management, Lender, Sponsor). The panel scales by adding columns. Keep the live column last and clearly separated.
  • Assumptions that do not differ by scenario (tax rate, depreciation lives). Exclude them from the panel and list them once as "common assumptions".
  • Asymmetric scenarios. Bull and Bear need not be symmetric around Base. Indian cyclicals often have a deeper downside than upside. Explain the asymmetry in the assumption log (s05).
  • Snapshot staleness. Any stored output snapshot must be dated and checked. A stale Bull ROCE on the panel undermines the whole dashboard.
  • Printing the wrong case. Add a print check: if Scen_Switch <> 1 when printing a "Base case" pack, warn the user, either through the visible label or through VBA.

Key takeaways

  • Show only the 5–8 drivers that differ by scenario and matter for outputs, side by side, with the live column highlighted.
  • One named Scen_Label drives every header, chart title, printed page and output file name.
  • Add a stress-mode marker to the label so stressed outputs are never confused with scenario outputs.
  • Use formula-driven commentary so the scenario story updates automatically.
  • If scenario outputs are shown side by side, generate them with a data table or a dated, checked snapshot.

Practice Questions

Q1. Select the drivers [L1]

For an Indian cement company, choose six key assumptions for the scenario panel and give plausible Base, Bull and Bear values for each (with units). Justify why each one differs between scenarios and why you excluded the tax rate and depreciation rates.

Q2. Label formulas [L2]

Write formulas for (a) the scenario label from a switch, (b) a full label that appends " + STRESS" when Stress_On = 1 and " (breaker off)" when Circ_Switch = 0, (c) a chart title using the full label, and (d) a conditional formatting rule that highlights the live column in a Base/Bull/Bear block in columns C:E.

💡 Hint: Concatenate with & and use IF for each optional suffix.

Q3. Side-by-side outputs [L2]

The committee wants to see FY30E revenue, EBITDA margin, PAT, ROCE and net debt/EBITDA for all three scenarios on one panel, always up to date. Compare a data table approach with a macro snapshot approach on accuracy, speed, circularity handling and auditability, and recommend one for a model with iterative interest.

Q4. Misprint incident [L3]

A Bear-case dashboard was printed and circulated to the board as the Base case. As the model owner, describe (a) the immediate corrective actions, (b) three model changes that make a repeat impossible or obvious, and (c) how you would explain the incident and the fix to the CFO in two or three sentences.
