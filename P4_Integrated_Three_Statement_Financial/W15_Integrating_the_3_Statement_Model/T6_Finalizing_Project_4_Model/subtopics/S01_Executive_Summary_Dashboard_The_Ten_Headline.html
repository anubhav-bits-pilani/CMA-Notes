Subtopic note

Executive Summary Dashboard: The Ten Headline Metrics

Scope: Executive summary dashboard: revenue, EBITDA, margins, PAT, EPS, FCF, net debt, ROCE, ROE

Why it matters

Most readers of a model never open the schedules. A CFO, investment committee member, credit officer or interviewer looks at one page and decides within minutes whether the forecast is credible and what it implies. The executive summary dashboard is that page. It must answer four questions. How fast does the business grow? How profitable is it? How much cash does it generate? How strong is the balance sheet and the return on capital? The nine metrics in scope, with margins treated as a family, cover exactly those questions. Getting definitions right matters, because Indian reporting has its own conventions (Schedule III expense-by-nature P&L, "other income" below revenue, Ind AS 116 effects on EBITDA and net debt, EPS under Ind AS 33). A dashboard that mixes EBITDA definitions across years or uses year-end equity for ROE will be challenged in any review.

Core notes

Layout

A one-page landscape layout (A4 or A3) usually has five zones:

  1. Header: company, ticker (for example, NSE: XYZ), model version, live scenario label, master check status (red/green), units ("₹ crore unless stated").
  2. KPI cards: FY25A vs FY30E (or FY28E for a 3-year focus) for revenue, EBITDA margin, PAT, EPS, net debt/EBITDA and ROCE, each with a 5-year CAGR or change.
  3. Summary table: FY23A–FY30E columns (actuals shaded differently from forecasts) with the rows below.
  4. Charts: revenue and EBITDA margin combo, FCF trend, net debt/EBITDA trajectory (s02).
  5. Scenario and checks panel: key assumptions by scenario (s03) and check status.

Summary table rows and definitions

Metric Definition (state on sheet) Notes
Revenue from operations Schedule III line, excluding other income Growth % row below
EBITDA Revenue − COGS (materials consumed, purchases, change in inventories) − employee benefits − other expenses State whether post-Ind AS 116 (excludes lease rent) and excluding other income
EBITDA margin EBITDA ÷ revenue Also gross margin if relevant
EBIT EBITDA − D&A
PAT Profit for the year (consolidated) Show PAT to owners separately if NCI is material
PAT margin PAT ÷ revenue
EPS (basic) PAT to owners ÷ weighted average shares (Ind AS 33.10) Diluted EPS if ESOPs/convertibles are material (Ind AS 33.31)
Free cash flow CFO − capex (incl. CWIP) − lease payments State definition: FCFF, FCFE, or "FCF after leases"
Net debt Borrowings (current + non-current + revolver) − cash and cash equivalents − liquid investments; leases shown separately or included State treatment of leases and other bank balances
Net debt/EBITDA Net debt ÷ EBITDA Same lease treatment in numerator and denominator
ROCE EBIT ÷ average capital employed (c01) State the capital employed definition
ROE PAT to owners ÷ average equity attributable to owners (c02)

Consistency rule for Ind AS 116: if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 (lease rent excluded, so EBITDA is higher), net debt should include lease liabilities for leverage ratios, or the ratio flatters the company. If you present pre-Ind AS 116 "rent-adjusted" EBITDA (common in retail and QSR concall disclosures), exclude leases from net debt. Say which on the sheet.

EPS: use the weighted average number of shares, adjusted for bonus issues and splits retrospectively (Ind AS 33.64). In a forecast, buybacks and new issues change the weighted average: use the average of opening and closing shares for mid-year events. Treasury shares held by an ESOP trust are excluded from the shares outstanding.

Implementation

  • All values link to a single Outputs sheet (or directly to the statements), never recalculated on the dashboard (c04).
  • CAGR formula: =(End/Start)^(1/Years)-1, with IFERROR or a sign check, because CAGR is undefined when start and end have opposite signs (for example, net debt turning to net cash).
  • Number formats: ₹ crore with one decimal, percentages with one decimal, multiples as 0.0"x", EPS in ₹ with two decimals.
  • Conditional formatting: forecast columns in a lighter fill, sanity-band breaches (c05) in amber, and the check status cell red or green.

A reader takes away: double-digit growth, gentle margin expansion, net cash by FY30E, and ROCE rising about 6 points. The last item needs a sanity-band check (c05): is ROCE above the historical range and peer range, and is it driven by margin or by capex falling?

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Net cash companies. Net debt/EBITDA is negative. Show "net cash" and the ratio as "n.m." rather than a negative multiple. Gearing-based charts need a floor at zero or a separate net cash bar.
  • Loss years. PAT margin, EPS and ROE turn negative, and CAGRs from or to a negative base are meaningless. Show "n.m." and use absolute change.
  • NCI-heavy groups. Show both consolidated PAT and PAT to owners. EPS and ROE use owners' figures.
  • Exceptional items. Forecasts usually carry none (Topic 1). The historical columns should show "PAT before exceptional items" alongside reported PAT so trends are comparable.
  • Standalone vs consolidated. Use consolidated for listed groups unless the brief says otherwise, and label it.
  • Fiscal labels. Use FY25A, FY26E and so on (FY ending 31 March) consistently. Avoid calendar-year labels, which confuse Indian readers.

Key takeaways

  • The dashboard answers growth, profitability, cash generation and balance-sheet/returns questions on one landscape page.
  • State every definition on the sheet: EBITDA (pre/post Ind AS 116, excluding other income), FCF, net debt (leases, other bank balances), ROCE capital employed.
  • Keep lease treatment consistent between EBITDA and net debt, and use average balances for return ratios.
  • EPS uses PAT to owners over weighted average shares (Ind AS 33), adjusted for buybacks and issues.
  • Show "n.m." where ratios lose meaning (net cash, losses, sign changes), and link every number from outputs.

Practice Questions

Q1. Definitions sheet [L1]

Write a definitions box (maximum 10 lines) for the bottom of a Project 4 dashboard covering EBITDA, FCF, net debt, ROCE, ROE and EPS. It must be precise enough that a reviewer could recompute each figure from the statements.

Q2. Compute the headline row [L2]

For Tunga Foods Ltd FY27E (₹ crore): revenue 3,600, COGS 2,160, employee cost 290, other expenses 610, D&A 120, other income 25, finance costs 48, tax at 25.17%, NCI share of PAT 14, weighted average shares 18 crore, CFO 410, capex 230, lease payments 35, borrowings 520, lease liabilities 140, cash 190, average equity attributable to owners 2,050, average capital employed (equity + net debt incl. leases) 2,600. Compute EBITDA, EBITDA margin, PAT, PAT to owners, EPS, FCF, net debt (with and without leases), net debt/EBITDA (consistent basis), ROCE and ROE.

💡 Hint: Decide the lease treatment once and apply it to both net debt and the leverage ratio.

Q3. CAGR edge cases [L2]

Write an Excel formula for a 5-year CAGR that returns "n.m." when the start or end value is zero or negative, and explain why CAGR cannot be computed when the start is negative and the end positive. Then propose what to show on the dashboard for a company whose net debt goes from ₹300 crore to net cash of ₹150 crore.

Q4. Interview: the two-minute summary [L3]

Using the Kaveri Consumer illustration table in these notes, prepare a two-minute verbal summary of the Base case for an investment committee. Include one point on each of growth, margins, cash, leverage and returns, and one risk the Bear case is designed to test. Then list three questions the committee is likely to ask and the dashboard element that answers each.
