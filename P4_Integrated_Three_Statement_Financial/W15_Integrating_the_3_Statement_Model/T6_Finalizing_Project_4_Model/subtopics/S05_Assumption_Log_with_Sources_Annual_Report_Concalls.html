Subtopic note

Assumption Log with Sources: Annual Report, Concalls, Presentations

Scope: Assumption log with sources (annual report, concalls, investor presentations)

Why it matters

Every forecast is a set of assumptions, and every assumption will be challenged. "Why 12% volume growth?" "Where does 44% gross margin come from?" "Is ₹1,200 crore capex what management guided?" An assumption log answers these questions in writing, before they are asked. For each key input it records the value by scenario, the source, the date and the rationale. It is the modelling equivalent of audit working-paper referencing: an assertion with no evidence is not accepted. In equity research, sell-side models are expected to tie guidance to concall commentary. In FP&A, budget assumptions must tie to business unit submissions and board-approved plans. In transaction work, the assumption log becomes the basis of the "key assumptions" section of an information memorandum or credit note. For Project 4 it is also your interview preparation, because you will be asked to defend at least two assumptions with sources.

Core notes

Practices that make the log useful

  • Cite precisely: document, date, page, slide or timestamp. "Concall" is not a source. "Q2 FY26 concall, 6 Nov 2025, CFO response to analyst on capex, transcript p. 6" is.
  • Quote guidance verbatim for key numbers ("We expect EBITDA margin to be in the 18–19% band for FY27"). It protects you when guidance changes.
  • Separate fact from judgement. "Guided 18–19%" is fact. "Base 18.5%, Bear 16% reflecting FY23 RM spike" is judgement. Record both.
  • Link to the model. A model cell reference (or a hyperlink from the log row to the input cell and back) lets reviewers jump between assumption and evidence. Better still, show the log ID next to each input on the Inputs sheet.
  • Rank by sensitivity. Use a quick one-at-a-time sensitivity (±10% on each input, output FY30E PAT or ROCE) to rank assumptions. Spend sourcing effort on the top five.
  • Track changes. When guidance changes (a new quarter's concall), mark the old row Superseded, add a new row, and note it in the version log.
  • Mind date-sensitivity. State the cut-off date. Company guidance, tax rates and regulations (for example, the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026) change.
  • Respect information rules. Use only public information. SEBI's Prohibition of Insider Trading Regulations apply to unpublished price-sensitive information. In an FP&A role with internal data, mark the model confidential.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • No guidance available. Use history, peers and industry data, and say so. Never invent a "management guided" source.
  • Conflicting sources (presentation says 2 MTPA, concall says 2.5 MTPA). Record both, explain which you used, and use the conflict to set scenario spread.
  • Stale guidance. Guidance from four quarters ago may be superseded. Check the latest quarterly call before finalising.
  • Segment-level assumptions for diversified companies. The log can become long, so group by segment and keep the top-sensitivity items in a summary.
  • Consolidated vs standalone data. Concall guidance is often for the standalone or "India business". Map carefully to the consolidated model.
  • Assumptions embedded in formulas (a hardcoded 365, a growth cap) should also be logged or moved to inputs (Topic 4 s06).

Key takeaways

  • Every key input needs a value by scenario, a precise source (document, date, page/slide), a rationale and a model cell reference.
  • Indian public sources: annual reports and MD&A, quarterly results, concall transcripts, investor presentations, rating rationales, industry bodies and DRHPs.
  • Separate fact (guidance, history) from judgement (scenario values), and quote key guidance verbatim.
  • Rank assumptions by sensitivity and focus sourcing effort on the top five.
  • Track superseded assumptions and state the information cut-off date. Use only public information for listed-company models.

Practice Questions

Q1. Source mapping [L1]

For a listed Indian hospital chain, name the best public source (document and section) for each of the following, and what you would extract: (a) bed additions over the next three years, (b) ARPOB and occupancy trends, (c) maturity profile of borrowings, (d) lease liabilities and their maturity, (e) unused tax losses and DTA recognised, (f) dividend policy.

Q2. Write log entries [L2]

Write complete assumption log entries (all fields in these notes) for three assumptions of your Project 4 company: one revenue driver, one margin driver and one balance-sheet driver. Use real public sources with dates and page or slide references, and give Base, Bull and Bear values with rationale.

💡 Hint: Start from the latest quarterly concall and the most recent annual report's notes.

Q3. Sensitivity ranking [L2]

Describe how you would rank 20 inputs by their impact on FY30E ROCE using a one-at-a-time ±10% test, including the Excel setup (data table or macro), the output metric, and how you would display the ranking (for example, a tornado chart). Explain one limitation of one-at-a-time testing.

Q4. Defending an assumption [L3]

An interviewer challenges your Base EBITDA margin of 19% for FY28E, noting that the company's best historical margin was 17.2%. Using the assumption log structure, prepare a defence covering evidence (sources), drivers (mix, operating leverage, RM prices), peer comparison and what the Bear case assumes. Then state what evidence would make you revise the assumption downwards.
