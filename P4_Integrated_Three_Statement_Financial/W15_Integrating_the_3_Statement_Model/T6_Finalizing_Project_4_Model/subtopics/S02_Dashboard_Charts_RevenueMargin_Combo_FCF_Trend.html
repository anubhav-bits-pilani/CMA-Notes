Subtopic note

Dashboard Charts: Revenue–Margin Combo, FCF Trend, Leverage Path

Scope: Charts: revenue and EBITDA margin combo, FCF trend, net debt/EBITDA trajectory

Why it matters

Three charts carry most of the story of an integrated model. Growth with profitability shows whether the company is scaling well. Cash generation shows whether profits turn into cash. The leverage path shows whether the balance sheet strengthens or weakens. Investment committees, credit committees and interview panels read charts before tables. A well-built chart also catches errors a table hides: a margin spike in one year, an FCF dip from a capex timing error, or a leverage line that jumps when the revolver draws. In Indian equity research and corporate presentations these three charts appear in almost every initiation report and investor deck, so readers know how to interpret them. Built from live links, they update with the scenario switch and become part of the stress-testing evidence.

Core notes

Chart 1: Revenue and EBITDA margin combo

Purpose: growth and profitability together.

Build:

  • Clustered columns for revenue (₹ crore) on the primary axis. Line with markers for EBITDA margin (%) on the secondary axis. In Excel: Insert → Combo Chart → Clustered Column – Line on Secondary Axis.
  • Timeline FY23A–FY30E. Format actuals and forecasts differently. The simplest method is two revenue series, "Revenue (A)" and "Revenue (E)", each with blanks (NA()) in the other's years, in solid and lighter (or patterned) fills.
  • Secondary axis: start at a sensible minimum (for example 10%) rather than zero, if margins move within a narrow band. Label it clearly, because dual axes can mislead. Add data labels to the margin line rather than relying on the axis.
  • Title linked to a cell so it shows the scenario: select the title, type = in the formula bar and click the cell containing ="Revenue and EBITDA margin – "&Scen_Label.

What to look for: margin steps that do not match any assumption change, or revenue growth with flat margins despite stated operating leverage.

Chart 2: FCF trend

Purpose: cash generation and conversion.

Build options:

  • Columns of FCF (₹ crore) by year, with an FCF conversion line (FCF ÷ PAT) on the secondary axis (c03).
  • Or stacked columns showing CFO, capex (negative) and lease payments (negative), with an FCF marker line. This "FCF bridge by year" shows why FCF moves.
  • Negative FCF years should display in a contrasting colour. Use "Invert if negative" in the series fill options, or a separate negative series.

What to look for: FCF far above PAT for several years (usually capex too low or working capital releasing implausibly), FCF negative in Base without an explanation (a capex programme), or a one-year spike from a working capital reversal.

Chart 3: Net debt/EBITDA trajectory

Purpose: deleveraging path and covenant headroom.

Build:

  • Line of net debt/EBITDA (x) by year, actuals and forecasts.
  • Horizontal covenant line: add a series equal to Cov_ND_Max in every year, formatted as a dashed red line, labelled "Covenant 3.0x".
  • Optionally, net debt in columns on the primary axis with the ratio line on the secondary axis.
  • Net cash years: the ratio turns negative and stops being meaningful. Floor the plotted ratio at zero and annotate "net cash", or plot net debt (₹ crore) instead, with net cash shown as negative bars.

What to look for: jumps when the revolver draws (stress cases), lines approaching the covenant, and inconsistency with the lease treatment in the dashboard definitions (s01).

General chart rules (from Phase 3 dashboard principles)

  • One message per chart, stated in the title ("Net debt/EBITDA falls below 1.0x by FY27E"). In a live model, use a neutral title with the scenario label and put the message in linked commentary text.
  • No 3D, no shadows, minimal gridlines, consistent colours across charts (for example, actuals dark, forecasts light, warnings red).
  • Units in axis titles (₹ crore, %, x). Indian number formatting with #,##0 groups digits in thousands, not lakhs. If you want lakh-style grouping (12,34,567), use a custom format such as [>=10000000]##\,##\,##\,##0;[>=100000]##\,##\,##0;##,##0. In ₹ crore models, thousands grouping is fine and conventional.
  • Charts should reference dashboard or output ranges, not the statements directly, so chart sources sit with the rest of the presentation layer (c04).
  • Fix the axis bounds per chart only if you have checked all scenarios. A fixed axis that clips the Bear case hides the very thing being tested. "Auto" is safer for live models.

Illustration

Kaveri Consumer Ltd, Base:

Year FY25A FY26E FY27E FY28E FY29E FY30E
Revenue (₹ crore) 5,000 5,500 6,160 6,900 7,660 8,430
EBITDA margin 15.2% 16.0% 16.4% 16.7% 16.8% 17.0%
FCF (₹ crore) 210 280 340 410 480 560
Net debt/EBITDA 0.68x 0.48x 0.33x 0.18x 0.05x n.m.
Covenant line 3.0x 3.0x 3.0x 3.0x 3.0x 3.0x

Chart 1 shows steady column growth with a gently rising margin line. Chart 2 shows FCF almost tripling. Chart 3 shows a line well below the dashed 3.0x covenant, ending at net cash. Switching to Bear should visibly change all three: the margin line dips, FCF falls (perhaps negative in one year) and the leverage line rises towards the covenant. If a chart does not move when the scenario changes, a series is pasted or points at a static range.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Secondary-axis distortion. Scaling the margin axis from 15% to 17% makes a 2-point change look dramatic. Choose bounds that reflect the economic significance, and label the values.
  • Mixed signs in FCF. If capex causes negative FCF in early years, a conversion ratio line becomes negative or explodes when PAT is near zero. Cap the plotted ratio or omit it in those years with NA().
  • Data labels and scenarios. Labels that overlap in Bull (larger numbers) can become unreadable. Test label placement in all scenarios.
  • Chart ranges that do not extend when you add a year. Use named ranges or Excel tables for chart sources, so the range expands automatically.
  • Printing. Charts set to "Move and size with cells" can distort when rows are grouped or collapsed. Use "Don't move or size with cells" for dashboard charts (Format Chart Area → Properties).
  • Power BI handoff. If the dashboard will also be built in Power BI, keep chart source tables in tidy (long) format on an output sheet to ease import.

Key takeaways

  • The three core charts answer growth with profitability, cash generation, and leverage with covenant headroom.
  • Distinguish actuals from forecasts visually, and link chart titles to the live scenario label.
  • Add the covenant threshold as a dashed series on the leverage chart, and handle net cash years explicitly.
  • Charts reference dashboard/output ranges and must move when the scenario changes. A static chart signals a pasted series.
  • Avoid misleading dual-axis scaling, test labels and axis bounds in every scenario, and keep charts unaffected by grouping.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build specification [L1]

Write a build specification for the revenue and EBITDA margin combo chart for FY23A–FY30E, including series names and source ranges (named ranges of your choice), axis assignment and bounds, actual vs forecast formatting, data labels and a scenario-linked title formula.

Q2. FCF chart diagnosis [L2]

Your FCF chart for Pench Pharma Ltd shows FCF (₹ crore) of 150, 160, 420, 170, 180 for FY26E–FY30E, while PAT is 200, 220, 240, 265, 290. (a) Compute FCF conversion for each year. (b) Propose three possible causes of the FY28E spike, and the schedule you would inspect for each. (c) Explain whether the spike could be correct, and what you would add to the dashboard if it is.

💡 Hint: Large one-year FCF spikes usually come from working capital or capex timing.

Q3. Leverage chart with net cash [L2]

Net debt for FY26E–FY30E is ₹380, ₹210, ₹40, −₹120 and −₹300 crore, and EBITDA is ₹400, ₹440, ₹480, ₹520 and ₹560 crore. (a) Compute net debt/EBITDA. (b) Design the leverage chart so that net cash years are shown meaningfully, and the covenant line (2.5x) remains visible. (c) Explain why plotting the raw negative ratio is misleading.

Q4. Scenario-proof charts [L3]

Describe a test procedure that proves all three dashboard charts are live, correctly ranged and readable in Base, Bull, Bear and an extreme recession case. List the specific failures you would look for (at least five) and the fix for each.
