Key concept

ROCE: Two Definitions of Capital Employed

Concept: ROCE = EBIT ÷ (Equity + Net debt) or EBIT ÷ (Total assets − Current liabilities)

Statement

Return on capital employed measures operating profit per rupee of capital tied up in the business:

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Capital employed}} \]

with two common definitions of capital employed:

\[ \text{CE}_{\text{financing}} = \text{Equity} + \text{Net debt} \]

\[ \text{CE}_{\text{assets}} = \text{Total assets} - \text{Current liabilities} \]

Here EBIT is earnings before interest and tax (EBITDA − D&A, usually before other income and exceptional items). Equity is total equity, including NCI if EBIT is consolidated. Net debt is total borrowings (plus lease liabilities, if included) minus cash and liquid investments. Use average capital employed (opening + closing ÷ 2) for flow-to-stock consistency.

Explanation

The two definitions look at the same balance sheet from opposite sides:

  • Financing side (equity + net debt): the capital providers' funds. Cash is deducted, so a cash-rich company's ROCE reflects operating assets only.
  • Asset side (total assets − current liabilities): equals equity + non-current liabilities. It includes cash in capital employed and includes non-debt non-current liabilities (DTL, long-term provisions). Current borrowings sit in current liabilities, so they are deducted, which is a well-known distortion when short-term debt is large.

The two give different answers whenever cash, current borrowings or non-debt non-current liabilities are material. Neither is "right". What matters is stating the definition and applying it consistently across history, forecast and peers.

Indian disclosure context: Schedule III (as amended in 2021) requires ROCE among the ratios disclosed in the notes, with explanations for changes over 25%. The ICAI Guidance Note on Schedule III suggests ROCE = EBIT ÷ capital employed, where capital employed = tangible net worth + total debt + deferred tax liability. That is a third, practitioner-relevant variant. Companies' own definitions vary, so read the ratio note before comparing.

Consistency points:

  • Other income: exclude it from EBIT if cash is deducted from capital employed (financing definition). Otherwise the numerator includes treasury income while the denominator excludes the cash that earns it.
  • Ind AS 116: post-Ind AS 116 EBIT excludes lease interest (only ROU depreciation is in D&A). For consistency, include lease liabilities in net debt (or the ROU asset in capital employed).
  • Goodwill: acquisitive companies show lower ROCE including goodwill. Some analysts also show ROCE excluding goodwill ("organic" or "operating" ROCE).
  • Pre-tax vs post-tax: ROCE is pre-tax. ROIC usually uses NOPAT (EBIT × (1 − t)) over invested capital, and is compared with WACC in Week 16.
  • CWIP: large CWIP depresses ROCE during build phases, because the capital is employed but not yet earning. Some practitioners show ROCE excluding CWIP for capex-heavy companies.

Illustration

Narmada Cement Ltd, FY27E (₹ crore). EBIT 900 (excluding other income of 60). Average balances: equity 4,000, borrowings 2,200, lease liabilities 150, cash and liquid investments 700, total assets 8,600, current liabilities 1,900 (including current borrowings of 400).

Definition Capital employed ROCE
Equity + net debt (incl. leases) 4,000 + 2,200 + 150 − 700 = 5,650 15.9%
Total assets − current liabilities 8,600 − 1,900 = 6,700 13.4%

The difference comes mainly from cash of 700, which is in the asset-side denominator but deducted in the financing definition. Current borrowings of 400 also leave the asset-side measure, and the asset-side measure includes non-debt non-current liabilities. On the asset-side basis, a consistent numerator would arguably include the other income of 60, giving 960 ÷ 6,700 = 14.3%.

Connections

ROCE is a headline dashboard metric (s01) and a key sanity band (c05). Forecast ROCE drifting far above history is a topic pitfall. It links to Phase 2 ratio analysis, to the DuPont-style decomposition ROCE = EBIT margin × capital turnover, to t3 related concepts (ROCE and gearing from the forecast BS), and to Week 16, where ROIC vs WACC frames value creation. In FP&A, ROCE by business unit drives capital allocation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both definitions [L1]

Krishna Auto Ltd FY28E (₹ crore): EBIT 640, other income 35, average equity 2,900, average borrowings 900 (of which current 250), average lease liabilities 80, average cash 420, average total assets 5,300, average current liabilities 1,450. Compute ROCE on both definitions, stating your treatment of other income and leases in each.

Q2. Explaining a divergence [L2]

Company A's ROCE is 32% on the financing definition and 19% on the asset-side definition. Company B's ROCE is 18% and 17% on the same definitions. Explain what balance sheet features most likely cause A's gap, and which definition you would use to compare A and B with their peers in a dashboard.

💡 Hint: Think about where cash and current borrowings sit in each definition.

Q3. ROCE during a capex cycle [L2]

A company's ROCE falls from 22% to 14% over FY26E–FY28E while it builds a new plant (CWIP rises from ₹200 crore to ₹1,800 crore before capitalisation in FY29E). Explain how you would present ROCE on the dashboard so that readers understand the dip, and what ROCE you would expect in FY30E if the plant earns the same EBIT margin and capital turnover as existing assets.
