Key concept

ROE on Average Equity and the Three-Step DuPont Identity

Concept: ROE = PAT ÷ Average equity; DuPont: ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier

Statement

\[ \text{ROE} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\overline{E}} \]

\[ \text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{Net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\overline{A}}}_{\text{Asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\overline{A}}{\overline{E}}}_{\text{Equity multiplier}} \]

where PAT is profit after tax attributable to owners of the parent (in consolidated statements), \( \overline{E} \) is average equity attributable to owners, \( \frac{\text{opening} + \text{closing}}{2} \), \( \overline{A} \) is average total assets, and revenue is revenue from operations. The identity holds exactly because revenue and average assets cancel.

Explanation

Why average equity: PAT is earned over the year, while equity is a point-in-time balance. Year-end equity already includes the year's retained profit, so it understates ROE for a growing company. Opening equity ignores mid-year issues and buybacks. The average is the standard compromise. Schedule III's ROE ratio and most Indian analysts use average shareholders' equity.

Owners' basis: if PAT includes the NCI share, use total equity including NCI. If PAT is attributable to owners, use owners' equity only. Mixing them distorts ROE in groups with large NCI.

Reading the DuPont components:

  • Net margin captures pricing, cost control, interest burden and tax.
  • Asset turnover captures capital intensity and working capital efficiency.
  • Equity multiplier (leverage) captures how much of the asset base is funded by liabilities, including non-debt liabilities such as payables.

A rising ROE driven by margin is usually healthier than one driven by leverage. In a forecast model, DuPont is a plausibility tool. If ROE rises because asset turnover climbs steeply, check whether capex is too low relative to growth. If it rises because the equity multiplier increases, check debt-funded buybacks or dividends.

Five-step extension: net margin can be split further into tax burden (PAT ÷ PBT), interest burden (PBT ÷ EBIT) and EBIT margin (EBIT ÷ revenue), giving ROE = tax burden × interest burden × EBIT margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier. This is useful when finance costs or the ETR move a lot.

Edge cases: negative equity (accumulated losses) makes ROE meaningless, so show "n.m.". Large buybacks shrink equity and inflate ROE without operating improvement. Cash-rich companies show depressed ROE because idle cash inflates the asset base and equity.

Illustration

Kaveri Consumer Ltd (₹ crore):

Item FY26E FY30E
Revenue 5,500 8,430
PAT to owners 494 868
Average total assets 4,600 6,900
Average owners' equity 3,100 5,000
Net margin 8.98% 10.30%
Asset turnover 1.196x 1.222x
Equity multiplier 1.484x 1.380x
ROE 15.9% 17.4%

ROE rises by 1.5 points. Net margin contributes the most, asset turnover a little, and leverage falls as the company moves to net cash. That is a healthy, plausible pattern. If instead the FY30E asset turnover were 1.8x with flat margins, the reviewer would ask why the asset base grew so much more slowly than revenue.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-step DuPont [L1]

Ganga Paints Ltd FY27E (₹ crore): revenue 6,200, PAT to owners 620, opening total assets 5,000, closing total assets 5,600, opening owners' equity 3,400, closing owners' equity 3,800. Compute ROE and its three DuPont components, and verify the identity.

Q2. Diagnose a ROE jump [L2]

A forecast shows ROE rising from 14% (FY26E) to 24% (FY30E). The components move as follows: net margin 7.0% → 7.5%, asset turnover 1.25x → 1.60x, equity multiplier 1.60x → 2.00x. (a) Quantify each component's contribution (use a sequential substitution approach). (b) List the model assumptions you would check for the asset turnover and leverage changes. (c) State whether the trajectory is plausible for a mature FMCG company.

💡 Hint: Change one component at a time, holding the others at their FY26E values, then the next.

Q3. Average vs closing equity [L2]

PAT to owners is ₹300 crore, opening equity ₹1,500 crore and closing equity ₹1,740 crore, after a ₹60 crore dividend and no other movements. Compute ROE on opening, closing and average equity. Explain which one you would show on the dashboard, and how a ₹400 crore buyback at year-end would affect each measure.
