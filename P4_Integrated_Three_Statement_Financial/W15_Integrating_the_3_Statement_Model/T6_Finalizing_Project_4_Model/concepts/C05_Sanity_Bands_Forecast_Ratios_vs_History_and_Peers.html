Key concept

Sanity Bands: Forecast Ratios vs History and Peers

Concept: Sanity bands: forecast margins, ROCE and capex/sales vs historical and peer ranges

Statement

For each key forecast ratio \( r_t \), define a sanity band \( [L, U] \) from evidence:

\[ L = \min(\text{historical low},\; \text{peer low}) - m, \qquad U = \max(\text{historical high},\; \text{peer high}) + m \]

and flag any forecast year where \( r_t < L \) or \( r_t > U \). Here the historical range is typically the last 5–10 years of the company's own ratio (covering at least one cycle), the peer range comes from 3–6 listed comparables on the same definition, and \( m \) is an optional tolerance margin (for example, 1–2 percentage points for margins). A flag does not mean the forecast is wrong. It means the forecast needs an explicit, sourced justification.

Explanation

Integrity checks prove the model is internally consistent. Sanity bands test whether it is economically plausible. A model can balance perfectly while forecasting ROCE of 45% for a cement company or capex/sales of 1% for a steel producer, simply because individually reasonable assumptions compound. The topic pitfall "forecast ratios drifting implausibly (e.g., ROCE doubling) unchecked" is exactly this failure.

Ratios to band (minimum set):

Ratio Why it drifts Typical evidence
Gross and EBITDA margin Operating leverage plus price assumptions compound 10-year history covering the RM cycle; peer margins
ROCE Margin up and capex down together History; peers; Schedule III ratio note
Capex/sales and capex/D&A Capex held flat in rupees while revenue grows History; guidance; maintenance capex estimates
Asset turnover Revenue grows faster than the asset base History; capacity utilisation limits
Working capital days Small day changes compound History; peers; industry norms
ETR Tax schedule errors, MAT/115BAA transitions Statutory rate 25.17% plus permanent differences
Revenue growth vs capacity Volume growth exceeding installed capacity Capacity disclosures, utilisation in investor presentations

Practical rules:

  • Same definitions. Peer ratios must use your definitions (EBITDA excluding other income, the same ROCE capital employed). Otherwise bands are meaningless.
  • Cycle coverage. Indian cyclicals (cement, steel, chemicals, autos) need 8–10 years of history to capture a full cycle. Five years can cover only an upswing.
  • Capacity constraints. Revenue growth that implies utilisation above about 90–95% needs capex. If capex is not there, either growth or capex is wrong.
  • Capex/D&A below 1.0x for many years implies a shrinking asset base. That is plausible only for a company in harvest mode.
  • Display: show the band next to each ratio row on Outputs and the dashboard (for example, "ROCE 27.4% | band 16–26% | FLAG"), with amber conditional formatting when outside.
  • Explain, don't suppress. A flagged ratio needs a note in the assumption log (s05), such as "margin above historical high due to mix shift to premium; Bull only." Do not widen the band to make the flag go away.
Band_Low  = MIN(Hist_Low, Peer_Low) - Band_Margin
Band_High = MAX(Hist_High, Peer_High) + Band_Margin
Flag      = --OR(Ratio < Band_Low, Ratio > Band_High)

Illustration

Vindhya Cement Ltd (₹ crore), Base forecast vs bands:

Ratio FY30E forecast History FY16–FY25 Peers (5) Band (±1 pp) Flag
EBITDA margin 24.5% 14.0–21.5% 15.0–23.0% 13.0–24.0% Yes
ROCE (pre-tax) 31.0% 9.0–19.0% 10.0–22.0% 8.0–23.0% Yes
Capex/sales 2.5% 6.0–14.0% 5.0–15.0% 4.0–16.0% Yes
Capex/D&A 0.6x 1.2–3.0x 1.1–3.2x — Yes
Volume growth 9% 3–10% 4–11% 2–12% No

Three linked flags tell one story. Capex was held flat in rupees while volumes grew 9% a year, so capacity utilisation implicitly exceeds 100% by FY29E, the asset base shrinks (capex/D&A 0.6x) and ROCE balloons. Fix: add capacity-linked capex (a brownfield expansion in FY28E). ROCE then falls to about 20% and capex/sales rises to about 7%, both within bands.

Connections

Sanity bands implement the topic pitfall on ratio drift and complement the integrity checks of Topic 5 (s07). They use the ratios defined in c01 (ROCE), c02 (ROE and DuPont, which locate the source of drift) and c03 (FCF conversion above 100% for years is a band breach). Bands draw on Phase 2 ratio analysis and peer benchmarking and are cited in the assumption log (s05). In Week 16, the same discipline applies to terminal-year margins and reinvestment rates, which must be consistent with growth (reinvestment rate = g ÷ ROIC). In FP&A, bands become "reasonableness tests" on business-unit budget submissions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the bands [L1]

For an Indian two-wheeler maker, historical EBITDA margins over FY16–FY25 ranged from 11.5% to 17.8%, and four peers ranged from 10.0% to 15.5%. Using a 1 percentage point margin, compute the band. State whether forecasts of 16.5%, 18.5% and 9.0% would be flagged, and what evidence you would need to support the 18.5%.

Q2. Linked flags diagnosis [L2]

A Project 4 model shows, for FY30E, ROE 28% (band 12–20%), asset turnover 2.1x (band 1.2–1.6x), net margin 9% (band 6–10%) and an equity multiplier of 1.5x (band 1.4–1.8x). (a) Use DuPont to identify which component drives the ROE flag. (b) List three model assumptions that could cause the asset turnover drift. (c) Describe the fix and how you would document it.

💡 Hint: Check which component is outside its band and trace it to capex and working capital.

Q3. Capacity sanity check [L2]

A cement company has installed capacity of 20 MTPA, FY25 volumes of 16 MT and Base volume growth of 8% a year with no capacity addition in the model. (a) Compute the implied utilisation for FY26E–FY30E. (b) Identify the first year that breaches a 92% practical utilisation ceiling. (c) Propose the model change (capacity, capex, timing) and state which sanity bands it would bring back into range.
