How this week fits

Week 15 sits at the centre of Phase 4 (Integrated Three-Statement Financial Modelling & Valuation). Week 13 set up the model architecture — inputs, scenario switch, formula-integrity rules, the revenue and cost builds. Week 14 built the supporting schedules — working capital, PPE/CWIP/intangibles/ROU corkscrews, debt and interest with the revolver and cash sweep, the circularity and circuit breaker, income tax and deferred tax, and the equity/NCI corkscrews. This week joins them: the income statement pulls from the schedules down to PAT and EPS (t1), the indirect cash flow statement is derived from the P&L and BS movements (t2), and the balance sheet takes every line from a schedule with cash from the CFS (t3). The remaining topics of the week — debugging diagnostics, stress testing and finalising Project 4 with a dashboard — rest on this integration.

Prerequisites: the Phase 1 work on Ind AS 1/Schedule III presentation, Ind AS 7 classification, Ind AS 12, Ind AS 28 and Ind AS 116; the Phase 2 ratio and cash-conversion analysis; the Phase 3 Excel auditing and dashboard skills; and your own Week 14 schedules for the Project 4 company. Next week (Week 16) bolts DCF and comparable-company valuation onto this model: FCFF is built from the same blocks as the CFS, net debt comes from the forecast BS, and sensitivity grids run on the same scenario switch. A model that does not balance in every scenario cannot be valued credibly — which is why the Project 4 deliverable this week is a scenario check table showing 0.00 everywhere.

Refresh before starting

Double-entry: every BS movement has a counterpart

Double-entry: every BS movement has a cash or non-cash counterpart

Recall that the balance sheet identity holds in changes: \( \Delta\text{Cash} = \Delta L + \Delta E - \Delta\text{Non-cash assets} \). Every movement in a non-cash BS line is matched either by cash (receivables collected, capex paid, debt repaid) or by another non-cash entry (depreciation against PAT, deferred tax against PAT or OCI, new leases against the lease liability, SBP charge against the SBP reserve, share of associates against the investment). The indirect CFS is simply a list of these counterparts. Self-test: for each of D&A, ESOP charge, new lease, dividend declared but unpaid, and actuarial loss in OCI, name the two BS lines affected and whether any CFS line appears.

Ind AS 7 Indian format and classification choices

Ind AS 7 Indian CFS format and classification choices

Indian companies use the indirect method starting from profit before tax, add back D&A and finance costs, remove interest and dividend income and investment gains, show "operating profit before working capital changes", apply working capital movements to get "cash generated from operations", and deduct income taxes paid. Classification choices under Ind AS 7.33–7.35 for non-financial entities: interest paid in CFF, interest and dividends received in CFI, dividends paid in CFF, taxes in CFO. Ind AS 116 lease principal is CFF and lease interest follows interest paid (CFF). Cash and cash equivalents are investments with original maturity of three months or less; other bank balances move through CFI. The Ind AS 7.44A–44E financing-liability reconciliation separates cash and non-cash changes.

Schedule III Division II line items

Schedule III Division II P&L and Balance Sheet line items

P&L (by nature): revenue from operations; other income; cost of materials consumed; purchases of stock-in-trade; changes in inventories of FG, WIP and stock-in-trade; employee benefits expense; finance costs; depreciation and amortisation expense; other expenses; profit before exceptional items and tax; exceptional items; PBT; current tax, deferred tax; profit for the period; OCI (two categories); profit and TCI attributable to owners and NCI; EPS (basic and diluted). Balance sheet: non-current assets (PPE, CWIP, investment property, goodwill, other intangibles, intangibles under development, ROU where shown separately, financial assets, DTA, other non-current assets); current assets (inventories, financial assets — investments, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents, other bank balances, loans, others — current tax assets, other current assets); equity (share capital, other equity) and NCI; non-current and current liabilities (borrowings, lease liabilities, trade payables split MSME/others, other financial liabilities, provisions, DTL, other liabilities, current tax liabilities). The 2021 amendment moved current maturities of long-term debt into current borrowings.

Week 14 schedule outputs and sign conventions

Week 14 schedule outputs and sign conventions

List each schedule's outputs and where they go: WC — closing balances (BS) and line-wise movements (CFO); PPE/CWIP/intangibles/ROU — closing net balances (BS), D&A (IS, CFO), capex spend (CFI); debt — closing balances by tranche, current maturities, revolver, interest (IS, CFF); leases — liability split, interest (IS), payments (CFF), new leases (non-cash); tax — current tax, deferred tax (IS), DTL/DTA and CTL (BS), taxes paid (CFO); equity — share capital, other equity components, NCI (BS), dividends and equity flows (CFF). Confirm the sign convention: schedules output expenses and outflows as positive numbers and the statements apply the sign, or schedules store signed values and statements add them. Mixed conventions are the commonest cause of 2X imbalances. Write the convention on the Cover sheet.

Cross-cutting concepts

Linking order with the breaker off

Linking order: P&L → CFS → BS cash → checks, with breaker off during build

Integration is done in a fixed order so each step can be tested before the next. First, with the circuit breaker on (interest on average balances set to zero or switched to opening balances), link the P&L from the schedules down to PAT, NCI and EPS, and confirm the KPI rows look sensible. Second, build the CFS: CFO from PBT (Indian format) or PAT, CFI from the asset and investment schedules, CFF from the debt, lease and equity corkscrews, and the cash roll-forward. Third, link every non-cash BS line to its schedule (or hold it flat), and only then link BS cash to CFS closing cash. Fourth, build the checks: the balance check =ROUND(Total_Assets-Total_Equity-Total_Liabilities,3), the cash check, the financing-liability reconciliation and the BS–CFS mapping table. Only when every check is zero with the breaker on do you switch it off and let the circularity iterate. Building with the breaker off means any error loops through interest and cash, oscillates across iterations and can trap #REF! or #DIV/0! in the loop — the debugging nightmare that t4 warns about. If the model balances with the breaker on but not off, the error is in the circular items themselves (interest income or expense links).

Interest on average balances through the breaker

Interest on average debt and cash routed through the circuit breaker

Interest expense on average debt (including the revolver) and interest income on average cash are economically correct because balances change through the year, but they create the loop: interest → PBT → tax → PAT → CFO → cash before revolver → revolver draw and closing cash → average balances → interest. Excel's iterative calculation (100 iterations, maximum change 0.001) resolves it. The circuit breaker is a single switch on the Inputs sheet that all average-balance interest formulas reference: =IF(Breaker=1,0,Rate*AVERAGE(Opening,Closing)) (or an opening-balance alternative). Every interest line that reaches the P&L, the CFS (finance-cost add-back, interest received, interest paid) and the net-debt reconciliation must reference the same switched cell; if the CFS links to an unswitched raw calculation, the model balances in one breaker state and not the other. Lease interest, computed on the opening lease liability, is not circular and need not pass through the breaker. Keep a "circularity converged" check on the dashboard — for example, the difference between interest computed with the current and previous iteration's balances — and document the breaker state at delivery (off).

Interview flow test: depreciation +₹10

Interview flow test: Depreciation +₹10 → PAT −7.48 (t = 25.17%), CFO +2.52, PPE −10, Cash +2.52, Equity −7.48

The classic question assumes that higher depreciation is deductible for tax (book and tax depreciation move together, or tax is computed simply on PBT). P&L: D&A +10, EBIT −10, PBT −10, tax −2.52 (10 × 25.168%), PAT −7.48. CFS (PAT start): PAT −7.48, D&A add-back +10, CFO +2.52; CFI and CFF unchanged; cash +2.52. In the Indian format: PBT −10, D&A +10, taxes paid −2.52 lower, CFO +2.52 — same answer. BS: PPE (net block) −10, cash +2.52, total assets −7.48; retained earnings −7.48, so equity −7.48. Balanced. Variations interviewers use: (i) if tax depreciation is unchanged (only book life changed), current tax does not change; the 2.52 is a deferred tax credit, so DTL falls 2.52, CFO is unchanged and cash is unchanged; (ii) with circularity on, the extra cash earns interest income, a second-round effect; (iii) with NCI, PAT to owners falls by the parent's share only. Practise saying the walk-through in under 60 seconds in the order P&L → CFS → BS.

Scenario check table: 5 years × 3 scenarios

Exercise: Check Row = 0.00 for 5 years × 3 scenarios, captured in a scenario check table

A model that balances in Base proves little; scenario invariance — balancing for any input — is the real test. The Project 4 deliverable includes a scenario check table: rows Base, Bull, Bear (and optionally extreme cases such as zero growth, margin collapse, 3× capex), columns FY27E–FY31E, each cell showing the balance check for that scenario and year. Because the model shows only one scenario at a time, populate it either with a one-variable data table on the scenario switch (row input = scenario number, output = a cell that concatenates or sums the five checks) or with a small macro that loops through scenarios and pastes values into a log clearly labelled as a static record with a timestamp. Add the cash check and the negative-cash/revolver-limit flags as further rows. The table is shown on the Cover and the dashboard; every cell must display 0.00. A non-zero cell in Bull or Bear only usually points to logic that bites only when inputs change sign or cross thresholds: MAX/MIN floors on tax and dividends, DTL flipping to DTA, the revolver hitting its limit, or a held-flat line whose CFS driver is scenario-dependent.

No plugs, linked openings, full lease and NCI mapping

No plugs; all opening balances linked; lease and NCI flows fully mapped

Three integrity rules close the integration. No plugs: cash, the revolver and every "other" line are driven, never residual; a balancing figure hides errors rather than fixing them. All opening balances linked: each corkscrew's FY27E opening equals the FY26A BS closing by formula, and each later year's opening equals the prior column's closing — a typed opening balance creates a constant difference from that year onward. Lease and NCI flows fully mapped: leases touch five places — ROU asset (new leases, depreciation), lease liability (new leases, interest, payments, current split), P&L (ROU depreciation in D&A, interest in finance costs), CFS (D&A and finance-cost add-backs in CFO, principal and interest in CFF) and the non-cash disclosure for new leases. NCI touches the P&L split (NCI share of PAT), the NCI corkscrew (share of PAT and OCI, dividends to NCI), group CFO (which starts from total PAT) and CFF (dividends paid to NCI). Missing any one of these produces an imbalance of exactly that flow, which the mapping table localises.
