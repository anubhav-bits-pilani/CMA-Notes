Key concept

The Balance Check Row Formula

Concept: Balance check: =ROUND(Total_Assets-Total_Equity-Total_Liabilities,3) = 0 every year

Statement

For every historical and forecast year \( t \):

\[ \text{Check}_t = \text{round}\left(A_t - E_t - L_t,\; 3\right) = 0 \]

where \( A_t \) is total assets, \( E_t \) is total equity including non-controlling interests, \( L_t \) is total liabilities (non-current + current), and rounding is to three decimal places in the model's unit (in a ₹ crore model, 0.001 crore = ₹10,000, so the tolerance is well below ₹1 lakh).

BS!F62   =ROUND(Total_Assets-Total_Equity-Total_Liabilities,3)   ' names scoped to each year's column, or explicit row refs
Checks!F5 =BS!F62
Checks!C5 =IF(SUMPRODUCT(ABS(Checks!F5:J5))=0,"OK","ERROR")

Explanation

The check is the summary test that the CFS captured every BS movement (s01). Design choices behind the formula:

  • Subtraction, not equality. =A=E+L returns TRUE/FALSE and hides the size of the difference; the size and its pattern across years are the main diagnostic clues (t4: constant → opening error, growing → recurring flow error; X vs 2X → omission vs sign).
  • Sign convention. Assets − equity − liabilities: a positive result means assets are too high (or L+E too low). Keep the same orientation across all checks.
  • ROUND to 3 decimals. Floating-point arithmetic leaves residues such as 1.13687E-13; without rounding, a genuinely balanced model can display a non-zero check and trip the error flag. Three decimals in ₹ crore is a tolerance of ₹10,000 — tight enough to catch any real omission. Never round to 0 decimals: a ₹0.4 crore (₹40 lakh) omission would be hidden.
  • Every year, including historicals. Historical columns must also show zero; if the annual report's rounded figures do not foot, add a documented rounding line in history only.
  • Total equity includes NCI. Omitting NCI from \( E \) produces an "imbalance" equal to NCI.
  • Aggregate flag. A single cell summing the absolute checks across all years feeds the Cover and dashboard, and the scenario check table (5 years × 3 scenarios) required in the Project 4 exercise.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): total assets 10,452.0004; total equity 5,928.4 + 96.6 + 463.6 = 6,488.6; total liabilities 3,963.4.

\[ \text{Check} = \text{round}(10{,}452.0004 - 6{,}488.6 - 3{,}963.4, 3) = \text{round}(0.0004, 3) = 0.000 \]

If the ESOP add-back of 22 were missing from the CFS, cash and total assets would fall by 22 and the check would show −22.000 in FY27E (and growing cumulatively if the ESOP charge recurs).

Practice Questions

Q1. Write the check and flag [L1]

Total assets are in row 30, share capital row 35, other equity row 36, NCI row 37, total non-current liabilities row 48 and total current liabilities row 60, with forecast years in columns F–J. Write the check formula for column F and a single summary flag that returns "OK" only if all five years are zero.

Q2. Rounding tolerance [L2]

A model in ₹ crore shows check values of 0.0000000002, −0.004, 0.0003, 0.12 and 0 across FY27E–FY31E before rounding. State which years pass at ROUND(…,3), which would pass at ROUND(…,0), and explain why ROUND(…,0) is dangerous.

💡 Hint: Convert each tolerance into ₹ lakh to judge materiality.

Q3. NCI omitted from equity [L2]

A modeller's check formula is =ROUND(Total_Assets-Equity_attributable_to_owners-Total_Liabilities,3). NCI is ₹420 crore at FY26A and grows by ₹43.6 crore in FY27E. Predict the check-row values for FY26A and FY27E, and explain why this is a formula error rather than a model error.
