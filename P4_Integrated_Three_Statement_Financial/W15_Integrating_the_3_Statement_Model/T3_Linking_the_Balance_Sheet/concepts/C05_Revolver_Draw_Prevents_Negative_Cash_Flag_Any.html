Key concept

Revolver Draw Prevents Negative Cash; Flag Any Residual

Concept: Negative cash prevented by revolver draw; flag any residual negative cash

Statement

\[ \text{CBR}_t = C_{t-1} + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}^{\text{ex-rev}}_t \]

\[ \text{Draw}_t = \min\left(\max(0,\; C^{\min} - \text{CBR}_t),\; \text{Limit} - \text{Rev}_{t-1}\right) \]

\[ \text{Repay}_t = \min\left(\max(0,\; \text{CBR}_t - C^{\min}),\; \text{Rev}_{t-1}\right) \]

\[ \text{Rev}_t = \text{Rev}_{t-1} + \text{Draw}_t - \text{Repay}_t, \qquad C_t = \text{CBR}_t + \text{Draw}_t - \text{Repay}_t \]

\[ \text{Flag}^{\text{neg}}_t = 1 \text{ if } C_t < 0; \qquad \text{Flag}^{\text{min}}_t = 1 \text{ if } C_t < C^{\min} \]

where \( \text{CBR}_t \) is cash before revolver, \( \text{CFF}^{\text{ex-rev}}_t \) all financing flows other than the revolver (mandatory repayments, dividends, interest, leases, equity), \( C^{\min} \) minimum operating cash, \( \text{Limit} \) the sanctioned revolver/cash-credit limit and \( \text{Rev} \) the revolver balance.

Explanation

The revolver is the model's automatic liquidity buffer (Week 14 cash waterfall). It draws only what is needed to restore minimum cash, up to the limit, and repays first whenever there is surplus. Because the draw is computed from cash before the revolver, cash is still determined by the CFS — the revolver is a CFF line, not a plug (c02). The BS balances whether or not the revolver draws.

What happens when the limit binds: \( \text{Draw}_t \) is capped, so \( C_t \) can fall below \( C^{\min} \) and even below zero. The model must still balance — negative cash is arithmetically valid — but it represents an unfunded deficit: in reality the company would need new equity, new term debt, asset sales or a dividend cut. The model's job is to make this visible, not to hide it:

Debt!F50  =MIN(MAX(0,Inputs!$C$40-Debt!F45),Inputs!$C$41-Debt!E52)   ' Draw
Debt!F51  =MIN(MAX(0,Debt!F45-Inputs!$C$40),Debt!E52)                ' Repay
Checks!F8 =--(CFS!F47<0)                                              ' Negative cash flag
Checks!F9 =--(Debt!F52>Inputs!$C$41)                                  ' Revolver above limit
Checks!F10=--(CFS!F47<Inputs!$C$40)                                   ' Below minimum cash

An unbounded revolver (no limit) silently funds any deficit, so a Bear case shows ever-rising debt with no warning — the stress-testing pitfall. A limit with a flag keeps the model honest. The circularity matters: revolver interest depends on the average revolver balance, which depends on cash, which depends on interest — keep the circuit breaker available.

Presentation: negative cash should not be reclassified by formula into "bank overdraft" unless the overdraft is modelled as a financing facility with its own CFF line; otherwise the reclass would break the cash link.

Illustration

FY29E Bear case (₹ crore): opening cash 150; CFO 220; CFI (480); CFF ex-revolver (310); minimum cash 100; limit 400; opening revolver 150.

\[ \text{CBR} = 150 + 220 - 480 - 310 = -420 \]

\[ \text{Draw} = \min(\max(0, 100 + 420), 400 - 150) = \min(520, 250) = 250 \]

\[ C = -420 + 250 = -170, \quad \text{Rev} = 400 \]

Flags: revolver at limit; cash below minimum; cash negative (−170). Shortfall versus minimum cash = 270. The BS still balances (cash −170 on the asset side; revolver 400 in current borrowings).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute draw and flags [L1]

Opening cash ₹80 crore; CFO ₹310 crore; CFI (₹520 crore); CFF ex-revolver (₹140 crore); minimum cash ₹100 crore; limit ₹300 crore; opening revolver ₹50 crore. Compute cash before revolver, the draw, closing cash, closing revolver and the flags.

Q2. Surplus year repayment [L2]

In the following year, cash before revolver is ₹460 crore with minimum cash ₹100 crore and an opening revolver of ₹300 crore. Compute the repayment and closing cash, and explain why the waterfall repays the revolver before any optional cash sweep of term debt.

💡 Hint: Revolver is typically the most expensive and most flexible facility.

Q3. Remove the limit? [L3]

A senior asks you to remove the revolver limit "so the Bear case stops showing negative cash". Write a short response explaining the risks, an alternative modelling approach (e.g., a funding-gap line or an equity-raise toggle with its own CFF line), and how the dashboard should present the result.
