Key concept

Cash Comes from the CFS, Never as a Residual

Concept: Cash is computed by the CFS, never as Total liabilities & equity − Other assets

Statement

\[ \text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t = C^{\text{CFS}}_t = C^{\text{CFS}}_{t-1} + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}_t \]

and never

\[ \text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t = (E_t + L_t) - \text{NCA}_t \]

where \( C^{\text{CFS}}_t \) is the CFS closing cash and cash equivalents, \( E_t \) total equity incl. NCI, \( L_t \) total liabilities and \( \text{NCA}_t \) all non-cash assets.

Explanation

Both formulas give the same answer if and only if the model is correct. The difference is what happens when it is not. With the residual formula, the BS always balances, so the check row is permanently zero and every error — a missing ESOP add-back, a sign flip on payables, a lease addition in capex — is silently absorbed into cash. The CFS then shows one closing cash and the BS another; if nobody builds a cash check, the error survives into valuation (net debt, interest income) and into the dashboard.

Computing cash from the CFS converts the BS check into a real test: any movement missed by the CFS shows up as a difference. That is why every professional standard (FAST, ICAEW Financial Modelling Code, bank and Big 4 house styles) builds cash this way, and why interviewers treat "I made cash the plug" as an automatic fail.

Corollaries:

  • The revolver must also not be a residual; it is computed from pre-financing cash flow in the debt schedule.
  • No "other" line (other liabilities, other equity, suspense) may balance the model.
  • A cash check row (CFS closing − BS cash) should be zero; it catches any overwrite of the BS cash link.
  • Historical cash is hardcoded from the annual report — the CFS rule applies to forecast years.

Illustration

A model's CFS omits SBP add-backs of ₹22 crore (FY27E) and ₹24.5 crore (FY28E).

Cash from CFS (correct design) Cash as residual (wrong design)
FY27E BS cash 789.8 (811.8 − 22) 811.8
FY27E check −22.0 0.0
FY28E check −46.5 0.0
CFS closing vs BS cash equal 789.8 vs 811.8 (if anyone looks)

The correct design exposes the error immediately with a growing −22/−46.5 pattern equal to the cumulative missing add-back; the residual design hides it.

Practice Questions

Q1. Explain in an interview [L1]

An interviewer asks: "Your model balances. How do I know it's right?" Answer in 4–6 sentences, referring to how cash is computed and what the check proves.

Q2. Find the plug [L2]

You inherit a model with BS cash =BS!F60-SUM(BS!F13:F29) (total equity and liabilities less non-cash assets) and a check row of zero in every year. The CFS closing cash is 811.8, 1,071.8, 1,350.4, 1,602.0, 1,910.7 for FY27E–FY31E; BS cash is 811.8, 1,046.8, 1,298.4, 1,519.1, 1,795.0. Compute the implied hidden errors by year, describe the pattern and suggest what kind of error it is.

💡 Hint: Look at the year-on-year change in the difference.

Q3. Redesign [L2]

Describe the steps to convert the inherited model in Q2 to a correct design without losing work, including the order in which you would relink cells and the checks you would add.
