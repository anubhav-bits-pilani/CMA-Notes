Key concept

Current Maturities Reclassification Has No Cash Flow

Concept: Current maturities reclassification is presentation only: no cash flow

Statement

For each long-term borrowing \( k \):

\[ D^{\text{NC}}_{k,t} = D_{k,t} - R_{k,t+1}, \qquad D^{\text{CM}}_{k,t} = R_{k,t+1}, \qquad D^{\text{NC}}_{k,t} + D^{\text{CM}}_{k,t} = D_{k,t} \]

and the CFS financing lines use only the total movement:

\[ \text{CFF}^{\text{debt}}_{k,t} = D_{k,t} - D_{k,t-1} = \text{Drawdowns}_{k,t} - \text{Repayments}_{k,t} \]

where \( D_{k,t} \) is the closing carrying amount of tranche \( k \), \( R_{k,t+1} \) the scheduled repayment falling due within twelve months after the reporting date (year \( t+1 \)), \( D^{\text{NC}} \) the non-current portion and \( D^{\text{CM}} \) the current maturities presented within current borrowings (Schedule III Division II, as amended in 2021).

Explanation

Ind AS 1.69 requires a liability to be presented as current if it is due within twelve months of the reporting date. Each year, the next instalment of a term loan therefore moves from non-current to current borrowings. No cash moves: the lender has not been paid; the company has not borrowed. It is a reclassification between two BS lines with an identical total.

Errors arise when the CFS is built off individual BS lines rather than the debt schedule:

  • Treating the fall in non-current borrowings as "repayment of long-term borrowings" and the rise in current borrowings as "proceeds from short-term borrowings" leaves total CFF unchanged but grossly misstates both lines (and breaches the Ind AS 7.21 gross-flow requirement in substance).
  • Mapping only one of the two lines to the CFS (e.g., Δnon-current borrowings in CFF, current maturities ignored or treated as an operating liability in NWC) creates an imbalance equal to the change in current maturities — or puts a financing movement into CFO.

The safe design: CFF debt lines link to drawdowns and repayments in the debt schedule; the BS split is computed separately; the financing-liability reconciliation (t2/s06) uses current + non-current together, so the reclass nets to zero; current maturities are excluded from the NWC schedule (Week 14).

Illustration

Term loan ₹2,000 crore at 31 March 2026, repayable ₹300 crore per year from FY27.

31 March Total Current maturities Non-current Cash repaid in year
2026 2,000 300 1,700 —
2027 1,700 300 1,400 300
2028 1,400 300 1,100 300

CFF each year: repayment (300). Non-current falls by 300 a year, but that is the reclassification of the next instalment, not the cash repayment — in FY27 the 300 repaid came out of the current maturities line, which was replenished by the next 300 reclassified. If the repayment profile steps up to 500 in FY29, current maturities at 31 March 2028 would be 500 while cash repaid in FY28 is still 300.

Connections

Supports s03 (borrowings current/non-current split) and t2/s04 (CFF from the debt schedule), t2/s06 (net-debt reconciliation). Relevant to Phase 1 Schedule III classification and to Phase 2 liquidity ratios (current ratio, quick ratio) which change with the reclassification even though cash does not. In t4 debugging, a difference equal to Δcurrent maturities is a signature of this error.

Practice Questions

Q1. Split and CFF [L1]

A ₹1,200 crore loan at 31 March 2027 is repayable ₹200 crore in FY28, ₹400 crore in FY29 and ₹600 crore in FY30. Compute current and non-current borrowings at 31 March 2027, 2028 and 2029, and the CFF repayment line for FY28 and FY29.

Q2. Current maturities in NWC [L2]

A model's WC schedule includes "current maturities of long-term debt" in other current liabilities. Using Q1's numbers, compute the effect on CFO, CFF and the BS check for FY28 and FY29 if CFF repayments are correctly linked to the debt schedule.

💡 Hint: The ΔNWC line now picks up the change in current maturities as an operating inflow or outflow.

Q3. Current ratio optics [L2]

At 31 March 2029 a ₹1,000 crore NCD becomes due within 12 months (bullet in FY30). Current assets are ₹3,600 crore and current liabilities excluding the NCD ₹2,700 crore. Compute the current ratio before and after reclassification, and explain how you would present this on the dashboard so management does not misread a liquidity crisis.
