Roadmap topic: Linking the Balance Sheet: Closing Cash from Cash Flow Statement; Working Capital, PPE, Debt, and Equity from respective corkscrew schedules

Overview

Linking the balance sheet is the final integration step of Project 4. After the P&L (t1) and the CFS (t2) are in place, every forecast BS line is populated by a link: operating assets and liabilities from the working-capital schedule; PPE, CWIP, intangibles and ROU assets from the asset corkscrews; borrowings, the revolver and lease liabilities from the financing schedules; deferred tax and current tax balances from the tax schedule; share capital, other equity and NCI from the equity corkscrew; and cash from the CFS closing cash. Lines with no driver are held flat at the last actual — with zero CFS movement. Nothing is a residual. The balance check, =ROUND(Total_Assets-Total_Equity-Total_Liabilities,3), then tests whether the CFS captured every movement.

This architecture is what employers mean by "a model that balances properly". In equity research and transaction advisory, the forecast BS produces net debt, ROCE, gearing and working-capital intensity; in credit and treasury roles it produces covenant headroom and liquidity; in FP&A it supports balance-sheet forecasting and cash planning. A model whose BS is forced to balance with a plug produces all of these numbers wrongly — and no reviewer will trust it. Interviewers commonly ask how your model balances, what happens to the BS when an input changes, and why current maturities do not create a cash flow.

The learning sequence: begin with the principle that cash from the CFS is the sole balancing mechanism and that the check row tests CFS completeness (s01, c01, c02). Link operating lines — NWC from the WC schedule and long-term operating assets from the asset schedules (s02). Link financing liabilities with the current/non-current split, the revolver and leases (s03, c04), then tax balances (s04) and equity including NCI (s05). Close with lines held flat and the consistency they require (s06). The BS–CFS mapping table (c03) ties every line to its schedule or to "held constant", and the revolver-and-flag logic (c05) makes sure negative cash is prevented where possible and exposed where not.

This topic consumes Week 14's schedules and t2's closing cash and produces the inputs for t4 (debugging), t5 (stress testing) and t6 (the dashboard: net debt, ROCE, ROE).

Related concepts to study

Week 14 schedules and the Week 15 CFS

→ Week 14 schedules; → Week 15 (w15_t2) CFS

Every forecast BS line in this topic is a closing balance from a Week 14 schedule: WC (receivables, inventories, payables, other operating items), PPE/CWIP and intangibles corkscrews, the lease schedule (ROU asset and lease liability), the debt schedule with revolver and current-maturity outputs, the tax schedule (DTL/DTA, current tax liability, advance tax), and the equity and NCI corkscrews. The CFS from topic t2 supplies closing cash. Before linking, confirm that each schedule's closing row equals opening + movements, that the schedule's movements are the same cells the CFS links to, and that the column layout matches the BS. A schedule whose closing balance is right but whose movement rows are not used by the CFS is a disguised imbalance waiting to happen.

Schedule III Division II balance sheet classification

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Schedule III Division II balance sheet classification

Phase 1 covered the Schedule III Division II balance sheet format for Ind AS companies: non-current and current assets and liabilities, financial assets and liabilities shown separately, the 2021 amendments (current maturities within current borrowings, lease liabilities as separate lines, MSME payables split, ageing schedules for receivables and payables, CWIP ageing), and equity presented as equity share capital, other equity and NCI with SOCIE detail. The operating-cycle test in Ind AS 1.66–1.70 determines current versus non-current. The forecast BS should mirror the reported face so historicals reconcile and ratio definitions are comparable; the model can collapse immaterial lines, but must keep the current/non-current split for liquidity ratios and covenant testing.

Net debt/EBITDA, ROCE and gearing from the forecast BS

→ Phase 2 (W5–8): net debt/EBITDA, ROCE, gearing from forecast balance sheet

The forecast BS feeds the ratios used in Phase 2 analysis and in the t6 dashboard. Net debt = borrowings (current + non-current, including revolver) ± lease liabilities (state the choice) − cash and cash equivalents − other bank balances − liquid investments. Net debt/EBITDA must pair the debt definition with the matching EBITDA (pre- or post-Ind AS 116). ROCE = EBIT ÷ capital employed (equity + net debt, or total assets − current liabilities), on average balances. Gearing = net debt ÷ equity. Because these ratios come straight from linked BS lines, a plug or a wrong link distorts them directly — which is why ratio trends are also a check on the BS.

Common pitfalls

Plugging cash or "other liabilities"

Plugging cash or "other liabilities" so the model balances but is wrong

The mistake: cash is computed as total liabilities and equity less other assets, or a line such as "other current liabilities" or "other equity" absorbs the difference. Why it happens: deadline pressure, an inherited template, or a belief that a balancing model is a correct model. Why it is wrong: the check row becomes permanently zero and every downstream error is absorbed silently; CFS closing cash and BS cash diverge; net debt, interest income and valuation are wrong. Correct treatment: BS cash links only to CFS closing cash; every other line comes from a schedule or is held flat; fix the root cause of any imbalance. Detection: add a cash check (CFS closing − BS cash) and inspect the formula in the BS cash cell and in any "other" line; a "balancing figure" row or a formula subtracting totals is the giveaway.

Holding a BS line constant while its CFS line moves

Holding a BS line constant while its CFS line still moves (or vice versa)

The mistake: long-term provisions are held flat but the CFS still adds back a provision charge; investments are flat but "purchase of investments" grows with revenue; or a BS line moves via a schedule while the CFS row is zero. Why it happens: CFS rows copied from history with drivers, or BS lines simplified after the CFS was built. Why it is wrong: cash moves without a BS counterpart (or vice versa), producing an imbalance equal to the flow, usually cumulative. Correct treatment: tag each BS line in the mapping table (c03) as schedule-driven or held constant, and set the CFS rows of held-constant lines to zero. Detection: a growing check difference whose annual increment equals a CFS line's value; the mapping table's difference column localises it.

Linking to prior-year closing

Linking to prior-year closing instead of current-year closing

The mistake: a BS cell in column F (FY27E) links to the schedule's column E (FY26A) closing, or to the schedule's opening row for the year. Why it happens: misaligned columns between sheets, copying formulas across sheets with different start columns, or confusing opening and closing rows. Why it is wrong: the BS shows last year's balance while the CFS uses this year's movement, so the check difference equals the movement — varying year by year — and the BS lags reality. Correct treatment: align the year columns on every sheet (Week 13 rule), link to the closing row of the same column, and use the prior column only for opening balances inside corkscrews. Detection: Trace Precedents on BS cells, a column-header check row (=BS!F$5=WC!F$5) and a difference pattern that matches the line's movement each year.

Practice Questions

Q1. Link a forecast BS and prove it balances [L3]

FY27E schedule outputs (₹ crore): receivables 1,105; inventories 950; other operating CA 210; PPE 4,465; CWIP 554; intangibles 310; ROU 418; goodwill 850 (flat); associate investment 550; DTA (net) 52.6; other bank balances 0 (flat); payables 980; other operating CL 420; long-term provisions 220 (includes 10 OCI increase); current tax liability 15; borrowings: term loan 1,700 (next-year repayment 300), NCD 1,000 (bullet FY30), revolver 150; lease liability 437.8 (current portion 60.6); share capital 96.6; other equity 5,928.4; NCI 463.6; CFS closing cash 811.8. (a) Lay out the Schedule III-style BS with current/non-current splits. (b) Compute the check. (c) If the check is not zero, list three likely causes from this topic and how you would test each.

💡 Hint: Total all lines first; then think about which inputs here are not fully consistent with each other.

Q2. Three linking errors [L3]

In a model, (i) BS trade payables in each forecast year link to the WC schedule's prior column; (ii) the CFF debt lines are computed as the changes in non-current and current borrowings on the BS; (iii) the gratuity provision is held flat but the CFS adds back a provision charge of ₹12 crore per year. Payables are 840, 890, 960, 1,020, 1,100 for FY26A–FY30E; repayments due within 12 months are 300 every year, except 500 at 31 March 2029. For each error, predict its effect on the check row for FY27E–FY30E, its effect on presentation, and the fix.

Q3. Equity and NCI linkage [L2]

A 60%-owned subsidiary earns PAT of ₹200 crore and pays a dividend of ₹80 crore in FY28E. The group model's equity corkscrew credits retained earnings with 100% of group PAT, the NCI line is held flat, and the CFF shows dividends to NCI of ₹32 crore. Identify the errors, the effect on the check row and on equity attributable to owners, and correct the linkage.

Q4. Stress scenario BS review [L3]

In the Bear case for FY29E the BS shows: cash −170; revolver 400 (limit 400); a DTA of 45 replacing a DTL of 60; current borrowings including the full ₹1,800 crore term loan because a covenant is breached at year-end; and the check = 0. Write a review memo covering: whether each presentation is correct (refer to Ind AS 1.74 and the Indian carve-out), which dashboard flags should be on, and what the model is telling management about funding.
