Subtopic note

Borrowings, Revolver and Lease Liabilities on the Balance Sheet

Scope: Borrowings (current/non-current split), revolver and lease liabilities from financing schedules

Why it matters

Financing liabilities determine leverage, covenant headroom and the cost of capital — and they are the BS lines that credit committees, rating agencies and lenders read first. In a forecast model they also have the most moving parts: multiple tranches with different repayment profiles, a revolver that draws and repays with the cash waterfall, current maturities that must be reclassified each year under Schedule III, and Ind AS 116 lease liabilities split between current and non-current. Each must come from its Week 14 schedule, reconcile to CFF, and present correctly under Schedule III Division II. A model that shows ₹3,000 crore of "borrowings" in one line without the current/non-current split cannot produce a current ratio, and a model that treats reclassification as a cash flow will not balance.

Core notes

Schedule III Division II presentation

Under Non-current liabilities → Financial liabilities: (i) Borrowings, (ia) Lease liabilities, (ii) Trade payables, (iii) Other financial liabilities. Under Current liabilities → Financial liabilities: (i) Borrowings, (ia) Lease liabilities, (ii) Trade payables, (iii) Other financial liabilities. The 2021 amendment requires current maturities of long-term borrowings to be presented within current borrowings (previously they sat in other current financial liabilities), and lease liabilities as separate lines.

Ind AS 1.69 classifies a liability as current if it is due to be settled within twelve months after the reporting period, or the entity does not have a right at the end of the reporting period to defer settlement for at least twelve months (Ind AS 1.69(d)). The IASB has since amended IAS 1 on classification and covenants (effective internationally from 2024); check the latest MCA notification before assuming the corresponding Ind AS wording applies to a given year.

Breach of a loan covenant on or before the reporting date that makes the loan repayable on demand normally makes it current (Ind AS 1.74). India has a carve-out from IAS 1 here: under Ind AS 1.74 the liability is not classified as current if the lender agreed, after the reporting period but before approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach. (Under IAS 1 the waiver must be obtained by the reporting date.) This matters in stress cases where a covenant is breached.

Linking borrowings

The debt schedule (Week 14) gives, per tranche, opening balance, drawdowns, repayments, closing balance and the repayment due in the next 12 months. The BS links:

\[ \text{Current borrowings}_t = \text{ST facilities}_t + \text{Revolver}_t + \text{Repayments due in year } t+1 \]

\[ \text{Non-current borrowings}_t = \text{Total LT debt}_t - \text{Repayments due in year } t+1 \]

BS!F38  =Debt!F25-Debt!G18          ' Non-current borrowings = closing LT debt − next year's scheduled repayment
BS!F44  =Debt!F30+Debt!F35+Debt!G18 ' Current borrowings = CP/WCDL + revolver + current maturities

Note the reference to the next year's repayment (column G) — that is the one place where a forward column is correct. For the final forecast year, add a repayment input for the year beyond the horizon (or the schedule's year-6 maturity) so the split does not collapse.

The total of current + non-current borrowings always equals total closing debt, so the reclassification has no cash effect (c04): the CFS uses the change in total debt by tranche (drawdowns and repayments).

Revolver

The revolver closing balance comes from the cash waterfall: draw when cash before revolver falls below minimum cash, repay first when there is surplus, subject to the limit. It is current (cash credit and WCDL are repayable on demand or within 12 months). The BS link is to the revolver closing row; the CFF line is the change in that row. Do not compute the revolver from the BS (s01).

Lease liabilities

The lease schedule gives opening liability + new leases + interest − payments = closing. The current portion is the principal to be repaid in the next 12 months:

\[ \text{Current lease liability}_t = \text{Payments}_{t+1} - \text{Interest}_{t+1} \]

BS!F39  =Leases!F30-BS!F45          ' Non-current lease liabilities
BS!F45  =Leases!G25-Leases!G18      ' Current: next year's payment less next year's interest

Many forecast models simplify by holding the current portion at a historical % of the total lease liability; acceptable if documented, because the split is presentation only.

Worked illustration (₹ crore, 31 March 2027)

Term loan closing 1,700, next-year repayment 300; NCD 1,000 bullet in FY30; CP 0; revolver 150; lease liability 437.8, next-year payment 100 with interest 39.4.

BS line Amount
Non-current borrowings (1,700 − 300 + 1,000) 2,400.0
Current borrowings (150 + 300) 450.0
Non-current lease liabilities (437.8 − 60.6) 377.2
Current lease liabilities (100 − 39.4) 60.6
Total financing liabilities 3,287.8

Check: 2,400 + 450 = 2,850 = 1,700 + 1,000 + 150 ✓; 377.2 + 60.6 = 437.8 ✓. On 31 March 2029 the NCD (bullet FY30) moves entirely into current borrowings.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Covenant breach at the reporting date in a stress case: the whole loan becomes current under Ind AS 1.74 unless the Indian carve-out applies (waiver after year-end but before approval of the accounts) — a model can flag this rather than reclassify automatically.
  • Interest accrued but not due on borrowings is an other current financial liability, not borrowings; it moves with interest paid in CFF.
  • Unamortised upfront fees under EIR reduce the borrowing carrying amount; small, usually ignored in forecasts.
  • Foreign-currency borrowings are restated at closing rates; forecasts usually hold FX constant.
  • Preference shares classified as financial liabilities under Ind AS 32 (redeemable) sit in borrowings.
  • Cash credit netted with cash in some historical CFS presentations — align with the BS definition (t2/s05).

Key takeaways

  • Borrowings link from the debt schedule: non-current = total LT debt − next-year repayment; current = ST facilities + revolver + current maturities.
  • The revolver links from the cash-waterfall row and is current.
  • Lease liabilities link from the lease schedule, split into current (next-year principal) and non-current.
  • Current/non-current splits are presentation only; the CFS uses total movements by instrument.
  • Watch the last forecast year: provide next-year repayment data so the split is complete.

Practice Questions

Q1. Current/non-current split [L1]

At 31 March 2028 term-loan A has a closing balance of ₹1,400 crore with ₹350 crore due in FY29; term-loan B ₹600 crore repayable in equal instalments of ₹150 crore; NCD ₹1,000 crore bullet in FY30; CP outstanding ₹200 crore; revolver ₹0. Compute current and non-current borrowings.

Q2. Reclassification and the CFS [L2]

A junior's CFS computes "repayment of long-term borrowings" as the change in non-current borrowings, and "proceeds from short-term borrowings" as the change in current borrowings. Using Q1 data and a 31 March 2027 position of non-current ₹2,900 crore and current ₹500 crore (with ₹450 crore of actual repayments and no new borrowing in FY28), show what the junior's CFF lines are, whether total CFF is right, and why the presentation is still wrong.

💡 Hint: Total debt movements are right only if both lines are read together; Ind AS 7.21 requires gross cash flows.

Q3. Lease liability current portion [L2]

Lease liability at 31 March 2028 is ₹820 crore; FY29 payments ₹190 crore; IBR 9% on opening balance; no new leases. Compute the current and non-current lease liabilities at 31 March 2028.

Q4. Covenant breach presentation [L3]

In the Bear case, net debt/EBITDA reaches 4.2× at 31 March 2029 against a 3.5× covenant tested on that date; the lender's waiver is received on 20 May 2029. Explain the Ind AS 1 classification of the ₹1,800 crore term loan at 31 March 2029, the disclosure needed, and how you would represent this in the model's BS and dashboard without breaking the check.
