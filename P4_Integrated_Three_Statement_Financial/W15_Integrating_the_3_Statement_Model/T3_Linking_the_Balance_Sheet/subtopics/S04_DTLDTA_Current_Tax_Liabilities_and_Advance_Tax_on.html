Subtopic note

DTL/DTA, Current Tax Liabilities and Advance Tax on the BS

Scope: DTL/DTA, current tax liabilities/advance tax from tax schedule

Why it matters

Tax balances are small relative to total assets for most companies, but they are a disproportionate source of imbalances because they sit at the intersection of the P&L (tax expense), the CFS (taxes paid, deferred tax) and two or three BS lines (deferred tax liability or asset, current tax liability, advance tax/income-tax asset). In India the presentation adds complexity: Schedule III shows deferred tax assets (net) and deferred tax liabilities (net) separately under non-current items; "current tax liabilities (net)" under current liabilities; and "income tax assets (net)" or advance tax, often non-current because refunds for past assessment years can take years. Linking each line from the Week 14 tax schedule — and making the CFS deduct cash taxes paid rather than tax expense — is what keeps the triad consistent.

Core notes

The tax schedule outputs

The Week 14 tax schedule produces, per year:

Output Used by
Current tax expense IS
Deferred tax charge/(credit) through P&L IS; CFS (PAT start)
Deferred tax through OCI OCI, equity (other equity)
Closing net DTL (or DTA) BS
Advance tax paid in the year (15/45/75/100% instalments) Tax schedule; CFS taxes paid
Closing current tax liability (provision − advance tax), or income-tax asset BS
Income taxes paid (net of refunds) CFS (PBT start)

Deferred tax on the BS

Ind AS 12 requires deferred tax on temporary differences between carrying amounts and tax bases (Ind AS 12.15, 12.24). The schedule computes, for each category:

\[ \text{DTL}_t = t \times (\text{Book NBV}_t - \text{Tax WDV}_t) + \sum t \times \text{other taxable differences} - \sum t \times \text{deductible differences} \]

with deductible differences including gratuity/leave provisions (Sec 43B), ECL allowance, lease liability net of ROU, and tax losses carried forward (recognised only if probable future taxable profit exists, Ind AS 12.34). The net position is presented as DTL (net) or DTA (net) — offsetting is required only when there is a legally enforceable right to set off and the balances relate to the same taxation authority and taxable entity (Ind AS 12.74). In consolidated statements, one subsidiary's DTA cannot be netted against another's DTL, so both lines may appear.

Movement reconciliation:

\[ \text{Net DTL}_t = \text{Net DTL}_{t-1} + \text{Deferred tax charge (P\&L)}_t + \text{Deferred tax on OCI}_t \pm \text{Acquisitions}_t \]

The P&L component is the CFO add-back (PAT start) — or is absorbed by starting from PBT (Indian format). The OCI component changes the DTL and other equity together with no CFS line.

Current tax liability and advance tax

Indian companies pay advance tax during the year (Sec 208–211: 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December, 100% by 15 March). At 31 March, the provision for current tax for the year is largely offset by advance tax paid; the net (plus any past-year disputed amounts) is presented as "current tax liabilities (net)" or "income tax assets (net)".

\[ \text{CTL}_t = \text{CTL}_{t-1} + \text{Current tax}_t - \text{Taxes paid}_t \]

\[ \text{Taxes paid}_t = \text{Advance tax}_t + \text{Settlement of prior-year balance}_t - \text{Refunds}_t \]

A simple forecast assumption: advance tax = 100% × current tax for the year (so CTL stays at the last actual), or a fixed percentage (e.g., 95%) with the balance paid in the following year. Long-standing income-tax assets (refunds pending for past assessment years, deposits under protest) are usually held flat.

BS!F40  =MAX(0,Tax!F80)              ' Deferred tax liabilities (net)
BS!F22  =MAX(0,-Tax!F80)             ' Deferred tax assets (net)
BS!F49  =Tax!F90                     ' Current tax liabilities (net)
BS!F23  =Tax!F95                     ' Income tax assets (net) — usually held flat
CFS!F22 =-Tax!F70                    ' Income taxes paid

The MAX(0, …) pair lets a net position flip between liability and asset without breaking the BS: the CFS should use the change in the net position (DTL − DTA) to avoid errors when it flips.

Worked illustration (₹ crore, FY27E)

Opening net DTA 40; deferred tax credit through P&L 10.1; deferred tax on OCI (actuarial loss) 2.5 credit; current tax 306.0; advance tax paid 291.0; opening CTL 15, settled in FY27E.

  • Closing net DTA = 40 + 10.1 + 2.5 = 52.6.
  • Taxes paid = 291.0 + 15 = 306.0; closing CTL = 15 + 306.0 − 306.0 = 15.0 (the FY27 balance of 306.0 − 291.0 = 15.0).
  • CFS (PBT start): taxes paid (306.0). PAT start: deferred tax credit (−10.1) and ΔCTL 0.
  • The OCI deferred tax of 2.5 increases DTA and other equity; no CFS line.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Flip from DTL to DTA in loss years (stress tests): ensure the CFS uses the net movement and the BS splits with MAX/MIN, else the model breaks only in Bear cases.
  • MAT credit (old-regime companies) is a deferred tax asset in India's Ind AS practice (per ICAI guidance), used against future normal tax; it disappears on switching to Sec 115BAA unless utilised.
  • Tax on dividends (DDT) no longer applies since FY21; dividends are taxed in shareholders' hands. TDS deducted by the company is a liability until deposited, not a tax balance of the company.
  • Uncertain tax treatments (Appendix C to Ind AS 12) may add provisions; forecast as flat.
  • Rate changes remeasure all deferred tax; a one-off charge/credit through P&L — not modelled unless legislated.
  • Advance tax timing: holding CTL flat implies taxes paid = current tax every year; a growing company with a constant % unpaid shows a rising CTL and a small CFO benefit.

Key takeaways

  • DTL/DTA, current tax liability and income-tax assets all link from the tax schedule's closing balances.
  • Net DTL moves by deferred tax through P&L (CFO) and through OCI (equity, no cash).
  • CTL moves by current tax less taxes paid; taxes paid from advance-tax assumptions drive the Indian-format CFS line.
  • Use MAX/MIN splits on the BS and net movements in the CFS so a DTL/DTA flip does not break the check.
  • Hold legacy income-tax assets and disputed balances flat with no CFS movement.

Practice Questions

Q1. Deferred tax roll-forward [L1]

Opening net DTL ₹180 crore. FY28E: book NBV of PPE ₹4,465 crore; tax WDV ₹3,520 crore; deductible temporary differences (gratuity, leave, ECL) ₹160 crore; rate 25.168%. Compute closing net DTL and the deferred tax charge through P&L, assuming no OCI items.

Q2. Advance tax and CTL [L2]

Current tax for FY28E is ₹420 crore; advance tax paid in instalments totals ₹395 crore; the FY27E balance of ₹18 crore is paid in June 2027; a refund of ₹12 crore for AY 2022-23 is received with interest of ₹2 crore. Compute taxes paid for the CFS, closing CTL, and state where the refund interest appears.

💡 Hint: Interest on refunds is other income, not a reduction of taxes paid.

Q3. DTL/DTA flip in a Bear case [L2]

In the Bear case the net DTL of ₹60 crore becomes a net DTA of ₹45 crore at 31 March 2029 because of tax losses. The BS links DTL = Tax!F80 and DTA = 0 (held flat), and the CFS adds back the deferred tax charge from the tax schedule. Describe what the BS and check row show, and the corrected linking.

Q4. Consolidated offsetting [L3]

The parent has a DTL of ₹220 crore and a subsidiary has a DTA of ₹70 crore arising from unused tax losses. An analyst nets them to ₹150 crore DTL. Discuss whether Ind AS 12.74 permits offsetting, the effect on presented totals and on the BS check, and whether the subsidiary's DTA should be recognised at all given its loss history.
