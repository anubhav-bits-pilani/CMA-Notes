Subtopic note

Cash from the CFS as the Sole Balancing Mechanism

Scope: Cash linked from CFS closing cash as the sole balancing mechanism

Why it matters

In a properly integrated model, nothing is forced to make the balance sheet balance. Every non-cash line comes from a schedule; equity comes from its corkscrew; and cash — the one line left — comes from the cash flow statement, which has itself been built from the movements in all the other lines. If the CFS is complete, the BS balances as a consequence, not as a constraint. This is the architectural idea behind every bank, Big 4 and PE model, and it is what lets the check row be a genuine test: if cash were computed as "whatever makes it balance", the check would always be zero and would prove nothing. Interviewers frequently ask "how does your model balance?" — the right answer is "cash comes from the CFS, every other line from a schedule, and the check row tests that the CFS captured every movement".

Core notes

The logic

Write the balance sheet as:

\[ \text{Cash}_t + \text{NCA}_t = \text{L}_t + \text{E}_t \]

where \( \text{NCA} \) is all non-cash assets, \( \text{L} \) all liabilities and \( \text{E} \) total equity (including NCI). Taking first differences:

\[ \Delta\text{Cash}_t = \Delta\text{L}_t + \Delta\text{E}_t - \Delta\text{NCA}_t \]

The CFS computes the right-hand side line by line (each CFO/CFI/CFF line is a BS movement or a non-cash P&L item whose BS counterpart is in \( \text{L} \), \( \text{E} \) or \( \text{NCA} \)). So if the opening BS balances and the CFS captures every movement exactly once with the correct sign, the closing BS balances. The BS check is then a test of CFS completeness.

Implementation

Step Cell logic
1. Historical BS Hardcoded from annual report; checks = 0 in every historical year
2. Forecast non-cash assets Linked from WC, PPE/CWIP, intangibles, ROU, tax, investments schedules
3. Forecast liabilities Linked from debt, revolver, lease, tax, provisions schedules
4. Forecast equity Share capital, other equity, NCI from equity corkscrew
5. Forecast cash =CFS!F47 (CFS closing cash and cash equivalents)
6. Check =ROUND(Total_Assets-Total_Equity-Total_Liabilities,3) 
BS!F12   =CFS!F47                              ' Cash and cash equivalents
BS!F13   =Treasury!F20                         ' Other bank balances (separate schedule)
BS!F30   =SUM(BS!F8:F29)                       ' Total assets
BS!F60   =SUM(BS!F35:F59)                      ' Total equity and liabilities
BS!F62   =ROUND(BS!F30-BS!F60,3)               ' Check (0 every year)

Why "sole" mechanism

Cash must be the only line that is not driven by its own schedule, and it must not be a residual. Three common violations:

  1. Cash as residual (=Total L&E − Other assets) — the BS always balances, the CFS closing cash and BS cash diverge silently, and errors are hidden (c02).
  2. Revolver as residual (=Total assets − Other L&E) — the same disguise on the liability side; the revolver should come from the cash waterfall in the debt schedule, which uses CFS cash before revolver.
  3. "Other liabilities" or "other equity" plugs — a balancing figure parked in a line nobody looks at (topic pitfall).

The revolver is not a contradiction of the rule: it is computed in the debt schedule from pre-financing cash flow and minimum cash, appears in CFF, and the resulting closing cash still flows from the CFS. Cash is still the sole balancing mechanism — the revolver merely ensures cash is not negative (c05).

Running example (₹ crore, FY27E)

CFS closing cash 811.8 (t2/c05). BS: cash 811.8; other bank balances 0 (flat); all other assets from schedules total 9,640.2; liabilities from schedules 4,620.0; equity 5,832.0.

\[ \text{Total assets} = 811.8 + 9{,}640.2 = 10{,}452.0 = 4{,}620.0 + 5{,}832.0 \]

Check = 0.000. If a modeller now overwrote BS cash with 850 "to match management guidance", the check would show +38.2 — the test is working.

Circularity and the breaker

Cash feeds interest income (average cash) and the revolver; interest feeds PAT; PAT feeds CFO and cash. With the circuit breaker on, interest on average balances is zero (or opening balance), the loop is cut, and the BS should still balance — because the CFS is still complete. A model that balances only with circularity on (or only off) has a link error, not a circularity problem.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • First forecast year: opening cash must equal last historical BS cash (same definition). A difference breaks year 1 and every year after by the same constant amount.
  • Other bank balances and liquid investments are separate BS lines driven by a treasury schedule (or held flat), with movements in CFI — they are not alternative balancing lines.
  • Negative cash: if the revolver limit binds, cash can go negative; the model should still balance (cash is negative on the asset side) but flag the funding gap. Do not move negative cash to borrowings by formula unless you also model the overdraft in CFF.
  • Historical years: if historicals do not balance (rounding in the annual report to ₹ lakh or crore), fix with a documented rounding adjustment in history only — never in forecast.
  • Consolidated vs standalone: cash in the BS is consolidated; trapped cash in subsidiaries is still in the line, but the dashboard may separately show parent-level cash.

Key takeaways

  • BS cash = CFS closing cash; every other BS line comes from a schedule or is held flat.
  • The BS balances as a consequence of a complete CFS; the check row tests that completeness.
  • Never compute cash, the revolver or any "other" line as a residual.
  • The revolver comes from the debt-schedule waterfall; it keeps cash non-negative without breaking the rule.
  • The model must balance with the circuit breaker on and off.

Practice Questions

Q1. Explain the balancing logic [L1]

In no more than 150 words, explain to a non-finance manager why your Project 4 balance sheet balances, and why the check row would be useless if cash were the residual.

Q2. Cash overwrite [L1]

FY28E CFS closing cash is ₹1,072 crore. A user overwrites BS cash with ₹1,000 crore. State the check-row result (amount and sign as Assets − Equity − Liabilities) and the cash-check result.

Q3. Revolver as residual [L2]

A model computes the revolver as =MAX(0,BS!F30-SUM(BS!F35:F58)) (total assets less all other liabilities and equity), and the CFF revolver line as the change in that cell. Explain why this is circular in logic, what it hides, and how to rebuild it from the debt-schedule cash waterfall.

💡 Hint: Ask what drives the revolver in reality: the cash shortfall before financing.

Q4. Breaker on vs off [L2]

With the circuit breaker off, all five forecast years balance. With it on (interest on average balances set to zero), FY27E–FY31E show check differences of 31.4, 33.0, 34.9, 36.2, 38.0. The IS interest-income row and the CFS PBT-start reversal of interest income both link to the breaker-switched cell on the Debt sheet, but the CFI "interest received" line links to a raw yield × average cash row that ignores the breaker. Explain what is happening and how you would fix it.
