Subtopic note

Lines Held Flat with Zero CFS Movement

Scope: Lines held flat (goodwill, investments, long-term provisions) with zero CFS movement

Why it matters

Not every balance sheet line deserves a driver. Goodwill from past acquisitions, strategic or long-term investments, long-term provisions, deferred government grants, security deposits, non-current tax assets and a dozen small "other" lines are typically held constant at their last actual value. This is good modelling: it keeps the model simple and transparent, and forecasting such lines usually adds noise rather than insight. But holding a line flat is an assertion with consequences — it means no cash flow and no P&L effect related to that line. If a CFS line still moves (say, "purchase of investments" grows with revenue) while the BS line is flat, or the BS line drifts while the CFS shows nothing, the model is out of balance by exactly that amount. The discipline is to treat "held flat" as a deliberate tag on the BS–CFS mapping table, with both sides consistent.

Core notes

Candidates for holding flat

BS line Why hold flat Conditions/caveats
Goodwill Not amortised (Ind AS 103/36); impairment not forecast No acquisitions planned; no impairment expected
Other intangibles from acquisitions with indefinite life (brands) Not amortised Finite-life acquired intangibles amortise — use the intangibles schedule
Investments in associates/JVs (if immaterial) Share of profit and dividends small Then also set share of profit = 0 and dividends received = 0
Non-current investments (strategic equity, FVOCI) Fair value changes not forecast No FV gain/loss in OCI; no purchases/sales
Current investments (liquid MFs) Treasury policy constant Treasury yield must be cash income (CFI), not FV accretion
Long-term provisions (gratuity, leave, warranties, decommissioning) Net movement small; actuarial changes not forecast Service cost = payments assumed; unwinding of discount nil or offset
Security deposits, long-term loans to employees Immaterial, stable
Non-current tax assets (refunds pending), deposits under protest Timing unknowable
Deferred government grants Amortisation offset by new grants or immaterial If amortised, the P&L credit must be removed in CFO
Other bank balances, earmarked accounts Policy stable Or forecast via treasury schedule with CFI line

The consistency rule

For every held-flat line \( B \):

\[ B_t = B_{t-1} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{all CFS lines mapped to } B = 0 \text{ and all P\&L items generated by } B = 0 \text{ (or offset)} \]

Implementation:

BS!F20  =BS!E20                    ' Goodwill held flat (prior column link, not hardcode)
CFS!F30 =0                         ' Acquisition / investment flows — explicit zero, or remove row
Map!F20 ="Held constant"           ' Mapping table tag

Linking to the prior column (=E20) rather than typing the number means a restated historical carries through. Leave related CFS rows present but zero (so the structure is visible), or delete them; do not leave them driven by a formula.

P&L items that depend on held-flat lines

  • Share of associates — if the investment is held flat, the P&L share must be zero, or the share and the dividends received must be equal (investment unchanged by construction).
  • Treasury income — if current investments are flat, the yield must be cash income (interest/dividends received in CFI or redemption gains reinvested at same carrying value). FV gains on growth MF units would increase the investment.
  • Provisions — if the gratuity provision is flat, the P&L charge (in employee cost) must be assumed to equal cash contributions/payments; otherwise the provision would grow.
  • Government grant amortisation — if deferred grant income is flat, no amortisation credit should be in other income.

Worked illustration (₹ crore)

A model holds goodwill at 850, investments in a small associate at 45, non-current investments at 120 and long-term provisions at 210. Two errors are found:

  1. The IS shows share of associate profit of 6 per year (copied from history) while the investment is flat and no dividend is modelled. PAT includes 6; the CFS (Indian format starts at PBT; associate share presented after tax) neither reverses nor receives anything — equity rises 6 more than net assets: check difference −6 each year (liabilities + equity exceed assets), cumulative −6, −12, −18…
  2. The CFS shows "purchase of investments" of 2% of revenue (₹30–40 crore) while non-current investments are flat: cash falls by that amount with no asset increase: check difference −30, −64, −102… (cumulative, growing).

Fix: either drive the associate investment with share of profit (and forecast dividends), or set the P&L share to zero; delete the investment purchase driver or link the BS investment line to a schedule.

When not to hold flat

  • Goodwill when an acquisition is in the plan — add the acquisition block.
  • Long-term provisions for a company with large, growing obligations (mining restoration, nuclear decommissioning) — model the unwinding (finance cost) and the provision corkscrew.
  • Investments for a treasury-driven company where income is a large share of PBT — model the treasury schedule.
  • Any line > 5% of total assets whose historical growth tracks revenue — consider a driver.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cumulative difference pattern: a flow error on a held-flat line produces a difference that grows (it accumulates), while a single opening-balance slip is constant (t4).
  • Dividend from associate in CFI with a flat investment and zero share of profit implies the dividend reduces the investment — conflict; choose one.
  • OCI on FVOCI investments not forecast — consistent with flat balances.
  • Deferred revenue/grant lines in infra companies may be large; check before holding flat.
  • Historical restatements: link flat lines to the last actual cell so restatement flows.

Key takeaways

  • Holding a line flat asserts zero cash flow and zero related P&L for that line.
  • Tag every flat line "held constant" in the BS–CFS mapping table, and set its CFS rows to zero.
  • Link flat lines to the prior column, not a typed number.
  • Make dependent P&L items (associate share, treasury FV gains, grant amortisation, provision charges) consistent with the flat balance.
  • Revisit flat lines when they are material or when the plan includes acquisitions, new provisions or treasury changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify lines [L1]

For an NSE-listed specialty chemicals company, decide which of the following you would hold flat in Project 4 and why: goodwill ₹310 crore; non-current FVOCI equity investment ₹95 crore; liquid MFs ₹1,200 crore (income 7% of PBT); gratuity provision ₹140 crore; capital advances ₹260 crore (large expansion under way); security deposits ₹18 crore.

Q2. Predict the imbalance [L2]

Long-term provisions are held flat at ₹210 crore, but the CFS (Indian format) adds back "provision for employee benefits" of ₹12 crore, ₹13 crore and ₹14 crore in FY27E–FY29E because a non-cash add-back row was copied from history. Predict the check-row differences and explain the fix.

💡 Hint: An add-back with no BS movement creates cash out of nothing — and the effect accumulates.

Q3. Associate held flat [L2]

An associate investment of ₹45 crore is held flat. The model shows share of profit ₹6 crore in the IS (after tax) and dividends received ₹6 crore in CFI, and the Indian-format CFS does not adjust for the share. Determine whether the model balances, and whether the presentation is correct.

Q4. Materiality review [L3]

A reviewer argues that all of the following should be modelled rather than held flat: goodwill (planned ₹600 crore acquisition in FY28E), decommissioning provision of ₹900 crore with 8% discount rate, and deferred government grants of ₹400 crore amortised over 20 years. For each, describe the schedule you would add, the P&L, CFS and BS lines affected, and the check you would add to prove consistency.
