How this week fits

Week 14 sits in Phase 4 (Integrated Three-Statement Financial Modelling & Valuation). Week 13 gave you the model architecture, formatting discipline, historical normalisation, scenario switch and the operating drivers — store additions, revenue per sq ft, COGS and opex builds for an Indian listed company such as Trent or Pidilite. Those drivers produce a P&L down to EBITDA but no Balance Sheet movement and no cash. This week adds the supporting schedules that turn operating assumptions into balance sheet lines and cash flows: working capital (days-based), PPE and depreciation (layered corkscrews with CWIP), debt and interest (tranches, cash waterfall, revolver and sweep), interest circularity management, income tax and deferred tax, and the equity corkscrew.

Prerequisites are the Phase 1 technical accounting (Ind AS 12, 16, 109, 115, 116), Phase 2 ratio analysis (working-capital days, ROCE, coverage ratios) and Phase 3 Excel skills (named ranges, SUMPRODUCT, iterative calculation, basic VBA). Week 15 integrates these schedules into a balancing three-statement model with an indirect cash flow statement and a debugging routine (where the circuit breaker matters); Week 16 uses the outputs — NOPAT at 25.17%, D&A, capex, ΔNWC and net debt — in a DCF.

Refresh before starting

Corkscrew logic

Corkscrew logic: Opening + Additions − Reductions = Closing

Every balance-sheet schedule this week is a corkscrew. Opening balance of year \( t \) is the closing of year \( t-1 \) (a link, not a formula re-derivation). Additions and reductions are each driven by one cause and one source tab: capex and depreciation for PPE; draws, mandatory repayments and sweeps for debt; new leases, interest and payments for leases; PAT and dividends for retained earnings; current tax and payments for tax payable. Keep the four rows visually aligned (opening, plus, minus, closing), use the same column structure on every tab, and add a check row that recomputes the closing from the components. Corkscrews make errors visible: if a closing balance does not equal the next opening, the schedule is broken.

Ind AS measurement basics for the schedules

Ind AS 12, 16, 109 and 116 measurement basics

  • Ind AS 16: PPE at cost (or revaluation), depreciation of the depreciable amount (cost − residual) over useful life from the date available for use; annual review of life, residual and method; componentisation of significant parts.
  • Ind AS 109: borrowings at amortised cost using the effective interest rate (upfront fees amortised); trade receivables and contract assets with lifetime ECL under the simplified approach.
  • Ind AS 116: lessee recognises lease liability (PV of fixed payments at the implicit rate or IBR) and ROU asset; ROU depreciated over the shorter of lease term and useful life; interest on liability; exemptions for short-term and low-value leases; variable payments not linked to an index are expensed.
  • Ind AS 12: current tax on taxable profit; deferred tax on temporary differences between carrying amount and tax base at enacted or substantively enacted rates; DTA only to the extent probable future taxable profit is available; the 2023 amendment removed the initial-recognition exemption for transactions creating equal taxable and deductible differences (leases, decommissioning).

Income-tax depreciation, Sec 115BAA and loss set-off

Income-tax depreciation blocks, Sec 115BAA conditions and loss set-off rules

  • Block-wise WDV (Sec 32 read with Rule 5): plant and machinery 15%, buildings 10% (residential 5%), furniture and fittings 10%, computers and software 40%, intangibles 25%. Depreciation on the block's opening WDV plus additions less sale proceeds; only half the rate on additions put to use for less than 180 days in the year of acquisition.
  • Sec 115BAA: domestic company tax at 22% plus 10% surcharge plus 4% cess = 25.168% (rounded to 25.17%); MAT under Sec 115JB does not apply; no specified deductions/incentives (e.g., additional depreciation under Sec 32(1)(iia), Sec 35AD, Sec 10AA, most Chapter VI-A deductions other than Sec 80JJAA and 80M); losses and unabsorbed depreciation attributable to those deductions cannot be set off; the option, once exercised by the return due date, is irrevocable.
  • Loss set-off: business loss carried forward for eight assessment years against business income only, provided the return of the loss year was filed in time (Sec 72, Sec 80); unabsorbed depreciation carried forward indefinitely and set off against any income (Sec 32(2)); Sec 79 can lapse losses of closely held companies on a change in shareholding of more than 49%.
  • Date-sensitivity: the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections; verify mappings before modelling FY27 onwards.

Excel mechanics

Excel iterative calculation, SUMPRODUCT, MIN/MAX capping logic

  • Iterative calculation: File → Options → Formulas → Enable iterative calculation; typical settings 100 iterations, maximum change 0.001. It is an application-level setting — it follows the first workbook opened in the session — so do not rely on it being on or off.
  • SUMPRODUCT multiplies arrays element-wise and sums; Boolean tests inside it act as 1/0 filters, e.g. =SUMPRODUCT(Capex_Row*(Year>=Vintage_Row)*(Year<Vintage_Row+Life))/Life for a depreciation waterfall. Arrays must be the same size; year labels must be numeric.
  • MIN/MAX capping: MIN(x, cap) imposes a ceiling, MAX(x, floor) a floor, and MIN(MAX(x, floor), cap) both — the pattern behind the revolver, sweep, depreciation cap and loss set-off.

Cross-cutting concepts

Every schedule feeds three places

Every schedule feeds three places: P&L charge, BS closing balance, CFS movement

Each supporting schedule exists to produce exactly three outputs, and a schedule is not finished until all three are linked. The PPE schedule produces depreciation (P&L), closing net block and CWIP (BS) and cash capex and disposal proceeds (CFS). The working-capital schedule produces no P&L charge of its own (the P&L items that drive it are already in revenue and costs) but produces closing balances and the −ΔNWC line in CFO; the ECL charge, if modelled, is its P&L element. The debt schedule produces finance cost (P&L), closing borrowings split current/non-current (BS) and draws, repayments and interest paid (CFS). The tax schedule produces current and deferred tax expense, closing tax payable and DTL, and cash tax paid with the ΔDTL add-back. Leases produce ROU depreciation and lease interest, ROU and lease liability, and lease payments in CFF. Building a "three-place" link map — a small table at the top of each tab listing each output, its destination cell and a check — makes Week 15 integration mechanical: when the Balance Sheet does not balance, you look for the output that reached only one or two of its three destinations.

Link the first forecast opening balance

First forecast opening balance = last actual closing balance via link, never retyped

The opening balance of the first forecast year in every schedule must be a direct cell link to the last actual Balance Sheet (e.g., =Hist!H45), not a typed number and not a re-computation. Retyping creates three risks: a transcription error that silently unbalances the model; a stale value when historicals are restated (a revised annual report, an Ind AS restatement, a reclassification); and a break in the audit trail that reviewers rely on. For multi-line schedules — gross block and accumulated depreciation by class, each debt tranche, tax WDV by block — link each component, then add a check that the sum of schedule openings equals the Balance Sheet total. Where the opening must be adjusted (e.g., to separate current maturities from borrowings, or to reclassify supplier finance as debt), do it in a visible adjustment row with a note, not by overwriting the link. The same principle applies within the forecast: every opening equals the prior closing by link, and the formula is identical across all forecast columns.

Cash waterfall order

Cash waterfall order: CFO → capex → mandatory debt → dividends → revolver → sweep

The waterfall allocates cash in order of obligation. Operating cash flow is generated first; capex (committed projects and maintenance) is spent next because the business cannot run without it; mandatory debt service is contractual and missing it is a default; dividends, once declared, are obligations (and Sec 123 restricts them to distributable profits); only then does the revolver either fund a deficit or absorb a surplus; and whatever remains, after minimum cash, may be swept to term debt by seniority. Reversing the order produces classic errors: sweeping before mandatory repayment double-uses cash; ignoring minimum cash leaves the company with no operating buffer; drawing the revolver for dividends without a lock-up hides an aggressive payout. Some structures alter the order — dividend lock-ups when covenants fail, capex deferral switches in downturns, equity cures — but each change must be explicit and documented. In Excel, the waterfall is a vertical block in priority order with each line linked from its source schedule; the revolver and sweep formulas (MIN/MAX logic) read only from this block, and closing cash flows to the CFS and Balance Sheet.

Lease liability corkscrew

Lease liability corkscrew: Opening + New leases + Interest − Lease payments = Closing

Under Ind AS 116 the lease liability accretes interest at the discount rate (the rate implicit in the lease or the incremental borrowing rate at commencement) and is reduced by lease payments:

\[ \text{LL}_t = \text{LL}_{t-1} + \text{New leases}_t + r \times \text{LL}^{\text{basis}}_t - \text{Lease payments}_t \pm \text{Remeasurements}_t \]

New leases are non-cash (driven by store additions × fixed rent × annuity factor); interest is a finance cost; payments split into principal (CFF) and interest (CFF or CFO per policy, Ind AS 7.17(c), Ind AS 116.50). The paired ROU corkscrew (opening + new leases − depreciation) feeds D&A. For a retailer, keep this corkscrew separate from bank debt: the rate is not the bank rate, it cannot be prepaid by a sweep, and most bank covenants exclude it. In the waterfall, lease payments are a committed use alongside mandatory debt. Fixed versus variable rent must be split — revenue-share rents stay in opex and EBITDA. Interest on the opening liability is a reasonable simplification; for large mid-year additions use a half-year factor on new leases.

Linking schedules to Week 13 drivers with a breaker

Exercise: link schedules to Week 13 drivers (store adds → capex, revenue → DSO, COGS → DIO/DPO) with breaker

The exercise integrates this week's schedules with the Week 13 operating model through links, never re-entry. Store additions from the revenue tab drive growth capex (stores × sq ft × capex per sq ft), new leases (stores × fixed rent × annuity factor), and inventory if a per-store driver is used. Revenue drives receivables via DSO and statutory dues as a % of revenue; COGS drives inventory via DIO and payables via DPO (or purchases, computed from COGS and the inventory change). Opex drives accruals. The PPE schedule's book NBV and the tax-block WDV meet in the deferred-tax schedule. The debt schedule reads CFO and CFI from the integrated cash flow and returns interest to the P&L — a loop. A named circuit-breaker cell (Circ: 1 = average-balance interest, 0 = opening-balance or zero) must exist before the loop is closed, so that a trapped error can be flushed by setting the breaker to 0, fixing the source and switching back. Test by flexing store additions under the scenario switch and confirming capex, leases, NWC, debt and tax all respond.

Required checks

  • Every first-forecast opening equals the last actual closing (by link) — check row per schedule.
  • Sum of WC CFS lines = −ΔNWC.
  • NBV ≥ 0 and AccDep ≤ depreciable cost by class; no depreciation on land.
  • Revolver 0 ≤ balance ≤ limit; every tranche ≥ 0; closing cash ≥ minimum (or funding-gap flag).
  • Interest convergence: difference between successive iterations (or vs a paste-values row) < ₹0.01 crore.
  • MSE DPO ≤ 45 days; DTL = (book NBV − tax WDV) × 25.17% reconciled.
  • Breaker test: model calculates with Circ = 0 and = 1 without errors.

Practice-ready checklist

  • I can forecast NWC by days and push ΔNWC to CFS with correct sign Self-test: from a blank sheet, forecast receivables, inventory and payables for three years from given days, then link line-wise CFS movements and confirm the check SUM(CFS WC lines) + ΔNWC = 0; flex DSO by +5 days and confirm CFO falls by about revenue × 5 ÷ 365.
  • I can build a vintage depreciation waterfall that stops at full depreciation Self-test: write the SUMPRODUCT waterfall for ten years of capex with a 5-year life, verify against a manual triangle, and confirm each vintage's cumulative depreciation equals its cost and that NBV never goes negative after capex stops.
  • I can build a revolver and sweep that never breach limits or go negative Self-test: run a cash-flow series with deep deficits and large surpluses; confirm RCF stays between zero and the limit, the term loan never goes negative, the sweep follows mandatory repayment, and a funding-gap flag appears when the limit binds.
  • I can toggle circularity off/on and recover from a trapped #REF! Self-test: deliberately introduce a #REF! in an average-balance interest cell with iteration on, then recover using the breaker within five minutes and show the convergence check returns zero difference after switching back.
  • I can compute DTL from book NBV vs tax WDV at 25.17% Self-test: for a three-year capex plan, build book NBV (SLM) and tax WDV (block-wise 15% with half rate for < 180 days) and reconcile closing DTL, deferred tax expense and the ETR to the statutory rate.
