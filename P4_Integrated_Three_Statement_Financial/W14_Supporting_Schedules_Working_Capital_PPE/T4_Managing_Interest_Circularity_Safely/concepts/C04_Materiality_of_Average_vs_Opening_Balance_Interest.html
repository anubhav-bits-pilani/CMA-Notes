Key concept

Materiality of Average vs Opening-Balance Interest

Concept: Materiality: average vs opening interest gap ≈ Rate × ΔDebt ÷ 2; document the chosen method

Statement

For a tranche with opening balance \(D_{t-1}\), closing balance \(D_t\) and rate \(r\):

\[ I^{\text{avg}}_t - I^{\text{open}}_t = r \times \frac{D_{t-1} + D_t}{2} - r \times D_{t-1} = r \times \frac{\Delta D_t}{2} \]

where \(\Delta D_t = D_t - D_{t-1}\). The post-tax effect on PAT is approximately

\[ \Delta \text{PAT} \approx -\,r \times \frac{\Delta D_t}{2} \times (1 - t) \]

with \(t\) the tax rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA). The approximation is exact for the first-round effect; second-round effects through the revolver or sweep are small (multiplier ≈ \(r(1-t)/2\)).

Explanation

Sign. When debt is falling (\(\Delta D < 0\)), opening-balance interest overstates interest; when debt is rising, it understates. A deleveraging LBO is therefore conservative under opening-balance interest for PAT but understates the speed of deleveraging; a growth company drawing its revolver looks better than it is.

Materiality benchmarks. Compare the gap, summed across tranches and net of the interest-income gap on cash, with:

  • PAT and EPS — e.g., more than 1% of PAT is usually noticeable in an equity-research context.
  • Covenant headroom — the DSCR or interest-cover impact versus the distance to the covenant threshold.
  • Valuation — for DCF on FCFF the gap does not affect enterprise value (FCFF is pre-financing), but it affects net debt at the valuation date only through the forecast closing balances, which are usually not used. For equity-value models (FCFE, LBO IRR) the gap matters directly.
  • Model purpose — a board plan tolerates a different level of approximation than a lender's base case.

Documentation. Whatever method is chosen, record on the Cover or in the assumptions book: the method (average with iteration and breaker; opening balance; timing factor; macro), the reason, the materiality assessment (largest annual gap in ₹ crore and as % of PAT, and covenant headroom impact) and the date of assessment. A reviewer can then accept a non-circular model on the evidence rather than argue the principle.

Mid-year timing alternative. If debt movements are known in timing (scheduled repayments), a timing factor gives \(I = r \times (D_{t-1} + \alpha \Delta D_t)\); \(\alpha = 0.5\) reproduces average-balance interest without a loop, but only for movements not dependent on current-period cash.

Illustration

Hospital chain LBO, FY27E, tax 25.168%:

Tranche Opening (₹ cr) Closing (₹ cr) Rate Gap = r × ΔD ÷ 2 (₹ cr)
Senior term loan 1,800 1,420 10.5% −19.95
Revolver 120 0 11.0% −6.60
Cash (interest income) 60 60 6.5% 0.00
Net interest gap −26.55

Opening-balance interest overstates finance cost by about ₹26.6 crore; PAT is understated by about ₹19.9 crore. Against forecast PAT of ₹210 crore, that is roughly 9.5% — material; average-balance interest (circular with breaker, or macro) is justified. For a stable listed FMCG company with debt moving from ₹300 crore to ₹280 crore at 8%, the gap is ₹0.8 crore — immaterial against PAT of ₹1,500 crore, and opening-balance interest is defensible.

Practice Questions

Q1. Gap and PAT impact [L1]

A term loan falls from ₹750 crore to ₹600 crore at 9.2%; a revolver rises from ₹0 to ₹90 crore at 10.4%; tax rate 25.168%. Compute the gap per tranche, the net gap and the PAT impact of using opening-balance interest.

Q2. Covenant materiality [L2]

A borrower's covenant requires interest cover (EBITDA ÷ interest) of at least 3.0×. FY27E EBITDA ₹240 crore; average-balance interest ₹76 crore; debt falls by ₹300 crore during the year at a blended 9.6%. Compute interest cover under both methods and assess whether the method choice is material for the covenant test.

💡 Hint: Work out opening-balance interest from the gap formula first.

Q3. Materiality memo [L3]

Draft a short memo (about 150 words) for the model file explaining that you have adopted opening-balance interest for a mid-cap pharma company's five-year plan, with a quantified materiality test using: debt ₹520 crore falling ₹70 crore a year, rate 8.4%, PAT ₹410 crore rising 12% a year, tax 25.168%.
