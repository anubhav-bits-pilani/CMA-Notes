FAST and ICAEW Stance on Circularity vs Banking Practice

Scope: FAST/ICAEW stance against circularity vs banking-practice acceptance

Why it matters

Whether a circular reference is acceptable depends on who will review the model. A project-finance model going to a lenders' model auditor, a public-sector PPP model, or an FP&A model governed by a corporate modelling policy built on FAST or the ICAEW Financial Modelling Code will usually be expected to have no circularity. An investment-banking LBO, a sell-side equity model or a PE deal model will usually be expected to have average-balance interest with a breaker. Candidates moving from audit into modelling roles need to articulate both positions, pick the right one for the engagement, and defend the choice in a review meeting or interview.

Core notes

The FAST Standard position

The FAST Standard (Flexible, Appropriate, Structured, Transparent), maintained by the FAST Standard Organisation and widely used in UK and Commonwealth project finance and infrastructure modelling, takes a strong line: circular references should be avoided. The reasoning:

  • Transparency: a circular model cannot be read as a simple left-to-right flow; a reviewer cannot trace a number to its inputs without understanding the iteration.
  • Robustness: errors trapped in the loop persist (s07); iteration can mask unintended circularities elsewhere (topic pitfall).
  • Stability: iteration can fail to converge or converge to different results depending on the starting point when MIN/MAX switches exist.
  • Analysis tools: data tables, goal seek and scenario macros behave poorly with live loops.

FAST-style models use opening-balance interest, timing factors, algebraic solutions or copy-paste macros (s05) instead. Where a circularity is genuinely necessary (some sculpted repayment profiles, DSRA sizing on next-period debt service), FAST practitioners typically resolve it by macro with paste-values, so the workbook itself contains no circular formula.

The ICAEW Financial Modelling Code

The ICAEW Financial Modelling Code (first published 2018, updated since) sets out principles for building models that are fit for purpose. Its guidance treats circular references as something to avoid wherever possible; if they are unavoidable, they should be deliberate, clearly identified, documented and controlled with a switch, and the model should be checked for convergence. The emphasis is on proportionality: the method should match the model's use and the materiality of the effect. In practice this aligns closely with FAST for formal models and permits controlled circularity where justified. (The Code is principle-based; check the current edition for exact wording rather than quoting it from memory.)

Banking and transaction practice

Investment-banking, PE and leveraged-finance teams routinely accept average-balance circularity, for several reasons:

  • Economic accuracy: in LBOs with heavy cash sweeps, debt can fall 20–30% a year; opening-balance interest overstates interest materially and understates the deleveraging that drives equity returns.
  • Revolver realism: revolver draws and repayments genuinely occur through the year; average balance is closer to reality.
  • Speed and convention: the iterative approach is quick to build, universally recognised in banking training programmes, and expected in deal models.
  • Controls: a breaker switch, convergence checks and documentation answer most of FAST's objections.

The banking convention therefore is: average-balance interest + iteration + breaker + documentation + convergence check.

Indian practice

In India both traditions coexist:

  • Project finance / infrastructure (roads, renewables, transmission): lenders' engineers and model auditors — often Big 4 or specialist firms — frequently apply FAST-like review checklists; banks' credit appraisal notes rely on DSCR outputs, so circularity is usually avoided or macro-resolved. Annual or half-yearly models with opening-balance or timing-factor interest are common.
  • Equity research and sell-side: models for NSE-listed companies often use opening-balance or simple average interest on a fixed debt forecast; many avoid the revolver altogether by holding debt as an input. Circularity appears mainly in leveraged sectors.
  • PE / investment banking / M&A: follow global banking practice — circular with breaker.
  • Corporate FP&A: usually non-circular; finance costs are forecast from the treasury plan, and models are shared widely, so fragility is costly.

Making the decision

Documenting the chosen stance

Whatever the choice, the Cover or Model map should state it: "Interest on revolver and term loan B calculated on average balances; model contains an intended circularity controlled by switch Cover!F12; iterative calculation required (100 / 0.001)". Or: "Interest calculated on opening balances with mid-year timing factor on scheduled repayments; no circular references; materiality assessed at under 0.5% of PAT in all forecast years".

Illustration

A 250 MW solar SPV in Rajasthan with a ₹1,100 crore 18-year sculpted term loan at 9.25%. Annual principal is around ₹45–80 crore. Opening vs average interest differ by roughly 9.25% × ₹60 crore ÷ 2 ≈ ₹2.8 crore a year. Against a cash flow available for debt service of about ₹190 crore, the impact on DSCR is about 0.02× — not decisive for a 1.30× minimum. A FAST-compliant semi-annual model with opening-balance interest is defensible. Contrast an LBO of a hospital chain with ₹2,000 crore of debt being swept down by ₹400 crore in year 1 at 11%: the gap is about ₹22 crore of interest, material to equity IRR — circularity with breaker or a macro is warranted.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Client policy overrides preference: if the client's modelling policy bans circularity, comply even in a deal model, using macro or algebra.
  • Hidden circularity in "FAST" models: a DSRA sized on next year's debt service that itself depends on this year's sweep can create a loop the builder did not notice. Test with iteration off.
  • Regulatory submissions: tariff petitions to CERC/SERCs or concession models are typically reviewed by parties who expect non-circular calculation; avoid loops.
  • Audit firm reviews of impairment models (Ind AS 36): value-in-use models rarely need financing circularity since cash flows are pre-financing; circularity there is usually a WACC-iteration issue, discussed in Week 16.
  • Stance is not a substitute for controls: a circular model is acceptable only with a breaker and a convergence check; a non-circular model still needs a materiality note.

Key takeaways

  • FAST discourages circularity on transparency, robustness and stability grounds; alternatives are opening-balance, algebra or macros.
  • The ICAEW Code is principle-based: avoid if possible; if used, make it deliberate, documented, switchable and checked.
  • Banking practice accepts average-balance circularity for economic accuracy, with breaker and documentation.
  • In India, project finance and FP&A lean non-circular; PE and investment banking lean circular-with-breaker.
  • Decide by reviewer, user, debt dynamics, period length, analysis needs and materiality — and document the decision.

Practice Questions

Q1. Articulate both positions [L1]

In two paragraphs, set out the FAST/ICAEW argument against circular references and the investment-banking argument for them, citing at least three specific reasons on each side.

Q2. Engagement decision [L3]

You are engaged to build (a) a lender base-case model for a ₹3,200 crore greenfield road HAM project reviewed by a Big 4 model auditor; (b) a buy-side LBO model for a ₹4,500 crore specialty-chemicals acquisition with 100% sweep. For each, state whether you would use circularity, the method of interest calculation, and the wording you would put on the Cover.

Q3. Materiality for the stance [L2]

A sell-side model for a cement company has opening net debt ₹2,400 crore falling to ₹1,950 crore by year-end; average interest rate 8.6%; PAT ₹780 crore; tax 25.168%. Estimate the PAT impact of using opening-balance interest instead of average, and state whether you think a non-circular approach is defensible in the research note.

💡 Hint: Use the Rate × ΔDebt ÷ 2 approximation and apply the tax shield.

Q4. Interview probe [L2]

An interviewer says: "Circular references are always bad modelling." Write a structured response of no more than 200 words that agrees, disagrees or qualifies, with reasons.
