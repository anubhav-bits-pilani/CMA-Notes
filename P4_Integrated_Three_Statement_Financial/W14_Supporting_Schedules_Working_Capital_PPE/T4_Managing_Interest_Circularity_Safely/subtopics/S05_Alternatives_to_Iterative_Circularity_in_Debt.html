Subtopic note

Alternatives to Iterative Circularity in Debt Models

Scope: Alternatives: opening-balance interest, closed-form algebra for simple structures, copy-paste VBA macro

Why it matters

Iteration is not the only way to deal with interest that depends on closing debt. Many model-review standards prefer models with no circular references at all, many corporate finance teams cannot be relied upon to keep iteration enabled, and some structures — data-table-heavy valuation files, Monte Carlo models, files shared with lenders — behave badly with live loops. Knowing three alternatives and when each is appropriate lets you choose a method that is defensible to a reviewer and robust in the hands of the end user. Interviewers frequently ask "how would you avoid circularity?" expecting all three.

Core notes

Alternative 1: Opening-balance interest

Interest is computed on the opening balance of each period:

\[ I_t = r_t \times D_{t-1} \]

where \(I_t\) is interest in period \(t\), \(r_t\) the period rate and \(D_{t-1}\) the closing debt of the previous period (= opening of \(t\)). Opening balances are already known when period \(t\) begins, so there is no loop.

When it is acceptable:

  • Debt changes little during the year (the gap is Rate × ΔDebt ÷ 2, c04).
  • Monthly or quarterly models, where the period is short.
  • Repayments occur near the start of the period (opening is then closer to the true average).
  • Net-cash companies where interest income is a small part of PBT.

Refinement — timing factor: use \(I_t = r_t \times (D_{t-1} + \alpha \times \text{Scheduled change}_t)\) where \(\alpha\) is an input (0.5 for mid-year), applied only to known movements (scheduled amortisation, planned drawdowns) and not to revolver/sweep movements. This captures most of the average-balance effect for term loans without creating a loop.

Alternative 2: Closed-form algebra

For simple structures the circular system can be solved analytically. Take a single revolver that absorbs the whole deficit, average-balance interest and tax at rate \(t\):

\[ D_1 = D_0 + F + (1-t)\,I \]

\[ I = r \times \frac{D_0 + D_1}{2} \]

where \(D_0\) is opening revolver, \(D_1\) closing revolver, \(F\) the pre-interest funding need (deficit positive), \(r\) the revolver rate. The post-tax interest \((1-t)I\) is itself funded by the revolver. Substituting the first equation into the second and collecting the interest terms:

\[ I = \frac{r\left(D_0 + \frac{F}{2}\right)}{1 - \frac{r(1-t)}{2}} \]

The formula is non-circular: every term on the right is known. Similar closed forms exist for a 100% sweep of a single tranche and for interest income on cash.

Limits: the algebra breaks as soon as MIN/MAX logic switches regime (revolver hits its limit, debt is fully repaid, sweep is capped), when there are several tranches with different rates and seniority, or when tax has loss set-off. You then need piecewise solutions for each regime — feasible for one tranche, unmanageable for an LBO. Closed forms are best for teaching, for checking an iterative result and for compact corporate models.

Alternative 3: Copy-paste VBA macro

The macro breaks the formula loop by making interest depend on a hard-pasted helper row rather than on live closing debt:

  1. Interest formula uses the helper row: =Rate*AVERAGE(Open_Debt, Close_Debt_Paste) — not circular because the helper row is values.
  2. The macro copies live closing debt (or live interest) and pastes values into the helper row.
  3. Recalculate.
  4. Compare the new live values with the pasted values; if the maximum absolute difference exceeds tolerance, repeat.
  5. Stop when converged or after a maximum loop count; report status.
Sub SolveInterest()
    Dim i As Integer, diff As Double
    Application.Calculation = xlCalculationManual
    For i = 1 To 50
        Range("Close_Debt_Paste").Value = Range("Close_Debt_Live").Value
        Application.Calculate
        diff = Application.WorksheetFunction.SumXMY2(Range("Close_Debt_Live"), Range("Close_Debt_Paste"))
        If diff < 0.000001 Then Exit For
    Next i
    Application.Calculation = xlCalculationAutomatic
    Range("Solve_Status").Value = IIf(diff < 0.000001, "Converged in " & i, "NOT converged")
End Sub

(SUMXMY2 returns the sum of squared differences — a convenient single convergence metric. See c02 for the concept statement.)

Strengths: no circular references; errors cannot be trapped because the helper row is values; iteration can stay off globally; FAST-compliant in spirit. Weaknesses: the model is only correct after the macro is run — any input change leaves interest stale until the user reruns it. Mitigate with a stale-flag check: a cell comparing live and pasted rows that turns red when they differ. Also requires a macro-enabled file (.xlsm), which some lenders' IT policies block.

Choosing among the methods

Criterion Iteration + breaker Opening balance Closed form Copy-paste macro
Accuracy Exact average Approximate Exact (simple cases) Exact after run
Circular references Yes, controlled None None None
Robust to errors Needs breaker High High High
Data tables Slow/unstable Good Good Needs macro-driven sensitivities
Complex structures Handles Handles Poor Handles
User skill needed Medium Low Medium Medium (macros)

Illustration

Opening revolver ₹200 crore; funding need ₹50 crore; rate 9%; tax 25.168%.

  • Opening balance: \(I = 0.09 \times 200 = 18.00\) crore.
  • Closed form: \(I = 0.09 \times (200 + 25) \div (1 - 0.09 \times 0.74832 \div 2) = 20.25 \div 0.966326 = 20.96\) crore.
  • Iteration converges to the same ₹20.96 crore; the macro after three to four passes also gives ₹20.96 crore.

The gap of about ₹2.96 crore between opening and exact (about 16% of interest) reflects both the ₹50 crore funding need and the post-tax interest that is itself funded by the revolver.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Opening-balance with a large new tranche: if a ₹1,000 crore acquisition loan is drawn on day 1 of the year, opening-balance interest records zero interest on it for the whole year — clearly wrong. Model known drawdowns with a timing factor or treat them as opening balances.
  • Closed form with tax losses: set \(t = 0\) in the formula while losses are being absorbed; the regime change at the point losses run out requires a piecewise check.
  • Macro and Undo: running a macro clears Excel's undo stack; save before running.
  • Macro security: files from email are opened in Protected View with macros disabled; document how to enable, or provide a non-macro opening-mode fallback.
  • Hybrid approach: many banks use iteration for the working model and switch to opening mode or a pasted-values snapshot for client-facing outputs and data tables.

Key takeaways

  • Opening-balance interest removes the loop at a materiality cost of roughly Rate × ΔDebt ÷ 2; a timing factor on scheduled movements recovers most of the accuracy.
  • Closed-form algebra gives exact, non-circular interest for single-tranche structures but fails with regime-switching MIN/MAX logic.
  • The copy-paste macro solves complex structures without circular references, at the cost of a run step and a stale-value risk.
  • Always add a stale/convergence flag when using the macro.
  • Choose the method based on structure complexity, end-user skill, data-table needs and the review standard applied.

Practice Questions

Q1. Derive the closed form [L2]

Derive the closed-form interest for a single revolver absorbing a funding need \(F\), with average-balance interest at rate \(r\) and tax rate \(t\). Then compute interest for opening revolver ₹75 crore, funding need ₹30 crore, rate 10.5%, tax 25.168%.

💡 Hint: Express closing revolver in terms of interest, substitute into the average, and collect interest terms on one side.

Q2. Timing-factor design [L2]

A term loan of ₹900 crore has scheduled repayments of ₹150 crore on 30 September each year; rate 8.9%. Compute FY27E interest using (a) opening balance, (b) simple average and (c) a timing factor reflecting the actual repayment date. Explain which you would use in an annual model and why.

Q3. Macro build [L3]

Write (in pseudocode or VBA) a copy-paste macro for a model with three tranches and interest income, using a helper row for each loop-closing balance. Include a maximum loop count, a tolerance, a status cell and a stale-values check formula for the worksheet.

Q4. Method choice under constraints [L3]

A lender's credit committee requires that the base-case model contains no circular references and no macros. The borrower has a revolver and a 50% cash sweep. Propose a modelling approach that meets the constraint, estimate its materiality for a typical year (opening revolver ₹80 crore, closing ₹20 crore, rate 10%), and draft the disclosure note.
