Subtopic note

Defining Operating NWC: What to Exclude and Why

Scope: Excluding cash, debt, lease liabilities, tax balances and dividends payable from operating NWC

Why it matters

The accounting definition of working capital (current assets minus current liabilities) mixes operating, financing, investing and tax items. A model that uses it wholesale will double count: cash already sits in the cash line, current maturities of debt are already in the debt schedule, lease liabilities in the lease corkscrew, current tax in the tax schedule, and dividends payable in the equity schedule. Double counting breaks the Balance Sheet, or worse, balances it while misstating CFO and free cash flow — and in a DCF, mis-scoping NWC moves enterprise value directly. Every serious modeller fixes the NWC perimeter before forecasting a single line.

Core notes

Principle: each Balance Sheet line has exactly one owner schedule

Operating NWC contains only balances that arise from the operating cycle — buying, making, selling, and paying operating expenses — and whose movement belongs in CFO before interest and tax. Everything else is owned by another schedule:

Excluded item Why excluded Owner schedule CFS section
Cash and cash equivalents, other bank balances It is the output of the model, the plug that the CFS calculates Cash/revolver (t3) Closing cash
Current investments (liquid MFs, T-bills) Treasury surplus, financing in nature Cash/investments CFI
Short-term borrowings, CC/WCDL, commercial paper Financing, interest-bearing Debt (t3) CFF
Current maturities of long-term debt Part of the term loan; reclassified only for presentation (Ind AS 1.69–76) Debt (t3) CFF
Interest accrued on borrowings Part of the financing cost; settles with debt service Debt (t3) CFF (interest paid, per policy under Ind AS 7.33)
Lease liabilities — current portion Financing under Ind AS 116; principal repayment in CFF Lease corkscrew CFF
Current tax liabilities / advance tax (net) Tax has its own cash timing Tax (t5) CFO (taxes paid shown separately)
Deferred tax assets/liabilities Non-current and non-cash Tax (t5) Non-cash adjustment
Dividends payable / unpaid dividends Distribution to owners Equity CFF
Derivative assets/liabilities (hedges of debt) Financing/hedging Debt/FX CFF or OCI
Capital creditors, capital advances Investing PPE/capex (t2) CFI
Assets held for sale (Ind AS 105) Disposal group, not ongoing operations Separate line CFI on sale
Loans to related parties, inter-corporate deposits Investing Investments CFI

Why current maturities of debt is the classic mistake

Schedule III requires current maturities of long-term borrowings to be shown under "Current liabilities – Financial liabilities – Borrowings". A modeller who sums all current liabilities will include them in NWC. Then when the debt schedule repays the loan, the repayment appears twice: once in CFF and once as a fall in "NWC liabilities" in CFO. The fix: map the line to debt at the historicals stage and let the debt schedule produce both the current and non-current split for presentation (see t3 s07).

Lease liabilities

Since Ind AS 116 (effective 1 April 2019), lessees recognise lease liabilities for almost all leases. For a retailer these are often larger than bank debt. They are interest-bearing financial liabilities, the principal portion of payments goes to CFF and the interest portion to CFF or CFO per policy (Ind AS 7.17(c) and Ind AS 116.50). They must never enter NWC — and they need their own corkscrew (week cross-cutting item). Security deposits paid to landlords are a different matter: they are operating assets for retailers in practice and can stay in NWC or be held flat; be consistent with how you treat leases in valuation.

Tax balances

Current tax payable and advance tax/TDS receivable represent timing between current tax expense and cash tax. They are forecast in the tax schedule (t5) from instalment rules. Keeping them in NWC would cause the tax payable to be driven by revenue % — economically meaningless — and break the reconciliation between current tax and cash tax.

Note that GST, TDS payable and other indirect statutory dues do stay in NWC: they are pass-through operating items tied to revenue and expenses (s04). Only income-tax balances are excluded.

Dividends payable

Under Ind AS 10, dividends declared after the reporting date are not liabilities. A dividend payable appears only when declared before year-end and unpaid (e.g., interim dividend declared in March, paid in April) or unclaimed dividends. Both belong to the equity/dividend schedule; the cash outflow is in CFF.

The grey zone: debt-like and cash-like items

Some balances are operating in accounting form but financing in substance. A valuation-grade model decides explicitly:

  • Supplier finance / reverse factoring with extended terms → debt-like.
  • Acceptances / buyers' credit for imports → debt-like.
  • Customer advances with significant financing component → may be treated as debt-like (s05).
  • Provisions for gratuity and leave encashment (unfunded) → typically treated as debt-like in EV-to-equity bridges, excluded from NWC.
  • Earmarked bank balances (margin money for LCs/BGs) → restricted cash; exclude from free cash and often from net debt reduction.
  • Security deposits from customers or dealers (FMCG, gas distribution) → operating but long-dated; often non-current.

The rule is consistency with the valuation bridge (Week 16): whatever is excluded from NWC as debt-like must be included in net debt, and vice versa — otherwise the item disappears or counts twice.

Implementation

Add a mapping column on the historical Balance Sheet tab with codes: NWC, CASH, DEBT, LEASE, TAX, EQUITY, CAPEX, OTHER. Then:

=SUMIFS(BS_Amount_Col, Map_Col, "NWC")

computes historical NWC directly and gives an audit trail. A check that SUMIFS over all codes equals total assets (or total liabilities) confirms every line is mapped exactly once.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bank overdraft repayable on demand and integral to cash management can be part of cash and cash equivalents under Ind AS 7.8; in a model, keep it in the revolver/CC line, not NWC.
  • Interest receivable on deposits — exclude (investing income).
  • Excess inventory held as strategic stock (e.g., a pharma API stockpile) is operating but non-recurring; consider as a separate line rather than embedding in DIO.
  • Non-current trade receivables/payables (retention money in EPC, long-term dealer deposits) are operating in nature even though non-current; include them in the operating capital perimeter but forecast separately.
  • Held-for-sale reclassification removes balances from NWC lines in the last actual year; restate history to avoid an artificial ΔNWC.

Key takeaways

  • Operating NWC excludes cash, investments, all borrowings including current maturities, lease liabilities, income-tax balances, dividends payable and capex items.
  • Each Balance Sheet line must have exactly one owner schedule; map it at the historicals stage.
  • GST/TDS dues stay in NWC; income-tax payable/advance tax goes to the tax schedule.
  • Debt-like operating items must be treated consistently between NWC and the net-debt bridge.
  • A SUMIFS mapping column plus a completeness check prevents double counting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying current balances [L1]

From the following FY25 current balances (₹ crore) of a listed consumer company, compute accounting working capital and operating NWC: cash 220; liquid mutual funds 400; trade receivables 650; inventory 900; GST input credit 70; advance tax (net of provision) 35; trade payables 1,050; current maturities of term loan 180; current lease liabilities 140; interest accrued on borrowings 12; statutory dues (GST/TDS) 85; interim dividend payable 60; capital creditors 45; accrued expenses 160.

Q2. Double-counting diagnosis [L2]

A model includes current maturities of long-term debt (₹200 crore at FY25, ₹350 crore at FY26) inside "other current liabilities" driven at a % of revenue, while the debt schedule separately repays ₹200 crore in FY26. Explain the effect on FY26 CFO, CFF and closing cash, and why the Balance Sheet may still appear to balance. Describe the fix.

💡 Hint: Trace the same rupee of repayment through both schedules.

Q3. Debt-like items and valuation consistency [L3]

A retailer has ₹600 crore of supplier-finance payables (bank pays suppliers at 30 days; the company pays at 120 days), ₹250 crore of unfunded gratuity, and ₹180 crore of margin money deposits against LCs. For each, decide NWC vs net-debt treatment for a DCF, and explain the effect on FCFF and on equity value if you got the classification wrong in each direction.

Q4. Model build: mapping column [L2]

Describe how you would set up a mapping column and SUMIFS-based NWC calculation for a Schedule III Balance Sheet with 45 line items, including the completeness check that every line is mapped once, and how the mapping feeds the forecast Balance Sheet.
