Subtopic note

Other Current Assets, Accruals and Statutory Dues in NWC

Scope: Other current assets/liabilities: advances, accrued expenses, statutory dues (GST, TDS) as % of revenue/opex

Why it matters

After receivables, inventory and payables, Indian Balance Sheets carry a long tail of "other current" lines: advances to suppliers, prepaid expenses, GST input credit, employee liabilities, accrued expenses, statutory dues payable and security deposits. Individually small, together they are often 5–15% of revenue and can swing CFO by hundreds of crores — for example when GST input credit accumulates under an inverted duty structure or when a large bonus accrual reverses. A model that ignores them either breaks the balance sheet (if they are held flat while revenue triples) or makes ΔNWC look artificially stable. The skill is choosing the right driver per line and deciding which lines are operating at all.

Core notes

Mapping the Schedule III lines

Schedule III spreads these items across several captions. Build a mapping table on the historicals tab so each line is classified once:

Interest accrued on deposits No (financing/investing) With cash schedule
Accrued expenses / provisions for expenses Yes % of opex
Employee benefits payable (salary, bonus) Yes % of employee cost

Choosing a driver

The principle is the same as for the big three: pick the P&L line that causes the balance.

  • Advances to suppliers — days of purchases. Retailers paying advances for private-label production will see this rise with the sourcing model.
  • Prepaid expenses — % of other expenses (insurance, AMC, software subscriptions are paid ahead).
  • Accrued expenses — % of other expenses or days of cash opex: \( \text{Accrued}_t = \text{Opex}_t \times \text{days} \div 365 \). Accruals represent costs incurred but not invoiced at year-end (power, freight, marketing).
  • Employee payables — % of employee benefit expense; bonus accruals spike in good years.
  • GST payable — net output GST less input credit for the last month(s). GST for March is payable by 20 April (GSTR-3B), so year-end GST payable approximates one month's net liability:

\[ \text{GST payable} \approx \frac{\text{Revenue} \times \text{Output rate} - \text{Taxable purchases} \times \text{Input rate}}{12} \]

In practice model it as % of revenue calibrated to history. GST input credit receivable (in "balances with government authorities") is a separate asset; it becomes large under an inverted duty structure (input rate > output rate, e.g., textiles, footwear components) and should be % of purchases. - TDS payable — TDS deducted in March is payable by 30 April; others by the 7th of the next month. The balance is roughly one month of TDS deducted on salaries, rent, contractor and professional payments. Model as % of (employee cost + rent + other expenses subject to TDS) or simply % of opex. - PF/ESI payable — % of employee cost (one month's contribution).

When to hold flat vs drive

  • Hold flat (or grow at inflation) when the balance is structurally unrelated to volume: security deposits for a fixed head office, long-standing disputed GST deposits paid under protest, export incentives receivable under a discontinued scheme.
  • Drive with a % when the line scales with activity.
  • Collapse into a single "other operating current assets, net" line at % of revenue when the lines are immaterial and noisy — but document the mapping so it can be expanded later.

=Opex_t*Accrued_Pct          'accrued expenses
=Revenue_t*Stat_Dues_Pct     'GST/TDS payable
=AVERAGE(hist_pct_range)     'calibrate % from 3-year history

Keep each % as a blue input on the assumptions tab, with the three-year historical % shown alongside in black for review.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disputed taxes paid under protest (GST, customs) sit in other assets and are non-operating in substance — they are contingent recoveries. Exclude from NWC or hold flat; never drive with revenue.
  • Accumulated GST input credit under inverted duty structure can grow indefinitely; refunds under Sec 54 of the CGST Act are slow. Model refund timing explicitly if material.
  • Advances from customers belong in contract liabilities (s05), not "other current liabilities", even if the company presents them there.
  • Warranty and sales-return provisions are operating; Ind AS 115 refund liabilities for expected returns behave like contract balances.
  • Current portion of gratuity/leave — some analysts include, others exclude as debt-like; whichever you choose, apply it consistently across NWC and net debt (s07).
  • Year-end bonus accrual reversals create one-off CFO swings; do not extrapolate the % from a single year.

Key takeaways

  • Map every Schedule III other-current line once as operating or non-operating before forecasting.
  • Drive each operating line with its causal P&L base: purchases, opex, employee cost or revenue.
  • GST and TDS payable approximate about one month of net liability because March dues are paid in April.
  • GST input credit under an inverted duty structure is a growing asset — model it on purchases, with refunds.
  • Hold non-volume items flat; never revenue-drive disputed deposits or capital advances.
  • Show the historical % beside each forecast % so reviewers can test reasonableness.

Practice Questions

Q1. Driver selection for other current items [L1]

For each of the following, state whether it belongs in operating NWC and propose a forecasting driver: (a) GST input credit receivable; (b) capital advances to a machinery supplier; (c) interest accrued on fixed deposits; (d) bonus payable; (e) customs duty paid under protest; (f) TDS payable; (g) prepaid insurance.

Q2. Forecasting accruals and statutory dues [L2]

A pharma company reports FY25 (₹ crore): revenue 6,000; employee cost 1,200; other expenses 1,500; accrued expenses 135; employee payables 84; statutory dues payable 96; prepaid expenses 45. FY26 revenue grows 11%, employee cost 9%, other expenses 10%. Forecast each line using FY25 ratios and compute the net cash impact on CFO from these lines alone.

Q3. Inverted duty structure [L2]

A textile processor sells fabric at 5% GST but buys dyes and chemicals at 18%. FY26E revenue ₹1,000 crore, taxable purchases of inputs ₹450 crore, input services ₹100 crore at 18%. Refunds of accumulated credit under Sec 54 are received with a six-month lag. Estimate the annual credit accumulation and the closing GST credit receivable if refunds are claimed for the full accumulation, and explain how you would model this line.

💡 Hint: Compare annual output tax with annual input tax; the excess accumulates until refunded.

Q4. Collapsed vs granular approach [L3]

A reviewer objects that your model forecasts 11 separate other-current lines with individual percentages, totalling 3% of revenue, and asks you to collapse them into one line. Argue for and against, and propose a design that satisfies both the reviewer and the requirement that the model reconciles to the Schedule III notes.
