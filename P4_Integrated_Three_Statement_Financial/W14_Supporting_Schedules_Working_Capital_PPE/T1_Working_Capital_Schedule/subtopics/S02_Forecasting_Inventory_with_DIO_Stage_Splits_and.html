Subtopic note

Forecasting Inventory with DIO, Stage Splits and Retail Days

Scope: Inventory via DIO on COGS; optional RM/WIP/FG split; retail inventory days

Why it matters

Inventory is the working-capital line that absorbs the most cash in Indian retail, consumer durables, pharma and specialty chemicals. For a fashion retailer adding 150 stores a year, inventory build can consume more cash than store capex. Lenders size working-capital limits on inventory (drawing-power statements), category managers are measured on inventory days, and analysts read a DIO spike as either a demand miss or a channel-stuffing risk. In the model, inventory days are the bridge between the cost build (COGS from Week 13) and the cash needed to fund growth.

Core notes

DIO on COGS, not revenue

Inventory is carried at cost (lower of cost and net realisable value, Ind AS 2.9), so the matching flow measure is cost of goods sold, not revenue:

\[ \text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365, \qquad \text{Inventory}_t = \text{COGS}_t \times \frac{\text{DIO}_t}{365} \]

Using revenue would embed gross margin into the days figure: if margins expand, revenue-based "days" fall even though stock holding is unchanged, and the forecast inventory becomes margin-sensitive for no economic reason.

What counts as COGS for an Indian P&L. Schedule III does not present a COGS line. Construct it as:

\[ \text{COGS} = \text{Cost of materials consumed} + \text{Purchases of stock-in-trade} + \text{Changes in inventories of FG, WIP and stock-in-trade} \]

Whether to add manufacturing overheads (power, consumables, contract labour, factory depreciation) depends on what your Week 13 cost build calls COGS. The rule is consistency: if historical DIO used the narrow material-only COGS, the forecast must use the narrow figure too. For a pure trader/retailer the narrow definition is correct because inventory contains only purchased goods.

Worked illustration

Vastra Retail Ltd (fashion retailer, FY ending 31 March):

₹ crore FY25A FY26E FY27E
Purchases of stock-in-trade 7,050 — —
Change in inventories (250) — —
COGS 6,800 8,160 9,630
Closing inventory 1,490 1,722 1,979
DIO (days) 80.0 77.0 75.0

FY26E inventory \( = 8{,}160 \times 77 \div 365 = 1{,}721.4 \). The two-day improvement per year is an input, justified by a replenishment-system upgrade disclosed in the investor presentation.

Optional RM / WIP / FG split

Manufacturers disclose inventory by stage (raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods, stock-in-trade, stores and spares, goods-in-transit). A stage-wise schedule is worth the extra rows when the stages behave differently:

Stage Natural base Why
Raw materials (incl. in transit) Material consumed RM is held against consumption, not against total COGS
Work-in-progress Total production cost (COGS) Reflects process cycle time
Finished goods COGS Reflects distribution lead time and safety stock
Stock-in-trade Purchases of traded goods Trading inventory is a function of purchase flow
Stores & spares Fixed % of gross block or flat Maintenance-driven, not volume-driven

Example: a cement company with material cost ₹2,000 crore and RM days of 30 holds ₹164 crore of RM; WIP (clinker) at 15 days of total cost of ₹4,200 crore is ₹173 crore; FG at 7 days is ₹81 crore. If a new grinding unit is commissioned with clinker bought from a sister plant, RM days rise while WIP days fall — a blended DIO would miss the reshuffle. Stores and spares in cement and steel are large and sticky; forecasting them on COGS days overstates their sensitivity to volume.

The split also matters for commodity pass-through: RM inventory valued at rising input prices inflates DIO in rupee terms even when tonnage is flat. In a volume × price build, model RM in tonnes × price for precision.

Retail inventory days

Retailers reason in several inventory metrics, and the model should speak the same language as the investor presentation:

  • Inventory days on COGS — the modelling driver above.
  • Inventory days on sales (at MRP/selling price) — often used by management; convert to cost basis by multiplying by \( (1 - \text{gross margin}) \) before applying to COGS.
  • Inventory per sq ft or per store — useful when the revenue build is store-driven: closing inventory = stores × sq ft per store × inventory per sq ft. This links store additions (Week 13 drivers) directly to working capital, and shows the "fill" cost of new stores that open in Q4.
  • Inventory turns \( = 365 \div \text{DIO} \) — fashion retailers target 4–6 turns; grocery 10–15.

Retail-specific judgements: consignment and sale-or-return (SOR) stock from vendors is not the retailer's inventory and keeps DIO low (common in department stores); a shift to own-brand (private label) sourcing raises inventory days because the retailer bears the stock; and end-of-season sale timing (EOSS in January/July) moves the year-end balance.

NRV write-downs

Inventory write-downs to NRV (Ind AS 2.28–33) reduce the carrying amount and are part of COGS. For forecasting, embed a normal obsolescence rate in DIO; model an explicit provision corkscrew only where write-downs are material (fashion, electronics, pharma near-expiry).

Excel implementation

Historical DIO:   =IFERROR(Inventory/COGS*365,0)
Forecast:         =COGS_t*DIO_t/365
Store-based:      =Closing_Stores_t*Avg_SqFt*Inv_per_SqFt_t/10^7   'convert ₹ to ₹ crore

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Year-end stock build ahead of a price increase or festive season inflates DIO; check quarterly inventory (half-yearly Balance Sheet under LODR Reg 33) before extrapolating.
  • Goods-in-transit on imported RM can swing by a full month of consumption depending on shipping cycles.
  • Inventory funded by vendor financing or supplier credit (reverse factoring) — check whether the payable is trade or borrowing (see s03, s07).
  • Changes in inventory accounting policy (FIFO to weighted average) or capitalisation of overheads change the level; restate history before computing days.
  • Retailers with large store openings in March carry new-store fill inventory with negligible COGS contribution; per-store inventory is the better driver.
  • Negative "change in inventories" in the P&L just means inventory rose — do not double count it when building COGS.

Key takeaways

  • Inventory = COGS × DIO ÷ 365; never revenue, because inventory is carried at cost.
  • Build COGS from Schedule III lines consistently with the Week 13 cost definition.
  • Split RM/WIP/FG/stores only when the stages have different drivers or a structural change is coming.
  • For store-driven retail models, inventory per store or per sq ft links working capital to the expansion plan.
  • Convert any management "days on sales" metric to a cost basis before using it.
  • Treat consignment, private-label mix and EOSS timing as explicit judgements in retail DIO.

Practice Questions

Q1. Constructing COGS and DIO from Schedule III [L1]

A listed FMCG company reports for FY25 (₹ crore): cost of materials consumed 4,100; purchases of stock-in-trade 900; changes in inventories of FG, WIP and stock-in-trade (−120) (i.e., an increase); closing inventory 980. Compute COGS on the narrow definition and the DIO. Forecast FY26 inventory if COGS grows 9% and DIO is held flat.

Q2. Stage-wise inventory for a specialty chemical maker [L2]

FY26E material consumed ₹1,800 crore, total COGS ₹2,600 crore, gross block ₹3,000 crore. Days assumptions: RM 45 (on material consumed), WIP 20 (on COGS), FG 30 (on COGS); stores and spares at 2% of gross block. A new plant commissioned in FY27 raises material consumed by 25%, COGS by 22% and gross block by ₹1,200 crore, and management expects RM days to rise to 55 because of imported feedstock. Build the FY26 and FY27 stage-wise inventory and the total DIO.

Q3. Store-driven inventory for a retailer [L2]

Vastra Retail closes FY25 with 600 stores averaging 9,000 sq ft and inventory of ₹1,490 crore. It plans 120 net additions in FY26 and 140 in FY27, with new stores averaging 10,000 sq ft. Inventory per sq ft is expected to fall 3% per year due to better replenishment. Forecast closing inventory for FY26 and FY27, and compute implied DIO if COGS is ₹8,160 crore and ₹9,630 crore respectively.

💡 Hint: Compute total retail area first; derive FY25 inventory per sq ft from the actuals.

Q4. Days on sales vs days on cost [L1]

A department-store chain's management says inventory is "60 days of sales". Gross margin is 38% and FY26E revenue is ₹5,000 crore. Convert the metric to DIO on COGS and compute the forecast inventory balance. Explain why the two figures differ.

Q5. Judgement: private-label shift [L3]

A retailer currently sources 60% of merchandise on sale-or-return terms and plans to move to 70% private label within three years. Explain the effect on DIO, gross margin and cash conversion, how you would phase the DIO assumption in the model, and what a credit analyst would ask about inventory risk.
