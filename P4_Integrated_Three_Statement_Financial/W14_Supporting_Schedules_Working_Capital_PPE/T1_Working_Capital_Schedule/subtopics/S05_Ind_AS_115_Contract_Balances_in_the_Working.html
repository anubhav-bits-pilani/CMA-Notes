Subtopic note

Ind AS 115 Contract Balances in the Working Capital Schedule

Scope: Ind AS 115 contract balances: contract assets, advances from customers, deferred revenue

Why it matters

For project, subscription and real-estate businesses, contract balances drive working capital more than trade receivables do. An EPC contractor's unbilled revenue can be 100+ days of sales; a real-estate developer funds construction largely with customer advances; a SaaS or education company collects fees a year ahead and carries deferred revenue that makes its NWC negative. Even a fashion retailer carries contract liabilities for gift vouchers and loyalty points. Modelling these lines as generic "other current" items hides the most important operational story — billing and collection milestones relative to revenue recognition.

Core notes

The three balances under Ind AS 115

Ind AS 115.105 requires an entity to present a contract as a contract asset or contract liability depending on the relationship between performance and payment, and any unconditional right to consideration separately as a receivable.

Balance Definition Arises when Typical examples
Receivable Unconditional right to consideration — only the passage of time is required (Ind AS 115.108) Invoice raised and payment due Billed debtors
Contract asset Right to consideration for goods/services transferred, conditional on something other than time, e.g., future performance or milestone certification (Ind AS 115.107) Revenue recognised over time ahead of billing Unbilled revenue on fixed-price IT projects; EPC work done but not certified
Contract liability Obligation to transfer goods/services for which consideration has been received or is due (Ind AS 115.106) Cash/invoice ahead of performance Customer advances, deferred revenue, unearned subscription fees, loyalty points, gift vouchers

Contract assets are within the scope of Ind AS 109 impairment — the simplified ECL approach applies to them as for trade receivables.

Indian companies label these inconsistently: "unbilled revenue" (may be a receivable if the right is unconditional, e.g., time-and-material billing lag, or a contract asset for fixed-price work), "advances from customers", "income received in advance", "deferred revenue", "contract liabilities". Read the accounting policy note to classify, then model by economic behaviour.

Advances from customers

Common in real estate (booking amounts and construction-linked instalments), capital goods (10–20% mobilisation advances), defence, shipbuilding and B2B education. Drivers:

  • Days of revenue or % of order inflow for capital goods: advances are received when orders are booked and unwind as revenue is recognised. A better driver is order inflow:

\[ \text{Closing advances} = \text{Opening} + \text{Advance \% } \times \text{Order inflow} - \text{Advance \% } \times \text{Revenue recognised} \]

  • Collections vs revenue for real estate: closing customer advances = opening + collections − revenue recognised (on project completion or over time, per the contract and Ind AS 115.35 assessment).

Advances with a significant financing component (payment more than 12 months before transfer) require interest accretion under Ind AS 115.60–65: revenue is grossed up and a finance cost is recognised. The practical expedient in Ind AS 115.63 allows ignoring it when the gap is ≤ 12 months. For modelling, the accreted interest is non-cash — it increases the contract liability and is a finance cost, not operating working capital movement.

Deferred revenue

Fees billed or collected before the service is performed: SaaS subscriptions, annual maintenance contracts, education fees, extended warranties sold as service-type warranties, membership fees. Model as a corkscrew or as days of revenue:

\[ \text{Closing deferred revenue} = \text{Opening} + \text{Billings} - \text{Revenue recognised} \]

For a subscription business, model billings (new + renewal contract value) as the driver and let revenue be the recognised portion; deferred revenue then falls out. For simpler businesses, days of revenue is adequate.

Retail-specific contract liabilities: gift cards/vouchers and loyalty points. Under the customer-option guidance (Ind AS 115 Appendix B, B39–B43), loyalty points that give a material right are a separate performance obligation; consideration is allocated to them and deferred until redemption or expiry. Breakage on unexercised rights is recognised in proportion to the pattern of exercise (B44–B47). Model as % of revenue.

Contract assets (unbilled revenue)

Fixed-price IT services, EPC and infrastructure contractors recognising revenue over time (input/output method) build contract assets when work outpaces milestone billing. Driver: days of revenue, calibrated to history, with judgement about the order mix. An EPC company with government contracts and slow certification may carry 60–120 days of contract assets on top of 60–90 days of receivables — so "DSO" must be read as DSO plus unbilled days.

Illustration: capital-goods maker

FY26E revenue ₹4,000 crore, order inflow ₹5,000 crore, customer advance 15% of order value, opening advances ₹480 crore. Contract-asset days 40.

₹ crore FY26E
Opening customer advances 480
+ Advances received (15% × 5,000) 750
− Advances adjusted against revenue (15% × 4,000) (600)
Closing customer advances 630
Contract assets (4,000 × 40 ÷ 365) 438

The ₹150 crore increase in advances is a CFO inflow; any increase in contract assets is an outflow.

Disclosure hooks for calibration

Ind AS 115.116–118 require disclosure of opening and closing contract balances, revenue recognised from opening contract liabilities, and explanation of significant changes. Ind AS 115.120 requires the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations (order backlog) and expected timing. These disclosures let you calibrate the corkscrew and test whether the forecast is consistent with backlog conversion.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Contract asset vs receivable classification differs across peers; compare total "billed + unbilled" days, not DSO alone.
  • Large mobilisation advances on a single mega-order can make NWC negative temporarily; do not extrapolate.
  • Real estate on completed-contract recognition (point in time): revenue lumps at handover while advances build for years — model project-wise.
  • Significant financing component accretion is a finance cost; exclude it from operating ΔNWC and from EBITDA.
  • Contract liabilities in NWC vs net debt — customer advances are generally operating; some lenders and analysts treat very large, long-dated advances as debt-like in valuation. State your choice.
  • Breakage estimates for gift cards change revenue and the liability simultaneously; a change in estimate is not a cash flow.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 115 distinguishes unconditional receivables, conditional contract assets and contract liabilities.
  • Forecast contract assets as days of revenue; customer advances via order inflow and revenue unwinding; deferred revenue via billings less revenue.
  • Retail gift vouchers and loyalty points are contract liabilities — model them as % of revenue.
  • Use Ind AS 115.116–120 disclosures (balance movements, remaining performance obligations) to calibrate.
  • Financing-component accretion is non-cash finance cost, not working capital.
  • Compare peers on billed + unbilled days, because classification differs between companies.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying contract balances [L1]

Classify each as receivable, contract asset or contract liability under Ind AS 115: (a) time-and-material IT services for March, invoiced on 5 April with payment due 30 days later; (b) fixed-price project 60% complete, billing due on next milestone certification; (c) ₹50 crore mobilisation advance on a turbine order; (d) annual software licence and support fee collected in advance; (e) loyalty points outstanding on a retailer's app.

Q2. Customer advance corkscrew [L2]

A defence electronics company has opening customer advances of ₹1,100 crore. Forecasts: FY26 order inflow ₹3,200 crore, FY27 ₹2,000 crore; revenue FY26 ₹2,400 crore, FY27 ₹2,800 crore. Customers pay 25% of order value as advance, adjusted pro rata against revenue. Build the advance corkscrew for both years and quantify the CFO effect. Comment on the sustainability of FY26 cash flow.

💡 Hint: Advances are received on orders and released on revenue; the two drivers move at different speeds.

Q3. Deferred revenue for an edtech platform [L2]

An edtech company bills annual subscriptions upfront. FY25 closing deferred revenue is ₹420 crore. FY26 billings are ₹1,300 crore and revenue recognised ₹1,150 crore; FY27 billings ₹1,250 crore and revenue ₹1,280 crore. Build the corkscrew, compute deferred revenue days for both years and explain what the FY27 numbers signal about growth.

Q4. Unbilled revenue and true DSO [L3]

Two listed EPC contractors report DSO of 70 and 95 days. The first carries contract assets of 110 days of revenue; the second 30 days. Explain which has the stronger working-capital position, how you would standardise the comparison, and what forecast assumptions you would set for the first company if it guides to faster milestone certification on new metro orders.
