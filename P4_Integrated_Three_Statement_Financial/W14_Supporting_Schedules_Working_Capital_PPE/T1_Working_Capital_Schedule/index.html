Roadmap topic: Working Capital Schedule: Forecasting Trade Receivables (DSO), Inventory (DIO), Other Current Assets, Trade Payables (DPO), and Accrued Expenses, plus computing annual ΔNWC

Overview

The working-capital schedule converts the Week 13 revenue and cost forecasts into the operating Balance Sheet lines that sit between EBITDA and cash. Each line — receivables, inventory, payables, contract balances, accruals, advances and statutory dues — is forecast as "days" or "%" of the P&L flow that causes it, and the schedule ends with a single number per year, ΔNWC, which flows (sign-reversed) into cash flow from operations and later into free cash flow. For a high-growth Indian retailer or manufacturer, ΔNWC can decide whether expansion is self-funded or needs a bigger revolver.

For FP&A and modelling roles the schedule is where commercial reality enters the model: credit terms to distributors, supplier terms constrained by the MSMED Act and Sec 43B(h), seasonal inventory builds ahead of Diwali, GST timing, customer advances on capital-goods orders and gift-voucher liabilities at a fashion retailer. Reviewers and interviewers probe it heavily because it is where sign errors, wrong bases and scope mistakes hide — and because a mis-scoped NWC moves enterprise value in a DCF.

The learning sequence is: first the three core "days" lines — receivables with ECL (s01), inventory with stage and retail metrics (s02), and payables with the MSE constraint (s03); then the long tail of other operating items (s04) and Ind AS 115 contract balances (s05); then aggregation into NWC, ΔNWC and the cash conversion cycle with seasonality caveats (s06); and finally the perimeter discipline that keeps cash, debt, leases, tax and dividends out (s07). The key concepts formalise the formulas (c01–c04) and the two consistency traps that silently bias forecasts: GST inside receivables (c05) and day-count/balance basis (c06).

Related concepts to study

Phase 2 working-capital analysis

→ Phase 2 (W5–8): working-capital ratios, peer CCC benchmarking, rising-DSO forensic flags

The schedule's historical rows are the same ratios you computed in Phase 2 financial statement analysis, now turned into forecast drivers. Peer CCC benchmarking (e.g., Trent vs Avenue Supermarts vs Aditya Birla Fashion; Pidilite vs Asian Paints) tells you whether a forecast DIO or DSO glide path is plausible. The forensic lens matters too: rising DSO with flat revenue, receivables growing faster than sales, ageing buckets shifting beyond six months, or a sudden DPO jump coinciding with a supplier-finance programme are all signals that the reported days are not a stable base. Before extrapolating any historical days figure, run the Phase 2 checks and normalise.

Ind AS 115 and Ind AS 109 foundations

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 115 contract balances, Ind AS 109 expected credit loss

Phase 1 covered the recognition rules; this week uses them to forecast. Ind AS 115 determines whether a balance is a receivable (unconditional right), a contract asset (conditional) or a contract liability (advance/deferred revenue), and the disclosure of remaining performance obligations gives you a backlog-based check on contract-balance forecasts. Ind AS 109's simplified approach requires lifetime ECL on trade receivables and contract assets, typically through a provision matrix; that is why the model forecasts the net balance and why a downside scenario may flex the allowance percentage. Revisit both standards when a company's contract-balance or impairment note is large.

Sec 43B(h) and the DPO constraint

Income-tax Sec 43B(h): MSME dues beyond 45 days disallowed until paid, constraining DPO

From FY 2023-24 any amount owed to a micro or small enterprise beyond the MSMED Act Sec 15 time limit (agreed period up to 45 days; 15 days without agreement) is deductible only when actually paid — with no relief for payment before the return due date. Combined with MSMED Sec 16 compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate (non-deductible under Sec 23), this makes stretching MSE suppliers expensive. In the model: segment payables into MSE and non-MSE using the Schedule III split, cap MSE days at 45 (ideally with a buffer), and feed any overdue balance to the tax schedule's disallowance line. Confirm the section mapping under the Income-tax Act, 2025 for FY27 onwards.

ΔNWC downstream in CFO and FCFF

→ Week 15 (w15_t2): ΔNWC in CFO; → Week 16 (w16_t1): ΔNWC in FCFF

The schedule's output is consumed twice. In Week 15 the indirect-method CFO starts from PBT or PAT, adds back non-cash items and then shows line-wise working-capital movements that must sum to −ΔNWC; the cash line then closes the Balance Sheet without a plug. In Week 16 FCFF subtracts ΔNWC alongside capex, so the NWC perimeter must be consistent with the net-debt bridge: anything excluded from NWC as debt-like (supplier finance, unfunded gratuity) must be in net debt. Terminal-year ΔNWC should reflect steady-state intensity times terminal growth, not the last forecast year's noise.

Common pitfalls

Adding an increase in receivables to CFO

Sign errors: adding an increase in receivables to CFO

The mistake: linking =AR_t-AR_t_1 straight into the cash flow statement, so a ₹100 crore increase in receivables shows as +₹100 crore of cash. It happens because modellers compute "changes" uniformly and forget that assets and liabilities flip in opposite directions, or because the CFS is built after the BS and the cash line is plugged, so nothing breaks. Correct treatment: asset increases reduce CFO (=AR_t_1-AR_t), liability increases raise it (=AP_t-AP_t_1). Detect it by a check cell comparing the sum of CFS working-capital lines with −ΔNWC, by eliminating any cash plug so the BS check catches it, and by a sense test: in a fast-growing positive-NWC company, the working-capital line should be negative.

Wrong base for inventory and payables days

Using revenue as the base for inventory or payables days

The mistake: computing DIO and DPO as balance ÷ revenue × 365 because revenue is the first line on the P&L and makes all three days "comparable". Inventory is carried at cost and payables arise from purchases, so a revenue base embeds gross margin. When margins expand in the forecast, revenue-based days imply falling inventory and payables in physical terms — or, if days are held flat, balances that grow with price rather than volume. Correct treatment: DIO on COGS; DPO on purchases (or COGS when inventory is stable). Detect it by reading the formula for the denominator, recomputing historical days from the annual report, and checking whether forecast inventory moves when you flex only gross margin.

Cash and current debt inside NWC

Including cash or current maturities of long-term debt in NWC

The mistake: using total current assets minus total current liabilities as NWC. Cash then sits in NWC and in the cash line; current maturities of long-term debt appear in NWC and in the debt schedule. Repayments are counted in both CFO and CFF, and an increase in cash appears as a working-capital outflow. It happens because Schedule III groups these items with operating balances and because accounting working capital is a familiar credit-analysis measure. Correct treatment: define the perimeter with a mapping column (NWC, CASH, DEBT, LEASE, TAX, EQUITY, CAPEX) and compute NWC with SUMIFS on the NWC code only. Detect it by checking that every BS line has exactly one owner schedule and that ΔNWC does not move when you change debt repayment assumptions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full NWC schedule for a fashion retailer [L3]

Vastra Retail Ltd (FY ending 31 March) reports FY25: revenue ₹12,000 crore; COGS ₹6,800 crore; other expenses ₹1,800 crore; employee cost ₹1,100 crore; net receivables ₹493 crore; inventory ₹1,490 crore (opening FY25 ₹1,240 crore); trade payables ₹1,860 crore; accrued expenses ₹150 crore; employee payables ₹66 crore; statutory dues ₹180 crore; gift-voucher and loyalty contract liabilities ₹96 crore; advances to suppliers ₹110 crore; current maturities of term loans ₹120 crore; current lease liabilities ₹420 crore; cash ₹380 crore. Forecasts: revenue growth 20%, 18%, 16% for FY26–FY28; gross margin constant; other expenses and employee cost grow 18% a year. Assumptions: DSO flat; DIO falls 1.5 days a year; DPO on purchases held at FY25 level; other items at FY25 ratios to their natural base. (a) Classify each balance as operating NWC or excluded. (b) Build the FY26–FY28 schedule, NWC, ΔNWC and line-wise CFO movements. (c) Compute CCC each year. (d) Identify which assumption you would stress first and why.

💡 Hint: Purchases require opening and closing inventory; derive FY25 purchases first to calibrate DPO.

Q2. Manufacturer with MSME suppliers and ECL stress [L3]

A specialty-chemicals company has FY26E revenue ₹5,000 crore, COGS ₹3,500 crore, purchases ₹3,600 crore. 30% of purchases are from micro and small enterprises currently paid at 60 days; other suppliers at 80 days. Gross receivables DSO 90 days; ECL allowance 4% of gross in the base case, 9% in the downside. Inventory: RM 50 days of material consumed (₹2,600 crore), WIP 15 days and FG 30 days of COGS. (a) Build base-case receivables (gross, allowance, net), stage-wise inventory and segmented payables with MSE days capped at 45. (b) Quantify the cash cost of complying with the MSMED cap and the tax cost avoided under Sec 43B(h) at 25.17%. (c) Compute the downside ECL impact on net receivables and explain why it does not change CFO directly in the year of the charge.

Q3. Capital-goods contract balances and seasonality [L2]

An NSE-listed transformer maker has FY25 revenue ₹3,650 crore, receivables ₹700 crore, contract assets ₹300 crore, customer advances ₹450 crore, and an order book of ₹7,300 crore. FY26 order inflow is forecast at ₹5,500 crore and revenue at ₹4,380 crore; customers pay 15% advance on order, adjusted pro rata against revenue; contract-asset and receivable days held at FY25 levels. The September 2025 half-year Balance Sheet showed receivables plus contract assets of ₹1,350 crore. (a) Forecast FY26 receivables, contract assets and advances. (b) Compute combined billed + unbilled days and the contract-balance contribution to ΔNWC. (c) Estimate the intra-year peak funding gap implied by the half-year balance and its implication for the CC limit.

Q4. Review of a peer's NWC tab [L3]

You are reviewing a colleague's model for an FMCG company. You find: DSO computed on closing receivables but forecast with average-basis formulas; DIO on revenue; a "net current assets" line including liquid mutual funds and current tax liabilities; historical DSO of 38 days at 18% GST while the forecast uses management's stated 30-day credit terms; and a CFS that shows "Increase in inventories" as a positive number. Write a structured review note listing each issue, its likely direction of bias on FY27 CFO and DCF value, and the corrective change.
