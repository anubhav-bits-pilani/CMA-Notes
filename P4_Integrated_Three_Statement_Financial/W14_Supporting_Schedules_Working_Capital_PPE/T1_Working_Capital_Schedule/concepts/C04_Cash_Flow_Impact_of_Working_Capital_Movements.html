Key concept

Cash Flow Impact of Working Capital Movements

Concept: CFS impact = −ΔNWC: −ΔReceivables − ΔInventory + ΔPayables + ΔAccruals

Statement

\[ \text{CFO}_{\text{WC}} = -\Delta\text{NWC} = -\Delta\text{AR} - \Delta\text{Inv} - \Delta\text{Other OCA} + \Delta\text{AP} + \Delta\text{Accruals} + \Delta\text{Other OCL} \]

where each \( \Delta X = X_t - X_{t-1} \) is the change in the closing balance of that operating line, and \( \text{CFO}_{\text{WC}} \) is the working-capital adjustment in the indirect-method cash flow from operations (Ind AS 7.18(b) and 7.20(a)).

Explanation

The indirect method starts with profit, which is accrual-based. An increase in receivables means revenue was booked but not collected — cash is lower than profit, so subtract. An increase in inventory means cash was spent on goods not yet expensed — subtract. An increase in payables means expenses were booked (or goods bought) without paying — cash is higher than profit, so add. Accruals and other liabilities behave like payables.

The mnemonic: assets up, cash down; liabilities up, cash up.

Presentation under Ind AS 7.20 lists "changes during the period in inventories and operating receivables and payables" as adjustments to profit. Indian listed companies typically show separate lines for trade receivables, inventories, other financial and non-financial assets, trade payables, other liabilities and provisions. The model's CFS should mirror the schedule line by line — it makes the bridge auditable — and a check cell must confirm the sum equals \( -\Delta\text{NWC} \).

Common sign errors:

  • Linking =AR_t-AR_t_1 into CFS without negating — adds a receivables increase to CFO (the classic pitfall).
  • Negating liabilities as well as assets — turns supplier credit into a cash outflow.
  • Using the change in gross receivables while the P&L already contains the ECL charge — double counting of the non-cash loss. Use the change in the net balance, or add back ECL separately and use gross.

A robust practice is to compute the CFS line as =Prior-Current for assets and =Current-Prior for liabilities, never relying on a later negation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Line-wise CFS movements [L1]

Opening and closing balances (₹ crore): receivables 300/270; inventory 410/480; prepaid expenses 20/25; trade payables 350/330; accrued expenses 60/75; statutory dues 30/36. Compute each CFS line and the total working-capital adjustment. State whether working capital absorbed or released cash.

Q2. Spot the sign errors [L2]

A junior's CFS shows: "Increase in trade receivables +120; Increase in inventories −80; Increase in trade payables −95; Increase in provisions −10". The underlying balances all increased by the stated amounts. Correct each line, compute the impact on CFO of the errors, and explain why the Balance Sheet check might still pass if cash is a plug.

💡 Hint: A plugged cash line will hide any CFS error; the real test is cash computed from the CFS.

Q3. ECL and net vs gross [L2]

Gross receivables rise from ₹800 crore to ₹900 crore and the ECL allowance from ₹40 crore to ₹70 crore (charge ₹30 crore, no write-offs). Show the CFO presentation under (a) the net-balance approach and (b) the gross-balance approach with ECL added back, and demonstrate that both give the same CFO.
