Key concept

Net Working Capital and Its Annual Change

Concept: NWC = Operating current assets − Operating current liabilities; ΔNWC = NWC_t − NWC_t−1

Statement

\[ \text{NWC}_t = \text{OCA}_t - \text{OCL}_t \]

\[ \Delta\text{NWC}_t = \text{NWC}_t - \text{NWC}_{t-1} \]

where \( \text{OCA}_t \) is operating current assets at the end of year \( t \) (trade receivables, inventory, contract assets, operating advances, prepaid expenses, GST credit and similar), and \( \text{OCL}_t \) is operating current liabilities (trade payables, accrued expenses, contract liabilities, employee and statutory dues, operating provisions). Cash, investments, borrowings (including current maturities), lease liabilities, income-tax balances, dividends payable and capex-related items are excluded (t1 s07).

Explanation

The word "operating" is the whole concept. Accounting working capital (total current assets − total current liabilities) is a liquidity measure used in credit analysis and the current ratio; it includes cash and short-term debt. Operating NWC is the capital locked into the business model itself, and its change is a use of cash in the same sense as capex.

A positive ΔNWC means the company invested more in working capital during the year — cash outflow. A negative ΔNWC means working capital was released — cash inflow. The sign flip to the cash flow statement is covered in c04.

Two derived ratios help in forecasting and review:

  • NWC % of revenue \( = \text{NWC}_t \div \text{Revenue}_t \) — a single-number intensity measure; stable for mature businesses.
  • Incremental NWC % of incremental revenue \( = \Delta\text{NWC}_t \div \Delta\text{Revenue}_t \) — what each additional rupee of sales costs in working capital. In steady state the two converge.

Some shortcut models forecast NWC directly as % of revenue. That is acceptable for valuation of a simple business, but a line-wise days build is preferred because it exposes the drivers, uses the right bases, and lets the CFS show each movement.

Indian context: Schedule III presentation of "current" follows the operating-cycle test (Ind AS 1.66–70), so some operating balances (EPC retention money, long-tenor dealer deposits) can be non-current. Include them in the operating capital perimeter if they arise from operations, forecasting separately.

Revenue grows from ₹6,500 crore to ₹7,200 crore: NWC % of revenue is 6.1% → 6.4%, and incremental NWC % is \( 65 \div 700 = 9.3\% \) — a signal that working capital is growing faster than sales and worth explaining.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute NWC and ΔNWC [L1]

For FY25 and FY26 (₹ crore): receivables 400/460; inventory 520/610; contract assets 0/40; trade payables 380/420; accrued expenses 90/105; customer advances 60/50; current maturities of debt 100/120; cash 150/90. Compute operating NWC for both years and ΔNWC. State which items you excluded and why.

Q2. Incremental intensity [L2]

A company's NWC % of revenue has been 18% for five years. The FY26 forecast shows revenue up ₹1,000 crore and ΔNWC of ₹40 crore. Compute incremental NWC intensity, explain what assumption change could produce this, and list three questions you would ask the model builder.

💡 Hint: Compare incremental intensity with the steady-state ratio.

Q3. Shortcut vs line-wise build [L2]

Argue when an NWC-as-%-of-revenue shortcut is acceptable in a DCF and when it is dangerous, referring to a business with large customer advances and an MSME-heavy supplier base.
