Subtopic note

Income-tax WDV Blocks and the 180-Day Half-Rate Rule

Scope: Income-tax WDV blocks: P&M 15%, buildings 10%, computers 40%, furniture 10%; half rate if used < 180 days

Why it matters

Tax depreciation is the largest deduction in the taxable income bridge for most industrial and retail companies, and it is the source of the largest deferred tax balance — the DTL on PPE. Indian tax depreciation is computed on a block of assets under the written-down value method, not asset by asset, and it has its own conventions (the 180-day rule, block extinction, capital gains on sale). A model that simply applies 15% to book gross block, or uses SLM, will misstate current tax every year and produce a DTL that cannot be reconciled to the reported note. The tax WDV schedule is the tax-side twin of the PPE corkscrew in w14_t2.

Core notes

Block of assets

Sec 2(11) defines a block of assets as a group of assets falling within a class (tangible: buildings, machinery, plant, furniture; intangible: know-how, patents, copyrights, trademarks, licences, franchises) in respect of which the same percentage of depreciation is prescribed. Depreciation is allowed on the WDV of the whole block (Sec 32(1)(ii)), with rates in Appendix I to the Income-tax Rules (Rule 5). WDV is defined in Sec 43(6).

Principal rates (post AY 2018-19, maximum 40%)

Block Rate
Buildings — residential (other than hotels/boarding houses) 5%
Buildings — general (factory, office, store premises) 10%
Buildings — temporary structures/purely temporary erections 40%
Furniture and fittings (incl. electrical fittings) 10%
Plant and machinery — general 15%
Motor cars (other than used in business of hiring) 15%
Motor vehicles used in business of running them on hire 30%
Computers and computer software 40%
Specified energy-saving/renewable energy devices, certain pollution-control equipment 40%
Intangible assets (know-how, patents, licences, etc.) 25%
Goodwill of a business or profession Not depreciable from AY 2021-22

For a retailer, store fit-outs are typically furniture & fittings (10%) or P&M (15%) depending on nature; leasehold improvements on rented premises are allowed as "building" at 10% via Explanation 1 to Sec 32(1). Model at the block level the company actually uses — the tax audit report (Form 3CD clause 18) lists blocks, rates, opening WDV, additions split by 180-day test, deletions and depreciation.

The 180-day rule

The second proviso to Sec 32(1) restricts depreciation to 50% of the normal rate for an asset acquired during the year and put to use for less than 180 days in that year. In practice: assets put to use on or after (approximately) 4 October of the FY get half the rate in the year of acquisition. The full WDV (cost less half-rate depreciation) carries into the next year at full rate — the "lost" half is not recovered separately, it simply remains in WDV and is depreciated over future years.

(For additional depreciation under Sec 32(1)(iia), the unutilised 50% of additional depreciation is allowed in the next year by the third proviso — but this does not apply to 115BAA companies.)

Block arithmetic

\[ \text{Tax dep}_t = \left(\text{WDV}_{open} + \text{Add}^{\ge 180}_t - \text{Sale}_t\right) \times r + \text{Add}^{<180}_t \times \frac{r}{2} \]

\[ \text{WDV}_{close} = \text{WDV}_{open} + \text{Add}^{\ge 180}_t + \text{Add}^{<180}_t - \text{Sale}_t - \text{Tax dep}_t \]

where \(r\) is the block rate, \(\text{Add}^{\ge 180}\) and \(\text{Add}^{<180}\) are additions put to use for at least and less than 180 days, and \(\text{Sale}_t\) is moneys payable on sale/discard (including scrap value). Strictly, sale proceeds are first reduced from the block before depreciation; if proceeds exceed opening WDV plus additions, the half-rate additions are also consumed. Most models net sales from the full-rate portion, which is correct unless sales are large.

Block extinction and capital gains (Sec 50)

  • If sale proceeds exceed (opening WDV + additions), the excess is a short-term capital gain irrespective of holding period, and the block WDV becomes nil.
  • If all assets of a block are sold and proceeds are less than WDV + additions, the deficit is a short-term capital loss; the block ceases to exist.
  • Depreciation is not allowed on a block with nil WDV even if some assets physically remain.

For forecasting, disposals are usually immaterial; enter sale proceeds as a % of the book NBV of disposals and test that the block never goes negative with a MAX(0, …) guard plus a flag.

Illustration

P&M block, FY27E. Opening WDV ₹500 crore. Additions: ₹120 crore put to use in July 2026 (≥ 180 days), ₹80 crore commissioned in January 2027 (< 180 days). Sale of old machines ₹10 crore.

Step ₹ crore
Opening WDV + full-period additions − sale = 500 + 120 − 10 610.0
Depreciation at 15% 91.5
Half-year additions ₹80 crore × 7.5% 6.0
Tax depreciation FY27E 97.5
Closing WDV = 610 + 80 − 97.5 592.5

Book depreciation on the same additions (SLM over 15 years, pro rata) would be roughly ₹8 crore in year one versus ₹24 crore of tax depreciation (120 × 15% + 80 × 7.5%) — a temporary difference that builds the DTL.

Excel implementation

Build one block per row group with these inputs: rate, % of capex allocated to the block, % of capex put to use < 180 days (often driven by the capex phasing — capex in H2 of the FY is a sensible proxy for < 180 days if commissioning is immediate; if capex sits in CWIP, tax depreciation begins only when the asset is put to use, mirroring book capitalisation).

Add_Full  = Capex_Capitalised * Block_Share * (1 - H2_Share)
Add_Half  = Capex_Capitalised * Block_Share * H2_Share
Tax_Dep   = MAX(0, Open_WDV + Add_Full - Sale) * Rate + Add_Half * Rate / 2
Close_WDV = Open_WDV + Add_Full + Add_Half - Sale - Tax_Dep
Check     = IF(MIN(Close_WDV_Row) < 0, "WDV NEGATIVE", "OK")

Total tax depreciation = SUM across blocks; it feeds the taxable income bridge (s03), and total closing WDV feeds the DTL computation (c02). Opening WDV for the first forecast year comes from the latest tax audit report or the deferred tax note (back-solve: Tax WDV = Book NBV − DTL on PPE ÷ rate), never from book gross block.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Asset-wise SLM is wrong for tax (topic pitfall). Only power-generating undertakings may opt for SLM under Rule 5(1A); everything else is block-wise WDV.
  • Capital subsidies and GST input credit reduce actual cost (Sec 43(1), Explanation 10); ITC claimed on capital goods is excluded from tax cost, just as it is from book cost.
  • Foreign exchange differences on loans for imported assets are adjusted to actual cost only on payment (Sec 43A), unlike Ind AS where they are mostly expensed — another temporary difference.
  • Ind AS 116 ROU assets are not tax-depreciable for the lessee; lease rentals are deducted instead. Do not put ROU assets in any tax block.
  • Amalgamations split depreciation between predecessor and successor by days of use (fifth proviso to Sec 32(1)); relevant in M&A models.
  • No additional depreciation for 115BAA companies (c06); for old-regime companies model 20% (35% in notified backward areas of specified states) on eligible new P&M, half in year one if < 180 days.

Key takeaways

  • Tax depreciation is block-wise WDV at Appendix I rates: P&M 15%, buildings 10% (residential 5%), furniture 10%, computers 40%, intangibles 25%.
  • Assets put to use for less than 180 days in the year of acquisition get half the rate in that year only.
  • Sale proceeds reduce the block; excess over block WDV is STCG under Sec 50, and a block with nil WDV earns no depreciation.
  • Tax depreciation starts on "put to use", so CWIP timing matters for both book and tax.
  • Seed opening WDV from the tax audit report or back-solve from the deferred tax note.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-year block schedule [L2]

Computers block: opening WDV 1 April 2026 ₹12 crore. FY27 additions ₹6 crore in May 2026 and ₹4 crore in November 2026; sale proceeds ₹0.5 crore in FY27. FY28 additions ₹8 crore in June 2027. Compute tax depreciation and closing WDV for FY27 and FY28.

Q2. Block extinction [L2]

A furniture block has opening WDV of ₹3 crore and FY27 additions of ₹0.4 crore (put to use in August). All furniture is sold for ₹4.1 crore in March 2027 on relocation. Compute FY27 depreciation and the tax consequence of the sale.

💡 Hint: Think about Sec 50 before computing any depreciation.

Q3. Back-solving opening WDV [L2]

The deferred tax note shows a DTL on "property, plant and equipment and intangible assets" of ₹186 crore at 31 March 2026 and the balance sheet shows NBV of PPE and intangibles (excluding ROU and goodwill) of ₹2,140 crore. The company is under 115BAA. Estimate aggregate tax WDV and explain why this estimate may be imprecise.

Q4. Building a multi-block schedule [L3]

Your Week 13 retail model forecasts capex of ₹400 crore, ₹450 crore and ₹500 crore for FY27E–FY29E: 60% store fit-outs (furniture & fittings), 25% P&M (ACs, escalators, DG sets), 15% computers and POS. 40% of each year's capex is commissioned after 3 October. Opening WDVs are ₹900 crore, ₹600 crore and ₹80 crore respectively. Lay out the schedule rows, write the formulas, and add the checks you would include.

Q5. Interview: block vs asset [L1]
