Subtopic note

Cash Tax Timing: Advance Tax, Tax Balances and Refunds

Scope: Cash tax timing: advance tax instalments (15/45/75/100%), Δtax payable/receivable, refunds

Why it matters

Current tax tells you how much tax relates to the year; cash tax tells you when it leaves the bank. For annual models the difference is usually the movement in the current tax balances on the balance sheet, but for quarterly or monthly cash forecasting — treasury planning, covenant headroom in a tight quarter, 13-week cash flows — the advance tax calendar dominates: 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March are four of the biggest outflow days in an Indian company's year. On the other side, Indian companies routinely carry large income tax assets because TDS deducted by customers and advance tax exceed final liability, and refunds arrive one to several years later. A model that equates current tax with cash tax will overstate cash in years of rising profits and miss the cash benefit of refunds.

Core notes

Advance tax obligations

Advance tax is payable when estimated tax liability for the year (after TDS/TCS) is ₹10,000 or more (Sec 208). For companies the cumulative instalments under Sec 211 are:

Due date Cumulative % of estimated tax
On or before 15 June 15%
On or before 15 September 45%
On or before 15 December 75%
On or before 15 March 100%

Shortfalls attract interest: Sec 234C (deferment of instalment — 1% per month, typically for 3 months on each shortfall, 1 month on the March instalment), and Sec 234B (1% per month from 1 April of the AY if advance tax paid is less than 90% of assessed tax). Any balance is paid as self-assessment tax (Sec 140A) before filing the return (due 31 October for companies requiring a tax audit report, or 30 November for those with transfer pricing reports). Interest under 234B/C is not income tax for Ind AS 12 purposes.

TDS suffered: customers deduct tax at source on many receipts (e.g., Sec 194C contracts, 194J professional fees, 194Q purchase of goods above ₹50 lakh). TDS is credited against the company's own liability and is in substance an advance payment of tax — cash collected from customers is lower by the TDS, which appears as a tax asset.

Balance sheet presentation

Indian companies typically present:

  • Income tax assets (net) — non-current (if refunds are expected beyond 12 months, common for disputed years) or current: advance tax + TDS − provision for tax for the years concerned;
  • Current tax liabilities (net) — provision for tax exceeding payments for years where tax is still payable.

Netting is year-wise and entity-wise under Ind AS 12.71 (legally enforceable right and intention to settle net); this is why both an asset and a liability often appear together.

The annual cash tax formula

\[ \text{Cash tax}_t = \text{Current tax}_t - (\text{Tax payable}_{t} - \text{Tax payable}_{t-1}) + (\text{Tax receivable}_{t} - \text{Tax receivable}_{t-1}) \]

A rising liability means tax expensed but unpaid (cash tax < current tax); a rising asset (advance tax/TDS paid in excess) means cash tax > current tax. Refunds received reduce the asset and therefore reduce cash tax in the year received.

Forecasting approach

  1. Annual model, simple: hold tax payable at a % of current tax (e.g., the historical year-end liability ÷ current tax, often 0–10% for companies that pay advance tax properly) and hold income tax assets flat unless refunds are expected.
  2. Annual model, refined: current-year tax mostly paid as advance tax; assume X% (say 5%) of current tax paid as self-assessment tax in the next year; TDS-driven excess builds up as an asset and is refunded with a lag of N years (often 2 years for non-scrutiny years with interest under Sec 244A at 0.5% per month).
  3. Quarterly/monthly cash model: schedule instalments at 15%, 30%, 30%, 25% of the estimated annual liability in June, September, December and March, less TDS credits accruing through the year.
Adv_Tax_Q1 = Est_Annual_Tax * 15%  - TDS_Q1
Adv_Tax_Q2 = Est_Annual_Tax * 45%  - TDS_cum_Q2 - Adv_Tax_Q1
Adv_Tax_Q3 = Est_Annual_Tax * 75%  - TDS_cum_Q3 - Adv_Tax_Q1 - Adv_Tax_Q2
Adv_Tax_Q4 = Est_Annual_Tax * 100% - TDS_cum_Q4 - SUM(Adv_Tax_Q1:Adv_Tax_Q3)
Self_Assess_Next_Year = MAX(0, Actual_Tax - Est_Annual_Tax)

Illustration

FY27E current tax ₹200 crore, all at 25.168%; TDS suffered through the year ₹30 crore; the company pays advance tax on an estimate of ₹190 crore. FY26 self-assessment tax of ₹8 crore is paid in FY27. A refund of ₹12 crore for AY 2024-25 is received in FY27.

Cash flows in FY27 (₹ crore) Amount
Advance tax (190 − 30 TDS), paid June–March 160.0
TDS deducted by customers (cash short-received) 30.0
Self-assessment tax for FY26 8.0
Refund received (AY 2024-25) (12.0)
Income taxes paid (net of refunds), Ind AS 7 186.0

Balance-sheet view: FY27 liability at year-end = 200 − 190 = ₹10 crore (payable as self-assessment tax); FY26 liability ₹8 crore settled; refund receivable falls by ₹12 crore. Cash tax = 200 − (10 − 8) + (−12) = ₹186 crore — reconciles.

CFS presentation

Ind AS 7.35 requires cash flows from taxes on income to be separately disclosed and classified as operating unless specifically identifiable with investing/financing activities. Indian reported CFS start from PBT, so the operating section shows "Income taxes paid (net of refunds)" as a deduction after working capital changes. A model starting from PAT instead must add back deferred tax and adjust for ΔTax payable/receivable within operating activities — the two presentations give the same CFO.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disputed demands paid under protest (often 20% of the demand to obtain a stay) increase income tax assets; they may be outstanding for years. Do not assume refund unless a favourable order is expected.
  • Vivad se Vishwas 2024 and similar schemes can trigger lump-sum settlement cash outflows with partial P&L charges; treat as one-offs.
  • Interest on refunds (Sec 244A) is taxable income; interest paid under 234B/C is not deductible — a permanent difference.
  • Seasonality: a company with a March-heavy profit profile underpays early instalments based on budget, then faces a large March instalment and 234C interest; quarterly models must reflect the estimate, not the actual.
  • Group cash pooling: advance tax is per entity; a holding company's cash cannot be "used" to pay a subsidiary's tax without intercompany funding.
  • Income-tax Act, 2025: the instalment calendar is carried into the new Act from 1 April 2026; confirm section references before citing.

Key takeaways

  • Companies pay advance tax cumulatively 15/45/75/100% by 15 June, September, December and March.
  • Cash tax = current tax − Δtax payable + Δtax receivable (refunds reduce the receivable and cash tax).
  • TDS suffered is an advance payment of tax and explains persistent income tax assets.
  • Ind AS 7 requires separate disclosure of taxes paid within operating activities.
  • Quarterly cash forecasts must schedule instalments explicitly; annual models can use balance movements.

Practice Questions

Q1. Instalment schedule [L1]

Estimated FY27 tax liability is ₹320 crore; TDS expected ₹40 crore evenly across the year. Compute each advance tax instalment amount and its due date.

Q2. Interest exposure [L2]

A company estimated FY26 tax at ₹100 crore and paid instalments of ₹15 crore, ₹30 crore, ₹30 crore and ₹25 crore on the due dates. Actual tax turned out to be ₹140 crore and it paid self-assessment tax on 30 September 2026. Explain which interest sections are triggered and approximate the interest; state how it is presented under Ind AS.

Q3. Reconcile tax paid [L2]

FY27E: current tax ₹260 crore; current tax liabilities (net) opening ₹22 crore, closing ₹31 crore; income tax assets (net) opening ₹95 crore, closing ₹80 crore after a refund of ₹28 crore and TDS-driven excess payments of ₹13 crore. Compute income taxes paid (net of refunds) and show both the formula approach and the cash-component approach.

Q4. 13-week cash forecast [L3]

You are building a 13-week cash forecast starting 1 March 2027 for a company with estimated FY27 tax of ₹180 crore, cumulative advance tax paid of ₹128 crore and cumulative TDS of ₹16 crore to date. Explain what tax outflows you would schedule, on what dates, and what risk you would flag if Q4 profits are tracking 20% above budget.

💡 Hint: The 15 March instalment is based on the revised estimate, not the budget.

Q5. Interview: why the tax asset keeps growing [L1]

An IT services company's "income tax assets (net)" has grown from ₹600 crore to ₹1,100 crore in four years. Give three plausible reasons and their modelling implications.
