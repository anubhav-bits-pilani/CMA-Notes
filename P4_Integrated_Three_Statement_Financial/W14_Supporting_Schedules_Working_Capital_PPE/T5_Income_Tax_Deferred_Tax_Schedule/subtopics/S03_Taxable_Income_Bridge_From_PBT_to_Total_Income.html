Subtopic note

Taxable Income Bridge: From PBT to Total Income

Scope: Taxable income: PBT + book depreciation − tax depreciation ± 43B disallowances, provisions, permanent differences

Why it matters

Current tax is not PBT × rate. It is the rate applied to total income computed under the Income-tax Act, and the bridge from accounting PBT to total income is exactly the computation a tax auditor reviews in Form 3CD and the assessing officer reviews in scrutiny. For a model it serves two purposes: it gives a defensible current tax figure, and it identifies which adjustments are temporary (creating deferred tax) versus permanent (moving the ETR away from 25.168%). Without this bridge you cannot build a deferred tax schedule, explain ETR, or forecast cash tax in a year when the company swings between profit and loss.

Core notes

The bridge

\[ \text{Total income} = \text{PBT} + \text{Book D\&A} - \text{Tax depreciation} \pm \text{43B timing items} \pm \text{Provision movements} \pm \text{Permanent differences} - \text{Loss set-off} \]

The loss set-off step is covered in s06; this note covers the items before it. A practical layout on the tax tab:

Row FY27E (₹ crore) Nature
Profit before tax (Ind AS) 600.0 —
Add: Book depreciation and amortisation (incl. ROU) 180.0 Temporary
Less: Tax depreciation under Sec 32 (230.0) Temporary
Add: Provision for gratuity/leave encashment (net of payments) 12.0 Temporary
Add: Increase in ECL allowance (net of write-offs) 8.0 Temporary
Add: Sec 43B(h) MSME dues unpaid beyond 45 days, net of prior-year amounts paid 5.0 Temporary
Add: Ind AS 116 interest + ROU depreciation − lease rentals paid 6.0 Temporary
Add: CSR expenditure (Explanation 2 to Sec 37(1)) 9.0 Permanent
Add: Penalties and interest on delayed tax payments 1.0 Permanent
Add: Sec 14A disallowance on exempt income 0.5 Permanent
Less: Fair value gain on mutual funds (taxed only on sale) (4.0) Temporary
Less: Exempt income (2.0) Permanent
Gross total income before loss set-off 585.5

Note that book depreciation added back must include ROU asset depreciation and amortisation of intangibles, because tax law gives its own deduction (Sec 32 for owned assets, lease rentals under Sec 37/30 for leased assets).

Book depreciation vs tax depreciation

Book depreciation follows Ind AS 16/Schedule II (SLM or WDV over useful life; w14_t2). Tax depreciation follows Sec 32 block-of-assets WDV rates (s04). The difference is usually the largest single temporary difference; in early years of a capex programme tax depreciation exceeds book depreciation (15% WDV on P&M vs ~4.75–6.67% SLM), creating a DTL that unwinds later.

Sec 43B disallowances

Sec 43B allows certain expenses only in the year of actual payment, regardless of when they are accrued. The specified items include:

  • (a) taxes, duties, cess or fees (e.g., GST, customs, municipal levies);
  • (b) employer contributions to provident fund, superannuation, gratuity or other welfare funds;
  • (c) bonus or commission to employees;
  • (d)/(e) interest on loans from public financial institutions, state financial corporations, scheduled banks and co-operative banks (and NBFCs under (da));
  • (f) leave encashment;
  • (g) payments to Indian Railways for use of railway assets;
  • (h) amounts payable to a micro or small enterprise beyond the time limit in Sec 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006 (inserted by Finance Act 2023, effective AY 2024-25).

The proviso allows deduction for items (a)–(g) if paid on or before the due date of filing the return for that year; there is no such relief for 43B(h) — an MSME payable outstanding beyond 15/45 days at year-end and paid after 31 March is deductible only in the year paid. Employee contributions to PF/ESI withheld from salary are governed by Sec 36(1)(va) and are deductible only if deposited by the due date under the PF/ESI law (Checkmate Services, SC 2022) — failure creates a permanent disallowance since it is treated as income.

In a model, 43B items appear as: addback of closing unpaid amount, deduction of the opening unpaid amount (paid this year). Equivalently, add back the increase in the unpaid balance:

43B_Adjustment = Unpaid_43B_Close - Unpaid_43B_Open

Provisions

Under the Act, liabilities that are contingent or estimated are generally not deductible until crystallised. Common provision adjustments:

  • ECL allowance / provision for doubtful debts: deductible only on actual write-off (Sec 36(1)(vii)), except for specified banks and NBFCs under 36(1)(viia). Add back net increase in allowance.
  • Gratuity: provision not deductible (Sec 40A(7)); contributions to an approved gratuity fund deductible under Sec 36(1)(v), and direct payments to employees deductible when paid.
  • Leave encashment: 43B(f), when paid.
  • Warranty: a provision estimated on a scientific basis is deductible (Rotork Controls India, SC 2009), so often no adjustment arises — check the company's tax audit report.
  • Inventory write-downs to NRV: deductible (inventory valued at lower of cost or NRV under ICDS II), so usually no adjustment.

Permanent differences

Permanent differences never reverse and therefore create no deferred tax; they are the main reason ETR departs from 25.168% (concept c04). Typical Indian items:

Permanent difference Direction
CSR spend (Explanation 2 to Sec 37(1)); CSR contributions to specified funds may still qualify under Sec 80G (Chapter VI-A Part B, available even under 115BAA) Add back (net of any 80G deduction claimed)
Penalties, fines, interest on delayed income-tax, Explanation 1 to Sec 37(1) Add back
Sec 14A expenses relating to exempt income (Rule 8D) Add back
Exempt income (e.g., share of LLP profit Sec 10(2A), tax-free bond interest) Deduct
Sec 80M inter-corporate dividend deduction (to extent distributed) Deduct
Goodwill amortisation/impairment (no tax depreciation since FY 2020-21) Add back
Share of profit of associates/JVs in consolidated PBT Deduct (taxed in investee)
ESOP expense under Ind AS 102 Add back; tax deduction on exercise is debated — treat per company practice

Sec 40(a)(i)/(ia) disallowances for TDS not deducted are temporary (allowed in the year TDS is deposited, at 30% for residents), not permanent.

Excel implementation

Keep each adjustment as its own row driven from the relevant schedule: tax depreciation from the WDV block schedule, ECL from the working capital tab, 43B balances from accrued expenses, lease adjustments from the lease corkscrew. Permanent items are typically forecast as % of revenue (CSR is 2% of average net profit under Sec 135 for qualifying companies — model it as 2% × average PBT of the three preceding years where Sec 135 applies).

CSR_t = 2% * AVERAGE(Net_Profit_s198_t-1, Net_Profit_s198_t-2, Net_Profit_s198_t-3)
Taxable_Pre_Loss = PBT + Book_DA - Tax_Dep + SUM(Temporary_Adj) + SUM(Permanent_Adj)

Tag each row Temporary or Permanent in a helper column; SUMIF on that tag feeds the ETR reconciliation and the deferred tax schedule.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disallowance is not a deferral when the payment never happens. A 43B(h) amount later waived by the MSME supplier becomes income under Sec 41(1), not a deduction.
  • Ind AS transition adjustments (ICDS vs Ind AS differences, e.g., revenue timing) can create recurring temporary differences — check the tax audit report's ICDS disclosure.
  • Consolidated PBT includes items (associate share, consolidation eliminations) that never enter any entity's tax computation; compute taxable income entity by entity for material subsidiaries.
  • Capital gains are computed separately with their own rate; keep them out of the business-income bridge.

Key takeaways

  • Total income = PBT + book D&A − tax depreciation ± 43B, provision and permanent adjustments, then loss set-off.
  • 43B items are deductible only when paid; 43B(h) MSME dues have no "paid before return date" relief.
  • ECL, gratuity and leave provisions are deductible only on write-off/payment — temporary differences creating DTAs.
  • CSR, penalties, 14A and goodwill amortisation are permanent; they move ETR, not deferred tax.
  • Tag each adjustment Temporary/Permanent so the bridge feeds both the deferred tax schedule and the ETR reconciliation.
