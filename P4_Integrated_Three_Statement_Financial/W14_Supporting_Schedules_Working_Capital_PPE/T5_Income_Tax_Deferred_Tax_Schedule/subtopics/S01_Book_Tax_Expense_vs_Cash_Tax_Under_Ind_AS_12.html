Subtopic note

Book Tax Expense vs Cash Tax Under Ind AS 12

Scope: Book tax (Ind AS 12) = current tax + deferred tax; cash tax vs book tax

Why it matters

The tax line is where most three-statement models quietly lose integrity. Analysts often forecast "tax = PBT × ETR", put that number in the P&L, and then deduct the same number in the cash flow statement as if every rupee of tax expense left the bank that year. In reality the P&L tax line under Ind AS 12 is an accounting number made up of two parts with very different cash behaviour: current tax, which is broadly payable (or already paid as advance tax) in respect of the year, and deferred tax, which is an accrual for tax that will be paid or saved in future years because book and tax rules recognise income and expense at different times. For capex-heavy Indian businesses — cement, steel, telecom towers, retail with heavy store fit-outs — the gap between book tax and cash tax can be 5–15% of the tax line for years, and in the year of a big capex programme it can be far larger. An FP&A team forecasting cash, a lender assessing debt service, and a valuer computing FCFF each need cash tax, not book tax.

Core notes

The Ind AS 12 composition of tax expense

Ind AS 12 defines tax expense (tax income) as the aggregate amount included in determining profit or loss for the period in respect of current tax and deferred tax (Ind AS 12.5).

\[ \text{Tax expense}_{P\&L} = \text{Current tax} + \text{Deferred tax expense (income)} \]

  • Current tax is the amount of income taxes payable (recoverable) in respect of the taxable profit (tax loss) for the period (Ind AS 12.5). It is measured at the rates enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date (Ind AS 12.46). In an Indian model it is the 115BAA rate (25.168%) applied to taxable income computed under the Income-tax Act after loss set-off.
  • Deferred tax expense is the movement in net deferred tax liability (DTL net of DTA) recognised through profit or loss. Deferred tax liabilities are amounts payable in future periods in respect of taxable temporary differences; deferred tax assets are amounts recoverable in future periods in respect of deductible temporary differences, unused tax losses and unused tax credits (Ind AS 12.5).
  • Tax relating to items recognised in OCI or directly in equity is recognised in OCI or equity respectively, not in P&L (Ind AS 12.61A). So the P&L deferred tax charge equals the total movement in net DTL less the part booked in OCI (e.g., on gratuity remeasurement or FVOCI gains).

Current-year adjustments for earlier years (e.g., excess/short provision of tax relating to prior years after assessment) are part of current tax and are usually shown separately in the notes as "tax relating to earlier years" under Schedule III's statement of profit and loss line "Tax expense: (1) Current tax (2) Deferred tax".

Book tax vs cash tax

Cash tax is what actually moves through the bank in the year: advance tax instalments, self-assessment tax, TDS suffered (which is a prepayment of the company's own tax), less refunds received. Three things separate it from book tax:

  1. Deferred tax — entirely non-cash in the year it is booked. It reverses into current tax in later years.
  2. Timing of current tax payments — advance tax for FY t is mostly paid within FY t (15/45/75/100% by 15 June, 15 September, 15 December, 15 March), but any shortfall, self-assessment tax and assessment demands are paid later, and refunds arrive one to three years after the year. The movement in current tax payable/receivable on the balance sheet captures this (subtopic s07).
  3. Items charged to OCI/equity — their tax is part of total tax cash flows but not of P&L tax expense.

The reconciliation that every model should carry:

\[ \text{Cash tax}_t = \text{Current tax}_t - \Delta\text{Current tax payable}_t + \Delta\text{Advance tax / tax receivable}_t \]

and, in the indirect CFS, the deferred tax charge is added back as a non-cash item (or, more commonly in Indian reported CFS, PBT is the starting point, total tax expense is excluded, and "income taxes paid (net of refunds)" is shown as a separate line within operating activities as required by Ind AS 7.35–36).

Illustration

FY27E for a cement company (₹ crore):

Line Amount
PBT 800.0
Book depreciation 250.0
Tax depreciation (WDV) 330.0
Taxable income (ignoring other differences) 720.0
Current tax @ 25.168% 181.2
Book NBV of PPE at 31 March 2027 2,400.0
Tax WDV at 31 March 2027 1,900.0
Closing DTL = 500 × 25.168% 125.8
Opening DTL (difference ₹420 crore) 105.7
Deferred tax expense 20.1
Total tax expense (P&L) 201.3
ETR = 201.3 ÷ 800 25.17%

The ETR equals the statutory rate because the only difference is temporary — deferred tax "fills the gap". If current tax payable fell from ₹30 crore to ₹20 crore during the year, cash tax is 181.2 + 10.0 = ₹191.2 crore; ₹20.1 crore of the P&L charge is non-cash.

Model layout

A tax tab normally has four blocks in this order: (i) taxable income bridge (s03), (ii) loss memorandum (s06), (iii) current tax and cash tax with advance tax timing (s07), (iv) deferred tax schedule by temporary-difference category (s05), with the tax WDV block schedule (s04) either on the same tab or on the PPE tab. Outputs: P&L current tax and deferred tax as separate rows, BS DTL/DTA and current tax balances, CFS add-back of deferred tax and tax paid line.

P&L Tax expense      = Tax!Current_Tax + Tax!Deferred_Tax_PL
CFS add-back         = Tax!Deferred_Tax_PL
BS Net DTL (closing) = Tax!Net_DTL_Close
Check                = Tax!Net_DTL_Close - Tax!Net_DTL_Open - Tax!Deferred_Tax_PL - Tax!Deferred_Tax_OCI   'must be 0

Presentation

Schedule III requires current tax and deferred tax to be shown separately on the face of the P&L; on the balance sheet, deferred tax assets (net) appear under non-current assets and deferred tax liabilities (net) under non-current liabilities — deferred tax is never classified as current (Ind AS 1.56). Current tax assets (net) and current tax liabilities (net) are separate lines. Offsetting is permitted only when there is a legally enforceable right to set off and the balances relate to the same taxation authority and the same taxable entity (Ind AS 12.71, 12.74).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Uncertain tax positions. Appendix C to Ind AS 12 (uncertainty over income tax treatments) requires measuring current/deferred tax using the most likely amount or expected value where it is not probable the authority will accept a treatment. Large Indian taxpayers carry provisions for disputed demands; in a model, keep these as a static balance unless a settlement is expected, and note that a settlement is a cash outflow with no P&L charge.
  • Tax on OCI items. If you forecast actuarial losses in OCI (most models forecast zero), their deferred tax must go to OCI, not P&L — otherwise the ETR drifts.
  • Dividend distribution tax was abolished from 1 April 2020; dividends are taxed in shareholders' hands. Historical P&Ls before FY21 do not include DDT in tax expense (it was charged to equity), but older models sometimes mixed it in.
  • Interest under Sec 234B/234C and 244A is not income tax under Ind AS 12; it is generally presented as finance cost/other income (an accounting policy choice to be applied consistently).
  • A group pays tax entity by entity; consolidated cash tax is the sum of entity-level computations — losses of one subsidiary do not shelter another's profits in India (no group relief).

Key takeaways

  • Tax expense = current tax + deferred tax (Ind AS 12.5); only current tax relates to cash, and even that with a timing lag.
  • Deferred tax expense = movement in net DTL recognised in P&L; movements on OCI items go to OCI.
  • Cash tax = current tax adjusted for movement in tax payable/receivable; deferred tax is a non-cash add-back.
  • Deferred tax balances are always non-current; offset only under Ind AS 12.74 conditions.
  • Build the tax tab as taxable-income bridge → loss memo → current/cash tax → deferred tax, with a check that ΔNet DTL equals the P&L plus OCI deferred tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. Splitting the tax line [L1]

A company reports PBT of ₹450 crore and total tax expense of ₹118 crore for FY26. Its opening net DTL was ₹62 crore and closing net DTL ₹74 crore; no deferred tax was recognised in OCI. Compute current tax and deferred tax and state which part is non-cash.

Q2. Cash tax from the balance sheet [L2]

For FY27E, current tax is ₹240 crore. Current tax liabilities (net) move from ₹35 crore to ₹18 crore, and income tax assets (net, advance tax and TDS in excess of provision) move from ₹50 crore to ₹62 crore. A refund of ₹9 crore relating to FY23 was received and is included in the asset movement. Compute cash taxes paid for the CFS and explain each adjustment.

💡 Hint: A fall in a liability and a rise in an asset both mean cash paid exceeded the expense.

Q3. OCI deferred tax and ETR [L2]

PBT is ₹300 crore, statutory rate 25.168%, no permanent differences. During the year, actuarial losses of ₹20 crore on gratuity are recognised in OCI, creating a deferred tax asset. An analyst books the ₹5.03 crore deferred tax credit in P&L. Show the effect on reported ETR and draft the correct entries.

Q4. Interview: why book tax is not cash tax [L1]

In under five sentences, explain to an FP&A manager why a retailer opening 200 stores a year shows a P&L tax charge well above taxes actually paid, and when the gap reverses.

Q5. Model integrity check [L3]

Design the check rows you would put on a tax tab to prove (a) P&L tax ties to current plus deferred tax, (b) the BS DTL rolls forward, and (c) the CFS tax paid ties to the BS current tax balances. Write the Excel formulas using named ranges of your choice and explain what each failure would typically indicate.
