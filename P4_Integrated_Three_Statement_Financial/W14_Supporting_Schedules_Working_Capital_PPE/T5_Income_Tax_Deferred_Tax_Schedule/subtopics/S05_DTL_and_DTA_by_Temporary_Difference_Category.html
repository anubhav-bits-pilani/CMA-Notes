Subtopic note

DTL and DTA by Temporary-Difference Category

Scope: DTL/DTA on PPE and other temporary differences (gratuity, leave encashment, ECL, leases)

Why it matters

Ind AS 12 uses the balance-sheet approach: deferred tax is the tax effect of differences between the carrying amount of an asset or liability and its tax base. For an Indian operating company the deferred tax note in the annual report typically has four to eight lines — PPE and intangibles (usually a DTL), and a set of DTAs on employee benefits, ECL allowance, leases, 43B items and occasionally tax losses. A forecast deferred tax schedule should mirror that note line by line so that the opening balances tie to the audited figures, each line has a clear driver in another schedule, and the P&L vs OCI split is right. Lumping everything into "DTL = x% of PPE" makes the ETR, the CFS add-back and the balance sheet all unexplainable.

Core notes

Definitions

  • Tax base of an asset: the amount deductible for tax against future taxable economic benefits when the carrying amount is recovered (Ind AS 12.7). For PPE it is the tax WDV.
  • Tax base of a liability: carrying amount less any amount that will be deductible for tax in future periods (Ind AS 12.8). For a gratuity provision not yet deductible, the tax base is nil.
  • Temporary difference = carrying amount − tax base (Ind AS 12.5).
  • Taxable temporary difference: asset carrying amount > tax base, or liability carrying amount < tax base → DTL.
  • Deductible temporary difference: asset carrying amount < tax base, or liability carrying amount > tax base → DTA.
  • Recognition: DTL for all taxable temporary differences except specified exemptions such as goodwill initial recognition (Ind AS 12.15); DTA to the extent it is probable that taxable profit will be available (Ind AS 12.24).
  • Measurement at enacted/substantively enacted rates expected to apply on reversal (Ind AS 12.47), reflecting the manner of recovery (Ind AS 12.51); no discounting (Ind AS 12.53).

\[ \text{Deferred tax}_{i} = (\text{Carrying amount}_i - \text{Tax base}_i) \times t \]

where \(i\) is each category and \(t\) is the enacted rate (25.168% under 115BAA). Sign convention: positive = DTL, negative = DTA.

Gratuity and leave encashment

Gratuity under Ind AS 19 is a defined benefit plan. The net liability changes through service cost and net interest (P&L) and remeasurements (OCI). Deferred tax follows the item: DTA movement relating to P&L charges goes to P&L; the part relating to actuarial gains/losses goes to OCI (Ind AS 12.61A). Contributions to an approved gratuity fund are deductible under Sec 36(1)(v), so the tax base of a funded plan's net liability is still nil — the DTA is on the net liability. Leave encashment (a long-term employee benefit with remeasurements through P&L under Ind AS 19) creates a DTA on the full provision, all through P&L.

ECL

The allowance for expected credit losses under Ind AS 109 reduces the carrying amount of receivables; the tax base is the gross amount because the deduction comes only on write-off (Sec 36(1)(vii)). DTA = ECL allowance × t. When a receivable is written off, the allowance is used, tax deduction arises and the DTA reverses — current tax falls, deferred tax rises, total tax unchanged.

Leases and the 2023 amendment

Under Ind AS 116 a lessee recognises an ROU asset and a lease liability at commencement. For tax, the lessee deducts lease rentals as paid; neither the ROU asset nor the liability has a tax base. Before the amendment, many entities applied the initial recognition exemption (Ind AS 12.15(b), 12.24) and recognised no deferred tax. The amendment notified by MCA on 31 March 2023 (effective for periods from 1 April 2023) narrows the exemption: it does not apply to a transaction that gives rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences at initial recognition (Ind AS 12.15(b)(iii), 12.22A). Hence a DTL on the ROU asset and a DTA on the lease liability are recognised gross (subject to DTA recoverability), and presented net if Ind AS 12.74 offset criteria are met. Same logic applies to decommissioning liabilities capitalised into PPE.

Over the lease life, the liability (front-loaded interest) exceeds the ROU NBV (straight-line depreciation), so the net position is a DTA that rises and then reverses to nil at lease end.

Excel structure

DT_PPE    = (Book_NBV - Tax_WDV) * Tax_Rate
DT_Grat   = -Gratuity_Liab * Tax_Rate
DT_Lease  = (ROU_NBV - Lease_Liab) * Tax_Rate
Net_DTL   = DT_PPE + DT_Grat + DT_Leave + DT_ECL + DT_Lease + DT_43B - DTA_Losses
DT_PL     = Net_DTL - Net_DTL_Prev - DT_OCI

Keep the tax rate as a single named input with a year-by-year row so that a future rate change automatically remeasures every category.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Goodwill. No DTL on initial recognition of goodwill (Ind AS 12.15(a)); since goodwill is no longer tax-depreciable, tax-deductible goodwill from older slump sales is a separate case.
  • Investments in subsidiaries. DTL on undistributed profits is not recognised if the parent controls timing and reversal is not probable in the foreseeable future (Ind AS 12.39); with dividends now taxed in the parent (subject to Sec 80M), this matters in consolidated models.
  • Indexation/capital-gains rate. FVTPL gains on equity and debt funds are measured at the applicable capital-gains rate, not 25.168%.
  • Offsetting. DTA of one entity cannot be netted against DTL of another in consolidation (Ind AS 12.74); present gross per entity.
  • DTA cap. DTAs on deductible differences are recognised to the extent of reversing DTLs plus probable future profits (Ind AS 12.28–29); a loss-making company's DTAs on gratuity and ECL may also be unrecognised.

Key takeaways

  • Deferred tax = (carrying amount − tax base) × enacted rate, category by category, undiscounted.
  • PPE usually gives a DTL; employee benefits, ECL and 43B items give DTAs.
  • Since 1 April 2023, leases and decommissioning obligations create gross DTL and DTA under the amended Ind AS 12.22A.
  • Deferred tax follows the underlying item: gratuity remeasurement DT goes to OCI.
  • Mirror the annual-report deferred tax note line by line so the opening balance ties.

Rate 25.168%. Prepare the deferred tax schedule and net DTL.

Q2. P&L vs OCI split [L2]

Opening net DTL ₹46.0 crore; closing net DTL ₹52.5 crore. During the year, gratuity remeasurement losses of ₹8 crore and FVOCI equity gains of ₹20 crore (deferred tax measured at the LTCG rate of 12.5% plus 10% surcharge and 4% cess, i.e., 14.30%) were recognised in OCI. Compute deferred tax through P&L and through OCI, and draft the entries.

Q3. Lease deferred tax over the term [L3]

A five-year store lease with annual payments of ₹10 crore in arrears, discount rate 9%. Build a schedule of ROU NBV, lease liability, DTL, DTA and net deferred tax at 25.168% for each year-end, and explain the pattern.

💡 Hint: Front-loaded interest makes the liability exceed the ROU asset in the middle years.

Q4. Judgement: ECL write-off [L1]

Q5. Model review case [L3]

A model shows a single row "DTL = 8% of net block" growing steadily. The company has large leases, a growing unfunded gratuity and rising ECL. Explain to the model owner what is wrong, what the revised schedule should contain, and how you would source opening balances.
