Subtopic note

Tax Loss Carry-Forward, Unabsorbed Depreciation and DTA Recognition

Scope: Loss carry-forward (8 years) and unabsorbed depreciation (indefinite); DTA recognition probability test

Why it matters

Turnaround cases, new-age companies approaching profitability, project SPVs and companies emerging from IBC resolution all carry tax losses. In a model these losses do two things: they shelter future taxable income (so current tax is zero or low for several years — a major driver of FCFF), and they may sit on the balance sheet as a deferred tax asset whose recognition is a significant audit judgement. Analysts frequently get both wrong: they forget the 8-year expiry for business losses, ignore the order of set-off, or assume a large DTA will be booked the moment the company turns profitable. Lenders and acquirers also care: Sec 79 can extinguish losses on a change of control.

Core notes

Statutory framework

Unabsorbed depreciation 32(2) Against any head except salary (and casual income) Indefinite No time limit; no requirement to file return of loss in time

Long-term capital loss 74 LTCG only 8 years Against LTCG only
Loss of specified business (Sec 35AD) 73A Only against specified business Indefinite —

Order of set-off in a later year. Current year depreciation is deducted first (it is part of computing current year business income), then brought-forward business losses (oldest first, to use them before expiry), then unabsorbed depreciation (because Sec 32(2) gives it effect only after Sec 72 set-off). This order maximises utilisation, since unabsorbed depreciation never expires.

Sec 79. For a company that is not one in which the public are substantially interested (i.e., unlisted/closely held), brought-forward business losses are lost if beneficial ownership of shares carrying more than 49% of voting power changes between the loss year and the set-off year (exceptions for IBC resolution plans approved under Sec 31 of IBC and certain start-ups). Unabsorbed depreciation is not affected by Sec 79.

115BAA interplay. Losses and unabsorbed depreciation attributable to forgone deductions (e.g., additional depreciation under 32(1)(iia)) cannot be set off by a 115BAA company (c06). In practice companies recompute the WDV and losses as if the additional depreciation had not been claimed.

Loss memorandum in the model

Loss_Avail_Open   = prior Loss_Avail_Close
Loss_Expired      = loss vintage reaching year 9 not used (track by vintage)
Loss_Used         = MIN(Loss_Avail_Open - Loss_Expired, MAX(0, Taxable_Pre_Loss))
Loss_Created      = MAX(0, -Taxable_Pre_Loss)
Loss_Avail_Close  = Loss_Avail_Open - Loss_Expired - Loss_Used + Loss_Created
Taxable_Income    = MAX(0, Taxable_Pre_Loss - Loss_Used)

For precision, keep two memos — business loss by vintage (for expiry) and unabsorbed depreciation (no expiry) — and use business loss first. The vintage layout is a small waterfall: each year's loss is a row, with utilisation allocated FIFO via cumulative MIN logic.

Illustration

A quick-commerce company files returns on time. Tax losses: FY24 business loss ₹300 crore (of which ₹120 crore is unabsorbed depreciation), FY25 business loss ₹200 crore (₹80 crore depreciation). Forecast taxable income before set-off: FY27E ₹150 crore, FY28E ₹280 crore, FY29E ₹400 crore. Pools at 1 April 2026: business loss ₹300 crore (180 + 120), unabsorbed depreciation ₹200 crore (120 + 80).

Note: when a year's loss includes depreciation, the depreciation component is carried as unabsorbed depreciation (Sec 32(2)) and only the balance is a business loss subject to the 8-year limit.

DTA recognition — the probability test

Ind AS 12.34: a DTA for carry-forward of unused tax losses and credits is recognised to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profit will be available against which they can be utilised. Ind AS 12.35: the existence of unused losses is strong evidence that future taxable profit may not be available; an entity with a history of recent losses recognises a DTA only to the extent it has sufficient taxable temporary differences or convincing other evidence. Ind AS 12.36 criteria:

  1. Sufficient taxable temporary differences (DTLs) with the same authority and entity, reversing in periods when the losses can be used;
  2. Probability of taxable profits before the losses expire;
  3. Whether losses result from identifiable causes unlikely to recur;
  4. Availability of tax planning opportunities (Ind AS 12.30).

Ind AS 12.37: unrecognised DTAs are reassessed at each reporting date and recognised when recovery becomes probable — producing a one-off deferred tax credit (an ETR distortion). Ind AS 12.56: carrying amount of recognised DTAs is reviewed and reduced if no longer probable. Ind AS 12.82: disclose the amount of a DTA and the evidence supporting recognition when utilisation depends on future profits beyond reversing DTLs and the entity has suffered a loss in the current or preceding period. Unrecognised losses and their expiry dates are disclosed under Ind AS 12.81(e).

SEBI and NFRA review comments have repeatedly questioned DTA recognition on losses based on optimistic business plans; auditors expect board-approved projections, sensitivity, and consistency with the impairment (Ind AS 36) cash flows.

Modelling DTA on losses

Two acceptable approaches:

  • Recognised DTA approach (match reported balance): DTA_losses = recognised pool × 25.168%; utilisation reduces DTA and creates deferred tax expense equal to the current tax saving — total tax expense ≈ 25.168% × PBT.
  • Unrecognised approach: no DTA; current tax is zero while losses are used, so book tax expense is also near zero and ETR is near nil — then jumps to 25.168% after losses are exhausted. If you expect the company to recognise the DTA at a future date, model a one-off deferred tax credit in that year and flag it.

In valuation, the cash benefit is what matters: the PV of tax saved by losses is part of FCFF regardless of whether a DTA is recognised. Do not count it twice by also adding the DTA balance to equity value.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Late filing forfeits carry-forward of business losses (not unabsorbed depreciation) — check whether historical returns were filed by the Sec 139(1) due date.
  • Amalgamation under Sec 72A: accumulated losses of the amalgamating company are deemed losses of the amalgamated company for a fresh 8 years, subject to conditions; relevant in merger models.
  • IBC acquisitions: Sec 79 relief applies, but MAT and 115BAA interplay and haircuts on loss quantum under assessment are common judgements.
  • Expiry cliffs: a big FY19 loss expires after AY 2027-28; if profits arrive late, losses lapse — show the expiry row explicitly.
  • Capital losses cannot shelter business income; keep them in a separate memo.

Key takeaways

  • Business losses carry forward 8 years and only if the return was filed on time; unabsorbed depreciation is indefinite.
  • Use business losses first, then unabsorbed depreciation; track vintages to catch expiry.
  • Sec 79 can kill business losses of closely held companies on a > 49% ownership change.
  • A DTA on losses needs convincing evidence of future taxable profit when there is a history of losses (Ind AS 12.34–36).
  • In valuation, value the cash tax shield; do not add the DTA balance on top.

Practice Questions

Q1. Loss memo with expiry [L2]

Business losses (excluding depreciation) by year: FY19 ₹80 crore, FY21 ₹60 crore, FY23 ₹50 crore; unabsorbed depreciation ₹90 crore. Taxable income before set-off: FY27E ₹40 crore, FY28E ₹70 crore, FY29E ₹120 crore. Business losses expire after 8 AYs following the AY of loss. Build the memo by vintage, identify any expiry, and compute current tax at 25.168%.

Q2. Write the FIFO formulas [L3]

Design an Excel layout (rows = loss vintages, columns = forecast years) that allocates utilisation FIFO, applies expiry, and feeds total utilisation to the tax schedule. Write the formulas for one cell of utilisation and for the expiry test.

💡 Hint: Cumulative utilisation available minus cumulative usage by older vintages gives each vintage's share.

Q3. DTA recognition judgement [L3]

A listed EV-component maker has losses for four years, ₹400 crore of unused business losses (expiring FY29–FY32) and a DTL on PPE of ₹30 crore. Management's plan shows taxable profits of ₹60 crore, ₹120 crore and ₹200 crore over the next three years based on a new OEM contract signed in February 2026. Evaluate how much DTA could be recognised, citing the relevant Ind AS 12 paragraphs, and list the evidence an auditor would ask for.

Q4. Sec 79 in an acquisition [L2]

A PE fund buys 60% of a private company with ₹150 crore of business losses and ₹70 crore of unabsorbed depreciation. Explain what survives and how you would show this in the acquisition model.

Q5. Interview: losses and valuation [L1]

Why should a DCF include the value of tax losses through lower cash taxes rather than by adding the balance-sheet DTA to equity value?
