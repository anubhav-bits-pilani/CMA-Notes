Key concept

Tax WDV Roll-Forward and Tax Depreciation Formula

Concept: Tax WDV_t = Opening WDV + Additions − Disposals − Tax dep; Tax dep = (Opening + Additions ≥180d) × rate + Additions <180d × rate ÷ 2

Statement

For each block of assets under Sec 32 of the Income-tax Act:

\[ \text{WDV}_t = \text{WDV}_{t-1} + A^{\ge 180}_t + A^{<180}_t - D_t - \text{TaxDep}_t \]

\[ \text{TaxDep}_t = \left(\text{WDV}_{t-1} + A^{\ge 180}_t - D_t\right) \times r + A^{<180}_t \times \frac{r}{2} \]

where \(\text{WDV}_{t-1}\) is opening written-down value of the block (Sec 43(6)), \(A^{\ge 180}_t\) additions put to use for 180 days or more in year \(t\), \(A^{<180}_t\) additions put to use for less than 180 days, \(D_t\) moneys payable on sale, discard, demolition or destruction (including scrap value), and \(r\) the Appendix I rate for the block. The concept text writes disposals in the WDV roll-forward only; in practice disposals must also reduce the base on which full-rate depreciation is computed, as shown.

Explanation

The block is a pool: individual assets lose their identity on entry, so depreciation is computed on the pool balance, not on each asset's cost. The 180-day restriction (second proviso to Sec 32(1)) halves the rate only in the year of acquisition; from the next year the addition sits in opening WDV at full rate. Since the half-year portion is never "caught up", WDV after year one is higher than it would be with a full-year charge, and depreciation is spread slightly later.

Conditions and exceptions:

  • If \(D_t\) exceeds \(\text{WDV}_{t-1} + A_t\), depreciation is nil, the excess is short-term capital gain (Sec 50), and WDV is nil.
  • If the block is emptied with a deficit, the deficit is a short-term capital loss and the block ceases.
  • Depreciation starts when the asset is "used for the purposes of business" — capital work-in-progress earns no tax depreciation.
  • Cost excludes GST input tax credit claimed and capital subsidies (Sec 43(1)).
  • 115BAA companies get no additional depreciation; old-regime manufacturers may add 20% under Sec 32(1)(iia) on new P&M.
  • Additional formula guard in models: MAX(0, Opening + Add_Full - Disposals) to prevent negative bases.

Illustration

Building block (10%): opening WDV ₹200 crore; FY27 additions — warehouse ₹50 crore put to use 1 June 2026, store premises ₹30 crore put to use 15 December 2026; no disposals.

\[ \text{TaxDep} = (200 + 50) \times 10\% + 30 \times 5\% = 25.0 + 1.5 = ₹26.5 \text{ crore} \]

\[ \text{WDV}_{FY27} = 200 + 50 + 30 - 26.5 = ₹253.5 \text{ crore} \]

FY28 depreciation (no additions) = 253.5 × 10% = ₹25.35 crore.

Excel, with a < 180-day share input:

=MAX(0, Open_WDV + Add_Total*(1-Half_Share) - Disposals)*Rate + Add_Total*Half_Share*Rate/2

Connections

Feeds the taxable income bridge (s03) and the tax WDV used in the DTL computation (c02); additions come from capitalisation in the PPE/CWIP schedule (w14_t2 s03), and the half-year share should be consistent with commissioning timing in the store-rollout driver from the Week 13 operating model. Contrasts with book vintage SLM depreciation (w14_t2 c03). Block-wise WDV vs asset-wise SLM is a topic pitfall.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the formula [L1]

P&M block: opening WDV ₹340 crore; additions ₹90 crore (put to use August) and ₹60 crore (put to use February); sale proceeds ₹15 crore. Compute FY27 tax depreciation and closing WDV.

Q2. Disposal exceeding the block [L2]

A computers block has opening WDV ₹2 crore and additions of ₹1 crore in April. In March the company sells old servers for ₹3.4 crore. Compute depreciation, WDV and the capital gain, and state how the model formula must be modified to handle this.

Q3. Model build [L3]

Write a single-row Excel formula set for a five-year tax WDV schedule that takes capex from the PPE tab, a block allocation %, a half-year share %, disposals as % of opening WDV, and returns depreciation and closing WDV with a negative-WDV flag. Explain each named input.

💡 Hint: Keep the base guard inside MAX(0, …) and flag separately rather than silently clipping.
