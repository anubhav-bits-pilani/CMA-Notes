Key concept

Cash Taxes in the CFS and the Deferred Tax Add-Back

Concept: CFS: Cash taxes = Current tax − ΔTax payable (+ ΔAdvance tax); ΔDTL is a non-cash add-back

Statement

\[ \text{Cash taxes}_t = \text{CT}_t - \left(\text{TP}_t - \text{TP}_{t-1}\right) + \left(\text{AT}_t - \text{AT}_{t-1}\right) \]

\[ \text{CFO}_t \ni + \Delta\text{Net DTL}^{P\&L}_t \]

where \(\text{CT}_t\) is current tax charged in P&L, \(\text{TP}\) current tax liabilities (net) on the balance sheet, \(\text{AT}\) income tax assets (net: advance tax and TDS in excess of provisions), and \(\Delta\text{Net DTL}^{P\&L}\) the deferred tax expense recognised in profit or loss (positive when net DTL rises). In a PAT-start indirect CFS the deferred tax expense is added back; in a PBT-start CFS (standard in Indian reporting) total tax is never deducted, and only cash taxes appear as "Income taxes paid (net of refunds)".

Explanation

Total tax expense in P&L = current tax + deferred tax (s01). Deferred tax has no cash effect in the year, so if CFO starts from PAT it must be added back — only the deferred component, never the whole tax expense (topic pitfall). Current tax is cash except for timing, and the balance sheet tax lines absorb the timing: an increase in tax payable means some of this year's tax is unpaid; an increase in the tax asset means more was paid than expensed (or refunds are pending).

Both presentations reconcile:

PAT-start (model style) PBT-start (Indian reported style)
PAT PBT
+ Deferred tax expense (no tax lines in adjustments)
+ ΔTax payable − ΔTax asset ... working capital ...
= includes tax effect − Income taxes paid (net of refunds)

Rules and exceptions:

  • Deferred tax recognised in OCI has no P&L or CFO effect — exclude it from the add-back.
  • Deferred tax acquired in a business combination moves the DTL without any P&L or cash effect; exclude it from ΔDTL when deriving the add-back from balance sheet movements.
  • Tax paid on capital gains on sale of an investment may be classified as investing if specifically identifiable (Ind AS 7.36); most models keep all tax in CFO.
  • In FCFF (Week 16), use operating cash taxes — tax on EBIT adjusted for deferred tax — rather than the CFS line.

Illustration

FY27E (₹ crore): PBT 600; current tax 145; deferred tax 12; PAT 443. Current tax liabilities opening 18, closing 25. Income tax assets opening 70, closing 64 (refund of ₹6 crore received).

\[ \text{Cash taxes} = 145 - (25 - 18) + (64 - 70) = 145 - 7 - 6 = ₹132 \text{ crore} \]

PAT-start CFO tax-related lines: PAT 443 + deferred tax 12 + ΔTax payable 7 + decrease in tax asset 6 = 468, which equals PBT 600 − cash tax 132 = 468. Consistent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute cash taxes [L1]

Current tax ₹88 crore; deferred tax expense ₹9 crore; tax payable opening ₹6 crore, closing ₹2 crore; income tax asset opening ₹30 crore, closing ₹41 crore. Compute cash taxes and the PAT-start CFO adjustments.

Q2. Spot the error [L2]

A model's CFS adds back "Tax expense" of ₹160 crore (current ₹140 crore, deferred ₹20 crore) and deducts "Tax paid" of ₹135 crore. Explain the error, its effect on CFO and on the balance check, and correct it.

💡 Hint: Ask what the add-back plus deduction implies about the tax payable balance.

Q3. Acquired DTL [L2]

During FY27 a company acquires a subsidiary whose PPE fair-value uplift creates a DTL of ₹25 crore. Consolidated net DTL rises from ₹110 crore to ₹150 crore. Deferred tax through OCI is nil. Compute the CFS deferred tax add-back and explain your treatment.
