Key concept

Effective Tax Rate Reconciliation

Concept: ETR reconciliation: PBT × statutory rate ± permanent differences ± rate-change effects = Tax expense

Statement

\[ \text{Tax expense}_t = \text{PBT}_t \times t^{stat} + \sum_k P_k \times t^{stat} + \Delta t \times TD^{open} + U_t + O_t \]

\[ \text{ETR}_t = \frac{\text{Tax expense}_t}{\text{PBT}_t} \]

where \(t^{stat}\) is the applicable statutory rate (25.168% for a 115BAA company), \(P_k\) permanent differences (positive = disallowed expense, negative = exempt income), \(\Delta t \times TD^{open}\) the rate-change remeasurement of opening temporary differences, \(U_t\) the effect of unrecognised DTAs (losses of the year not recognised, or previously unrecognised losses now used/recognised), and \(O_t\) other items (prior-year adjustments, income at different rates, uncertain tax positions). Temporary differences do not appear: deferred tax neutralises them.

Explanation

Ind AS 12.81(c) requires an explanation of the relationship between tax expense and accounting profit, either as a numerical reconciliation of tax expense to PBT × applicable rate, or of the average effective rate to the applicable rate, disclosing the basis of the applicable rate. Indian annual reports almost always present the first form.

For forecasting, the reconciliation is used in reverse: start from the statutory rate, add only those permanent items you expect to recur (CSR, Sec 14A, non-deductible ESOP cost, associate share in consolidated PBT), and set rate-change effects, prior-year adjustments and DTA recognition events to zero. That is why forecasts should not use a historical ETR averaged over years containing the 2019 115BAA remeasurement, a one-off DTA write-up or a large tax settlement (topic pitfall).

Key behaviours:

  • Permanent differences scale ETR by \(P / \text{PBT}\); a fixed CSR spend at 2% of average profit adds roughly 0.5 percentage points to ETR.
  • Rate-change effects are one-offs.
  • Losses with no DTA make ETR meaningless (tax ≈ 0 on a loss or on sheltered profit).
  • In consolidated statements, subsidiaries at different rates (overseas entities, old-regime subsidiaries) create a rate-differential line.

Materiality and documentation. In the model, show the reconciliation as a check: the computed ETR should be explainable line by line. Unexplained residual > 0.5 percentage points usually means a missing temporary difference or a DTA/DTL not rolled correctly.

Illustration

FY26 (₹ crore), 115BAA company, PBT ₹500:

Line Amount Rate effect
PBT × 25.168% 125.8 25.17%
CSR ₹8 crore not deductible 2.0 0.40%
Sec 14A disallowance ₹2 crore 0.5 0.10%
Exempt dividend under Sec 80M ₹6 crore (1.5) (0.30%)
Share of associate profit ₹20 crore (in consolidated PBT) (5.0) (1.01%)
Remeasurement on subsidiary switching to 115BAA (9.0) (1.80%)
Tax of earlier years 3.2 0.64%
Tax expense 116.0 23.20%

Forecast ETR, keeping only recurring items (CSR, 14A, 80M, associate): ≈ 24.4% of PBT; for standalone forecasts without associates ≈ 25.4%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a reconciliation [L2]

PBT ₹840 crore; tax expense ₹232 crore. Items: CSR ₹14 crore; penalty ₹3 crore; exempt income ₹10 crore; DTA on FY25 losses of a subsidiary recognised for the first time ₹6 crore; tax of earlier years (short provision) ₹9 crore. Prepare the reconciliation at 25.168% and identify any unexplained residual.

Q2. Forecast ETR [L2]

From the reconciliation in Q1, derive a forecast ETR assumption for FY27E–FY29E, explaining which items you would keep, which you would drop and how you would drive CSR.

Q3. Interview: low ETR red flag [L3]

A mid-cap company has reported ETR of 15–17% for three years while claiming to be under 115BAA. List the possible explanations and the specific notes and disclosures you would check to distinguish genuine items from aggressive positions.

💡 Hint: Look for associate income, unrecognised loss utilisation, special-rate income and uncertain tax provisions.
