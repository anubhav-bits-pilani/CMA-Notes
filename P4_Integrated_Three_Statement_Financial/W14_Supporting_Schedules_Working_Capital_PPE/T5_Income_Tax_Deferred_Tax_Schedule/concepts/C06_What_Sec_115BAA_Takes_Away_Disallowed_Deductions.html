Key concept

What Sec 115BAA Takes Away: Disallowed Deductions and Losses

Concept: 115BAA disallows additional depreciation Sec 32(1)(iia) and specified deductions and losses attributable to them

Statement

A company exercising the Sec 115BAA option must compute total income:

\[ TI^{115BAA} = TI^{normal} + \sum_j \text{Ded}_j + \text{AddDep}_{32(1)(iia)} - \Delta \text{NormalDep} + L^{attrib} \]

where \(\text{Ded}_j\) are the forgone specified deductions (Sec 10AA, 32AD, 33AB, 33ABA, 35(1)(ii)/(iia)/(iii), 35(2AA), 35(2AB), 35AD, 35CCC, 35CCD and Chapter VI-A Part C other than 80JJAA and 80M), \(\text{AddDep}\) the additional depreciation no longer claimable, \(\Delta \text{NormalDep}\) the change in normal depreciation arising because the WDV is now higher (additional depreciation never reduced it), and \(L^{attrib}\) brought-forward losses or unabsorbed depreciation attributable to those deductions, which can no longer be set off. Depreciation must be determined "in the prescribed manner" (Rule 5 as amended), i.e., at normal rates only.

Explanation

The concessional rate is a trade: lower rate in exchange for a cleaner base. Three modelling consequences follow.

  1. Additional depreciation. Old-regime manufacturers claim 20% (35% for notified backward areas) additional depreciation on new P&M in the year of installation (half if used < 180 days, balance next year). Under 115BAA this disappears. Since WDV is not reduced by additional depreciation that was never claimed, normal depreciation in later years is slightly higher — partly offsetting.
  2. Specified deductions. SEZ (10AA), R&D weighted deduction (35(2AB)) and infrastructure/industrial undertaking deductions (80-IA, 80-IB etc.) vanish. Retained: 80JJAA (30% additional deduction for new employee cost for three years) and 80M (inter-corporate dividends passed on). Chapter VI-A Part B deductions such as 80G are not barred.
  3. Attributable losses and unabsorbed depreciation. Losses or unabsorbed depreciation that arose because of the forgone deductions in earlier years cannot be set off. For unabsorbed additional depreciation, the amended Rule 5 did not simply extinguish the amount: where the option was exercised for AY 2020-21, the opening WDV of the P&M block was increased by the unabsorbed additional depreciation, so the amount is recovered through normal depreciation over future years rather than lost. CBDT Circular 29/2019 further clarified that a 115BAA company may still claim deductions other than those specified, and that MAT credit cannot be used. For an opt-in in a later year, confirm the mechanics with the company's tax team and the current Rules before applying them in a deliverable.

Also: MAT credit lapses (s02); claiming a barred deduction in a later year makes the option invalid for that year and onwards.

Connections

Defines the regime conditions in s02; changes the tax WDV schedule (s04, c01) and the loss memo (s06). The switch-year NPV analysis compares old-regime tax including these deductions with 115BAA tax. Relevant to capex-heavy manufacturing models in Phase 6 capital budgeting, where additional depreciation was historically part of project IRR. The Income-tax Act, 2025 carries equivalent provisions under new numbering effective 1 April 2026.

Practice Questions

Q1. Recompute under 115BAA [L2]

Old-regime FY27 computation: income after normal depreciation ₹520 crore; Sec 10AA SEZ deduction ₹60 crore; additional depreciation ₹30 crore; Sec 80JJAA ₹8 crore; Sec 80G donations deduction ₹2 crore. Compute total income and tax under both regimes and the saving or cost of switching.

Q2. Attributable losses [L3]

A company has unabsorbed depreciation of ₹90 crore at 1 April 2026, of which ₹35 crore arises from additional depreciation claimed in FY24–FY25. It opts for 115BAA from FY27. Explain how the ₹35 crore is treated, how the opening WDV and the loss memo in your model change, and what you would verify with the company's tax team.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the disallowed amount is lost outright or restored to the block.
