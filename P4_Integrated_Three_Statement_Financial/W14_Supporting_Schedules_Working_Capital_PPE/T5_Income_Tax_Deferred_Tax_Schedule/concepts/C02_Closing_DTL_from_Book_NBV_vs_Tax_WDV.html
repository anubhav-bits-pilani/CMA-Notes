Key concept

Closing DTL from Book NBV vs Tax WDV

Concept: Closing DTL = (Book NBV − Tax WDV) × enacted rate; Deferred tax expense = ΔNet DTL

Statement

\[ \text{DTL}^{PPE}_t = \left(\text{NBV}^{book}_t - \text{WDV}^{tax}_t\right) \times t^{enacted} \]

\[ \text{Deferred tax expense}_t = \text{Net DTL}_t - \text{Net DTL}_{t-1} \; (- \text{ amounts recognised in OCI/equity}) \]

where \(\text{NBV}^{book}_t\) is the Ind AS carrying amount of depreciable PPE and intangibles at year-end, \(\text{WDV}^{tax}_t\) the aggregate tax written-down value of the corresponding blocks, \(t^{enacted}\) the rate enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date for the period of reversal (25.168% under Sec 115BAA), and Net DTL the sum across all categories (DTLs positive, DTAs negative). A negative result for PPE is a DTA.

Explanation

The carrying amount of PPE will be recovered through use, generating taxable income; the deduction available against that income is the tax WDV. If book NBV exceeds tax WDV, future taxable profit will exceed accounting profit by the difference, so tax is owed in future — a liability today (Ind AS 12.15–16). Measurement uses the enacted rate (Ind AS 12.47) without discounting (Ind AS 12.53).

The second line is the balance-sheet approach in income-statement form: the P&L deferred tax charge is simply the change in the balance, after removing the portion that belongs to OCI (e.g., DTA on gratuity remeasurement) or equity, or that arises from a business combination (goodwill). A rate change remeasures the entire opening difference, so deferred tax expense includes a "rate-change effect" equal to opening difference × (new rate − old rate) — shown separately in the ETR reconciliation (c04).

Points to watch:

  • Exclude assets with no tax base (ROU assets — handled in the lease category; goodwill — no DTL on initial recognition, Ind AS 12.15(a); freehold land — non-depreciable for both, though indexation-related differences may exist).
  • Revalued PPE (Ind AS 16 revaluation model): DTL on revaluation surplus goes to OCI.
  • Net DTL must equal the BS deferred tax line after offsetting under Ind AS 12.74.

Illustration

₹ crore FY26A FY27E FY28E
Book NBV (excluding ROU, land) 1,800 2,050 2,250
Tax WDV 1,420 1,600 1,740
Temporary difference 380 450 510
DTL @ 25.168% 95.6 113.3 128.4
Deferred tax expense (PPE line) — 17.6 15.1

If in FY28 a Finance Act raised surcharge so the rate became 26.0%, closing DTL would be 510 × 26.0% = ₹132.6 crore; the FY28 charge of ₹19.3 crore would split into a rate-change effect on the opening difference (450 × 0.832% = ₹3.7 crore) and the originating difference effect (60 × 26.0% = ₹15.6 crore).

Connections

Book NBV comes from the PPE corkscrew (w14_t2 c01, c05); tax WDV from c01 and s04. The PPE line is one category in the full schedule (s05). Deferred tax expense is a non-cash add-back in the CFS (c05) and appears in the balance check of Week 15. In valuation, a growing DTL is a quasi-permanent deferral that boosts FCFF via lower cash taxes (Week 16). Topic pitfall: using ETR rather than this mechanism to derive tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute DTL and expense [L1]

Q2. Rate change decomposition [L2]
