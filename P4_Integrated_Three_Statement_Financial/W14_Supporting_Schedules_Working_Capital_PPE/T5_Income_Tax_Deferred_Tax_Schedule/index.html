Roadmap topic: Income Tax & Deferred Tax Schedule: Modeling cash taxes vs book tax expense and tracking timing differences between Ind AS depreciation and Income Tax Act WDV depreciation

Overview

The tax schedule converts the operating model's PBT into three distinct outputs: the P&L tax expense (current plus deferred, Ind AS 12), the balance sheet tax balances (net DTL/DTA, current tax liabilities and income tax assets), and the cash taxes that leave the business each year. In Indian three-statement models these three numbers are routinely collapsed into one — "tax = PBT × ETR" — which is harmless only for a steady-state company with no capex growth, no losses and no regime transitions. For the companies FP&A teams and analysts actually model — retailers rolling out hundreds of stores, cement and steel producers in capex cycles, platform companies turning profitable on accumulated losses — the gap between book tax and cash tax drives cash flow, debt capacity and valuation.

The schedule rests on two parallel asset registers. On the book side, Ind AS 16 and Schedule II produce net block through straight-line or WDV depreciation over useful life (w14_t2). On the tax side, Sec 32 of the Income-tax Act produces block-wise WDV depreciation at statutory rates with a half-rate rule for assets used less than 180 days. The difference between book NBV and tax WDV, multiplied by the enacted rate, is the deferred tax liability on PPE — usually the largest deferred tax balance. Around that core sit smaller temporary differences (gratuity, leave encashment, ECL, leases, 43B items), the regime decision under Sec 115BAA (25.168%, no MAT, irrevocable), the loss memo with its 8-year expiry and indefinite unabsorbed depreciation, and the advance tax calendar that times the cash.

The learning sequence follows the build order of the tax tab. Start with the accounting identity (s01: book tax = current + deferred; cash ≠ book), fix the rate and regime (s02), compute taxable income from PBT (s03) using tax depreciation from the WDV block schedule (s04), compute deferred tax category by category (s05), handle losses and DTA recognition (s06), and finally time the cash (s07). The concepts formalise each step: the WDV roll-forward (c01), the DTL formula (c02), current tax after loss set-off (c03), the ETR reconciliation that proves the schedule (c04), the CFS treatment (c05) and the 115BAA trade-offs (c06). Interest from the debt schedule (w14_t3) enters PBT, so when interest is computed on average balances the tax schedule sits inside the circularity loop managed in w14_t4.

For target roles this matters in concrete ways: technical accounting teams prepare the Ind AS 12 note and the rate reconciliation; financial reporting analysts explain ETR movements to audit committees; FP&A teams forecast advance tax for treasury; and valuers use the marginal 25.17% in NOPAT and the after-tax cost of debt.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 12 DTL schedule and the 2023 single-transaction amendment

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 12 DTL schedule, 2023 amendment on leases/decommissioning (single-transaction deferred tax)

Phase 1 built the accounting skeleton: tax bases, temporary differences, recognition exceptions and the rate reconciliation. The modelling schedule here is that note projected forward. The 2023 amendment (MCA notification of 31 March 2023, effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023) removed the initial recognition exemption for transactions that create equal taxable and deductible temporary differences — principally Ind AS 116 leases and decommissioning liabilities capitalised into PPE. A forecast must therefore carry a gross DTL on ROU assets and a gross DTA on lease liabilities (net presentation if offset criteria are met), driven from the lease corkscrew. Re-read the Phase 1 notes on Ind AS 12.15, 12.22A, 12.24 and the offsetting rule in 12.74 before building the lease line of the deferred tax schedule.

Book depreciation from the PPE corkscrew

→ Week 14 (w14_t2): book depreciation feeds the temporary difference

The PPE schedule produces book depreciation by layer (existing assets run-off plus new-capex vintages) and closing net block. Both are inputs here: book depreciation is added back in the taxable income bridge, and book NBV is compared with tax WDV to compute the DTL. Capex from the same schedule drives tax additions, and the CWIP capitalisation timing determines when tax depreciation starts and whether the 180-day half rate applies. Consistency matters: if the PPE tab excludes ROU assets and goodwill, the tax WDV comparison must exclude them too. Any change to useful lives or residual values on the PPE tab automatically changes the DTL — a useful sensitivity for testing whether the link is live.

Marginal 25.17% in NOPAT and after-tax cost of debt

→ Week 16 (w16_t1, w16_t2): marginal 25.17% in NOPAT and Kd × (1 − t)

Valuation uses the marginal tax rate, not the reported ETR. NOPAT in FCFF is EBIT × (1 − 25.17%) for a 115BAA company in steady state, because one-off items, permanent differences tied to non-operating income and rate-change effects do not recur. During loss utilisation, explicit-period FCFF should use cash taxes from this schedule (lower than 25.17% × EBIT), with the terminal year at the full marginal rate. Similarly, the tax shield on debt in WACC is Kd × (1 − 25.17%), assuming the company is tax-paying at the margin — if it carries large unused losses, the shield is deferred and Kd × (1 − t) overstates it. The tax schedule built here supplies both numbers.

Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbering

Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026): renumbered sections; verify mapping of 115BAA/Sec 32 equivalents

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act for tax years beginning 1 April 2026 and introduces the "tax year" concept in place of previous year/assessment year. It was designed as a simplification and re-numbering exercise: the concessional corporate regime, block-of-assets depreciation, carry-forward rules and advance tax calendar are carried over, but section numbers change. For models covering FY27 onwards, label inputs by concept (concessional regime rate, P&M block rate) rather than section number, keep a mapping table on the inputs sheet from 1961-Act sections to 2025-Act equivalents, and verify each mapping against the enacted text and CBDT guidance before citing sections in client or audit deliverables. Historical years (FY26 and earlier) remain governed by the 1961 Act.

Common pitfalls

Forecasting with a distorted historical ETR

Forecasting with a one-off-distorted historical ETR instead of the statutory 25.17%

The mistake: averaging the last three to five years' reported ETR and applying it to forecast PBT. People do it because ETR is a single visible number and averaging feels conservative. But historical ETRs in India are full of one-offs: the FY20 remeasurement of DTLs on moving to 115BAA, MAT credit write-offs, first-time DTA recognition on losses, prior-year tax adjustments after assessments, and settlements under dispute-resolution schemes. Correct treatment: start from 25.168%, add back only recurring permanent differences identified from the rate reconciliation (Ind AS 12.81(c)), and let deferred tax be driven by the temporary-difference schedule. Detection in review: compare forecast ETR with the reconciliation note; if the model's ETR equals a historical average to two decimals, ask which reconciling items are expected to recur.

Asset-wise SLM tax depreciation

Tax depreciation computed asset-wise on SLM instead of block-wise WDV

The mistake: setting tax depreciation equal to book depreciation, or applying SLM at tax rates to each asset. It happens because modellers reuse the book vintage waterfall for tax. Indian tax law (other than power undertakings under Rule 5(1A)) requires block-wise WDV at Appendix I rates with the 180-day half rate. Correct treatment: a separate WDV block schedule (s04) for each relevant block, seeded with opening WDV from the tax audit report or back-solved from the deferred tax note. Detection: if tax depreciation equals book depreciation, the DTL on PPE stays flat or zero despite heavy capex — a red flag; also check that tax depreciation declines geometrically on a block with no additions.

Adding back total tax expense in the CFS

Adding total tax expense back in CFS instead of only the deferred component

The mistake: a PAT-start CFS adds back the full P&L tax charge and then deducts cash tax — or adds back total tax without deducting anything. It arises from mixing the PBT-start Indian reporting format with a PAT-start model format. Correct treatment: in a PAT-start CFS add back only deferred tax expense (non-cash) and adjust for movements in current tax payable/receivable; in a PBT-start CFS, show only "income taxes paid (net of refunds)" (c05, Ind AS 7.35). Detection: the balance sheet fails to balance by the current tax amount, or CFO exceeds EBITDA less cash tax computed independently; build a check that cash tax per CFS = current tax − Δpayable + Δreceivable.

DTA on losses without convincing evidence

Recognising DTA on losses without convincing evidence of future taxable profit

The mistake: booking a DTA on the whole loss pool as soon as the forecast turns profitable, inflating equity and producing a large deferred tax credit. Analysts do it to "smooth" ETR, and management may push for it to protect net worth or covenants. Ind AS 12.35 says unused losses are strong evidence that future profits may not be available; recognition requires sufficient reversing DTLs or convincing other evidence, and Ind AS 12.82 requires disclosure of the supporting evidence. Correct treatment: recognise only to the extent of reversing DTLs plus profits that are probable on evidence that would satisfy an auditor (contracts, history of meeting plans, non-recurring causes of losses); model unrecognised losses through the loss memo, which still reduces cash tax. Detection: compare DTA on losses with the next three years' forecast taxable income and the expiry profile; check consistency with Ind AS 36 impairment cash flows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full tax schedule build for a store-led retailer [L3]

A Nifty-500 apparel retailer (115BAA, rate 25.168%) has the following FY26A closing balances (₹ crore): book NBV of owned PPE 1,450; tax WDV — furniture & fittings 520, P&M 260, computers 40; gratuity liability 30; leave encashment 18; ECL allowance 6; ROU assets 1,900; lease liabilities 2,150; current tax liabilities (net) 12; income tax assets (net) 45; no tax losses. Forecast FY27E–FY29E: PBT 600, 720, 860; book depreciation on owned PPE 160, 185, 210; capex (all capitalised in year) 380, 420, 460 split 60% furniture, 30% P&M, 10% computers, with 40% commissioned after 3 October each year; gratuity and leave liabilities grow 10% p.a.; ECL allowance 0.5% of revenue of 8,000, 9,400, 11,000; ROU assets and lease liabilities grow 15% p.a.; CSR at 2% of prior-year PBT; current tax liabilities at 5% of current tax; income tax assets constant. (a) Build the tax WDV block schedules. (b) Build the taxable income bridge and current tax. (c) Build the deferred tax schedule by category and the deferred tax expense. (d) Compute cash taxes. (e) Prepare an ETR reconciliation for each year.

💡 Hint: Treat the change in (ROU − lease liability) and in the employee and ECL provisions as temporary adjustments in the bridge so current and deferred tax stay consistent.

Q2. Regime switch with MAT credit and losses [L3]

An old-regime specialty chemicals company has MAT credit of ₹55 crore, unabsorbed depreciation of ₹80 crore (₹25 crore from additional depreciation), and an SEZ unit whose Sec 10AA deduction (₹40 crore p.a.) ends after FY28. Forecast income before SEZ deduction and additional depreciation: FY27E ₹350 crore, FY28E ₹390 crore, FY29E ₹430 crore; additional depreciation available ₹20 crore p.a. Book profit for MAT ₹380 crore, ₹420 crore, ₹460 crore. Opening DTL on PPE ₹70 crore measured at 34.944%. (a) Compute tax under both regimes for each year. (b) Recommend the opt-in year. (c) Draft the journal entries in the opt-in year for deferred tax remeasurement and MAT credit. (d) Explain how the opt-in affects reported ETR and cash taxes in that year.

Q3. Loss-making platform turning profitable [L3]

A listed quick-commerce company has, at 31 March 2026, business losses of ₹1,200 crore (vintages: FY21 ₹300 crore, FY22 ₹400 crore, FY23 ₹500 crore) and unabsorbed depreciation of ₹350 crore; no DTA is recognised. Forecast taxable income before set-off: FY27E ₹(150) crore, FY28E ₹200 crore, FY29E ₹600 crore, FY30E ₹900 crore. Book NBV exceeds tax WDV by ₹120 crore, growing ₹30 crore p.a. (a) Build the loss memo with expiry (8 AYs). (b) Compute current tax. (c) Evaluate whether and when a DTA could be recognised under Ind AS 12.34–36, and model the P&L effect if recognised at 31 March 2028. (d) Explain how the loss shield should be reflected in a DCF versus the balance sheet.

Q4. Quarterly cash tax and covenant headroom [L2]

A cement company estimates FY27 current tax at ₹480 crore (TDS ₹30 crore evenly) and has a minimum cash covenant of ₹200 crore tested quarterly. Opening cash on 1 April 2026 is ₹650 crore; quarterly pre-tax operating cash flow is ₹120 crore, ₹60 crore, ₹180 crore and ₹260 crore; capex ₹150 crore per quarter; FY26 self-assessment tax of ₹25 crore is payable in July 2026. (a) Schedule advance tax by instalment. (b) Project quarter-end cash. (c) Identify any covenant pressure and propose two mitigations, with their tax-interest consequences under Sec 234C.

Q5. Review of a client tax tab [L2]

You inherit a model where: tax = PBT × 27.3% (five-year average ETR); DTL is held flat; the CFS adds back "provision for tax" and deducts "tax paid = 95% of tax expense"; tax depreciation equals book depreciation. Write a review memo listing each issue, the pitfall it represents, its likely direction of error on FY27E–FY29E PAT, CFO and FCFF, and the corrected design.
