Subtopic note

Schedule II Lives, Residual Value, SLM vs WDV and Componentisation

Scope: Schedule II useful lives, residual value ≤ 5% of cost, SLM vs WDV; Ind AS 16 componentisation

Why it matters

The depreciation assumptions in a model — life, residual value and method — must be defensible against the company's accounting policy note, which in India is anchored to Schedule II of the Companies Act 2013 and Ind AS 16. An analyst who assumes a 20-year life for plant that the company depreciates over 15 years understates depreciation by a quarter; one who uses WDV for a company on SLM front-loads expense. Componentisation matters for asset-heavy sectors (power, airlines, refineries, telecom) where significant parts — turbines, aircraft engines, catalyst beds, towers vs electronics — have different lives, and for the major-inspection and overhaul costs that would otherwise be expensed. These choices also determine the book-tax difference that drives deferred tax (t5).

Core notes

Schedule II framework

Schedule II (Part A) sets the rule that depreciation is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount (cost less residual value) over the useful life, and includes amortisation of intangibles. Part C prescribes indicative useful lives. Key points:

  • For companies in general, the useful life should not ordinarily differ from Part C, and residual value shall not exceed 5% of original cost; if a company uses a different life or residual value, it must disclose the justification (Part A, para 3(i)).
  • For Ind AS companies (the class prescribed under Sec 133), useful lives and residual values may differ from Part C based on technical assessment, with disclosure of justification (Part A, para 3(ii)). In practice, most Indian companies stay close to Part C lives and the 5% residual cap, with technical-evaluation-based departures disclosed (e.g., moulds, specific plant).
  • Lives in Part C assume single-shift working. For assets other than those marked NESD (no extra shift depreciation), depreciation increases by 50% for the period of double-shift use and by 100% for triple-shift use.
  • Useful lives of significant parts with different lives must be determined separately (componentisation note to Part C, mandatory from FY 2015-16).
  • Depreciation on assets added or disposed during the year is on a pro-rata basis.

SLM vs WDV

Schedule II permits either method; Ind AS 16.60–62 requires the method to reflect the pattern in which future economic benefits are consumed, reviewed at least at each financial year-end (Ind AS 16.61); a change is a change in accounting estimate under Ind AS 8, applied prospectively. Revenue-based depreciation is not appropriate (Ind AS 16.62A), with a Schedule II carve-out permitting revenue-based amortisation of toll-road intangibles under service concession arrangements.

Straight-line (SLM):

\[ \text{Dep} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual value}}{\text{Useful life}} \]

Written-down value (WDV): a constant rate \( r \) on opening NBV, chosen so that NBV reaches residual value at the end of life:

\[ r = 1 - \left( \frac{\text{Residual value}}{\text{Cost}} \right)^{1/n} \]

where \( n \) is useful life in years. With residual 5% and 15 years, \( r = 1 - 0.05^{1/15} \approx 18.10\% \); for 10 years, \( r \approx 25.89\% \). WDV with zero residual is mathematically undefined (never reaches zero) — a reason the 5% residual matters for WDV users.

Most large Indian listed companies use SLM for books (a few, such as some PSUs and older mid-caps, use WDV). Tax depreciation under the Income-tax Act is block-wise WDV at prescribed rates (t5), unrelated to book method.

Residual value

Residual value is the estimated amount the entity would currently obtain from disposal, net of disposal costs, if the asset were already of the age and condition expected at the end of its life (Ind AS 16.6). It must be reviewed at least annually (Ind AS 16.51). For modelling: use 0–5% of cost per the policy; for vehicles or aircraft with active secondary markets, residuals may be higher with justification. If residual value rises to equal or exceed carrying amount, depreciation is zero until it falls below (Ind AS 16.54).

Ind AS 16 componentisation

Ind AS 16.43: each part of an item of PPE with a cost that is significant in relation to total cost must be depreciated separately. Parts with the same life and method may be grouped (16.45); the remainder is depreciated together (16.46).

Examples:

  • Power plant: boiler, turbine, generator, civil works at different lives.
  • Aircraft: airframe, engines, landing gear, cabin interiors; heavy maintenance checks as separate components.
  • Retail store (owned): building shell (60 years), HVAC (15 years), fit-outs (8 years).
  • Telecom tower: steel structure vs electronics/batteries.

Major inspections and overhauls (Ind AS 16.14): the cost of a major inspection is recognised in the carrying amount as a replacement if recognition criteria are met; any remaining carrying amount of the previous inspection is derecognised. In the model, overhaul cycles become capex every \( n \) years with a separate depreciation layer, rather than opex.

Replacements (Ind AS 16.13, 16.70): the replaced part's carrying amount is derecognised even if it was not depreciated separately — model as a disposal with NBV estimated.

Spare parts (Ind AS 16.8): major spares and standby equipment meeting the PPE definition are capitalised; others are inventory.

Modelling implementation

  • Map each asset class to a life and residual on the assumptions tab, with Schedule II reference and company policy side by side.
  • Use a single named range per class for life, so the vintage waterfall (s04, c03–c04) and the cap (c05) use the same figure.
  • For WDV companies, depreciation per vintage = rate × opening NBV of that vintage; a SUMPRODUCT waterfall does not apply directly, so use a vintage-by-year NBV triangle.
  • Add a shift-factor input for P&M where multi-shift working is significant (e.g., 1.5 for double shift), multiplying SLM charge, with the cap still applying.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Transition to Schedule II in 2014 forced many companies to adopt shorter lives; carrying amounts of assets whose remaining life was nil were adjusted to retained earnings. Historical depreciation trends around FY15 are not comparable.
  • Technical evaluation departures (e.g., moulds depreciated over 3 years against 15) are common in auto and FMCG; follow the company's disclosed lives.
  • Land — freehold not depreciated; leasehold land under long-term leases is an ROU asset under Ind AS 116 (s06).
  • Change in useful life after a technology shift (e.g., 4G equipment once 5G rolls out) accelerates depreciation prospectively.
  • Componentisation of inherited assets on Ind AS transition with deemed cost — components may not be separately identifiable; disclosures may be aggregate.

Key takeaways

  • Schedule II Part C gives indicative lives; residual value is capped at 5% of cost unless justified and disclosed.
  • Ind AS companies can depart from Part C lives with technical justification; model the company's policy, not the table.
  • SLM is the norm for Indian books; WDV rate \( = 1 - (\text{RV} \div \text{Cost})^{1/n} \).
  • Componentise significant parts with different lives; capitalise major inspections as separate components.
  • Lives, residuals and methods are reviewed annually; changes are prospective estimate changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. SLM and WDV comparison [L1]

A machine costing ₹12 crore has a 10-year life and 5% residual value. Compute the WDV rate, and the depreciation for years 1–3 under SLM and WDV. State which method a typical NSE-listed manufacturer would use for books and why.

Q2. Componentisation of a power plant [L2]

A ₹3,000 crore thermal plant comprises: civil works ₹900 crore (life 40 years), boiler ₹800 crore (25 years), turbine-generator ₹1,000 crore (25 years), control systems ₹300 crore (10 years). Residual value 5% for all. A major overhaul costing ₹120 crore is required every 5 years. Compute annual depreciation with componentisation versus a single 25-year life, and show how the overhaul is modelled.

💡 Hint: Group components with the same life; treat the overhaul as a 5-year component replaced each cycle.

Q3. Multi-shift depreciation [L2]

A press line costing ₹60 crore (P&M, 15-year life, 5% residual, not NESD) works single-shift for 4 months, double-shift for 5 months and triple-shift for 3 months of FY26. Compute FY26 depreciation under Schedule II's extra-shift rule.

Q4. Judgement: useful life departure [L3]

A smartphone component maker depreciates its SMT lines over 6 years against the Schedule II P&M life of 15 years, citing technical evaluation. A competitor uses 15 years. Explain how you would compare their EBIT margins, adjust for valuation, and what disclosure you would expect to see.
