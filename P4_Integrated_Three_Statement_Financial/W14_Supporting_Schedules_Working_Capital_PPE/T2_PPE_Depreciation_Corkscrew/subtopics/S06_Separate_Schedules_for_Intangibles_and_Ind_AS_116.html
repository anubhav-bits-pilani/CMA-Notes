Subtopic note

Separate Schedules for Intangibles and Ind AS 116 ROU Assets

Scope: Separate schedules for intangibles (amortisation) and Ind AS 116 ROU assets

Why it matters

Tangible PPE, intangible assets and right-of-use (ROU) assets follow different standards, have different drivers and feed different lines of the financial statements. For an Indian fashion retailer, ROU assets on store leases are often larger than owned PPE, and ROU depreciation plus lease interest replaced rent expense when Ind AS 116 took effect from 1 April 2019 — lifting EBITDA by the full rent amount. For a telecom operator, spectrum licences are the dominant asset; for a pharma or IT company, software and acquired customer relationships drive amortisation. Lumping them into one PPE corkscrew misstates EBITDA, mixes capex with lease additions (which are non-cash), and hides the lease liability that belongs in net debt. Separate schedules keep each economic story and each Schedule III line clean.

Core notes

Intangible asset schedule (Ind AS 38)

Corkscrew, identical in form to PPE:

\[ \text{Intangibles NBV}_t = \text{Opening} + \text{Additions (incl. from intangibles under development)} - \text{Amortisation} - \text{Impairment} - \text{Disposals} \]

Key rules:

  • Finite-life intangibles are amortised over useful life on a systematic basis reflecting consumption of benefits (Ind AS 38.97); residual value is assumed zero unless there is a commitment by a third party to buy, or an active market (Ind AS 38.100). Revenue-based amortisation is rebuttably presumed inappropriate (Ind AS 38.98A), except for the toll-road service-concession carve-out in Schedule II.
  • Indefinite-life intangibles (some brands, certain licences) are not amortised but tested for impairment annually under Ind AS 36, with the indefinite-life assessment reviewed every period (Ind AS 38.107–110).
  • Goodwill is not amortised under Ind AS; it is tested annually for impairment. Forecast goodwill flat unless acquisitions are modelled.
  • Internally generated intangibles: research expensed; development capitalised only when the Ind AS 38.57 criteria (technical feasibility, intention, ability, future benefits, resources, reliable measurement) are met.
  • Intangible assets under development — Schedule III requires a separate line and ageing, analogous to CWIP.

Typical forecast drivers:

Intangible Addition driver Amortisation life (typical)
Computer software / ERP % of revenue or IT capex plan 3–6 years
Spectrum / telecom licences Auction calendar, deferred payment terms Licence period (often 20 years)
Acquired brands, customer relationships (PPA) Only on acquisitions Per PPA; brands may be indefinite
Product development (pharma ANDAs, auto platforms) R&D capitalisation % 5–10 years
Toll-road concession rights Construction capex Concession period, possibly revenue-based

Model amortisation with the same vintage waterfall as PPE (s04) and an existing-layer run-off. Present amortisation within "Depreciation and amortisation expense"; in a valuation, separate PPA amortisation (non-cash, non-recurring in substance) for adjusted EPS.

ROU asset schedule (Ind AS 116)

At commencement a lessee recognises a lease liability at the present value of unpaid lease payments, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease or the incremental borrowing rate (Ind AS 116.26), and an ROU asset at (Ind AS 116.24):

\[ \text{ROU}_0 = \text{Lease liability}_0 + \text{Prepaid lease payments} + \text{Initial direct costs} + \text{Restoration costs} - \text{Lease incentives received} \]

The ROU is depreciated SLM over the shorter of lease term and useful life, or over useful life if ownership transfers or a purchase option is reasonably certain (Ind AS 116.31–32).

ROU corkscrew:

\[ \text{ROU}_t = \text{ROU}_{t-1} + \text{New leases} \pm \text{Remeasurements/modifications} - \text{ROU depreciation} - \text{Terminations} \]

New leases are non-cash additions: they do not appear in capex or CFI. The cash outflow appears later as lease payments in CFF (principal) and CFF/CFO (interest), per Ind AS 7.17(c) and Ind AS 116.50. The paired lease-liability corkscrew (Opening + New leases + Interest − Lease payments = Closing) is a week cross-cutting item and belongs with the debt schedules, but its new-lease input is shared with the ROU schedule.

Driving new leases from store additions

For a retailer the Week 13 store driver is the anchor:

\[ \text{New lease liability}_t = \text{New stores}_t \times \text{Annual fixed rent per store} \times \text{PV annuity factor}(r, n) \]

where \( r \) is the incremental borrowing rate and \( n \) the lease term including reasonably certain extensions (Ind AS 116.18–19). Example: 120 new stores, fixed rent ₹1.2 crore per store a year, 9-year term, IBR 9%: annuity factor ≈ 5.995, so new leases ≈ \( 120 \times 1.2 \times 5.995 = 863 \) crore. ROU depreciation for this vintage ≈ ₹863 crore ÷ 9 ≈ ₹96 crore a year.

Exclusions from the lease liability matter in Indian retail:

  • Variable lease payments not based on an index or rate (revenue-share rent, common in malls) are expensed as incurred (Ind AS 116.38) and remain in EBITDA.
  • Short-term (≤ 12 months) and low-value leases may be expensed under the exemption (Ind AS 116.5–8).

So a model must split rent into fixed (Ind AS 116) and variable/short-term (opex) components.

Why separate from PPE

Issue If mixed into PPE
EBITDA ROU depreciation would look like capex-driven D&A; EBITDA vs EBITDA-pre-Ind AS 116 comparisons become impossible
Cash flow New leases would be treated as cash capex, overstating CFI outflow and understating FCF; or omitted, breaking the BS
Net debt Lease liabilities would not be identified for the EV bridge or covenant definitions (many bank covenants exclude them)
Tax Lease deferred tax (ROU vs liability; 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment) needs its own base (t5)
Ratios Fixed-asset turnover and capex/sales should be computed on owned PPE only

Excel implementation

New leases:       =New_Stores_t*Fixed_Rent*(1-(1+IBR)^-Term)/IBR
ROU close:        =ROU_open+New_Leases-ROU_Dep-Terminations
Intangibles amort:=SUMPRODUCT(IntAdds_Row*(Year>=Vintage_Row)*(Year<Vintage_Row+IntLife))/IntLife

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Lease modifications and remeasurements (rent escalations based on CPI, extensions) adjust both liability and ROU — non-cash.
  • Store closures terminate leases: derecognise ROU and liability; any difference is a P&L gain or loss.
  • Sale-and-leaseback (Ind AS 116.98–103) — only the rights transferred generate gains.
  • Leasehold land on 99-year leases is an ROU asset (often prepaid, no liability) — depreciate over the lease term.
  • Spectrum deferred payments to DoT create a financial liability similar to debt, not a lease.
  • Impairment of ROU assets for loss-making stores under Ind AS 36 — model if store rationalisation is planned.

Key takeaways

  • Run separate corkscrews for PPE, intangibles (Ind AS 38) and ROU assets (Ind AS 116).
  • Finite-life intangibles amortise with zero residual; indefinite-life intangibles and goodwill are impairment-tested, not amortised.
  • ROU additions are non-cash; their cash cost appears as lease payments in CFF/CFO.
  • Drive new leases from store additions × fixed rent × annuity factor at the IBR.
  • Keep variable and short-term rent in opex; only fixed lease payments enter the lease liability.

Practice Questions

Q1. ROU and lease liability for new stores [L2]

A retailer opens 80 stores in FY26, each with fixed rent of ₹90 lakh a year payable annually in arrears, a 10-year lease term and an IBR of 9.5%. Initial direct costs are ₹5 lakh per store and lease incentives received ₹10 lakh per store. Compute the initial lease liability, initial ROU asset and annual ROU depreciation for the FY26 vintage, and state the CFS treatment of the additions.

💡 Hint: The annuity factor for 10 years at 9.5% is needed first; incentives reduce the ROU, not the liability.

Q2. Intangible amortisation waterfall [L1]

An IT services company capitalises software of ₹60, ₹75 and ₹90 crore in FY26–FY28 (life 5 years, SLM, full-year convention). Its existing software NBV at 31 March 2025 is ₹120 crore with RUL 3 years. Compute total amortisation for FY26–FY28.

Q3. Fixed vs variable rent split [L2]

A mall-based retailer pays rent of ₹500 crore in FY25: 60% fixed under multi-year leases, 30% revenue-share, 10% short-term. Revenue grows 15% a year. Explain which components enter the Ind AS 116 schedules, which stay in opex, and forecast FY26 opex rent and the EBITDA impact compared with treating all rent as Ind AS 116 lease payments.

Q4. Case: mixing ROU into PPE [L3]

A model adds new store leases of ₹850 crore a year to PPE "additions" and links PPE additions to "purchase of PPE" in the CFS. Explain the effect on FCF, net debt, EBITDA, fixed-asset turnover and valuation, and outline the corrected schedule structure.
