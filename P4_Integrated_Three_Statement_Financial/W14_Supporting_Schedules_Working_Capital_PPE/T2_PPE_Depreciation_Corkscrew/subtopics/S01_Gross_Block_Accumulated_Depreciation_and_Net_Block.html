Subtopic note

Gross Block, Accumulated Depreciation and Net Block Corkscrews

Scope: Gross block, accumulated depreciation and net block corkscrews with disposals

Why it matters

PPE is the largest asset for most Indian manufacturers, utilities, telecom and infrastructure companies, and a substantial one for retailers with company-owned stores. The PPE schedule feeds three statements at once: depreciation to the P&L, the closing net block to the Balance Sheet, and capex and disposal proceeds to investing cash flow. It also feeds the deferred tax schedule (book NBV vs tax WDV) and return metrics such as ROCE and fixed-asset turnover. Indian annual reports disclose PPE as a gross-block reconciliation table under Schedule III and Ind AS 16.73, so a three-corkscrew model (gross, accumulated depreciation, net) reconciles directly to what auditors and analysts read.

Core notes

The three corkscrews

Gross block (cost):

\[ \text{Gross}_t = \text{Gross}_{t-1} + \text{Additions}_t - \text{Cost of disposals}_t \]

Accumulated depreciation:

\[ \text{AccDep}_t = \text{AccDep}_{t-1} + \text{Depreciation}_t - \text{AccDep on disposals}_t \]

Net block (NBV):

\[ \text{NBV}_t = \text{Gross}_t - \text{AccDep}_t = \text{NBV}_{t-1} + \text{Additions}_t - \text{Depreciation}_t - \text{NBV of disposals}_t \]

Additions here are transfers into PPE — direct purchases plus capitalisation from CWIP (s03) — not cash capex. Impairment under Ind AS 36 is usually shown as a separate column in the accumulated-depreciation-and-impairment corkscrew.

Why model gross and accumulated separately rather than just NBV? Three reasons: (1) depreciation on SLM is a function of gross cost, not NBV, so gross block must be tracked; (2) the annual report's PPE note is in gross/accumulated form, so separate corkscrews reconcile; (3) "fully depreciated but still in use" assets sit in gross block with equal accumulated depreciation, which matters for the full-depreciation cap and for asset-age ratios (accumulated ÷ gross).

Disposals

On disposal the asset leaves both gross block and accumulated depreciation (Ind AS 16.67–72). The P&L gain or loss is:

\[ \text{Gain/(loss)} = \text{Sale proceeds} - \text{NBV of disposals} = \text{Proceeds} - (\text{Cost disposed} - \text{AccDep disposed}) \]

Gains are not classified as revenue (Ind AS 16.68); they sit in other income (or loss in other expenses). In the CFS, proceeds go to CFI, and the gain is deducted (loss added back) in CFO.

Forecasting disposals is usually simple: either zero (most models) or a % of opening gross block with an assumed accumulated-depreciation ratio. For retailers closing loss-making stores, disposals of leasehold improvements can be meaningful and often carry write-offs (NBV disposed with zero proceeds).

Disposal assumptions Rule
Cost disposed % of opening gross block (e.g., 1–2%) or specific asset
AccDep on disposed Cost disposed × (opening AccDep ÷ opening gross), or near 100% for old assets
Proceeds NBV × recovery % (0% for store fit-outs, 50–100% for land/vehicles)

Asset classes

Run the corkscrew by asset class where useful lives differ materially: freehold land (not depreciated), buildings, plant and machinery, furniture and fixtures, leasehold improvements, vehicles, office equipment and computers. A retailer's model might have just three classes (buildings, store fit-outs/leasehold improvements, IT); a cement or steel model needs P&M split into major plants. Freehold land must be separate because it is never depreciated — mixing it into a blended SLM rate overstates depreciation.

Layout and links

Place the PPE schedule on its own tab. Columns are years; rows are the corkscrews by class, then totals, then checks:

  • Opening gross/AccDep in the first forecast year links to the last actual closing.
  • Each year's opening links to the prior year's closing (=prior column closing), never re-derived.
  • Total closing NBV links to the Balance Sheet; depreciation to the P&L; additions and disposal proceeds to CFS (with CWIP and capital creditors adjustments, c06).
  • Check rows: NBV ≥ 0 by class; AccDep ≤ Gross − residual (c05); NBV = Gross − AccDep.
Gross close:   =Gross_open+Additions-Disposal_Cost
AccDep close:  =AccDep_open+Dep-Disposal_AccDep
NBV check:     =ROUND(Gross_close-AccDep_close-NBV_close,2)=0

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Revaluation model (Ind AS 16.31–42) for land/buildings: revaluation surplus goes to OCI and other equity; depreciation is on the revalued amount. Rare in India except some land; if used, add a revaluation column.
  • Deemed cost on Ind AS transition (Ind AS 101 D7AA): many companies carried forward Indian GAAP NBV as deemed cost, resetting gross block and accumulated depreciation; historical gross/accumulated ratios before transition are not comparable.
  • Impairment: book in accumulated depreciation and impairment; subsequent depreciation is on the reduced carrying amount over remaining life.
  • Assets held for sale (Ind AS 105) stop being depreciated and move out of PPE.
  • Capital-goods subsidies and government grants (Ind AS 20) may be deducted from cost or shown as deferred income — check the policy before computing capex intensity.
  • Exchange differences: Indian GAAP para 46/46A FX capitalisation on long-term foreign currency loans could be continued for pre-transition loans under Ind AS 101 D13AA; affects additions.

Key takeaways

  • Model gross block, accumulated depreciation and net block as three linked corkscrews by asset class.
  • Additions are capitalisations into PPE (from CWIP or direct), not cash capex.
  • Disposals remove cost and accumulated depreciation; gain/loss = proceeds − NBV disposed.
  • Keep freehold land separate and undepreciated.
  • Link openings to prior closings; check NBV ≥ 0 and AccDep ≤ depreciable cost every year.
  • Proceeds go to CFI; gain/loss is reversed in CFO.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a three-line corkscrew [L1]

Opening FY26 gross block ₹2,500 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹900 crore. FY26 additions ₹400 crore, depreciation ₹170 crore, disposal of assets costing ₹60 crore with accumulated depreciation ₹48 crore, proceeds ₹20 crore. Build the gross, accumulated and net block corkscrews, compute the gain or loss, and pass the disposal journal entry.

Q2. Disposals as a forecasting assumption [L2]

A retailer plans to close 25 stores a year for FY26–FY28. Each closed store's fit-out cost averages ₹3 crore and is, on average, 60% depreciated; recovery is nil. Opening FY26 leasehold-improvement gross block is ₹1,800 crore with accumulated depreciation ₹700 crore; additions are ₹450 crore a year and depreciation ₹260 crore, ₹290 crore and ₹320 crore. Build the three corkscrews for leasehold improvements and the annual write-off charged to P&L, and state its CFS treatment.

💡 Hint: Disposal cost and disposal accumulated depreciation both leave their corkscrews; the NBV with zero proceeds is a loss.

Q3. Reconciling to the annual report [L2]

The FY25 PPE note shows gross block opening ₹6,200 crore, additions ₹900 crore, disposals ₹150 crore, closing ₹6,950 crore; accumulated depreciation opening ₹2,100 crore, charge ₹480 crore, on disposals ₹120 crore, impairment ₹40 crore, closing ₹2,500 crore. The cash flow statement shows "purchase of PPE" of ₹1,050 crore and "proceeds from sale" of ₹25 crore. Compute the net block, the loss/gain on disposal, and explain three possible reasons why cash capex differs from additions.

Q4. Interview: why not model only NBV? [L1]

An interviewer asks why you keep a gross block and accumulated depreciation corkscrew instead of modelling net block only. Give three reasons tied to depreciation mechanics, reconciliation and asset-age analysis.
