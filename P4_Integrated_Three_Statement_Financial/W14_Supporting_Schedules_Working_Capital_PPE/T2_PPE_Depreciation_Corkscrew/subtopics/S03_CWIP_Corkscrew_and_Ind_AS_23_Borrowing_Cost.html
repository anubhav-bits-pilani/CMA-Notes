Subtopic note

CWIP Corkscrew and Ind AS 23 Borrowing Cost Capitalisation

Scope: CWIP corkscrew: capex spend vs capitalisation timing; Ind AS 23 borrowing cost capitalisation

Why it matters

For capacity-led Indian businesses — cement, steel, chemicals, power, airports, data centres — large projects sit in capital work-in-progress (CWIP) for two to four years before they start depreciating. If the model capitalises capex into gross block the moment it is spent, depreciation starts too early, EBIT and ROCE are understated during construction, and the deferred tax schedule is distorted. Conversely, interest on project loans is capitalised into CWIP under Ind AS 23 rather than expensed, so finance cost in the P&L understates the true cost of debt during construction. Both effects move EPS, interest coverage covenants and valuation multiples. Schedule III also requires a CWIP ageing schedule and a completion schedule for overdue or over-budget projects, which gives you the data to model timing.

Core notes

The CWIP corkscrew

\[ \text{CWIP}_t = \text{CWIP}_{t-1} + \text{Capex spend}_t + \text{Capitalised borrowing cost}_t - \text{Transfers to PPE}_t - \text{Write-offs}_t \]

Transfers to PPE (capitalisation) are the "additions" in the gross block corkscrew (s01). Depreciation starts when the asset is available for use — in the location and condition necessary to operate as intended by management (Ind AS 16.55) — not when it is first used commercially or when cash is spent.

Project-wise modelling is best for large projects:

₹ crore, 3 MTPA plant FY26E FY27E FY28E
Opening CWIP 0 655 1,760
Capex spend (30/50/20% of ₹2,100 crore) 630 1,050 420
Capitalised borrowing cost (gross) 35 75 95
Less: interest income on temporary investment (10) (20) (5)
Transfer to PPE on commissioning (31 March 2028) — — (2,270)
Closing CWIP 655 1,760 0

Depreciation on the ₹2,270 crore starts in FY29 (or in FY28 for the days after commissioning, if commissioned mid-year). Routine capex (store fit-outs, small machinery) can bypass CWIP with a simple assumption — e.g., a fixed % of each year's capex remains in CWIP at year-end.

Timing: capex spend vs capitalisation

  • Spend schedule comes from management guidance, project cost disclosures and the Schedule III CWIP ageing (amounts in CWIP for < 1, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years).
  • Capitalisation happens on commissioning; for multi-phase projects, phase-wise.
  • Delays are common: Schedule III requires disclosure of projects whose completion is overdue or has exceeded the original plan, with expected completion dates. A conservative model adds a delay scenario.
  • Pre-operative and trial-run costs: directly attributable costs (site preparation, installation, testing, professional fees, employee costs of construction staff) are capitalised (Ind AS 16.16–17). Since the 2022 amendment to Ind AS 16.20A (effective FY 2022-23), sale proceeds of items produced during testing are recognised in P&L, not deducted from cost.

Ind AS 23 borrowing cost capitalisation

Ind AS 23.8: borrowing costs directly attributable to the acquisition, construction or production of a qualifying asset (one that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale) are capitalised as part of its cost. Other borrowing costs are expensed.

Key mechanics:

  • Specific borrowings (Ind AS 23.12): capitalise actual borrowing costs incurred during the period, less investment income on temporary investment of those borrowings.
  • General borrowings (Ind AS 23.14): apply a capitalisation rate (weighted average cost of general borrowings) to expenditure on the asset; capitalised amount cannot exceed borrowing costs actually incurred. The 2019 amendment clarifies that specific borrowings remaining outstanding after the asset is ready are added to the general pool.
  • Commencement (Ind AS 23.17): when expenditure is incurred, borrowing costs are incurred, and activities to prepare the asset are under way.
  • Suspension (Ind AS 23.20): during extended periods when active development is suspended.
  • Cessation (Ind AS 23.22): when substantially all activities to prepare the asset are complete.
  • Borrowing costs include interest calculated using the EIR method under Ind AS 109, finance charges on lease liabilities, and exchange differences to the extent regarded as an adjustment to interest cost (Ind AS 23.6).

Formula for a project funded by a specific term loan:

\[ \text{Capitalised BC}_t = r \times \overline{\text{Specific loan}}_t \times \frac{\text{Months under construction}}{12} - \text{Investment income}_t \]

Model plumbing

  1. The debt schedule (t3) computes total interest on all borrowings.
  2. The CWIP schedule computes the capitalised portion.
  3. P&L finance cost = total interest − capitalised borrowing cost.
  4. Cash interest paid = total interest (capitalised interest is still paid in cash). Under Ind AS 7.32, total interest paid is disclosed whether expensed or capitalised; capitalised interest paid is commonly shown within capex (CFI) or interest paid (CFF) — pick one and be consistent.
  5. Depreciation on commissioned assets includes the capitalised interest, so the cost is expensed over the asset's life.

Interest coverage covenants computed by lenders often use total interest (including capitalised), so the covenant calculation in t3 should not use P&L finance cost alone.

Excel implementation

CWIP close:      =CWIP_open+Spend+Cap_BC-Transfers-WriteOffs
Transfers:       =IF(Year=Commission_Year,CWIP_open+Spend+Cap_BC,0)
Cap_BC:          =IF(AND(Year>=Start_Year,Year<=Commission_Year),Rate*AVERAGE(Loan_open,Loan_close)-Inv_Income,0)
P&L finance cost:=Total_Interest-Cap_BC

Note: capitalised interest based on average loan balance creates circularity through debt (t4) — use the breaker or opening balances.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Abandoned projects: CWIP written off through P&L (impairment); exclude from capitalisation. Long-ageing CWIP (> 3 years) is an impairment and governance red flag.
  • Inventories produced over a long period (real estate, aged liquor) can be qualifying assets; many real-estate developers capitalise interest into inventory, not CWIP.
  • Assets measured at fair value (biological assets) and inventories produced in large quantities on a repetitive basis are excluded from mandatory capitalisation (Ind AS 23.4).
  • Exchange differences on foreign currency borrowings — the Ind AS 23.6(e) portion qualifies; legacy Indian GAAP para 46A treatment continues only for grandfathered loans.
  • Tax treatment: interest capitalised for books may be allowed differently under the Income-tax Act (Sec 36(1)(iii) proviso: interest for acquisition of an asset for extension of business is not deductible until the asset is first put to use) — a timing difference for the tax schedule.

Key takeaways

  • CWIP_t = Opening + Spend + Capitalised BC − Transfers to PPE − Write-offs.
  • Depreciation starts when the asset is available for use, not when cash is spent.
  • Capitalise borrowing costs on qualifying assets; net investment income for specific borrowings; use a capitalisation rate for general borrowings.
  • P&L finance cost = total interest − capitalised interest; cash interest includes both.
  • Use the Schedule III CWIP ageing and overdue-project disclosures to set timing and delay scenarios.

Practice Questions

Q1. CWIP corkscrew for a greenfield plant [L2]

A specialty chemicals company starts a ₹1,200 crore plant on 1 April 2025, spending 40% in FY26 and 60% in FY27, commissioning on 31 March 2027. It is funded by a ₹800 crore specific term loan at 9.5%, drawn ₹320 crore on 1 April 2025 and ₹480 crore on 1 April 2026. Surplus loan funds earned interest income of ₹8 crore in FY26 and ₹3 crore in FY27. Build the CWIP corkscrew, the capitalised borrowing cost, and the amount transferred to PPE.

💡 Hint: Capitalise interest on amounts drawn for the months under construction, then deduct investment income.

Q2. General borrowing capitalisation rate [L2]

A company has no specific project borrowings. Its general borrowings in FY26 were: ₹500 crore NCDs at 8.2% and ₹300 crore term loan at 9.4%, both outstanding all year. Weighted average accumulated expenditure on a qualifying asset during FY26 was ₹250 crore. Compute the capitalisation rate and the borrowing cost capitalised. Explain the cap on the capitalised amount.

Q3. P&L, BS and CFS effects [L2]

Total interest on all borrowings for FY27 is ₹180 crore, of which ₹55 crore is capitalised into CWIP. Capex spend on the project is ₹900 crore. Show the effects on P&L finance cost, the CWIP balance and the CFS, under the policy that capitalised interest paid is shown within purchase of PPE. Comment on the implication for the interest coverage ratio used by lenders.

Q4. Delay scenario [L3]

The plant in Q1 is delayed: commissioning moves to 30 September 2027 and cost overruns add ₹150 crore spent in H1 FY28. Explain the changes to CWIP, capitalised interest, depreciation start, P&L finance cost and EBITDA in FY28, and describe how you would build a delay toggle in the model.
