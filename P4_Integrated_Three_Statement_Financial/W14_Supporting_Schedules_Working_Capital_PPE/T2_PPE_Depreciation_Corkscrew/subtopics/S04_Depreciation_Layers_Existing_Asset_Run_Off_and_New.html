Subtopic note

Depreciation Layers: Existing Asset Run-Off and New-Capex Vintages

Scope: Depreciation layers: existing assets run-off over remaining life vs new-capex vintage waterfall

Why it matters

Depreciation forecast as a % of revenue or % of opening gross block is quick but wrong in exactly the situations that matter: when a company is in a heavy capex cycle (depreciation should step up as new plants commission), when old assets are close to fully depreciated (depreciation should fall even though gross block is flat), and when the asset mix shifts toward short-lived IT or store fit-outs. A layered approach — existing assets running off over their remaining life, plus a waterfall for each year's new capex — produces a depreciation line that is mechanically tied to the asset base, stops at full depreciation, and responds correctly to capex timing. It is also the layout auditors of a model expect, and it feeds the deferred-tax schedule cleanly.

Core notes

Layer 1: existing assets

At the last actual balance sheet date you know (by asset class) gross block, accumulated depreciation and net block. You do not know each asset's age, so estimate a remaining useful life (RUL). The run-off for SLM is:

\[ \text{Dep}^{\text{exist}}_t = \min\left( \frac{\text{Opening depreciable base}}{\text{RUL}},\ \text{Remaining NBV}_{t-1} - \text{Residual value} \right) \]

Ways to estimate RUL:

  • From the accounts: RUL ≈ (Net block − Residual value) ÷ last-year depreciation on that class. If net block is ₹1,200 crore and FY25 depreciation ₹150 crore, RUL ≈ 8 years.
  • From asset age: Total life × (1 − AccDep ÷ Gross) for SLM — e.g., P&M life 15 years, 60% depreciated → RUL 6 years.
  • From disclosures: some companies disclose the weighted average remaining life or the policy lives; cross-check against Schedule II lives.

Two simplifications are common and both acceptable if documented: (a) straight-line run-off of NBV over RUL (constant charge until NBV reaches residual), and (b) matching last year's charge and capping it at remaining NBV. Option (a) is cleaner. Because many assets inside the class are already fully depreciated, a straight-line run-off of the net block, not the gross block, is the right approach — applying the policy rate to gross block will over-depreciate.

Layer 2: new capex vintages

Each forecast year's capitalised additions form a vintage. Under SLM with life \( L \), a vintage capitalised in year \( v \) depreciates \( \text{Capex}_v \div L \) per year for years \( v, v+1, \ldots, v+L-1 \) (with optional half-year convention in year \( v \)).

A waterfall (triangle) lays this out — rows are vintages, columns are years:

₹ crore, life 10 yrs FY26 FY27 FY28 FY29 FY30
Vintage FY26 (capex 500) 50 50 50 50 50
Vintage FY27 (capex 600) — 60 60 60 60
Vintage FY28 (capex 700) — — 70 70 70
Vintage FY29 (capex 650) — — — 65 65
Vintage FY30 (capex 700) — — — — 70
New-capex depreciation 50 110 180 245 315

With a half-year convention, each vintage's first-year charge halves (25, 30, 35 …) and the final half-year falls in year \( v + L \). The half-year convention approximates capex spread through the year; Ind AS requires depreciation from the date available for use (Ind AS 16.55), so for a large project commissioned on a known date, pro-rate exactly instead.

The full waterfall can be collapsed into a single formula row using SUMPRODUCT (c04), avoiding a large triangle when the forecast horizon or the number of asset classes is large.

Total depreciation

\[ \text{Total dep}_t = \text{Dep}^{\text{exist}}_t + \sum_{v \le t} \text{Dep}^{\text{new}}_{v,t} \]

computed per asset class (different lives) and summed. Run the waterfall for each class with its own life, or apply a weighted average life if classes are immaterial.

Note the cliff in FY32 when the existing layer is fully depreciated — real, not a bug, but the model should show it and analysts should expect depreciation to step down.

Why layering matters for analysis

  • Margin optics: a commissioning wave raises depreciation before revenue ramps — EBIT margins dip in the first two years of a new plant.
  • Deferred tax: book depreciation by layer vs tax WDV by block (t5) drives the DTL.
  • ROCE: new capacity's capital employed hits ROCE before its earnings.
  • Valuation: in the terminal year, depreciation must be consistent with steady-state capex; a terminal year just after the existing-asset cliff understates D&A relative to capex.

Excel implementation

Existing:   =MIN(Exist_NBV_0/RUL, MAX(0, Exist_NBV_open - Residual))
Vintage:    =IF(AND(Year>=Vintage, Year<Vintage+Life), Capex_v/Life, 0)
Half-year:  =IF(Year=Vintage,0.5,IF(AND(Year>Vintage,Year<Vintage+Life),1,IF(Year=Vintage+Life,0.5,0)))*Capex_v/Life

Keep a separate accumulated-depreciation tracker for the existing layer so the MIN cap works.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disposals of existing assets reduce the existing layer's base; proportionally reduce its future depreciation.
  • Mixed classes inside "plant and machinery" with very different lives — use a weighted RUL or split if disclosed.
  • Impairment of existing assets resets the existing layer's NBV and RUL.
  • Change in estimate of useful life (Ind AS 8 prospective): recompute remaining-life depreciation from the change date.
  • Assets acquired in business combinations at fair value form their own vintage with remaining life at acquisition.
  • Leasehold improvements depreciate over the shorter of useful life and lease term — store closure assumptions shorten effective life.

Key takeaways

  • Separate depreciation into an existing-asset layer and new-capex vintages.
  • Run off existing net block over an estimated remaining life, capped at remaining NBV.
  • Depreciate each vintage SLM over its life, starting when available for use; half-year convention is optional.
  • Total depreciation = existing run-off + sum of active vintages, by asset class.
  • Expect and show the cliff when the existing layer is fully depreciated.

Practice Questions

Q1. Estimate remaining life and run-off [L1]

At 31 March 2025 a company's buildings have gross block ₹900 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹300 crore and FY25 depreciation ₹30 crore; residual value is 5% of gross. Estimate RUL and project existing-layer depreciation until fully depreciated.

Q2. Build a vintage waterfall with half-year convention [L2]

Capex (capitalised) for FY26–FY30 is ₹300, ₹450, ₹500, ₹400 and ₹350 crore; life 8 years; half-year convention in the first and final years. Build the waterfall and total new-capex depreciation for FY26–FY30, and compare with the full-year convention.

💡 Hint: With half-year convention, each vintage depreciates over L + 1 calendar years, with half charges at each end.

Q3. Commissioning wave and margin [L2]

A cement company commissions a ₹2,400 crore plant (life 25 years, residual 5%) on 1 October 2027. Existing depreciation is ₹600 crore a year with RUL 12 years. The new plant reaches 70% utilisation in FY29 generating incremental EBITDA of ₹420 crore. Compute FY28 and FY29 total depreciation and explain the EBIT trajectory for FY28–FY29.

Q4. Review: depreciation on gross block at policy rate [L3]

A model applies 6.67% (1/15) to the full P&M gross block of ₹5,000 crore every year, although 40% of the gross block relates to assets that are already fully depreciated. Explain the error, quantify the overstatement in year 1, and redesign the depreciation block using layers.
