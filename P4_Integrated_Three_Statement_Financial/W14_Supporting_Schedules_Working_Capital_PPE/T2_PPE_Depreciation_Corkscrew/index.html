Roadmap topic: PPE & Depreciation Corkscrew Schedule: Opening Gross/Net Block + Maintenance & Growth Capex – Depreciation (existing vs new asset layers) = Closing Net Block

Overview

The PPE schedule converts the physical expansion plan in the Week 13 operating model — stores, tonnes, towers, seats — into capex, depreciation and the closing net block. It is the second large supporting schedule after working capital and is where the model's growth story must be paid for. Each forecast year starts with the prior year's closing gross block and accumulated depreciation, adds capitalised capex (maintenance plus growth, routed through CWIP for long projects), deducts depreciation computed in layers, and removes disposals. The outputs land in three places: depreciation in the P&L, net block and CWIP on the Balance Sheet, and cash capex in investing cash flow.

For FP&A, credit and valuation roles the schedule matters because depreciation drives EBIT and therefore tax, interest coverage and ROCE; capex drives free cash flow and funding needs; and the book net block feeds the deferred-tax schedule against Income-tax WDV. In Indian practice the inputs are anchored to Schedule II useful lives and the 5% residual cap, Ind AS 16 componentisation, Ind AS 23 borrowing cost capitalisation on qualifying assets, and the Schedule III PPE and CWIP ageing disclosures. Intangibles (Ind AS 38) and right-of-use assets (Ind AS 116) run in parallel schedules because their drivers and cash-flow treatment differ.

A sound learning sequence is: the three corkscrews and disposals (s01); the capex build split into maintenance and growth (s02); CWIP timing and capitalised interest (s03); depreciation layers — existing run-off and new-capex vintages (s04); the accounting inputs for life, residual value, method and components (s05); separate intangible and ROU schedules (s06); and finally the sanity-check ratios that test the economic plausibility of the whole (s07). The key concepts give the corkscrew identity (c01), the existing-layer formula (c02), vintage SLM and its SUMPRODUCT implementation (c03, c04), the depreciation ceiling (c05) and the cash capex reconciliation (c06).

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 16, 36 and 116 foundations

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 16, Ind AS 36 impairment, Ind AS 116 ROU assets

This topic applies the Phase 1 standards to forecasting. Ind AS 16 supplies recognition (directly attributable costs, major inspections, spare parts), depreciation from the date available for use, annual review of life, residual and method, and derecognition. Ind AS 36 determines when carrying amounts must be written down — for example a loss-making plant or store cluster — which resets the existing depreciation layer. Ind AS 116 creates ROU assets and lease liabilities that must be modelled separately from owned PPE. Revisit the impairment indicators and the lease-term judgement (reasonably certain extension options) before setting long-run assumptions for asset-heavy or store-heavy businesses.

Book vs tax depreciation and DTL

→ Week 14 (w14_t5): book vs Income-tax WDV depreciation drives DTL

Book depreciation here is SLM by asset vintage over Schedule II-based lives; tax depreciation under the Income-tax Act is block-wise WDV at prescribed rates (15% plant and machinery, 10% buildings and furniture, 40% computers), with half rate for assets used less than 180 days in the year of acquisition. Tax WDV usually falls faster than book NBV in early years, so book NBV exceeds tax WDV and a deferred tax liability builds at the enacted rate (25.17% under Sec 115BAA). The PPE schedule therefore must expose closing book NBV by year (excluding freehold land if tax treats it differently) for the t5 computation, and the capex additions feed the tax-block corkscrew.

Capital budgeting and capex appraisal

→ Phase 6 (W21–24): capital budgeting and capex appraisal

Growth capex in this schedule is an input; in Phase 6 it becomes a decision. A new plant or store cohort should be appraised on NPV and IRR against the cost of capital, with incremental revenue, EBITDA, working capital and tax depreciation, and with sensitivity to delays and cost overruns — the same CWIP timing and Ind AS 23 mechanics covered in s03. When modelling an expansion plan, cross-check that the implied project returns (incremental EBIT after tax over incremental capital) are plausible; a capex plan that implies a 40% post-tax IRR in a commoditised industry signals an aggressive revenue build or understated capex.

Asset turnover and ROCE implications

→ Phase 2 (W5–8): asset turnover and ROCE implications

ROCE decomposes into EBIT margin times capital turnover, and fixed-asset turnover is the dominant part of capital turnover for manufacturers. The PPE schedule therefore drives the forecast ROCE path: a commissioning wave depresses ROCE (capital employed rises before revenue ramps and depreciation hits EBIT), while an ageing asset base flatters net-block turnover. Phase 2 tools — DuPont-style decomposition, peer turnover benchmarking and asset-age analysis — become forecast diagnostics in s07. Present ROCE on both net and gross block for capital-intensive businesses so reviewers can separate genuine productivity from depreciation effects.

Common pitfalls

Depreciation as a flat percentage of revenue

Depreciation as a flat % of revenue, disconnected from the asset base

The mistake: forecasting D&A as, say, 3.5% of revenue because that was the historical ratio. It is popular because it is one line and appears stable historically. But depreciation is a function of the asset base and its age, not of sales: a price-led revenue increase raises "depreciation" with no capex, while a capex wave commissioning ahead of revenue is ignored. The correct treatment is the layered build — existing net block run-off over remaining life plus vintage SLM on new capex. Detect it by checking whether depreciation changes when only capex is changed, by computing capex/D&A and depreciation/opening gross block in the forecast, and by testing whether NBV ever goes negative.

Depreciating assets beyond cost

Assets depreciated beyond cost, producing negative net block

The mistake: applying a policy rate to gross block every year without a stopping rule, or running the existing layer on gross cost, so cumulative depreciation exceeds cost less residual value and net block turns negative in later forecast or terminal years. It happens because the error appears only after several years, often beyond the review window, and because cash plugs hide the balance-sheet effect. The correct treatment: cap each layer's charge at remaining NBV less residual (c05) and use SLM windows for vintages. Detect it with check rows (NBV ≥ 0 by class; AccDep ≤ Gross − Land − RV), and by scanning the final years of the forecast, where the problem surfaces.

Revenue growth without matching capex

New stores/capacity in the revenue build without matching capex

The mistake: the revenue tab adds 150 stores a year or assumes volume growth beyond installed capacity, while the capex tab holds capex at a historical run-rate or % of sales. It happens because revenue and capex are built on different tabs, sometimes by different people, and because low capex flatters FCF and valuation. The correct treatment: drive growth capex from the same unit drivers (new stores × sq ft × capex per sq ft; Δcapacity × capex per tonne), route project spend through CWIP, and add a utilisation check that flags volume above ~90% of capacity. Detect it by linking — not re-typing — store additions into the capex schedule and reviewing capex/sales and utilisation rows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full PPE schedule for a retailer [L3]

Vastra Retail Ltd at 31 March 2025: gross block ₹3,200 crore (freehold land ₹200 crore; buildings ₹600 crore; fit-outs and P&M ₹2,000 crore; IT ₹400 crore); accumulated depreciation ₹1,250 crore (buildings ₹120 crore, fit-outs ₹900 crore, IT ₹230 crore); FY25 depreciation: buildings ₹20 crore, fit-outs ₹220 crore, IT ₹80 crore; residual nil. Store additions FY26–FY28: 120, 140, 150 at 10,000 sq ft and ₹3,000 per sq ft (+3% a year); maintenance capex 3% of opening gross block (excluding land), allocated 50% fit-outs, 50% IT; new fit-outs life 8 years, IT 4 years, half-year convention. No disposals; no CWIP. (a) Build gross, accumulated and net block by class for FY26–FY28. (b) Compute existing and new depreciation layers. (c) Compute capex/sales and capex/D&A given revenue of ₹14,400, ₹17,000 and ₹19,700 crore. (d) Comment on whether maintenance capex is adequate.

💡 Hint: Estimate each class's remaining life from FY25 net block and depreciation before building the vintages.

Q2. Cement greenfield with CWIP and Ind AS 23 [L3]

A cement company plans a 3 MTPA plant costing ₹2,100 crore, spent 30/50/20% over FY26–FY28, commissioned on 1 October 2027. A specific term loan of ₹1,400 crore at 9% is drawn in proportion to spend at the start of each year; temporary investment income is ₹10 crore in FY26 and nil thereafter. Existing net block is ₹6,000 crore with RUL 12 years (residual nil); maintenance capex ₹450 per tonne on 15 MTPA. Plant life 25 years, residual 5%. (a) Build the CWIP corkscrew with capitalised borrowing cost. (b) Compute FY28 and FY29 depreciation including pro-rata on commissioning. (c) Show P&L finance cost and cash capex for each year. (d) Explain the impact on ROCE in FY28–FY29.

Q3. Review case: depreciation and capex disconnect [L2]

A colleague's model for an auto-ancillary forecasts D&A at 4% of revenue (FY25 ratio), capex at ₹300 crore a year, revenue growth of 15% a year for five years, and shows FY30 net block of –₹120 crore for one class. FY25 data: revenue ₹6,000 crore, gross block ₹4,500 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹2,700 crore, utilisation 84%. Write a review note identifying each issue, quantifying where possible, and specifying the redesigned schedule and checks.

Q4. ROU vs owned stores and sanity checks [L2]

Two retailers each add 100 stores a year. Retailer A owns stores (capex ₹25 crore per store including land and building, life 30 years excluding land at 40% of cost). Retailer B leases (fit-out capex ₹3 crore per store, life 8 years; fixed rent ₹1.2 crore per store a year, 9-year lease at 9% IBR). For year 1 of the programme, compute capex, D&A (including ROU depreciation), new ROU additions and lease liability, and fixed-asset turnover if each new cohort generates revenue of ₹1,500 crore. Explain how you would make their capex intensity and FAT comparable.
