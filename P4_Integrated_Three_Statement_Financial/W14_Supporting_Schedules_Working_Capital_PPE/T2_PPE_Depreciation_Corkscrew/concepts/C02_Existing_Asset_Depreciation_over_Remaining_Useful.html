Key concept

Existing-Asset Depreciation over Remaining Useful Life

Concept: Existing assets: Depreciation = Opening depreciable base ÷ Remaining useful life, capped at remaining NBV

Statement

For the asset base existing at the last actual balance sheet date (time 0), by asset class:

\[ \text{Dep}^{\text{exist}}_t = \min\left( \frac{B_0}{\text{RUL}_0},\ \max\left(0,\ \text{NBV}^{\text{exist}}_{t-1} - \text{RV}\right) \right) \]

where \( B_0 = \text{NBV}^{\text{exist}}_0 - \text{RV} \) is the opening depreciable base (net block less residual value); \( \text{RUL}_0 \) is the estimated remaining useful life at time 0; \( \text{NBV}^{\text{exist}}_{t-1} \) is the existing layer's net block at the start of year \( t \); and \( \text{RV} \) is the residual value attributable to the existing assets (often 5% of their gross cost, or nil). The cap ensures cumulative depreciation never exceeds the depreciable base.

Explanation

Historical assets have mixed ages and the model does not know each asset's purchase date. Treating the existing block as one pool depreciating straight-line over its average remaining life is the standard simplification. Using the net depreciable base (not gross cost) is essential: some assets inside gross block are already fully depreciated and must not be charged again.

Estimating RUL:

  • \( \text{RUL} \approx (\text{NBV}_0 - \text{RV}) \div \text{Dep}_{\text{last actual}} \) — the implied run-off at the current charge (most common).
  • \( \text{RUL} \approx L \times (1 - \text{AccDep} \div \text{Gross}) \) where \( L \) is the policy life — uses the age ratio.
  • Weighted RUL across sub-classes if the notes disclose them.

Depreciation on the existing layer is constant until the final year, when the cap trims it to the remaining NBV, after which it is zero. In practice the cliff is gradual because the pool contains assets of many ages; if the model shows a sharp cliff that the business would not experience, a two-tranche run-off (e.g., half over RUL × 0.6 and half over RUL × 1.4) smooths it. Document the choice.

Disposals of existing assets reduce \( \text{NBV}^{\text{exist}} \) and should proportionally reduce the annual charge. Impairments reset the base, and a change in useful life estimate (Ind AS 8, prospective; Ind AS 16.51) recalculates RUL from the date of change.

Illustration

Buildings and P&M at 31 March 2025: NBV ₹1,320 crore; residual value ₹70 crore; last actual depreciation ₹250 crore.

\[ B_0 = 1{,}320 - 70 = 1{,}250 \qquad \text{RUL} = 1{,}250 \div 250 = 5 \text{ years} \]

₹ crore FY26 FY27 FY28 FY29 FY30 FY31
Opening NBV (existing) 1,320 1,070 820 570 320 70
Depreciation (min of 250 and NBV − 70) 250 250 250 250 250 0
Closing NBV 1,070 820 570 320 70 70

If RUL had been computed on gross block (say ₹3,000 crore ÷ 15-year policy life = ₹200 crore a year), the charge would continue long after net block hit residual unless capped — exactly the negative-NBV pitfall.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute RUL and the run-off schedule [L1]

At 31 March 2025, plant & machinery has gross block ₹2,000 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹1,240 crore, FY25 depreciation ₹120 crore and residual value 5% of gross. Compute RUL, the annual existing-layer charge and the year in which it ends.

Q2. Existing layer with disposals [L2]

Using Q1, in FY27 the company sells existing machines with NBV ₹60 crore. Recompute existing-layer depreciation from FY27, keeping RUL unchanged for the remaining pool, and explain your proportional adjustment.

💡 Hint: Reduce both the remaining base and the charge in proportion to the NBV removed.

Q3. Two methods compared [L2]

A company's existing furniture has gross ₹400 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹280 crore, residual nil, policy life 10 years, last-year depreciation ₹30 crore. Compute RUL by the run-off method and by the age-ratio method, explain why they differ, and state which you would use.
