Key concept

Vintage Straight-Line Depreciation for New Capex

Concept: Vintage SLM: Dep_v,t = Capex_v ÷ Life for t ≥ v and t < v + Life (half-year convention optional)

Statement

For a vintage of capitalised additions \( \text{Capex}_v \) placed in service in year \( v \), with useful life \( L \) and residual value \( \text{RV}_v \):

\[ \text{Dep}_{v,t} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\text{Capex}_v - \text{RV}_v}{L} & \text{if } v \le t < v + L \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

With the half-year convention, the first-year and the year \( t = v + L \) charges are halved:

\[ \text{Dep}^{\text{HY}}_{v,t} = \frac{\text{Capex}_v - \text{RV}_v}{L} \times \begin{cases} 0.5 & t = v \text{ or } t = v + L \\ 1 & v < t < v + L \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Total new-capex depreciation in year \( t \) is \( \sum_v \text{Dep}_{v,t} \). (The concept text omits residual value; with RV = 0 the formula reduces to \( \text{Capex}_v \div L \).)

Explanation

Each year's additions form a cohort that depreciates on its own straight line and stops after exactly \( L \) years — so total depreciation is the sum of active cohorts, and old cohorts drop off automatically. This guarantees cumulative depreciation per vintage equals its depreciable amount, which is what prevents assets being depreciated beyond cost.

Why the half-year convention? In an annual model, capex is assumed to happen on 31 March (full-year convention: charge starts the next year) or 1 April (full charge in year \( v \)). Neither is realistic for routine capex spread through the year. Charging half a year in year \( v \) approximates mid-year capitalisation; the remaining half falls in year \( v + L \) so the vintage still totals its depreciable amount. Under Ind AS 16.55 depreciation starts when the asset is available for use, so for a single large project with a known commissioning date, use an exact pro-rata factor (e.g., commissioned 1 October = 0.5; 1 January = 0.25) instead of a convention.

Timing conventions must match: if the debt schedule assumes capex is funded mid-year, or the CWIP schedule capitalises on 31 March, the depreciation start must be consistent.

Different lives per class: run separate vintages by class (fit-outs 8 years, P&M 15, buildings 30, IT 3–6) or use a weighted life if immaterial.

Illustration

Capex FY26 ₹500 crore, FY27 ₹600 crore; life 5 years; RV nil.

Each vintage totals its cost under both conventions; the half-year version spreads over \( L + 1 \) fiscal years.

Full-year:  =IF(AND(Year>=Vintage,Year<Vintage+Life),Capex_v/Life,0)
Half-year:  =Capex_v/Life*IF(OR(Year=Vintage,Year=Vintage+Life),0.5,IF(AND(Year>Vintage,Year<Vintage+Life),1,0))

Practice Questions

Q1. Vintage table [L1]

Capitalised additions are ₹200, ₹250 and ₹300 crore for FY26–FY28; life 4 years; residual nil; full-year convention. Build the vintage table for FY26–FY31 and total depreciation by year.

Q2. Half-year vs exact pro-rata [L2]

A ₹1,600 crore plant (life 20 years, residual 5%) is commissioned on 1 January 2027 (FY27). Compute FY27 and FY28 depreciation under (a) the half-year convention and (b) the exact pro-rata start, and explain which the auditors would expect.

💡 Hint: The fiscal year runs April to March, so 1 January leaves three months in FY27.

Q3. Convention mismatch [L2]

A model capitalises capex on 31 March in the CWIP schedule but depreciates vintages with a full charge in the year of capitalisation. Explain the bias in depreciation, EBIT and the DTL, and how you would align conventions.
