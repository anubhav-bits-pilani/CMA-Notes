Key concept

SUMPRODUCT Depreciation Waterfall Formula

Concept: Waterfall: =SUMPRODUCT(Capex_Row*(Year>=Vintage_Row)*(Year<Vintage_Row+Life))/Life

Statement

=SUMPRODUCT(Capex_Row*(Year>=Vintage_Row)*(Year<Vintage_Row+Life))/Life

Mathematically, for year \( t \):

\[ \text{Dep}^{\text{new}}_t = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{v} \text{Capex}_v \times \mathbf{1}[t \ge v] \times \mathbf{1}[t < v + L] \]

where Capex_Row is the horizontal range of capitalised additions by year; Vintage_Row is the matching range of year labels (e.g., 2026, 2027 … or period numbers 1, 2 …); Year is the current column's year label (mixed reference, row-locked); Life is the useful life in years; and each comparison returns TRUE/FALSE, which multiplies as 1/0. The result equals the sum of all active vintages' straight-line charges.

Explanation

The SUMPRODUCT collapses the vintage triangle (c03) into one formula per year. For each vintage it tests whether the current year falls in the active window \( [v, v + L) \); active vintages contribute \( \text{Capex}_v \), inactive ones zero; dividing by life gives SLM depreciation. Benefits: one row instead of a triangle, works for any horizon, and the formula is identical across columns (a model-integrity rule from Week 13).

Implementation details that trip people up:

  • Anchoring: Capex_Row and Vintage_Row must be absolute ($D$20:$M$20); Year must be column-relative (D$5). Copying across then works.
  • Year labels must be numbers, not text like "FY26E"; use a numeric helper row (2026, 2027 …) or period counter.
  • Ranges must be the same size; mismatched ranges return #VALUE!.
  • Only forecast capex should be in Capex_Row — historical additions are in the existing layer (c02); including them double counts.
  • Half-year convention needs a weighted version:
=SUMPRODUCT(Capex_Row*((Year>Vintage_Row)*(Year<Vintage_Row+Life)+0.5*(Year=Vintage_Row)+0.5*(Year=Vintage_Row+Life)))/Life
  • Residual value: multiply Capex_Row by (1-RV_Pct).
  • Varying life by class: one SUMPRODUCT row per class with its own Life.
  • Performance: SUMPRODUCT over short ranges is fast; avoid full-column references.

Verification: build the full triangle once on a check tab and confirm row totals match the SUMPRODUCT result; or check that cumulative depreciation per vintage at the end of the horizon does not exceed its cost (c05).

Illustration

Row 5 (Year): 2026 … 2030 in D5:H5. Row 20 (Capex): 500, 600, 700, 650, 700 in D20:H20. Life = 10 in cell C22.

In H21 (FY30):

=SUMPRODUCT($D$20:$H$20*(H$5>=$D$5:$H$5)*(H$5<$D$5:$H$5+$C$22))/$C$22

All five vintages are active in 2030: \( (500 + 600 + 700 + 650 + 700) \div 10 = 315 \). In 2036 the FY26 vintage (2026 + 10 = 2036) drops out: the test 2036 < 2036 is FALSE, so the FY26 capex no longer contributes.

For a 5-year life in FY31 (2031): vintages 2026 (2031 < 2031 FALSE) drop out, leaving \( (600 + 700 + 650 + 700) \div 5 = 530 \) assuming no FY31 capex.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write and evaluate the formula [L1]

Years 2026–2032 are in E4:K4; capitalised capex of 120, 150, 180, 160, 140, 130, 150 is in E30:K30; life is 4 years in D32. Write the SUMPRODUCT for cell J31 (2031) and compute its value.

Q2. Debug a broken waterfall [L2]

A colleague's waterfall returns #VALUE! in some cells and, in others, depreciation that never declines after the first vintage expires. The year row contains "FY26E", "FY27E" …, Capex_Row is referenced as D20:H20 without $, and historical capex for FY21–FY25 is included in the range. List each fault and the fix.

💡 Hint: Check data types, anchoring, and which capex belongs in which layer.

Q3. Extend for half-year and residual value [L2]

Modify the formula for a 5% residual value and the half-year convention, then compute FY27 depreciation for capex of ₹400 crore (FY26) and ₹500 crore (FY27) with a 10-year life.
