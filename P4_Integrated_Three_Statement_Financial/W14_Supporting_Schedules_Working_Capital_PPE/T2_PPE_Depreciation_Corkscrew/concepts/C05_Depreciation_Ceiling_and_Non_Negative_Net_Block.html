Key concept

Depreciation Ceiling and Non-Negative Net Block

Concept: Cumulative depreciation ≤ Cost − Residual value; net block never negative

Statement

For every asset, vintage or pool \( i \) and every year \( t \):

\[ \sum_{s \le t} \text{Dep}_{i,s} \le \text{Cost}_i - \text{RV}_i \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \text{NBV}_{i,t} \ge \text{RV}_i \ge 0 \]

where \( \text{Cost}_i \) is gross cost, \( \text{RV}_i \) is residual value (≤ 5% of cost under Schedule II unless justified), and \( \text{NBV}_{i,t} \) is net block at the end of year \( t \). In model form, each year's charge is capped:

\[ \text{Dep}_{i,t} = \min\left( \text{Dep}^{\text{formula}}_{i,t},\ \max(0,\ \text{NBV}_{i,t-1} - \text{RV}_i) \right) \]

Explanation

Ind AS 16.50 allocates the depreciable amount — cost less residual value — over useful life. Once that amount is fully allocated, depreciation stops even if the asset remains in use (it stays in gross block, fully depreciated). If residual value increases to an amount ≥ carrying amount, the charge is zero until it falls below (Ind AS 16.54). A model that charges depreciation as a percentage of gross block or revenue has no such stopping rule and eventually drives net block below zero — a balance-sheet impossibility that also inflates D&A add-backs and distorts deferred tax.

Where the ceiling must be enforced:

  • Existing layer (c02): MIN against remaining NBV.
  • Vintages (c03, c04): the window \( t < v + L \) enforces it automatically for SLM; WDV vintages require explicit tracking.
  • Disposals: accumulated depreciation removed must not exceed the cost removed.
  • Impairment: subsequent charges are on the reduced carrying amount.
  • Total block check: \( \text{AccDep}_t \le \text{Gross}_t - \text{Land}_t - \text{RV} \) and \( \text{NBV}_t \ge 0 \) by class.

A model-wide error check should flag any violation; a negative NBV should never be "fixed" by hard-coding depreciation.

Illustration

A model forecasts depreciation as 8% of gross block (₹1,000 crore, no capex), with opening accumulated depreciation of ₹700 crore and residual nil.

₹ crore FY26 FY27 FY28 FY29 FY30
Uncapped dep (8% × 1,000) 80 80 80 80 80
Uncapped NBV 220 140 60 (20) (100)
Capped dep 80 80 80 60 0
Capped NBV 220 140 60 0 0

The uncapped version overstates FY29–FY30 depreciation by ₹100 crore in total and shows negative NBV — while the CFS adds back depreciation that has no economic basis.

Cap:    =MIN(Dep_Formula, MAX(0, NBV_open-RV))
Check:  =IF(OR(MIN(NBV_Row)<0, MAX(AccDep_Row-(Gross_Row-Land_Row))>0.01),1,0)

Roll the check flag into the model's master error check on the cover sheet.

Connections

Enforces the ceiling for c02 (existing layer), c03–c04 (vintages) and s01 (corkscrews). Directly addresses the topic pitfalls on depreciation as a flat % of revenue and assets depreciated beyond cost. The same MIN/MAX capping logic (refresh item) appears in the revolver and cash sweep (t3), and in the existing-asset depreciation layer of the week exercise.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the cap [L1]

A pool has cost ₹500 crore, residual value ₹25 crore, opening accumulated depreciation ₹410 crore, and a formula charge of ₹35 crore a year. Compute capped depreciation for the next three years and the closing NBV each year.

Q2. Detect violations [L2]

Design a check block for a PPE tab with five asset classes (including freehold land) that flags (a) negative NBV, (b) accumulated depreciation exceeding depreciable cost, and (c) depreciation on land. Write the Excel formulas and describe how the flags roll up to a master check.

💡 Hint: Land should have zero accumulated depreciation; compare by class, not only in total.

Q3. Residual value increase [L2]

At the start of FY27 a fleet of aircraft engines has carrying amount ₹180 crore. Management revises residual value to ₹190 crore due to strong secondary-market prices. Explain the depreciation consequence under Ind AS 16 and how the model's cap formula handles it.
