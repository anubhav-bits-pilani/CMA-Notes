Key concept

Cash Capex in the Cash Flow Statement

Concept: CFS capex = −(Additions to PPE + ΔCWIP) adjusted for Δcapital creditors if modelled

Statement

\[ \text{CFS capex}_t = -\left( \text{Additions to PPE}_t + \Delta\text{CWIP}_t + \Delta\text{Capital advances}_t - \Delta\text{Capital creditors}_t \right) \]

where \( \text{Additions to PPE}_t \) is capitalisations into gross block (including transfers from CWIP); \( \Delta\text{CWIP}_t = \text{CWIP}_t - \text{CWIP}_{t-1} \); \( \Delta\text{Capital advances}_t \) is the change in advances paid to equipment suppliers (non-current assets); and \( \Delta\text{Capital creditors}_t \) is the change in amounts owed to suppliers of capital goods. The result is the "Purchase of property, plant and equipment (including CWIP and capital advances)" line in cash flows from investing activities (Ind AS 7.16(a)), shown as a negative number.

Explanation

Additions to PPE include amounts transferred from CWIP — spend that may have been paid in earlier years. Adding ΔCWIP converts capitalisations into total spend during the year:

\[ \text{Spend}_t = \text{Additions}_t + \text{CWIP}_t - \text{CWIP}_{t-1} \]

because \( \text{CWIP}_t = \text{CWIP}_{t-1} + \text{Spend}_t - \text{Transfers}_t \) and transfers are part of additions. If additions include both direct purchases and CWIP transfers, the identity holds.

Spend is an accrual concept; cash differs by:

  • Capital creditors: an increase means capex was booked but not yet paid — less cash out.
  • Capital advances: an increase means cash paid ahead of booking — more cash out.
  • Capitalised borrowing costs (Ind AS 23, s03): included in CWIP additions but paid as interest. Choose a policy — either deduct from capex and show within interest paid, or leave in capex — and avoid double counting with the debt schedule's cash interest.
  • Non-cash additions: assets acquired via leases (ROU, s06), business combinations, government grants deducted from cost, asset swaps, and capitalised decommissioning provisions are excluded from cash capex (Ind AS 7.43).

In most forecasting models, capital creditors and advances are not modelled (held flat), so cash capex = additions + ΔCWIP = spend. When they are modelled, keep them out of operating NWC (t1 s07) and in this capex reconciliation.

Illustration

FY26E, ₹ crore: direct additions 180; transfers from CWIP 470 (total additions 650); opening CWIP 820, closing 1,100; capital creditors rise from 90 to 140; capital advances fall from 60 to 45; capitalised interest 40 shown within interest paid.

Component ₹ crore
Additions to PPE 650
+ ΔCWIP (1,100 − 820) 280
= Capex spend (accrual) 930
− Increase in capital creditors (50)
− Decrease in capital advances (60 → 45; paid in earlier years) (15)
− Capitalised interest (reported in interest paid) (40)
Cash capex (CFI) (825)

Check: spend = CWIP opening + spend − transfers → \( 820 + \text{CWIP spend} - 470 = 1{,}100 \), so CWIP spend = 750 (of which 40 is capitalised interest), plus direct additions 180 = total spend 930 ✓.

Connections

Closes the loop between t2 s01 (additions), s03 (CWIP and Ind AS 23) and s06 (non-cash ROU additions excluded). It is the PPE version of the cross-cutting rule — schedule → P&L charge (depreciation), BS balance (NBV, CWIP), CFS movement (capex). Feeds CFI in Week 15 and capex in FCFF (Week 16, w16_t1), and the cash waterfall in t3 (CFO → capex → mandatory debt …). Capital creditors' exclusion from NWC links to t1 s07.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute cash capex [L1]

Additions to PPE ₹540 crore; CWIP opening ₹300 crore, closing ₹420 crore; capital creditors opening ₹70 crore, closing ₹50 crore; capital advances opening ₹25 crore, closing ₹40 crore. Compute capex spend and cash capex for the CFS.

Q2. Non-cash additions [L2]

A retailer's FY26 additions comprise ₹480 crore of store fit-outs (cash), ₹900 crore of new ROU assets, and ₹60 crore of PPE acquired in a business combination. A provision for restoration of ₹15 crore was capitalised into ROU. Compute cash capex and list the disclosures required for non-cash investing activities.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 7.43 excludes non-cash investing transactions from the CFS but requires disclosure.

Q3. Reconcile reported cash flow [L2]

A company's FY25 annual report shows additions of ₹1,240 crore, CWIP up ₹160 crore, and CFS "purchase of PPE" of ₹1,310 crore. Capitalised borrowing cost was ₹35 crore. Propose two combinations of capital-creditor and capital-advance movements that would explain the difference, and describe what you would look for in the notes.
