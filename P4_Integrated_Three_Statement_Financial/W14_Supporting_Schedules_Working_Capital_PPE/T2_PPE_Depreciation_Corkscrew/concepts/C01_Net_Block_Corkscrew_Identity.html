Key concept

Net Block Corkscrew Identity

Concept: Closing NBV = Opening NBV + Capex − Depreciation − NBV of disposals

Statement

\[ \text{NBV}_t = \text{NBV}_{t-1} + \text{Capex}_t - \text{Dep}_t - \text{NBV}^{\text{disp}}_t \]

where \( \text{NBV}_t \) is closing net block (gross block less accumulated depreciation and impairment) at the end of year \( t \); \( \text{Capex}_t \) is additions capitalised into PPE in year \( t \) (direct purchases plus transfers from CWIP — not cash capex); \( \text{Dep}_t \) is the depreciation charge; and \( \text{NBV}^{\text{disp}}_t = \text{Cost}^{\text{disp}}_t - \text{AccDep}^{\text{disp}}_t \) is the carrying amount of assets disposed of. Impairment losses (Ind AS 36), if any, are a further deduction.

Explanation

The identity is the net form of two underlying corkscrews — gross block and accumulated depreciation (t2 s01) — and is the PPE instance of the generic corkscrew: Opening + Additions − Reductions = Closing. Its power in modelling is that every term links to exactly one other place:

Term Source Destination
\( \text{NBV}_{t-1} \) Prior-year closing (first forecast year: last actual, by link) —
\( \text{Capex}_t \) Capex schedule / CWIP transfers CFS (via cash capex, c06)
\( \text{Dep}_t \) Depreciation layers (s04) P&L; CFS non-cash add-back
\( \text{NBV}^{\text{disp}}_t \) Disposal assumptions P&L gain/loss vs proceeds; CFS
\( \text{NBV}_t \) — Balance Sheet

Conditions and cautions:

  • "Capex" in the identity means capitalised additions. If you put cash capex here and also have a CWIP line, the spend is double counted. If you have no CWIP line, cash capex ≈ additions, but then the Balance Sheet CWIP must be held at its last actual or zero.
  • Revaluations (Ind AS 16.31), FX translation of foreign subsidiaries' PPE and acquisitions through business combinations are additional movements in real annual reports; in forecasts they are usually zero.
  • The identity alone does not prevent negative NBV — that needs the depreciation cap (c05).

Illustration

FY26E, ₹ crore: opening NBV 2,400; capitalised additions 650; depreciation 300; disposals at cost 80 with accumulated depreciation 70 (NBV 10).

\[ \text{NBV}_{\text{FY26}} = 2{,}400 + 650 - 300 - 10 = 2{,}740 \]

This equals gross (4,000 + 650 − 80 = 4,570) less accumulated depreciation (1,600 + 300 − 70 = 1,830) — a check row in the model should confirm it.

=NBV_open+Additions-Dep-(Disp_Cost-Disp_AccDep)
=ROUND(NBV_close-(Gross_close-AccDep_close),2)=0

Connections

The identity underpins t2 s01 (three corkscrews), s03 (additions from CWIP), s04 (depreciation layers) and c06 (cash capex reconciliation). It mirrors the week's corkscrew refresh item and the cross-cutting rule that every schedule feeds a P&L charge, a BS closing balance and a CFS movement. The closing NBV feeds the deferred-tax computation in t5 (book NBV vs tax WDV) and ROCE in Phase 2 analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the identity [L1]

Opening NBV ₹1,850 crore; capitalised additions ₹420 crore; depreciation ₹210 crore; assets disposed at cost ₹95 crore with accumulated depreciation ₹70 crore and sale proceeds ₹30 crore; impairment ₹15 crore. Compute closing NBV and the gain or loss on disposal.

Q2. Cash capex vs additions [L2]

A model has a CWIP line and uses cash capex of ₹900 crore as "additions" in the NBV corkscrew, while CWIP also rises by ₹300 crore from the same spend. Explain the error, its effect on the Balance Sheet and depreciation, and the correct link.

💡 Hint: Trace where the ₹300 crore appears in both schedules.

Q3. Reverse-engineer missing data [L2]

A company's PPE note shows opening NBV ₹5,200 crore and closing ₹5,650 crore, depreciation ₹610 crore and disposals at NBV ₹40 crore. The CFS shows proceeds from sale of PPE of ₹55 crore. Derive capitalised additions and the gain/loss on disposal.
