Key concept

Debt Corkscrew Identity

Concept: Closing debt = Opening + Drawdowns − Mandatory amortisation − Optional sweep

Statement

For each tranche \( k \) in year \( t \):

\[ D_{k,t}^{\text{close}} = D_{k,t}^{\text{open}} + \text{Draw}_{k,t} - \text{Mand}_{k,t} - \text{Sweep}_{k,t} \]

where \( D_{k,t}^{\text{open}} = D_{k,t-1}^{\text{close}} \) (first forecast year: linked to last actual); \( \text{Draw}_{k,t} \ge 0 \) is new borrowing (capex-linked draws, refinancing, revolver draws); \( \text{Mand}_{k,t} = \min(\text{Scheduled}_{k,t}, D^{\text{open}}_{k,t}) \) is contractual repayment; and \( \text{Sweep}_{k,t} \ge 0 \) is optional or contractual prepayment from excess cash, capped so that \( D^{\text{close}}_{k,t} \ge 0 \). For a revolver, draws and repayments are combined into one signed change. Total debt is \( \sum_k D_{k,t}^{\text{close}} \).

Explanation

The identity is the debt instance of the generic corkscrew (Opening + Additions − Reductions = Closing). Its value lies in the separation of flows by cause, because each flow is driven by a different part of the model:

Flow Driver Position in waterfall
Drawdowns Capex schedule × debt share; refinancing decisions; revolver on deficit Sources, before revolver
Mandatory amortisation Repayment schedule in loan documents Contractual, before dividends
Optional sweep Excess cash × sweep %, by seniority Last, after revolver

Conditions that keep the identity honest:

  • Every term is a link: draws from capex/financing assumptions, mandatory from repayment tables, sweep from the waterfall.
  • Mandatory is capped at opening (or opening + draws); sweep is capped at balance after mandatory (c04).
  • Balances are non-negative; revolver closing ≤ limit.
  • Non-cash movements — FX translation of foreign-currency loans, Ind AS 109 EIR amortisation of fees, accrued interest capitalised to principal (PIK) — need extra rows if present.

The CFS shows draws as proceeds and repayments (mandatory + sweep) as repayments in CFF, gross for term debt (Ind AS 7.17), typically net for revolving short-term borrowings (Ind AS 7.22–23).

Illustration

Term loan A, FY27E (₹ crore): opening 450; drawdowns 0; scheduled repayment 150; sweep 75.

\[ D^{\text{close}} = 450 + 0 - 150 - 75 = 225 \]

In FY28, scheduled repayment of 150 would leave 75; if FY28 sweep is 120, the cap gives sweep = MIN(120, 225 − 150) = 75 and the loan closes at zero. In FY29, the remaining scheduled instalment of 150 is capped at the zero opening balance.

Mand:   =MIN(Sched_t, Open_t+Draw_t)
Sweep:  =MIN(Sweep_Pool_Rem, Open_t+Draw_t-Mand_t)
Close:  =Open_t+Draw_t-Mand_t-Sweep_t
Check:  =Close_t>=-0.01

Connections

Core of t3 s01 (tranches), s02 (mandatory and draws), s04 (revolver variant) and s05 (sweep). It feeds the Balance Sheet (current/non-current split, s07), CFF in Week 15, and the average balance on which interest is computed (c05), which in turn creates the circularity managed in t4. Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the corkscrew [L1]

A tranche opens FY26 at ₹800 crore; capex-linked draws ₹200 crore; scheduled repayment ₹160 crore; sweep pool available ₹950 crore. Compute the mandatory repayment, sweep and closing balance with the caps in place.

Q2. Multi-year corkscrew with caps [L2]

A ₹600 crore loan has scheduled repayments of ₹150 crore a year for FY26–FY29. Sweeps available to it are ₹100, ₹200 and ₹150 crore in FY26–FY28. Build the corkscrew FY26–FY29 with mandatory and sweep caps, and state in which year the loan is fully repaid.

💡 Hint: Apply mandatory first, then the sweep capped at the remaining balance.

Q3. CFS presentation [L1]

For Q2, show the CFF lines for FY26 and FY27, distinguishing term-loan repayments from any revolver movements, and cite the Ind AS 7 basis for gross vs net presentation.
