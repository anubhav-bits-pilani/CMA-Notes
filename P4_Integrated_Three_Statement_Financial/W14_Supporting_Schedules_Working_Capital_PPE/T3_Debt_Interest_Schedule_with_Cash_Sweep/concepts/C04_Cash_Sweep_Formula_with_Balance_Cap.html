Key concept

Cash Sweep Formula with Balance Cap

Concept: Sweep = MIN(MAX(Cash after revolver, 0) × Sweep %, Opening debt − Mandatory repayment)

Statement

\[ S_{k,t} = \min\left( \max(\text{CAR}_t, 0) \times s,\ D^{\text{open}}_{k,t} + \text{Draw}_{k,t} - \text{Mand}_{k,t} \right) \]

where \( \text{CAR}_t \) is cash after revolver, i.e. cash available (c02) less revolver repayment (zero if the revolver drew); \( s \) is the sweep percentage (0–100%, possibly leverage-linked); \( D^{\text{open}}_{k,t} \) is the tranche's opening balance; \( \text{Draw}_{k,t} \) any same-year draws (often zero); and \( \text{Mand}_{k,t} \) its mandatory repayment. For multiple tranches in seniority order, replace \( \max(\text{CAR}, 0) \times s \) with the pool remaining after more senior sweeps.

Excel:

=MIN(MAX(Cash_After_Rev,0)*Sweep_Pct, Debt_Open-Mand_Repay)

Explanation

Three safeguards are packed into the formula:

  1. MAX(…, 0) — no negative sweep. In a deficit year the revolver has drawn and cash after revolver is zero (or negative if a funding gap exists); without the floor, a negative sweep would become an unintended drawdown on the term loan.
  2. × Sweep % — only the contracted or policy share of excess cash is applied; the remainder builds cash above minimum (or funds permitted dividends in some structures).
  3. MIN(…, Opening − Mandatory) — the sweep cannot exceed what is left after mandatory repayment, so the tranche never goes negative. Missing this cap is a named pitfall: in strong cash years the sweep pushes debt below zero, which the Balance Sheet then shows as a negative borrowing — effectively an asset — and interest becomes interest income.

Priority logic: mandatory comes before the sweep (it is contractual), and the revolver is repaid before the sweep (cheapest to repay, usually required by lenders). Placing the sweep before mandatory repayment double-uses cash.

With multiple tranches:

Pool_1:   =MAX(Cash_After_Rev,0)*Sweep_Pct
Sweep_1:  =MIN(Pool_1, TL1_Open-TL1_Mand)*TL1_Flag
Pool_2:   =Pool_1-Sweep_1
Sweep_2:  =MIN(Pool_2, TL2_Open-TL2_Mand)*TL2_Flag

Circularity: a sweep lowers closing debt → lowers average-balance interest → raises cash → raises the sweep. The breaker (t4) cuts the loop.

Illustration

Cash available ₹380 crore; revolver opening ₹80 crore (repaid in full); sweep 50%; term loan opening ₹250 crore, mandatory ₹100 crore.

Step ₹ crore
Cash after revolver = 380 − 80 300
Pool = 300 × 50% 150
Cap = 250 − 100 150
Sweep = MIN(150, 150) 150
Closing term loan = 250 − 100 − 150 0
Cash retained above minimum 150

At 75% sweep: pool 225, cap 150, sweep 150 — the cap binds, and ₹150 crore stays as cash (or flows to the next tranche).

Q1. Apply the formula [L1]

Q2. Three-year sweep [L2]

A ₹500 crore loan has mandatory repayments of ₹100 crore a year. Cash after revolver is ₹150, ₹320 and ₹400 crore in FY26–FY28; sweep 75%. Build the corkscrew for each year, showing where the cap binds and the cash retained.

💡 Hint: Remember the cap uses the balance after mandatory repayment each year.
