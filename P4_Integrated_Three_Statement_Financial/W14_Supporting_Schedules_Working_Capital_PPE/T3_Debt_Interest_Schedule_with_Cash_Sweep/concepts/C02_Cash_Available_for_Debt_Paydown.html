Key concept

Cash Available for Debt Paydown

Concept: Cash available = Opening cash + CFO + CFI − Dividends − Mandatory repayment − Minimum cash

Statement

\[ \text{CA}_t = C_{t-1} + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t - \text{Div}_t - \text{Mand}_t - C^{\min}_t \]

where \( C_{t-1} \) is opening cash (prior closing, linked); \( \text{CFO}_t \) is cash from operating activities (after tax and ΔNWC, and after interest if interest is in CFO — otherwise subtract interest paid separately); \( \text{CFI}_t \) is investing cash flow (negative for net capex; includes disposal proceeds and, per policy, interest received); \( \text{Div}_t \) is cash dividends paid (and buybacks if modelled); \( \text{Mand}_t \) is total mandatory debt repayment across tranches; and \( C^{\min}_t \) is the minimum operating cash balance. In practice add committed drawdowns and equity raised as sources. \( \text{CA}_t < 0 \) is a deficit to be funded by the revolver; \( \text{CA}_t > 0 \) is a surplus available to repay the revolver and then sweep.

Explanation

The formula is the waterfall (t3 s03) compressed into one line. It embeds the priority CFO → capex → mandatory debt → dividends → revolver → sweep: everything on the right-hand side is either generated by operations or contractually committed; the revolver and sweep are the balancing decisions.

Points of care:

  • Interest placement: Indian CFS presentation usually shows interest paid in CFF. If CFO excludes interest, subtract interest paid on all debt explicitly — otherwise cash available is overstated by the whole interest bill. Interest on the revolver and swept tranches depends on the outcome — the circularity.
  • Lease payments shown in CFF must also be subtracted (they are contractual).
  • Minimum cash is subtracted so that the surplus excludes the operating buffer; adding it back at the end gives closing cash ≥ minimum.
  • Restricted cash is excluded from opening cash.
  • Committed drawdowns (capex-linked) are added; omitting them makes the revolver fund capex that the term loan was meant to fund.
  • The sign convention is that CFI is usually negative; "+ CFI" subtracts net capex.

Illustration

FY26E (₹ crore): opening cash 300; CFO before interest 780; interest paid on existing debt (CFF presentation) 110; CFI −640; dividends 95; mandatory repayment 180; lease payments 60; capex-linked draw 150; minimum cash 200.

\[ \text{CA} = 300 + 780 - 110 - 640 + 150 - 95 - 180 - 60 - 200 = -55 \]

A deficit of ₹55 crore: the revolver draws ₹55 crore (if headroom allows), and closing cash equals the ₹200 crore minimum.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute cash available [L1]

Opening cash ₹150 crore; CFO (after interest) ₹420 crore; CFI −₹310 crore; dividends ₹60 crore; mandatory repayment ₹120 crore; minimum cash ₹100 crore. Compute cash available and state whether the revolver draws or repays.

Q2. Interest placement error [L2]

A model computes cash available using CFO from a CFS that shows interest paid of ₹140 crore in CFF, but omits the interest line in the waterfall. Cash available shows +₹90 crore. Recompute it, explain the consequences for the revolver and sweep, and describe a check that would catch the error.

💡 Hint: Reconcile waterfall closing cash with CFS closing cash.

Q3. Design choice: minimum cash [L2]

A retailer's history shows year-end cash of 20–35 days of cash opex; management targets ₹250 crore minimum; the lender requires a DSRA equal to two quarters of debt service (₹90 crore). Propose how to set minimum cash and restricted cash in the formula, and explain the effect of an overly low minimum on forecast leverage.
