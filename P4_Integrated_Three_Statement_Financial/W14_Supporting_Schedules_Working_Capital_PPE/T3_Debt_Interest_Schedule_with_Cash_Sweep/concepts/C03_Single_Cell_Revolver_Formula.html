Key concept

Single-Cell Revolver Formula

Concept: Revolver Δ: =MIN(MAX(-Rev_Open,-Cash_Avail),Rev_Limit-Rev_Open) (positive = draw, negative = repay)

Statement

=MIN(MAX(-Rev_Open,-Cash_Avail),Rev_Limit-Rev_Open)

\[ \Delta R_t = \min\left( \max\left( -R^{\text{open}}_t,\ -\text{CA}_t \right),\ L_t - R^{\text{open}}_t \right), \qquad R^{\text{close}}_t = R^{\text{open}}_t + \Delta R_t \]

where \( R^{\text{open}}_t \) is the opening revolver balance (Rev_Open), \( \text{CA}_t \) is cash available before the revolver (Cash_Avail, c02; negative = deficit), and \( L_t \) is the effective limit (Rev_Limit, the lower of sanctioned limit and drawing power). \( \Delta R_t > 0 \) is a draw, \( \Delta R_t < 0 \) a repayment.

Explanation

Read it inside-out:

  1. \( -\text{CA}_t \) is the "need": positive in a deficit (need to borrow), negative in a surplus (can repay).
  2. MAX(-Rev_Open, …) floors the change at \( -R^{\text{open}} \): you cannot repay more than you owe. In a surplus larger than the balance, repayment = balance and the excess passes to the sweep.
  3. MIN(…, Rev_Limit-Rev_Open) caps a draw at headroom: you cannot borrow beyond the limit. If the need exceeds headroom, the revolver maxes out and a funding gap remains (closing cash falls below minimum — flag it).

Guarantees: \( 0 \le R^{\text{close}} \le L \) whenever \( 0 \le R^{\text{open}} \le L \). If the limit is cut below the opening balance (facility step-down), \( L - R^{\text{open}} < 0 \) and the formula forces a repayment even in a deficit — mathematically consistent with a mandatory paydown, but if cash is insufficient, closing cash goes below minimum and must be flagged.

Sign convention matters: CFF shows a draw as an inflow and a repayment as an outflow, so link \( \Delta R \) directly (positive = inflow). In a cash-flow-positive convention for Cash_Avail, the negatives in the formula are essential; dropping one is a common bug that makes the revolver draw on surpluses.

Circularity: Cash_Avail depends on interest; interest on average revolver balance depends on \( R^{\text{close}} \). With a circuit breaker (t4), switch interest to opening balance to break the loop.

Illustration

Limit ₹300 crore.

Year Rev_Open Cash_Avail MAX(−Open, −CA) Headroom ΔR Rev_Close
FY26 0 −120 120 300 +120 120
FY27 120 −230 230 180 +180 300
FY28 300 70 −70 0 −70 230
FY29 230 260 −230 70 −230 0

FY27 has a ₹50 crore funding gap (need 230, headroom 180). FY29 repays the full balance and passes ₹30 crore to the sweep.

Two-row equivalent:

Draw:   =MIN(MAX(0,-Cash_Avail),Rev_Limit-Rev_Open)
Repay:  =MIN(MAX(0,Cash_Avail),Rev_Open)

Connections

Implements t3 s04 using c02's cash available; its residual feeds the sweep (c04). The MIN/MAX capping pattern is a week refresh item and recurs in the depreciation cap (t2 c05) and loss set-off (t5). It is the heart of the Saturday drill: a standalone revolver with interest circuit breaker built from a blank sheet; circularity mechanics are in t4.

Practice Questions

Q1. Trace the formula [L1]

Limit ₹500 crore. Compute ΔR and closing balances for four consecutive years with cash available of −₹200, −₹380, ₹150 and ₹400 crore, starting from a zero balance. Identify any funding gap and any amount passed to the sweep.

Q2. Debug sign errors [L2]

A colleague writes =MIN(MAX(-Rev_Open,Cash_Avail),Rev_Limit-Rev_Open). Using a deficit year (Cash_Avail −₹100 crore, Rev_Open ₹50 crore, limit ₹300 crore) and a surplus year (Cash_Avail +₹100 crore, Rev_Open ₹50 crore), show what the faulty formula does and explain the fix.

💡 Hint: Evaluate each nested function with the numbers, step by step.

Q3. Limit step-down [L2]

The bank cuts the CC limit from ₹400 crore to ₹200 crore from FY28; FY28 opening balance is ₹320 crore and cash available is −₹40 crore. Evaluate the formula, interpret the result, and describe what the model should display and what management would need to do.
