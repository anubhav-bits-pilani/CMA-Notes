Key concept

Interest on Average vs Opening Debt Balances

Concept: Interest = Rate × (Opening + Closing) ÷ 2 (circular) or Rate × Opening (non-circular)

Statement

\[ I^{\text{avg}}_t = r_t \times \frac{D^{\text{open}}_t + D^{\text{close}}_t}{2} \qquad \text{(circular)} \]

\[ I^{\text{open}}_t = r_t \times D^{\text{open}}_t \qquad \text{(non-circular)} \]

where \( r_t \) is the tranche's annual rate (benchmark + spread, or fixed coupon), \( D^{\text{open}}_t \) the opening balance and \( D^{\text{close}}_t \) the closing balance after draws, mandatory repayment and sweep. The average-balance version is circular whenever \( D^{\text{close}}_t \) depends on cash flow that itself depends on \( I_t \) (via revolver or sweep).

Explanation

Why average? Debt moves during the year: instalments are paid quarterly, draws occur with capex, the revolver fluctuates. The average of opening and closing approximates the time-weighted balance assuming flows occur evenly. Opening-balance interest overstates interest on a loan being repaid (and understates on one being drawn); for a loan repaid evenly from ₹400 crore to ₹200 crore, opening-basis interest at 9% is ₹36 crore vs ₹27 crore on average — a ₹9 crore gap, i.e., \( r \times \Delta D \div 2 \).

Why circular? Average interest uses closing debt. Closing revolver and swept balances depend on cash available, which depends on CFO after interest (or interest paid in CFF), which depends on interest. Excel either errors ("circular reference") or needs iterative calculation.

Where it is not circular: tranches whose closing balance is fully predetermined — scheduled amortisation, fixed draws, bullets — can use average balance without circularity because their closing does not depend on cash. Circularity arises only through the revolver, sweep and interest income on cash. A practical hybrid: average-balance interest on scheduled tranches; opening-balance interest (or breaker-controlled average) on the revolver and sweepable tranches.

Choosing and documenting: the difference is material when balances change a lot within the year (large capex draws, big sweeps, revolver swings). Many institutional standards (FAST, ICAEW) discourage circularity; banking practice commonly accepts it with a breaker. State the method on the assumptions tab. Circularity mechanics, the breaker and iteration settings are covered in t4.

Illustration

Revolver: opening ₹200 crore, rate 9.5%. Cash available before revolver interest is −₹60 crore; revolver interest is paid in the year and reduces cash.

Opening basis: \( I = 9.5\% \times 200 = 19.0 \); deficit = 60 + 19 = 79; closing = 279.

Average basis (solve the loop): closing \( D = 200 + 60 + 0.095 \times (200 + D) \div 2 \) → \( D (1 - 0.0475) = 260 + 9.5 \) → \( D = 282.9 \); interest = \( 0.095 \times (200 + 282.9) \div 2 = 22.9 \).

Difference ₹3.9 crore ≈ \( r \times \Delta D \div 2 = 0.095 \times 82.9 \div 2 \).

Breaker form:  =IF(Circ=1,Rate*AVERAGE(Open,Close),Rate*Open)

Practice Questions

Q1. Opening vs average [L1]

A term loan opens at ₹750 crore and closes at ₹600 crore after scheduled repayments; rate 8.9%. Compute interest on both bases and the difference; verify it equals Rate × ΔDebt ÷ 2.

Q2. Solve the loop algebraically [L2]

A revolver opens at ₹100 crore with rate 10%. Cash available before revolver interest is −₹150 crore; interest is paid in the year. Solve for closing revolver and interest under the average basis without iteration, and compare with the opening basis.

💡 Hint: Write closing as opening + deficit + interest, substitute the interest formula and solve the linear equation.

Q3. Hybrid design [L2]

A model has a scheduled term loan, a bullet NCD, a sweepable acquisition loan and a CC revolver. Propose which tranches use average and which use opening (or breaker-controlled) interest, justifying each choice with respect to circularity and accuracy.
