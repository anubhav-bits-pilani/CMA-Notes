Subtopic note

Covenants and Current vs Non-Current Classification of Debt

Scope: Covenants: Net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, interest coverage; current vs non-current classification

Why it matters

Debt schedules exist to answer a lender's questions as much as a valuer's: will the company meet its financial covenants in every forecast year, and with how much headroom? Indian bank sanction letters and NCD trust deeds carry covenants such as Net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, interest coverage, TOL/TNW and a minimum current ratio, tested annually or half-yearly on audited numbers. A forecast breach triggers waivers, penal interest, rating pressure or acceleration — and under Ind AS 1 it can force reclassification of long-term debt as current, worsening the current ratio and liquidity optics. A covenant block with headroom analysis turns the model into a credit tool and is standard in FP&A, treasury and rating-agency work.

Core notes

Net debt / EBITDA

\[ \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{EBITDA}} = \frac{\text{Total borrowings} - \text{Cash and liquid investments}}{\text{EBITDA}} \]

Definitional choices that must match the loan agreement:

  • Debt — gross borrowings at face or carrying amount; whether lease liabilities are included (many Indian bank covenants are drafted on pre-Ind AS 116 definitions and exclude them; rating agencies often include them for retailers); whether guarantees and supplier finance count.
  • Cash — unrestricted cash and liquid investments; DSRA and margin money usually excluded.
  • EBITDA — reported, or adjusted for exceptional items; pre- or post-Ind AS 116 (if leases are excluded from debt, EBITDA should be after rent — consistency).
  • Test basis — closing debt vs trailing twelve-month EBITDA.

Typical thresholds: 2.5–3.5× for investment-grade corporates; up to 4–5× in acquisition finance at close, stepping down.

DSCR (Indian lender definition)

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Principal repayment}} \]

where principal repayment is scheduled (mandatory) repayment of term debt in the year — not revolver repayments or voluntary prepayments — and interest is on term debt (some banks include all interest). Indian banks typically require minimum DSCR of 1.20–1.30× and average DSCR of ~1.30–1.50× over the loan tenor in project appraisals. Project finance often uses CFADS-based DSCR instead (cash flow after tax, capex and ΔNWC ÷ debt service). See c06 for the formula and caveats.

Interest coverage ratio (ICR)

\[ \text{ICR} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest expense}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Interest expense}} \]

Rating agencies and lenders specify which; minimums of 2.0–3.0× are common. Use total interest including capitalised interest if the agreement says "interest accrued" — P&L finance cost understates during construction (t2 s03).

Other Indian covenants

  • TOL/TNW (total outside liabilities ÷ tangible net worth), often ≤ 2.5–3.0×.
  • Current ratio ≥ 1.33× (legacy of MPBF/Tandon method for working-capital limits).
  • Debt/equity ≤ 1.5–2.0×; FACR (fixed asset coverage ratio) ≥ 1.25–1.33× for secured term loans.
  • Negative covenants: restrictions on dividends, further borrowing, change in shareholding/control, disposal of secured assets.

Covenant block in the model

Covenant Threshold FY26E FY27E FY28E Headroom FY28
Net debt / EBITDA ≤ 3.0× 2.6× 2.9× 2.2× EBITDA can fall 27%
DSCR ≥ 1.25× 1.42× 1.18× 1.55× —
ICR (EBIT/interest) ≥ 2.5× 3.4× 3.1× 4.0× —
Breach flag 0 1 0

Headroom is best shown as "EBITDA cushion": % by which EBITDA can fall before breach:

\[ \text{EBITDA cushion} = 1 - \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{Threshold} \times \text{EBITDA}} \]

FY28: \( 1 - 2.2 \div 3.0 \approx 27\% \).

The FY27 DSCR breach (a year with a large instalment) would prompt actions: re-profiling repayments, equity infusion, dividend cut, or a waiver — model a scenario toggle.

Current vs non-current classification

Ind AS 1.69: a liability is current if it is due to be settled within twelve months after the reporting period, or the entity does not have the right at the end of the reporting period to defer settlement for at least twelve months, among other criteria. For the model:

  • Current portion at 31 March of year \( t \) = scheduled repayments in year \( t+1 \) + bullets maturing in year \( t+1 \) + revolver/CC + CP. Schedule III (2021 amendment) shows current maturities of long-term borrowings within current borrowings.
  • Refinancing: if the entity expects and has the discretion to refinance or roll over an obligation for at least twelve months under an existing loan facility, it is non-current (Ind AS 1.73); merely planned refinancing does not qualify.
  • Covenant breach: if a breach on or before the reporting date makes the liability payable on demand, it is current (Ind AS 1.74). Indian carve-out: under Ind AS 1.74 as notified, if the lender agrees before the approval of the financial statements not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach, the liability need not be classified as current — unlike IAS 1, which requires the waiver to be obtained by the reporting date. Check whether MCA has notified the IASB's later amendments on classification and covenants (IAS 1, 2020/2022) when modelling FY27 onwards.
  • Schedule III disclosures: defaults in repayment (period and amount) and, since 2021, additional regulatory information including wilful defaulter status, utilisation of borrowed funds, and reconciliation of quarterly current-asset statements filed with banks.

In the model, a forecast covenant breach with no assumed waiver should move the entire tranche to current borrowings in the BS presentation for that year — a presentation toggle linked to the breach flag.

Excel implementation

Net_Debt:     =Total_Debt-Cash-Liquid_Inv
ND_EBITDA:    =IFERROR(Net_Debt/EBITDA,0)
DSCR:         =(PAT+Dep+Int_Term)/(Int_Term+Mand_Repay)
ICR:          =IFERROR(EBIT/Total_Interest,99)
Breach:       =OR(ND_EBITDA>ND_Max,DSCR<DSCR_Min,ICR<ICR_Min)*1
Current_Debt: =IF(AND(Breach,NOT(Waiver)),Total_Term_Debt,Next_Yr_Mand)+Rev_Close+CP

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative EBITDA makes Net debt/EBITDA meaningless — show "n.m." and flag as breach.
  • Equity cure rights in acquisition finance allow sponsors to inject equity to cure a breach — model as a toggle.
  • Half-yearly testing on trailing EBITDA requires quarterly data; annual models approximate.
  • Pro-forma EBITDA for acquisitions — agreements usually allow including acquired EBITDA for the full year.
  • DSCR in bullet years collapses — lenders typically exclude refinanced bullets from principal; check the definition.
  • Lease-adjusted metrics — CRISIL/ICRA may compute lease-adjusted leverage for retailers even when bank covenants do not.

Key takeaways

  • Build a covenant block with thresholds, forecast ratios, breach flags and EBITDA-cushion headroom.
  • Definitions (lease inclusion, cash, EBITDA basis, interest scope) must match the agreement.
  • Indian DSCR = (PAT + Depreciation + Interest) ÷ (Interest + Principal); minimums around 1.2–1.3×.
  • Current portion = next year's scheduled repayments + maturing bullets + revolver + CP.
  • A covenant breach makes debt current unless the Ind AS 1.74 carve-out applies (waiver before approval of the financial statements).

Q1. Compute covenant ratios [L1]

FY27E: EBITDA ₹900 crore; depreciation ₹250 crore; interest on term debt ₹160 crore, total interest ₹190 crore; PAT ₹340 crore; total borrowings ₹2,800 crore; cash ₹350 crore; scheduled principal repayment ₹420 crore. Compute Net debt/EBITDA, DSCR (Indian definition) and ICR on EBIT. Assess compliance against 3.0×, 1.25× and 2.5×.

Q2. EBITDA cushion and breach scenario [L2]

Using Q1, compute the EBITDA cushion for the leverage covenant. Then determine the EBITDA decline at which the DSCR covenant breaches, assuming PAT falls by 74.83% of the EBITDA decline (tax at 25.17%) and other items are unchanged.

💡 Hint: Express DSCR as a function of the EBITDA decline and solve for the threshold.

Q3. Classification after a breach [L2]

At 31 March 2026 a company breaches its DSCR covenant on a ₹900 crore term loan (₹150 crore scheduled for FY27). The bank issues a letter on 25 April 2026 agreeing not to demand repayment; the board approves the financial statements on 20 May 2026. Explain the classification under Ind AS 1 as notified in India versus IAS 1, and the effect on the current ratio if current assets are ₹1,800 crore and other current liabilities ₹1,100 crore.

Q4. Lease liabilities in covenants [L3]

A fashion retailer has bank debt of ₹400 crore, lease liabilities of ₹3,200 crore, cash of ₹500 crore, post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA of ₹1,900 crore and rent (fixed lease payments) of ₹750 crore. Compute Net debt/EBITDA on (a) a pre-Ind AS 116 basis and (b) a lease-inclusive basis. Explain which definition a bank vs a rating agency would use, and how the model's covenant block should be designed to show both.
