Subtopic note

Floating vs Fixed Interest and Interest Income on Cash

Scope: Interest on floating debt (MCLR/EBLR/repo-linked + spread) vs fixed coupon; interest income on cash

Why it matters

Interest is where the debt schedule meets the P&L. For an Indian borrower with a mix of bank loans and NCDs, the interest line depends on benchmark rates set by RBI policy and bank funding costs, on negotiated spreads that move with credit ratings, and on the balance profile created by repayments, revolver draws and sweeps. A 100 bp move in the repo rate passes through to MCLR- and repo-linked loans with different lags, while fixed-coupon NCDs are insulated until refinancing. Interest income on surplus cash is the mirror image — small for most companies, material for cash-rich IT, FMCG and pharma groups. Getting the rate mechanics, balance basis and CFS classification right matters for EPS, interest coverage covenants, and the Kd used in WACC.

Core notes

Indian floating-rate benchmarks

  • Repo rate: RBI's policy rate set by the Monetary Policy Committee; the anchor of the rate system. Check the current rate and the policy stance when setting forecasts — it is date-sensitive.
  • MCLR (Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate): introduced by RBI from April 2016; each bank publishes MCLRs by tenor (overnight to 1 year, some up to 3 years) monthly, based on its marginal cost of funds, negative carry on CRR, operating costs and a tenor premium. Loans are priced at MCLR + spread and reset at a reset frequency (typically annually on the 1-year MCLR). Transmission of policy changes is gradual — often lagging repo moves by two or more quarters.
  • EBLR (External Benchmark-based Lending Rate): since 1 October 2019 RBI has required new floating-rate retail and MSE loans to be linked to an external benchmark (repo rate, 3-month or 6-month T-bill yield, or other FBIL benchmark), with reset at least once every three months. Large corporates can negotiate repo- or T-bill-linked pricing too; transmission is faster.
  • Spreads reflect credit risk (rating), tenor, security and relationship; they can be reset on rating changes or at contractual reset dates.

Floating rate in the model:

\[ r_t = \text{Benchmark}_t + \text{Spread} \]

with the benchmark as a forecast input row (e.g., repo path from consensus or RBI guidance; MCLR as repo + historical gap, lagged), and the spread as a tranche input with an optional rating-linked step.

Fixed coupons

NCDs, most CP (discount), some ECBs (after swapping) and fixed-rate term loans carry rates fixed until maturity or reset. In the model: rate input fixed per tranche; at maturity, if refinanced, the new tranche takes the then-prevailing benchmark plus spread. This is where refinancing risk appears in interest: a 7.2% NCD issued in a low-rate year refinanced at 8.5% raises interest even with constant debt.

Interest rate swaps convert floating to fixed (or vice versa); under Ind AS 109 hedge accounting, the effective rate is the swapped rate. Model the all-in hedged rate for the hedged notional.

Interest expense calculation

\[ \text{Interest}_t = r_t \times \text{Balance basis}_t + \text{Fees}_t \]

The balance basis is either opening balance (non-circular) or the average of opening and closing (circular; t4 and c05):

Basis Formula When to use
Opening \( r \times \text{Opening} \) Simple models; balances stable; no mid-year draws
Average \( r \times (\text{Opening} + \text{Closing}) \div 2 \) Large intra-year draws/repayments; revolver-heavy models; with breaker
Instalment-weighted \( r \times \sum \text{balance} \times \text{days outstanding} \div 365 \) Precise scheduled amortisation; outside revolver loop

For an amortising loan repaid quarterly, average-balance interest is a good approximation; opening-balance interest overstates interest on loans being repaid and understates on loans being drawn.

Effective interest rate (Ind AS 109): the P&L charge is EIR × amortised cost; upfront fees and transaction costs are spread over the life. For forecasting, either include amortisation of fees as a separate line (cash paid at inception, non-cash amortisation thereafter) or use an all-in rate. Unamortised fees reduce the carrying amount on the Balance Sheet.

Capitalised interest (Ind AS 23, t2 s03) is deducted from P&L finance cost but is still cash interest.

Interest income on cash

\[ \text{Interest income}_t = r^{\text{cash}}_t \times \overline{\text{Cash}}_t \]

  • Rate: bank FD rates, liquid/overnight MF yields or T-bill yields — typically 50–200 bp below the repo/benchmark-linked borrowing cost.
  • Basis: average cash creates a second circular loop (cash depends on interest income); opening cash is non-circular.
  • Apply only to cash above operating minimum if the minimum is held in non-interest-bearing current accounts.
  • Gains on liquid mutual funds held at FVTPL are presented as "net gain on financial assets at FVTPL", not interest income — the model can treat them alike but label correctly.
  • Net interest = interest expense − interest income; many valuation and credit metrics use net finance cost.

CFS classification

Ind AS 7.31–34: interest paid and received must be disclosed separately and classified consistently. For non-financial entities, Indian practice typically presents interest paid in financing and interest received in investing (Ind AS 7.33), whereas some companies present interest paid in operating. Since the indirect CFO starts from PBT in Indian presentation, finance costs are added back and interest income deducted in CFO, then shown in CFF/CFI. Follow the company's policy in the historical CFS.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rate floors in loan agreements (spread over benchmark with a minimum all-in rate) limit benefit from cuts.
  • Lagged MCLR transmission — reflect with a lag factor rather than instant pass-through.
  • Rating downgrades raise spreads at reset; link spread to a covenant/rating scenario.
  • FX loans — interest in foreign currency; translate at forecast rates; hedging cost adds to the rate.
  • Penal interest on overdue amounts; RBI's 2023 guidelines on penal charges require them as charges rather than added interest — rarely modelled but relevant in distressed cases.
  • Negative carry: holding large cash while borrowing at higher rates; the waterfall order should minimise it.

Key takeaways

  • Floating rate = benchmark (repo, T-bill, MCLR) + spread, with resets and transmission lags.
  • Fixed coupons protect until maturity; refinancing re-prices at the then-prevailing rate.
  • Interest on average balance is more accurate but circular; opening-balance is simpler.
  • Interest income on average cash is a second circular loop; apply only to interest-bearing cash.
  • Classify interest paid and received per the company's Ind AS 7 policy, usually CFF and CFI.

Practice Questions

Q1. Floating vs fixed under a rate cut [L1]

A company has ₹1,000 crore of term loans at 1Y MCLR + 0.50% (MCLR 9.00%, reset annually on 1 April) and ₹800 crore of NCDs at 7.90% fixed. RBI cuts repo by 50 bp in October 2025 and MCLR falls by 30 bp before the April 2026 reset. Compute FY26 and FY27 interest on opening balances (no repayments) and explain the transmission lag.

Q2. Opening vs average interest [L2]

A term loan opens FY26 at ₹600 crore with ₹150 crore of quarterly repayments in FY26 and a ₹300 crore drawdown on 1 October 2025 for capex; rate 9.1%. Compute FY26 interest on (a) opening balance, (b) average of opening and closing, (c) a time-weighted basis, and comment on which is most accurate.

💡 Hint: For the time-weighted basis, track the balance quarter by quarter.

Q3. Interest income and net finance cost [L2]

A cash-rich FMCG company holds average surplus cash of ₹4,500 crore in liquid funds and FDs yielding 6.8% and has ₹300 crore of working-capital debt at 8.2%. Compute net finance income, and explain how presentation of MF gains, CFS classification and the choice of average vs opening cash affect the model.

Q4. Refinancing risk [L3]

A company rated CRISIL A+ has ₹1,200 crore of NCDs at 7.4% maturing in FY28. Forecast refinancing rate assumptions under a base case (repo flat, spread unchanged) and a stress case (repo +75 bp, downgrade adding 125 bp to spread). Describe how you would build the refinancing tranche and quantify the FY29 interest difference.
