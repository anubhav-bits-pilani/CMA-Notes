Subtopic note

Mandatory Amortisation, Bullet Maturities and Capex-Linked Draws

Scope: Mandatory amortisation per repayment schedule; bullet maturities; capex-linked drawdowns

Why it matters

Mandatory debt service is the first claim on cash after operations and capex, and it is contractual — missing it is a default, not a choice. The repayment profile determines the current portion of borrowings on the Balance Sheet, the DSCR that lenders test, and the maturity wall that drives refinancing risk and rating actions. Drawdowns, meanwhile, are tied to capex: project term loans are disbursed against certified spend, so the debt schedule must link to the capex and CWIP schedules or the model will show cash shortfalls during construction and idle cash afterwards. Getting these mechanics right is the foundation for the cash waterfall, revolver and sweep that follow.

Core notes

Repayment profiles

1. Equal instalments (straight-line amortisation) — most common for Indian corporate term loans: principal repaid in equal quarterly or monthly instalments after any moratorium.

\[ \text{Mandatory}_t = \min\left( \text{Opening}_t,\ \frac{\text{Original principal}}{\text{Number of instalments}} \times \text{Instalments in year } t \right) \]

2. Percentage schedule — the sanction letter specifies a repayment table (e.g., 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 25% of principal over six years). Model as an input row of percentages × original principal.

3. Ballooning / back-ended — common where cash flows ramp up (new plants, renewables); same mechanism as a percentage table.

4. DSCR-sculpted — project finance (renewables, roads): repayment sized each period so that DSCR equals a target (e.g., 1.25×):

\[ \text{Principal}_t = \frac{\text{CFADS}_t}{\text{Target DSCR}} - \text{Interest}_t \]

where CFADS is cash flow available for debt service. This is circular with interest and is usually solved by a macro or algebra; note it for project-finance models.

5. Bullet — the full principal at maturity (NCDs, some ECBs, CP). Model as:

=IF(Year=Maturity_Year, Opening_Balance, 0)

with a refinancing assumption toggle: repay from cash/revolver, or refinance via a new tranche drawn in the same year (gross presentation in CFF: repayment and fresh borrowing both shown).

6. Annuity (EMI) — constant total payment, rising principal portion; occasionally used for equipment loans. Principal = EMI − interest on opening balance.

Moratoriums and partial years

Repayment start dates rarely align with 1 April. Convert to annual amounts by counting instalments falling in each FY. Example: 20 quarterly instalments of ₹30 crore starting 30 September 2025 → FY26 has 3 instalments (Sep, Dec, Mar) = ₹90 crore; FY27–FY30 4 each = ₹120 crore; FY31 1 = ₹30 crore. An instalment-count helper row avoids errors.

The cap that matters

Mandatory repayment must never exceed the opening balance (plus same-year drawdowns, if the contract allows repayment of amounts drawn in the year). Without MIN(Opening, Scheduled), a sweep in an earlier year that partly prepaid the loan causes later scheduled repayments to drive the balance negative. Contracts usually apply prepayments in inverse order of maturity or pro rata to remaining instalments — the model's simplest consistent treatment is to keep the scheduled amount and cap it at the opening balance, which approximates inverse-order application.

Capex-linked drawdowns

Project and capex loans are drawn against spend, typically with a debt:equity funding ratio (e.g., 70:30):

\[ \text{Drawdown}_t = \min\left( \text{Debt share} \times \text{Capex spend}_t,\ \text{Undrawn commitment}_{t} \right) \]

Requirements in the model:

  • Link capex spend from the PPE/CWIP schedule (t2 s03), not re-typed.
  • Cap draws at the sanctioned amount; track undrawn commitment (on which a commitment fee may apply).
  • Stop draws after the availability period ends (e.g., 24 months from first disbursement).
  • Equity share must come from internal accruals or fresh equity — the cash waterfall must show it.
  • Capitalised interest during construction (Ind AS 23) depends on the drawn balance — link to CWIP.

Illustration

Term loan B: sanction ₹500 crore, 70% of capex funded by debt, capex spend FY26 ₹400 crore, FY27 ₹350 crore; moratorium until 31 March 2028; then 12 equal quarterly instalments.

₹ crore FY26 FY27 FY28 FY29 FY30 FY31
Opening 0 280 500 500 333 167
Drawdown (70% × capex, capped) 280 220 0 0 0 0
Mandatory repayment 0 0 0 (167) (167) (167)
Closing 280 500 500 333 167 0

FY27 draw is capped: 70% × 350 = 245, but only 220 remains of the sanction. Instalments ₹41.7 crore a quarter → ₹166.7 crore a year.

Presentation: current maturities

Schedule III (2021 amendment) presents current maturities of long-term borrowings within current borrowings. The model's current portion at 31 March of year \( t \) = scheduled mandatory repayment in year \( t+1 \) (plus bullets maturing in \( t+1 \)). Classification judgement in covenant breaches is covered in s07.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Refinancing a bullet — avoid assuming refinancing at the same rate if ratings or markets have moved; run a stress case where it is repaid from the revolver.
  • Restructured loans with step-up repayments or conversion options need bespoke tables.
  • FX-denominated loans (ECB) — repayment in rupees changes with exchange rates; include translation or hedged rate.
  • Prepayment penalties when sweeping — deduct from cash and expense (or include in EIR recalculation under Ind AS 109).
  • Mid-year drawdowns — if interest is on opening balance, a large mid-year draw creates zero interest in year 1; use an average or a timing factor (t4).
  • Partial-year modelling at transaction close dates (LBO/acquisition) — stub periods require pro-rated instalments.

Key takeaways

  • Choose the repayment profile from the sanction letter: equal instalments, percentage table, ballooning, sculpted, bullet or EMI.
  • Count instalments per fiscal year; moratoriums and start dates rarely align with 1 April.
  • Cap mandatory repayment at the opening balance so earlier sweeps never cause negative debt.
  • Drive drawdowns from linked capex × debt share, capped at the undrawn sanction and availability period.
  • Current portion = next year's scheduled repayments and maturing bullets.

Practice Questions

Q1. Instalment counting [L1]

A ₹480 crore term loan is repayable in 24 equal quarterly instalments starting 31 December 2025. Compute annual mandatory repayment for FY26 to the final year and the current maturities at each 31 March.

Q2. Percentage schedule with a prior sweep [L2]

A ₹1,000 crore loan has a repayment table of 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30% of original principal for FY26–FY30. In FY26 the company also sweeps ₹300 crore. Build the corkscrew with and without the MIN(Opening, Scheduled) cap and identify the year in which the uncapped version breaks.

💡 Hint: Sum the scheduled amounts remaining after the sweep and compare with the balance.

Q3. Capex-linked draws with an availability period [L2]

A ₹900 crore capex loan funds 75% of a plant's spend; availability period ends 31 March 2027. Capex spend: FY26 ₹600 crore, FY27 ₹500 crore, FY28 ₹100 crore. Compute drawdowns, the equity funding required each year and the undrawn commitment at each year-end. Explain the cash-waterfall implication for FY28.

Q4. Bullet refinancing toggle [L3]

A ₹1,500 crore NCD matures in FY28. Design a refinancing toggle with three options — repay from cash and revolver, refinance with a new 5-year NCD, or partial refinance — and explain the effect of each on CFF presentation, interest cost, covenant ratios and the maturity profile.
