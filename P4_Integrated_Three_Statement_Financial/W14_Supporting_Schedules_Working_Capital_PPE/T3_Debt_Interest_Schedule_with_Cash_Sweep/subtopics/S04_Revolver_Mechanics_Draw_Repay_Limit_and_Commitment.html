Subtopic note

Revolver Mechanics: Draw, Repay, Limit and Commitment Fee

Scope: Revolver mechanics: draw on deficit, repay first on surplus, limit and commitment fee

Why it matters

The revolver is the model's automatic liquidity balancer. In Indian practice it is usually the cash credit or WCDL limit (or a committed revolving facility for larger groups); in LBO and US-style models it is the revolving credit facility (RCF). It ensures cash never falls below the minimum when there is headroom, and that surplus cash retires the most expensive, most flexible debt first. Mis-specifying it is one of the commonest model errors — revolvers that draw beyond the limit, never repay, or repay below zero — and the assessment for this week asks you to build one from a blank sheet, with a circuit breaker, in an hour.

Core notes

Behaviour

  1. Draw on deficit: if cash available (s03) is negative, draw the shortfall, up to remaining headroom (limit − opening balance).
  2. Repay first on surplus: if cash available is positive, repay the revolver first — up to its opening balance — before any sweep or cash build-up. Revolvers are typically the most expensive floating debt and freely repayable, and lenders' sweep clauses usually require them cleared first.
  3. Never negative: repayment cannot exceed the opening balance.
  4. Never above limit: closing balance ≤ committed limit (and ≤ drawing power, if modelled).

The single-cell formula

With positive = draw and negative = repay (c03):

Rev_Change = MIN(MAX(-Rev_Open, -Cash_Avail), Rev_Limit - Rev_Open)

How it works:

  • \( -\text{Cash\_Avail} \) is the amount needed (positive in a deficit, negative in a surplus).
  • MAX(-Rev_Open, -Cash_Avail) floors repayment at the opening balance: in a surplus you repay the smaller of the surplus and the balance.
  • MIN(…, Rev_Limit - Rev_Open) caps a draw at the remaining headroom.

Closing revolver = Rev_Open + Rev_Change.

Some modellers split into two rows for readability:

Draw:    =MIN(MAX(0,-Cash_Avail), Rev_Limit-Rev_Open)
Repay:   =MIN(MAX(0,Cash_Avail), Rev_Open)

Both forms are equivalent; the two-row version makes the CFS presentation (proceeds vs repayments) easier.

Worked cases

Limit ₹500 crore.

Case Rev_Open Cash_Avail −Cash_Avail MAX(−Rev_Open, −Cash_Avail) Headroom Rev_Change Closing
Deficit within limit 100 −150 150 150 400 +150 250
Deficit exceeds headroom 400 −180 180 180 100 +100 500
Surplus < balance 250 90 −90 −90 250 −90 160
Surplus > balance 60 200 −200 −60 440 −60 0
No revolver, surplus 0 300 −300 0 500 0 0

In case 2 the revolver is maxed out and an ₹80 crore funding gap remains: closing cash falls ₹80 crore below minimum — the check row must flag it.

Limit, drawing power and step-downs

  • Limit is an input by year (sanctioned limits are renewed annually and can step up with turnover; banks use the projected-turnover or MPBF methods to assess limits).
  • Drawing power cap (s01): Effective_Limit = MIN(Sanctioned, DP).
  • Step-down or expiry: if the facility expires, the limit goes to zero and any balance must be repaid — the formula then forces a negative change bounded by cash available, and a residual balance above the zero limit should be flagged.

Interest and commitment fee

\[ \text{Revolver interest}_t = r_{\text{rev}} \times \overline{\text{Balance}}_t \qquad \text{Commitment fee}_t = f \times \left( \text{Limit}_t - \overline{\text{Balance}}_t \right) \]

where \( \overline{\text{Balance}} \) is the average of opening and closing balance (circular, t4) or opening (non-circular), and \( f \) is the fee on the undrawn portion (in Indian bank CC limits, typically small or charged as a commitment/non-utilisation charge, e.g., 0.25–0.50% on unutilised limits beyond a threshold; in syndicated RCFs 30–40% of margin). Commitment fees are finance costs in the P&L and cash outflows in CFF/CFO with interest.

Because revolver interest depends on the closing balance, which depends on cash available, which depends on CFO after interest — the revolver is the main source of circularity. Use the circuit breaker (t4).

Presentation

  • Balance Sheet: revolver within current borrowings (repayable on demand / annual renewal).
  • CFS (Ind AS 7.22–23): cash flows for items with quick turnover, large amounts and short maturities may be reported on a net basis — Indian companies commonly show "proceeds from/(repayment of) short-term borrowings (net)". The model can show net revolver change in one line.

Excel implementation

Rev_Open:    =prior Rev_Close
Rev_Change:  =MIN(MAX(-Rev_Open,-Cash_Avail),Eff_Limit-Rev_Open)
Rev_Close:   =Rev_Open+Rev_Change
Rev_Int:     =IF(Circ=1,Rev_Rate*AVERAGE(Rev_Open,Rev_Close),Rev_Rate*Rev_Open)
Commit_Fee:  =Fee_Pct*MAX(0,Eff_Limit-AVERAGE(Rev_Open,Rev_Close))
Check:       =AND(Rev_Close>=-0.01,Rev_Close<=Eff_Limit+0.01)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Revolver used for permanent funding (always drawn near the limit) — a sign the company needs term debt or equity; in valuation, treat it as debt.
  • Sign convention errors — mixing "positive = repay" and "positive = draw" conventions across rows is a frequent bug; label the row.
  • Minimum cash and revolver at the same time — a company with cash above minimum and a drawn revolver is irrational in the model (it would repay); in reality it happens because of trapped cash in subsidiaries or timing; model trapped cash explicitly.
  • Multiple revolvers — prioritise by cost; the second revolver draws only when the first is maxed.
  • Interest income on cash vs revolver cost — a model that holds surplus cash while revolver is drawn overstates net interest; the waterfall order prevents this.

Key takeaways

  • The revolver draws on deficit up to headroom and repays first on surplus, never below zero.
  • =MIN(MAX(-Rev_Open,-Cash_Avail),Rev_Limit-Rev_Open) encodes all four rules in one cell.
  • Effective limit = lower of sanctioned limit and drawing power; step-downs force repayment.
  • Interest on average balance creates circularity; add a commitment fee on undrawn limit.
  • Flag funding gaps when the revolver is maxed rather than letting cash go below minimum.

Practice Questions

Q1. Evaluate the revolver formula [L1]

Limit ₹400 crore. For each case compute the revolver change and closing balance: (a) opening 0, cash available −₹120 crore; (b) opening 350, cash available −₹90 crore; (c) opening 200, cash available ₹50 crore; (d) opening 120, cash available ₹310 crore.

Q2. Five-year revolver with interest and fee [L2]

Cash available before revolver for FY26–FY30 is −₹80, −₹140, ₹60, ₹150 and ₹200 crore (assume these already reflect revolver interest for simplicity). Opening revolver ₹50 crore; limit ₹250 crore; rate 9.25% on opening balance; commitment fee 0.40% on undrawn limit at opening. Build the revolver schedule, interest and fee, and identify any funding gap.

💡 Hint: Run the MIN/MAX formula year by year; carry each closing balance to the next opening.

Q3. Drawing-power step-down [L2]

In a downturn year, inventory and receivables fall so that drawing power drops to ₹180 crore, while the opening revolver balance is ₹260 crore and cash available is ₹40 crore. Explain what the formula does, what the bank would require, and how the model should present the outcome.

Q4. Blank-sheet build [L3]

Without reference material, specify the rows, formulas, named ranges and checks for a revolver block that handles draws, repayments, a limit that steps up 10% a year, drawing power, average-balance interest with a circuit breaker and a commitment fee — as you would in the Saturday drill.
