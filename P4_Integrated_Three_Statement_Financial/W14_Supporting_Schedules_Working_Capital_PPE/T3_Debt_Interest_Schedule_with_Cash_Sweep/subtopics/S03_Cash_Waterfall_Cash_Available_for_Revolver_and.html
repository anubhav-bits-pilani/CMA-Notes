Subtopic note

Cash Waterfall: Cash Available for Revolver and Sweep

Scope: Cash waterfall: opening cash + pre-financing cash flow − minimum cash − mandatory repayment − dividends

Why it matters

The cash waterfall is the decision engine of a three-statement model. It answers one question each period: after the business has generated or consumed cash, paid for capex, met contractual debt service and paid shareholders, is there a surplus to repay revolving debt and sweep term debt, or a deficit that the revolver must fund? Without a waterfall, closing cash is just a plug and can go negative; with one, the model behaves like a treasury function. Lenders' cash-sweep clauses, LBO structures and FP&A liquidity forecasts all run on the same logic, and the order of priority embedded in it is what reviewers check first.

Core notes

The standard ordering

The priority of uses (cross-cutting rule for this week): CFO → capex → mandatory debt → dividends → revolver → sweep. Contractual and operational items come first; discretionary items last.

\[ \text{Cash available}_t = \text{Opening cash}_t + \underbrace{\text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t}_{\text{pre-financing cash flow}} - \text{Mandatory repayment}_t - \text{Dividends}_t + \text{Committed drawdowns}_t \pm \text{Other financing}_t - \text{Minimum cash}_t \]

where:

  • Opening cash is the prior closing cash (first forecast year: last actual, linked).
  • Pre-financing cash flow = CFO + CFI = cash from operations (after tax and working capital) plus investing flows (capex, disposals, investments, interest income if classified there). CFO here is before revolver/sweep effects but includes interest on existing debt — which is why interest can make the waterfall circular (t4).
  • Mandatory repayment — scheduled amortisation and bullets on all tranches (s02).
  • Dividends — cash dividends paid in the year (final dividend for the prior year plus interim dividends), plus buybacks if modelled; lease payments if leases are shown in CFF.
  • Committed drawdowns — capex-linked term-loan draws and fresh equity raised are sources and enter before the revolver.
  • Minimum cash — operating cash buffer that cannot be used to repay debt (e.g., 15–30 days of opex, or a fixed ₹ amount; lenders may also require a debt service reserve account).

If cash available is negative, the revolver draws (s04); if positive, the revolver repays first, then any surplus may be swept to term debt (s05), with the residual accumulating as cash above the minimum.

Why dividends before the revolver?

Most corporate models treat declared dividends as committed once declared (and Sec 123 limits them to distributable profits). Placing them before the revolver means the model will borrow on the revolver to pay a dividend — which highlights an aggressive payout policy. An alternative, used in leveraged or covenant-constrained models, is a dividend lock-up: dividends paid only if cash available (before dividends) is positive and covenants are met. Lenders' sanction terms often restrict dividends when DSCR falls below a threshold or during default. Make the order a documented design choice.

Worked illustration

Specialty chemicals company, FY27E (₹ crore):

Line Amount
Opening cash 180
CFO (after interest and tax) 620
CFI (capex net of disposals) (540)
Pre-financing cash flow 80
Term loan B drawdown (capex-linked) 220
Mandatory repayment — term loan A (150)
NCD bullet 0
Dividends paid (90)
Minimum cash (150)
Cash available for revolver / (deficit) 90
Opening revolver (CC) 150
Revolver repayment: MIN(150, 90) (90)
Cash after revolver for sweep 0
Closing cash = minimum + residual 150
Closing revolver 60

If CFO had been ₹400 crore, cash available would be −₹130 crore and the revolver would draw ₹130 crore (subject to its limit).

Closing cash

\[ \text{Closing cash}_t = \text{Minimum cash}_t + \text{Cash available}_t + \text{Revolver draw}_t - \text{Revolver repay}_t - \text{Sweep}_t \]

which, by construction, is ≥ minimum cash unless the revolver hits its limit. A check row should flag Closing cash < Minimum cash — that means the revolver limit is binding and the company needs another funding source (equity, new tranche, capex deferral). Never let the model silently go below minimum or negative.

Excel layout

Build the waterfall as a block on the debt tab, rows in priority order, columns as years. Every row is a link (CFO from the CFS, capex from PPE, mandatory from each tranche, dividends from equity). Name key rows (Cash_Avail, Rev_Open, Rev_Limit) for readable revolver and sweep formulas (c03, c04).

Cash_Avail: =Cash_Open+CFO+CFI+Draws-Mandatory_Total-Dividends-Min_Cash
Closing:    =Min_Cash+Cash_Avail+Rev_Change-Sweep_Total
Flag:       =IF(Closing_Cash<Min_Cash-0.01,"FUNDING GAP","")

Interaction with the CFS

The CFS must reflect the waterfall's outcomes: revolver draws/repayments and sweeps appear in CFF; the CFS closing cash must equal the waterfall's closing cash and the Balance Sheet cash. Interest on the revolver and swept debt feeds back into CFO → that loop is the circularity managed in t4.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Seasonal intra-year deficits are invisible in an annual waterfall; size the revolver limit for peak (t1 s06).
  • Restricted cash (DSRA, margin money) is excluded from opening cash available.
  • Interest income on cash is part of CFO/CFI and depends on cash balance — second circular loop.
  • Capex flexibility — in a downturn, management defers growth capex; a "capex deferral" switch before the revolver can model this.
  • Equity raises (QIP, rights) are sources; include them before the revolver with timing.
  • Minimum cash definition — too low a buffer flatters debt paydown; too high traps cash. Calibrate to history (cash as days of opex) or lender requirements.

Key takeaways

  • Order: CFO → capex → mandatory debt → dividends → revolver → sweep.
  • Cash available = opening cash + pre-financing cash flow + committed draws − mandatory repayment − dividends − minimum cash.
  • Negative cash available is funded by the revolver; positive repays the revolver first, then sweeps.
  • Closing cash must never fall below minimum without a visible funding-gap flag.
  • Every waterfall row is a link from another schedule; outcomes flow back to CFF and the Balance Sheet.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute cash available [L1]

FY26E: opening cash ₹240 crore; CFO ₹510 crore; capex ₹380 crore; proceeds from asset sale ₹20 crore; mandatory repayments ₹160 crore; dividends ₹70 crore; capex-linked drawdown ₹100 crore; minimum cash ₹120 crore. Compute cash available for the revolver and closing cash if the opening revolver balance is ₹80 crore and no sweep applies.

Q2. Deficit year with a limit [L2]

Same company, FY27E: CFO ₹260 crore; capex ₹520 crore; mandatory repayments ₹160 crore; dividends ₹75 crore; no drawdowns; minimum cash ₹120 crore; revolver limit ₹300 crore. Using FY26 closing balances from Q1, compute the revolver draw, closing revolver, closing cash and whether a funding gap arises. Propose two management actions and show how you would model them.

💡 Hint: The revolver can only draw up to the limit less the opening balance.

Q3. Dividend placement [L2]

Explain the difference in outcomes between placing dividends before the revolver and making them conditional on positive cash available, using a year in which pre-financing cash flow is −₹50 crore and the declared dividend is ₹80 crore. Which design would a lender prefer, and how would Sec 123 of the Companies Act 2013 constrain the dividend in any case?

Q4. Model-build: waterfall block [L3]

Specify, row by row, a cash waterfall block for a model with three term loans, one NCD bullet, a CC revolver, a cash sweep on two tranches and a dividend lock-up when Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 3.0×. Include the named ranges, the order of calculation, the checks and how outputs link to the CFS and Balance Sheet.
