Key concept

Closing Retained Earnings Formula

Concept: Closing retained earnings = Opening + PAT (owners) − Dividends declared ± Transfers to reserves

Statement

\[ RE_t = RE_{t-1} + \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t - \text{Div}^{\text{declared}}_t \pm T_t \]

where \(RE_t\) is closing retained earnings for period \(t\); \(RE_{t-1}\) opening retained earnings (prior closing); \(\text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t\) profit for the period attributable to owners of the parent (consolidated PAT less NCI share); \(\text{Div}^{\text{declared}}_t\) equity dividends recognised in the period (interim dividends declared in \(t\) plus the final dividend for \(t-1\) approved at the AGM in \(t\)); and \(T_t\) net transfers between retained earnings and other components of equity (to general reserve, CRR, DRR, statutory reserves; from FVOCI equity reserve, revaluation surplus, SBP reserve on lapse).

Explanation

This is the corkscrew that ties the P&L to the balance sheet. Three features make it more than "opening + profit − dividends":

  1. Owners' share only. Under Ind AS 110.B94, profit is attributed between owners and NCI; only the owners' share enters retained earnings. The NCI share enters the NCI corkscrew.
  2. Dividends when declared, not when "relating to". Ind AS 10.12–13 prevents recognising a final dividend recommended after the reporting date; it is recognised on shareholder approval. Under old Indian GAAP proposed dividends were provided — a legacy habit that causes errors.
  3. Transfers are within equity. Every \(T_t\) is mirrored by an opposite entry in another reserve; total equity does not change. Transfers to general reserve are discretionary under the Companies Act 2013; CRR transfers are mandatory on buybacks out of free reserves (Sec 69); NBFCs transfer 20% of PAT to a statutory reserve (Sec 45-IC RBI Act).

OCI does not pass through this formula via PAT; OCI accumulates in its own reserves (c04), except that some companies present DB remeasurements directly in retained earnings through OCI, and FVOCI equity cumulative gains may be transferred to RE on derecognition — both are captured as transfers.

Model checks: (i) the first forecast opening links to the last actual closing; (ii) a check row confirms closing RE − (opening + PAT − dividends ± transfers) = 0; (iii) a reasonableness check that RE never turns negative while dividends are being declared without free reserves.

Illustration

FY27E: opening RE ₹1,540 crore; consolidated PAT ₹390 crore, NCI share ₹22 crore; FY26 final dividend ₹2.50 × 40 crore shares approved at the July 2026 AGM; FY27 interim ₹1.50 × 40 crore shares; transfer to general reserve ₹30 crore; revaluation surplus realised ₹4 crore.

\[ RE_{\text{FY27}} = 1{,}540 + 368 - (100 + 60) - 30 + 4 = 1{,}722 \]

Closing RE ₹1,722 crore. Note the FY27 final dividend (recommended May 2027) is disclosed but not deducted.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the formula [L1]

Opening RE ₹620 crore; standalone PAT ₹145 crore; FY27 interim ₹1.20 per share and FY26 final ₹2.00 per share (approved September 2026), both on 15 crore shares; transfer to DRR ₹8 crore. Compute FY27 closing RE.

Q2. Reconcile a reported SOCE [L2]

A company's reported FY26 SOCE shows: opening RE ₹2,210 crore, profit ₹508 crore (of which NCI ₹31 crore), remeasurement loss on DB plans ₹6 crore (net of tax, taken to RE), dividends ₹240 crore, transfer from FVOCI reserve ₹18 crore, closing RE ₹2,459 crore. Test whether the reported closing balance reconciles with the formula and identify any unexplained difference.

💡 Hint: Check whether the profit figure entering RE should be consolidated or owners' share.

Q3. Legacy-GAAP error [L2]

An analyst deducts the FY27 final dividend of ₹150 crore from FY27 RE and records a ₹150 crore liability at 31 March 2027. Explain the error under Ind AS 10, its effect on FY27 and FY28 balance sheets and SOCE, and whether cash flows are affected.
