Key concept

Total Equity and NCI Presentation Within Equity

Concept: Total equity = Share capital + Other equity; NCI presented within equity (Ind AS 110)

Statement

For a standalone entity under Schedule III Division II:

\[ \text{Total equity} = \text{Equity share capital} + \text{Other equity} \]

For a consolidated group:

\[ \text{Total equity} = \underbrace{\text{Equity share capital} + \text{Other equity}}_{\text{Equity attributable to owners of the parent}} + \text{Non-controlling interests} \]

where equity share capital is face value × paid-up shares; other equity comprises reserves and surplus (securities premium, general reserve, CRR, SBP reserve, retained earnings, other reserves), OCI reserves, equity component of compound instruments, share application money pending allotment and money received against warrants; and NCI is the equity in subsidiaries not attributable to the parent (Ind AS 110.22; Ind AS 110 Appendix A definition).

Explanation

Presentation rules. Schedule III Division II shows "Equity share capital" and "Other equity" as separate line items under "Equity" on the face of the balance sheet; consolidated statements add "Non-controlling interests" within equity. Preference shares classified as financial liabilities under Ind AS 32 are in borrowings, not share capital. The Statement of Changes in Equity has two parts — equity share capital and other equity — with a column for each component of other equity, and (in consolidated statements) columns for owners' total, NCI and total.

Why NCI is equity. Under the entity concept adopted by Ind AS 110, NCI holders are owners of the group, not creditors: the group has no obligation to deliver cash to them except as dividends. Hence NCI is within equity, profit is attributed to it, and transactions with NCI that do not change control are equity transactions (Ind AS 110.23).

Modelling implications:

  • Balance-sheet check: Total assets = Total liabilities + Equity attributable to owners + NCI. Omitting NCI from equity, or showing it among liabilities, breaks the check.
  • Ratios: leverage and ROE should be computed consistently — ROE on owners' equity uses owners' PAT; net debt/equity on total equity uses consolidated equity including NCI.
  • Book value per share uses equity attributable to owners only (less any preference capital classified within equity).
  • Valuation bridge: NCI is deducted from EV like a claim, even though it is presented in equity.

Illustration

Consolidated balance sheet at 31 March 2028 (₹ crore): equity share capital 64; securities premium 820; general reserve 450; CRR 12; SBP reserve 26; retained earnings 2,940; FVOCI equity reserve 38; FCTR 57; NCI 410.

  • Other equity = 820 + 450 + 12 + 26 + 2,940 + 38 + 57 = ₹4,343 crore.
  • Equity attributable to owners = 64 + 4,343 = ₹4,407 crore.
  • Total equity = 4,407 + 410 = ₹4,817 crore.
  • With 32 crore shares (face value ₹2), book value per share = 4,407 ÷ 32 = ₹137.7.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and total [L1]

From the following, compute equity attributable to owners and total equity, classifying each item correctly (₹ crore): equity share capital 120; 8% redeemable preference shares 50; securities premium 300; retained earnings 900; debenture redemption reserve 20; cash flow hedge reserve −5; NCI 140; money received against share warrants 15.

Q2. ROE consistency [L2]

Consolidated PAT ₹520 crore (NCI share ₹70 crore); opening and closing owners' equity ₹3,000 crore and ₹3,300 crore; opening and closing NCI ₹400 crore and ₹450 crore. Compute ROE on owners' equity and on total equity, and explain which numerator goes with which denominator.

💡 Hint: Match owners' PAT with owners' equity and consolidated PAT with total equity, using averages.

Q3. Presentation error [L2]

A model shows NCI as a non-current liability and adds consolidated PAT in full to retained earnings. Explain the two errors, show why the balance sheet may still balance, and describe the effect on book value per share and ROE.
