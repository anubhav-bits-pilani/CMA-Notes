Key concept

OCI Flows to Reserves, Usually Forecast at Zero

Concept: OCI flows to reserves, not to retained earnings via P&L; usually forecast at zero

Statement

For each period:

\[ \text{TCI}_t = \text{PAT}_t + \text{OCI}_t \]

\[ \text{Reserve}^{k}_t = \text{Reserve}^{k}_{t-1} + \text{OCI}^{k}_t \;(\text{net of tax}) - \text{Reclassified to P\&L}^{k}_t \pm \text{Transfers within equity}^{k}_t \]

where \(\text{TCI}_t\) is total comprehensive income; \(\text{OCI}^{k}_t\) the OCI of component \(k\) (FVOCI equity, FVOCI debt, DB remeasurement, FCTR, cash flow hedge, cost of hedging, revaluation surplus); reclassification applies only to components that recycle (FVOCI debt, FCTR on disposal, cash flow hedges); and transfers within equity move cumulative balances to retained earnings for non-recycling items. PAT, not TCI, enters retained earnings and EPS. In most forecasts, \(\text{OCI}^{k}_t = 0\) for all future periods.

Explanation

OCI bypasses the P&L. Items such as remeasurement of defined benefit obligations (Ind AS 19), fair-value changes on FVOCI equity investments (Ind AS 109), translation differences on foreign operations (Ind AS 21) and the effective portion of cash flow hedges (Ind AS 109) are recognised in OCI and accumulated in reserves within other equity. They do not affect PAT, EPS or retained earnings directly — although some components are later reclassified to P&L (recycled) and some are transferred within equity to retained earnings.

Why forecast at zero. OCI items are typically market-driven (equity prices, discount rates, exchange rates, hedge fair values) and mean-reverting or unpredictable. Forecasting them adds noise and false precision, and creates linked complexity (deferred tax on OCI, reclassification). The convention is to hold OCI reserves flat at the last actual balance, which keeps the balance sheet consistent and avoids implying a market view.

When not to forecast zero:

  • A cash flow hedge reserve with a large opening balance that will be reclassified to P&L as hedged sales occur over the next year — model its unwinding (it affects forecast revenue/COGS).
  • FCTR for a company whose results are mostly from foreign subsidiaries, when the model explicitly includes FX scenarios.
  • Revaluation surplus — the annual transfer to retained earnings equal to excess depreciation (a transfer, not OCI).
  • Planned disposal of a foreign operation or FVOCI debt portfolio, triggering reclassification.

Cash flow. OCI is non-cash: it does not appear in the indirect-method CFO (which starts from PBT). If an OCI item is modelled, the counter-entry must be in the related balance (investment, pension liability, translated net assets, derivative) so the balance sheet still balances — another argument for zero.

Illustration

FY27 actual: FVOCI equity reserve ₹62 crore; DB remeasurement reserve −₹14 crore; FCTR ₹35 crore; cash flow hedge reserve ₹9 crore (forward contracts hedging next year's USD sales, maturing within FY28).

FY28E forecast: FVOCI equity, DB remeasurement and FCTR held flat; cash flow hedge reserve reclassified fully to revenue in FY28 — revenue increases by ₹12 crore (pre-tax), reserve falls by ₹9 crore (net of ₹3 crore deferred tax reversing), closing reserve nil. FY29E onwards: all OCI reserves flat. Retained earnings receive only PAT (which now includes the ₹12 crore hedge gain less tax).

Practice Questions

Q1. Trace the flow [L1]

For each item, state whether it affects PAT, OCI, retained earnings or another reserve in the year it arises, and whether it is ever recycled: (a) actuarial gain ₹5 crore; (b) FVOCI equity fair-value loss ₹11 crore; (c) cash flow hedge gain of ₹7 crore on a hedge of next quarter's imports; (d) FCTR gain of ₹20 crore.

Q2. Unwinding a hedge reserve [L2]

Opening cash flow hedge reserve ₹24 crore (net of tax at 25.168%) relates to forward contracts hedging USD export receipts in FY28 (60%) and FY29 (40%). Model the reserve and the P&L effect for FY28 and FY29, assuming no new hedges.

💡 Hint: Gross up the net reserve for tax before reclassifying to revenue.

Q3. Defend the zero assumption [L2]

A reviewer asks why your forecast shows no OCI despite historical DB remeasurement losses of ₹8–15 crore each year. Write a short justification and describe the conditions under which you would change the assumption.
