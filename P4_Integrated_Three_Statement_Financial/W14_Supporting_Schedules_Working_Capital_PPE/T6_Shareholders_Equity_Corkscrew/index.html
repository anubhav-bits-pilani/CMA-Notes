Roadmap topic: Shareholders' Equity Corkscrew: Share capital, Retained Earnings (Opening + PAT – Dividends), and Other Reserves

Overview

The equity schedule is the last of the supporting schedules and the one that closes the model. Every other schedule hands something to it: the P&L delivers PAT attributable to owners and to non-controlling interests, the debt and cash waterfall decides whether dividends and buybacks can be afforded, the share-based payment charge creates a reserve, and OCI items (fair-value changes, actuarial remeasurements, translation differences, hedges) flow into separate reserves. If the equity corkscrew is wrong, the balance sheet will not balance, the cash flow from financing activities will be wrong and EPS — the single number most quoted for NSE-listed companies — will be misstated.

For the target roles the schedule matters in three ways. In financial reporting, it is the forecast version of the Statement of Changes in Equity required by Ind AS 1 and Schedule III Division II, with "Equity share capital" and "Other equity" presented separately and NCI shown within equity under Ind AS 110. In modelling and valuation, dividends and buybacks are the main uses of surplus cash after debt service, and the share count drives per-share value and EPS. In FP&A and corporate finance, the schedule tests capital-allocation decisions against the legal constraints of the Companies Act 2013 — dividends only out of profits (Sec 123), buybacks within Sec 68 limits, capital redemption reserve under Sec 69 — and against the dividend distribution policy that the top 1,000 listed entities must publish under SEBI LODR.

The topic is sequenced as follows: start with share capital and securities premium, including ESOP allotments (s01); build the retained-earnings corkscrew (s02, c01); add the other components of equity and OCI reserves (s03, c04); model dividend policy and the timing gap between final dividend declaration and payment (s04, c02); handle buybacks and their capital, reserve and tax consequences (s05); build the NCI corkscrew for consolidated models (s06, c03); and finish with the share-count schedule that feeds basic and diluted EPS (s07, c05).

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 33, Ind AS 102 and Ind AS 10 on dividends

→ Phase 1 (W1–4): Ind AS 33 EPS, Ind AS 102 share-based payment, Ind AS 10 dividends as non-adjusting events

Three Phase 1 standards underpin this schedule. Ind AS 33 defines basic and diluted EPS, the weighted-average share count, retrospective adjustment for bonus issues and splits, and the treasury stock method for options — the rules the share-count schedule (s07) applies. Ind AS 102 requires equity-settled ESOPs to be expensed over the vesting period with a credit to a share-based payment reserve, which is transferred to share capital and premium on exercise (s01, s03). Ind AS 10.12–13 states that dividends declared after the reporting period are not a liability at the reporting date; they are disclosed (Ind AS 1.137). This is why the final dividend for FY t is deducted from retained earnings in FY t+1 (s04, c02).

Sec 123 dividends and Sec 68 buyback limits

Companies Act 2013: Sec 123 dividends out of profits, Sec 68 buyback limits

Sec 123 permits dividends only out of current-year profits after depreciation, undistributed profits of earlier years, or both, after setting off brought-forward losses and unprovided depreciation; unrealised, notional and fair-value gains are excluded when computing profits available. Declaring out of free reserves in a loss year is subject to the conditions in the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014. Sec 68 restricts buybacks to free reserves, securities premium or proceeds of a different issue; to 25% of paid-up capital plus free reserves (10% with board approval only); equity bought back to 25% of paid-up equity capital in a year; and post-buyback debt-to-equity of 2:1 unless a higher ratio is notified. The model should test every forecast distribution against these limits.

Dividends and buybacks in CFF

→ Week 15 (w15_t2): dividends and buybacks in CFF

Week 15 integrates the statements. Dividends paid to owners of the parent, dividends paid to NCI, buyback consideration and transaction costs, proceeds from share issues (including ESOP exercise money) and any tax paid by the company on distributions all appear in cash flow from financing activities (Ind AS 7.17; dividends paid may be classified as financing or operating under Ind AS 7.34, with financing the norm in India). The equity schedule must supply these as cash amounts, which differ from the amounts deducted in equity when dividends are declared but unpaid at year-end (dividends payable) or when buyback cash settles after year-end.

Diluted share count via the treasury stock method

→ Week 16 (w16_t4): diluted share count via treasury stock method

In valuation, equity value is converted to value per share using the fully diluted share count. The treasury stock method assumes in-the-money options and warrants are exercised and the proceeds used to buy back shares at the current market price, so net new shares = options × (1 − exercise price ÷ share price). Convertibles are added on an if-converted basis when in the money. Week 16 builds this for the DCF output. The share-count schedule here provides the basic shares, the ESOP pool outstanding, exercise prices and any convertible instruments that Week 16 needs, and Ind AS 33 diluted EPS uses the same mechanics with average market price.

Common pitfalls

Recording final dividend in the year it relates to rather than when paid

The mistake: the FY27 final dividend (recommended by the board in May 2027, approved at the August 2027 AGM) is deducted from FY27 retained earnings and treated as FY27 cash outflow. Why it happens: under pre-2016 Indian GAAP (old AS 4), proposed dividends were provided for in the year they related to; many analysts still think of dividends "for the year". Correct treatment: under Ind AS 10, the final dividend is a non-adjusting event; it is recognised as a deduction from equity and a liability when approved by shareholders, i.e., in FY28, and the cash is paid within 30 days of declaration (Sec 127) — also in FY28. Interim dividends declared and paid in the year belong to that year. Detection: check that FY t dividends in the SOCE equal final DPS of FY t−1 plus interim DPS of FY t, and that historical columns reconcile with reported SOCE dividends.

NCI share of profit left inside parent retained earnings

The mistake: consolidated PAT is added in full to retained earnings, and NCI is held flat at its last actual balance. Why it happens: the P&L shows "Profit for the year" and the split between owners and NCI is often placed below it and overlooked. Correct treatment: Ind AS 110.B94 and Schedule III require profit and total comprehensive income to be attributed to owners of the parent and to NCI. Retained earnings take only the owners' share; the NCI corkscrew takes the NCI share less dividends paid to NCI (s06). Detection: compare NCI-to-consolidated-PAT ratios in history and forecast; check that EPS uses owners' PAT; test that RE movement equals owners' PAT less owners' dividends.

Dividends paid with no check against cash availability or distributable profits

The mistake: dividends are set as a payout ratio of PAT with no link to cash or legal capacity, so in weak years the model pays dividends funded by revolver draws, or pays dividends from a negative retained earnings balance. Why it happens: payout ratios are simple and match historical behaviour in good years. Correct treatment: cap dividends at the lower of policy amount, cash available after debt service and minimum cash (in the waterfall), covenant-permitted distributions under loan agreements, and distributable profits under Sec 123. Detection: a check row flagging dividends > distributable profit, dividends paid in any year with a revolver draw, or retained earnings turning negative while dividends continue.

Practice Questions

Q1. Five-year equity corkscrew for a listed company [L3]

Konkan Consumer Ltd (NSE-listed, FY ending 31 March) has, at 31 March 2026: 50 crore equity shares of ₹2 face value; securities premium ₹420 crore; general reserve ₹300 crore; share-based payment reserve ₹18 crore; FVOCI equity reserve ₹25 crore; retained earnings ₹2,140 crore; NCI ₹96 crore. Forecast consolidated PAT: FY27 ₹610 crore, FY28 ₹690 crore, FY29 ₹770 crore, of which NCI share is 6%. Dividend policy: 45% payout of owners' PAT, split one-third interim (paid in the year) and two-thirds final (paid after the AGM in the following year); FY26 final dividend of ₹5.50 per share is approved at the August 2026 AGM. Subsidiary dividends to NCI equal 40% of NCI share of PAT, paid in the same year. ESOP expense ₹12 crore a year; 0.30 crore options exercised in FY28 at ₹180 per share, with ₹9 crore of SBP reserve relating to them. (a) Build the share capital, securities premium, SBP reserve, retained earnings and NCI corkscrews for FY27–FY29. (b) Compute cash dividends to owners and to NCI each year. (c) Compute basic EPS each year. (d) Identify where each item appears in the CFS.

💡 Hint: Remember the FY27 cash outflow includes the FY26 final dividend and none of the FY27 final.

Q2. Buyback feasibility and accounting [L3]

Deccan Engineering Ltd has paid-up equity capital of ₹200 crore (20 crore shares of ₹10), securities premium ₹600 crore, general reserve ₹900 crore, retained earnings ₹1,100 crore and borrowings ₹1,800 crore. It proposes a tender-offer buyback of 2 crore shares at ₹550 per share in FY28, funded from cash. (a) Test the proposal against the Sec 68 limits and the 2:1 debt-equity condition. (b) Pass the journal entries, assuming the premium over face value is charged first to securities premium and then to general reserve, and CRR is created as required. (c) Explain the tax position of the company and of a resident individual shareholder for a buyback after 1 October 2024, noting what you would verify in the latest Finance Act. (d) Show the effect on the share-count schedule and FY28 weighted-average shares if the buyback settles on 1 December 2027.

Q3. Reviewing a broken equity schedule [L2]

A reviewer finds in a model: total equity rises each year by consolidated PAT less dividends; NCI is constant at ₹45 crore; the FY27 SOCE deducts the FY27 final dividend; an actuarial loss of ₹8 crore is deducted from PAT; and the balance sheet balances only because of a "plug" in other reserves. Consolidated PAT is ₹300 crore with NCI share 10%. For each issue, explain the error, the correct treatment and the expected effect on balance-sheet equity and EPS.

Q4. Dividend capacity under constraints [L3]

Vindhya Cement Ltd forecasts FY28 owners' PAT of ₹140 crore after a loss-making FY27 that left a debit balance in retained earnings of ₹60 crore; free reserves (general reserve) are ₹220 crore. Its term-loan agreement restricts dividends to 30% of PAT when net debt/EBITDA exceeds 3.0×; forecast net debt/EBITDA is 3.4×. Minimum cash ₹80 crore; projected cash after debt service ₹110 crore. The board wants to maintain ₹4 per share on 25 crore shares. Determine the binding constraint, the dividend you would model, and the check rows you would add.
