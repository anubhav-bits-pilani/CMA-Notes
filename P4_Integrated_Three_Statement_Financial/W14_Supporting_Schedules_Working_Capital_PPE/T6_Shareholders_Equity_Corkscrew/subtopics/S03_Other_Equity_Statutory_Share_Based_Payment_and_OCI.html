Subtopic note

Other Equity: Statutory, Share-Based Payment and OCI Reserves

Scope: Other equity: general reserve, capital redemption reserve, share-based payment reserve, OCI reserves (FVOCI, DB remeasurement, FCTR, hedge)

Why it matters

"Other equity" under Schedule III Division II is a single line on the face of the balance sheet but a dozen components in the notes and the SOCE. Each component has its own source of movement — board transfers, statutory requirements under the Companies Act, Ind AS 102 expense, or OCI items under Ind AS 109, 19 and 21 — and its own rules on whether it can be distributed or recycled to profit or loss. A forecast model needs a corkscrew for each material reserve, both to reconcile to the reported SOCE and to compute distributable reserves for dividends and buybacks. Analysts who lump them into one "reserves" line cannot answer "how much can the company legally distribute?"

Core notes

Schedule III components of other equity

Schedule III Division II lists: share application money pending allotment; equity component of compound financial instruments; reserves and surplus (capital reserve, securities premium, other reserves — specifying nature and purpose — and retained earnings); debt instruments through OCI; equity instruments through OCI; effective portion of cash flow hedges; revaluation surplus; exchange differences on translating the financial statements of a foreign operation; other items of OCI (specify nature); and money received against share warrants. Below are the components most often modelled.

General reserve

Created by voluntary transfers from retained earnings. Under the Companies Act 1956 and the Companies (Transfer of Profits to Reserves) Rules, 1975, transfers were required before declaring dividends above certain rates; the Companies Act 2013 (Sec 123(1)) makes such transfers discretionary. Many older Indian companies carry large general reserves from that era. General reserve is a free reserve — available for dividends (subject to the 2014 Dividend Rules when declaring out of accumulated reserves) and buybacks. Model: opening + transfers from RE − utilisation (e.g., buyback premium, bonus issue).

Capital redemption reserve (CRR)

Required by Sec 69 when a company buys back shares out of free reserves or securities premium: a sum equal to the nominal value of shares bought back is transferred to CRR. Also required by Sec 55 when preference shares are redeemed out of profits. CRR is not a free reserve; it can be used only for issuing fully paid bonus shares. Model: opening + nominal value of buyback (or preference redemption out of profits) − bonus capitalisation.

Share-based payment reserve

Credited with the Ind AS 102 equity-settled ESOP expense; debited on exercise (transfer to share capital and securities premium, s01) and on lapse of vested options (transfer to general reserve or RE). Not a free reserve. Model:

Row Logic
Opening prior closing
+ ESOP expense for the year from opex build (often a % of employee costs or a fixed schedule)
− Transfer on exercise fair value of options exercised
− Transfer on lapse fair value of vested options lapsed
Closing sum

Unvested forfeitures reverse through the P&L expense (true-up), not as a reserve transfer.

OCI reserves

Items of other comprehensive income accumulate in separate reserves. Whether they are ever recycled (reclassified) to profit or loss depends on the standard:

Reserve Source standard Movement Recycled to P&L? Typical forecast
FVOCI — equity instruments Ind AS 109.5.7.5 Fair-value changes on strategic equity investments designated at FVOCI, net of deferred tax No; cumulative gain/loss may be transferred within equity (to RE) on derecognition Zero, or explicit price assumption if large
FVOCI — debt instruments Ind AS 109.4.1.2A Fair-value changes less amounts recognised in P&L (ECL, EIR interest, FX) Yes, on derecognition Zero
Remeasurement of defined benefit plans Ind AS 19.120(c), 122 Actuarial gains/losses and return on plan assets excluding interest, net of tax No; may be transferred within equity Zero
Foreign currency translation reserve (FCTR) Ind AS 21.39, 48 Translation of foreign operations' net assets and results Yes, on disposal of the foreign operation Zero, or FX-scenario driven
Cash flow hedge reserve Ind AS 109.6.5.11 Effective portion of hedging instrument gains/losses Yes, when hedged item affects P&L, or basis adjustment to non-financial asset Zero, or reversal of opening balance over hedge horizon
Cost of hedging reserve Ind AS 109.6.5.15–16 Time value of options/forward points if elected Yes, as per hedged item Zero
Revaluation surplus Ind AS 16.39–41 Upward revaluation under revaluation model No; transferred to RE as realised Excess-depreciation transfer

In Schedule III presentation, OCI items are split into those that will not be reclassified (FVOCI equity, DB remeasurement, revaluation surplus) and those that will be (FVOCI debt, FCTR, cash flow hedges), each with the related income tax.

Deferred tax on OCI items

OCI items are presented net of tax; the deferred tax is recognised in OCI (Ind AS 12.61A). In the model, if an OCI item is forecast, the corresponding deferred tax balance moves too — another reason most models forecast OCI at zero (c04).

Corkscrew design

Build one block per material reserve with opening, additions, transfers and closing, then sum into "Total other equity". Add a check that the sum of transfers across all reserves and RE is zero, because transfers move value between components without changing total equity.

Illustration

FY28E for a mid-cap chemicals company: general reserve opening ₹410 crore, transfer from RE ₹40 crore; CRR opening ₹6 crore, buyback of 0.5 crore shares of ₹10 out of general reserve → CRR + ₹5 crore, general reserve − ₹5 crore (plus the premium charged as per s05); SBP reserve opening ₹15 crore, expense ₹8 crore, exercise transfer ₹6 crore → closing ₹17 crore; DB remeasurement reserve opening −₹9 crore, forecast nil; cash flow hedge reserve opening ₹3 crore, assumed to unwind fully to P&L in FY28 → closing nil. Total other-equity movement from these blocks: +40 (offset in RE) + 0 (CRR/GR swap) + 2 − 3.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Free reserves definition (Sec 2(43)): excludes unrealised gains, notional gains, revaluation and fair-value changes; FVOCI and revaluation reserves are not free reserves for dividends or buybacks.
  • Capital reserve: arises on bargain purchases (Ind AS 103.34–36, recognised in OCI and accumulated in capital reserve, or directly in capital reserve where no clear evidence of bargain purchase) and on common-control combinations (Appendix C). Not distributable.
  • DB remeasurement presentation: some companies keep a separate reserve; others take it directly to RE through OCI. Follow the company's historical presentation for reconciliation.
  • Hedge reserve reversal: if the opening balance is material, model its unwinding to P&L (or basis adjustment to inventory/PPE) rather than leaving it frozen.
  • Debenture redemption reserve: after the August 2019 amendment to Rule 18(7), DRR is not required for listed companies or for NBFCs/HFCs; unlisted companies maintain 10% of outstanding debentures. Model only where applicable.
  • Money received against share warrants: equity until conversion or forfeiture; on forfeiture, transfer to capital reserve.

Key takeaways

  • Model each material reserve as its own corkscrew; sum to other equity and check transfers net to zero.
  • General reserve is free; CRR, SBP reserve, OCI reserves and revaluation surplus are not.
  • CRR equals the nominal value of shares bought back out of free reserves or premium (Sec 69).
  • SBP reserve rises with ESOP expense and falls on exercise or lapse.
  • Know which OCI reserves recycle (FVOCI debt, FCTR, cash flow hedges) and which do not (FVOCI equity, DB remeasurement, revaluation surplus).
  • OCI items carry deferred tax in OCI; most forecasts set them at zero.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and treat [L1]

For each of the following, state the reserve affected, whether it is a free reserve, and whether it will ever be reclassified to P&L: (a) actuarial loss of ₹6 crore; (b) ₹14 crore gain on a strategic equity stake designated at FVOCI; (c) ₹9 crore translation gain on a Singapore subsidiary; (d) ₹4 crore effective portion of a forward contract hedging USD sales; (e) ₹3 crore nominal value of shares bought back from general reserve.

Q2. Five-block other-equity schedule [L2]

Opening balances (₹ crore): general reserve 520, CRR 0, SBP reserve 24, FVOCI equity reserve 41, cash flow hedge reserve −6. FY28E: transfer from RE to general reserve 30; buyback of 1 crore shares of ₹10 face value at ₹420, with premium charged to general reserve; ESOP expense 11; options exercised with fair value 9; FVOCI equity investment sold with cumulative gain 41 transferred to RE; hedge reserve fully reclassified to P&L. Build each block and total other equity.

💡 Hint: The buyback reduces share capital by face value; CRR is created out of the reserve used.

Q3. Distributable reserves [L3]

A company's other equity totals ₹2,300 crore: securities premium 700, general reserve 600, retained earnings 650, CRR 20, SBP reserve 30, FVOCI equity reserve 180, revaluation surplus 120. Determine the reserves available for (a) dividend and (b) buyback under the Companies Act 2013, explaining each inclusion and exclusion.

Q4. Forecasting OCI [L2]

An Indian IT company with large USD-denominated subsidiaries has an FCTR of ₹1,240 crore. The analyst wants to forecast FCTR using a rupee depreciation assumption of 2% a year. Discuss whether this is appropriate, the data you would need, and the alternative of forecasting OCI at zero.
