Subtopic note

Retained Earnings Corkscrew

Scope: Retained earnings: opening + PAT attributable to owners − dividends ± transfers

Why it matters

Retained earnings is the account through which the P&L reaches the balance sheet. In an integrated model it is the single most important link for balancing: PAT flows in, dividends flow out, and every other change in retained earnings must be explained by a named transfer. Reviewers reconcile the forecast retained-earnings movement against PAT and dividends first when the balance sheet fails to balance. For Indian companies the account also carries Ind AS transition adjustments, items transferred from OCI reserves (FVOCI equity on derecognition, revaluation surplus realised), and legal significance — distributable profit under Sec 123 starts from it.

Core notes

The corkscrew

\[ RE_t = RE_{t-1} + \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t - \text{Div}^{\text{declared}}_t \pm \text{Transfers}_t \]

where \(RE\) is retained earnings ("Surplus in the Statement of Profit and Loss" in older presentations), \(\text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}\) is profit attributable to owners of the parent, \(\text{Div}^{\text{declared}}\) is equity dividends recognised in the period (interim dividends declared in the year plus the prior year's final dividend approved at the AGM in this year), and transfers are movements to or from other components of equity.

Each element

Opening balance. The first forecast opening must be a link to the last actual closing balance on the historical balance sheet — never retyped. Historical columns should themselves reconcile to the reported SOCE.

PAT attributable to owners. In consolidated models, profit for the year is split between owners of the parent and NCI (Ind AS 110.B94; Schedule III requires the split on the face of the P&L). Only the owners' share enters retained earnings; the NCI share goes to the NCI corkscrew (s06). In standalone models, PAT is entirely the owners'.

Two subtleties:

  • Share of profit of associates and JVs (equity method under Ind AS 28) is part of consolidated PAT and enters retained earnings, even though no cash is received until the associate pays dividends.
  • Discontinued operations: profit from discontinued operations is part of PAT and enters retained earnings.

Dividends declared. Equity dividends are recognised when declared — the interim dividend when the board declares it, the final dividend when shareholders approve it at the AGM (Ind AS 10.12–13). In a 31 March year-end model, FY t dividends recognised = final for FY t−1 + interim for FY t (s04, c02). Dividends on preference shares classified as financial liabilities are finance costs in the P&L, not deductions from retained earnings.

Transfers (±). Typical transfers in Indian companies:

Transfer Direction for RE Trigger
To general reserve − Board decision; no longer mandatory under the Companies Act 2013
To capital redemption reserve − Buyback out of free reserves (Sec 69) or preference redemption out of profits (Sec 55)
To/from debenture redemption reserve − / + Unlisted companies issuing debentures (Sec 71 and Rule 18(7)); release on redemption
From FVOCI equity reserve + / − Cumulative gain/loss transferred on derecognition of the equity investment
From revaluation surplus + Realised on use (excess depreciation) or derecognition (Ind AS 16.41)
Remeasurement of defined benefit plans ± Some companies present DB remeasurements directly in RE (via OCI)
From SBP reserve + Lapse of vested options, if company policy transfers there
Statutory reserve (NBFCs) − 20% of PAT under Sec 45-IC of the RBI Act
Buyback premium/tax charged to RE − If free reserves used are retained earnings

Each transfer must have an equal and opposite entry in another component of equity, so total equity is unaffected.

Model rows

Row Formula
Opening RE = prior closing (first year = last actual)
+ PAT attributable to owners = P&L link
− Interim dividend declared = Interim DPS × shares on record date
− Final dividend (prior year) approved = Prior-year final DPS × shares on record date
± Transfers to/from other reserves = links to s03 schedule
− Buyback amount charged to RE = link to s05
Closing RE = sum
Check: closing RE − (opening + PAT − dividends ± transfers) = 0

Illustration

FY28E, consolidated: opening RE ₹1,920 crore; consolidated PAT ₹480 crore, NCI share ₹36 crore; FY27 final dividend ₹4.00 × 30 crore shares = ₹120 crore (approved August 2027); FY28 interim ₹2.00 × 30 crore = ₹60 crore; transfer to general reserve ₹50 crore; FVOCI equity investment sold, cumulative gain of ₹12 crore transferred from FVOCI reserve.

\[ RE_{\text{FY28}} = 1{,}920 + (480 - 36) - 120 - 60 - 50 + 12 = 2{,}146 \]

Closing RE ₹2,146 crore. Total equity rises by ₹444 − ₹180 = ₹264 crore from these items (the general-reserve and FVOCI transfers net to zero within equity).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative retained earnings: a debit balance is presented as a negative within other equity (not as an asset). It affects dividend capacity (Sec 123 requires set-off of prior losses) and some loan covenants.
  • Ind AS transition adjustments: large opening adjustments sit in retained earnings; distributable-profit questions on them have been addressed in ICAI/ITFG guidance — check before treating them as distributable.
  • Prior-period errors (Ind AS 8): restate opening retained earnings of the earliest period presented; Schedule III (2021) requires showing restated balances in the SOCE.
  • Change in ownership without loss of control (Ind AS 110.23): the difference between consideration and NCI adjustment is recognised directly in equity attributable to owners — often in retained earnings or a separate reserve — not in P&L.
  • Dividends to ESOP trust: dividends on treasury shares held by a consolidated trust eliminate on consolidation.
  • Hybrid perpetual instruments classified as equity: distributions on them are deducted from retained earnings and from profit available to ordinary shareholders for EPS.

Key takeaways

  • Closing RE = opening + PAT attributable to owners − dividends declared ± transfers; nothing else should move it.
  • Only the owners' share of consolidated PAT enters RE; NCI share goes to the NCI corkscrew.
  • Dividends are deducted when declared/approved, so FY t includes the FY t−1 final and the FY t interim.
  • Every transfer has an equal and opposite entry in another reserve — total equity unchanged.
  • Link the first forecast opening to the last actual closing and add a corkscrew check row.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic corkscrew [L1]

Opening RE ₹860 crore; standalone PAT ₹210 crore; FY26 final dividend approved in FY27: ₹3.50 per share on 18 crore shares; FY27 interim dividend ₹1.50 per share on 18 crore shares; transfer to general reserve ₹25 crore. Compute closing RE for FY27.

Q2. Consolidated with transfers [L2]

Consolidated PAT ₹740 crore, of which NCI ₹58 crore and share of profit of associates ₹44 crore. Dividends: prior-year final ₹6 per share on 25 crore shares; interim ₹3 per share. Revaluation surplus realised through excess depreciation ₹7 crore; FVOCI equity loss of ₹15 crore transferred on sale; CRR created ₹4 crore on a buyback out of free reserves (RE). Opening RE ₹3,410 crore. Compute closing RE and list the counter-entries in other components of equity.

💡 Hint: A cumulative loss transferred from the FVOCI reserve reduces retained earnings.

Q3. Plug detection [L2]

In a model, closing RE each year is calculated as total equity less share capital less other reserves (a balancing figure). Explain why this is dangerous, what errors it can hide, and how you would rebuild the schedule.

Q4. NBFC statutory reserve [L2]

An NBFC forecasts PAT of ₹320 crore in FY28. Explain the transfer required under Sec 45-IC of the RBI Act, model it in the RE corkscrew, and comment on its effect on dividend capacity.
