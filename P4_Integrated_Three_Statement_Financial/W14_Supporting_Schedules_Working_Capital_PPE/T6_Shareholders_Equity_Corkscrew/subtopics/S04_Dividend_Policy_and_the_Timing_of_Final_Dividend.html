Subtopic note

Dividend Policy and the Timing of Final Dividend Cash Flows

Scope: Dividend policy: payout ratio or DPS × shares; final dividend paid after AGM in the following year

Why it matters

Dividends are the largest discretionary use of cash for most mature NSE-listed companies — FMCG, IT services, PSUs, oil and gas, utilities. Getting both the amount and the timing right matters: the amount drives cash, retained earnings and payout-based valuation (dividend discount models, yield comparisons), and the timing determines in which financial year the cash leaves and the deduction from equity is recognised. Indian companies typically pay one or more interim dividends during the year and a final dividend after the AGM, which falls in the next financial year. A model that ignores the lag overstates cash outflow in the first forecast year by the current year's final dividend and misses the prior year's.

Core notes

Two ways to set the amount

Payout ratio approach.

\[ \text{Dividend}_t = \text{Payout ratio} \times \text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t \]

Simple and policy-consistent; used when the company states a payout policy (e.g., "at least 30% of consolidated PAT" in a dividend distribution policy). DPS is derived as dividend ÷ shares.

DPS approach.

\[ \text{Dividend}_t = \text{DPS}_t \times \text{Shares eligible on record date} \]

Used when the company manages a stable or progressive DPS (e.g., grow DPS 10% a year), or when the analyst forecasts DPS directly. The implied payout ratio is then a check.

Many models combine them: DPS = MAX(prior DPS, payout ratio × EPS) — a progressive policy that never cuts the dividend — with a cap from cash and distributable profits.

Interim and final dividends

Element Declared by Typical timing (31 March year-end) Recognised in equity Cash paid
Interim dividend(s) Board (Sec 123(3)) With Q1–Q3 results (Jul–Feb) When declared, FY t Within 30 days of declaration (Sec 127), usually FY t
Final dividend Recommended by board with annual results (Apr–May); declared by shareholders at AGM AGM by 30 September (Sec 96: within six months of year-end) When approved at AGM, FY t+1 Within 30 days of AGM, FY t+1
Special dividend Board (as interim) or AGM Ad hoc When declared Within 30 days

Under Ind AS 10.12–13, a final dividend recommended after the reporting date is not a liability at year-end; it is disclosed in the notes (Ind AS 1.137), with the amount per share. So the FY27 final dividend appears in FY28's SOCE and FY28's CFS.

Mechanics of the timing lag

\[ \text{Cash dividends}_t = \text{Final DPS}_{t-1} \times N_{t}^{\text{record (final)}} + \text{Interim DPS}_t \times N_{t}^{\text{record (interim)}} \]

where \(N\) is the number of eligible shares on the respective record dates (c02). In an annual model, use opening shares for the final dividend and average or closing shares for the interim, depending on allotment timing.

A residual timing item: an interim dividend declared in late March and paid in April is recognised in FY t equity but paid in FY t+1, creating a dividends payable liability at year-end. Model it only if the company has a pattern of this (some PSUs and MNC subsidiaries declare in March).

Model rows

Row Logic
Payout ratio (input) e.g., 40%
Total dividend for the year (relating to FY t) Payout × owners' PAT, or DPS × shares
Interim share of total (input) e.g., 40%
Interim dividend FY t Total × interim share
Final dividend relating to FY t Total − interim
Dividends recognised in FY t equity Interim FY t + Final FY t−1
Cash dividends paid FY t = recognised ± Δ dividends payable
DPS relating to FY t Total ÷ shares
Cap check MIN against cash available and distributable profits

Taxation of dividends

Since 1 April 2020 (Finance Act 2020), dividend distribution tax under the old Sec 115-O is abolished; dividends are taxed in shareholders' hands. Companies deduct TDS under Sec 194 for resident shareholders (10%, with a threshold raised from ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 per shareholder per year by Finance Act 2025) and under Sec 195 for non-residents at treaty rates; TDS is a withholding, not a company cost. A domestic company receiving dividends from another company can claim a deduction under Sec 80M to the extent it distributes dividends one month before the return due date, avoiding cascading — relevant for holding companies. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026) section numbers change; verify the mapping.

Governance and disclosure

  • SEBI LODR Reg 43A: the top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation must have a dividend distribution policy disclosed on the website and in the annual report — the source for payout assumptions.
  • Record date: intimated to stock exchanges under LODR Reg 42.
  • Unpaid dividends: transferred to an Unpaid Dividend Account within 7 days after the 30-day payment window (Sec 124); after seven years, to IEPF.

Illustration

FY27E owners' PAT ₹800 crore; payout 50%; interim share 40%; 40 crore shares throughout; FY26 final DPS ₹6.00 (approved August 2026).

  • Total dividend relating to FY27 = ₹400 crore (DPS ₹10.00): interim ₹160 crore (₹4.00), final ₹240 crore (₹6.00).
  • FY27 dividends recognised and paid = FY26 final ₹240 crore (₹6.00 × 40) + FY27 interim ₹160 crore = ₹400 crore.
  • FY27 final ₹240 crore is disclosed at 31 March 2027 and paid in FY28.

If PAT jumps to ₹1,000 crore in FY28, total FY28 dividend relating = ₹500 crore, but FY28 cash = FY27 final ₹240 crore + FY28 interim ₹200 crore = ₹440 crore. The lag smooths cash outflows relative to the year's earnings.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Loss years: Sec 123 and the 2014 Dividend Rules restrict declaration out of reserves (rate not exceeding the average of the three preceding years, withdrawal not exceeding 10% of paid-up capital and free reserves, prior losses set off first). Model a dividend floor only if reserves permit.
  • Bonus or split between periods: restate DPS history; apply the final DPS to the post-bonus share count if the record date is after the bonus.
  • Covenants: term-loan agreements often restrict dividends while DSCR or leverage tests are breached; integrate with the debt waterfall.
  • PSUs: DIPAM guidelines set minimum payout expectations for CPSEs (e.g., a minimum percentage of PAT or net worth); use them for government-owned companies but check the latest guidelines.
  • Dividends on partly paid shares: proportionate to amount paid up (Sec 51).
  • Subsidiary dividends in consolidated models: dividends from subsidiaries to the parent eliminate; only dividends to NCI leave the group (s06).

Key takeaways

  • Set dividends by payout ratio or DPS × shares; check one against the other and cap by cash and distributable profits.
  • Final dividends are recognised and paid in the following year, after AGM approval; interim dividends in the current year.
  • FY t cash dividends = prior-year final + current-year interim (± Δ dividends payable).
  • Disclose, not provide, the recommended final dividend at year-end (Ind AS 10).
  • Dividends are taxed in shareholders' hands with TDS by the company; no DDT since FY21.
  • Use the LODR dividend distribution policy as the starting point for assumptions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Timing schedule [L1]

A company with 25 crore shares pays FY26 final DPS ₹8 (approved July 2026), FY27 interim ₹3 (paid November 2026) and recommends FY27 final DPS ₹9 in May 2027. State the amounts recognised in equity and paid in cash in FY27 and FY28 (assuming FY28 interim of ₹3.50), and the disclosure at 31 March 2027.

Q2. Progressive DPS with payout floor [L2]

Owners' PAT forecast: FY27 ₹520 crore, FY28 ₹460 crore, FY29 ₹610 crore; shares 32 crore; FY26 DPS ₹6.50. Policy: DPS never falls and must be at least 35% of EPS. Interim is 30% of the year's DPS. Compute DPS relating to each year, cash dividends each year, and implied payout ratios.

💡 Hint: Compute EPS first, then apply MAX(prior DPS, 35% × EPS).

Q3. Dividends payable at year-end [L2]

A PSU declares a second interim dividend of ₹2.40 per share on 25 March 2027 on 120 crore shares, paid on 18 April 2027. Show the effect on FY27 equity, balance sheet and cash flow, and on FY28 cash flow. Write the Excel logic you would use to model dividends payable.

Q4. Excel build [L3]

Design an Excel block (rows, formulas with named ranges) that computes dividends relating to each year from a payout ratio, splits interim/final, lags the final to the next year, caps total cash dividends at the lower of cash available after debt service and distributable profits, and flags whenever the cap binds.
