Subtopic note

Equity Share Capital, Securities Premium and ESOP Allotments

Scope: Equity share capital (face value × shares) and securities premium; ESOP allotments

Why it matters

Share capital and securities premium change only on specific events — fresh issues (IPO, QIP, rights, preferential allotment), ESOP exercises, bonus issues, splits and buybacks — but each event changes the share count, which drives EPS and value per share. In Indian listed companies, ESOP allotments are the most frequent event: IT services, new-age tech and financial-services companies allot shares under ESOP schemes every quarter. A model that holds share capital flat while the share count grows, or that books the full ESOP exercise money as share capital, will fail a reconciliation with the reported SOCE and the shareholding pattern filed under SEBI LODR.

Core notes

Equity share capital

Equity share capital in the balance sheet is the nominal (face) value of shares issued and paid up:

\[ \text{Share capital} = \text{Face value per share} \times \text{Number of shares issued and paid up} \]

Common face values for NSE-listed companies are ₹1, ₹2, ₹5 and ₹10. Schedule III Division II requires disclosure of authorised, issued, subscribed and fully paid capital, a reconciliation of shares at the beginning and end of the period, rights attached, shares held by the holding company, shareholders holding more than 5%, shares reserved for ESOPs, shares allotted as bonus or for consideration other than cash in the last five years, and (since the 2021 amendment) promoter shareholding with changes during the year. The Statement of Changes in Equity presents equity share capital separately from other equity, including changes due to prior-period errors.

Securities premium

When shares are issued above face value, the excess is credited to securities premium (part of "Other equity — Reserves and surplus"). Its uses are restricted by Sec 52: issuing fully paid bonus shares; writing off preliminary expenses; writing off expenses, commission or discount on issue of shares or debentures; providing for premium payable on redemption of preference shares or debentures; and purchasing its own shares under Sec 68. It cannot be distributed as dividend.

Share issue costs (merchant banker fees, legal fees, stamp duty, exchange listing fees) that are incremental and directly attributable to an equity issue are deducted from equity under Ind AS 32.37, typically against securities premium, net of any tax benefit. In an IPO with an offer-for-sale component, costs attributable to the selling shareholders are recovered from them and not charged to the company's equity.

ESOP allotments — accounting mechanics

Under Ind AS 102 for equity-settled options:

  1. Over vesting: Dr Employee benefits expense (share-based payment), Cr Share-based payment reserve (often titled "Share options outstanding account"), at grant-date fair value × options expected to vest, spread over the vesting period and trued up for forfeitures (Ind AS 102.19–20).
  2. On exercise: cash received = options exercised × exercise price.

  1. On lapse after vesting: the SBP reserve relating to lapsed vested options is transferred within equity, commonly to general reserve or retained earnings (Ind AS 102.23 prohibits reversal through P&L).

ESOP trusts and treasury shares

Many Indian companies administer ESOPs through an ESOP trust that buys shares from the market (permitted under the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021) or subscribes to fresh shares. Where the trust is controlled and consolidated, the shares it holds are treasury shares: their cost is deducted from equity (Ind AS 32.33) and they are excluded from the weighted-average share count for EPS until transferred to employees. In the model, keep a separate "treasury shares" line within other equity and a separate share-count row.

Bonus issues and splits

A bonus issue capitalises reserves (securities premium, free reserves or CRR) into share capital: total equity is unchanged, the share count rises, and per-share data for all periods presented is restated (Ind AS 33.64). A split changes face value and share count without changing share capital. In the model, apply the bonus/split factor to historical per-share data and to DPS assumptions.

Illustration

Opening: 40 crore shares of ₹2 (share capital ₹80 crore); securities premium ₹510 crore; SBP reserve ₹22 crore. FY28E: 0.50 crore options exercised at ₹250; SBP reserve relating to them ₹14 crore.

  • Cash received = 0.50 × 250 = ₹125 crore.
  • Share capital increase = 0.50 × 2 = ₹1 crore → closing ₹81 crore (40.50 crore shares).
  • Securities premium increase = 125 + 14 − 1 = ₹138 crore → closing ₹648 crore.
  • SBP reserve: opening 22 + FY28 expense (say ₹10 crore) − 14 = ₹18 crore.
  • Total equity increases by cash ₹125 crore plus the ₹10 crore expense credited to reserve (with an equal reduction in retained earnings via PAT).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Partly paid shares: share capital includes only called-up and paid-up amounts; calls in arrears are deducted. Rare in listed companies after a rights issue with staged calls (e.g., partly paid rights entitlements).
  • Share application money pending allotment: presented within other equity if refund is not required; otherwise a liability. Common at year-end in QIPs closing near 31 March.
  • Equity component of compound instruments: CCDs/CCPS that convert to a fixed number of shares are equity in part or whole (Ind AS 32); preference shares redeemable at a fixed amount are financial liabilities. Classification drives whether "capital" is in equity or debt in the model.
  • Cash-settled SARs: liability, not equity; remeasured each period.
  • Tax on ESOPs: employees pay perquisite tax on exercise; the company's tax deduction, if any, differs in timing from the book expense — a deferred tax point (t5).
  • Forecasting ESOP dilution: model allotments as a % of opening shares (e.g., 0.3–1% a year) based on the outstanding option pool, vesting schedule and in-the-money status.

Key takeaways

  • Share capital = face value × paid-up shares; build the share count first and derive capital from it.
  • Premium on issue goes to securities premium, whose uses are limited by Sec 52; issue costs are deducted from equity.
  • ESOP exercise: cash plus the related SBP reserve is credited to share capital (FV) and securities premium (balance).
  • Treasury shares held by a consolidated ESOP trust are deducted from equity and excluded from EPS shares.
  • Bonus issues and splits change the share count but not total equity; restate per-share history.
  • Schedule III requires a share reconciliation and promoter shareholding disclosure.

Practice Questions

Q1. ESOP exercise journal [L1]

A company with ₹5 face-value shares receives exercise money for 12 lakh options at ₹340 per share. The grant-date fair value recognised in the SBP reserve for these options is ₹96 per option. Pass the journal entry and state the closing effect on share capital and securities premium.

Q2. Share capital corkscrew with multiple events [L2]

Opening FY27: 120 crore shares of ₹1; securities premium ₹1,850 crore. FY27 events: QIP of 6 crore shares at ₹410 on 15 September 2026 with issue costs of ₹24 crore (tax benefit at 25.168%); ESOP allotments of 0.8 crore shares at ₹150 with ₹38 crore SBP reserve transferred; 1:1 bonus issue on 1 March 2027 capitalised from securities premium. Build the share-count and share-capital corkscrews and the securities premium corkscrew for FY27.

💡 Hint: Process events in date order; the bonus applies to shares outstanding on the record date.

Q3. Treasury shares in EPS [L2]

An ESOP trust consolidated by the company holds 1.2 crore shares bought from the market at an average cost of ₹780. During FY28 it transfers 0.4 crore shares to employees on exercise at ₹500. Explain the presentation in equity, the treatment of the difference between cost and exercise price, and the effect on the EPS share count.

Q4. Classification judgement [L3]

A start-up preparing for an IPO has issued CCPS convertible into a variable number of equity shares based on the IPO price, and OCRPS redeemable at a 12% premium if not converted. Explain how each would be classified under Ind AS 32, how they would appear in the forecast equity schedule or debt schedule, and what happens on conversion at IPO.
