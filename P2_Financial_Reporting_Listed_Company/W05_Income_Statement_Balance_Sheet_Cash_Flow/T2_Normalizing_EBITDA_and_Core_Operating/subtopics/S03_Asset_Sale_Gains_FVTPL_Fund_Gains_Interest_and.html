Subtopic note

Asset Sale Gains, FVTPL Fund Gains, Interest and Dividends in Other Income

Scope: Gains on sale of PPE/investments, fair value (FVTPL) gains on mutual funds, interest and dividend income within other income

Why it matters

Other income in Indian listed companies is large: cash-rich consumer, IT, pharma and auto companies routinely report other income of 5–15% of PBT, and for some it exceeds 25%. Most of it is treasury income — interest on bank deposits, fair value gains on debt and liquid mutual funds, dividends — plus occasional gains on sale of assets. None of it is generated by the operating business. Leaving it in EBITDA inflates operating margins and multiples; removing it without also removing the related cash from valuation understates value. Correctly identifying, removing and then re-using these items is central to core EBITDA, core PAT and the EV bridge.

Core notes

What sits in other income

Schedule III Div II requires other income to be disclosed separately, with a note analysing interest income, dividend income and other non-operating income (net of expenses directly attributable). A typical note:

Other income (₹ crore) FY2025
Interest income on bank deposits (amortised cost) 95
Interest on income-tax refunds 12
Interest on loans to subsidiaries / ICDs 18
Dividend income from equity investments 8
Net gain on financial assets measured at FVTPL (mutual funds) 140
Net gain on sale of investments 22
Profit on sale of PPE (net) 35
Rental income 14
Liabilities no longer required written back 26
Miscellaneous income 10
Total 380

Item-by-item treatment

Interest on deposits and FVTPL gains on mutual funds. Treasury returns on surplus cash. Under Ind AS 109, investments in mutual fund units fail the SPPI test (units are not solely payments of principal and interest) and are measured at FVTPL, so both realised and unrealised gains flow through other income each period. Exclude from core EBITDA. Their cash and investment balances are added in the EV-to-equity bridge (net debt deduction) — so they are counted once, in value, not in EBITDA.

Dividends. Ind AS 109.5.7.1A — dividends recognised in P&L when the right to receive is established. Dividends from strategic equity stakes (often designated at FVOCI, with fair value changes in OCI but dividends still in P&L) are non-operating. Exclude; value the stake separately.

Interest on loans to subsidiaries/ICDs. Non-operating in standalone; eliminated on consolidation for subsidiaries. Exclude.

Interest on income-tax refunds. Non-operating and non-recurring; exclude.

Gains on sale of PPE. Ind AS 16.68 — gains on derecognition are not classified as revenue. Remove gains (and add back losses) from core EBITDA; they relate to past investment, not current operations. Exception: businesses whose model is to sell assets regularly (car rental fleets selling used vehicles) — treat as operating and recurring.

Gains on sale of investments. Treasury or strategic; exclude.

Rental income. Operating if it is sub-letting of excess store/office space linked to operations (some include it as operating income); non-operating if it is investment property income (Ind AS 40). Decide per policy.

Liabilities written back. Often recurring at a low level (old creditors, stale advances), but large write-backs are one-offs or reversals of earlier over-provisioning. If the original provision was charged through operating expenses, the write-back is arguably operating but non-recurring — remove if material.

Miscellaneous income. Read the details; scrap sales are sometimes here instead of in other operating revenue.

Mechanics

\[ \text{Core EBITDA} = \text{Mechanical EBITDA} - \text{Other income} + \text{Operating items reclassified from other income} \]

From the note above, a policy that treats sub-letting rent as operating and everything else as non-operating gives other income excluded = 380 − 14 = ₹366 crore.

Consistency with valuation — the double-count trap

Choice EBITDA EV bridge Result
Exclude treasury income from EBITDA Lower Add cash + liquid investments (deduct from net debt) Correct
Include treasury income in EBITDA Higher Do not add cash Acceptable only if multiples are applied consistently, rarely done
Include in EBITDA and also add cash Higher Add cash Double count — overstates value
Exclude from EBITDA and ignore cash Lower Ignore cash Understates value

In PAT-based valuation (P/E), treasury income is in PAT; either keep it and value the company including cash, or use core PAT ex-treasury and add net cash per share separately — never mix.

Yield check

A useful test: other income treasury yield = (interest + FVTPL gains + dividends on treasury) ÷ average (cash + bank balances + liquid investments). A yield far above prevailing money-market rates (say 6.5–7.5% for FY2025) suggests one-off gains (duration gains when rates fall) or non-treasury items mixed in; far below suggests idle or restricted cash.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mark-to-market volatility: FVTPL gains on debt funds rise when yields fall (duration gains) and can be negative when yields rise. A year with large gains is not a recurring run-rate.
  • Debt fund taxation: under s.50AA, gains on units of "specified mutual funds" acquired on or after 1 April 2023 are deemed short-term capital gains regardless of holding period (the definition was revised from 1 April 2025 to funds investing more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments), so a s.115BAA company pays 25.168% on them; deferred tax on unrealised FVTPL gains is recognised under Ind AS 12. Use the applicable rate when tax-effecting, and check the renumbered provision under the Income-tax Act 2025 for periods from 1 April 2026.
  • Operating treasury: for NBFCs and insurers, interest and investment income is operating revenue — different framework entirely (Schedule III Div III).
  • Customer advance float: jewellers and subscription businesses earn interest on customer money; some analysts treat this as operating because the float arises from operations. If so, treat the related cash as operating (not surplus) in Topic 3 — keep it consistent.
  • Foreign exchange gains in other income: separate operating forex from financing forex (s04).
  • Government grants in other income: assess under s05 (other operating revenue judgement).

Key takeaways

  • Other income in India is mostly treasury: interest, FVTPL gains on mutual funds (Ind AS 109), dividends, gains on sale of investments — exclude from core EBITDA.
  • Gains on sale of PPE are not revenue (Ind AS 16.68) and are non-recurring unless asset sales are the business model.
  • Exclusion from EBITDA must be matched by adding the related cash and investments in the EV bridge — never both include and add.
  • Run a treasury-yield check to spot one-off gains or misclassified items.
  • Decide sub-letting rent, write-backs and scrap per a written policy and apply consistently.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the other income note [L1]

Other income (₹ crore): interest on FDs 64; interest on delayed payments from customers 9; FVTPL gain on liquid funds 48; dividend from a 9% stake in a listed supplier 6; profit on sale of an old factory 85; export incentive (RoDTEP) 22; liabilities written back 7; scrap sales 15. Classify each as operating or non-operating under a stated policy and compute the amount excluded from core EBITDA.

Q2. Treasury yield test [L2]

A company's cash and cash equivalents, other bank balances and current investments in liquid/debt funds averaged ₹2,400 crore in FY2025. Interest income was ₹92 crore and FVTPL gains ₹128 crore. Compute the treasury yield, compare it with a money-market benchmark of 7.0%, and list three explanations for the difference that you would verify.

💡 Hint: Consider duration gains on debt funds and non-treasury items in the note.

Q3. EV double count [L2]

A company has core EBITDA (excluding other income) ₹900 crore, treasury income ₹150 crore, borrowings ₹200 crore and cash plus liquid funds ₹2,500 crore. Peers trade at 18x core EBITDA. Compute equity value under (a) correct treatment, (b) double-count treatment, (c) cash ignored with treasury excluded, and explain which a careless analyst is most likely to produce.

Q4. Deferred tax on FVTPL gains [L2]

At 31 March 2025, a company holds debt fund units with cost ₹600 crore and fair value ₹660 crore; ₹35 crore of the ₹60 crore unrealised gain arose in FY2025. It pays tax under s.115BAA. Pass the journal entries for the FY2025 fair value gain and deferred tax, and compute the post-tax amount to remove when moving from reported PAT to core PAT.

Q5. Customer float judgement [L3]

A jeweller holds ₹1,800 crore in bank deposits funded largely by customer advances under jewellery purchase schemes, earning ₹115 crore of interest. Argue both sides of treating this interest as operating income, state your recommended policy, and explain the consistent treatment in net debt and invested capital.
