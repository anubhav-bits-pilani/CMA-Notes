Subtopic note

Other Operating Revenue: Scrap, RoDTEP and PLI as Recurring or Not

Scope: Other operating revenue judgement: scrap, export incentives (RoDTEP), PLI incentives (Ind AS 20 grants) as recurring or not

Why it matters

Schedule III Div II splits revenue from operations into sale of products, sale of services and other operating revenue. That last bucket holds items that are operating in nature but are not sales to customers: scrap sales, export incentives, production-linked incentives (PLI), duty drawback, government grants related to income and miscellaneous operating receipts. Because these items are inside revenue from operations, they flow straight into EBITDA and margins. In sectors such as electronics manufacturing, auto components, pharma APIs, textiles, solar modules and food processing, PLI and export incentives can be 2–8% of revenue — and they are time-bound government schemes. Whether they are "recurring" determines sustainable EBITDA, terminal-year margins in DCF, and the multiple an investor should pay.

Core notes

Accounting basis

Ind AS 20 Accounting for Government Grants and Disclosure of Government Assistance:

  • Grants are recognised only when there is reasonable assurance that the entity will comply with the conditions and the grant will be received (Ind AS 20.7).
  • Grants are recognised in P&L on a systematic basis over the periods in which the entity recognises the related costs (Ind AS 20.12).
  • Grants related to income may be presented as income (separately or under "other income") or deducted from the related expense (Ind AS 20.29). Indian companies commonly present them as other operating revenue.
  • Grants related to assets (capital subsidies): the original Ind AS 20 allowed only presentation as deferred income amortised over the asset's useful life; a subsequent MCA amendment restored the IAS 20 option of deducting the grant from the asset's carrying amount (Ind AS 20.24) — read the company's policy, because the choice moves the benefit between other operating revenue/other income and depreciation.

Export incentives such as RoDTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products, from 1 January 2021) and RoSCTL are refunds of embedded taxes, credited as transferable e-scrips on export. They are typically recognised when the right to receive is established and there is reasonable certainty of realisation, at the scrip value (net of expected discount on sale of scrips), and presented as other operating revenue. Duty drawback is similar.

PLI schemes (launched from 2020 for 14 sectors: mobile phones and electronics, pharma, auto and auto components, ACC batteries, textiles, food processing, specialty steel, white goods, solar PV modules, telecom, drones, IT hardware, medical devices) pay incentives of a percentage of incremental sales over a base year, subject to minimum investment and sales thresholds, for a defined tenure (typically 4–6 years). They are grants related to income under Ind AS 20, recognised when thresholds for the year are met and there is reasonable assurance of receipt (approval of the claim is often delayed by many months — receivables build up).

Scrap sales are revenue under Ind AS 115 (or part of other operating revenue), generated by the production process.

Recurring or not — decision criteria

Item Default view Factors that change it
Scrap sales Recurring operating (linked to production volumes) Scrap from a one-off plant demolition is non-recurring; metal-price windfalls inflate it
RoDTEP, duty drawback Recurring while the scheme continues; economically a reduction of export cost Rate changes, scheme budget caps, WTO challenges; scheme continuation is policy-dependent
PLI incentives Recurring only for the scheme tenure; non-recurring for terminal value Thresholds missed in a year, retrospective claims for prior years booked in one year (non-recurring catch-up)
State investment subsidies (SGST reimbursement, power subsidy) Recurring for the tenure; capped by investment Cap reached, scheme withdrawal
Insurance claims, compensation Non-recurring Recurring only if insurance is the business
Liquidated damages received from suppliers Non-recurring —

The analyst framework

  1. Isolate each incentive from the revenue note and the Ind AS 20 grants disclosure (nature, extent, unfulfilled conditions and contingencies — Ind AS 20.39).
  2. Split amounts into current-year accrual vs prior-year catch-up (often visible in quarterly results commentary).
  3. Present two margins: EBITDA including incentives (the near-term economics) and EBITDA excluding incentives (the stand-alone competitiveness).
  4. Forecast explicitly: model PLI by scheme year with its rate and sunset; do not bake it into a margin percentage that extends into the terminal year.
  5. Check receivables: PLI receivables (other financial assets) growing faster than recognised income signal approval risk — a quality-of-earnings issue.

Illustration (₹ crore)

An electronics manufacturing services company:

FY2024 FY2025
Sale of products 9,200 13,800
PLI incentive — current year 240 380
PLI incentive — FY2023 claim approved late — 150
Scrap sales 30 42
Revenue from operations 9,470 14,372
Reported EBITDA 520 905

Reported margin FY2025 = 905 ÷ 14,372 = 6.3% (FY2024: 5.5%). Excluding the prior-year catch-up: 755 ÷ 14,222 = 5.3%. Excluding all PLI: (905 − 530) ÷ (14,372 − 530) = 375 ÷ 13,842 = 2.7% (FY2024: 280 ÷ 9,230 = 3.0%). The business without the incentive has a 2.7–3.0% margin; the reported margin expansion is entirely PLI-driven. If the scheme ends in FY2028, terminal EBITDA margins should be anchored nearer 3%, not 6%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Grants netted against expense: a peer deducting PLI from COGS shows lower revenue and the same EBITDA; margins differ. Reclassify to a common basis.
  • Grants recognised before approval: aggressive recognition without reasonable assurance — check auditor emphasis, receivable ageing and cash receipts.
  • Clawback conditions: some schemes require repayment if investment or employment conditions are breached later; Ind AS 20.32 requires repayment to be treated as a change in estimate. Disclose as a contingent risk.
  • Scheme-dependent business models: a plant set up mainly to earn PLI may not be viable after tenure — value the post-scheme business separately.
  • Tax treatment: PLI incentives are revenue receipts and taxable; incentives related to capital assets may reduce actual cost under Explanation 10 to s.43(1) — tax-effect accordingly.
  • GST refunds under inverted duty structures are not income; they reduce input tax credit receivables.

Key takeaways

  • Other operating revenue (scrap, RoDTEP, PLI, other grants) sits inside revenue from operations and directly lifts EBITDA.
  • Ind AS 20 governs PLI and grants: recognise on reasonable assurance, systematically with related costs; presentation as income or deduction from expense.
  • Scrap is generally recurring; export incentives recur while schemes last; PLI is recurring only for the scheme tenure and never in terminal value.
  • Separate prior-year catch-ups and present margins with and without incentives.
  • Monitor incentive receivables for approval risk and reclassify peers to a common presentation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Margins with and without incentives [L1]

An auto-component company reports sale of products ₹4,600 crore, RoDTEP ₹55 crore, PLI ₹120 crore (of which ₹40 crore relates to the prior year), scrap ₹38 crore and EBITDA ₹690 crore. Compute reported EBITDA margin, margin excluding the prior-year PLI catch-up, and margin excluding all government incentives.

Q2. Terminal-year treatment [L2]

A solar module maker's FY2026 EBITDA of ₹1,150 crore includes PLI of ₹420 crore under a scheme that ends in FY2030. Explain how you would treat the PLI in a 10-year DCF (explicit forecast and terminal year), and compute the terminal-year EBITDA margin if FY2031 revenue is forecast at ₹14,000 crore and the ex-incentive margin is 6.5%.

💡 Hint: Model the incentive explicitly by year and let it fall away before the terminal period.

Q3. Reasonable assurance and journal entries [L2]

In FY2025 a pharma company meets the PLI sales threshold for the year, computing an incentive of ₹85 crore; the claim is filed in June 2025 and approved in February 2026, with cash received in March 2026. Explain when the income should be recognised under Ind AS 20, pass the journal entries for FY2025 and FY2026, and describe what an analyst should look for if the claim had been rejected.

Q4. Peer reclassification [L2]

Company A presents PLI of ₹200 crore as other operating revenue (revenue ₹5,200 crore, EBITDA ₹600 crore). Company B deducts PLI of ₹180 crore from cost of materials consumed (revenue ₹4,800 crore, EBITDA ₹560 crore). Restate both on a consistent basis and compare EBITDA margins with and without incentives.

Q5. Quality-of-earnings red flag [L3]

A textile exporter's PLI and RoDTEP receivables rose from ₹60 crore to ₹310 crore over two years while incentive income recognised rose from ₹70 crore to ₹160 crore per year. Write a short quality-of-earnings note explaining the risk, the disclosures you would request, and how you would adjust core EBITDA and working capital in the model.
