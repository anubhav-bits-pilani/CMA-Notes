Subtopic note

Discontinued Operations, Associates/JVs and ESOP Cost as Adjustments

Scope: Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105), share of associates/JVs, ESOP cost (Ind AS 102) as adjustments

Why it matters

Three items sit at the boundary of "operating earnings" and are treated inconsistently across companies and brokers. Discontinued operations are a business the company is exiting; including or excluding them changes both the base for forecasting and the perimeter for valuation. Share of profit of associates and JVs is real profit attributable to shareholders but is not part of the consolidated operating business — Trent's JVs with Tesco (Star) and Inditex (Zara) are classic examples. ESOP cost is a non-cash charge that many new-age and IT companies add back in "adjusted EBITDA" — but it represents real dilution of shareholders. Each needs a consistent policy, tied to how the related value is treated in the EV bridge.

Core notes

1. Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105)

A discontinued operation is a component of an entity that has been disposed of or is classified as held for sale, and represents a separate major line of business or geographical area, is part of a single co-ordinated plan to dispose of one, or is a subsidiary acquired exclusively for resale (Ind AS 105.32).

Presentation (Ind AS 105.33–34):

  • A single amount in the P&L: post-tax profit or loss of the discontinued operation plus post-tax gain/loss on remeasurement to fair value less costs to sell or on disposal. Schedule III Div II shows "Profit/(loss) from discontinued operations", "Tax expense of discontinued operations" and the net amount below profit from continuing operations.
  • Analysis of revenue, expenses, PBT, tax and the gain/loss in the notes.
  • Comparatives re-presented for the prior period so that the discontinued operation is excluded from continuing lines.
  • Net cash flows attributable to operating, investing and financing activities disclosed.

Analyst treatment:

  • Core EBITDA and core PAT exclude discontinued operations — they are already outside the continuing lines. Use "profit from continuing operations" as the PAT starting point.
  • For valuation, the discontinued business is valued at expected disposal proceeds (cash to be received, net of tax) and added in the EV bridge — or deducted if a loss-making exit requires cash.
  • For trend analysis, re-present older years (beyond the one comparative) using the segment note (s04 of Topic 1).

2. Share of profit of associates and joint ventures (Ind AS 28)

Associates (significant influence, generally 20–50%) and joint ventures (Ind AS 111 joint control with rights to net assets) are equity-accounted in consolidated statements: the investor's share of post-tax profit appears as a single line, "Share of profit/(loss) of associates and joint ventures", presented below "profit before exceptional items and tax" or below PBT depending on the company's format (Schedule III Div II for CFS places it after exceptional items and before tax in its general instructions, though practice varies).

Analyst treatment:

  • Exclude from EBITDA and EBIT: the associate's revenue, costs, assets and debt are not in the consolidated lines, so its profit should not be in the operating numerator.
  • Include in PAT (it is attributable to shareholders), but keep track of it separately.
  • In valuation, add the value of the stake (market value if listed; multiple of share of earnings or book value otherwise) in the EV bridge — consistent with excluding its profit from EBITDA.
  • Note that share of profit is post-tax: do not tax-effect it again in the core PAT bridge.
  • If an associate is integral to operations (e.g., a JV that manufactures all of the parent's product), some analysts proportionately consolidate it for operating analysis; disclose the choice.

3. ESOP cost (Ind AS 102)

Equity-settled share-based payments are measured at grant-date fair value and expensed over the vesting period, with credit to "share options outstanding account" (other equity) (Ind AS 102.10–11, 19–20). The charge sits in employee benefits expense. It is non-cash, but it is a cost: the company is paying employees with equity instead of cash, and existing shareholders are diluted.

Positions:

View Argument When used
Keep ESOP cost in core EBITDA (recommended) Real economic compensation; cash-settled equivalent would be in EBITDA; peers paying cash bonuses are comparable Valuation, peer comparison, FP&A
Add back ESOP cost Non-cash; lumpy grants distort trends; matches some company "adjusted EBITDA" Covenant calculations, CFO bridge (non-cash add-back in Ind AS 7 indirect method)

If ESOP cost is added back, the analyst must capture dilution elsewhere — fully diluted share count including all outstanding options using the treasury stock method in valuation, and future grants as an ongoing dilution assumption. Adding back the cost and ignoring future dilution double-benefits the equity value.

Cash-settled share-based payments (SARs settled in cash) are liabilities remeasured each period; changes in fair value pass through P&L — share-price volatility enters EBITDA. Normalize the fair value swing, keep the underlying service cost.

Illustration (₹ crore, consolidated)

FY2025
Revenue from operations 15,000
EBITDA (incl. ESOP cost ₹120 crore) 2,250
Share of profit of JVs/associates 180
Profit from continuing operations 1,180
Profit from discontinued operations (net of tax) (95)
Profit for the year 1,085

Core EBITDA (keeping ESOP, excluding associates) = ₹2,250 crore; margin 15.0%. Company's "adjusted EBITDA" adding back ESOP and including share of JVs = 2,250 + 120 + 180 = ₹2,550 crore (17.0%). Core PAT starting point = ₹1,180 crore continuing (before other normalization), with share of JVs ₹180 crore shown as a separate line so that a P/E on core operating earnings can exclude it and the JV be valued separately.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Held-for-sale not qualifying as discontinued: a single plant held for sale is not a discontinued operation; it stays in continuing lines. Impairment on classification (Ind AS 105.20) is a non-recurring item.
  • Partial exits: disposal of a subsidiary with loss of control but retained interest (now an associate) produces a remeasurement gain (Ind AS 110.25) — non-recurring and non-operating.
  • Associates losses capped: equity method stops at zero carrying amount (Ind AS 28.38) unless obligations exist; a sudden reappearance of share of profit may reflect recovery of unrecognised losses.
  • ESOP trusts: shares held by an employee welfare trust are treated as treasury shares (deducted from equity) — check the weighted share count.
  • Lumpy ESOP grants at IPO: one-time grants before listing can create very large charges for 2–3 years; consider showing both bases and a normalized annual grant cost.
  • Standalone statements: dividends from associates appear as other income — non-operating.

Key takeaways

  • Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105) are already a single post-tax line; start core PAT from profit from continuing operations and value the exit separately.
  • Share of associates/JVs is post-tax and below EBIT: exclude from EBITDA and EBIT, include separately in PAT, and add the stake's value in the EV bridge.
  • ESOP cost (Ind AS 102) is a real cost; keep it in core EBITDA, or if added back, capture dilution in the share count and future grants.
  • Apply the same treatment across peers and years; reconcile to company "adjusted EBITDA" figures.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build core metrics [L1]

Consolidated data (₹ crore): EBITDA 3,100 including ESOP cost 90 and including a ₹60 crore gain on remeasurement of a retained stake on loss of control (in other income, which the company includes in EBITDA); share of profit of associates 240; profit from discontinued operations ₹(130) crore. Compute core EBITDA under a policy that keeps ESOP cost, excludes associates and excludes non-recurring gains.

Q2. ESOP add-back and dilution [L2]

A listed internet company reports EBITDA ₹420 crore after ESOP cost of ₹260 crore and adjusted EBITDA ₹680 crore. It has 500 million shares outstanding and 40 million options outstanding with an average exercise price of ₹120; the share price is ₹600. Compute diluted shares using the treasury stock method, and explain how valuation should change if the analyst uses adjusted EBITDA.

💡 Hint: Under the treasury stock method, assumed exercise proceeds buy back shares at the market price.

Q3. Discontinued operation journal and presentation [L2]

On 1 January 2026 the board approves a plan to sell a loss-making division, meeting Ind AS 105 held-for-sale and discontinued-operation criteria. Carrying amount of the disposal group is ₹480 crore; fair value less costs to sell is ₹400 crore. The division's FY2026 post-tax loss from operations is ₹55 crore. Pass the impairment entry, show the P&L presentation, and explain the effect on core EBITDA and core PAT trend tables for FY2025 and FY2024.

Q4. Trent-type JV valuation consistency [L3]

A retailer's consolidated P&L includes share of profit of two JVs of ₹160 crore; its EBITDA is ₹2,600 crore. The JVs are unlisted, with a combined book value of investment of ₹900 crore. A broker values the retailer at 35x EBITDA including the JV share of profit and also adds the JV book value in the EV bridge. Identify the inconsistency, propose two consistent approaches, and state the information you would need to value the JVs separately.

Q5. Interview: is ESOP cost real? [L1]

An interviewer says, "ESOP cost is non-cash, so I always add it back." Give a concise rebuttal with one numerical example and state the circumstance in which adding it back is acceptable.
