Subtopic note

The Exceptional Items Line: Impairment, VRS, Litigation, Labour Codes

Scope: Exceptional items line (Schedule III Div II): impairment, VRS, restructuring, litigation settlements, Labour Codes past-service cost (Nov 2025)

Why it matters

Schedule III Div II has a line "Exceptional items" between "Profit before exceptional items and tax" and "Profit before tax". It is the first place analysts look for non-recurring items — and the first place managements put items they would like analysts to ignore. Normalizing EBITDA and PAT correctly depends on understanding what belongs there, what the standards actually require, and when "exceptional" items are in fact recurring. The Labour Codes implementation in November 2025 produced a wave of exceptional charges in Q3 FY2026 results across Indian corporates, making this a live issue for FY2026 normalization.

Core notes

The legal and standards basis

  • Schedule III Div II includes the line "Exceptional items" in the statement of profit and loss, but does not define it.
  • Ind AS 1.97 requires that when items of income or expense are material, their nature and amount be disclosed separately. Ind AS 1.98 lists circumstances that give rise to such disclosure: write-downs of inventories to NRV or PPE to recoverable amount (and reversals), restructurings and reversals of restructuring provisions, disposals of PPE, disposals of investments, discontinued operations, litigation settlements, and other reversals of provisions.
  • Ind AS 1.87 prohibits presenting any items as "extraordinary items" in the P&L, OCI or notes.
  • The ICAI Guidance Note on Division II indicates that exceptional items are those of such size, nature or incidence that their separate disclosure is relevant to explain performance — the same items Ind AS 1.98 lists are typical.

So "exceptional" is a presentation judgement by management under Ind AS 1.97–98; it is not a classification that guarantees non-recurrence. The analyst must re-test every item.

Typical exceptional items and analyst treatment

Item Standard EBITDA treatment Recurrence test
Impairment of goodwill, PPE, intangibles, investments Ind AS 36; Ind AS 109 for financial assets Non-cash, below EBITDA in mechanical EBITDA too (if in exceptional line, add back to get core) Repeated impairments of the same business signal over-investment — keep a note, and recheck D&A run-rate
Voluntary retirement scheme (VRS) Ind AS 19 termination benefits (recognised when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer) Add back as non-recurring if a discrete programme Companies running VRS every year (PSUs, legacy manufacturers) — treat as recurring cost
Restructuring (plant closure, relocation) Ind AS 37 (constructive obligation, detailed formal plan) Add back if discrete Serial restructurers — keep a 3–5 year average in core
Litigation settlements, regulatory penalties Ind AS 37 provisions Add back if genuinely one-off Industries with chronic litigation (pharma, telecom) — partially recurring
Gain/loss on sale of PPE, business, investments Ind AS 16, Ind AS 110, Ind AS 109 Remove (gains deducted, losses added back) Asset-recycling models (real estate, telecom towers) may recur
Labour Codes past-service cost (FY2026) Ind AS 19 past service cost Add back as one-off catch-up The ongoing increase in service cost is recurring — do not add that back
COVID costs, fire/flood losses net of insurance Ind AS 16, 37; insurance claim recognised when virtually certain (Ind AS 37) Add back loss; deduct later insurance recovery Check whether recovery is booked in a later year as income

Labour Codes (effective 21 November 2025)

The four Labour Codes (Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020) were brought into force on 21 November 2025. The key accounting effect comes from the uniform definition of "wages": if allowances excluded from wages exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages. Gratuity, and for many companies leave encashment, are computed on wages, so the defined benefit obligation increases. Fixed-term employees also become eligible for gratuity after one year of service.

Under Ind AS 19, a plan amendment that changes benefits for past service gives rise to past service cost, recognised immediately in profit or loss (Ind AS 19.103) — not in OCI. Many listed companies recognised this catch-up in Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 31 December 2025) and presented it as an exceptional item, citing Ind AS 1.97–98 materiality. For leave encashment (an other long-term employee benefit), the entire remeasurement goes to P&L.

Analyst treatment:

  • Add back the one-time past service cost to core EBITDA and core PAT (tax-effected).
  • Do not add back the higher ongoing service cost in employee benefits expense from FY2026 onwards — it is the new normal and should be in forecasts.
  • If a company presented the catch-up within employee benefits expense rather than exceptional items, adjust it out of the operating line for comparability with peers.
  • Watch for estimates being trued up in Q4 FY2026 or FY2027 (actuarial reassessment once rules were notified) — a second "exceptional" charge may arise.

Deduct the land gain of ₹140 crore. Core PBT: FY2024 1,140; FY2025 1,265; FY2026 1,340. Ongoing service cost increase from the Labour Codes is already inside "profit before exceptional items" and stays there.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Items not in the exceptional line: inventory write-downs, provision reversals and one-off legal costs often sit in COGS or other expenses. Scan the notes, the auditor's key audit matters, and quarterly results commentary.
  • Exceptional gains: managements are quick to label losses exceptional and leave gains in other income. Apply symmetric tests.
  • Impairment reversals (Ind AS 36.114–117, not permitted for goodwill): treat as non-recurring gains.
  • Consolidated vs standalone: a standalone impairment of investment in a subsidiary eliminates on consolidation (goodwill impairment may replace it) — do not double-count.
  • Tax on exceptional items: often disclosed separately in quarterly results; otherwise apply the marginal rate (c03). Capital gains on land may be taxed at a different rate.
  • Discontinued operations are a separate line (Ind AS 105), not an exceptional item.

Key takeaways

  • "Exceptional items" is a Schedule III line based on Ind AS 1.97–98 materiality, not a guarantee of non-recurrence; Ind AS 1.87 bans "extraordinary items".
  • Re-test each item: recurring VRS, restructuring and write-downs belong in core earnings (or as an average).
  • Labour Codes (21 November 2025): add back the one-time past service cost; keep the higher ongoing service cost.
  • Search operating lines for unlabelled one-offs and apply symmetric treatment to gains and losses.
  • Tax-effect every adjustment on the way to core PAT.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify exceptional items [L1]

A company's exceptional items note shows: impairment of CWIP for an abandoned project ₹42 crore; VRS ₹18 crore (similar VRS of ₹15–20 crore in each of the last four years); profit on sale of a warehouse ₹65 crore; Labour Codes past service cost ₹27 crore; settlement of a customs dispute ₹12 crore. For each, state whether you would adjust core EBITDA and core PBT, with a one-line reason.

Q2. Labour Codes adjustment and tax [L2]

In Q3 FY2026, a company recognised a past service cost of ₹64 crore for gratuity and a ₹18 crore increase in leave encashment liability arising from the new wage definition, both shown as exceptional items. Its actuary estimates annual current service cost will rise by ₹11 crore. The company pays tax under s.115BAA. Compute the adjustments to FY2026 core EBITDA and core PAT, and the change you would make to the FY2027 employee cost forecast.

💡 Hint: Separate the one-time catch-up from the recurring run-rate increase; tax-effect only what you adjust.

Q3. Journal entries for restructuring [L2]

On 15 February 2026 the board approved and announced a detailed plan to close a plant: severance ₹30 crore, lease termination penalty ₹6 crore, impairment of plant ₹48 crore, retraining of retained staff ₹4 crore. Pass the journal entries at 31 March 2026 under Ind AS 37, Ind AS 19 and Ind AS 36, and indicate which amounts you would add back to core EBITDA.

Q4. Serial exceptional items [L3]

A listed FMCG company has reported "restructuring costs" as exceptional items in seven of the last eight years, averaging 1.2% of revenue. Management guides analysts to EBITDA "before exceptional items". Write a short memo to your investment committee recommending a treatment for valuation, citing Ind AS 1.97–98, and quantify the effect on an EV/EBITDA multiple if EBITDA margin is 18% and the stock trades at 40x reported EBITDA.

Q5. Hidden one-offs in operating lines [L2]

List six places in an Indian annual report and quarterly results where non-recurring items may be embedded outside the exceptional items line, and for each give one example.
