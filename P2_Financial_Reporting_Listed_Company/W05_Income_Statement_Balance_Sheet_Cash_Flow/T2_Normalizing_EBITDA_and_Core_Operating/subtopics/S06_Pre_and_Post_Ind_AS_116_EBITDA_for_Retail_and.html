Subtopic note

Pre- and Post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA for Retail and Airline Peers

Scope: Ind AS 116 pre/post EBITDA: rent-adjusted (pre-Ind AS 116) EBITDA for retail/airline peers

Why it matters

For lease-heavy businesses — apparel and value retail, quick-service restaurants, multiplexes, hospitals operating leased premises, airlines with leased aircraft — Ind AS 116 changed EBITDA more than any operational event. Fixed rent and aircraft lease rentals moved out of operating expenses into ROU depreciation and interest on lease liabilities, lifting reported EBITDA by 5–15% of revenue. Since rent is a real, recurring, cash operating cost of running a store or flying a plane, many investors, lenders and managements (Trent, Avenue Supermarts, Jubilant FoodWorks, InterGlobe Aviation all discuss it) track a rent-adjusted or pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA. Choosing the right basis — and keeping it consistent with net debt — is the single biggest comparability issue in Indian consumer and airline analysis.

Core notes

What Ind AS 116 does to the P&L

At commencement, a lessee recognises an ROU asset and a lease liability equal to the present value of lease payments (Ind AS 116.26). Subsequently:

  • ROU depreciation (straight-line over the shorter of lease term and useful life) → D&A.
  • Interest on lease liability (effective interest method, front-loaded) → finance costs.
  • Variable lease payments not depending on an index or rate (e.g., revenue-share rent), short-term leases (≤12 months) and low-value leases (Ind AS 116.5–6) → other expenses (still inside EBITDA).

In the cash flow statement, lease payments are split into principal (financing) and interest (financing for non-financial entities, consistent with Ind AS 7 policy) — so CFO is also inflated (Topic 4 s03).

Two EBITDA bases

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{pre-116}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{post-116}} - \text{Lease payments (principal + interest) on ROU leases} \]

Using lease payments from the cash flow statement approximates the old straight-line rent. Alternative: \(\text{EBITDA}_{\text{post-116}} - \text{ROU depreciation} - \text{lease interest}\), which equals the lease P&L charge (higher than cash rent in early lease years because of front-loaded interest).

Basis Lease cost in EBITDA? Matching net debt Matching EV multiple
Post-Ind AS 116 (EBITDA) No — fixed lease cost below EBITDA Include lease liabilities in net debt EV including lease liabilities ÷ EBITDA
Pre-Ind AS 116 (EBITDAR-less-rent, "rent-adjusted EBITDA") Yes — deducted as operating cost Exclude lease liabilities EV excluding lease liabilities ÷ rent-adjusted EBITDA
EBITDAR (airlines) No — rent added back explicitly Include capitalised leases EV + leases ÷ EBITDAR

Consistency rule: whatever basis you use for EBITDA, the numerator of EV/EBITDA and the leverage ratio (net debt/EBITDA) must use the same treatment of leases. Mixing post-116 EBITDA with EV excluding lease liabilities overstates value; mixing rent-adjusted EBITDA with EV including lease liabilities understates it.

Illustration: two retailers (₹ crore)

Retailer A (own stores) Retailer B (leased stores)
Revenue 10,000 10,000
Reported EBITDA (post-116) 1,300 1,900
Lease payments (CFS) 80 720
Rent-adjusted EBITDA 1,220 1,180
Reported EBITDA margin 13.0% 19.0%
Rent-adjusted margin 12.2% 11.8%
Lease liabilities 300 4,100
Borrowings net of cash 900 (200)

On reported numbers B looks far more profitable; on a rent-adjusted basis the two are similar, and A has slightly better store economics. Net debt for leverage: A = 900 + 300 = ₹1,200 crore post-116 (0.9x EBITDA); B = −200 + 4,100 = ₹3,900 crore (2.1x) — B is more levered once leases are counted. On a pre-116 basis: A = ₹900 crore (0.7x), B = net cash.

Airlines

Airlines have historically used EBITDAR (EBITDA before aircraft rentals) and lease-adjusted net debt (capitalising operating leases at a multiple of rent, often 7x, pre-2019). After Ind AS 116, reported EBITDA approximates EBITDAR for fixed leases, and lease liabilities on the balance sheet replace the 7x convention. Supplementary rentals and maintenance reserves paid to lessors are often variable and remain in operating costs. Sale-and-leaseback gains (Ind AS 116.98–103: gain restricted to rights transferred) are a further normalization item, recurring for airlines that fund fleet growth this way.

Implementation

Rent-adjusted EBITDA:   =EBITDA_post116 - (LeasePrincipalPaid + LeaseInterestPaid)
Lease P&L charge:       =ROU_Depreciation + Lease_Interest
Front-loading gap:      =(ROU_Depreciation + Lease_Interest) - (LeasePrincipalPaid + LeaseInterestPaid)

Sources: lease note (Ind AS 116.53: depreciation by class, interest expense, short-term and variable lease expense, total cash outflow for leases, additions to ROU); cash flow statement financing section; maturity analysis of lease liabilities (Ind AS 107/116.58).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Total cash outflow for leases (Ind AS 116.53(g)) includes short-term and variable payments already in EBITDA; subtract only payments on ROU leases.
  • Rent concessions (COVID practical expedient, Ind AS 116.46A) recognised in P&L as negative variable lease payments inflated FY2021–FY2022 EBITDA — remove for normalization.
  • Lease modifications and terminations generate gains in other income (derecognition of lease liability above ROU) — non-recurring, exclude.
  • Growing store networks: lease payments in a growth year include new leases for part of the year; for run-rate, annualise payments on stores open at year-end.
  • Revenue-share rent: retailer with mostly variable rent (mall revenue share) already has rent in EBITDA; a fixed-rent peer does not — pre-116 basis is the only like-for-like comparison.
  • US GAAP peers (ASC 842): operating lease cost remains a single straight-line expense in operating expenses — their EBITDA is effectively pre-116; adjust Ind AS peers accordingly.
  • Management guidance: some companies guide "EBITDA pre-Ind AS 116" using straight-line rent from internal records — reconcile to your CFS-based figure.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 116 moves fixed lease cost into D&A and finance cost, inflating EBITDA for lease-heavy companies.
  • Rent-adjusted (pre-116) EBITDA = post-116 EBITDA − lease payments (principal + interest) on ROU leases.
  • Match the basis: post-116 EBITDA with lease liabilities in net debt; rent-adjusted EBITDA without lease liabilities.
  • Use rent-adjusted EBITDA to compare retailers with different own/lease/revenue-share mixes and to compare with US GAAP peers.
  • Remove rent concessions and lease modification gains; annualise payments for growing networks.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute rent-adjusted EBITDA [L1]

A QSR chain reports EBITDA ₹1,050 crore, ROU depreciation ₹310 crore, interest on lease liabilities ₹170 crore, lease principal paid ₹290 crore, lease interest paid ₹170 crore, and variable/short-term lease expense in other expenses ₹60 crore. Compute rent-adjusted EBITDA (CFS method and P&L-charge method), and explain the difference.

Q2. Leverage on matching bases [L2]

A retailer has post-116 EBITDA ₹2,400 crore, lease payments ₹780 crore, lease liabilities ₹5,200 crore, borrowings ₹600 crore and cash ₹900 crore. Compute net debt/EBITDA on post-116 and pre-116 bases, and explain which a bank would use for a working-capital limit and which an equity analyst would use for EV/EBITDA.

💡 Hint: Use lease liabilities in net debt only when EBITDA is post-116.

Q3. Multiple mismatch [L2]

A broker values Retailer B (from a peer table) at 30x post-116 EBITDA of ₹1,900 crore and deducts only borrowings net of cash (net cash ₹200 crore) to arrive at equity value. Lease liabilities are ₹4,100 crore and lease payments ₹720 crore. Compute the broker's equity value, the corrected equity value on a post-116 basis, and the equity value if 30x is applied to rent-adjusted EBITDA with leases excluded from net debt.

Q4. Airline EBITDAR and sale-and-leaseback [L3]

An airline reports EBITDA ₹9,800 crore, which includes ₹620 crore of gains on sale-and-leaseback transactions; supplementary rentals of ₹1,400 crore are in other expenses; lease payments on aircraft are ₹6,900 crore. Explain how you would compute normalized EBITDAR and rent-adjusted EBITDA, whether sale-and-leaseback gains are recurring for this airline, and which metric you would use for comparison with a peer that owns most of its fleet.

Q5. Growth-year run-rate [L3]

A value retailer opened 200 stores evenly through FY2025, ending with 800 stores; FY2025 lease payments were ₹540 crore. Assuming new stores have the same rent as existing stores, estimate the run-rate annual lease payment at year-end and the run-rate rent-adjusted EBITDA if FY2025 post-116 EBITDA was ₹1,500 crore and new stores' EBITDA contribution is annualised by ₹90 crore.
