Key concept

Pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA: Deducting Lease Payments on ROU Leases

Concept: Pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA = Reported EBITDA − lease payments (principal + interest) on ROU leases

Statement

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{pre-116}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{post-116}} - \left(P_{\text{lease}} + I_{\text{lease}}\right) \]

where \(\text{EBITDA}_{\text{post-116}}\) is EBITDA as computed from Ind AS statements (with ROU depreciation in D&A and lease interest in finance costs), \(P_{\text{lease}}\) is principal repaid on lease liabilities and \(I_{\text{lease}}\) is interest paid on lease liabilities during the period, both from the financing section of the cash flow statement or the lease note, covering only leases recognised as ROU assets (not short-term, low-value or variable payments already expensed in EBITDA).

Explanation

Ind AS 116 (effective 1 April 2019) removed the operating-lease rent line for lessees and replaced it with ROU depreciation and lease interest, both below EBITDA. For lease-heavy businesses this lifts EBITDA by the full amount of fixed rent. Deducting actual lease payments restores a figure close to the old Ind AS 17 EBITDA, where straight-line rent was an operating expense.

Why payments rather than the P&L lease charge?

  • Lease payments approximate the cash rent and the old straight-line rent (for leases with fixed escalations, Ind AS 17 straight-lined escalations; payments rise over time — a small difference).
  • The P&L charge (ROU depreciation + interest) is front-loaded in early lease years because interest is higher on a larger liability; for a growing network with many young leases, the P&L charge exceeds payments.
  • Payments tie directly to cash flow analysis (FCF after leases, Topic 4).

Conditions and adjustments:

  • Exclude payments that are already in EBITDA (short-term, low-value, variable lease payments) — Ind AS 116.53(g) "total cash outflow for leases" includes them.
  • Exclude one-off payments (lease termination penalties, which are non-recurring).
  • For growing networks, annualise payments on the year-end store base for a run-rate view.
  • Rent concessions received (practical expedient) reduce payments and are credited to P&L — remove both effects in normalization.
  • Interest paid on leases may be classified in operating or financing depending on the entity's Ind AS 7 policy; collect it wherever it appears.

Consistency with net debt: when using pre-116 EBITDA, exclude lease liabilities from net debt and EV; when using post-116 EBITDA, include them.

Illustration

A value fashion retailer (₹ crore):

FY2025
Revenue from operations 16,500
Reported EBITDA (post-116) 2,700
Lease principal paid (financing) 520
Lease interest paid (financing) 330
Variable rent in other expenses (already in EBITDA) 90
Pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA 1,850
Margin post-116 / pre-116 16.4% / 11.2%
ROU depreciation + lease interest (P&L charge) 910
Front-loading gap (P&L charge − payments) 60

The pre-116 margin of 11.2% is the comparable figure for a peer reporting under US GAAP (ASC 842 operating leases keep a single straight-line lease cost in operating expenses) or for comparison with the company's own pre-FY2020 history.

Connections

This concept is the formula behind s06 (pre/post Ind AS 116 EBITDA for retail and airline peers) and the topic pitfall on mixing bases. It links to Topic 3 (lease liabilities as debt-like items; net debt definition c03) and Topic 4 s03 (Ind AS 116 inflating CFO and FCF). In valuation (Week 16), the lease basis must be consistent across EBITDA, EV and net debt; in Topic 1 s04, it bridges the FY2019-20 structural break.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both bases [L1]

A multiplex operator reports EBITDA ₹1,480 crore; lease note: total cash outflow for leases ₹760 crore, of which short-term and revenue-share rentals ₹110 crore; CFS shows lease principal ₹380 crore and lease interest ₹270 crore. Revenue is ₹6,100 crore. Compute pre-116 EBITDA and both margins.

Q2. Reconcile to the P&L charge [L2]

For the same operator, ROU depreciation is ₹420 crore and interest on lease liabilities is ₹270 crore. Compute EBITDA after the P&L lease charge, compare it with pre-116 EBITDA from payments, and explain why the two differ and which one an analyst should use for a mature versus a fast-growing network.

💡 Hint: Think about where young leases sit on the interest curve.

Q3. Matching EV basis [L2]

The operator has lease liabilities ₹3,900 crore, borrowings ₹1,200 crore and cash ₹350 crore. Peers trade at 12x pre-116 EBITDA. Compute the implied equity value using pre-116 EBITDA, then show the EV/EBITDA multiple on a post-116 basis that produces the same equity value.
