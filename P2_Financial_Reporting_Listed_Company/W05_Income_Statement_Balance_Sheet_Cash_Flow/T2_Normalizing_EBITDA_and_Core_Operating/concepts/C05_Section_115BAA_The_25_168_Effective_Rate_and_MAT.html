Key concept

Section 115BAA: The 25.168% Effective Rate and MAT Exclusion

Concept: s.115BAA: 22% base + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%; MAT (s.115JB) not applicable (Income-tax Act 2025 renumbers from 1 Apr 2026)

Statement

\[ t_{\text{115BAA}} = 22\% \times (1 + 10\%) \times (1 + 4\%) = 22\% \times 1.10 \times 1.04 = 25.168\% \]

where 22% is the base rate under s.115BAA of the Income-tax Act 1961, 10% is the surcharge (levied at a flat rate irrespective of income for companies opting for s.115BAA) and 4% is the health and education cess applied on tax plus surcharge.

Minimum Alternate Tax under s.115JB does not apply to a company that has opted for s.115BAA (s.115JB(5A)), and brought-forward MAT credit cannot be utilised after the option.

From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act for tax year 2026-27 onwards; it retains the concessional regime but renumbers the provisions. Pre-FY2027 references remain to the 1961 sections.

Explanation

Introduced by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act 2019 with effect from assessment year 2020-21 (FY2019-20), s.115BAA lets a domestic company pay tax at 22% if it forgoes specified deductions and incentives — including s.10AA (SEZ units), additional depreciation under s.32(1)(iia), s.35AD, most Chapter VI-A deductions (other than s.80JJAA and s.80M), and set-off of losses or unabsorbed depreciation attributable to those deductions. The option, once exercised, cannot be withdrawn. Section 115BAB offered 15% (effective 17.16%) for new manufacturing companies commencing production by 31 March 2024 — now closed to new entrants.

Comparison with the old regime (for a company with turnover above ₹400 crore):

Regime Base Surcharge (income > ₹10 crore) Cess Effective
Normal (s.115BAA not opted) 30% 12% 4% 34.944%
Normal, turnover ≤ ₹400 crore 25% 12% 4% 29.12%
s.115BAA 22% 10% 4% 25.168%
s.115BAB (new manufacturing) 15% 10% 4% 17.16%
MAT (old regime only) 15% of book profit 12% 4% 17.472%

Why it matters in normalization and modelling:

  • Marginal rate for tax-effecting adjustments: most large Indian companies have opted for s.115BAA; 25.168% is the default marginal rate for deductible/taxable business items in core PAT (c03).
  • Transition effects: companies that opted for s.115BAA remeasured deferred tax balances at the new rate (Ind AS 12.47) and wrote off unusable MAT credit — one-time tax items to remove when normalizing PAT in the year of switching (many switched in FY2019-20 to FY2022-23).
  • Companies still on the old regime: those with large unused MAT credit or SEZ/80-IA benefits stayed on the old regime and plan to switch later; forecast the ETR change in the switching year.
  • Capital gains: rates for capital gains are separate (with surcharge capped for companies under the concessional regime at 10%); don't apply 25.168% blindly to asset-sale gains.
  • Dividends received: taxable in the hands of the company; s.80M deduction for dividends distributed onward remains available under s.115BAA.

Illustration

A company has PBT ₹1,000 crore, including a non-deductible CSR expense of ₹20 crore. Under s.115BAA, current tax ≈ (1,000 + 20) × 25.168% = ₹256.7 crore; ETR = 25.7%.

In the year of switching, it had a net DTL of ₹300 crore measured at 34.944%. Remeasurement at 25.168%: 300 × 25.168 ÷ 34.944 = ₹216.1 crore — a one-time deferred tax credit of ₹83.9 crore. It also wrote off MAT credit of ₹60 crore. Net one-time tax effect = credit ₹23.9 crore, to be removed from core PAT.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute effective rates [L1]

Compute the effective tax rate (base × surcharge × cess) for: (a) a s.115BAA company; (b) an old-regime company with turnover ₹300 crore and income ₹50 crore; (c) MAT on book profit for an old-regime company with income above ₹10 crore. Show each calculation.

Q2. Switching-year normalization [L2]

A company opting for s.115BAA in FY2025 has opening net DTL ₹420 crore at 34.944% and MAT credit of ₹95 crore that becomes unusable. FY2025 PBT is ₹1,600 crore and reported tax expense ₹310 crore. Compute the one-time tax effects, the reported ETR and the normalized ETR.

💡 Hint: Remeasure the DTL at the new rate and treat both the remeasurement and the MAT write-off as non-recurring.

Q3. Regime choice in a model [L3]

An old-regime company has unutilised MAT credit of ₹240 crore and pays MAT for the next two years under its plan, after which normal tax exceeds MAT. Build the logic (in words or Excel pseudo-formulas) for a model switch that tests the NPV of tax payments under staying on the old regime until MAT credit is exhausted versus switching to s.115BAA now, using an 11% discount rate.
