Key concept

Ind AS 1 Paras 87 and 97: No Extraordinary Items, Disclose Material Items

Concept: Ind AS 1 para 87 prohibits "extraordinary items"; para 97 requires separate disclosure of material income/expense items

Statement

Ind AS 1.87: An entity shall not present any items of income or expense as extraordinary items, in the statement of profit and loss or in the notes.

Ind AS 1.97: When items of income or expense are material, an entity shall disclose their nature and amount separately.

Ind AS 1.98 lists circumstances that would give rise to separate disclosure, including: (a) write-downs of inventories to net realisable value or of PPE to recoverable amount, and reversals of such write-downs; (b) restructurings of activities and reversals of restructuring provisions; (c) disposals of items of PPE; (d) disposals of investments; (e) discontinued operations; (f) litigation settlements; and (g) other reversals of provisions.

Explanation

The prohibition of "extraordinary items" (a category that existed under old Indian GAAP AS 5 and pre-2005 IFRS) reflects the view that all items of income and expense arise from the entity's normal risks — floods, expropriations and strikes included. There is no "outside the business" bucket in which management can park losses.

What remains is disclosure of material items. Para 97 does not create a category with special measurement; it requires that material items be visible. Schedule III Division II gives them a home on the face of the statement — the "Exceptional items" line between profit before exceptional items and tax and PBT — and the Division II additional information also asks for "details of items of exceptional nature". So Indian practice is:

  • Material, unusual items (per para 97–98) → "Exceptional items" line, with a note describing each item.
  • Material but recurring items (e.g., an annual inventory write-down) → disclosed by nature in notes but often left in operating lines.

Implications for normalization:

  • "Exceptional" is a management label, not a standard-defined class. The list in para 98 is about disclosure, not non-recurrence: disposals of PPE and write-downs of inventory happen every year in many businesses.
  • The disclosure is useful: para 97 guarantees that material items are separately visible, so the analyst can find and test them. Items below the materiality threshold may be buried, but they are by definition small.
  • Consistency: companies should apply a consistent policy for what goes into exceptional items (Ind AS 1.45). A change in what is classified as exceptional is a presentation change requiring comparatives to be reclassified.
  • Regulatory attention: SEBI and NFRA review findings have questioned the use of exceptional items for recurring costs; auditors may address significant exceptional items in key audit matters.

Illustration

A consumer company's five-year exceptional items (₹ crore):

Inventory write-down (obsolescence) (35) (28) (40) (32) (38)
Impairment of a brand — (90) — — —
Profit on sale of land — — 120 — —
Flood loss net of insurance (25) — — — —

Under para 87 the company could not label the flood loss "extraordinary" — correctly placed as exceptional. But the inventory write-down appears every year at ₹28–40 crore: it is a normal cost of the business and belongs in core EBITDA, even though para 97 makes its disclosure appropriate. The analyst adds back only the brand impairment, the flood loss and deducts the land gain.

Practice Questions

Q1. Extraordinary vs exceptional [L1]

A company wants to present a ₹140 crore loss from a cyclone that destroyed a warehouse as an "extraordinary item" below profit after tax. Explain why this is not permitted, how it should be presented under Ind AS and Schedule III Div II, and how the related insurance claim should be recognised.

Q2. Recurrence test on para 98 items [L2]

Using the illustration table above, compute core PBT adjustments for each year under (a) the company's "exclude all exceptional items" approach and (b) your approach treating the inventory write-down as recurring. Comment on the effect on a 5-year average PBT.

💡 Hint: Sum the add-backs and deductions year by year under each approach.

Q3. Change in classification policy [L2]

In FY2025 a company begins classifying "store closure costs" (₹45 crore in FY2025, ₹38 crore in FY2024 previously in other expenses) as exceptional items. Explain the Ind AS 1 requirements for comparatives and consistency, and describe how you would treat the change in your trend and core EBITDA tables.
