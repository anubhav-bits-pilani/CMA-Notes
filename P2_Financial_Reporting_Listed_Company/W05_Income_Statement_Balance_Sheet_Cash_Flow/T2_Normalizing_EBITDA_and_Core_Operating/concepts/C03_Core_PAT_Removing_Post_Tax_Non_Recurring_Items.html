Key concept

Core PAT: Removing Post-Tax Non-Recurring Items

Concept: Core PAT = Reported PAT − post-tax non-recurring items, tax-effected at the marginal rate (e.g., 25.168% under s.115BAA)

Statement

\[ \text{Core PAT} = \text{PAT}_{\text{reported}} - \sum_{k} A_k \times (1 - t_k) - \text{NR}_{\text{tax}} \]

where \(A_k\) is the pre-tax amount of non-recurring item \(k\) (positive for gains, negative for losses), \(t_k\) is the marginal tax rate applicable to that item (25.168% for a domestic company under s.115BAA for ordinary business items; the applicable capital-gains rate for asset sales; zero for items that are not deductible or not taxable), and \(\text{NR}_{\text{tax}}\) is any non-recurring tax item itself (e.g., deferred tax remeasurement on a rate change, reversal of tax provisions on settlement of old assessments, MAT credit write-off on switching to s.115BAA), removed in full.

For consolidated statements, use profit attributable to owners and remove only the owners' share of each item:

\[ \text{Core PAT}_{\text{owners}} = \text{PAT}_{\text{owners}} - \sum_k A_k (1 - t_k)\, s_k \]

where \(s_k\) is the owners' share in the entity where item \(k\) arose.

Explanation

Adjusting EBITDA is pre-tax; adjusting PAT and EPS requires the tax effect, because the reported tax charge already includes the tax on (or tax saving from) each item. Removing a ₹100 crore exceptional loss without its ₹25.168 crore tax shield overstates core PAT by the shield amount.

Choosing \(t_k\):

  • Deductible business expenses and taxable business income (VRS, restructuring, provision reversals, inventory write-downs): marginal corporate rate — 25.168% under s.115BAA (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess), or the old-regime rate if the company has not opted.
  • Capital gains on land, buildings or investments: the capital-gains rate actually applicable (long-term rates differ from business rates; indexation changes from 23 July 2024 apply), or the rate disclosed in the tax note.
  • Non-deductible items: goodwill impairment (no tax deduction; no deferred tax on initial goodwill recognition — Ind AS 12.15(a)), penalties (Explanation 1 to s.37(1)), CSR expenditure (not deductible under s.37) — \(t_k = 0\).
  • Deferred tax items: impairment of PPE creates a deferred tax asset if the tax base is unchanged; the tax effect still flows through the P&L tax charge, so use the marginal rate.
  • Items in a loss-making entity with no DTA recognised: \(t_k = 0\), because no tax effect was recognised.

Where the company discloses the tax on exceptional items (common in Reg 33 results), use that figure.

Income-tax Act 2025: from 1 April 2026 (tax year 2026-27) the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act and renumbers sections; the concessional regime and its 25.168% effective rate continue, but cite the new section numbers only after confirming them.

Illustration

₹ crore Pre-tax Tax rate Tax effect Post-tax
Reported PAT 1,060
Remove: exceptional VRS (loss) (80) 25.168% 20.1 (59.9) → add back 59.9
Remove: goodwill impairment (loss) (120) 0% 0 (120) → add back 120
Remove: gain on sale of land (gain) 150 14.30% (LTCG 12.5% + surcharge + cess, assumed) (21.5) 128.5 → deduct 128.5
Remove: deferred tax credit on a one-off rate change (35) → deduct 35
Core PAT 1,076.4

If the analyst had used 25.168% on all items and ignored the deferred tax credit, core PAT would be 1,060 + 59.9 + 89.8 − 112.2 = ₹1,097.5 crore — ₹21 crore too high. That looks small, but on a 50x P/E it is over ₹1,000 crore of market value.

Connections

This concept completes the bridge from core EBITDA (c01) to core PAT and core EPS, and uses the rate from c05 (s.115BAA) and the reconciliation in c06 (normalized ETR). It addresses the topic pitfall on forgetting tax effects. Core PAT feeds P/E valuation and consensus EPS comparisons (Week 16) and the normalized PAT required in this week's Saturday assessment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Tax-effect the adjustments [L1]

Reported PAT ₹740 crore. Exceptional items: restructuring cost ₹60 crore (deductible), penalty paid to a regulator ₹15 crore (not deductible), profit on sale of an office building ₹90 crore (tax on it disclosed at ₹18 crore). The company is under s.115BAA. Compute core PAT.

Q2. Owners' share in consolidated PAT [L2]

Consolidated PAT ₹1,500 crore, of which NCI ₹180 crore. A 60%-owned subsidiary recorded an exceptional impairment of PPE of ₹100 crore (deductible via depreciation in future; DTA recognised at 25.168%). The parent recorded an exceptional gain of ₹50 crore on sale of investments, taxed at 12.5% plus 10% surcharge and 4% cess (effective 14.30%). Compute core PAT attributable to owners.

💡 Hint: Apply the owners' share only to the subsidiary's item.

Q3. Core EPS and P/E effect [L2]

Using your answer to Q1, compute reported and core EPS if the company has 40 crore shares outstanding, and the implied P/E on each if the share price is ₹950. Explain which P/E you would quote in an initiation report and why.
