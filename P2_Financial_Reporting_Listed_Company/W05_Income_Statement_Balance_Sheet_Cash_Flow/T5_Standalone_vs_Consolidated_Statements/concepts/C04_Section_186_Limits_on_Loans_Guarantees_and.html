Key concept

Section 186 Limits on Loans, Guarantees and Investments

Concept: s.186: loans/guarantees/investments beyond 60% of paid-up capital + free reserves + securities premium, or 100% of free reserves + premium, need special resolution

Statement

Under Sec 186(2), Companies Act 2013, a company shall not directly or indirectly:

  • give any loan to any person or other body corporate;
  • give any guarantee or provide security in connection with a loan to any other body corporate or person; or
  • acquire by way of subscription, purchase or otherwise the securities of any other body corporate,

exceeding the higher of:

\[ L = \max\left(0.60 \times (\text{PUC} + \text{FR} + \text{SP}),\; 1.00 \times (\text{FR} + \text{SP})\right) \]

where PUC = paid-up share capital, FR = free reserves, SP = securities premium account. Exceeding \( L \) (in aggregate, including the proposed transaction) requires prior approval by a special resolution at a general meeting (Sec 186(3)).

Explanation

The section protects shareholders and creditors from funds being diverted to group or third-party entities. Key conditions and exceptions:

  • Board approval at a meeting, with unanimous consent of directors present, and prior approval of the public financial institution if a term loan is outstanding (Sec 186(5)).
  • Interest on loans not lower than the prevailing yield of 1-, 3-, 5- or 10-year government security closest to the tenor (Sec 186(7)).
  • Exemptions (Sec 186(3) proviso and Sec 186(11)): loans, guarantees or security given by a holding company to its wholly owned subsidiary or a JV, or acquisition of securities of a WOS, do not need a special resolution; they still require board approval and disclosure. Whether they are counted in the aggregate when testing the limit for other transactions is a matter of interpretation — practice varies, so state the view taken and test both. Banking companies, insurance companies, NBFCs whose principal business is financing, and companies in the business of providing infrastructure facilities are exempt from most of the section for loans/guarantees in the ordinary course.
  • Layers: investment through not more than two layers of investment companies (Sec 186(1)), subject to exceptions.
  • Disclosure: full particulars of loans, guarantees, securities and investments, and the purpose, in the financial statements (Sec 186(4)); a register (Form MBP-2).
  • Penalty for contravention under Sec 186(13).
  • Distinct from Sec 185, which restricts loans to directors and entities in which directors are interested.

Analytical use: headroom under Sec 186 indicates whether a parent can keep supporting subsidiaries without a shareholder vote; repeated special resolutions to raise limits flag a group relying on parent support.

Illustration

Parent (₹ crore): PUC 200; free reserves 3,400; securities premium 600. Existing loans/guarantees/investments (outside WOS exemptions) ₹2,300 crore. Proposed guarantee of ₹600 crore to a 74% subsidiary.

Limb Calculation Limit
60% limb 0.60 × (200 + 3,400 + 600) 2,520
100% limb 1.00 × (3,400 + 600) 4,000
Applicable limit (higher) 4,000
Aggregate after proposal 2,300 + 600 2,900

No special resolution is required; board approval and disclosure are. Note that free reserves exclude unrealised gains, revaluation reserve and amounts representing Ind AS remeasurement (Sec 2(43)).

Connections

Links to s05 (guarantees and structural subordination), s04 (upstream loans as an alternative to dividends), c05 (layers rules), and the related-party framework under Sec 188 and LODR Reg 23. In audit, Sec 186 compliance is a CARO 2020 reporting item (clause 3(iii) and 3(iv)).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the limit [L1]

A company has paid-up capital ₹150 crore, free reserves ₹900 crore, securities premium ₹250 crore, capital reserve ₹40 crore and revaluation reserve ₹120 crore. Compute the Sec 186(2) limit.

Q2. Special resolution needed? [L2]

Using the Q1 company, existing loans to a JV ₹300 crore, investments in a WOS ₹400 crore and guarantees for a 60% subsidiary ₹250 crore. It proposes a ₹200 crore loan to an unrelated body corporate. Determine whether a special resolution is needed and list other compliance requirements.

💡 Hint: Identify which existing transactions fall under the WOS/JV proviso, then test the aggregate both including and excluding them.

Q3. Audit and analysis [L3]

A mid-cap company's parent passes special resolutions every year to raise its Sec 186 limit, and the loans note shows interest-free loans to promoter-group entities. Discuss the Sec 186(7) issue, CARO reporting implications and how you would adjust invested capital and normalised other income.
