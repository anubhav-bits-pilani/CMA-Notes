Key concept

Implied Subsidiary Contribution

Concept: Implied subsidiary contribution = Consolidated metric − Standalone metric (approximate; adjust for eliminations)

Statement

\[ \text{Implied subsidiary contribution}_{M} = M_{\text{consolidated}} - M_{\text{standalone}} \pm \text{Elimination adjustments} \]

where \( M \) is any metric (revenue, EBITDA, PAT, borrowings, cash), \( M_{\text{consolidated}} \) is the figure from the consolidated statements or Reg 33 consolidated results, \( M_{\text{standalone}} \) is the parent's separate figure, and elimination adjustments add back intra-group items removed in consolidation (intra-group sales, dividends received by the parent, intercompany interest, royalties, unrealised profit).

Explanation

The simple difference is the fastest way to see what the subsidiaries add, and it is available every quarter. But it is only approximate because the consolidated number is after eliminations, while the standalone number is before them:

  • Revenue: parent sales to subsidiaries are in standalone revenue but eliminated in consolidated. The raw difference understates subsidiaries' external revenue.
  • PAT: dividends from subsidiaries are in standalone PAT but eliminated. In a year with large dividends, the raw difference can even be negative though subsidiaries are profitable. Purchase price allocation amortisation, goodwill impairment and unrealised profit adjustments also sit only in consolidated.
  • Associates/JVs: share of profit appears only in consolidated; separate it from subsidiary contribution.
  • NCI: the difference in total PAT includes NCI; for owners' contribution use attributable PAT.
  • Debt: parent loans to subsidiaries sit in the parent's standalone assets, not its borrowings, and are eliminated against the subsidiaries' liabilities. So the borrowings difference already approximates the subsidiaries' external debt; the subsidiaries' total debt in their own books is that difference plus the eliminated parent loans (relevant when assessing subsidiary-level covenants).

Use AOC-1, the Schedule III additional information table and Ind AS 112 notes (s03) to refine the estimate annually, and the quarterly difference for trend.

Illustration

Reg 33 results for a quarter (₹ crore):

Metric Consolidated Standalone Raw difference
Revenue 14,500 13,200 1,300
PAT 1,020 1,060 (40)

Notes: parent's sales to subsidiaries ₹600 crore; dividend from a subsidiary ₹150 crore in standalone other income; share of profit of JV ₹25 crore; PPA amortisation (after tax) ₹10 crore.

Adjusted subsidiary revenue = 1,300 + 600 = ₹1,900 crore. Adjusted subsidiary PAT = −40 + 150 − 25 + 10 = ₹95 crore (before PPA amortisation). The raw figures would have suggested loss-making subsidiaries.

Practice Questions

Q1. Raw vs adjusted contribution [L1]

Consolidated EBITDA ₹3,400 crore, standalone EBITDA ₹2,900 crore. Parent earns royalty income of ₹120 crore from subsidiaries (in standalone EBITDA) and sells goods to them at a profit of ₹40 crore, of which ₹10 crore remains unrealised at year-end. Estimate subsidiaries' EBITDA contribution.

Q2. Quarterly trend [L2]

Over four quarters, consolidated minus standalone PAT (₹ crore) was 80, 95, −20, 110. In Q3 the parent received a ₹130 crore interim dividend from a subsidiary. Recompute the adjusted series and comment on the trend.

💡 Hint: Only one quarter needs a dividend adjustment; think about which entity's PAT contains it.

Q3. Interview: why not just subtract? [L2]

An interviewer asks why "consolidated minus standalone" is not a reliable measure of subsidiary profit. Give four reasons with an example of each.
