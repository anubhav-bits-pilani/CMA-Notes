Key concept

Owners' Share vs NCI in Consolidated PAT

Concept: Consolidated PAT = Owners' share + NCI share; use owners' share for EPS and ROE

Statement

\[ \text{PAT}_{\text{consolidated}} = \text{PAT}_{\text{owners of the parent}} + \text{PAT}_{\text{NCI}} \]

\[ \text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{PAT}_{\text{owners}} - \text{Preference dividends (if any)}}{\text{Weighted average equity shares}} \qquad \text{ROE} = \frac{\text{PAT}_{\text{owners}}}{\text{Average equity attributable to owners}} \]

where NCI is the equity in a subsidiary not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent (Ind AS 110 Appendix A), and equity attributable to owners excludes NCI.

Explanation

Consolidation brings in 100% of each subsidiary's profit, so total consolidated PAT includes profit belonging to minority shareholders. Ind AS 1.81B requires the P&L to show profit attributable to owners of the parent and to NCI, and Ind AS 110.B94 attributes profit and OCI to both, even if NCI becomes negative. Ind AS 33.10 defines basic EPS using profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent.

Consistency rule: numerator and denominator must belong to the same claimants.

Ratio Numerator Denominator
ROE Owners' PAT Owners' equity (excl. NCI)
ROE incl. NCI (rarely used) Total PAT Total equity incl. NCI
P/E Market cap Owners' PAT
EV/EBITDA EV including NCI (at market/fair value) Consolidated EBITDA (100%)
ROCE EBIT (100%) Capital employed incl. NCI

Mixing total PAT with owners' equity overstates ROE; mixing EV excluding NCI with 100% EBITDA understates the multiple. Dividends paid to NCI are a financing cash outflow in consolidated statements; NCI's share of cash is not available to parent shareholders.

Illustration

FY2025 (₹ crore): consolidated PAT 1,450, of which NCI 210. Equity attributable to owners: opening 8,600, closing 9,600; NCI: opening 1,300, closing 1,450. Weighted average shares 120 crore.

Measure Correct Mixed (wrong)
ROE 1,240 ÷ 9,100 = 13.6% 1,450 ÷ 9,100 = 15.9%
EPS 1,240 ÷ 120 = ₹10.33 1,450 ÷ 120 = ₹12.08

The wrong calculation overstates ROE by more than two percentage points.

Practice Questions

Q1. Attribute and compute [L1]

Consolidated PAT ₹860 crore; parent owns 70% of S1 (PAT ₹200 crore) and 100% of S2; other entities wholly owned. Compute NCI share and owners' share of PAT, then EPS for 50 crore weighted shares.

Q2. Consistent ratio set [L2]

Group data (₹ crore): consolidated PAT 2,300 (NCI 340); owners' equity opening 14,000, closing 15,600; NCI equity opening 2,200, closing 2,450; EBIT 3,600; borrowings 4,800; cash 1,100; market cap 42,000; fair value of NCI 5,000. Compute ROE, P/E and EV/EBIT on a consistent basis (EBITDA not needed).

💡 Hint: Decide for each ratio whether the claimants in the numerator and denominator include NCI.

Q3. Negative NCI [L2]

A 60%-owned subsidiary has accumulated losses such that NCI is a debit balance of ₹45 crore. Explain how it is presented, how it affects owners' equity and ROE, and whether you would adjust it in an EV bridge.
