Key concept

Equity-Accounted Profit, EBITDA and ROCE

Concept: Equity-accounted share of profit sits below EBIT; exclude from EBITDA and ROCE numerator unless investment included in capital

Statement

Share of profit or loss of associates and joint ventures accounted for using the equity method is a single post-tax line. For analysis:

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{core}} \text{ excludes } \text{Share of profit of associates/JVs} \]

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT} \left(+ \text{Share of profit of associates/JVs}\right)}{\text{Capital employed} \left(+ \text{Carrying amount of equity-accounted investments}\right)} \]

where the bracketed terms are included together or not at all.

Explanation

Schedule III Division II does not prescribe where the share of profit of associates/JVs goes; Indian companies usually present it after exceptional items and before tax, or immediately before profit before tax, as "Share of net profit/(loss) of associates and joint ventures accounted using equity method". Because it is already after the investee's interest, depreciation and tax, adding it into EBITDA mixes a post-tax, post-interest number with a pre-tax, pre-interest one. It also has no cash counterpart — the cash received is the dividend, shown in investing.

Consistency rules:

  • EBITDA and EV/EBITDA: exclude the share of profit; add the investment (at market or fair value) to the EV-to-equity bridge as a non-operating asset (t3).
  • ROCE: either exclude both the share of profit and the investment from capital employed (pure operating ROCE), or include both (total ROCE). Including capital but not return depresses ROCE; including return but not capital inflates it.
  • Tax: when computing effective tax rate, exclude the share of profit from PBT, since tax on it is inside the investee (Ind AS 12 rate reconciliations usually show it as a reconciling item).
  • Strategic JVs: where the JV is integral (e.g. a captive manufacturing JV supplying the parent), analysts may proportionately include its EBITDA and debt for credit analysis — disclose this as a non-GAAP choice.

Illustration

Retailer (₹ crore): EBIT 1,600; share of profit of JVs 140; capital employed (operating) 6,400; investments in JVs (carrying amount) 1,100.

Basis Numerator Denominator ROCE
Operating ROCE 1,600 6,400 25.0%
Total ROCE (both included) 1,740 7,500 23.2%
Inconsistent: return excluded, capital included 1,600 7,500 21.3%
Inconsistent: return included, capital excluded 1,740 6,400 27.2%

A company like Trent, with material equity-accounted investees, shows how the choice moves ROCE by several percentage points.

Practice Questions

Q1. EBITDA definition [L1]

A company's P&L shows PBT ₹920 crore after share of profit of associates ₹75 crore, finance cost ₹110 crore, D&A ₹260 crore and other income ₹90 crore. Compute core EBITDA and explain why the associate share is excluded.

Q2. Consistent ROCE [L2]

EBIT ₹2,250 crore; share of JV profit ₹180 crore; JV dividends received ₹60 crore; operating capital employed ₹9,800 crore; investment in JVs ₹1,600 crore (market value of the JV stake estimated at ₹3,000 crore). Compute operating and total ROCE, and state which you would use to compare with a peer that owns all its businesses outright.

💡 Hint: Peer comparability depends on whether the peer's equivalent business is inside its EBIT.

Q3. Look-through EBITDA [L3]

Using Ind AS 112 summarised financials of a 50% JV (revenue ₹3,200 crore, EBITDA ₹380 crore, net debt ₹900 crore), show how a credit analyst would construct proportionate EBITDA and net debt for the parent, and discuss whether this is appropriate if the JV's debt is non-recourse.
