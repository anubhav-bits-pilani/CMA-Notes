Key concept

Material Subsidiary under LODR Regulation 16(1)(c)

Concept: LODR Reg 16(1)(c): material subsidiary = income or net worth exceeding 10% of consolidated figures

Statement

Under Regulation 16(1)(c) of SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015, a material subsidiary is a subsidiary whose income or net worth exceeds 10% of the consolidated income or consolidated net worth, respectively, of the listed entity and its subsidiaries in the immediately preceding accounting year.

\[ \text{Material if } \frac{\text{Income}_{\text{sub}}}{\text{Income}_{\text{consolidated}}} > 10\% \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{Net worth}_{\text{sub}}}{\text{Net worth}_{\text{consolidated}}} > 10\% \]

with both measured on the preceding year's audited figures.

Explanation

The threshold (reduced from 20% to 10% by the 2018 amendments following the Kotak Committee) triggers governance obligations under Regulation 24 and related provisions:

  • Board representation (Reg 24(1)): at least one independent director of the listed holding company must be on the board of an unlisted material subsidiary, Indian or foreign. For this sub-regulation, a material subsidiary uses a 20% income or net worth threshold.
  • Disposal restrictions (Reg 24(5)–(6)): reducing shareholding below 50% (or ceasing control) of a material subsidiary, or selling/leasing more than 20% of its assets in a year, requires a special resolution of the listed entity's shareholders, subject to exceptions (e.g. under a resolution plan under the IBC).
  • Secretarial audit (Reg 24A): unlisted material subsidiaries incorporated in India must undergo secretarial audit and the report is annexed to the listed entity's annual report.
  • Policy: the listed entity must frame a policy for determining material subsidiaries and disclose it on its website (Reg 16(1)(c) and Reg 46).
  • Audit committee and board oversight of unlisted subsidiaries' financial statements, investments and significant transactions (Reg 24(2)–(4)).
  • Related party transactions of subsidiaries also fall within the LODR Reg 23 framework.

Analytical relevance: the list of material subsidiaries tells you which entities management and regulators consider significant — a starting point for subsidiary-level analysis (s03), and for valuing groups where one subsidiary drives disproportionate value or risk. A subsidiary crossing the threshold also increases governance cost and scrutiny.

Illustration

FY2024-25 consolidated (₹ crore): total income 52,000; net worth 18,000. Unlisted subsidiaries:

Subsidiary Income Net worth Income % NW % Material (10%)? Reg 24(1) (20%)?
S1 6,100 1,200 11.7% 6.7% Yes No
S2 2,400 4,000 4.6% 22.2% Yes Yes
S3 4,800 1,500 9.2% 8.3% No No

For FY2025-26, S1 and S2 are material subsidiaries; S2 requires an independent director of the listed parent on its board; both need secretarial audit if incorporated in India.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify material subsidiaries [L1]

Consolidated FY2024-25: total income ₹9,500 crore, net worth ₹4,200 crore. Subsidiaries: A (income ₹1,020 crore, NW ₹300 crore); B (income ₹600 crore, NW ₹880 crore); C (income ₹400 crore, NW ₹390 crore). Identify material subsidiaries for FY2025-26 and those requiring an independent director under Reg 24(1).

Q2. Disposal approval [L2]

A listed company plans to sell 30% of its 70% stake in an unlisted material subsidiary to a PE investor, retaining 49%. Explain the LODR approvals required, the accounting consequence under Ind AS 110 (loss of control) and the normalisation treatment of any gain.

💡 Hint: Check whether shareholding falls below 50% and whether control is lost; both trigger different consequences.

Q3. Threshold design [L2]

Explain why the materiality test uses the preceding year's consolidated figures and two alternative measures, and describe a situation in which a loss-making subsidiary could still be material.
