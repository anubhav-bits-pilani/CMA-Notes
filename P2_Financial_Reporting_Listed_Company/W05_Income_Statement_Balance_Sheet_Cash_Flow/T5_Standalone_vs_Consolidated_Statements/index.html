Roadmap topic: Standalone vs Consolidated Financial Statements: Analyzing subsidiary performance, dividend upstreaming, and parent-level debt vs consolidated leverage

Overview

Every Indian company with subsidiaries publishes two parallel sets of numbers — standalone (separate) financial statements under Ind AS 27 and consolidated financial statements under Ind AS 110 and Sec 129(3) — and listed companies repeat this every quarter under Reg 33 LODR. The two sets answer different questions. Consolidated statements describe the economic group: total revenue, EBITDA, debt and the return on all capital deployed. Standalone statements describe the legal entity that issues the listed shares and much of the debt: its distributable profits, its ability to pay dividends and service its own borrowings, and its exposure through investments, loans and guarantees to subsidiaries.

For analysts and FP&A professionals the gap between the two is where the insight lies. A group can look healthy consolidated while the parent depends on dividends that subsidiary lenders can block; a parent can look lightly levered standalone while guaranteeing most of the group's debt; consolidated profit can rise while owners' profit stalls because growth sits in partly owned subsidiaries. Credit ratings, holdco valuations, SOTP analyses, M&A diligence and group treasury planning all require reading both sets together. For this week's exercise, Titan (with CaratLane and TEAL as subsidiaries) and Trent (with equity-accounted JVs/associates) show the two main patterns: a group whose consolidated numbers include subsidiaries line by line, and one whose significant businesses sit below EBIT as single-line equity-accounted results.

The learning sequence begins with which entities are consolidated and how (s01: control, significant influence, joint arrangements), and how investments are carried in the parent's own books (s02: cost, fair value, impairment). Next comes extracting entity-level data (s03: AOC-1, Ind AS 112, NCI) and quantifying subsidiary contribution (c01) and NCI (c02). Then the focus shifts to cash and risk at the parent: dividend upstreaming (s04), parent-level debt, structural subordination and guarantees (s05), company-law constraints (c04 Sec 186, c05 layering) and governance of material subsidiaries (c06). Eliminations (s06) explain why the pieces do not add up, and equity-accounted results need consistent treatment in EBITDA and ROCE (c03). Finally, quarterly Reg 33 data and the holding-company discount (s07) connect the analysis to market valuation.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 103 business combinations, goodwill and PPA

Ind AS 103 business combinations, goodwill and PPA → Week 22: M&A accounting

Consolidation begins when control is obtained, and that date triggers acquisition accounting under Ind AS 103: identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value, NCI at fair value or proportionate share, goodwill as the residual, and bargain purchase gains to capital reserve (an Ind AS carve-out). The purchase price allocation creates intangibles (brands, customer relationships) amortised only in consolidated statements, which is why consolidated PAT can be lower than the sum of entity profits, and why "PPA amortisation" is a common normalisation add-back. Goodwill is tested for impairment at CGU level in consolidated statements, while the parent tests its investment in the subsidiary in standalone statements (s02) — two different tests on the same economic exposure. Week 22 covers step acquisitions, common-control mergers (Appendix C) and PPA mechanics.

Sum-of-the-parts valuation

Sum-of-the-parts valuation for conglomerates and listed subsidiaries → Week 16

SOTP values each business or entity separately — by DCF or by multiples appropriate to its sector — and sums the parent's share, deducting holdco debt and applying discounts for listed stakes or holdco structures. It relies directly on this topic: entity-level data from AOC-1 and Ind AS 112 (s03), owners' vs NCI shares (c02), separation of equity-accounted stakes (c03), holdco net debt and guarantees (s05) and the holding-company discount (s07). Typical Indian applications include conglomerates (Reliance, Tata, Aditya Birla), parents with listed subsidiaries (Bajaj Finserv, M&M) and consumer groups with separate digital or international businesses. Errors usually come from double counting cash, ignoring NCI in listed subsidiaries or using consolidated debt in the holdco layer.

IFRS 10 vs US GAAP ASC 810

IFRS 10 identical to Ind AS 110; US GAAP ASC 810 VIE model differs for structured entities

Ind AS 110 is substantively identical to IFRS 10: a single control model (power, variable returns, link) applies to all entities, including structured entities. US GAAP ASC 810 uses two models: the voting interest model for most entities and the variable interest entity (VIE) model, under which the primary beneficiary — the party with power over the most significant activities and the obligation to absorb losses or right to receive benefits that could be significant — consolidates. Results usually converge, but can differ for securitisation vehicles, franchise structures, R&D partnerships and entities with kick-out or participating rights. When comparing an Indian group with a US peer, check whether off-balance-sheet entities under one framework are consolidated under the other, particularly in financing, leasing and franchise-heavy businesses.

Common pitfalls

Using standalone statements where value or debt sits in subsidiaries

Using standalone statements for a group where key profit or debt sits in subsidiaries

Mistake: analysing the standalone P&L and balance sheet of a group whose growth businesses, international operations or debt sit in subsidiaries — for instance, using standalone results for a company whose digital subsidiary or overseas acquisition is a large part of value. Why it happens: standalone results are reported first in Reg 33 filings and data vendors sometimes default to them. Correct treatment: start from consolidated statements for valuation, leverage and returns; use standalone for distributable profits, holdco debt service and legal-entity credit analysis; reconcile using AOC-1 and c01. Detection: compare standalone and consolidated revenue, PAT and borrowings — differences above roughly 10–15%, or any material subsidiary under Reg 16(1)(c), mean the standalone view is incomplete.

Consolidated ROE with total PAT over owners' equity

Computing consolidated ROE with total PAT (incl. NCI) over owners' equity

Mistake: dividing consolidated PAT, which includes NCI's share, by equity attributable to owners, which excludes NCI. Why it happens: the PAT line above the attribution is the most visible figure, and vendors label it "net profit". Correct treatment: ROE = PAT attributable to owners ÷ average equity attributable to owners; EPS uses the same numerator (Ind AS 33.10). Detection: recompute EPS from your numerator and compare with reported basic EPS — if your EPS is higher, you have included NCI. Groups with large partly owned subsidiaries (e.g. 51–75% stakes) show the largest distortion.

Ignoring parent guarantees in standalone leverage

Ignoring guarantees given by the parent when assessing standalone leverage

Mistake: concluding a parent is conservatively levered from its standalone borrowings while it has guaranteed large subsidiary loans. Why it happens: guarantees appear only in contingent liabilities and Sec 186 disclosures, and the financial guarantee liability recognised under Ind AS 109 is small relative to the exposure. Correct treatment: add guaranteed amounts (outstanding, not just sanctioned) to standalone debt for a guarantee-adjusted leverage ratio, or move to consolidated leverage when guarantees cover most subsidiary debt; read cross-default clauses. Detection: check the contingent liabilities note, Sec 186(4) disclosure, related party note and rating rationales, which usually describe the support structure.

Practice Questions

Q1. Integrated case: holdco credit analysis [L3]

Parent P (listed) has standalone EBITDA ₹700 crore, borrowings ₹2,500 crore, cash ₹200 crore, and has guaranteed ₹1,800 crore of loans of its 100% subsidiary S1. S1: EBITDA ₹650 crore, external debt ₹3,000 crore, covenant permits dividends only if net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.5x. S2 (60% owned, listed): EBITDA ₹900 crore, debt ₹600 crore, cash ₹800 crore, PAT ₹500 crore, payout 40%. Parent's free reserves ₹4,500 crore, securities premium ₹1,000 crore, paid-up capital ₹300 crore; existing loans/guarantees/investments for Sec 186 ₹4,800 crore. (a) Compute consolidated, standalone and guarantee-adjusted net debt/EBITDA. (b) Estimate cash upstreamable to P in the year and P's interest cover at 9% on its borrowings. (c) Determine whether a new ₹500 crore guarantee for S1 needs a special resolution. (d) Recommend which leverage view a parent NCD investor should rely on.

Q2. Integrated case: splitting group performance [L2]

Consolidated FY2025 (₹ crore): revenue 24,000; EBITDA 3,100; PAT 1,900 (NCI 150); share of profit of JVs 120. Standalone: revenue 20,500; EBITDA 2,700; PAT 1,850, including dividends from subsidiaries ₹260 crore and impairment of investment in a subsidiary ₹180 crore (exceptional). Parent's sales to subsidiaries ₹900 crore with unrealised profit at year-end ₹30 crore (pre-tax). (a) Estimate subsidiaries' revenue, EBITDA and owners' PAT contribution. (b) Compute consolidated ROE if owners' equity averaged ₹12,500 crore, and the ROE you would get by the common mistake. (c) Explain how the impairment is treated in consolidation.

💡 Hint: Work line by line from consolidated minus standalone, then add back what consolidation removed and take out what belongs to JVs.

Q3. Integrated case: Titan vs Trent comparability [L3]

Using the latest three annual reports of Titan Company and Trent, prepare: (a) a table of each group's entities classified as subsidiary, associate, JV or joint operation, with ownership; (b) consolidated EBITDA and ROCE excluding equity-accounted results; (c) look-through EBITDA and net debt including the parent's share of equity-accounted investees using Ind AS 112 data; (d) a commentary on how much of each group's growth and debt sits outside the parent entity, and which set of statements should anchor the comparison.

Q4. Integrated case: holdco valuation and quarterly signals [L2]

A holdco reports Reg 33 standalone Q1 PAT of ₹420 crore (mainly dividends) and consolidated Q1 PAT attributable to owners of ₹1,150 crore. It owns 45% of a listed OpCo (market cap ₹80,000 crore) and 100% of an unlisted NBFC (fair value ₹6,000 crore; RBI-linked dividend payout cap 20%). Holdco debt ₹1,500 crore; cash ₹700 crore; holdco market cap ₹21,000 crore. (a) Compute look-through NAV and the holdco discount. (b) Explain which quarterly figures are relevant for the holdco's dividend capacity versus its intrinsic value. (c) Identify whether the NBFC is a material subsidiary and what governance obligations follow, if consolidated net worth is ₹38,000 crore and the NBFC's net worth ₹5,200 crore.
