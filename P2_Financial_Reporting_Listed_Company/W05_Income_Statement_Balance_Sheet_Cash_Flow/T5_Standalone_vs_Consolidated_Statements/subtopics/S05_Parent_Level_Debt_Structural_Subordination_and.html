Subtopic note

Parent-Level Debt, Structural Subordination and Guarantees

Scope: Parent-level debt vs consolidated leverage; structural subordination; corporate guarantees given to subsidiaries

Why it matters

Consolidated net debt/EBITDA is the default leverage metric, but it answers only one question: how indebted is the group as a whole? Lenders to the parent and lenders to each subsidiary have claims on different pools of assets and cash flows. A parent with little standalone debt can be highly exposed through guarantees given to subsidiary lenders; a parent with heavy standalone debt may depend on dividends from subsidiaries whose own lenders rank first. Rating agencies in India (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings) explicitly choose between consolidated, standalone and "notch-up/notch-down" approaches based on these linkages, and SEBI LODR requires disclosure of guarantees and related-party exposures. The standalone vs consolidated debt comparison is a standard step in this week's checklist.

Core notes

Three views of leverage

View Debt EBITDA / cash flow Use
Consolidated All group borrowings (incl. lease liabilities per policy) Consolidated EBITDA Group capacity, EV bridge
Standalone parent Parent's own borrowings Parent's EBITDA + upstreamable dividends Parent creditor analysis, holdco
Proportionate / look-through Parent's share of each entity's debt Parent's share of EBITDA Groups with large NCI or JVs

Divergence between standalone and consolidated leverage signals where risk sits:

\[ \text{Subsidiaries' external debt} \approx \text{Consolidated borrowings} - \text{Standalone borrowings} \]

\[ \text{Subsidiaries' total debt (own books)} \approx \text{Subsidiaries' external debt} + \text{Loans from parent (eliminated)} \]

Loans from the parent are an asset in the parent's standalone balance sheet and are eliminated in consolidation, so the borrowings difference captures only third-party debt of subsidiaries (assuming the parent has not borrowed from its subsidiaries). The subsidiaries' own covenants, however, are tested on their total debt including parent loans unless those loans are subordinated.

Structural subordination

Creditors of a subsidiary have a direct claim on the subsidiary's assets and cash flows. The parent's creditors have only a claim on the parent's equity in the subsidiary — residual after the subsidiary's creditors are paid. Hence parent debt is structurally subordinated to subsidiary debt, even if both are "senior secured" in their own documents. Consequences:

  • In distress, subsidiary lenders are paid first from subsidiary assets; parent lenders receive only the residual equity value (and only the parent's share, net of NCI).
  • Subsidiary covenants can block dividends that the parent needs to service its own debt (see s04).
  • Mitigants: parent guarantees from subsidiaries (upstream guarantees, limited by Sec 185/186), pledge of subsidiary shares, cash pooling, intra-group loans that rank pari passu with external subsidiary debt.

Corporate guarantees given to subsidiaries

Indian parents routinely guarantee subsidiaries' bank loans and NCDs. Accounting and disclosure:

  • Ind AS 109: a financial guarantee contract is recognised initially at fair value (Ind AS 109.4.2.1(c)) and subsequently at the higher of the ECL allowance and the amount initially recognised less cumulative income recognised. If no fee is charged, the fair value is debited to investment in subsidiary (deemed capital contribution) in the parent's standalone statements.
  • Contingent liabilities note (Ind AS 37 and Schedule III): guarantees outstanding are usually disclosed; read the amount guaranteed (sanction) and the amount outstanding (utilised).
  • Sec 186 limits and disclosure of loans, guarantees and investments in the financial statements (Sec 186(4)); Reg 23 LODR and Sec 188 related party approvals.
  • In consolidated statements, the guarantee is eliminated — the guaranteed loan is already consolidated debt.

Journal entry: guarantee without fee (standalone, ₹ crore)

Parent guarantees a ₹500 crore subsidiary loan; fair value of the guarantee (interest differential) is ₹15 crore.

Investment in subsidiary (deemed equity contribution) 15

Over the loan tenure, the liability is amortised to "other income — guarantee commission" (non-cash, should be excluded from normalised EBITDA).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Letters of comfort / support undertakings are not legally guarantees but create constructive or reputational obligations; rating agencies often treat strong comfort letters like guarantees.
  • Cross-default clauses: a default by a subsidiary can accelerate parent debt; read borrowing notes for cross-default and change-of-control clauses.
  • Ring-fenced subsidiaries (regulated NBFCs, project SPVs with non-recourse debt) may justify excluding their debt from parent leverage — but only if truly non-recourse.
  • Pledge of subsidiary shares by the parent (or by promoters, disclosed under SEBI SAST Reg 31) adds hidden leverage at the promoter level.
  • Cash at subsidiaries with NCI or restrictions should not be netted against parent debt (see t3 pitfall).
  • Lease liabilities at retail subsidiaries add to consolidated but not standalone debt; align definitions.

Key takeaways

  • Consolidated, standalone and look-through leverage answer different questions; compute all three for holdcos.
  • Parent creditors are structurally subordinated to subsidiary creditors.
  • Guarantees to subsidiaries are financial guarantee contracts under Ind AS 109 and contingent exposures that must be added to standalone leverage.
  • Subsidiaries' external debt ≈ consolidated borrowings − standalone borrowings; add eliminated parent loans to get their total debt for covenant purposes.
  • Read cross-default, pledge and comfort-letter disclosures before concluding on parent risk.

Practice Questions

Q1. Locate subsidiary debt [L1]

Consolidated borrowings ₹6,400 crore; standalone borrowings ₹2,100 crore; parent's loans to subsidiaries ₹900 crore (eliminated). Estimate the subsidiaries' external debt and their total debt in their own books, and explain why the parent's loans affect one figure but not the other.

Q2. Guarantee accounting [L2]

On 1 April 2024 a parent guarantees a 5-year ₹800 crore term loan of its wholly owned subsidiary without charging a fee. The subsidiary would have paid 1.0% p.a. more without the guarantee. Discount rate 9%. Compute the initial fair value of the guarantee, pass the standalone journal entries for initial recognition and FY2024-25, and state the treatment in consolidated statements.

💡 Hint: The fair value can be approximated as the present value of the interest differential over the loan's outstanding balance.

Q3. Leverage under three views [L2]

Group data (₹ crore): parent EBITDA 1,100, debt 2,000; Sub A (100%) EBITDA 600, debt 2,400 (guaranteed by parent); Sub B (51%) EBITDA 400, debt 1,000 (non-recourse); cash at parent 300, at Sub B 500. Compute consolidated, standalone, guarantee-adjusted standalone and proportionate net debt/EBITDA, and explain which view a parent NCD investor should use.

Q4. Case: structural subordination in distress [L3]

A listed infrastructure holdco has ₹4,000 crore of NCDs; its operating subsidiaries have ₹12,000 crore of project debt secured on assets. Subsidiaries' enterprise value in a distressed sale is ₹13,500 crore and the holdco owns 74% of each. Estimate the recovery for holdco NCD holders and discuss how share pledges, cross-default and guarantees would change the analysis.
