Subtopic note

Reg 33 Standalone and Consolidated Results and the Holdco Discount

Scope: Reg 33 LODR quarterly standalone and consolidated results; holding-company discount in valuation

Why it matters

Quarterly financial results under Regulation 33 of SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015 are the most frequent and most market-moving financial disclosures of Indian listed companies. Analysts update models, compute LTM metrics and compare guidance every quarter from them, long before the annual report arrives. Because listed companies must publish both standalone and consolidated results, the difference between them is visible every quarter — and for holding companies, the gap between the market value of their listed stakes and their own market capitalisation (the holding-company discount) is a recurring valuation question in Indian markets (Bajaj Holdings, Tata Investment, Kama Holdings and many promoter holdcos).

Core notes

Reg 33 requirements (key elements)

  • Frequency and timing: quarterly and year-to-date results within 45 days of quarter-end; annual audited results within 60 days of financial year-end (Reg 33(3)). The Q4 figures are typically derived as balancing figures between audited full-year and published nine-month figures.
  • Standalone and consolidated: listed entities with subsidiaries submit quarterly consolidated results along with standalone results (made mandatory from FY2019-20). They may opt to publish both in newspapers (Reg 47); stock exchange filings carry both.
  • Format: the SEBI-prescribed format follows Schedule III Division II lines (revenue, other income, expenses by nature, exceptional items, tax, PAT, OCI, paid-up capital, EPS), plus segment information (Reg 33 read with the master circular) and, for half-years, a statement of assets and liabilities and a cash flow statement.
  • Assurance: limited review by the statutory auditor for quarterly results; audit report for annual results. For consolidated results, the auditor's review must cover at least 80% of consolidated revenue, assets and profits (SEBI circular requirement).
  • Audit qualifications: a statement on impact of audit qualifications (modified opinions) is filed with annual results.
  • Ind AS 34 principles (interim reporting) underpin recognition and measurement, though Reg 33 results are not a full Ind AS 34 interim report.

Using quarterly results analytically

Use Method
LTM metrics Last four quarters of consolidated results; or FY + YTD current − YTD prior
Subsidiary contribution trend Consolidated − standalone by quarter (approximate, see c01)
Seasonality Q3 (Oct–Dec) festive quarter for jewellery and apparel; compare YoY quarters, not sequential
Early warning Sudden divergence between standalone and consolidated losses (subsidiary stress)
Balance sheet checks Half-yearly statement of assets and liabilities (30 September) for working-capital trends

Q4 caution: being derived, Q4 often absorbs annual true-ups (inventory adjustments, provisions, tax), making it less comparable.

Holding-company discount

A holdco's market capitalisation is often below the market value of its stakes (look-through or sum-of-the-parts value). The discount is:

\[ \text{Holdco discount} = 1 - \frac{\text{Holdco market capitalisation}}{\text{Look-through NAV}} \]

\[ \text{Look-through NAV} = \sum_i (\text{Stake}_i \times \text{Market value}_i) + \text{Unlisted investments at fair value} + \text{Cash} - \text{Holdco debt} - \text{Holdco liabilities} \]

Reasons for the discount (Indian context, typical range 40–70% for pure holdcos, lower for operating parents):

  • Tax leakage: capital gains on eventual sale (long-term capital gains tax for listed shares), and dividend tax for shareholders without s.80M benefit at the individual level.
  • Lack of control and liquidity: holdco shareholders cannot access underlying assets; low free float.
  • Holdco costs: overheads, double layer of management.
  • Capital allocation risk: cash may be reinvested in low-return or related-party ventures.
  • Governance: promoter-controlled holdcos with no intention to distribute or unlock value.
  • Regulatory constraints: core investment company norms (RBI) for holdcos meeting CIC criteria.

Discount narrowing catalysts: buybacks, higher dividend payout, mergers of holdco into operating company, listing of subsidiaries (unlocking), demergers.

For an operating parent with listed subsidiaries (e.g. a parent whose subsidiary is separately listed), analysts apply a smaller discount (often 15–30%) to the listed stake in a sum-of-the-parts valuation (Week 16).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Restated quarters: results of earlier quarters may be restated for mergers (common-control schemes approved by NCLT with appointed dates in the past) or discontinued operations; re-pull comparatives.
  • Consolidated results excluding new acquisitions in the quarter of acquisition — check the notes for the date of control.
  • Reg 33 vs annual report differences: post-results adjustments (events after the reporting period) can make annual report figures differ from published annual results; the annual report prevails.
  • Holdcos with operating businesses: blend SOTP for the operating business with discounted stakes.
  • Treasury shares or cross-holdings inflate look-through NAV unless netted.
  • Tax on unrealised gains: deduct deferred capital gains tax explicitly rather than embedding it in an arbitrary discount.

Key takeaways

  • Reg 33 requires quarterly standalone and consolidated results within 45 days (60 days for annual), with limited review/audit, segment data and half-yearly balance sheet and cash flow.
  • Quarterly consolidated-minus-standalone gives a fast but approximate subsidiary trend.
  • Q4 figures are balancing figures and absorb annual true-ups; use YoY comparisons.
  • Holdco discount = 1 − market cap ÷ look-through NAV; driven by tax, control, liquidity, costs and governance.
  • In SOTP valuations, apply discounts to listed stakes explicitly and justify them.

Practice Questions

Q1. Timeline and content [L1]

For a listed company with a 31 March year-end and four subsidiaries, list the Reg 33 filing deadlines for Q1, H1, nine months and full year FY2025-26, and state which periods require a statement of assets and liabilities and a cash flow statement.

Q2. Deriving Q4 and LTM [L2]

Consolidated revenue (₹ crore): FY2024-25 audited 18,400; 9M FY2024-25 13,300; Q1 FY2025-26 4,900; Q1 FY2024-25 4,200. Compute Q4 FY2024-25 revenue and LTM revenue at 30 June 2025, and explain why Q4 margin may not be comparable with Q3.

💡 Hint: LTM equals the last full year plus the latest quarter less the corresponding quarter of the prior year.

Q3. Holdco discount [L2]

A holdco owns 40% of a listed company with market cap ₹45,000 crore and 25% of another with market cap ₹16,000 crore, plus cash of ₹1,200 crore and debt of ₹800 crore. Its own market cap is ₹8,900 crore. Compute the discount, then recompute it after deducting deferred capital gains tax at 12.5% on unrealised gains of ₹18,000 crore. Discuss which number is more meaningful.

Q4. Case: unlocking value [L3]

A promoter-controlled holdco trades at a 62% discount to look-through NAV. Evaluate three corporate actions — a buyback funded by selling 5% of a listed stake, a merger of the holdco into its main operating subsidiary, and raising dividend payout to 90% of dividend income — from the perspectives of minority shareholders, the promoter and taxation.
