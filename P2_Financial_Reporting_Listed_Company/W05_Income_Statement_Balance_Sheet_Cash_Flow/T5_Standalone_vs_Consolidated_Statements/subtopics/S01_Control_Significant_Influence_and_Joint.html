Subtopic note

Control, Significant Influence and Joint Arrangements

Scope: Ind AS 110 control (power, variable returns, link) vs Ind AS 28 equity method for associates/JVs and Ind AS 111 joint arrangements

Why it matters

Whether an investee is consolidated line by line, equity-accounted as a single line, or accounted for as a share of assets and liabilities changes almost every ratio an analyst computes: revenue, EBITDA, margins, net debt, ROCE and asset turnover. Two Indian groups owning similar businesses can look completely different because one controls its retail JV while the other has joint control. Trent, for example, equity-accounts Trent Hypermarket (the Star grocery business, a JV with Tesco) and its 49% investment in Inditex Trent Retail India (Zara), so their revenue and debt sit outside consolidated lines, while Titan consolidates CaratLane as a subsidiary. Before comparing groups, you must know which accounting model applies to each investee and why.

Core notes

Ind AS 110: the control model

An investor controls an investee when it has all three elements (Ind AS 110.7):

  1. Power over the investee — existing rights giving the current ability to direct the relevant activities (those that significantly affect returns), Ind AS 110.10–14. Rights can be voting rights, potential voting rights that are substantive (convertible instruments, options), contractual rights (management agreements), or de facto power through a large minority holding with dispersed other holders (Ind AS 110.B41–B46).
  2. Exposure, or rights, to variable returns — dividends, changes in value, fees, tax benefits, synergies (Ind AS 110.15–16).
  3. Link between power and returns — the ability to use power to affect the amount of returns (Ind AS 110.17–18); this is where the principal vs agent analysis (B58–B72) sits, e.g. an asset manager managing a fund.

Control is reassessed when facts change (Ind AS 110.8). Protective rights (vetoes over fundamental changes, lender covenants) do not give power. Consolidation results in 100% of assets, liabilities, income and expenses with NCI shown separately (Ind AS 110.22, B86).

Ind AS 28: significant influence and the equity method

Significant influence is the power to participate in financial and operating policy decisions without control. It is presumed at 20% or more of voting power and presumed absent below 20%, both rebuttable (Ind AS 28.5). Indicators include board representation, participation in policy-making, material transactions, interchange of managerial personnel and provision of essential technical information (Ind AS 28.6).

Under the equity method (Ind AS 28.10): initial cost, then adjusted for the investor's share of post-acquisition profit or loss (P&L) and OCI, reduced by dividends received. Goodwill is embedded in the carrying amount, not separately tested; the whole investment is tested for impairment under Ind AS 36 when Ind AS 28.41A–41C indicators exist. Losses beyond the carrying amount are recognised only to the extent of legal or constructive obligations (Ind AS 28.38–39). Unrealised profits on upstream and downstream transactions are eliminated to the extent of the investor's interest (Ind AS 28.28).

Ind AS 111: joint arrangements

Joint control is the contractually agreed sharing of control, existing only when decisions about relevant activities require unanimous consent of the parties sharing control (Ind AS 111.7). Joint arrangements are classified as:

Type Characteristic Accounting
Joint operation Parties have rights to assets and obligations for liabilities (e.g. unincorporated consortia, oil blocks, some contract-manufacturing JVs whose output is taken entirely by parties) Recognise own share of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses (Ind AS 111.20)
Joint venture Parties have rights to the net assets of the arrangement Equity method under Ind AS 28 (Ind AS 111.24)

Classification considers the legal form (a separate vehicle usually points to JV), contractual terms and other facts and circumstances such as whether output is sold only to the parties (Ind AS 111.B15–B33). Proportionate consolidation is not permitted for JVs.

Decision tree

Question Yes No
Does the investor alone have power, variable returns and the link? Subsidiary — consolidate Next
Is control shared contractually with unanimous consent? Joint arrangement — classify JO/JV Next
Does the investor have significant influence? Associate — equity method Financial asset — Ind AS 109 (FVTPL or FVOCI election)

Analytical impact — illustration (₹ crore)

Parent P owns 51% of S, which has revenue 1,000, EBITDA 150, net debt 400 and PAT 60. Compare consolidation with the equity method if P had only 50% with joint control:

Metric in P's consolidated statements S as subsidiary (51%) S as JV (50%)
Revenue +1,000 0
EBITDA +150 0
Net debt +400 0
Share of profit of JV (below EBIT) — +30
PAT attributable to owners +30.6 +30
NCI share of PAT +29.4 —

A one-percentage-point difference in stake changes reported revenue by ₹1,000 crore and net debt by ₹400 crore, with almost no change in owners' PAT.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Control with less than 50%: de facto control via large dispersed shareholding, or contractual control through shareholder agreements (board majority, casting vote). Indian promoter groups often hold 40–49% with management agreements.
  • No control above 50%: substantive participating rights of a minority (e.g. a private equity investor's veto over budgets and key appointments) may mean joint control.
  • Potential voting rights: CCPS, warrants and call options count only if substantive (exercisable, in the money or otherwise economically beneficial).
  • Structured entities and trusts: ESOP trusts are generally consolidated by the sponsoring company; securitisation trusts of NBFCs require a control assessment.
  • Changes in status: losing control triggers remeasurement of the retained interest at fair value with gain in P&L (Ind AS 110.25); obtaining control of an associate is a step acquisition under Ind AS 103.
  • Venture capital organisations, mutual funds and similar entities may elect FVTPL for associates and JVs (Ind AS 28.18).

Key takeaways

  • Control requires power, exposure to variable returns and the link between them; percentage ownership is only evidence.
  • Associates (significant influence, presumption at 20%) and JVs (joint control, rights to net assets) are equity-accounted as a single line.
  • Joint operations are accounted for line by line for the party's share; JVs never use proportionate consolidation.
  • Accounting status drives revenue, EBITDA, net debt and NCI; always read the "interest in other entities" note before comparing groups.
  • Status changes create remeasurement gains/losses that are non-recurring for normalisation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the investee [L1]

Classify each investee and state the accounting: (a) 45% stake, two other shareholders hold 30% and 25%, and a shareholder agreement gives the investor the right to appoint four of seven directors; (b) 60% stake, but the 40% holder's consent is needed for the annual budget, CEO appointment and capex above ₹10 crore; (c) 18% stake with two board seats and supply of key technology; (d) 50:50 incorporated JV whose entire output is purchased by the two parties at cost-plus prices.

Q2. Equity method roll-forward [L2]

P acquired 30% of A on 1 April 2024 for ₹420 crore when A's net assets at fair value were ₹1,200 crore. For FY2024-25, A reported PAT ₹150 crore, OCI loss ₹20 crore and paid dividends of ₹50 crore. P sold goods to A at a margin of ₹40 crore, of which 25% remained in A's inventory at 31 March 2025. Compute the carrying amount of the investment and the share of profit recognised.

💡 Hint: Identify embedded goodwill first, then eliminate the investor's share of the downstream unrealised profit.

Q3. Ratio impact of control [L2]

Using the illustration's data, compute P's consolidated net debt/EBITDA under both classifications if P's own EBITDA is ₹600 crore and net debt ₹900 crore. Explain why a rating agency might "proportionately consolidate" the JV for credit analysis even though Ind AS prohibits it.

Q4. Judgement: promoter group control [L3]

An Indian listed company owns 38% of a listed affiliate; the promoter family personally holds another 22%; the balance is widely held. The company's CMD is also chairman of the affiliate, and the two share a brand under a licence. Assess whether the company controls the affiliate under Ind AS 110 and what additional facts you need.
