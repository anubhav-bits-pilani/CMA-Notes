Subtopic note

Ind AS 27 Separate Statements and Investment Impairment

Scope: Ind AS 27 separate statements: investments in subsidiaries at cost or Ind AS 109 fair value; impairment of investments

Why it matters

Every Indian company with subsidiaries files two sets of statements: standalone (the "separate financial statements" under Ind AS 27) and consolidated (Sec 129(3), Companies Act 2013). Standalone statements determine distributable profits for dividends (Sec 123), the limits under Sec 186 and Sec 180, tax computations and many bank covenants. In them, a subsidiary appears only as an investment line — and how that line is measured, and when it is impaired, can produce large standalone losses (or gains) that have no counterpart in consolidated profit. Analysts who read standalone results for a holding company, or who see "exceptional impairment of investment in subsidiary" in Reg 33 results, must understand this mechanism.

Core notes

Measurement options (Ind AS 27.10)

In separate financial statements, investments in subsidiaries, joint ventures and associates are accounted for either:

Option Measurement Dividends from investee
(a) At cost Historical cost less impairment (Ind AS 36) Recognised in P&L when the right to receive is established (Ind AS 27.12)
(b) In accordance with Ind AS 109 Fair value (FVTPL, or FVOCI election for equity instruments) Recognised in P&L
(c) Equity method (as described in Ind AS 28) Option added to IAS 27 by the IASB in 2014 and reflected in Ind AS 27 as amended; rarely used in India Reduces carrying amount

The same accounting is applied to each category of investment (Ind AS 27.10). Indian companies overwhelmingly choose cost; confirm the option set in the Ind AS 27 text applicable to the year if a company claims to use the equity method in its separate statements. On first-time adoption, Ind AS 101.D15 allowed deemed cost equal to fair value or previous GAAP carrying amount at transition date — so some companies carry subsidiaries at transition-date fair value, inflating standalone net worth relative to historical cost.

Investments held for sale are measured under Ind AS 105. Other components: loans to subsidiaries at below-market interest create a "deemed investment" (difference between fair value and transaction value recognised as an addition to investment), and financial guarantees given free of charge for a subsidiary's loans create a deemed investment and a guarantee liability under Ind AS 109 (see s05).

Impairment of investments carried at cost

Investments in subsidiaries at cost are within the scope of Ind AS 36. Indicators (Ind AS 36.12), plus the specific dividend indicator of Ind AS 36.12(h):

  • carrying amount of the investment in separate statements exceeds the carrying amount of the investee's net assets (including goodwill) in consolidated statements; or
  • dividend exceeds total comprehensive income of the subsidiary in the period it is declared.

Recoverable amount = higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use (DCF of the subsidiary's expected cash flows, including dividends and terminal disposal). The impairment loss is recognised in standalone P&L, often presented as an exceptional item. Reversal is permitted if estimates change (Ind AS 36.110–117), up to original cost.

Standalone vs consolidated consequences

Event Standalone P&L Consolidated P&L
Subsidiary makes loss of ₹200 crore No effect (cost method) Full loss consolidated, NCI share separated
Impairment of investment in subsidiary ₹300 crore Loss (exceptional) Eliminated; instead goodwill/assets of subsidiary tested under Ind AS 36 at CGU level
Dividend from subsidiary ₹100 crore Income Eliminated
Sale of a subsidiary Gain = proceeds − cost of investment Gain = proceeds − share of net assets − goodwill + recycled OCI

The consolidated gain or loss on disposal therefore differs from the standalone one; both may be exceptional.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Parent invested ₹800 crore in S (100%) in FY2019. S's cumulative losses are ₹350 crore; its consolidated net assets including goodwill are ₹500 crore. Value in use of S's business is estimated at ₹560 crore.

Step Amount
Carrying amount of investment (cost) 800
Indicator: exceeds consolidated net assets (500) Yes
Recoverable amount (VIU) 560
Impairment loss in standalone P&L 240

Journal entry (standalone):

Account Dr Cr
Impairment loss on investment in subsidiary (exceptional item) 240
Provision for impairment / Investment in subsidiary 240

Consolidated: no ₹240 crore charge; the subsidiary's losses were already in consolidated profits over earlier years, and the CGU (with any goodwill) is tested separately.

Disclosures

Ind AS 27.16–17: list of significant investments, ownership interest, principal place of business and the method used. Schedule III Division II Note on non-current investments requires the aggregate amount of quoted and unquoted investments, market value of quoted investments and aggregate impairment.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Fair value option for holding companies: a holding company that elects Ind AS 109 FVTPL will show volatile standalone profits linked to subsidiaries' valuations; FVOCI keeps them out of P&L (no recycling).
  • Deemed cost at transition: subsidiaries carried at 2016–17 fair values may be impaired later even if the business performs at original-cost expectations.
  • Investments in preference shares or debentures of subsidiaries are financial assets under Ind AS 109 (not Ind AS 27) unless they are equity in substance — classification may create ECL or FVTPL effects.
  • Merger within the group (common control under Appendix C to Ind AS 103): investment cost is eliminated against the subsidiary's share capital; difference goes to capital reserve — not P&L.
  • Tax: impairment of investments is not deductible; a capital loss arises only on transfer.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 27 permits cost, Ind AS 109 fair value or the equity method for investments in separate statements; Indian companies mostly use cost.
  • Investments at cost are tested under Ind AS 36, with specific indicators linked to consolidated net assets and dividends exceeding total comprehensive income.
  • Standalone impairment losses and dividend income are eliminated in consolidation and should be normalised when analysing standalone earnings.
  • Deemed investments arise from concessional loans and free guarantees to subsidiaries.
  • Disposal gains differ between standalone and consolidated statements.

Practice Questions

Q1. Accounting option choice [L1]

Explain the three measurement options under Ind AS 27.10 for investments in subsidiaries in separate financial statements and state how a dividend from a subsidiary is treated under each.

Q2. Impairment test [L2]

A parent's standalone investment in a 100% overseas subsidiary is ₹1,250 crore (cost). In FY2024-25 the subsidiary declared a dividend of ₹90 crore against total comprehensive income of ₹40 crore. Consolidated net assets of the subsidiary (including goodwill of ₹200 crore) are ₹980 crore. Fair value less costs of disposal is ₹1,050 crore; value in use ₹1,120 crore. Determine whether an indicator exists, the impairment loss and the journal entry.

💡 Hint: Check both Ind AS 36.12(h) indicators, then compare carrying amount with the higher of the two recoverable measures.

Q3. Disposal gain in two sets of statements [L2]

P sells its 100% subsidiary S for ₹900 crore. Cost of investment in P's standalone books ₹500 crore. In consolidated statements, S's net assets are ₹620 crore, goodwill ₹80 crore and accumulated foreign currency translation reserve (credit) ₹30 crore. Compute the gain in standalone and consolidated P&L and explain the difference.

Q4. Analyst judgement on standalone results [L3]

A listed holding company's Q4 standalone results show PAT of ₹1,100 crore, of which ₹1,400 crore is dividend from subsidiaries and ₹600 crore is impairment of investment in a loss-making subsidiary (exceptional). Consolidated PAT is ₹950 crore. Explain how you would present "core standalone earnings" and which set of statements you would use for valuation and dividend-capacity analysis.
