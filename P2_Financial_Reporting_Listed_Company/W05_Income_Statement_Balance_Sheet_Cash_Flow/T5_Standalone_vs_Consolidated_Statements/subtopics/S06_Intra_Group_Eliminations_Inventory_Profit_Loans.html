Subtopic note

Intra-Group Eliminations: Inventory Profit, Loans, Fees and Royalties

Scope: Intra-group eliminations: unrealised profit in inventory, intercompany loans, management fees, royalties

Why it matters

Consolidated statements present the group as a single economic entity (Ind AS 110.B86), so transactions between group entities must be eliminated in full. For analysts, eliminations are why "standalone + subsidiaries" never equals consolidated, why subsidiary contribution estimates are only approximate (c01), and where groups can shift profits between entities — to a lower-tax jurisdiction, to an entity with NCI, or into inventory to defer recognition. For anyone preparing consolidations (a common FP&A and group reporting task), eliminations drive most of the reconciliation effort at quarter-end under Reg 33 timelines.

Core notes

The principle

Ind AS 110.B86(c): eliminate in full intra-group assets and liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows relating to transactions between entities of the group. Profits or losses resulting from intra-group transactions recognised in assets (inventory, PPE) are eliminated in full. Intra-group losses may indicate an impairment that requires recognition (Ind AS 110.B86(c)). Ind AS 12 applies to temporary differences arising from the elimination of unrealised profits — a deferred tax asset is usually recognised (at the buyer's tax rate, as the buyer will recover the asset).

Full elimination applies regardless of NCI. The allocation between owners and NCI depends on the direction:

Direction Elimination Allocation of unrealised profit
Downstream (parent → partly owned subsidiary) Full Entirely against owners of the parent
Upstream (partly owned subsidiary → parent) Full Shared between owners and NCI in ownership proportions
Associate/JV transactions Only investor's share (Ind AS 28.28) Investor's share

Unrealised profit in inventory

Parent P sells goods to 80%-owned subsidiary S at cost ₹400 crore plus 25% markup (₹500 crore). At year-end, S holds 30% of these goods. Unrealised profit = 30% × ₹100 crore = ₹30 crore; DTA at 25.168% = ₹7.55 crore.

The same logic applies to PPE transferred within the group (unrealised profit eliminated and depreciation adjusted over the remaining life).

Intercompany loans and interest

  • Loan receivable in lender and payable in borrower are eliminated; interest income and finance cost eliminated.
  • Foreign-currency intra-group loans: exchange differences do not eliminate because they arise in one entity's functional currency (Ind AS 21.45). If the loan is part of the net investment in a foreign operation, exchange differences go to OCI (FCTR) in consolidated statements (Ind AS 21.32).
  • Below-market loans: the standalone deemed-investment component (s02) is reversed on consolidation.
  • Analytical consequence: consolidated finance cost and debt include only external borrowings; subsidiary-level finance cost in AOC-1 or Ind AS 112 includes interest paid to the parent.

Management fees, royalties and shared services

  • Parent charges subsidiaries for brand use (royalty, often 1–2% of revenue), shared services, IT and management support. In consolidation, the fee income (other operating revenue or other income) and expense are eliminated with no profit effect — unless the subsidiary capitalised the charge into inventory or CWIP, which creates unrealised profit to eliminate.
  • Segment reporting: inter-segment charges may remain in segment results if the CODM reviews them that way (t6).
  • Tax and regulatory: transfer pricing (s.92–92F Income-tax Act), withholding tax on cross-border royalties, and SEBI LODR Reg 23 materiality for royalty payments by listed subsidiaries to unlisted parents (and vice versa).
  • Analytical consequence: a listed subsidiary's margins are reduced by royalty paid to the parent; the parent's standalone margins are boosted. Normalise when comparing standalone entities with independent peers.

Elimination checklist for model building

  1. Intra-group revenue/purchases matrix by entity pair (reconcile both sides; investigate mismatches from timing or goods in transit).
  2. Intra-group receivables/payables and loans by entity pair.
  3. Unrealised profit in closing inventory and PPE (with opening reversal).
  4. Intra-group dividends (eliminate against investor's income).
  5. Fees, royalties, guarantees, interest.
  6. Deferred tax on eliminations.
  7. NCI allocation based on direction.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mismatched balances due to goods in transit or cut-off differences — eliminate the agreed amount and record in-transit items in the receiving entity.
  • Different reporting dates (Ind AS 110.B93) — intra-group balances will not match without adjustment.
  • Losses on intra-group sales at below-cost prices may indicate impairment; do not reverse blindly.
  • Opening unrealised profit reverses into current-year profit as inventory is sold; a falling closing balance inflates consolidated margin.
  • Elimination with associates is partial; transactions with an associate are not intra-group but are related party transactions under Ind AS 24.

Key takeaways

  • All intra-group balances, transactions and unrealised profits are eliminated in full (Ind AS 110.B86).
  • Direction matters for NCI allocation: downstream against owners, upstream shared with NCI.
  • Deferred tax arises on unrealised profit eliminations under Ind AS 12.
  • Foreign-currency intra-group loans leave exchange differences in consolidated profit or OCI.
  • Fees and royalties shift margins between standalone entities without changing consolidated profit.

Practice Questions

Q1. Upstream unrealised profit [L2]

A 60%-owned subsidiary sells goods to the parent for ₹240 crore at a 20% margin on sales. At 31 March 2025, the parent holds 40% of these goods. Tax rate 25.168%. Pass the consolidation entries and compute the effect on owners' profit and on NCI.

💡 Hint: Margin on sales, not markup on cost, determines the unrealised profit; allocate after tax.

Q2. Intercompany loan in foreign currency [L2]

An Indian parent (functional currency INR) lends USD 20 million to its US subsidiary (functional currency USD) on 1 April 2024 at ₹83/USD; at 31 March 2025 the rate is ₹85.5/USD. The loan is repayable in three years. Explain the exchange difference in standalone and consolidated statements under two scenarios: (a) repayment planned; (b) settlement neither planned nor likely.

Q3. Royalty normalisation [L2]

A listed subsidiary pays a 1.5% brand royalty to its unlisted parent on revenue of ₹6,000 crore; its reported EBITDA margin is 12%. Independent peers do not pay royalties. Compute the subsidiary's royalty-adjusted EBITDA margin and explain the effect on the parent's standalone and the group's consolidated EBITDA.

Q4. Consolidation build in Excel [L3]

Design an Excel consolidation workbook for a parent with four subsidiaries (one foreign) for quarterly Reg 33 reporting: entity trial balance tabs, mapping to Schedule III lines, intercompany matrix, elimination journal tab, NCI calculation and checks. Specify the key formulas or lookups and the reconciliation checks.
