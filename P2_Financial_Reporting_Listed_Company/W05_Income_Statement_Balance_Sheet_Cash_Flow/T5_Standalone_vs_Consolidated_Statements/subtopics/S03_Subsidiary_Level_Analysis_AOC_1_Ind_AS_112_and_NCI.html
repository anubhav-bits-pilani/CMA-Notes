Subtopic note

Subsidiary-Level Analysis: AOC-1, Ind AS 112 and NCI

Scope: Subsidiary-level analysis: Form AOC-1, Ind AS 112 disclosures, NCI share of profit and equity

Why it matters

Consolidated statements blend the parent and every subsidiary into one set of numbers. To value a group, assess where cash and debt sit, or understand whether growth comes from the core business or a newly acquired unit, you need subsidiary-level data. Indian companies are required to provide more of it than most global peers: Form AOC-1 under Sec 129(3) gives a salient-features table of every subsidiary, associate and JV, while Ind AS 112 adds summarised financial information for subsidiaries with material NCI and material associates/JVs. Combined with Schedule III Division II additional information on consolidated net assets and profit by entity, this lets you approximately reconstruct the group by entity — a core skill for this week's checklist item on splitting consolidated performance.

Core notes

Form AOC-1 (Rule 5 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014)

Attached to the standalone financial statements, AOC-1 has:

  • Part A — Subsidiaries: name, reporting period (if different), reporting currency and exchange rate for foreign subsidiaries, share capital, reserves and surplus, total assets, total liabilities, investments, turnover, profit before taxation, provision for taxation, profit after taxation, proposed dividend and percentage of shareholding.
  • Part B — Associates and JVs: latest audited balance sheet date, shares held (number, amount, % holding), description of significant influence, reason for not consolidating, net worth attributable to shareholding, profit/loss for the year considered and not considered in consolidation.
  • Names of subsidiaries/associates yet to commence operations, liquidated or sold during the year.

Limitations: AOC-1 figures are entity-level (often local GAAP or Ind AS without consolidation adjustments), before intra-group eliminations and fair value adjustments from acquisition accounting, and give no cash flow or debt split (total liabilities only).

Schedule III Division II — additional information in consolidated statements

General Instructions for consolidated financial statements require, for each entity in the group (parent, subsidiaries Indian/foreign, NCI, associates, JVs), the share in net assets and profit or loss (and, after the 2021 amendments, OCI and total comprehensive income) as an amount and as a percentage of consolidated totals. This table is often the cleanest source for "where the profit sits", although it is presented after eliminations in some cases and before in others — read the footnote.

Ind AS 112 disclosures

Ind AS 112 requires disclosure of significant judgements and assumptions in determining control, joint control or significant influence (Ind AS 112.7–9), and for interests in subsidiaries:

  • composition of the group;
  • for each subsidiary with material NCI (Ind AS 112.12): proportion of ownership and voting rights held by NCI, profit allocated to NCI, accumulated NCI, dividends paid to NCI, and summarised financial information (current/non-current assets and liabilities, revenue, profit, total comprehensive income) per B10–B11;
  • significant restrictions on access to or use of assets and settlement of liabilities (Ind AS 112.13) — e.g. regulatory capital in an insurance subsidiary, exchange controls, lender restrictions;
  • consequences of changes in ownership without loss of control and of losing control;
  • interests in unconsolidated structured entities.

For material associates and JVs (Ind AS 112.21, B12–B13): summarised financial information, including cash, current financial liabilities, D&A, interest income/expense and tax for JVs — enough to compute the JV's EBITDA and net debt.

NCI share of profit and equity

NCI is presented in equity separately from owners of the parent (Ind AS 110.22). Profit and each component of OCI are attributed to owners and NCI (Ind AS 110.B94), even if NCI becomes negative. NCI at acquisition is measured at fair value or proportionate share of identifiable net assets (Ind AS 103.19).

Analytical uses:

  • Attributable metrics: EPS, ROE and P/E use owners' share (see c02).
  • EV bridge: deduct NCI at market or fair value (not book) when EV is built on consolidated EBITDA.
  • Cash access: dividends paid to NCI are a financing outflow in consolidated statements and reveal leakage.

Parent (standalone) 42,000 3,100 — 3,100

NCI share = 1% × 160 + 26% × 90 ≈ 25. Consolidated PAT 3,250 = owners 3,225 + NCI 25. Unexplained differences against reported consolidated figures typically come from dividends from subsidiaries in parent's standalone PAT (eliminated), unrealised profit, and purchase price allocation amortisation.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Dividend double count: the parent's standalone PAT includes dividends from subsidiaries; adding subsidiary PAT double counts unless dividends are removed.
  • Different reporting dates: foreign subsidiaries with December year-ends may be consolidated with a lag up to three months (Ind AS 110.B93), adjusted for significant transactions.
  • Step-down subsidiaries: AOC-1 lists them separately; aggregate carefully to avoid double counting intermediate holding companies' consolidated numbers.
  • Negative NCI: losses attributed to NCI can make NCI a debit balance; it is still equity, not an asset.
  • Materiality judgements: companies decide which NCI is "material" for Ind AS 112.12; immaterial subsidiaries may be aggregated in a way that hides a problem unit.

Key takeaways

  • AOC-1 gives entity-level revenue, PAT, assets, liabilities and holding % for every subsidiary, associate and JV.
  • Schedule III consolidated additional information shows each entity's share of net assets and profit.
  • Ind AS 112 adds summarised financials for material-NCI subsidiaries and material JVs/associates, plus restrictions on cash.
  • NCI shares profit and equity; owners' share is the basis for EPS and ROE.
  • Reconstructing the group requires removing intra-group dividends and eliminations.

Practice Questions

Q1. Locate the data [L1]

For each item, identify the best disclosure source (AOC-1 Part A, AOC-1 Part B, Schedule III consolidated additional information, Ind AS 112 note, segment note): (a) PAT of a 100% overseas subsidiary; (b) share of a JV's depreciation and cash; (c) restrictions on remitting cash from a Chinese subsidiary; (d) percentage of consolidated net assets held by a step-down subsidiary; (e) reasons why an associate is not consolidated.

Q2. NCI attribution [L2]

A parent owns 65% of S1 and 80% of S2. S1 reports PAT ₹240 crore and OCI loss ₹20 crore; S2 reports a loss of ₹50 crore. Parent standalone PAT ₹900 crore includes dividends of ₹78 crore from S1. Ignoring other eliminations, compute consolidated PAT, owners' share, NCI share and NCI's share of total comprehensive income.

💡 Hint: Remove the intra-group dividend before aggregating, then attribute each subsidiary's result by ownership.

Q3. Group split from AOC-1 [L3]

Using Titan Company's latest annual report, prepare a table splitting consolidated revenue and PAT into the standalone company, CaratLane, Titan Engineering & Automation and other subsidiaries using AOC-1 and the Schedule III additional information. Identify the reconciling items you cannot explain from published data and the further disclosures you would need.
