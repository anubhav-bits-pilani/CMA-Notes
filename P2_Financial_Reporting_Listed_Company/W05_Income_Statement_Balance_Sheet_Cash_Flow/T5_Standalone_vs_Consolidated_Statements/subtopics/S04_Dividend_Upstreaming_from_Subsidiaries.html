Subtopic note

Dividend Upstreaming from Subsidiaries

Scope: Dividend upstreaming: s.123 distributable profits, subsidiary leverage covenants, s.80M inter-corporate dividend deduction

Why it matters

Consolidated cash belongs to the group, but a parent can only spend what it can get. Holding companies service their own debt, pay their own dividends and fund new ventures from cash upstreamed by subsidiaries — mostly dividends, but also royalties, management fees and loan repayments. Whether a subsidiary can pay depends on company law (distributable profits), contracts (lender covenants and regulatory restrictions) and tax (the cost of moving the cash). Credit analysts rating a holding company, equity analysts valuing a holdco, and FP&A teams planning group treasury all model this. Several Indian groups (Tata, Aditya Birla, Bajaj) rely on dividend flows from listed and unlisted subsidiaries to service parent obligations.

Core notes

Sec 123: distributable profits

A company may declare dividend only out of (Sec 123(1)):

  • profits of the current year after providing for depreciation under Sec 123(2) (per Schedule II), or
  • accumulated profits of previous years after depreciation, or
  • both; or money provided by the government under a guarantee.

Key restrictions:

  • Before declaring dividend, a company may transfer such percentage of profits to reserves as it considers appropriate (no mandatory transfer since 2015 amendment).
  • Under the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014, declaring dividend out of accumulated profits when current-year profits are inadequate requires: rate not exceeding the average of the three preceding years, amount drawn not exceeding one-tenth of paid-up capital plus free reserves, and the balance of reserves after drawal not below 15% of paid-up capital.
  • Unrealised gains, notional gains, revaluation of assets and changes in carrying amount of assets/liabilities on fair value measurement are excluded from profits available for dividend (first proviso to Sec 123(1), inserted 2015) — so Ind AS fair value gains in a subsidiary do not increase its capacity.
  • Losses or depreciation of previous years not provided must be set off (Sec 123(1) second proviso).
  • Interim dividends: from the surplus in P&L and current-year profits; restrictions if current-period losses.
  • Sec 123(6): a company in default of repaying deposits (Sec 73/74) cannot declare dividend.

For foreign subsidiaries, local corporate law (e.g. solvency tests, statutory reserves in China) applies.

Subsidiary leverage covenants and regulatory limits

Even where profits are distributable, contracts may block upstreaming:

  • Term loan agreements commonly restrict dividends unless DSCR ≥ 1.2–1.5x, net debt/EBITDA ≤ a threshold, no event of default, and prior lender consent in project SPVs (cash waterfall with DSRA requirements).
  • NCD trust deeds and ECB conditions similarly restrict distributions.
  • Regulated subsidiaries: NBFCs (RBI dividend distribution guidelines link payout ratio to CRAR and net NPA), insurers (IRDAI solvency margin), banks (RBI dividend guidelines).
  • Ind AS 112.13 requires disclosure of significant restrictions — read it alongside the borrowings note of material subsidiaries.

Tax: s.80M inter-corporate dividend deduction

After the abolition of Dividend Distribution Tax from 1 April 2020, dividends are taxable in the hands of the recipient. To avoid cascading, section 80M of the Income-tax Act, 1961 allows a domestic company receiving dividend from another domestic company (or foreign company or business trust) a deduction equal to the dividend it distributes to its own shareholders at least one month before the due date of filing the return (under s.139(1)), capped at the dividend received.

  • Companies opting for s.115BAA can still claim s.80M (it is not among the deductions denied by s.115BAA).
  • Dividends from foreign subsidiaries were taxable at a concessional 15% under s.115BBD for earlier years; this concession has been withdrawn for later years, so verify the regime for the year analysed.
  • Withholding: TDS at 10% under s.194 on dividends paid to resident shareholders above the threshold, subject to exemptions for certain recipients; check current rules.
  • The Income-tax Act 2025 renumbers these provisions from 1 April 2026; the economic mechanism is retained.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Holdco H owns 70% of S (domestic). S has current-year profit after depreciation ₹500 crore, of which ₹120 crore is an unrealised FVTPL gain. S's term-loan covenant permits dividends up to 40% of PAT if net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5x (it is 2.2x).

Step Amount
Profit available under Sec 123 (500 − 120) 380
Covenant cap (40% × 500) 200
Maximum dividend (lower) 200
H's share (70%) 140
NCI leakage (30%) 60
H's tax: if H distributes ≥ ₹140 crore to its shareholders in time, s.80M deduction 140

Cash upstreamed to H = ₹140 crore (before TDS, which H can credit). To get ₹140 crore, the group must pay ₹60 crore to minority shareholders — a cost of upstreaming that consolidated cash does not reveal.

Analytical framework

Build a holdco cash flow: dividends receivable from each subsidiary (capacity-based, not aspiration), royalties and fees, minus holdco opex, interest and dividends to its shareholders. Coverage ratio:

\[ \text{Holdco interest cover} = \frac{\text{Upstreamable cash from subsidiaries} + \text{Holdco operating cash}}{\text{Holdco interest}} \]

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Buybacks by subsidiaries (taxed under the buyback rules applicable for the year — the 2024 change shifted tax to shareholders as deemed dividend) as an alternative upstreaming route.
  • Royalty and brand fees reduce subsidiary profits and are deductible; transfer pricing and Reg 23 LODR (related party transactions, royalty thresholds) constrain them.
  • Capital reduction under Sec 66 returns capital, not profits.
  • Loans from subsidiary to parent are an upstreaming route but are restricted by Sec 185/186 and create deemed dividend issues under s.2(22)(e) for closely held companies.
  • Accumulated losses at subsidiary block dividend even if the current year is profitable, until set off.

Key takeaways

  • Sec 123 limits dividends to realised profits after depreciation and set-off of past losses; Ind AS fair value gains do not count.
  • Lender covenants and sectoral regulators often bind more tightly than company law.
  • Section 80M removes cascading tax on inter-corporate dividends if passed on to shareholders in time.
  • NCI receives its share of every dividend — upstreaming from a partly owned subsidiary leaks cash.
  • Holdco credit analysis should rely on upstreamable cash, not consolidated cash.

Practice Questions

Q1. Distributable profit [L1]

A subsidiary's FY2024-25 PAT is ₹260 crore, which includes a ₹45 crore mark-to-market gain on an open forward contract not designated as a hedge and a ₹30 crore unrealised FVTPL gain on mutual funds. Brought-forward losses of ₹70 crore exist. Compute the maximum dividend under Sec 123 and explain each adjustment.

Q2. Holdco cash flow [L2]

Holdco H has standalone debt of ₹3,000 crore at 8.5%. Subsidiaries: S1 (100%, PAT ₹600 crore, covenant cap 50% of PAT); S2 (55%, PAT ₹400 crore, payout policy 30%); S3 (100% NBFC, PAT ₹200 crore, RBI-linked payout cap 20%). Holdco opex ₹40 crore, brand royalty income ₹90 crore. Build the holdco cash flow, compute interest cover and the amount available for H's own dividend.

💡 Hint: Apply each subsidiary's binding constraint first, then multiply by H's ownership share.

Q3. Tax cascade [L2]

H receives ₹500 crore dividend from a domestic subsidiary and ₹80 crore from a foreign subsidiary in FY2024-25. H declares and pays a final dividend of ₹450 crore on 15 September 2025 (return due date 31 October 2025). H is under s.115BAA. Compute H's s.80M deduction and the tax on dividend income, stating assumptions about the foreign dividend.

Q4. Judgement: dividend from a stressed subsidiary [L3]

A listed parent's subsidiary in an infrastructure SPV declares a ₹150 crore dividend out of accumulated profits in a year in which it made a loss of ₹40 crore, while its DSCR fell to 1.05x. Evaluate the legality under Sec 123 and the dividend rules, the likely lender reaction, and how you would treat the dividend in normalised standalone earnings of the parent.
