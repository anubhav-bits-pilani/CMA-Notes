Subtopic note

Base-Year Indexing of Schedule III Division II P&L Lines

Scope: Base-year indexing of 3–5 years of Schedule III Div II P&L lines: revenue from operations, COGS components, employee cost, other expenses, D&A, finance cost

Why it matters

Horizontal analysis answers one question: which lines are growing faster than the business? Rupee movements alone hide this, because a ₹40 crore rise in employee cost means nothing until you know revenue rose ₹600 crore or ₹60 crore. Indexing every line to a common base year (base = 100) puts all lines on one scale so that divergence is visible at a glance. In practice this is the first sheet an equity analyst, credit analyst or FP&A team builds when reviewing a listed company: it drives the questions asked on earnings calls, the variance commentary in board packs, and the assumptions that later go into the forecast columns of a 3-statement model. Auditors use the same technique in analytical procedures (SA 520) to identify lines that need substantive testing.

Core notes

The lines to index

Schedule III Division II (Ind AS companies other than NBFCs) prescribes a statement of profit and loss that classifies expenses by nature. The lines you index, in order, are:

Schedule III line What it captures Indexing notes
Revenue from operations Sale of products, sale of services, other operating revenue (Note 4 of Div II general instructions) Index both total and the components; other operating revenue (scrap, export incentives) can swing
Cost of materials consumed Opening RM + purchases − closing RM Manufacturing businesses
Purchases of stock-in-trade Traded goods bought for resale Retailers and distributors (Trent, Titan's traded watches)
Changes in inventories of FG, WIP and stock-in-trade Opening − closing inventory of these categories Can be negative (inventory build); never index alone — fold into COGS
Employee benefits expense Salaries, PF/ESI contributions, gratuity, ESOP cost (Ind AS 102), staff welfare Watch ESOP charges and Labour Code past-service cost (Nov 2025)
Finance costs Interest on borrowings, interest on lease liabilities (Ind AS 116), unwinding of discount, exchange differences regarded as borrowing cost Split lease interest from borrowing interest
Depreciation and amortisation expense PPE depreciation, ROU asset depreciation, intangibles amortisation Split ROU depreciation where the note allows
Other expenses Power, rent (short-term/low-value/variable), advertising, freight, royalty, CSR, repairs, legal, bad debts/ECL Index the major sub-lines from the note, not just the total

COGS is not a Schedule III line. You build it as cost of materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories. Index the derived COGS and gross profit, because the changes-in-inventory line alone flips sign and produces meaningless indices.

The mechanics

For each line and each year \(t\):

\[ \text{Index}_t = \frac{\text{Item}_t}{\text{Item}_{\text{base}}} \times 100 \]

Steps:

  1. Extract 3–5 years from the annual reports into a single sheet in ₹ crore, one row per Schedule III line, using the latest available comparative for each year (the FY2025 annual report's restated FY2024 column beats the FY2024 report's original column — see s03).
  2. Add derived rows: COGS, gross profit, EBITDA (revenue from operations − COGS − employee cost − other expenses), EBIT, PBT.
  3. Choose the base year (normally the earliest year) and check it is not abnormal (COVID FY2020-21, a year with a large one-off, a pre-Ind AS 116 or pre-GST year — see s04).
  4. Compute the index row-by-row; lock the base column with an absolute reference.
  5. Add a relative index row: line index ÷ revenue index × 100. A relative index above 100 means the line is growing faster than revenue — the margin is being eaten.
Index (cell D10):          =D5/$B5*100
Relative to revenue:       =D10/D$9*100        (row 9 = revenue index)
Guard against zero base:   =IF($B5=0,"n.m.",D5/$B5*100)

Illustration (₹ crore, standalone)

Line FY2022 FY2023 FY2024 FY2025 Index FY2025
Revenue from operations 4,000 4,600 5,200 5,800 145
COGS 2,400 2,806 3,224 3,654 152
Employee benefits expense 320 368 440 522 163
Other expenses 600 680 790 900 150
D&A 160 170 185 200 125
Finance costs 60 58 55 50 83

Revenue rose 45% but COGS rose 52% and employee cost 63%. Gross margin fell from 40.0% to 37.0% and employee cost moved from 8.0% to 9.0% of revenue. D&A at 125 lagging revenue at 145 suggests under-investment or an ageing asset base (cross-check capex). Finance cost falling while revenue rises indicates deleveraging. Each of these observations becomes a question for management or an assumption for the model.

Reading the series

  • Revenue components: index sale of products and sale of services separately; a growing services share changes the margin profile.
  • COGS components: for a manufacturer, materials consumed vs purchases of stock-in-trade shifting indicates a move from make to buy (outsourcing) — gross margin falls but employee cost and D&A may fall too.
  • Employee cost: separate ESOP charge and one-off past-service cost before indexing; otherwise the index spikes in the grant year.
  • Other expenses: index advertising, freight, power and rent separately. Freight and power track commodity prices; advertising is discretionary and often cut to protect margin.
  • D&A and finance cost: post-Ind AS 116, both contain lease elements. Index "D&A excluding ROU depreciation" and "interest on borrowings" separately to see the operating capex and leverage story.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative or near-zero base: an index from a base of ₹2 crore (e.g., finance cost of a debt-free company) produces huge numbers that mean nothing. Report "n.m." and use absolute change or % of revenue instead.
  • Sign changes: changes in inventories and exceptional items can flip sign; never index them in isolation.
  • Restated base year: if the base-year numbers were restated in a later report (Ind AS 8, regrouping), use the restated figures; mixing originals and restated is the single most common error.
  • Standalone vs consolidated: pick one and stay with it across every year. For groups with material subsidiaries (Titan with CaratLane), consolidated is usually the relevant set.
  • Base-year abnormality: if FY2020-21 is the earliest year available, either start from FY2019-20 or show a second index rebased to a normal year.
  • Inflation and price effects: a revenue index of 145 over three years in a 6% inflation environment is roughly 25% real growth. For commodity-linked businesses (gold jewellery), index volume metrics (grams sold, store count) alongside value.

Key takeaways

  • Index every Schedule III P&L line to base = 100; derive and index COGS, gross profit and EBITDA rather than the raw changes-in-inventory line.
  • The relative index (line index ÷ revenue index) is the fastest margin-compression detector.
  • Split employee cost, D&A and finance cost into their recurring and lease/one-off components before indexing.
  • Use the latest restated comparatives and one consistent basis (standalone or consolidated).
  • Treat small or sign-changing bases as "n.m."; pair value indices with volume indices for price-driven businesses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build an index table [L1]

A company reports (₹ crore) revenue from operations FY2022 2,500, FY2023 2,900, FY2024 3,250; cost of materials consumed 1,300, 1,540, 1,760; purchases of stock-in-trade 120, 150, 170; changes in inventories −20, −30, 10; employee benefits expense 210, 240, 285. Using FY2022 as base, compute the index for revenue, COGS and employee cost for each year, and the relative index of COGS to revenue for FY2024.

Q2. Make vs buy signal [L2]

Over FY2021–FY2025 a consumer durables company's cost of materials consumed index fell from 100 to 92 while purchases of stock-in-trade rose from 100 to 310 and revenue rose to 160. Employee cost index is 128 and D&A index is 105. Explain what business change these indices suggest and which notes in the annual report you would read to confirm it.

💡 Hint: Think about what happens to manufacturing headcount and plant when a company outsources production.

Q3. Excel layout for a 5-year index sheet [L2]

Design the Excel layout (rows, columns, formulas) for a 5-year index sheet that pulls Schedule III lines from a data tab, computes COGS and EBITDA, produces index and relative-index rows, and flags any line whose relative index exceeds 110. Write the formulas for one row, including handling of a zero base.

Q4. Finance cost index after Ind AS 116 [L2]

A retailer's finance cost index (base FY2019 = 100) reads 340 in FY2021, although borrowings were repaid in FY2020. Interest on lease liabilities in FY2021 is ₹180 crore out of total finance cost of ₹238 crore; FY2019 finance cost was ₹70 crore, all on borrowings. Recompute a meaningful index and explain what each version tells a credit analyst.

Q5. Interview: what would you index first? [L3]

You are handed five years of Schedule III statements for an NSE-listed apparel retailer the night before a management meeting. Explain which five P&L lines you would index first, which derived lines you would add, and which two abnormalities you would check in the base year before trusting the series.
