Subtopic note

Restating Comparatives Before Indexing: Regrouping and Ind AS 8

Scope: Restating comparatives for regrouping/reclassification notes and Ind AS 8 retrospective restatements before indexing

Why it matters

A trend series is only as good as the consistency of its inputs. Indian annual reports routinely carry a note such as "previous year figures have been regrouped/reclassified wherever necessary to conform to current year classification", and Ind AS 8 restatements (accounting policy changes, prior-period errors) change comparative numbers after they were first published. If you build a 5-year table by pasting each year's figures from its own annual report, the series will contain breaks that look like operating changes. Data vendors frequently make exactly this mistake. Restating comparatives is therefore the first quality-control step before any index or common-size calculation, and it is the step reviewers look for first when challenging a model.

Core notes

Three sources of comparative change

Source Standard What changes Where you find it
Reclassification / regrouping Ind AS 1.41 Line in which an item is presented; totals usually unchanged "Regrouping" note, last note in the financial statements; sometimes a reconciliation table
Change in accounting policy Ind AS 8.19–22 Measurement of comparatives retrospectively (unless transition provisions specify otherwise or impracticable) Accounting policy note; "restated" column headers
Correction of prior-period error Ind AS 8.42 Comparatives restated as if error never occurred; opening equity of earliest period adjusted Separate note with nature of error and amount per line (Ind AS 8.49)

Ind AS 1.41 requires that when presentation or classification is changed, comparative amounts are reclassified unless impracticable, with disclosure of the nature, amount and reason. Ind AS 1.40A requires a third balance sheet (opening of the preceding period) when a retrospective policy change, retrospective restatement or reclassification has a material effect on that opening balance sheet.

Changes in accounting estimates (useful lives, ECL inputs) are prospective under Ind AS 8.36 — comparatives are not restated, so the series carries a genuine break that you must note but cannot remove.

The rule for building the series

Always take each year's numbers from the latest annual report that presents them:

  • FY2025 and FY2024 from the FY2025 annual report (FY2024 is the restated comparative).
  • FY2023 from the FY2024 annual report.
  • FY2022 from the FY2023 annual report, and so on.

This "rolling latest comparative" approach captures restatements and regroupings automatically for adjacent years. But a regrouping made in FY2025 will only flow to FY2024 — the older years (FY2021–FY2023) still sit in the old classification. You must push the regrouping back manually.

Pushing regroupings back to older years

  1. Read the regrouping note in the latest report; identify the item, the old line and the new line (e.g., "Bank charges of ₹6.2 crore reclassified from finance costs to other expenses").
  2. Find the same item in older years' notes (other expenses breakup, finance cost breakup).
  3. Reclassify in your data tab with an explicit adjustment row, never by overwriting the reported number.
  4. Re-check that totals (total expenses, PBT) are unchanged by pure regroupings.

Ind AS 8 restatements

For a policy change or error, the restated comparative changes profit, not just presentation. Typical Indian examples:

  • Correction of revenue cut-off or unrecorded liabilities discovered in audit or forensic review.
  • Change from FIFO to weighted average for inventory (policy change).
  • Ind AS 116 adoption — note that most Indian companies used the modified retrospective approach (Ind AS 116.C5(b)) and did not restate FY2018-19; this is a structural break, not a restatement (see s04).
  • Common-control business combinations under Ind AS 103 Appendix C: the financial statements are restated from the beginning of the preceding period as if the combination occurred then (Appendix C para 9(iii)). Mergers of wholly owned subsidiaries into the parent therefore restate comparatives.
  • Schedule III amendments (2021) required regrouping — for example current maturities of long-term borrowings moved into short-term borrowings, and security deposits classified under other financial assets rather than loans.

For years before the restatement window, you cannot replicate the restatement precisely. Options: (a) estimate the adjustment from disclosed amounts and flag it, (b) start the series at the earliest restated year, or (c) show both bases with a break marker.

Documentation standard

Every adjustment should carry: year, line affected, amount, source (report, page, note number), type (regroup / policy / error / estimate break), and preparer's comment. In Excel, keep a separate Adjustments tab and compute the clean series as Reported + Σ adjustments, so the bridge is auditable.

Clean value (Data!F12):  =Reported!F12+SUMIFS(Adj!$E:$E,Adj!$B:$B,$B12,Adj!$C:$C,F$3)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unquantified regrouping note: the boilerplate "regrouped wherever necessary" without amounts. Compare the prior-year column across the two reports line by line; differences reveal the regrouping. A short variance check tab (old report vs new report for the same year) catches this mechanically.
  • Restated profit but unchanged opening equity in your model: an error correction adjusts opening retained earnings of the earliest period presented; update the BS series too, or retained-earnings roll-forwards will not tie.
  • Merger restatement (common control): the restated series is the "combined" entity; earlier years from older reports are the pre-merger parent only. Do not splice them without noting the break.
  • Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105.34): the P&L comparatives are re-presented to show the discontinued operation as a single line. Older years still include it line by line — re-present them using the segment or discontinued-operations note.
  • Impracticability: Ind AS 8.23–27 allows limited restatement where impracticable; the company's disclosure tells you which years are not comparable.
  • Audit qualification or emphasis of matter: read the auditor's report; a qualification on a restated year changes how much reliance you place on that column.

Key takeaways

  • Source each year from the latest annual report presenting it; this captures most restatements automatically.
  • Push reclassifications back to older years manually through an adjustment log, keeping totals unchanged.
  • Distinguish regrouping (Ind AS 1.41), policy change and error (Ind AS 8, retrospective) from estimate changes (prospective, true breaks).
  • Common-control mergers and Ind AS 105 re-presentation restate comparatives and create splice points.
  • Keep reported, adjustment and clean values separate and documented with note references.

Practice Questions

Q1. Regrouping push-back [L1]

The FY2025 annual report states: "Freight outward of ₹38 crore for FY2024, earlier netted from revenue from operations, has been reclassified to other expenses." FY2023 and FY2022 freight outward (from older notes) were ₹31 crore and ₹27 crore, also netted from revenue. Reported revenue from operations was FY2022 ₹2,140 crore and FY2023 ₹2,480 crore; FY2024 restated ₹2,860 crore. Prepare the adjustment log and the consistent revenue series.

Q2. Policy change vs estimate change [L2]

In FY2025 a company (i) changed its inventory cost formula from FIFO to weighted average, impact on FY2024 profit ₹12 crore, and (ii) revised the useful life of store fit-outs from 9 to 6 years, increasing FY2025 depreciation by ₹22 crore. Explain how each is reflected in the FY2025 report's comparatives, how each affects a 5-year D&A and gross-margin index, and what you would disclose in your model notes.

Q3. Error correction journal and series fix [L2]

During FY2025 a company discovered that FY2024 revenue included ₹45 crore of bill-and-hold sales not meeting Ind AS 115 criteria (cost of goods ₹30 crore, still in inventory). Pass the restatement entries in the FY2025 financial statements (ignore tax), and state which lines of your FY2024 trend and common-size tables change.

💡 Hint: The correction is retrospective — think about which period's revenue, COGS, inventory and retained earnings are affected.

Q4. Variance-check tab design [L3]

Design an Excel tab that automatically compares each line of the FY2024 column as published in the FY2024 report with the FY2024 comparative in the FY2025 report, flags any difference above ₹1 crore or 0.5% of the line, and links flagged items to the adjustment log. Specify columns, formulas and how you would document the source of each difference.

Q5. Merger splice [L3]

A listed parent merged its wholly owned distribution subsidiary with appointed date 1 April 2024; the FY2025 report restates FY2024 to include the subsidiary. You have a 5-year standalone series FY2021–FY2025. Explain the break, two methods of making the series comparable, and the disclosure you would give a portfolio manager reading your table.
