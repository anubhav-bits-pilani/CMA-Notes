Subtopic note

YoY Growth, CAGR, Index Series and LTM from Reg 33 Results

Scope: YoY growth vs CAGR vs index series; LTM view using quarterly Reg 33 results

Why it matters

Growth can be expressed three ways, and each answers a different question. Year-on-year (YoY) growth shows momentum and volatility; CAGR summarises the average compound rate between two points; an index series shows the path. Using the wrong one misleads: a CAGR hides a collapse in the middle of the period, and YoY growth after a bad year exaggerates recovery. Separately, annual reports are stale by the time they are published — in India, the FY annual report appears around June–August while Reg 33 quarterly results arrive within 45 days of each quarter-end. A last-twelve-months (LTM, also TTM) view built from quarterly results keeps trend and valuation tables current, and is the basis for trailing multiples quoted by brokers.

Core notes

YoY growth

\[ g_t = \frac{X_t}{X_{t-1}} - 1 \]

YoY growth is sensitive to base effects. After a down year, YoY is high (base effect); after a boom year, YoY is low. Always read YoY alongside a 2-year or 3-year view.

CAGR

\[ \text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{X_{\text{end}}}{X_{\text{begin}}}\right)^{1/n} - 1 \]

where \(n\) = number of years between endpoints (for FY2021 to FY2025, \(n = 4\), not 5). CAGR depends only on endpoints; it says nothing about the path. It is undefined (or meaningless) when the beginning value is zero or negative — common for PAT of loss-making years.

CAGR:                 =(F5/B5)^(1/(COLUMNS(B5:F5)-1))-1
Or:                   =RRI(4,B5,F5)
Guard negative base:  =IF(OR(B5<=0,F5<=0),"n.m.",RRI(4,B5,F5))

Index series

\[ I_t = \frac{X_t}{X_{\text{base}}} \times 100 \]

The index series preserves the path and allows multiple lines to be compared on one chart. The index and CAGR are linked: \(I_{\text{end}} = 100 \times (1+\text{CAGR})^n\).

Comparing the three

₹ crore FY2021 FY2022 FY2023 FY2024 FY2025
Revenue 1,800 2,900 3,300 3,450 3,600
YoY — 61.1% 13.8% 4.5% 4.3%
Index (FY2021 = 100) 100 161 183 192 200
Index (FY2022 = 100) — 100 114 119 124

CAGR FY2021–FY2025 = (3,600 ÷ 1,800)^(1/4) − 1 = 18.9%. CAGR FY2022–FY2025 = (3,600 ÷ 2,900)^(1/3) − 1 = 7.5%. If FY2021 was a COVID year, the 18.9% is a rebound artefact; the YoY series shows growth decelerating to ~4%, which is the relevant input for a forecast.

Rule of thumb: report a CAGR from a normal base, show YoY for the last 2–3 years, and chart the index from a normal base.

LTM using Reg 33 results

SEBI LODR Reg 33 requires listed entities to publish quarterly financial results (standalone and consolidated) within 45 days of quarter-end (audited annual results within 60 days of year-end), with a limited review by the statutory auditor for quarterly results. Half-yearly results include a balance sheet and cash flow statement (statement of assets and liabilities, and cash flow, at 30 September).

\[ \text{LTM} = \text{Latest full FY} + \text{Current YTD} - \text{Prior-year YTD} \]

Example: at 15 November 2025 (after Q2 FY2026 results), LTM revenue = FY2025 + H1 FY2026 − H1 FY2025. Alternatively, sum the last four reported quarters.

₹ crore FY2025 H1 FY2025 H1 FY2026 LTM Sep-2025
Revenue from operations 12,400 5,700 6,450 13,150
EBITDA 1,610 720 840 1,730
Exceptional items (40) (40) — —

LTM EBITDA margin = 1,730 ÷ 13,150 = 13.2% vs FY2025 13.0%.

LTM mechanics and cautions

  • Consistency of quarterly figures: quarterly results restate prior-quarter comparatives occasionally (mergers, discontinued operations). Use the latest published comparatives for prior-year quarters.
  • Q4 derived figures: Q4 is usually presented as the balancing figure between audited annual and published 9M figures; it can absorb year-end adjustments (impairment, provisions true-ups). LTM spanning a Q4 includes them.
  • Balance sheet items: LTM is a flow concept. For ratios (ROCE, receivable days), pair LTM flows with the latest balance sheet (half-yearly) or average of available balance sheets.
  • Seasonality: never annualise a single quarter (Q3 × 4) for festive-driven businesses like jewellery and apparel.
  • Exceptional items in quarters: strip them from each quarter before summing to get LTM core EBITDA (Topic 2).
  • Standalone vs consolidated: Reg 33 requires both; pick the same basis used for the annual series.

Excel implementation

LTM (single item):    =FY_latest + YTD_current - YTD_prior
Last 4 quarters:      =SUM(INDEX(QtrRange,0,MATCH(LatestQtr,QtrHeaders,0)-3):INDEX(QtrRange,0,MATCH(LatestQtr,QtrHeaders,0)))
YoY on quarter:       =XLOOKUP(Qtr,Hdr,Row)/XLOOKUP(EDATE(Qtr,-12),Hdr,Row)-1

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative endpoints: CAGR on losses is undefined; report absolute change or use EBITDA instead of PAT.
  • Different period lengths: a 15-month FY (change of year-end) breaks both YoY and CAGR; annualise carefully or exclude.
  • Acquisitions within LTM: LTM includes part-year acquired revenue; organic LTM needs the acquiree's quarterly contribution.
  • Restated quarterly comparatives after a demerger: the prior-year YTD in the latest quarterly release may differ from what was published a year ago — use the latest.
  • Arithmetic mean of YoY vs CAGR: the average of YoY rates exceeds CAGR when growth is volatile (volatility drag); never quote the arithmetic mean as "average growth".
  • Intra-year trend: sequential (QoQ) growth is meaningful only for non-seasonal businesses; otherwise use YoY on the same quarter.

Key takeaways

  • YoY = momentum and base effects; CAGR = endpoint summary with \(n\) = years between endpoints; index = path.
  • Choose a normal base for CAGR and indices; report YoY for recent years.
  • LTM = latest FY + current YTD − prior YTD, from Reg 33 results (45 days quarterly, 60 days annual).
  • Strip exceptional items quarter by quarter, keep standalone/consolidated consistency, never annualise seasonal quarters.
  • Use RRI or the explicit formula for CAGR with guards for negative or zero bases.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three growth measures [L1]

Revenue (₹ crore): FY2020 2,200, FY2021 1,650, FY2022 2,480, FY2023 2,900, FY2024 3,150, FY2025 3,420. Compute YoY growth for each year, the CAGR for FY2020–FY2025 and FY2021–FY2025, and an index with FY2020 = 100. State which figure you would quote as the 5-year growth rate and why.

Q2. LTM after Q3 [L2]

After Q3 FY2026 results, you have: FY2025 revenue ₹9,800 crore, EBITDA ₹1,420 crore; 9M FY2025 revenue ₹7,350 crore, EBITDA ₹1,090 crore; 9M FY2026 revenue ₹8,240 crore, EBITDA ₹1,160 crore. 9M FY2026 EBITDA includes a ₹35 crore one-off gain on a lease modification shown within other expenses. Compute reported and core LTM revenue, EBITDA and margin.

💡 Hint: Remove the one-off from the current-year YTD before computing core LTM.

Q3. Volatility drag [L2]

A company's YoY revenue growth over four years was +30%, −20%, +25%, −10%. Compute the arithmetic mean growth and the CAGR, and explain to a junior analyst why the two differ and which belongs in a pitch book.

Q4. Excel LTM engine [L3]

Design an Excel quarterly data tab (columns = quarters from Q1 FY2022 to Q2 FY2026, rows = revenue, EBITDA, exceptional items, PAT) and write formulas that compute, for any selected quarter-end, LTM revenue, LTM core EBITDA and YoY growth of LTM revenue. Explain how you would handle a quarter whose comparatives were restated after a demerger.

Q5. Seasonal annualisation error [L2]

A jewellery retailer reports Q3 FY2026 (October–December) revenue ₹18,500 crore and EBITDA ₹2,150 crore. A broker note annualises Q3 to show EBITDA of ₹8,600 crore for FY2026. Explain the error using what you know about Indian festive and wedding seasons, and describe the correct way to estimate FY2026 EBITDA at that date.
