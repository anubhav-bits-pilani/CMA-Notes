Subtopic note

Structural Breaks: Ind AS 116, GST, Acquisitions and Demergers

Scope: Structural breaks: Ind AS 116 adoption (FY2019-20), GST transition (Jul 2017, excise-inclusive revenue before), acquisitions, demergers

Why it matters

A structural break is a change in accounting or business perimeter that shifts a line item permanently, without any change in underlying performance. Indian 5–10 year series are littered with them: excise-inclusive revenue before GST, the Ind AS 116 shift of rent out of other expenses, and the frequent acquisitions, mergers and demergers of listed groups. An analyst who does not identify and bridge these breaks will report spurious margin expansion (Ind AS 116), spurious revenue deceleration (GST) or spurious growth (acquisitions). Credit committees and investment committees expect breaks to be flagged explicitly in every trend table.

Core notes

1. Ind AS 116 adoption (FY2019-20)

Ind AS 116 Leases applies to annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2019. Lessees recognise a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a lease liability for almost all leases (exemptions: short-term ≤12 months, low-value). The P&L effect:

Line Pre-Ind AS 116 (Ind AS 17 operating lease) Post-Ind AS 116
Other expenses (rent) Straight-line rent Only short-term, low-value and variable rent (e.g., revenue-share rent)
D&A PPE only PPE + ROU depreciation
Finance costs Borrowings Borrowings + interest on lease liabilities
EBITDA After rent Before fixed rent — higher
PBT in early lease years Lower expense Higher (front-loaded interest)

Most Indian companies used the modified retrospective approach (Ind AS 116.C5(b)) with the cumulative effect in opening retained earnings at 1 April 2019 and no restatement of FY2018-19. So the FY2019 → FY2020 break is embedded in reported numbers. For a lease-heavy retailer, EBITDA margin can jump 6–10 percentage points in FY2020 purely from the standard.

Bridging the break: either (a) present a pre-Ind AS 116 (rent-adjusted) EBITDA for all years by deducting lease payments from post-2019 EBITDA (lease payments are in the cash flow statement under financing — principal and interest), or (b) restate pre-2019 years to an "as if Ind AS 116" basis by adding back rent, which is harder because ROU depreciation and interest must be estimated. Option (a) is standard practice (see Topic 2 s06).

2. GST transition (1 July 2017)

Before GST, Indian manufacturers reported revenue gross of excise duty with excise shown as an expense line (under Ind AS 18, excise was a liability of the manufacturer and included in revenue; sales tax/VAT was excluded). From 1 July 2017, GST subsumed excise, and GST — like VAT — is collected on behalf of government and excluded from revenue. FY2017-18 therefore contains one quarter (April–June 2017) of excise-inclusive revenue and three quarters of GST-exclusive revenue.

Effects on trend analysis:

  • Reported revenue growth for FY2018 and FY2019 is understated for excise-heavy industries (cement, tobacco, FMCG, auto).
  • Every expense-as-%-of-revenue ratio is overstated in post-GST years relative to pre-GST years if you use gross revenue.

Bridge: compute "revenue net of excise" for pre-GST periods (revenue from operations − excise duty expense line) and use it as the base for indices and common-size. Most companies disclosed a "comparable revenue" figure in FY2018 and FY2019 reports; use that where available.

₹ crore FY2017 FY2018
Revenue from operations (reported) 10,000 10,400
Excise duty expense 1,100 280 (Q1 only)
Revenue net of excise 8,900 10,120
Reported growth 4.0%
Comparable growth 13.7%

For most new analyses (FY2021 onward), the GST break sits outside a 5-year window, but it matters for 10-year histories, long-term CAGR claims and terminal growth sanity checks.

3. Acquisitions

A business combination under Ind AS 103 consolidates the acquiree from the acquisition date. The first year contains a part-year contribution; the second year a full year. Both inflate group growth versus organic growth.

Bridging approaches:

  • Organic growth = group growth excluding acquired revenue for the first 12 months after acquisition. Use the acquiree's disclosed revenue since acquisition (Ind AS 103.B64(q)(i)) and the pro forma combined revenue as if acquired at the start of the year (B64(q)(ii)).
  • Pro forma series: add the acquiree's pre-acquisition revenue/EBITDA to prior years (from its own filings, e.g., MCA or Form AOC-1) to create a like-for-like series.
  • Step acquisitions (associate to subsidiary): the line moves from "share of profit of associates" below EBIT to full consolidation — EBITDA jumps, NCI appears.

4. Demergers and disposals

A demerger under a scheme (ss.230–232 Companies Act 2013) removes a business from the perimeter, usually from the appointed date. If the demerged business is presented as a discontinued operation (Ind AS 105), comparatives are re-presented (Ind AS 105.34). If the demerger is to shareholders under common control, Appendix C of Ind AS 103 may not apply to the demerging company, and comparatives may not be re-presented line by line — read the scheme note.

Bridge: remove the demerged business from all prior years using segment disclosures or the discontinued-operations note so that the series reflects only continuing operations.

Marking breaks in the model

  • Add a "break flag" row above the series with a short code (L = lease standard, G = GST, A = acquisition, D = demerger/disposal, P = policy change).
  • Show both reported and bridged series; never silently replace reported numbers.
  • In charts, draw a vertical line at the break year.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ind AS 116 full retrospective adopters: a few companies restated FY2019; check the transition note before applying the rent bridge, or you will double-adjust.
  • Variable rent: revenue-share rentals (common in malls, airports) remain in other expenses post-Ind AS 116. Two retailers with different rent structures show different EBITDA uplift.
  • COVID rent concessions: the practical expedient (Ind AS 116.46A) put rent concessions in P&L in FY2021–FY2022 as negative variable lease payments, inflating EBITDA temporarily — a second lease-related break.
  • Acquisition with simultaneous divestment: net perimeter change can be close to zero in revenue but very different in margin; bridge both sides.
  • GST-era anti-profiteering and price cuts: some post-GST revenue change is genuine price reduction, not presentation; do not attribute all of it to the excise bridge.
  • Demerger with mirror shareholding: post-demerger, sum-of-parts comparisons may need the demerged entity's own listed numbers.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 116 (from 1 April 2019, mostly modified retrospective, no restatement) lifts EBITDA and moves rent into D&A and finance cost; bridge via rent-adjusted EBITDA.
  • Pre-July 2017 revenue includes excise; use revenue net of excise for indices and common-size.
  • Acquisitions require organic vs inorganic separation using Ind AS 103 disclosures or pro forma series.
  • Demergers and disposals require continuing-operations series; Ind AS 105 re-presents only the comparative year.
  • Flag every break explicitly and keep reported and bridged series side by side.

Practice Questions

Q1. GST revenue bridge [L1]

A cement company reports revenue from operations FY2017 ₹7,800 crore (excise duty expense ₹1,050 crore), FY2018 ₹8,050 crore (excise ₹270 crore for Q1) and FY2019 ₹8,900 crore. Compute reported and comparable revenue growth for FY2018 and FY2019 and the 2-year comparable CAGR.

Q2. Ind AS 116 margin jump [L2]

An apparel retailer reports FY2019 EBITDA ₹420 crore on revenue ₹3,500 crore, with rent expense ₹310 crore. FY2020 revenue is ₹3,900 crore and EBITDA is ₹830 crore; the cash flow statement shows lease payments of ₹355 crore (principal ₹205 crore, interest ₹150 crore) and other expenses include ₹25 crore of variable rent. Compute reported and rent-adjusted EBITDA margins for both years and explain the true margin trend.

💡 Hint: Deduct total lease payments (principal plus interest) from post-adoption EBITDA; variable rent is already inside EBITDA.

Q3. Organic vs inorganic growth [L2]

A group's consolidated revenue rose from ₹12,000 crore to ₹14,100 crore. On 1 October it acquired a company whose revenue since acquisition was ₹900 crore; pro forma group revenue had the acquisition occurred on 1 April would have been ₹15,000 crore. Compute reported growth, organic growth and the acquiree's implied first-half revenue, and explain how next year's organic growth should be measured.

Q4. Demerger break [L3]

A diversified NSE-listed company demerged its consumer division (FY2024 revenue ₹2,300 crore, EBITDA ₹410 crore) with appointed date 1 April 2024. The FY2025 report presents the division as a discontinued operation. Your 5-year consolidated series is FY2021–FY2025. Explain which years are already re-presented, which are not, the data sources you would use to remove the division from the older years, and how the EBITDA margin trend could be misread if you do not.

Q5. Break-flag dashboard [L3]

Design a Power BI or Excel visual for a 10-year revenue and EBITDA margin trend of an FMCG company that clearly marks the GST break, the Ind AS 116 break and one acquisition. Specify the measures (or formulas), the visual type, and how a user toggles between reported and bridged series.
