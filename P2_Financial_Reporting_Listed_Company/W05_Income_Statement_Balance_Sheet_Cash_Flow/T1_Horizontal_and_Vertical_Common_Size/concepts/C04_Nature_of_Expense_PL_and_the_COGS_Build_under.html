Key concept

Nature-of-Expense P&L and the COGS Build under Schedule III Div II

Concept: Schedule III Div II P&L classifies expenses by nature; COGS = materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + change in inventories

Statement

\[ \text{COGS} = \text{CMC} + \text{PST} + \Delta\text{Inv} \]

where \(\text{CMC}\) = cost of materials consumed (opening raw materials + purchases of raw materials − closing raw materials), \(\text{PST}\) = purchases of stock-in-trade (goods bought for resale), and \(\Delta\text{Inv}\) = changes in inventories of finished goods, work-in-progress and stock-in-trade (opening − closing; positive when inventory falls, negative when it builds).

\[ \text{Gross margin} = 1 - \frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Revenue from operations}} \]

Explanation

Ind AS 1 para 99 (as modified for Ind AS) requires an analysis of expenses using the nature of expense method; the function-of-expense ("cost of sales") option available under IAS 1 has been removed. Schedule III Division II implements this with these expense lines: cost of materials consumed; purchases of stock-in-trade; changes in inventories of finished goods, work-in-progress and stock-in-trade; employee benefits expense; finance costs; depreciation and amortisation expense; other expenses.

Consequences for analysis:

  • COGS is an analyst construct. It contains only materials and traded goods. Factory labour, power and fuel, stores and spares, factory depreciation and other conversion costs are not in the derived COGS — they sit in employee benefits expense, other expenses and D&A. A "gross margin" computed this way is a material margin (revenue minus material cost), not an IFRS-style gross margin after conversion costs.
  • Changes in inventories absorb overhead effects. Under Ind AS 2, finished goods and WIP are valued including conversion costs. When inventory builds, the changes-in-inventories line is negative and includes capitalised conversion costs (labour, overheads) — so building stock reduces "COGS" by more than the material content and can flatter the material margin.
  • Some companies add their own sub-totals (e.g., "gross contribution") — permitted only within Ind AS 1.85–85A constraints (see c05).
  • Excise duty was shown as a separate expense pre-GST; it should be netted from revenue rather than treated as COGS for trend analysis.
  • Manufacturers vs traders: a trader (retailer) has mostly purchases of stock-in-trade; a manufacturer has mostly materials consumed. A shift between them signals make-vs-buy change.

For peer comparisons with global companies reporting cost of sales by function (IAS 1), reclassify carefully: their cost of sales includes conversion costs and production depreciation, so their gross margins are structurally lower than an Indian material margin.

Illustration

₹ crore FY2025
Revenue from operations 4,800
Cost of materials consumed 2,050
Purchases of stock-in-trade 410
Changes in inventories of FG, WIP and SIT (60)
COGS (derived) 2,400
Material/gross margin 50.0%
Employee benefits expense (incl. factory wages ₹180 crore) 420
Other expenses (incl. power & fuel ₹210 crore, stores ₹70 crore) 980
D&A (incl. plant ₹140 crore) 190

An IFRS-style cost of sales would add factory wages, power, stores and plant depreciation (180 + 210 + 70 + 140 = ₹600 crore), giving cost of sales ₹3,000 crore and a gross margin of 37.5% — 1,250 bps below the Schedule III-derived figure. The inventory build of ₹60 crore reduces derived COGS; if ₹20 crore of it represents capitalised conversion cost, material margin is flattered by about 40 bps.

Connections

The COGS build underlies s01 (indexing COGS components) and s02 (common-size gross margin), and drives the margin diagnosis in s05. The nature-vs-function distinction is the subject of the related IFRS/IAS 1 item in the topic overview and links to IFRS 18 (effective 2027). In modelling (Week 13), COGS is forecast as % of revenue or via unit economics, and inventory days are computed on this derived COGS.

Practice Questions

Q1. Derive COGS and margin [L1]

From the following (₹ crore), compute COGS and gross margin: revenue from operations 6,200; opening raw materials 340, purchases of raw materials 2,980, closing raw materials 390; purchases of stock-in-trade 620; opening FG/WIP/SIT 710, closing FG/WIP/SIT 805.

Q2. Reconcile to a global peer [L2]

A European peer reports gross margin of 41% under IAS 1 function-of-expense. Your Indian company's Schedule III-derived gross margin is 54%. Its notes disclose factory employee cost ₹260 crore, power and fuel ₹310 crore, consumables ₹90 crore and plant depreciation ₹150 crore on revenue ₹5,000 crore. Restate the Indian margin on a comparable basis and comment.

💡 Hint: Add conversion costs from the expense-by-nature notes to the derived COGS.

Q3. Inventory build and margin quality [L3]

A manufacturer's derived gross margin rose 150 bps in FY2025 while inventory days rose from 62 to 88 and capacity utilisation rose from 70% to 85% against flat volumes sold. Explain the mechanism by which the margin may have been flattered, how to quantify it from the notes, and which audit procedure (SA 501, Ind AS 2) would address it.
