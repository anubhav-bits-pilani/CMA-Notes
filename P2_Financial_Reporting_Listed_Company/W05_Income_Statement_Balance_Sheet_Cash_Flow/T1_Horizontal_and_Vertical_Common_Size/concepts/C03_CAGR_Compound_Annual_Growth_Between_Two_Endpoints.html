Key concept

CAGR: Compound Annual Growth Between Two Endpoints

Concept: CAGR = (Ending value ÷ Beginning value)^(1/n) − 1, n = number of years between endpoints

Statement

\[ \text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{V_{\text{end}}}{V_{\text{begin}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \]

where \(V_{\text{end}}\) is the value in the final year, \(V_{\text{begin}}\) is the value in the first year, and \(n\) is the number of years between the two endpoints (FY2020 to FY2025: \(n = 5\); a five-column table FY2021–FY2025 has \(n = 4\)).

Explanation

CAGR is the constant annual growth rate that would take the beginning value to the ending value. It is the standard way to summarise multi-year growth in research reports, offer documents and board presentations, and it feeds forecast sanity checks (is the forecast CAGR consistent with history and industry growth?).

Key properties and conditions:

  • Endpoint-only: the path in between is ignored. A business that grew 40% then shrank 30% has the same CAGR as one that grew steadily. Always show an index or YoY series alongside.
  • Base-year sensitivity: an abnormal beginning value (COVID FY2020-21) inflates CAGR; an abnormal ending value (a one-off boom year) does the same. Use normal endpoints or present multiple windows (3-year and 5-year).
  • Counting \(n\) correctly: the most frequent error is using the number of columns rather than the number of intervals. Overstating \(n\) understates CAGR.
  • Undefined for non-positive values: if either endpoint is zero or negative (losses, negative working capital), CAGR is not meaningful. Use absolute change, margin change, or a positive metric (EBITDA instead of PAT).
  • CAGR vs arithmetic mean of YoY: the arithmetic mean exceeds CAGR whenever growth varies (volatility drag; approximately mean − variance ÷ 2). Do not present the arithmetic mean as average growth.
  • Partial periods: for non-annual intervals use \(n\) in years with fractions (e.g., 3.5 years from 31 March 2022 to 30 September 2025), with LTM values at each end.
  • Real vs nominal: nominal CAGR includes inflation; real CAGR ≈ (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.
Explicit:         =(F5/B5)^(1/4)-1
Built-in:         =RRI(4,B5,F5)
Dynamic n:        =(F5/B5)^(1/(YEAR(F$2)-YEAR(B$2)))-1
Guarded:          =IF(MIN(B5,F5)<=0,"n.m.",RRI(4,B5,F5))

Illustration

Trent-type value retailer, revenue (₹ crore): FY2021 2,600, FY2022 4,500, FY2023 7,900, FY2024 12,400, FY2025 17,000.

\[ \text{CAGR}_{21\text{–}25} = \left(\frac{17{,}000}{2{,}600}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}} - 1 = 6.538^{0.25} - 1 \approx 59.9\% \]

\[ \text{CAGR}_{22\text{–}25} = \left(\frac{17{,}000}{4{,}500}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1 = 3.778^{0.333} - 1 \approx 55.7\% \]

FY2021 was COVID-affected, so the 4-year CAGR includes a rebound; the 3-year figure from a more normal base is the better summary, though the business is genuinely high-growth. Using \(n = 5\) by mistake on the first calculation would give about 45.6% — a large understatement.

(Figures are illustrative, not Trent's reported numbers.)

Connections

CAGR appears in s07 (YoY vs CAGR vs index), uses the same base-year discipline as c01 (trend index) and is linked to it by \(I_{\text{end}} = 100 \times (1+\text{CAGR})^n\). The pitfall of indexing from an abnormal base applies equally to CAGR. Practice Questions

Q1. Correct n and window choice [L1]

PAT (₹ crore): FY2019 410, FY2020 380, FY2021 120, FY2022 450, FY2023 520, FY2024 610, FY2025 700. Compute the CAGR for FY2019–FY2025, FY2021–FY2025 and FY2022–FY2025, and state which you would publish and why.

Q2. Volatility drag [L2]

Revenue grows +50%, −30%, +40%, −10% over four years from a base of ₹1,000 crore. Compute the ending value, the CAGR and the arithmetic mean of YoY growth, and explain the difference in one paragraph.

💡 Hint: Multiply the growth factors to get the ending value first.

Q3. Partial-period CAGR [L2]

LTM revenue at 30 September 2025 is ₹8,400 crore; FY2022 revenue (year to 31 March 2022) was ₹5,100 crore. Compute the CAGR using fractional years and write the Excel formula that calculates \(n\) from two dates.

Q4. Forecast sanity check [L3]

A management plan projects revenue rising from ₹6,000 crore (FY2025) to ₹15,000 crore (FY2030). Historical 5-year CAGR was 14% and industry growth is guided at 10–12%. Compute the implied plan CAGR and draft three challenge questions you would raise in an FP&A review.
