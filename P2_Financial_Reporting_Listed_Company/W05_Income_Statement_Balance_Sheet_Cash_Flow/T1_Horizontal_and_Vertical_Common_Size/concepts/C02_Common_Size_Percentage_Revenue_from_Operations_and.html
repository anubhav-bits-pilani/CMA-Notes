Key concept

Common-Size Percentage: Revenue from Operations and Total Assets

Concept: Common-size % = Line item ÷ Revenue from operations (P&L) or ÷ Total assets (BS)

Statement

For the statement of profit and loss:

\[ \text{CS}^{\text{P\&L}}_{i,t} = \frac{X_{i,t}}{\text{RFO}_t} \times 100 \]

For the balance sheet:

\[ \text{CS}^{\text{BS}}_{j,t} = \frac{B_{j,t}}{\text{TA}_t} \times 100 \]

where \(X_{i,t}\) is P&L line \(i\) in year \(t\), \(\text{RFO}_t\) is revenue from operations (net of GST, discounts, rebates and expected returns), \(B_{j,t}\) is balance-sheet line \(j\) at the end of year \(t\), and \(\text{TA}_t\) is total assets (= total equity and liabilities).

Explanation

Common-size analysis removes scale so that cost structure and asset/funding mix can be compared across years and companies.

Why revenue from operations and not total income. Schedule III Div II presents revenue from operations, other income and total income. Other income — interest on bank deposits, FVTPL gains on liquid and debt mutual funds, dividends, profit on sale of investments — is not generated by the operating cost base. Including it in the denominator understates every cost ratio and, if EBITDA includes other income, overstates margin. For Indian companies with large treasury books (common among consumer, IT and pharma companies), the distortion can be several percentage points. Present other income as a memo line below EBITDA.

Why total assets for the balance sheet. Total assets is the one figure that equals both sides of the balance sheet, so asset composition (fixed vs working capital vs treasury) and funding composition (equity, borrowings, lease liabilities, payables, advances) are expressed on a single base that sums to 100%.

Exceptions and supplements.

  • Where other operating revenue (export incentives, PLI, scrap) is large and volatile, add a second denominator: revenue from sale of products and services.
  • For working-capital lines, a revenue denominator (or days) is often more informative than total assets.
  • For treasury-heavy companies, add an operating-assets denominator (total assets − cash − liquid investments − non-operating assets) to see operating asset mix.
  • Agent vs principal (Ind AS 115.B34–B38) and commodity pass-through (gold in jewellery) make common-size gross margins non-comparable across peers.

Illustration

₹ crore Company A Company B
Revenue from operations 5,000 5,000
Other income 50 450
Employee benefits expense 450 450
EBITDA excl. other income 700 700
Employee % of RFO 9.0% 9.0%
Employee % of total income 8.9% 8.3%
EBITDA incl. OI ÷ total income 14.9% 21.1%

On the correct basis both companies have identical 14.0% EBITDA margins and 9.0% employee cost. On a total-income basis with other income included, B appears to earn a 21.1% margin — purely because of its treasury book.

Balance sheet: if B's total assets are ₹8,000 crore including ₹3,000 crore of cash and liquid funds (37.5%), its asset turnover on total assets (0.63x) is far below A's, though operating asset turnover may be identical.

Connections

This concept is applied in s02 (building common-size P&L and BS) and underlies s05 (margin decomposition in bps). The denominator choice links to the pitfall on total income and to Topic 2 (other income stripped from core EBITDA) and Topic 3 (separating surplus cash from operating assets). In practice, common-size tables feed the assumption sheet of the 3-statement model (Week 13), where costs are forecast as % of revenue and working capital as days.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both bases [L1]

Revenue from operations ₹3,600 crore; other income ₹240 crore (of which FVTPL gain on debt funds ₹150 crore); COGS ₹2,050 crore; employee cost ₹380 crore; other expenses ₹610 crore. Compute gross margin, employee %, other-expense % and EBITDA margin on revenue from operations, then compute EBITDA including other income as a % of total income, and quantify the distortion in bps.

Q2. Choosing a supplementary denominator [L2]

An auto-component exporter's revenue from operations of ₹2,200 crore includes RoDTEP and PLI incentives of ₹140 crore (FY2024: ₹40 crore). Its balance sheet total of ₹3,100 crore includes ₹700 crore of liquid funds. Explain which supplementary denominators you would add to the common-size P&L and BS, and why.

💡 Hint: Think about what would happen to margins if the incentive schemes ended.

Q3. Funding-mix reading [L2]

A retailer's common-size balance sheet shows lease liabilities 28% of total assets, trade payables 22%, equity 41%, borrowings 2%. A peer shows lease liabilities 6%, trade payables 12%, equity 52%, borrowings 18%. Explain what each structure implies for EBITDA comparability, net debt and liquidity risk.
