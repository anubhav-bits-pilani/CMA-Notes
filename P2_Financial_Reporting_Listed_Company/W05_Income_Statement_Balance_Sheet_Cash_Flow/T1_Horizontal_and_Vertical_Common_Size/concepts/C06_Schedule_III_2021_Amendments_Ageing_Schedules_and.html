Key concept

Schedule III 2021 Amendments: Ageing Schedules and the 11 Ratios

Concept: Schedule III 2021 amendments: ageing of receivables, payables, CWIP; 11 ratios with explanation when change exceeds 25%

Statement

MCA's notification of 24 March 2021 amended Schedule III (Divisions I, II and III), effective for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2021 (first applied to FY2021-22 financial statements). Among the additions relevant to trend analysis:

Disclosure Required content
Trade receivables ageing Undisputed and disputed, each split into considered good, significant increase in credit risk, credit impaired; buckets from due date: < 6 months, 6 months–1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years, > 3 years (plus not due, commonly shown)
Trade payables ageing MSME, others, disputed dues–MSME, disputed dues–others; buckets from due date: < 1 year, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years
CWIP ageing Projects in progress and projects temporarily suspended; < 1, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years; plus a completion schedule for projects overdue or whose cost exceeds the original plan
Intangible assets under development Same ageing and completion schedule as CWIP
Ratios 11 ratios for current and previous year, with explanation for any change of more than 25% versus the preceding year

The 11 ratios (Division II, "Additional Regulatory Information"):

  1. Current ratio
  2. Debt-equity ratio
  3. Debt service coverage ratio
  4. Return on equity ratio
  5. Inventory turnover ratio
  6. Trade receivables turnover ratio
  7. Trade payables turnover ratio
  8. Net capital turnover ratio
  9. Net profit ratio
  10. Return on capital employed
  11. Return on investment

The schedule prescribes the ratio names and requires numerator and denominator to be disclosed; the ICAI Guidance Note on Division II suggests formulae (e.g., ROCE = EBIT ÷ capital employed, where capital employed = tangible net worth + total debt + deferred tax liability).

Explanation

The amendment converted several analytical checks that analysts and auditors previously performed informally into mandatory disclosures, which makes them high-value inputs for horizontal analysis:

  • Ageing schedules expose deterioration that totals hide. A receivables balance growing in line with revenue can still be deteriorating if the > 1 year bucket is rising. Disputed balances signal litigation risk and ECL adequacy questions (Ind AS 109).
  • MSME payables ageing links to the MSMED Act 2006 (payment within 45 days) and to s.43B(h) of the Income-tax Act 1961 (from FY2023-24, deduction for dues to micro and small enterprises only on payment within the MSMED time limit) — overdue MSME payables now have tax consequences.
  • CWIP ageing surfaces stalled projects, the asset-bloat signal discussed in s06, and links to the Ind AS 23 suspension of borrowing-cost capitalisation.
  • Ratios with 25% explanations provide management's own narrative for big movements — a free source of explanations for your trend commentary. But formulas are company-chosen within the guidance, so they are not consistent across companies; recompute on your own definitions.

Other 2021 additional regulatory information items worth reading for quality checks: title deeds not held in the company's name, revaluation by registered valuer, loans to promoters/directors/KMPs/related parties repayable on demand, benami property proceedings, quarterly returns to banks not agreeing with books, wilful defaulter status, transactions with struck-off companies, compliance with layering rules, schemes of arrangement, utilisation of borrowed funds and share premium through intermediaries, undisclosed income, CSR and crypto holdings. Promoter shareholding (with changes) was also added to the share capital note.

Illustration

Trade receivables ageing (₹ crore, undisputed considered good):

Bucket FY2024 FY2025
Not due 520 560

6 months – 1 year 30 85
1–2 years 15 60

Total 750 920

Total receivables grew 22.7% against revenue growth of 20% — a mild bloat ratio. But balances overdue > 6 months rose from ₹50 crore to ₹170 crore (from 6.7% to 18.5% of the total). The reported receivables turnover ratio may have moved by less than 25%, avoiding an explanation, yet the ageing reveals a collection problem worth an ECL challenge.

Connections

These disclosures feed s06 (asset bloat: receivables, CWIP) and the restated comparatives discipline of s03 (the 2021 amendments themselves required regrouping of current maturities of long-term debt and security deposits). The ROCE and capital-employed definitions connect to Topic 3 c04 and c05 and to ROIC in Week 6. In audit practice, CARO 2020 and the ageing schedules are reviewed together; in modelling, ageing trends inform working-capital day assumptions (Week 13–14).

Practice Questions

Q1. Ratio change explanation [L1]

A company's FY2025 debt-equity ratio is 0.42 (FY2024: 0.31), trade receivables turnover 7.8 (FY2024: 9.6) and ROCE 18.2% (FY2024: 21.0%). State which ratios require an explanation under Schedule III and draft a one-sentence explanation template for each.

Q2. MSME payables ageing and tax [L2]

Trade payables to micro and small enterprises at 31 March 2025 are ₹62 crore, of which ₹14 crore are overdue beyond 45 days and remain unpaid on the date of filing the return. Explain the Schedule III ageing presentation, the effect under s.43B(h) on taxable income, and the consequence for your normalized effective tax rate.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the disallowance creates a timing difference and how it shows in the tax reconciliation.

Q3. CWIP completion schedule reading [L2]

A company's CWIP note shows a ₹380 crore greenfield project with original completion date March 2024, revised to March 2026, and original cost ₹300 crore revised to ₹420 crore. Explain how the CWIP ageing and completion schedule present this project and list three analytical implications for ROCE and the forecast capex in your model.

Q4. Recompute vs reported ratios [L3]

Two peers disclose ROCE of 24% and 19% under Schedule III. Peer A defines capital employed as total assets − current liabilities; Peer B uses tangible net worth + total debt + DTL, and includes lease liabilities in debt. Explain why the disclosed figures are not comparable and set out a common definition you would apply in your peer table.
