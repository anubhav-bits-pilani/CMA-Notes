Key concept

Ind AS 1 Paras 85/85A: Additional Line Items and Subtotals

Concept: Ind AS 1 para 85/85A: additional line items and subtotals allowed only if relevant, consistent and not more prominent than mandated totals

Statement

Ind AS 1.85: an entity shall present additional line items (including by disaggregating the line items required by para 82), headings and subtotals in the statement of profit and loss when such presentation is relevant to an understanding of its financial performance.

Ind AS 1.85A: when an entity presents subtotals under para 85, those subtotals shall:

  1. comprise line items made up of amounts recognised and measured in accordance with Ind AS;
  2. be presented and labelled in a manner that makes the line items constituting the subtotal clear and understandable;
  3. be consistent from period to period, in accordance with para 45; and
  4. not be displayed with more prominence than the subtotals and totals required by Ind AS for the statement of profit and loss.

Ind AS 1.85B further requires the line items in the statement of profit and loss to reconcile any additional subtotals with the subtotals or totals required by Ind AS.

Explanation

These paragraphs (introduced through the Disclosure Initiative amendments) govern how far a company can go in presenting its own performance measures — EBITDA, "operating profit", "gross contribution" — on the face of the statutory statement. Schedule III Division II itself permits additional line items where required by Ind AS or relevant, and the ICAI Guidance Note on Division II indicates that a company may present EBITDA as an additional line item on the face of the statement of profit and loss, provided its measurement basis is disclosed and applied consistently.

Implications for analysts:

  • A face-of-statement EBITDA is still company-defined. Ind AS does not define EBITDA. Whether it includes other income, exceptional items or lease effects varies by company. Read the accounting policy for the subtotal.
  • Consistency requirement: if the company changes the definition, comparatives must be restated (para 45 consistency, para 41 reclassification). A definition change without restatement is a review point.
  • No more prominence: company subtotals cannot be shown in bold or larger type than profit for the year or total comprehensive income; they must reconcile through the line items presented.
  • Measured per Ind AS: the subtotal must be built from Ind AS-measured amounts. "Adjusted" items (e.g., adding back ESOP cost, excluding Ind AS 116) cannot form part of a face-of-statement subtotal — those belong in investor presentations as non-GAAP measures, outside the financial statements.
  • Para 97–98: separately, material items must be disclosed by nature and amount (the basis of the "exceptional items" line); para 87 prohibits "extraordinary items" (see Topic 2).

Outside the financial statements (Reg 30 investor presentations, earnings calls), India has no SEC Regulation G-style non-GAAP regime. IFRS 18 (effective 2027 for IFRS reporters) will bring "management-defined performance measures" into an audited note with reconciliations; Ind AS has not yet notified an equivalent.

Illustration

A consumer company presents on the face of its P&L (₹ crore): Revenue from operations 8,000; Total expenses before D&A and finance cost 6,720; EBITDA 1,280; Other income 210; D&A 300; Finance costs 90; Profit before exceptional items and tax 1,100.

Review points:

  • EBITDA here excludes other income (other income is added after EBITDA) — acceptable if labelled and consistent.
  • If next year the company moves "other income" above EBITDA without restating the prior year, EBITDA appears to grow 16% (1,280 + 210 = 1,490) for no operational reason — breaches consistency and must be challenged.
  • If the investor presentation shows "Adjusted EBITDA ₹1,360 crore" excluding ₹80 crore ESOP cost, that figure cannot appear on the face of the statutory P&L as a subtotal because it is not built only from Ind AS line items as presented.

Connections

This concept supports s02 (which subtotals to rely on when building common-size tables) and s03 (definition changes as reclassifications). It connects to Topic 2 s01 (reported vs adjusted EBITDA) and c04 of Topic 2 (paras 87 and 97), and to the IFRS 18 related item in the topic overview. In FP&A practice, it frames how management MIS metrics can — and cannot — be mirrored in statutory reporting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Subtotal compliance check [L2]

A company proposes to present on the face of its Ind AS statement of profit and loss: (a) "Gross contribution" = revenue − materials consumed − freight outward; (b) "Adjusted EBITDA" excluding ESOP cost and Ind AS 116 effects; (c) "EBITDA" excluding other income, in bold larger font than profit for the year. Evaluate each against Ind AS 1.85–85B.

Q2. Definition change detection [L2]

FY2024 report: EBITDA ₹2,150 crore (excluding other income ₹180 crore). FY2025 report: EBITDA ₹2,640 crore, FY2024 comparative shown as ₹2,330 crore; a footnote says "EBITDA now includes other income". Compute the organic EBITDA growth on a consistent basis, and state what the company should have disclosed.

💡 Hint: Put both years on the same definition before computing growth.

Q3. Interview: why is EBITDA not defined? [L1]

Explain in four or five sentences why Ind AS does not define EBITDA, what paras 85/85A allow, and what an analyst must do before using a reported EBITDA figure.
