Key concept

Trend Index: Item in Year t over Base-Year Item × 100

Concept: Trend index = Item_t ÷ Item_base × 100

Statement

\[ I_t = \frac{X_t}{X_{b}} \times 100 \]

where \(I_t\) is the index value for year \(t\), \(X_t\) is the value of the line item (₹ crore) in year \(t\), and \(X_b\) is the value of the same line item in the chosen base year \(b\). By construction \(I_b = 100\). An index of 135 means the item is 35% larger than in the base year.

The relative index compares an item's path to revenue:

\[ R_t = \frac{I_t^{\text{item}}}{I_t^{\text{revenue}}} \times 100 \]

\(R_t > 100\) means the item grew faster than revenue since the base year.

Explanation

The trend index rescales every line to a common starting point so that lines of very different size — revenue of ₹20,000 crore and finance cost of ₹80 crore — can be read on one scale. It is the core tool of horizontal analysis.

Conditions for a meaningful index:

  • Positive, material base: if \(X_b\) is zero, negative or tiny, the index explodes or flips sign. Report "n.m." and use absolute change or common-size instead. This applies to changes in inventories, exceptional items, other income for a company with no treasury, and PAT in a loss year.
  • Normal base year: an abnormal base (COVID FY2020-21, a year with a large one-off, a strike or plant shutdown) makes every later index look like growth or decline that is merely recovery. Rebase to a normal year or show two bases.
  • Consistent basis: the same accounting basis (post-Ind AS 116 vs pre), the same perimeter (standalone vs consolidated, pre/post acquisition) and restated comparatives throughout.
  • Same unit: convert everything to ₹ crore before indexing; mixing lakh and crore extracts is a common data error.

The index is linked to CAGR: \(I_{\text{end}} = 100 \times (1 + \text{CAGR})^n\). It is also linked to YoY growth: \(I_t \div I_{t-1} - 1 = g_t\). The index is therefore a path-preserving complement to both.

Rebasing an index from base \(b\) to base \(c\) does not need the raw data: \(I_t^{(c)} = I_t^{(b)} \div I_c^{(b)} \times 100\).

Illustration

₹ crore FY2021 FY2022 FY2023 FY2024 FY2025
Revenue from operations 2,000 2,300 2,700 3,000 3,300
Other expenses 380 450 560 650 740
Revenue index 100 115 135 150 165
Other expenses index 100 118 147 171 195
Relative index 100 103 109 114 118

Other expenses grew 95% against revenue growth of 65%. Their share of revenue rose from 19.0% to 22.4%, a 340 bps compression. The relative index climbing every year signals persistent creep rather than a one-year blip — the next step is to index the sub-lines of the other expenses note (advertising, freight, power, rent) to find the source.

Rebasing to FY2022: the FY2025 revenue index becomes 165 ÷ 115 × 100 = 143.5.

Connections

The trend index is the engine of s01 (indexing Schedule III P&L lines), s05 (margin compression via relative indices) and s06 (asset bloat ratios). It depends on s03 (restated comparatives) and s04 (structural breaks) for clean inputs, and relates to c03 (CAGR) and s07 (YoY vs CAGR vs index). In modelling practice, the index sheet sits beside the historical columns of the 3-statement model (Week 13) and supports variance commentary in FP&A board packs.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and rebase [L1]

Employee benefits expense (₹ crore): FY2021 310, FY2022 342, FY2023 395, FY2024 448, FY2025 506. Revenue: 2,600, 2,950, 3,400, 3,800, 4,150. Compute the item index, revenue index and relative index with FY2021 = 100, then rebase the employee-cost index to FY2023 = 100 without using the raw data.

Q2. When not to index [L2]

For each of the following, state whether a trend index is meaningful and, if not, what you would use instead: (a) exceptional items of −₹40 crore, +₹15 crore, −₹120 crore; (b) finance cost of ₹1.2 crore rising to ₹48 crore after a debt-funded acquisition; (c) PAT of −₹60 crore in the base year; (d) changes in inventories of −₹35 crore, +₹20 crore, −₹50 crore.

💡 Hint: Consider sign changes and the materiality of the base.

Q3. Interview: index vs CAGR [L1]

Explain to an interviewer, in three or four sentences, what an index series shows that a CAGR does not, and give the formula linking the two.
