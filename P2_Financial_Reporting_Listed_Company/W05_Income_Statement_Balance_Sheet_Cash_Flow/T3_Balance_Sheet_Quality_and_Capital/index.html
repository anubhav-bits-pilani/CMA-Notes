Roadmap topic: Balance Sheet Quality & Capital Employed: Separating Operating Invested Capital from surplus cash/investments and non-operating assets

Overview

The Schedule III balance sheet tells you what a company owns and owes, sorted by liquidity and legal form. It does not tell you how much capital the operating business actually uses, how much sits idle in treasury or non-core holdings, what claims rank ahead of shareholders beyond reported borrowings, or how much of book equity is "soft". This topic rebuilds the balance sheet by economic function: operating invested capital on one side, surplus cash and non-operating assets alongside, and the full set of debt and debt-like claims and equity that finance them.

The output drives almost every downstream metric. ROIC and ROCE (Week 6) need a clean invested-capital denominator; the EV-to-equity bridge (Week 16) needs net debt, debt-like items, NCI and non-operating assets; credit appraisal needs tangible net worth and adjusted leverage; FP&A divisional performance needs capital charges on operating assets only. For someone coming from audit and GL finalisation, the advantage is knowing what sits behind each Schedule III line — the notes on other financial assets, provisions, other bank balances and contract balances — and the disadvantage to overcome is the habit of accepting the statutory grouping.

Sequence: start with the four-bucket reclassification (s01). Then define each bucket precisely: operating NWC (s02); surplus cash and treasury across five Schedule III lines (s03); non-operating assets — investment property, stakes, ICDs, held-for-sale (s04); soft assets — goodwill, intangibles under development, capital advances, DTA/MAT credit, tax refunds (s05); debt-like items — leases, gold metal loans, customer schemes, unfunded gratuity, NCI puts (s06); and two specific traps — stalled CWIP and earmarked bank balances (s07). The concepts formalise operating IC from the asset side (c01), the financing-side check (c02), net debt (c03), the Indian capital-employed definitions (c04), bank tangible net worth (c05) and Ind AS 32 equity-liability classification (c06).

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 109 ECL and Ind AS 115 contract balances

Ind AS 109 ECL on receivables and provision matrix; Ind AS 115 contract balances → Week 3/4 refresh

Trade receivables in operating NWC are measured net of an expected credit loss allowance. Under the simplified approach (Ind AS 109.5.5.15), lifetime ECL is recognised, commonly through a provision matrix of loss rates by ageing bucket, adjusted for forward-looking information. Allowance releases raise net receivables and profit, so quality-of-NWC analysis should look at gross balances, ageing (Schedule III 2021) and allowance coverage. Ind AS 115 contract assets (conditional rights, e.g., unbilled revenue) and contract liabilities (advances, deferred revenue, loyalty points) are operating NWC items that Schedule III does not show separately; the Ind AS 115.116–118 disclosures of contract balances and their movements are the source. Weeks 3–4 covered recognition; here the focus is classification and quality.

US GAAP ASC 842 single lease cost

US GAAP ASC 842 operating leases keep single lease cost in EBITDA; adjust before peer comparison

Under ASC 842, lessees recognise ROU assets and lease liabilities for operating leases (as under Ind AS 116), but the P&L shows a single straight-line operating lease cost within operating expenses — no split into depreciation and interest. So a US GAAP retailer's EBITDA is effectively pre-Ind AS 116 (rent inside EBITDA), while its balance sheet carries operating lease liabilities. Finance leases under ASC 842 are split, like Ind AS 116. For comparison, either deduct lease payments from Ind AS peers' EBITDA and exclude lease liabilities from their net debt, or add back operating lease cost to the US peer's EBITDA and include its operating lease liabilities in net debt. The invested-capital side must follow the same choice (ROU assets in or out).

ROIC and the EV bridge downstream

Invested capital feeds ROIC → Week 6 (w6_t1); EV bridge (net debt, NCI, investments) → Week 16

Operating invested capital is the denominator of ROIC (NOPAT ÷ IC) in Week 6, where it is combined with NOPAT built on the same boundaries — lease basis, other income excluded, operating taxes. Every bucket created here has a role in valuation in Week 16: net debt and debt-like items are deducted from EV, NCI is deducted (or the NCI put liability instead), surplus cash and non-operating assets (investment property at fair value, strategic stakes, associates) are added. If a balance is in operating IC it must not appear in the bridge, and vice versa. A consistent mapping table built this week is therefore reused directly in both later weeks.

Titan gold metal loans and customer schemes

Titan gold metal loans and customer schemes: debt vs operating liability judgement (relevant for this week's exercise)

Titan funds a large part of its gold inventory through gold metal loans from banks — gold borrowed, repaid in gold or its rupee value at maturity, with the price exposure offsetting inventory price risk (hedge accounting). It also collects customer advances under jewellery purchase schemes, structured to be appropriated within 365 days so as to fall outside the deposit rules. Both reduce net working capital substantially. Whether gold loans are debt (bank funding, interest-bearing) or operating (inventory-linked, hedge-neutral), and whether scheme advances are operating (settled in goods) or deposit-like (cash claims under stress), changes Titan's net debt, ROIC and comparability with Trent (whose main debt-like item is lease liabilities). For the exercise, state the policy explicitly, apply it to all three years, and show sensitivity to the alternative (s06).

Common pitfalls

All current investments treated as surplus

Treating all current investments as surplus when some are working-capital buffers or encumbered

Analysts sum current investments and cash and call the total surplus. But seasonal businesses hold cash at year-end that they will consume within months (jewellers before Akshaya Tritiya and the wedding season, fertiliser companies awaiting subsidies), some liquid funds or deposits are pledged as security for borrowings or guarantees, and some cash is held in escrow or unspent CSR accounts. The error comes from reading the balance sheet face rather than the notes. Correct treatment: deduct operating cash (stated method), exclude encumbered and earmarked balances, and test for seasonal buffers using half-yearly balance sheets (s03, s07). Detection: compare March and September treasury balances; read the borrowings security note and the other bank balances note for liens.

CWIP left in IC without disclosure

Leaving CWIP in invested capital and then penalizing ROIC for growth capex not yet earning returns, without disclosure

During a large expansion, CWIP can be 20–50% of operating IC. Including it while NOPAT reflects only the existing asset base depresses ROIC and makes a company that is investing wisely look like a poor allocator — or, if compared with a peer between expansion cycles, unfairly weak. The error arises from mechanically including everything under PPE. Correct treatment: choose a policy (include, exclude, or exclude only stalled projects), disclose it, and show ROIC on capital in use alongside total ROIC (s07, c01). Detection: compute CWIP as % of IC and the CWIP ageing profile; if large, check that the ROIC commentary addresses it.

Group cash netted against debt regardless of location

Netting cash against debt at the group level when cash sits in subsidiaries with NCI or restrictions

Consolidated net debt nets all group cash against all group debt. But cash in a 51%-owned listed subsidiary belongs 49% to minorities and can reach the parent only through dividends (subject to s.123 distributable profits and the subsidiary board's decisions); cash in overseas subsidiaries may face repatriation taxes or exchange controls; cash in project SPVs may be trapped by lender covenants. Treating it as available to repay parent debt understates parent credit risk. Correct treatment: for valuation, net consolidated cash but deduct NCI at market value; for credit, compute net debt on accessible cash, or analyse parent standalone plus dividend capacity (Topic 5). Detection: compare standalone and consolidated cash; read the Ind AS 112 note for significant restrictions on access to assets (Ind AS 112.13).

Practice Questions

Q1. Titan-type jeweller: full balance-sheet rebuild [L3]

A jeweller's consolidated balance sheet at 31 March 2026 (₹ crore): PPE 2,100; ROU 1,900; CWIP 350 (of which suspended > 2 years 60); goodwill 150; intangibles 220; non-current investments (strategic stake) 280; security deposits for stores 180; capital advances 140; DTA 160; non-current tax assets 120; inventories 38,000; trade receivables 1,300; current investments in liquid funds 2,600; cash and cash equivalents 900; other bank balances 1,400 (unpaid dividend 20, margin money 90); other current assets 1,750. Liabilities: equity 19,660; NCI 380; borrowings 3,200; gold metal loans 9,800 (shown within current borrowings and additional to the borrowings figure); lease liabilities 2,250; customer scheme advances 6,500; trade payables 3,400; other current liabilities 4,800 (statutory dues 700, capex creditors 110, unpaid dividends 20, other operating 3,970); provisions 1,560 (unfunded gratuity 160, other operating 1,400); DTL 0. Revenue FY2026 ₹66,000 crore; operating cash 0.5% of revenue.

(a) Verify the balance sheet balances. (b) Build operating NWC, operating IC (asset side) and the financing check under Policy A (GML as debt) and Policy B (GML as operating). (c) Compute net debt on post-116 basis under both policies. (d) Compute TNW. (e) Write the policy note you would attach to the Titan vs Trent exercise.

💡 Hint: Classify every line once in a mapping table before computing anything; the two IC computations must agree under each policy.

Q2. Trent-type retailer: leases, JVs and capital employed [L3]

A value retailer (₹ crore): PPE 3,800; CWIP 900; ROU 5,600; investment in JVs (equity-accounted) 1,100; inventories 2,700; receivables 120; other operating current assets 600; cash 300; liquid funds 1,800; trade payables 2,200; other operating liabilities 900; borrowings 700; lease liabilities 6,300; equity 6,820. Post-116 EBIT (excluding other income and share of JVs) ₹1,950 crore; lease payments ₹1,150 crore; ROU depreciation ₹820 crore; tax rate 25.168%.

(a) Compute operating IC (post-116) and ROIC including and excluding CWIP. (b) Compute rent-adjusted IC and ROIC. (c) Compute capital employed under both Indian definitions and the matching ROCE. (d) Explain the treatment of the JV investment in IC and in the EV bridge.

Q3. Mid-cap industrial: quality red flags [L3]

A manufacturer's balance sheet shows: capital advances ₹480 crore (₹350 crore to a related EPC contractor); ICDs to promoter-group companies ₹600 crore with interest accrued but unpaid ₹70 crore; CWIP ₹1,200 crore of which ₹700 crore is older than 3 years and ₹500 crore relates to projects temporarily suspended; DTA on carried-forward losses ₹260 crore; non-current tax assets ₹310 crore with disputed tax demands of ₹420 crore in contingent liabilities; investment property at cost ₹40 crore with fair value ₹380 crore; other bank balances ₹420 crore of which ₹300 crore is under lien for the promoter-group company's borrowings. Equity is ₹3,900 crore.

(a) Classify each item and estimate the value you would attribute in an EV bridge. (b) Compute soft assets as % of equity and an adjusted TNW. (c) List the Schedule III 2021, CARO 2020, Companies Act (ss.185, 186, 188) and LODR Reg 23 disclosures and approvals you would check. (d) Draft three questions for the statutory auditor.

Q4. Group cash and NCI puts [L2]

A listed parent (standalone borrowings ₹2,500 crore, standalone cash ₹150 crore) owns 60% of a listed subsidiary holding cash of ₹1,800 crore and no debt, and 75% of an unlisted subsidiary in which it has written a put over the 25% NCI (PV of redemption ₹400 crore). Consolidated borrowings are ₹2,900 crore and consolidated cash ₹2,100 crore.

(a) Compute consolidated net debt including the put liability. (b) Compute parent-accessible net debt for credit purposes. (c) Explain how NCI and the put liability enter the EV-to-equity bridge without double counting.
