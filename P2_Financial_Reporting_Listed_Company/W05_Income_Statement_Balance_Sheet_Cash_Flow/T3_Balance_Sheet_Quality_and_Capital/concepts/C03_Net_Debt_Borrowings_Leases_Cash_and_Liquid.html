Key concept

Net Debt: Borrowings, Leases, Cash and Liquid Investments

Concept: Net debt = Borrowings (+ lease liabilities, if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116) − Cash − Other bank balances − Liquid investments

Statement

\[ \text{ND} = B_{\text{NC}} + B_{\text{C}} + \delta \cdot LL - \text{CCE} - \text{OBB}_{\text{free}} - \text{LI} \]

where \(B_{\text{NC}}\) and \(B_{\text{C}}\) are non-current and current borrowings (current includes current maturities of long-term borrowings under Schedule III 2021 presentation, commercial paper, working-capital loans and overdrafts), \(LL\) is total lease liabilities (current + non-current), \(\delta\) equals 1 when the paired EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 and 0 when it is rent-adjusted, CCE = cash and cash equivalents, \(\text{OBB}_{\text{free}}\) = other bank balances excluding earmarked and lien-marked amounts, and LI = liquid investments (liquid and debt mutual funds, T-bills, CPs, deposits > 12 months in other financial assets) that are surplus. "Adjusted net debt" adds other debt-like items (s06).

Explanation

Net debt measures the claim of lenders net of the cash that could repay them. It drives leverage ratios (net debt/EBITDA, net debt/equity), the EV-to-equity bridge and WACC weights.

Key rules:

  • Lease consistency: include lease liabilities only when EBITDA is post-116 (lease cost below EBITDA). If you deduct lease payments to get rent-adjusted EBITDA, exclude lease liabilities — otherwise the lease is counted twice (once as cost in EBITDA, once as debt).
  • Gross borrowings include all interest-bearing funding: term loans, NCDs, ECBs, commercial paper, working-capital demand loans, cash credit, packing credit, buyer's credit for capex, and bills discounted with recourse if not derecognised.
  • Cash must be free: exclude earmarked balances (unpaid dividend, margin money, lien, escrow, unspent CSR) and operating cash needs (s03, s07).
  • Liquid investments at fair value; exclude strategic equity and non-liquid instruments (value them separately).
  • Group-level netting: cash in subsidiaries with NCI or repatriation restrictions is not fully available to service parent debt (topic pitfall); for credit analysis, net only accessible cash.
  • Timing: net debt is a point-in-time measure. Year-end cash may be seasonally high; use average or covenant-defined measures for leverage.
  • Interest accrued on borrowings: often included in "other financial liabilities"; lenders' covenants may include it.

Connections

It is a rearrangement of the financing check (c02). Practice Questions

Q1. Compute net debt on both bases [L1]

Borrowings: NCDs ₹1,000 crore, term loans ₹650 crore (current maturities ₹150 crore included), commercial paper ₹300 crore, cash credit ₹120 crore. Lease liabilities ₹1,900 crore. Cash and equivalents ₹410 crore; other bank balances ₹600 crore (₹70 crore margin money, ₹18 crore unpaid dividend); liquid funds ₹1,150 crore; strategic equity stake ₹500 crore. Compute net debt on post-116 and pre-116 bases.

Q2. Leverage with matching EBITDA [L2]

For the Q1 company, post-116 EBITDA is ₹2,600 crore and lease payments are ₹480 crore. Compute net debt/EBITDA on both bases and explain which a rating agency would use and why.

💡 Hint: Rent-adjusted EBITDA is post-116 EBITDA minus lease payments.

Q3. Net-debt roll-forward [L2]

Opening net debt (post-116) ₹2,000 crore. During the year: CFO ₹1,400 crore (after lease interest classified in financing), capex ₹700 crore, dividends ₹300 crore, lease principal repaid ₹260 crore, lease interest paid ₹150 crore, new leases recognised (non-cash) ₹420 crore, borrowing interest paid ₹90 crore, FVTPL gain on liquid funds (non-cash) ₹40 crore. Build the closing net-debt roll-forward and identify the non-cash movements.
