Key concept

Financing-Side Check: Invested Capital from Equity and Debt

Concept: Financing check: Invested capital = Total equity + NCI + Debt (+ lease liabilities) − Surplus cash − Non-operating assets

Statement

\[ \text{IC}_{\text{op}} = \text{E} + \text{NCI} + \text{D} + \text{LL} + \text{DL} - \text{SC} - \text{NOA} \pm \text{Tax}_{\text{net}} \]

where E = equity attributable to owners (share capital + other equity), NCI = non-controlling interests, D = borrowings (non-current, current and current maturities), LL = lease liabilities (included if ROU assets are in IC), DL = other debt-like items (unfunded employee benefits, NCI puts, deferred consideration, gold metal loans if treated as debt), SC = surplus cash and liquid investments, NOA = non-operating assets (investment property, strategic stakes, ICDs, assets held for sale), and \(\text{Tax}_{\text{net}}\) = net deferred and current tax balances (DTL added, DTA deducted) if tax balances are excluded from operating IC.

The financing-side figure must equal the asset-side build (c01). Any difference is a mapping error.

Explanation

Every rupee of operating capital is financed by equity holders or lenders (including quasi-lenders). Starting from the financing side provides an independent check on the asset-side build: the two must tie because the balance sheet balances. In practice, the check catches:

  • Items mapped twice (e.g., capex creditors counted as operating liabilities on the asset side and as debt on the financing side).
  • Items not mapped at all (e.g., interest accrued on deposits, derivative balances).
  • Inconsistent lease treatment (ROU in IC but lease liabilities omitted from financing).
  • Tax balances handled differently on each side.

Conceptual points:

  • NCI is included at book value because consolidated IC includes 100% of subsidiaries' operating assets.
  • Surplus cash and non-operating assets are deducted because they are financed by the same equity and debt but do not form part of operating capital.
  • Equity includes OCI reserves (FVOCI reserve on strategic stakes, hedge reserve, FCTR) — those reserves often relate to non-operating assets that are deducted, so the identity still holds.
  • Equity-classified compound instruments (CCPS, equity component of convertibles) sit in E; liability components in D.
  • The financing view also gives net debt (D + LL + DL − SC) and enterprise funding: IC + NOA = E + NCI + net debt.

Illustration

Using the company in c01 (operating IC including goodwill and CWIP ₹7,460 crore):

₹ crore Amount
Equity attributable to owners 6,350
NCI 310
Borrowings 950
Lease liabilities 1,850
Unfunded gratuity (post-tax) 90
Net DTL 160
Less: surplus cash and liquid funds (1,750)
Less: strategic stake and investment property (500)
Total 7,460

The financing side ties to the asset side. Net debt (borrowings + leases + gratuity − surplus cash) = ₹1,140 crore. If the analyst forgot lease liabilities, the financing side would be ₹5,610 crore — a ₹1,850 crore gap that flags the inconsistency immediately.

Connections

This concept is the control check for s01 (four-bucket reclassification) and c01 (asset-side IC). Its components define net debt (c03), the debt-like items of s06 and surplus cash of s03–s04. In valuation (Week 16), the same identity, rearranged, is the EV-to-equity bridge: equity value = EV − net debt − debt-like items − NCI + non-operating assets. In modelling (Week 13), a balance check row on this identity is a standard integrity test.

Practice Questions

Q1. Reconcile both sides [L1]

Operating IC from the asset side is ₹3,820 crore. Financing side: equity ₹3,450 crore; NCI ₹120 crore; borrowings ₹700 crore; lease liabilities ₹600 crore; net DTL ₹90 crore; surplus cash ₹980 crore; investment property ₹110 crore. Compute the financing-side IC and identify the size of any mapping difference.

Q2. Find the mapping error [L2]

The financing-side IC exceeds the asset-side IC by ₹75 crore. The balance sheet includes capex creditors of ₹85 crore (mapped as operating liabilities in NWC and also included in debt-like items), interest accrued on fixed deposits of ₹25 crore (unmapped), and unpaid dividends of ₹35 crore with matching earmarked cash (cash deducted as surplus; liability left unmapped). Identify each error, its direction, and show that correcting them closes the gap.

💡 Hint: Work out whether each error inflates the asset side or the financing side.

Q3. Excel balance check [L2]

Write the Excel formulas for an asset-side IC, a financing-side IC and a check cell that returns "OK" if the two agree within ₹1 crore, pulling from a mapping table with columns Year, Line, Amount, Bucket (Operating, NonOperating, Surplus, Debt, DebtLike, Equity, NCI, Tax).
