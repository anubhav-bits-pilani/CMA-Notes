Key concept

Tangible Net Worth in Bank Credit Appraisal

Concept: Tangible net worth (bank usage) = Net worth − intangibles − DTA − revaluation reserve

Statement

\[ \text{TNW} = \text{NW} - \text{GW} - \text{INT} - \text{DTA} - \text{RR} - \text{MiscExp} \]

where NW = net worth (equity share capital + other equity; for consolidated, owners' equity, with NCI treated per the lender's policy), GW = goodwill, INT = other intangible assets and intangibles under development, DTA = deferred tax assets including MAT credit entitlement (often net of DTL by some lenders, gross by others), RR = revaluation reserve (or, under Ind AS, the revaluation surplus/deemed cost uplift where identifiable), MiscExp = any miscellaneous expenditure not written off or fictitious assets. Many lenders also deduct investments in and loans to group companies (an "adjusted TNW").

Section 2(57) of the Companies Act 2013 defines net worth as paid-up share capital plus reserves created out of profits and securities premium, after deducting accumulated losses, deferred expenditure and miscellaneous expenditure not written off — but not including reserves created out of revaluation of assets, write-back of depreciation and amalgamation.

Explanation

Banks and rating agencies measure the equity cushion available to absorb losses before lenders are hit. Assets that would realise little in a distress sale — goodwill, intangibles, DTA dependent on future profits — and accounting uplifts that are not realised capital (revaluation reserves) are stripped out. TNW is used in:

  • Leverage ratios: TOL/TNW (total outside liabilities ÷ TNW), a standard bank covenant (often ≤ 3x for working-capital borrowers); debt/TNW.
  • Schedule III ROCE (ICAI Guidance Note): capital employed = TNW + total debt + DTL (c04).
  • Eligibility criteria: minimum TNW for tenders, licences and some regulatory approvals.
  • Credit-assessment memos: TNW trend as a key indicator of financial strength.

Ind AS-specific points:

  • Revaluation: Ind AS 16 revaluation model creates revaluation surplus in OCI (other equity); Ind AS 101 deemed-cost fair value on transition goes into retained earnings, not a separate reserve — lenders may ask for the transition uplift to be identified and deducted.
  • OCI reserves: FVOCI reserves on equity investments, cash flow hedge reserves and remeasurement of defined benefit plans sit in other equity. Lenders may exclude unrealised FVOCI gains.
  • MAT credit: presented within DTA under Ind AS — deducted.
  • Compound instruments: equity components of CCPS/CCDs (c06) are in net worth; some lenders treat promoter CCPS as quasi-equity, others insist on conversion.
  • Unsecured promoter loans subordinated to bank debt are often treated as quasi-equity and added to TNW in bank appraisal ("adjusted TNW").

Illustration

₹ crore Amount
Equity share capital 200
Other equity (retained earnings 2,600; securities premium 400; revaluation surplus 300; FVOCI reserve 120) 3,420
Net worth (book) 3,620
Less: goodwill (450)
Less: other intangibles and IAUD (180)
Less: DTA (incl. MAT credit ₹70 crore) (130)
Less: revaluation surplus (300)
TNW 2,560
Total outside liabilities 7,900
TOL/TNW 3.09x

On book net worth, TOL/NW = 2.18x — comfortable; on TNW, 3.09x — above a typical 3.0x covenant. If the lender also deducts ₹200 crore of loans to group companies, adjusted TNW = ₹2,360 crore and TOL/TNW = 3.35x.

Connections

TNW connects to s05 (soft assets — goodwill, intangibles, DTA/MAT credit), c04 (capital employed in the ICAI ROCE definition) and c06 (classification of compound instruments affecting net worth). In credit modelling and covenant compliance (Week 14 debt schedule), TNW-based covenants must be tested in forecasts; in the Saturday assessment, TNW-type adjustments help judge balance-sheet quality.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute TNW and TOL/TNW [L1]

Share capital ₹150 crore; other equity ₹2,350 crore (including revaluation surplus ₹180 crore); goodwill ₹320 crore; intangibles ₹90 crore; DTA ₹75 crore; total outside liabilities ₹5,900 crore. Compute TNW and TOL/TNW, and the TOL/TNW if the bank allows ₹250 crore of subordinated promoter loans as quasi-equity.

Q2. Ind AS 101 deemed cost [L2]

On transition to Ind AS on 1 April 2015, a company elected fair value as deemed cost for land, increasing carrying amount by ₹800 crore with the credit to retained earnings. Its current net worth is ₹4,000 crore. Explain why a bank may treat the ₹800 crore like a revaluation reserve, how you would identify it from the financial statements, and the effect on TNW.

💡 Hint: Look at the first Ind AS financial statements' transition reconciliation.

Q3. Covenant headroom forecast [L3]

A borrower has TNW ₹1,800 crore and TOL ₹5,100 crore with a covenant of TOL/TNW ≤ 3.0x. Its plan includes a ₹400 crore debt-funded acquisition creating ₹300 crore of goodwill, PAT of ₹220 crore and dividends of ₹60 crore. Compute the post-acquisition TOL/TNW and explain two structuring options that would keep the borrower within the covenant.
