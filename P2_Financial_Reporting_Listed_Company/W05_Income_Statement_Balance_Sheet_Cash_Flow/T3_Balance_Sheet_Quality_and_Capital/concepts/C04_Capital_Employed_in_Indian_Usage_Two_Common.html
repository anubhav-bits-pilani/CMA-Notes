Key concept

Capital Employed in Indian Usage: Two Common Definitions

Concept: Capital employed (common Indian usage) = Total equity + Total debt + deferred tax liability, or Total assets − Current liabilities

Statement

Funding-side definition (aligned with the ICAI Guidance Note on Schedule III Division II for ROCE):

\[ \text{CE}_{1} = \text{TNW} + \text{TD} + \text{DTL} \]

where TNW = tangible net worth (c05; many companies use total equity instead), TD = total debt (borrowings, often including lease liabilities), DTL = deferred tax liability.

Asset-side definition (traditional textbook usage):

\[ \text{CE}_{2} = \text{TA} - \text{CL} \]

where TA = total assets and CL = current liabilities (including or excluding short-term borrowings and current maturities, depending on the user).

ROCE per Schedule III ratio disclosure (ICAI Guidance Note):

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Capital employed}} \]

Explanation

"Capital employed" is used in Schedule III ratio disclosures (one of the 11 ratios), in management commentary, in bank appraisal and in older Indian textbooks — but with different definitions. Neither common definition matches operating invested capital (c01):

Difference CE₁ (equity + debt + DTL) CE₂ (TA − CL) Operating IC (c01)
Surplus cash and investments Included (not deducted) Included Excluded
Non-operating assets Included Included Excluded
Current borrowings Included in debt Excluded if in CL (understates CE) In financing side
Lease liabilities Usually included Current portion excluded if in CL Included if post-116
Customer advances, payables Netted implicitly Netted (current) Netted in NWC
Long-term provisions, non-current liabilities Excluded Included in CE Debt-like or operating per policy

Consequences:

  • Cash-rich companies' ROCE is understated under both Indian definitions (surplus cash sits in the denominator, while EBIT may or may not include other income). Many companies report ROCE excluding cash to show a "core" figure — read the definition.
  • TA − CL is sensitive to how short-term debt is classified: a company funding itself with short-term borrowings shows lower CE and higher ROCE than one using term loans — a pure presentation effect. Since 2021, current maturities of long-term borrowings sit in current borrowings, making this more pronounced.
  • EBIT numerator: should match the denominator. If CE includes surplus cash, EBIT should include other income (treasury returns); if CE excludes cash, EBIT should exclude other income.
  • Schedule III ratio comparability: since companies choose formulas, recompute from your own definitions for peer tables (Topic 1 c06).

  • CE₁ (TNW + TD incl. leases + DTL) = 3,700 + 2,300 + 150 = ₹6,150 crore; ROCE = (1,050 + 120) ÷ 6,150 = 19.0%.
  • CE₂ (TA − CL) = 9,000 − 2,700 = ₹6,300 crore; ROCE = 1,170 ÷ 6,300 = 18.6%.
  • Core ROCE excluding cash (CE₁ − surplus cash = 4,550) on EBIT excl. other income = 1,050 ÷ 4,550 = 23.1%.

Three defensible numbers, 18.6% to 23.1%, from one balance sheet.

Connections

This concept links to c05 (tangible net worth used in CE₁), c01–c02 (operating IC as the analytical alternative), and Topic 1 c06 (Schedule III ROCE ratio with 25% explanations). It is the bridge between Indian reporting practice and the ROIC framework of Week 6. In FP&A, divisional ROCE targets must specify the definition; in credit, lenders' "capital employed" in sanction letters often follows CE₁.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute CE and ROCE three ways [L1]

Total equity ₹2,800 crore (including revaluation reserve ₹150 crore); intangibles ₹200 crore; DTA ₹60 crore; borrowings ₹900 crore (current ₹350 crore); lease liabilities ₹400 crore (current ₹80 crore); DTL ₹110 crore; total assets ₹6,100 crore; current liabilities ₹1,900 crore (including current borrowings and current lease liabilities); surplus cash ₹700 crore; EBIT excluding other income ₹720 crore; other income ₹60 crore. Compute CE₁, CE₂ and core CE (excluding surplus cash), and the matching ROCE for each.

Q2. Short-term funding effect [L2]

Two otherwise identical companies each have total assets ₹5,000 crore and borrowings of ₹1,500 crore. Company X funds with 5-year term loans; Company Y funds with 90-day commercial paper rolled over. Other current liabilities are ₹1,000 crore for both and EBIT is ₹600 crore. Compute CE₂ and ROCE for each and explain why the difference is meaningless economically.

💡 Hint: Consider where each company's borrowings sit under Schedule III.

Q3. Matching numerator and denominator [L2]

A company reports ROCE of 32% using EBIT including other income of ₹400 crore over capital employed excluding surplus cash of ₹3,000 crore. Its EBIT excluding other income is ₹1,200 crore and CE excluding cash is ₹5,000 crore. Identify the inconsistency, recompute ROCE consistently on both bases, and draft a one-line footnote for your peer table.
