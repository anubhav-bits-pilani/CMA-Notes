Key concept

Ind AS 32 Equity vs Liability: Convertibles, Preference Shares, NCI Puts

Concept: Ind AS 32 classification: compulsorily convertible instruments, preference shares and NCI puts may be equity or liability

Statement

Under Ind AS 32.11 and 32.16, an instrument is equity only if both conditions are met:

  1. It includes no contractual obligation to deliver cash or another financial asset, or to exchange financial instruments under potentially unfavourable conditions; and
  2. If it will or may be settled in the issuer's own equity instruments, it is a non-derivative with no obligation to deliver a variable number of own equity instruments, or a derivative settled by exchanging a fixed amount of cash for a fixed number of own equity instruments ("fixed-for-fixed").

Otherwise it is a financial liability (or contains a liability component — compound instrument, Ind AS 32.28).

Ind AS 32.23: a contract containing an obligation for an entity to purchase its own equity instruments for cash — including a written put option over NCI shares in consolidated statements — gives rise to a financial liability for the present value of the redemption amount, even if the obligation is conditional on the holder exercising.

Explanation

Indian capital structures commonly use instruments whose legal label ("preference share", "debenture") does not determine classification:

Instrument Typical terms Ind AS 32 classification
CCPS convertible into a fixed number of shares, discretionary dividend No cash obligation; fixed-for-fixed Equity
CCPS with conversion ratio varying with fair value or future valuation (down-round protection, IPO-price-linked) Variable number of shares Liability (or liability + derivative)
CCPS with mandatory cumulative dividend Obligation to pay dividend Compound: liability (PV of dividends) + equity
Redeemable preference shares (fixed redemption) Obligation to pay cash Liability; dividends presented as finance cost
Compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), fixed number, coupon mandatory Coupon obligation Compound: liability (PV of coupons) + equity
Optionally convertible debentures (OCDs) Holder can demand cash Liability + conversion option (equity if fixed-for-fixed)
Written put on NCI shares Parent may have to pay cash Financial liability at PV of redemption amount (Ind AS 32.23)
Perpetual instruments with issuer discretion over coupons No obligation Equity

Analytical implications:

  • Liability-classified instruments are debt or debt-like for net debt, leverage and the EV bridge; their "dividends" appear in finance costs.
  • Equity-classified convertibles do not affect net debt but increase the diluted share count — include them in per-share valuation.
  • Compound instruments split: liability component in debt, equity component in other equity.
  • Start-up and PE-backed IPO companies often had CCPS with valuation-linked conversion classified as liabilities, with fair value changes through P&L — producing large, non-recurring losses pre-IPO. On conversion at IPO, the liability is reclassified to equity. Normalize these out of historical core PAT.
  • NCI puts: deduct the put liability in the EV bridge and avoid deducting NCI for the same shares; remeasurement gains/losses are non-recurring.
  • Bank and regulatory views differ: lenders may treat promoter CCPS as quasi-equity regardless of classification; FEMA pricing rules for CCPS issued to non-residents (conversion price not lower than fair value at issue) do not change Ind AS classification.

Illustration

A company issued ₹500 crore of CCDs on 1 April 2025: 5-year tenure, 8% annual coupon payable in cash, mandatory conversion into a fixed 2 crore shares at maturity. Market rate for similar non-convertible debt: 11%.

Liability component = PV of coupons = 40 × annuity factor (11%, 5 years) = 40 × 3.6959 = ₹147.8 crore. Equity component = 500 − 147.8 = ₹352.2 crore.

For FY2026: interest expense = 11% × 147.8 = ₹16.3 crore; coupon paid ₹40 crore reduces the liability. Net debt includes ₹124.1 crore at year-end; diluted shares include 2 crore shares.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify instruments [L1]

Classify each under Ind AS 32 and state its treatment in net debt and diluted shares: (a) 0.01% non-cumulative CCPS converting into 1 share each after 5 years; (b) CCPS converting into shares worth ₹1,000 crore at the IPO price; (c) 9% redeemable preference shares redeemable at par in 2029; (d) 7% OCDs convertible at the holder's option into a fixed number of shares; (e) a written put over a 20% NCI exercisable at fair value.

Q2. Compound instrument split [L2]

A company issues ₹300 crore of compulsorily convertible debentures with a 6% annual cash coupon for 4 years, converting into a fixed 1.5 crore shares; the market rate for equivalent straight debt is 10%. Compute the liability and equity components, pass the issue entry, and compute year-1 interest expense and closing liability.

💡 Hint: The liability component is the present value of the coupon stream only, since conversion settles the principal in shares.

Q3. Pre-IPO CCPS normalization [L3]

A newly listed company's FY2024 P&L shows a loss of ₹420 crore including a ₹510 crore fair value loss on CCPS classified as liabilities; the CCPS converted to equity at the FY2025 IPO. Explain why the CCPS were liabilities, how the loss should be treated in historical core PAT and in trend tables, and how the conversion affects net debt and share count.
