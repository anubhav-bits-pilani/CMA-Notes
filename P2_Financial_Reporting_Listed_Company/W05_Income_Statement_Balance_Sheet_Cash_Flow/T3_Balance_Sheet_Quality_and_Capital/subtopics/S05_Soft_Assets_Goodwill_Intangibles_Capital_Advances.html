Subtopic note

Soft Assets: Goodwill, Intangibles, Capital Advances, DTA and Tax Refunds

Scope: Soft assets: goodwill, intangibles under development, capital advances, DTA/MAT credit, non-current tax assets (refunds receivable)

Why it matters

"Soft" assets are balances whose realisable value depends heavily on management judgement, future events or the tax authorities — not on a market or a counterparty with a contractual obligation. Goodwill can be impaired overnight; intangibles under development may never be completed; capital advances may be unrecoverable or disguise fund diversion; deferred tax assets need future taxable profit; tax refunds depend on assessments and litigation. Banks strip several of them out when computing tangible net worth (c05); rating agencies haircut them; forensic analysts watch them for earnings management. For ROIC, the question is whether to include them in invested capital (yes for goodwill if measuring return on acquisition spend; no for "organic" ROIC) — and for valuation, whether their book value is cash-equivalent (rarely).

Core notes

Goodwill

Arises in a business combination (Ind AS 103) as consideration plus NCI plus previously held interest minus net identifiable assets. Not amortised; tested for impairment annually and when indicators exist (Ind AS 36.90), at the level of cash-generating units (CGUs). Impairment of goodwill cannot be reversed (Ind AS 36.124).

  • Analytical use: ROIC including goodwill measures returns on all capital spent, including acquisition premiums; ROIC excluding goodwill measures the operating economics of the underlying business. Report both (Week 6).
  • Quality checks: goodwill large relative to equity; impairment tests relying on terminal growth above long-run nominal GDP or recovery forecasts; headroom disclosures (Ind AS 36.134(f)) showing small margins; CGUs persistently underperforming.
  • Tax: goodwill on consolidation has no tax base; goodwill on asset purchases is not depreciable for tax after Finance Act 2021 (amendment to s.32) — impairment is not tax-deductible.

Other intangibles and intangibles under development

Intangible assets (brands, software, licences, customer relationships acquired, development costs) under Ind AS 38. Development costs are capitalised only when the six criteria of Ind AS 38.57 are met (technical feasibility, intention, ability to use/sell, probable future economic benefits, adequate resources, reliable measurement).

  • Intangibles under development (Schedule III line): capitalised development costs not yet ready for use — pharma dossiers, software platforms. Schedule III 2021 requires an ageing schedule and completion schedule (like CWIP).
  • Soft-asset risk: aggressive capitalisation of R&D raises EBITDA and assets; old balances in intangibles under development suggest stalled projects. For peer comparison with companies that expense R&D, consider expensing capitalised development cost.

Capital advances

Advances paid to suppliers of PPE, shown under other non-current assets. They are part of capex in the cash flow statement (investing outflow) but not yet PPE.

  • Legitimate for long-lead equipment, land purchases and EPC contracts.
  • Red flags: large relative to annual capex; ageing without capitalisation; advances to related parties or newly incorporated vendors; advances for land with title not transferred. These can be vehicles for fund diversion and are a known forensic signal in Indian corporate failures.
  • Treatment: include in operating capital as growth capex-in-progress (like CWIP) if genuine; otherwise treat as non-operating or impaired.

DTA and MAT credit

Deferred tax assets on deductible temporary differences and unused tax losses are recognised to the extent it is probable that future taxable profit will be available (Ind AS 12.24, 12.34). Under Ind AS, MAT credit entitlement is presented as part of deferred tax assets (the ICAI Guidance Note on MAT under Ind AS treats it as a DTA).

  • Soft-asset risk: DTA on losses depends on forecasts; Ind AS 12.82 requires disclosure of the evidence supporting recognition when an entity has suffered a loss and DTA depends on future profits.
  • MAT credit and s.115BAA: a company that opts for s.115BAA cannot use brought-forward MAT credit — any remaining balance is written off. Companies holding large MAT credit balances delay the switch; the balance is realisable only if they remain on the old regime long enough.
  • Treatment: in invested capital, operating DTA/DTL are often netted into a tax bucket or excluded (with NOPAT computed on cash taxes). In tangible net worth, banks deduct DTA.

Non-current tax assets (refunds receivable)

Advance tax and TDS in excess of provisions for tax, net, for assessment years under assessment, appeal or refund processing — shown as "non-current tax assets (net)" or "current tax assets (net)".

  • Soft-asset risk: refunds may be adjusted against demands, delayed for years, or depend on appellate outcomes. Large balances often co-exist with contingent liabilities for disputed demands — read both notes.
  • Treatment: non-operating; in valuation, include at a discounted, probability-weighted amount; exclude from NWC.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Item Amount Equity ₹5,000 crore — %
Goodwill (acquisitions 2018–2022) 1,200 24%
Intangibles under development (> 3 years: ₹180 crore) 260 5%
Capital advances (₹220 crore to one related EPC contractor) 340 7%
DTA including MAT credit ₹150 crore 410 8%
Non-current tax assets (refunds, AY2015-16 onwards) 230 5%
Total soft assets 2,440 49%

Half the book equity is soft. Tangible net worth for a bank = 5,000 − 1,200 − (other intangibles) − 410 − revaluation reserve. ROIC including goodwill will be materially lower than ex-goodwill ROIC; the ₹220 crore related-party capital advance and the old intangibles under development require explanation before inclusion in operating capital.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Goodwill vs acquired intangibles split (PPA): a PPA that allocates little to amortisable intangibles keeps EBITDA and EBIT higher (less amortisation); compare EBITA across acquirers.
  • Ind AS 101 deemed cost: first-time adopters could carry forward Indian GAAP goodwill — older amortisation history affects comparability.
  • Brand intangibles with indefinite life: not amortised; impairment testing judgement as for goodwill.
  • DTA on carried-forward losses in a turnaround: recognition may flip profit by large amounts in one year — remove from core PAT.
  • Tax refunds received: interest on refunds is other income (non-operating, non-recurring).
  • Capital advances for land where title is disputed — Schedule III 2021 title-deed disclosure helps.

Key takeaways

  • Soft assets — goodwill, intangibles under development, capital advances, DTA/MAT credit, tax refunds — depend on judgement or third-party action for realisation.
  • Report ROIC with and without goodwill; test impairment assumptions and headroom.
  • Use Schedule III ageing for intangibles under development; treat old balances as stalled.
  • Scrutinise capital advances for size, ageing and related-party counterparties.
  • DTA/MAT credit: check probability evidence and the s.115BAA switch; refunds: read with contingent liabilities.

Practice Questions

Q1. Soft-asset ratio [L1]

From the following (₹ crore) compute soft assets as % of total equity and tangible net worth (net worth less intangibles, DTA and revaluation reserve): equity 3,600 (including revaluation reserve 220); goodwill 640; other intangibles 180; intangibles under development 90; capital advances 260; DTA (including MAT credit 110) 240; non-current tax assets 150.

Q2. MAT credit and regime switch [L2]

A company carries MAT credit of ₹180 crore within DTA. Under the old regime it expects normal tax to exceed MAT by ₹45 crore a year from FY2027, allowing MAT credit use of ₹45 crore per year. Switching to s.115BAA would save ₹70 crore a year in tax from FY2027 but forfeit the credit. Using a 12% discount rate and a five-year horizon, explain how you would evaluate the switch and how the MAT credit should be valued in the meantime.

💡 Hint: Compare the present value of tax savings with the present value of credit utilisation.

Q3. Goodwill impairment test review [L3]

A consumer company carries goodwill of ₹900 crore on a CGU acquired in 2021. The impairment test uses 5-year revenue CAGR of 18% (historical since acquisition: 6%), terminal growth 6% and discount rate 11.5%; headroom is ₹45 crore. Draft four review questions and explain how you would treat goodwill in ROIC and in a bank's tangible net worth computation.

Q4. Capital advances forensic case [L3]

Over three years, a company's capital advances rose from ₹30 crore to ₹410 crore while CWIP was flat at ₹120 crore and capitalisation averaged ₹90 crore per year. ₹300 crore of the advances were paid to a two-year-old company whose director is the CFO's relative. Explain the accounting, governance and audit implications, the disclosures that should exist, and your analytical treatment of the balance.
