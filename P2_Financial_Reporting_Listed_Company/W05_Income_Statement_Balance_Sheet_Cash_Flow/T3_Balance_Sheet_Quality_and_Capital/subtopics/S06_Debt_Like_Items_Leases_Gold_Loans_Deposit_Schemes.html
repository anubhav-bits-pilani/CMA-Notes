Subtopic note

Debt-Like Items: Leases, Gold Loans, Deposit Schemes, Gratuity, NCI Puts

Scope: Debt-like items: lease liabilities, gold metal loans (jewellers), customer deposit schemes, unfunded gratuity, NCI put liabilities

Why it matters

Net debt in a broker table is often "borrowings minus cash". But equity holders rank behind every claim that is economically like debt — fixed obligations that must be settled in cash, often bearing explicit or implicit interest, and not part of the normal trade cycle. In India, the largest such items are lease liabilities (retail, QSR, airlines), gold metal loans (jewellers), customer advance schemes (jewellers, real estate), unfunded employee benefits (legacy manufacturers, PSUs) and NCI put options in groups with partly owned subsidiaries. Missing them overstates equity value in an EV bridge and understates leverage; including them inconsistently with EBITDA (leases) or NWC (customer schemes) double-counts. This week's Titan vs Trent exercise turns on exactly these judgements.

Core notes

The test for "debt-like"

An item is debt-like if most of the following hold:

  1. It is a contractual or constructive obligation to pay cash (or deliver an asset with a market price).
  2. It carries explicit or implicit interest or time value.
  3. It is not part of the normal operating cycle, i.e., it does not roll over automatically with sales.
  4. A buyer of the business would deduct it from the price (it would appear in an SPA's debt definition).
  5. Its cost sits below EBITDA (interest, unwinding) — so excluding it from net debt would make EV/EBITDA inconsistent.

Lease liabilities (Ind AS 116)

Present value of remaining lease payments (Ind AS 116.26), presented separately in Schedule III Div II (lease liabilities line under financial liabilities, added by the 2021 amendment).

  • Debt-like if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 (lease cost below EBITDA). If you use rent-adjusted (pre-116) EBITDA, exclude lease liabilities from net debt to avoid double counting the lease cost (Topic 2 s06, c02).
  • For credit analysis, include leases in debt regardless — they are fixed obligations — and use EBITDA post-116 (or EBITDAR-based coverage).

Gold metal loans (jewellers)

Jewellers such as Titan borrow physical gold from banks under the RBI-permitted Gold Metal Loan (GML) scheme, typically for 180 days, at a low interest rate (fee) on the gold value; repayment is in gold or in rupees at the gold price on maturity. The gold is used for manufacturing jewellery inventory; the price exposure on the loan offsets the price exposure on inventory — a natural or designated fair value hedge of inventory.

  • Accounting: the obligation is a liability measured at the gold price (fair value), with changes offsetting inventory hedging gains/losses. Presentation varies: some companies show it within borrowings, others within trade payables or other financial liabilities — read the note.
  • Debt-like or operating? Arguments for debt: interest-bearing, borrowed from banks, a funding substitute for working-capital loans, refinanced repeatedly. Arguments for operating: it finances inventory one-for-one and is hedge-linked, rolls with inventory levels like supplier credit, and the gold price risk is neutral.
  • Practice: credit analysts and many equity analysts treat GML as debt (it is bank funding); others treat it as part of operating NWC netted against gold inventory. Pick one policy, apply it to all years and peers, and match the treatment of the interest (if debt, the fee is a finance cost and stays below EBITDA).

Customer deposit schemes

Jewellery purchase schemes (e.g., the customer pays monthly instalments for 10 months, the jeweller adds a bonus, and the customer buys jewellery at maturity) and similar advance schemes.

  • Regulation: under the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules 2014, an advance received against supply of goods is excluded from "deposit" if appropriated against supply within 365 days (Rule 2(1)(c)(xii)(a)); schemes are structured to fit this exemption, otherwise they would be public deposits subject to Chapter V.
  • Accounting: contract liability / advance from customers (Ind AS 115); the jeweller's bonus is a discount on the sale or a significant financing component, depending on terms.
  • Debt-like or operating? Operating — they are paid back in goods, recur with the business, and fund inventory (like negative NWC). But they behave like cheap, short-term deposits, and if the scheme is suspended or customers redeem in cash, it is a cash claim. Practice: treat as operating (contract liability) but disclose the amount and the cash backing; do not also count the cash backing as surplus (Topic 3 s03 — consistency).

Unfunded gratuity and other employee benefits

Net defined benefit liability under Ind AS 19 = present value of defined benefit obligation − fair value of plan assets (e.g., LIC group gratuity fund).

  • Debt-like: it is a long-term obligation with an explicit discount rate (unwinding of interest in finance costs or employee cost). Include the unfunded portion (net of plan assets) in net debt, preferably post-tax (gratuity is deductible when paid — s.43B / s.40A(7)), i.e., × (1 − 25.168%).
  • Short-term leave and bonus: operating NWC.
  • Labour Codes (from 21 November 2025): increased obligations raised net liabilities — debt-like amounts increase.

NCI put liabilities

When a parent writes a put option allowing NCI holders to sell their shares to the parent, Ind AS 32.23 requires a financial liability at the present value of the redemption amount, even though the NCI may still be presented (depending on the parent's policy) — a gross obligation to pay cash.

  • Debt-like: yes — a contractual obligation to buy out minorities. In the EV bridge, deduct the put liability and do not also deduct NCI for the same shares (avoid double count).
  • Remeasurement: changes in the liability go to P&L or equity depending on policy — non-recurring items in core PAT.

Item Amount Policy A (strict) Policy B (operating view)
Borrowings (bank) 2,500 Debt Debt
Gold metal loans 6,800 Debt Operating NWC (offsets gold inventory)
Lease liabilities 2,000 Debt (post-116 EBITDA) Debt (post-116 EBITDA)

Same balance sheet, net debt of ₹8.8 thousand crore or ₹2.0 thousand crore. Under Policy B, operating NWC falls by ₹6,800 crore, raising ROIC; under Policy A, leverage is visible. The choice must flow consistently into EBITDA (GML fee below EBITDA in A) and the EV bridge.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Decommissioning/restoration provisions (store reinstatement, mine closure): debt-like, discounted (Ind AS 37), post-tax.
  • Deferred and contingent consideration on acquisitions: debt-like (financial liability at fair value under Ind AS 103).
  • Supplier finance and acceptances: trade payables backed by bank LCs/acceptances beyond normal terms are debt-like (Topic 4 s07).
  • Security deposits from dealers/distributors: interest-bearing and refundable — debt-like if large and interest-bearing; operating if non-interest-bearing and rolling with the network (s01 question).
  • Preference shares and compulsorily convertible instruments: classification under Ind AS 32 (c06) — liability-classified instruments are debt-like; equity-classified convertibles affect share count.
  • Dividends declared but unpaid: payable to equity holders — deduct as a claim if after the valuation date.

Key takeaways

  • Debt-like = cash obligation, time value, outside the normal operating cycle, deducted by a buyer, cost below EBITDA.
  • Lease liabilities are debt-like only if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 (equity valuation); always for credit.
  • Gold metal loans and customer schemes require an explicit, consistent policy — the Titan judgement.
  • Unfunded gratuity (post-tax) and NCI put liabilities belong in the EV bridge; avoid double counting NCI.
  • Match every debt-like choice with the EBITDA, NWC and surplus cash treatment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build an adjusted net debt [L1]

Compute adjusted net debt (₹ crore) under a policy that treats leases as debt (post-116 EBITDA), GML as debt, customer advances as operating and unfunded gratuity post-tax at 25.168%: borrowings 1,800; GML 3,400; lease liabilities 1,450; customer scheme advances 2,100; gratuity obligation 420, plan assets 260; deferred consideration on acquisition 150; cash and liquid funds 2,700 (earmarked 30).

Q2. GML policy effect on ROIC [L2]

A jeweller's EBIT is ₹4,500 crore (tax 25.168%). Operating invested capital excluding GML is ₹18,000 crore (gold inventory included at gross). GML outstanding is ₹7,000 crore; the GML fee is ₹180 crore within finance costs. Compute ROIC under the debt policy and the operating-NWC policy, and explain why the EBIT need not change between them while interest cover does.

💡 Hint: Under the operating view, GML reduces invested capital; consider where the fee sits.

Q3. NCI put journal and EV bridge [L2]

A parent owns 70% of a subsidiary and writes a put allowing the 30% NCI holders to sell their shares in three years for ₹600 crore. The discount rate is 10%. Pass the initial entry under a policy that derecognises NCI on recognition of the liability, compute the unwinding in year 1, and explain how the EV bridge should treat the put liability and the NCI.

Q4. Customer scheme stress case [L3]

A jeweller has ₹4,800 crore of customer scheme advances (10-month schemes with a one-instalment bonus) and ₹5,500 crore of cash and liquid funds. Under a stress scenario, regulators require cash refund options and 40% of customers redeem in cash. Explain how this would change your classification of the advances and of the cash, quantify the change in surplus cash and net debt, and describe the disclosure you would add to your valuation note.
