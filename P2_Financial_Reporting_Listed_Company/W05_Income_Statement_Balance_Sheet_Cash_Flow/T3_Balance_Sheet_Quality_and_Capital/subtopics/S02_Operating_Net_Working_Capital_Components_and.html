Subtopic note

Operating Net Working Capital: Components and Boundaries

Scope: Operating NWC: trade receivables, inventories, contract assets/liabilities, trade payables, customer advances, operating provisions

Why it matters

Operating net working capital (NWC) is the capital tied up in the operating cycle — buying, making, selling, collecting. It is usually the largest and most volatile component of invested capital for Indian consumer, retail, engineering and pharma companies, and its change each year (ΔNWC) is the main reason CFO differs from EBITDA. Working-capital days drive the balance sheet in any 3-statement model; NWC pegs are negotiated in M&A; drawing power for bank working-capital limits is computed on current assets. Defining NWC correctly — what is in, what is out — determines ROIC, FCF and the cash conversion story.

Core notes

Definition

\[ \text{Operating NWC} = \underbrace{\text{TR} + \text{Inv} + \text{CA} + \text{OCA}_{\text{op}}}_{\text{operating current assets}} - \underbrace{\text{TP} + \text{CL} + \text{Adv} + \text{OCL}_{\text{op}} + \text{Prov}_{\text{op}}}_{\text{operating current liabilities}} \]

where TR = trade receivables (net of ECL allowance), Inv = inventories, CA = contract assets (unbilled revenue), \(\text{OCA}_{\text{op}}\) = other operating current assets (advances to suppliers, GST/input credits, prepaid expenses, operating cash), TP = trade payables, CL = contract liabilities (deferred revenue), Adv = customer advances, \(\text{OCL}_{\text{op}}\) = other operating liabilities (statutory dues, employee payables, accrued expenses), \(\text{Prov}_{\text{op}}\) = operating provisions (warranty, short-term employee benefits, returns, onerous contracts).

Excluded: cash beyond operating needs, current investments, current maturities of borrowings, interest accrued on borrowings, dividends payable, capex creditors, current tax assets/liabilities (often), derivative balances on financing hedges, assets held for sale.

Components

Trade receivables (Ind AS 109, Ind AS 115). Unconditional rights to consideration. Measured at amortised cost less ECL; a provision matrix based on ageing and historical loss rates is the common simplified approach (Ind AS 109.5.5.15). Use net receivables; analyse gross and allowance separately (s06 of Topic 1, the ageing schedule). Watch factored or discounted receivables — derecognised balances flatter NWC.

Inventories (Ind AS 2). Raw materials, WIP, finished goods, stock-in-trade, stores and spares, goods in transit — at lower of cost and NRV. For jewellers, gold inventory may be hedged by gold metal loans (s06) — gross NWC looks very high; the financing is debt-like.

Contract assets and contract liabilities (Ind AS 115.105–109). A contract asset is a right to consideration conditional on something other than time (e.g., unbilled revenue on long-term contracts). A contract liability is an obligation to transfer goods or services for which consideration has been received or is due (advances from customers, deferred revenue, loyalty point liabilities, gift cards). Schedule III does not have separate lines; they sit in other financial assets/other current assets and other current liabilities — read the Ind AS 115 disclosure of contract balances.

Trade payables. Amounts due for goods and services in the normal course (Schedule III separates MSME dues). Exclude capex creditors. Supply chain finance can make payables look longer than supplier terms; read the Ind AS 107 liquidity note and any supplier finance disclosures (Topic 4 s07).

Customer advances. For businesses that collect cash before delivery — real estate, capital goods, education, subscriptions, jewellery purchase schemes — advances are a source of operating funding and make NWC negative. They are operating liabilities by default; judge whether deposit-like schemes are debt-like (s06).

Operating provisions (Ind AS 37). Warranty, product returns, onerous contracts and short-term employee provisions (bonus, leave expected to be availed within 12 months) arise from operations. Long-term employee benefit obligations (gratuity, long-term leave) are usually treated as debt-like when unfunded (s06). Decommissioning and restoration provisions (e.g., leased store reinstatement) are often debt-like.

Days and ratios

Metric Formula Basis
Receivable days TR ÷ Revenue × 365 Include contract assets for project businesses
Inventory days Inv ÷ COGS × 365 COGS per Schedule III build (Topic 1 c04)
Payable days TP ÷ COGS (or purchases) × 365 Use purchases if available
Cash conversion cycle Receivable days + Inventory days − Payable days
NWC % revenue Operating NWC ÷ Revenue Model driver

Use averages for flow comparisons, year-end for balance-sheet modelling — and state which.

ΔNWC = ₹420 crore — a cash outflow that explains why CFO lags EBITDA. NWC rose 51% vs revenue growth of 13%: inventory (+21%), receivables (+26%) and unbilled (+67%) outpaced sales. Excluded from this NWC: capex creditors ₹85 crore, interest accrued ₹12 crore, current maturities of borrowings ₹200 crore, liquid funds ₹600 crore.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Seasonal year-ends: an apparel retailer's March balance carries stock built for the summer season and end-of-season sale clearance; jewellers' NWC spikes ahead of Akshaya Tritiya and the wedding season. Use averages from half-yearly balance sheets.
  • Negative NWC businesses: quick-commerce, QSR and retail with supplier credit > inventory days — growth releases cash. Check that negative NWC is not driven by stretched MSME payables (s.43B(h) tax consequence and MSMED Act 45-day limit).
  • Factoring and channel financing: derecognised receivables and vendor-financed dealer purchases reduce NWC — add back for comparability if the company bears recourse or economic risk.
  • ECL allowance swings: a release of allowance increases net receivables and profit; analyse gross.
  • GST input credit build-up (inverted duty structure): a refund claim is operating but may be slow-moving; consider separating.
  • Contract liabilities in loyalty and gift cards: operating, but breakage estimates (Ind AS 115.B46) affect revenue — note any change.

Key takeaways

  • Operating NWC = operating current assets (receivables, inventories, contract assets, supplier advances, operating cash) − operating current liabilities (payables, contract liabilities, customer advances, accruals, operating provisions).
  • Exclude cash beyond operating needs, investments, borrowings, interest accrued, dividends payable, capex creditors and tax balances.
  • Read Ind AS 115 contract-balance and Ind AS 109 ECL notes; Schedule III lines do not map one-to-one.
  • Use days metrics and NWC % revenue as model drivers; prefer averages for seasonal businesses.
  • Adjust for factoring, supply chain finance and channel financing before peer comparison.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute NWC and days [L1]

Revenue ₹4,800 crore; COGS ₹3,100 crore. Year-end balances (₹ crore): trade receivables 520; inventories 690; unbilled revenue 40; advances to suppliers 55; trade payables 610; customer advances 120; statutory dues 45; warranty provision 25; capex creditors 70; interest accrued on borrowings 8. Compute operating NWC, receivable, inventory and payable days, and the cash conversion cycle.

Q2. Contract balances [L2]

An engineering company's Ind AS 115 note shows contract assets of ₹1,350 crore (FY2024: ₹980 crore) and contract liabilities of ₹640 crore (FY2024: ₹710 crore); trade receivables are ₹1,900 crore (FY2024: ₹1,750 crore); revenue grew 9%. Compute the change in each component as % of revenue, explain what the shift from receivables to contract assets may indicate, and list the disclosures you would review.

💡 Hint: Contract assets are conditional rights — ask what condition remains unsatisfied.

Q3. Factoring adjustment [L2]

A consumer durables company reports receivable days of 28. Its notes disclose receivables of ₹420 crore derecognised under a non-recourse factoring programme at year-end and ₹180 crore of channel-financing arrangements where the company provides a first-loss guarantee of 10%. Revenue is ₹7,300 crore. Compute adjusted receivable days under two assumptions and explain which you would use for peer comparison.

Q4. Seasonal averaging [L3]

A jeweller's inventories were ₹33,000 crore at 31 March 2025, ₹38,500 crore at 30 September 2025 and ₹36,200 crore at 31 March 2026; revenue FY2026 was ₹67,000 crore and COGS ₹56,500 crore. Compute inventory days using year-end and average balances, explain the gold-price effect on the comparison, and recommend how to forecast inventory in a model.
