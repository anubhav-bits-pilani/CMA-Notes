Subtopic note

Surplus Cash and Treasury: Equivalents, Long Deposits, FVTPL Funds

Scope: Surplus cash and treasury: cash equivalents, bank deposits over 12 months (other financial assets), liquid/debt MFs at FVTPL

Why it matters

Indian listed companies — especially consumer, IT, pharma and auto majors — hold large treasury portfolios, and they are scattered across at least five Schedule III lines: cash and cash equivalents, bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents, current investments, non-current investments and other financial assets (non-current). An analyst who picks up only "cash and cash equivalents" can understate surplus cash by more than half, overstating net debt, understating equity value and misreading leverage. Conversely, counting all of it as surplus, including operating and restricted cash, overstates value. Surplus cash identification connects directly to excluding treasury income from core EBITDA (Topic 2 s03): what is removed from EBITDA must be added in the EV bridge.

Core notes

Where treasury sits in Schedule III Div II

Line Content Ind AS basis
Cash and cash equivalents (current) Cash on hand, current accounts, deposits with original maturity ≤ 3 months, highly liquid investments readily convertible with insignificant risk of value change Ind AS 7.6–7.7
Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents (current) Deposits with original maturity > 3 months and remaining maturity ≤ 12 months; earmarked balances (unpaid dividend, margin money) Schedule III Div II note on cash
Current investments Liquid funds, overnight funds, debt mutual funds, commercial paper, T-bills, bonds maturing within 12 months, quoted equity held for trading Ind AS 109: mutual fund units at FVTPL
Other financial assets (non-current) Bank deposits with remaining maturity > 12 months; deposits under lien Schedule III Div II requires disclosure of bank deposits with more than 12 months maturity
Non-current investments Long-dated bonds, tax-free bonds, debt funds intended to be held > 12 months, strategic equity Amortised cost / FVOCI / FVTPL per Ind AS 109 business model and SPPI

Measurement points that matter for value

  • Mutual fund units fail SPPI (returns are not solely principal and interest on the principal), so they are measured at FVTPL — the balance-sheet figure is fair value (NAV), suitable for valuation.
  • Bonds and deposits at amortised cost are at carrying amount; fair value (disclosed under Ind AS 107/113) may differ for long-dated bonds — use fair value in the EV bridge if material.
  • Tax on unrealised gains: an FVTPL gain carries a DTL; strictly, surplus treasury value is net of tax payable on realisation. For large unrealised gains, deduct the related DTL.
  • Bank overdrafts repayable on demand that are integral to cash management are included in cash and cash equivalents for the cash flow statement (Ind AS 7.8) but shown as borrowings on the balance sheet — net them consistently.

What counts as surplus

\[ \text{Surplus cash} = \text{CCE} + \text{OBB} + \text{LI}_{\text{current}} + \text{BD}_{>12m} + \text{LI}_{\text{non-current}} - \text{Operating cash} - \text{Restricted or earmarked balances} \]

where CCE = cash and cash equivalents, OBB = other bank balances, LI = liquid/debt investments (current and non-current), \(\text{BD}_{>12m}\) = bank deposits with remaining maturity over 12 months in other financial assets, operating cash = minimum cash needed for operations, restricted = earmarked, lien-marked, escrow, unspent CSR accounts (s07).

Operating cash: no standard rule. Approaches: (a) 1–2% of revenue; (b) management's stated minimum liquidity; (c) the trough of cash across quarters (from half-yearly balance sheets); (d) zero, if credit lines are undrawn and ample. State the approach.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Line Amount Surplus?
Cash and cash equivalents 420 Yes, less operating cash
Other bank balances 1,150 Yes, less earmarked ₹40 crore (unpaid dividend ₹12 crore, margin money ₹28 crore)
Current investments — liquid and debt MFs (FVTPL) 2,300 Yes
Current investments — quoted equity of a supplier 90 Non-operating, but strategic — value separately
Other financial assets — bank deposits > 12 months 600 Yes (₹100 crore under lien for a bank guarantee — exclude)
Non-current investments — tax-free bonds (amortised cost; fair value ₹318 crore) 300 Yes, at fair value ₹318 crore
Revenue ₹14,000 crore; operating cash at 1% (140) Deduct

Surplus cash = (420 − 140) + (1,150 − 40) + 2,300 + (600 − 100) + 318 = ₹4,508 crore. Using only "cash and cash equivalents" (₹420 crore) would understate surplus by about ₹4,100 crore — on 50 crore shares, ₹82 per share.

Excel implementation

Surplus cash:   =SUMIFS(BS[Amount],BS[Bucket],"Surplus",BS[Year],Y) - OperatingCash - SUMIFS(BS[Amount],BS[Flag],"Restricted",BS[Year],Y)
Treasury yield: =(InterestIncome+FVTPLGain)/AVERAGE(Surplus_Open,Surplus_Close)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Working-capital buffers: seasonal businesses (jewellery ahead of Akshaya Tritiya, fertilisers awaiting subsidy) need cash in some months — part of year-end treasury is not surplus (topic pitfall).
  • Cash in subsidiaries with NCI or restrictions: only the owners' share is accessible; foreign cash may be subject to repatriation taxes or exchange controls (topic pitfall).
  • Customer advance floats: cash funded by customer advances (jewellery schemes, real-estate bookings) is not freely surplus if the advances are treated as operating liabilities — the cash is needed to deliver against them. Be consistent with s06.
  • Treasury used to fund announced capex or acquisitions: still surplus at the balance-sheet date, but forecast cash outflows — do not double count by also deducting future capex in DCF.
  • Credit-risk funds and side-pocketing: debt funds with stressed holdings (2019–2020 episodes) should be valued at realisable amounts.
  • Inter-corporate deposits: not cash; credit risk on a counterparty, often a group company — non-operating asset (s04), not surplus cash.

Key takeaways

  • Treasury is spread across cash, other bank balances, current and non-current investments and other financial assets (deposits > 12 months); collect all of it.
  • Mutual funds are FVTPL (Ind AS 109) — use NAV; bonds at amortised cost may need fair value.
  • Surplus cash = total treasury − operating cash − earmarked/lien/restricted balances.
  • Treasury identification must match the other income removed from core EBITDA.
  • Adjust for seasonal buffers, subsidiary-held cash, customer floats and tax on unrealised gains.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute surplus cash [L1]

From the following (₹ crore) compute surplus cash: cash and cash equivalents 260 (a bank overdraft of ₹40 crore repayable on demand is shown separately within current borrowings and netted only in the cash flow statement); other bank balances 540 (unpaid dividend 9, margin money 31); current investments in liquid funds 1,200; non-current other financial assets: deposits > 12 months 350 (of which 50 under lien); revenue 8,500 crore; operating cash 1.5% of revenue.

Q2. Reconcile treasury with other income [L2]

Using the Q1 company, other income includes interest on deposits ₹62 crore and FVTPL gains ₹84 crore. Average surplus cash over the year was ₹2,050 crore. Compute the treasury yield and explain what a yield of that level implies about whether all yielding assets have been captured.

💡 Hint: Compare the yield with prevailing deposit and liquid fund returns of roughly 6.5–7.5%.

Q3. Fair value and deferred tax on treasury [L2]

A company holds debt funds at FVTPL with NAV ₹1,500 crore (cost ₹1,280 crore) and tax-free bonds at amortised cost ₹400 crore (fair value ₹430 crore). Under s.115BAA, debt fund gains are taxed at 25.168% on redemption. Compute the post-tax treasury value for the EV bridge and explain why using the balance-sheet amounts may overstate or understate value.

Q4. Seasonal buffer judgement [L3]

A fertiliser company reports ₹3,000 crore of cash and liquid funds at 31 March, but its half-yearly balance sheet at 30 September shows ₹400 crore, with subsidy receivables of ₹5,200 crore. Explain how you would determine the surplus cash figure for valuation and for net debt in a leverage covenant, and draft the footnote for your model.
