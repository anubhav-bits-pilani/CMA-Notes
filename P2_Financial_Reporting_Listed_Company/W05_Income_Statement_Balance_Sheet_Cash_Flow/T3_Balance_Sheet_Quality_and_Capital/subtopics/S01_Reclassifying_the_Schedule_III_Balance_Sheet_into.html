Subtopic note

Reclassifying the Schedule III Balance Sheet into Analytical Buckets

Scope: Reclassifying the Schedule III balance sheet into operating, non-operating, financing and equity buckets

Why it matters

The Schedule III balance sheet is organised by liquidity and legal form — non-current vs current, financial vs non-financial, assets vs equity and liabilities. Analysis needs a different organisation, by economic function: what capital the operating business uses, what surplus and non-operating assets sit alongside it, and how the whole is financed. This reclassification is the foundation of invested capital, ROIC and ROCE (Week 6), the EV-to-equity bridge (Week 16), net debt and leverage covenants, and the balance-sheet side of the 3-statement model. Two analysts using the same balance sheet can produce ROIC figures that differ by half because they bucket items differently — so the mapping must be explicit and documented.

Core notes

The four buckets

Bucket Content Use
Operating Assets and liabilities used to run the business: PPE, ROU assets, CWIP (policy choice), goodwill and intangibles, operating NWC (receivables, inventories, contract balances, payables, customer advances, operating provisions), operating deposits Operating invested capital, ROIC denominator
Non-operating Surplus cash, bank deposits, liquid/debt mutual funds, investment property, strategic stakes, ICDs and loans to group companies, assets held for sale, non-operating receivables Added separately in the EV bridge; excluded from ROIC
Financing (debt and debt-like) Borrowings (non-current and current, including current maturities), lease liabilities, debt-like items (gold metal loans, customer deposit schemes where judged debt-like, unfunded employee benefits, NCI puts, deferred consideration) Net debt, leverage, WACC weights
Equity Equity share capital, other equity, NCI, equity components of compound instruments Book equity, ROE

Tax items (DTA/DTL, current tax assets/liabilities) are a fifth, swing category: treat operating tax balances as part of operating capital or exclude them, but be consistent with how NOPAT taxes are computed (Week 6).

The identity

After mapping, the balance sheet must still balance. In net form:

\[ \text{Operating invested capital} + \text{Non-operating assets} + \text{Surplus cash} = \text{Total equity (incl. NCI)} + \text{Debt and debt-like items} + \text{Net tax and other residuals} \]

Every line must appear exactly once. A check row (sum of buckets − total assets, and the funding side) prevents double counting.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Line Amount Bucket
PPE + ROU 3,400 Operating
CWIP 450 Operating (growth)
Goodwill + intangibles 600 Operating (soft)
Investment property 150 Non-operating
Strategic equity stake 300 Non-operating
Inventories + receivables + other current assets 2,900 Operating NWC
Cash, bank, liquid funds 1,700 Surplus (₹100 operating cash, ₹60 earmarked)
Trade payables + other operating liabilities (2,100) Operating NWC
Borrowings (900) Financing
Lease liabilities (1,800) Financing
Unfunded gratuity (120) Debt-like
Net DTL (180) Tax
Equity incl. NCI (4,400) Equity

Operating invested capital = 3,400 + 450 + 600 + (2,900 − 2,100) + 100 = ₹5,350 crore. Non-operating assets = 150 + 300 = ₹450 crore. Surplus cash = 1,700 − 100 − 60 = ₹1,540 crore. Earmarked cash ₹60 crore offsets unpaid dividends inside other liabilities. The bucket sums tie back to the balance sheet.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Operating cash: some cash is needed to run the business (store floats, collections in transit). A common proxy is 1–2% of revenue; treat it as operating NWC and the rest as surplus.
  • Financial vs operating classification by Schedule III: security deposits paid to landlords are "other financial assets" but economically operating.
  • Interest accrued: follows its parent — on deposits (non-operating), on borrowings (financing).
  • Capex creditors: liabilities for PPE purchased on credit; treat as part of capex funding (debt-like or deduct from CWIP/PPE) rather than operating payables, or ROIC is understated and NWC flattered.
  • Derivatives: hedges of trade exposures (operating), hedges of borrowings (financing).
  • Consolidated vs standalone: investments in subsidiaries are non-operating in standalone analysis only if valued separately; otherwise analyse consolidated.

Key takeaways

  • Re-sort Schedule III lines from legal/liquidity form into operating, non-operating, financing and equity buckets (plus tax).
  • Map every line exactly once and keep a check that buckets tie to totals.
  • Split mixed lines (other financial assets/liabilities, provisions, cash) using notes.
  • Keep the mapping consistent with EBITDA/NOPAT definitions (leases, other income, taxes).
  • Document the mapping table — it drives ROIC, net debt and the EV bridge.

Practice Questions

Q1. Map the lines [L1]

Assign each of the following to operating, non-operating, financing/debt-like, equity or tax, with a one-line reason: security deposits paid to mall landlords; fixed deposits with 18 months' maturity; interest accrued on borrowings; GST input tax credit receivable; capital creditors; dividend payable; investment in a 26% associate; current maturities of long-term borrowings; provision for warranty; unfunded gratuity liability; MAT credit entitlement.

Q2. Build the buckets [L2]

From the following balance sheet (₹ crore) compute operating NWC, operating invested capital, non-operating assets, surplus cash and debt-like items, and show the check that the buckets reconcile: PPE 2,200; ROU 1,100; CWIP 300; goodwill 250; investment property 90; non-current investments (strategic) 400; security deposits (leases) 140; inventories 1,600; receivables 500; other current assets 260; cash and equivalents 220; other bank balances 380 (including unpaid dividend account 15); current investments (liquid funds) 900; trade payables 1,300; other current liabilities 520 (including capex creditors 60 and unpaid dividends 15); borrowings 600; lease liabilities 1,250; gratuity (unfunded) 70; net DTL 110; equity 4,490.

💡 Hint: Confirm total assets equal total equity and liabilities before you start bucketing.

Q3. Mapping documentation in Excel [L2]

Design a mapping table in Excel (columns: Schedule III line, note reference, sub-item, bucket, sub-bucket, policy rationale) and the SUMIFS formulas that produce bucket totals for five years. Explain how you would flag a line that is unmapped or mapped twice.

Q4. Judgement case: dealer deposits [L3]

A paints company holds ₹1,100 crore of interest-bearing security deposits from dealers, refundable on termination of dealership; dealers rarely leave and the balance grows with the network. Argue whether these belong in operating NWC or debt-like items, and quantify the effect on invested capital and net debt for a company with operating invested capital of ₹6,000 crore and net cash of ₹2,000 crore.
