Subtopic note

CWIP Ageing, Stalled Projects and Earmarked Bank Balances

Scope: CWIP ageing and stalled projects; earmarked bank balances (unpaid dividend, margin money) excluded from free cash

Why it matters

Two balance-sheet lines can quietly mislead a capital-employed analysis. Capital work-in-progress is capital spent that is not yet producing revenue: legitimate growth investment if on time and on budget, dead weight (and a potential impairment) if stalled. Earmarked bank balances look like cash but cannot be used freely: they are held for unpaid dividends, as margin money behind bank guarantees and letters of credit, under lien, in escrow, or in unspent CSR accounts. Treating stalled CWIP as productive capital or earmarked balances as free cash both distort ROIC, net debt and liquidity. The 2021 Schedule III amendments made the necessary information available — CWIP ageing and completion schedules, and the split of other bank balances — so there is no excuse for missing them.

Core notes

CWIP: accounting and disclosure

CWIP accumulates costs of PPE under construction: civil work, equipment not yet installed, directly attributable costs (Ind AS 16.16–17), and capitalised borrowing costs on qualifying assets (Ind AS 23). It is not depreciated until the asset is available for use (Ind AS 16.55).

Schedule III 2021 requires:

  • CWIP ageing schedule: amounts in CWIP for (a) projects in progress and (b) projects temporarily suspended, aged < 1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years, > 3 years.
  • Completion schedule: for projects whose completion is overdue or whose cost has exceeded the original plan, the expected completion in < 1 year, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years — project-wise for suspended projects.
  • The same disclosures for intangible assets under development.

Identifying stalled projects

Signal What to check
Balances in > 2–3 year buckets Project-wise completion schedule; management commentary
"Temporarily suspended" projects Reason (approvals, litigation, demand), restart plan
Cost overrun vs original plan Revised cost vs original; funding plan
Capitalised borrowing costs continuing on suspended projects Ind AS 23.20: capitalisation is suspended during extended periods in which active development is suspended — continued capitalisation inflates CWIP and profit
CWIP flat while capex outflows continue Capex may be going into capital advances (s05) or being written off
No impairment despite suspension Ind AS 36 indicators — obsolescence, adverse change in market

CWIP in invested capital

There are three defensible treatments:

  1. Include CWIP in operating invested capital (standard; ROIC reflects the cost of growth). Disclose that ROIC is depressed by growth capex not yet earning returns.
  2. Exclude CWIP from invested capital and show "ROIC on capital in use", with CWIP separately as growth capital (useful during a large greenfield project).
  3. Include productive, exclude stalled: CWIP in progress included or excluded per policy, while stalled/suspended CWIP is treated as non-operating at a haircut or as impaired.

The topic pitfall applies: leaving CWIP in IC and penalising ROIC without disclosure. Whichever treatment is chosen, also treat capital advances and capex creditors consistently (capex creditors reduce the net capital deployed in CWIP/PPE).

Earmarked bank balances

Schedule III Div II requires bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents to disclose earmarked balances separately, and balances held as margin money or security against borrowings, guarantees and other commitments.

Balance Why it is not free Treatment
Unpaid dividend accounts s.123(4) requires dividend amounts to be deposited in a separate bank account within 5 days of declaration; s.124 requires unpaid/unclaimed amounts to be transferred to an Unpaid Dividend Account within 7 days after the 30-day payment period, and to the IEPF after 7 years Exclude from free cash; offset against the unpaid dividend liability (both excluded)
Margin money against BGs/LCs Lien in favour of the bank until the guarantee or LC expires Exclude from free cash; treat as operating (if trade LCs) or restricted
Deposits under lien for borrowings Security for loans Exclude, or net against the related borrowing
Unspent CSR account s.135(6): unspent amount relating to an ongoing project transferred to an Unspent CSR Account within 30 days of FY end, to be spent within 3 years Exclude; it is committed spending
Escrow (share buyback, open offer, project escrows) Legal restriction Exclude
Balances in countries with exchange controls Repatriation restrictions Haircut or exclude
Debt service reserve accounts (DSRA) Required by lenders Exclude, or net against debt

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mega-projects (refineries, semiconductor fabs, steel plants): CWIP may exceed net PPE for years; use project-level IRR and milestone tracking rather than ROIC.
  • Capitalisation date judgement: delaying "available for use" keeps costs in CWIP, defers depreciation and capitalises interest longer — check trial-run and commercial-operation dates.
  • Pre-operative expenses: only directly attributable costs can be capitalised; general overheads cannot (Ind AS 16.19).
  • Margin money for trade LCs: if the company uses LCs routinely to buy inventory, margin money is part of operating working capital — still not surplus.
  • Unclaimed dividend liability: excluded from operating liabilities along with its earmarked cash, so the net effect is zero.
  • Fixed deposits in "cash and cash equivalents" that are lien-marked: the restriction may be disclosed only in the borrowings note — read the security note.

Key takeaways

  • Use the Schedule III 2021 CWIP ageing and completion schedules to separate productive, delayed and suspended projects.
  • Stop capitalising borrowing costs on suspended projects (Ind AS 23.20); adjust core earnings if the company did not.
  • Choose and disclose a CWIP policy for ROIC — include, exclude, or exclude only stalled — and treat capital advances consistently.
  • Earmarked balances (unpaid dividend, margin money, lien, unspent CSR, escrow, DSRA) are not free cash.
  • Offset earmarked balances against their related liabilities rather than counting them as surplus.

Practice Questions

Q1. Free cash from other bank balances [L1]

Other bank balances are ₹1,240 crore, comprising: unpaid dividend accounts 22; margin money for bank guarantees 118; fixed deposits under lien for a term loan 150; unspent CSR account 34; escrow for a buyback 300; other fixed deposits (3–12 months) 616. Compute free cash from this line and explain the treatment of each excluded item in net debt.

Q2. CWIP ageing analysis [L2]

A company's CWIP is ₹1,480 crore: in progress < 1 year 520, 1–2 years 310, 2–3 years 140; suspended 2–3 years 90, > 3 years 420. Net PPE is ₹3,900 crore; NOPAT ₹620 crore; operating NWC ₹900 crore. Compute ROIC with all CWIP included, with all CWIP excluded, and with only suspended CWIP excluded. Recommend a policy and draft the disclosure note.

💡 Hint: Keep NOPAT the same across all three versions; only the denominator changes.

Q3. Borrowing cost on a suspended project [L2]

A ₹600 crore project funded by a dedicated 10% term loan was suspended on 1 July 2025 due to an environmental clearance dispute; active development is expected to restart in FY2028. The company continued capitalising interest for the full FY2026. Compute the amount that should have been expensed, pass the correcting entry, and state the effect on EBITDA, PBT and invested capital.

Q4. Unspent CSR and liquidity [L2]

A company's CSR obligation for FY2025 was ₹48 crore; it spent ₹20 crore and transferred ₹28 crore relating to an ongoing project to an Unspent CSR Account in April 2025. Explain the s.135(6) requirements, the presentation of the balance and the related liability (if any) in the FY2025 financial statements, and why the amount is excluded from free cash.
