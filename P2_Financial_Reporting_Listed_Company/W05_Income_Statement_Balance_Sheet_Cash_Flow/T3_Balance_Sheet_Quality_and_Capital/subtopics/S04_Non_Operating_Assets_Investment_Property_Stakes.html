Subtopic note

Non-Operating Assets: Investment Property, Stakes, ICDs, Held for Sale

Scope: Non-operating assets: investment property (Ind AS 40), strategic stakes, ICDs, loans to group companies, assets held for sale

Why it matters

Between operating capital and surplus cash lies a third class: assets that have value but do not contribute to operating earnings (EBITDA/NOPAT). Indian balance sheets often carry substantial amounts — land banks held as investment property, stakes in listed group companies or JVs, inter-corporate deposits (ICDs) and loans to promoter-group entities, and assets classified as held for sale. Left inside invested capital they depress ROIC; ignored in valuation they understate value; taken at book value without scrutiny they can overstate it (loans to group companies that will never be repaid). Governance analysts and lenders treat several of these — related-party loans especially — as red flags.

Core notes

Investment property (Ind AS 40)

Property (land or building) held to earn rentals or for capital appreciation, rather than for use in production/supply or administration, or sale in the ordinary course (Ind AS 40.5). Indian manufacturers frequently hold surplus land or let-out office floors here.

  • Measurement: Ind AS 40 permits only the cost model (carve-out from IAS 40's fair value option); fair value must be disclosed (Ind AS 40.79(e)), typically based on a registered valuer's report (Schedule III 2021 requires disclosure of whether fair value is based on a registered valuer's valuation).
  • Analysis: exclude from operating invested capital; exclude rental income from core EBITDA (Topic 2 s03); add fair value (net of tax on gains if a sale is contemplated) in the EV bridge.
  • Dual-use property (partly owner-occupied): classified as investment property only if the portions could be sold separately or the owner-occupied portion is insignificant (Ind AS 40.10).

Strategic stakes

Equity investments in non-subsidiary companies — listed group companies, suppliers, JVs and associates in consolidated statements, subsidiaries in standalone statements.

Type Measurement Analytical treatment
Associates/JVs (CFS) Equity method (Ind AS 28) Exclude from IC; exclude share of profit from EBITDA; add market or fair value in EV bridge
Other equity stakes FVOCI (irrevocable election) or FVTPL (Ind AS 109) Exclude from IC; value at market price, apply holding-company discount if illiquid or strategic
Subsidiaries (standalone) Cost or Ind AS 109 (Ind AS 27.10) Analyse consolidated; if standalone, value subsidiaries separately (SOTP)

ICDs and loans to group companies

Inter-corporate deposits and loans are financial assets at amortised cost (Ind AS 109) with ECL.

  • Treatment: non-operating, excluded from IC and surplus cash. Value at recoverable amount — not face value — after considering counterparty credit, related-party nature, interest servicing record and repayment history.
  • Regulatory checks: s.186 Companies Act (limits and special resolution, interest rate not lower than prevailing yield of government securities of similar tenor — s.186(7)); s.185 (loans to directors and entities in which directors are interested — restricted); Schedule III 2021 disclosure of loans to promoters, directors, KMPs and related parties repayable on demand or without specified terms; CARO 2020 clause 3(iii); Ind AS 24 related-party disclosure; SEBI LODR Reg 23 approval of material related-party transactions.
  • Red flags: loans growing while interest is accrued but not received; repeated rollovers; counterparties with weak financials; loans to entities that then pay the company's suppliers or promoters.

Assets held for sale (Ind AS 105)

Non-current assets (or disposal groups) whose carrying amount will be recovered principally through sale, available for immediate sale and highly probable to be sold within 12 months (Ind AS 105.6–8). Measured at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell; depreciation stops (Ind AS 105.25). Presented separately on the balance sheet (Schedule III Div II has a line "Non-current assets classified as held for sale" in current assets and related liabilities in current liabilities).

  • Treatment: non-operating; exclude from IC; add expected net sale proceeds (after tax) in the EV bridge; remove associated liabilities consistently.
  • Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105.32) attached to the assets: profit already excluded from continuing earnings (Topic 2 s07).

Book value and valuation differ in both directions: land understated at cost, ICD overstated at face. The ROIC denominator excludes all four (₹1,140 crore at book).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Land held for future expansion: classified as PPE (owner use intended) or investment property (undetermined use is investment property under Ind AS 40.8(b)). If it is genuinely for expansion, it is growth capital — similar to CWIP; disclose.
  • Strategic stakes that are operationally integral (a captive raw material supplier): consider proportionate consolidation for operating analysis, but avoid double counting in the EV bridge.
  • Loans to subsidiaries in standalone: eliminated on consolidation — irrelevant at group level, but crucial for standalone credit and s.186 compliance.
  • Interest income on ICDs: remove from core EBITDA; if accrued but not received, it inflates profit and receivables.
  • Held-for-sale assets that remain unsold beyond 12 months: Ind AS 105.9 allows extension only for events beyond the entity's control; otherwise reclassify and catch up depreciation (Ind AS 105.27).
  • Deferred tax on fair value: when adding fair value of land or stakes, deduct tax on the gain if sale is the expected route; if held indefinitely, many analysts apply a discount instead.

Key takeaways

  • Non-operating assets have value but no contribution to EBITDA: exclude from invested capital and add separately in valuation.
  • Investment property is at cost under Ind AS 40 (no fair value model) — use disclosed fair value, net of tax where relevant.
  • Strategic stakes: value at market with a holding discount; associates/JVs follow the same logic.
  • ICDs and group loans: value at recoverable amount; check s.185/186, Schedule III 2021, CARO 3(iii), Ind AS 24 and LODR Reg 23.
  • Assets held for sale: expected net proceeds; depreciation stops; associated liabilities removed consistently.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and value [L1]

A company's balance sheet shows: freehold land not used for operations, cost ₹45 crore (fair value disclosed ₹310 crore); 8% stake in a listed customer at FVOCI ₹220 crore; ICD of ₹150 crore to an unrelated NBFC, interest paid regularly; a warehouse held for sale at ₹60 crore (fair value less costs to sell ₹75 crore). Classify each and compute the value you would add in the EV bridge, assuming a 20% holding discount on the stake and tax of 14.30% on land gains.

Q2. Group loan red flags [L2]

A mid-cap's standalone balance sheet shows loans to a promoter-group company rising from ₹40 crore to ₹420 crore over three years, interest accrued but unpaid ₹55 crore, and a note stating the loans are "repayable on demand". The company's paid-up capital is ₹100 crore, free reserves ₹500 crore and securities premium ₹100 crore. Compute the s.186 thresholds, identify the disclosures and approvals required, and recommend an analytical treatment.

💡 Hint: Apply both the 60% and 100% tests in s.186(2) and take the higher limit.

Q3. Held-for-sale journal entries [L2]

On 31 December 2025 a plant with carrying amount ₹180 crore (cost ₹400 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹220 crore) meets Ind AS 105 held-for-sale criteria; fair value less costs to sell is ₹150 crore. Annual depreciation was ₹24 crore. Pass the entries for the year ended 31 March 2026, show the balance-sheet presentation, and state the effect on core EBITDA and invested capital.

Q4. ROIC distortion [L3]

A company reports NOPAT of ₹900 crore and book invested capital (equity + debt − cash) of ₹7,500 crore, including investment property ₹600 crore, a strategic stake ₹900 crore and ICDs ₹500 crore. Rental and ICD interest income of ₹70 crore is included in NOPAT. Compute reported ROIC and operating ROIC, and explain why the latter is the right measure for comparing with a pure-play peer.
