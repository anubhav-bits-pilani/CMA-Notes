Key concept

FCFF from EBIT: the Textbook Formula

Concept: FCFF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − Capex − ΔNWC

Statement

\[ \text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC} \]

where:

  • \( \text{EBIT} \) = operating profit before interest and tax, excluding non-operating other income and normalised for exceptional items;
  • \( t \) = the tax rate applicable to operating profit (marginal rate, e.g. 25.168% under s.115BAA, or a cash tax rate where justified);
  • \( \text{D\&A} \) = depreciation and amortisation charged in arriving at EBIT (non-cash);
  • \( \text{Capex} \) = cash (or accrual, consistently) investment in PPE and intangibles, including CWIP and capital advances;
  • \( \Delta\text{NWC} \) = increase in operating net working capital (an increase is a use of cash).

Explanation

FCFF is the cash generated by operations that is available to all capital providers — lenders and shareholders — after the business has paid operating taxes and made the investment needed to sustain and grow. It is the numerator in an enterprise DCF discounted at WACC.

The term \( \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) \) is NOPAT: tax is computed as if the company had no debt. The tax shield on interest is excluded here because WACC already uses the after-tax cost of debt; including it in both places would double count.

Conditions and choices that must be consistent:

  • Leases: if EBIT is post-Ind AS 116, it excludes rent but includes ROU depreciation; then lease liabilities must be treated as debt in the EV bridge and ROU additions considered as investment. If EBIT is rent-adjusted, no lease debt is deducted (see s03).
  • Other income: excluded from EBIT; surplus cash and investments are added separately in the EV-to-equity bridge.
  • ESOP cost: keep it as an operating expense (it is a real cost) unless dilution is modelled separately.
  • Tax rate: NOPAT tax at the statutory marginal rate is standard; companies with tax holidays (SEZ units under s.10AA, legacy 80-IA) may have lower cash taxes for a limited period — model them explicitly and taper.
  • Capex and ΔNWC must exclude capex creditors and non-operating items (see s06).

Illustration

FY2025 data for a consumer company (₹ crore): EBIT 1,400 (after excluding other income of 120), D&A 260 (excluding ROU — company uses rent-adjusted basis), capex 480, NWC opening 900, closing 1,020; tax rate 25.168%.

Line Amount
EBIT 1,400.0
Tax on EBIT at 25.168% (352.4)
NOPAT 1,047.6
Add D&A 260.0
Less capex (480.0)
Less ΔNWC (120.0)
FCFF 707.6

Reinvestment = capex − D&A + ΔNWC = 340; reinvestment rate = 340 ÷ 1,047.6 ≈ 32%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute FCFF [L1]

EBIT ₹860 crore, D&A ₹190 crore, capex ₹310 crore, NWC increased by ₹75 crore, tax rate 25.168%. Compute NOPAT and FCFF.

Q2. Normalise before computing [L2]

A company reports PBT ₹620 crore, finance cost ₹90 crore, other income ₹110 crore (of which ₹70 crore is treasury income and ₹40 crore is scrap sales considered operating), exceptional impairment ₹60 crore, D&A ₹150 crore. Capex ₹280 crore including ₹50 crore paid to capital creditors for last year's purchases; NWC increased by ₹40 crore of which ₹15 crore is an increase in interest accrued on deposits. Compute normalised EBIT and FCFF at 25.168%.

💡 Hint: Decide which other income stays in EBIT, add back the non-cash exceptional item, and clean ΔNWC for the non-operating accrual.

Q3. Interview: why not deduct interest? [L1]

Explain in under 120 words why FCFF does not deduct interest and why the tax is computed on EBIT rather than PBT.
