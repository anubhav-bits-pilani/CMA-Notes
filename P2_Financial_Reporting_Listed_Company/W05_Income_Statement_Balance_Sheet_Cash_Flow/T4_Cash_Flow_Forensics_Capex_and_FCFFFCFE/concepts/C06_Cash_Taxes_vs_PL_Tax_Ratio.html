Key concept

Cash Taxes vs P&L Tax Ratio

Concept: Cash taxes vs P&L tax: ratio = Income tax paid (CFO) ÷ Current tax expense

Statement

\[ \text{Cash tax ratio} = \frac{\text{Income tax paid (net of refunds), from CFO}}{\text{Current tax expense, from P\&L}} \]

A related measure uses total tax:

\[ \text{Cash ETR} = \frac{\text{Income tax paid}}{\text{PBT}}, \qquad \text{Book ETR} = \frac{\text{Current tax} + \text{Deferred tax}}{\text{PBT}} \]

where income tax paid is the line in the operating section of the cash flow statement (Ind AS 7.35) and current tax expense is from the tax expense note (Ind AS 12.79–80), including current tax relating to earlier years unless you choose to exclude it consistently.

Explanation

Current tax expense is the accrual for the year's taxable income; tax paid is cash, which includes advance tax instalments for the current year (15 June, 15 September, 15 December, 15 March), self-assessment tax for the prior year, TDS/TCS deducted by customers, demands paid under protest and refunds received. Over several years the ratio should be close to 100%; persistent deviations need explanation:

  • Below 100%: large refunds received, disputed demands not paid, accruals for uncertain tax positions (Appendix C to Ind AS 12) provided but not paid, or TDS receivables accumulating (common for companies with many corporate customers deducting TDS under s.194C/194J/194Q).
  • Above 100%: payment of earlier years' demands, deposits under protest (often 20% of demand to obtain a stay before the CIT(A)), TDS credit mismatches, or growth in taxable income making advance tax exceed the prior-year self-assessment catch-up.

Comparing cash ETR with book ETR reveals deferred tax effects. A company with large accelerated tax depreciation (the Income-tax Act WDV rates exceed Ind AS straight-line) has book ETR above cash ETR and a rising DTL — real cash savings, but temporary. A company whose cash tax persistently exceeds book tax may be building DTA from disallowances (e.g. s.43B items, provisions not deductible until paid) or carrying disputed deposits.

For FCFF forecasting, the steady-state tax rate should converge to the marginal rate (25.168% under s.115BAA), while near-term cash taxes may reflect timing benefits. For normalised PAT (t2) the book tax charge is adjusted; for FCF the cash tax is what matters.

Tax regime note: the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections; rates under the concessional corporate regime are retained. Verify section references when analysing FY2026-27 onwards.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and interpret [L1]

Current tax ₹410 crore; deferred tax ₹(25) crore credit; tax paid ₹470 crore; PBT ₹1,700 crore. Compute cash tax ratio, cash ETR and book ETR, and list two likely reasons for the pattern.

Q2. Deriving tax paid [L2]

Current tax expense ₹260 crore (including ₹15 crore for earlier years). Current tax liabilities: opening ₹22 crore, closing ₹18 crore. Non-current tax assets (advance tax and TDS net of provisions, refunds due): opening ₹140 crore, closing ₹175 crore. Tax on OCI items (current tax) ₹3 crore. Derive income tax paid and the cash tax ratio.

💡 Hint: Work through the net current tax position and remember OCI current tax also passes through the tax account.

Q3. Forecasting judgement [L3]

A company's cash ETR has averaged 17% for five years against a book ETR of 25% because of accelerated tax depreciation on a large capex programme that is ending. Explain how you would set the cash tax assumption for the next five forecast years in an FCFF model and what balance sheet items you would forecast to keep the model consistent.
