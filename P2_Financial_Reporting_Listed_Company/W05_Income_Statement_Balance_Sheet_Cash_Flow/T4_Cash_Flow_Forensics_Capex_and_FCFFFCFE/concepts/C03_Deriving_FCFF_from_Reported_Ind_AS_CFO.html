Key concept

Deriving FCFF from Reported Ind AS CFO

Concept: FCFF from Ind AS CFO (interest paid already in financing) ≈ CFO − Interest paid × t − Capex − lease payments (if EV excludes lease liabilities)

Statement

\[ \text{FCFF} \approx \text{CFO}_{\text{Ind AS}} - \text{Interest paid} \times t - \text{Capex} - \text{Lease payments}_{\text{(principal + interest)}} \]

where:

  • \( \text{CFO}_{\text{Ind AS}} \) = reported net cash from operating activities (interest paid excluded, lease payments excluded, taxes paid deducted);
  • \( \text{Interest paid} \times t \) = the interest tax shield embedded in taxes paid; it must be removed so that FCFF reflects taxes on an unlevered basis;
  • \( t \) = marginal tax rate (25.168% under s.115BAA);
  • \( \text{Capex} \) = purchase of PPE and intangibles, net of disposal proceeds if using a net definition;
  • the lease-payment term applies only when the EV bridge excludes lease liabilities (rent-adjusted approach). If lease liabilities are deducted as debt, omit it.

Explanation

Why the interest term? Under Ind AS 7.33 interest paid is in financing, so CFO is already "before interest" — you do not deduct interest itself. But taxes paid in CFO are lower because interest is tax-deductible. FCFF must be unlevered: the tax shield belongs in WACC, not in the cash flows. Deducting \( \text{Interest} \times t \) restores the tax that an all-equity company would have paid.

Contrast with US GAAP, where CFO is after interest:

\[ \text{FCFF}_{\text{US GAAP}} = \text{CFO} + \text{Interest paid} \times (1 - t) - \text{Capex} \]

Using the US formula on an Ind AS CFO would add back interest that was never deducted — overstating FCFF by the full after-tax interest.

Other adjustments needed before treating CFO as "operating":

  • Remove exceptional or non-recurring cash items (tax refunds of prior years, settlement receipts).
  • Remove interest and dividends received if your company has any classified in operating (rare for Ind AS non-financial entities).
  • Reverse distortions from factoring or SCF (s07).
  • If finance cost includes lease interest and you take approach "leases as debt", the interest tax shield on lease interest should also be removed for consistency.

The "≈" sign matters: taxes paid differ from the tax on EBIT because of timing (advance tax, refunds), deferred taxes and permanent differences. For valuation forecasts, the EBIT route (c02) is cleaner; the CFO route is used to test historical FCFF and to anchor the forecast to reported cash.

Illustration

A retailer (₹ crore), FY2025: CFO 1,520; interest paid on borrowings 60; lease principal 420; lease interest 210; capex 600; \( t \) = 25.168%. The valuation uses rent-adjusted EBITDA and excludes lease liabilities from net debt.

Line Amount
Reported CFO 1,520.0
Less interest tax shield on borrowings (60 × 25.168%) (15.1)
Less capex (600.0)
Less lease payments (420 + 210) (630.0)
FCFF (rent-adjusted basis) 274.9

Lease interest is not tax-shield-adjusted here because, on the rent-adjusted basis, the full lease payment is treated like a deductible rent expense — the tax benefit is part of operations. Under the "leases as debt" approach, FCFF would instead be 1,520 − (60 + 210) × 25.168% − 600 = 852.0, with lease liabilities deducted in the EV bridge.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ind AS vs US GAAP formula [L1]

CFO ₹900 crore (Ind AS), interest paid ₹120 crore (financing), capex ₹350 crore, tax 25.168%. Compute FCFF. Then show the figure an analyst would get by wrongly applying the US GAAP formula, and the size of the error.

Q2. Two lease approaches [L2]

A multiplex chain reports CFO ₹740 crore, borrowings interest paid ₹45 crore, lease principal ₹280 crore, lease interest ₹190 crore, capex ₹260 crore, lease liabilities ₹2,900 crore, borrowings ₹500 crore, cash ₹150 crore. Compute FCFF and net debt under the "leases as debt" and "leases as operating cost" approaches.

💡 Hint: Each approach changes both the FCFF and the debt deducted; keep the pair consistent.

Q3. Cleaning CFO first [L2]

Reported CFO of ₹1,200 crore includes an income tax refund of ₹80 crore relating to FY2018-19 and an increase of ₹150 crore in non-recourse factored receivables. Interest paid ₹100 crore, capex ₹420 crore, no leases. Compute reported-basis and adjusted FCFF and explain each adjustment.
