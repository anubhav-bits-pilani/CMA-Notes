Key concept

Maintenance Capex Proxies

Concept: Maintenance capex proxy = D&A, or Capex × (1 − growth capex share disclosed by management)

Statement

Two common proxies:

\[ \text{Maintenance capex}_{\text{D\&A proxy}} = \text{D\&A}_{\text{owned assets}} \]

\[ \text{Maintenance capex}_{\text{guidance proxy}} = \text{Capex} \times (1 - s_g) \]

where:

  • \( \text{D\&A}_{\text{owned assets}} \) = depreciation of owned PPE plus amortisation of internally developed or purchased software and similar replaceable intangibles; excludes ROU depreciation and amortisation of acquired brands/customer relationships;
  • \( \text{Capex} \) = total capex for the period;
  • \( s_g \) = share of capex that management discloses as growth (expansion), e.g. 0.75 if "₹750 crore of the ₹1,000 crore programme is for new capacity".

Explanation

The D&A proxy assumes the company must replace, every year, the portion of its asset base that is consumed. It is quick, objective and available for every company, but biased:

  • Inflation bias: historical-cost depreciation understates replacement cost — the older the asset base, the larger the understatement.
  • Asset-life bias: Schedule II useful lives (or management estimates) may differ from economic lives.
  • Growth contamination: a fast-growing company's D&A includes depreciation on recently added growth assets, which is still "maintenance" of the enlarged base — reasonable — but in very young asset bases D&A may be far below what replacements will cost later.
  • Lease exclusion: ROU depreciation reflects lease cost, not replacement capex; including it double counts lease payments.

The guidance proxy uses management's disclosed split from investor presentations, analyst meets or capex announcements (often under SEBI LODR Reg 30 disclosures of capacity additions). It is company-specific but self-interested: management has an incentive to classify more spend as growth, because that flatters "sustainable" FCF. Test it against depreciation and against the capex in years without expansion.

Best practice is to triangulate: compute both proxies, add a unit-economics estimate (per store, per MTPA — see s05) and present a range with a chosen point estimate, documented in the model's assumption sheet.

Illustration

A cement company FY2025 (₹ crore): total capex 3,200; depreciation on owned PPE 820; ROU depreciation 40; management states that 80% of capex is for the 5 MTPA expansion.

Proxy Calculation Maintenance capex
D&A proxy (owned assets only) 820 820
Guidance proxy 3,200 × (1 − 0.80) 640
Unit economics cross-check (5 MTPA × ₹500 crore growth capex) 3,200 − 2,500 700

The range is ₹640–820 crore. If CFO is ₹2,400 crore, sustainable FCF ranges from ₹1,580 to ₹1,760 crore, while reported FCF after total capex is −₹800 crore — the gap is the growth investment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply both proxies [L1]

A consumer company reports capex ₹540 crore, depreciation ₹310 crore (including ROU depreciation ₹95 crore), amortisation of acquired brands ₹25 crore. Management says 60% of capex is for new capacity. Compute maintenance capex under both proxies and comment on the gap.

Q2. Inflation-adjusting the D&A proxy [L2]

A steel plant's gross block of ₹12,000 crore has an average age of 14 years and is depreciated straight-line over 25 years. Construction cost inflation has averaged 6% a year. Estimate a replacement-cost-adjusted maintenance capex and explain the assumptions.

💡 Hint: Scale the historical-cost depreciation by the cumulative inflation factor over the average age.

Q3. Challenge the guidance [L3]

Over five years a hospital chain's capex was ₹600, ₹650, ₹1,400, ₹1,500 and ₹700 crore; management called 85% of each year's capex "growth". D&A on owned assets grew from ₹280 crore to ₹430 crore and bed count rose from 3,800 to 5,000. Evaluate the guidance using bed-based unit economics (assume ₹1.2 crore growth capex per bed) and the D&A proxy.
