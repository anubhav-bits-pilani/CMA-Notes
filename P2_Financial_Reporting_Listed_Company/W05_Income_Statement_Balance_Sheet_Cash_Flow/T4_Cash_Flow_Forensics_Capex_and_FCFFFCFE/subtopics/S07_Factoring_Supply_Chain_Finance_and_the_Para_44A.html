Factoring, Supply Chain Finance and the Para 44A Reconciliation

Scope: Factoring, bill discounting and supply chain finance distorting CFO; Ind AS 7 para 44A financing liabilities reconciliation

Why it matters

Working capital is the easiest lever to manage CFO. Selling receivables to a factor, discounting bills on TReDS or with a bank, and paying suppliers through a bank-sponsored supply chain finance (SCF) programme can all move cash between years and between the operating and financing sections without any change in underlying business performance. In India these tools are pervasive — MSME suppliers use TReDS (RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart), large corporates run vendor-financing and dealer-financing programmes, and the 45-day MSME payment rule under section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act (effective FY2023-24) has increased SCF usage. Analysts who read CFO without checking these arrangements systematically overestimate cash generation and underestimate debt.

Core notes

Receivables: derecognition determines the cash flow

Under Ind AS 109 (section 3.2), a transferred receivable is derecognised only if substantially all risks and rewards are transferred (or control is lost when risks are neither transferred nor retained).

Arrangement Typical Ind AS 109 outcome Cash flow on receipt from factor/bank Analytical treatment
Non-recourse factoring / TReDS without recourse Derecognised Operating inflow (looks like customer collection) Add back to receivables and to debt to compare with peers
With-recourse factoring Not derecognised; liability recognised Financing inflow (borrowing); customer collection later is operating with liability settled non-cash Already in debt; check the borrowings note
Bill discounting with recourse (LC-backed or bank discounting) Not derecognised Financing inflow Already in debt; check "secured borrowings — bills discounted"
Customer-paid early-payment discount Normal collection Operating No adjustment

When receivables are derecognised, CFO in the year of a programme's start or expansion gets a one-time boost equal to the increase in factored balance; DSO falls. The flattering effect reverses if the programme shrinks.

Payables: supply chain finance (reverse factoring)

In SCF the buyer confirms invoices to a bank, the bank pays the supplier early, and the buyer pays the bank on an extended date (e.g. 120 days instead of 60).

  • If the liability remains a trade payable (terms substantially the same, bank acts as paying agent), the buyer keeps it in trade payables; CFO benefits from the extended terms.
  • If the terms are substantially different (interest-bearing, much longer, bank-controlled), the liability is in substance a borrowing and should be presented as debt; payment to the bank is financing.
  • The IASB's 2023 amendments to IAS 7 and IFRS 7 (supplier finance arrangements) require disclosure of terms, carrying amounts, payment-due ranges and non-cash changes. Whether and from when equivalent amendments to Ind AS 7 and Ind AS 107 apply depends on MCA notification — verify the status for the reporting year you are analysing rather than assuming the IFRS effective date (see topic note).

Dealer and channel financing

Many auto and consumer durables companies have banks finance their dealers (often with OEM guarantees or first-loss default guarantees). Receivables collected via dealer finance look like immediate cash. Check contingent liabilities and guarantees for the OEM's exposure.

Ind AS 7 para 44A: changes in liabilities arising from financing activities

Ind AS 7.44A–44E require a reconciliation of opening and closing balances of liabilities arising from financing activities, separating cash changes from non-cash changes:

Liability Opening Cash flows Acquisitions Forex Fair value New leases Other Closing
Non-current borrowings
Current borrowings (incl. bills discounted)
Lease liabilities
Interest accrued

Uses in forensic work:

  • Confirms whether bill discounting and with-recourse factoring are inside borrowings.
  • Shows non-cash debt increases (buyer's credit, new leases, acquired debt) not visible in financing cash flows.
  • Lets you derive gross repayments and drawdowns where the cash flow statement nets them (net presentation permitted for quick-turnover items under Ind AS 7.22–23).

Detection checklist

  1. Read accounting policies for "derecognition of financial assets", "bills discounted", "channel financing".
  2. Read the borrowings note: secured working-capital loans, bills discounted, acceptances.
  3. Read the contingent liabilities note: "bills discounted with banks", "guarantees to banks for dealer finance".
  4. Compare DSO and DPO trends with peers and with quarterly data (Reg 33 half-year balance sheets).
  5. Read the financial risk note (Ind AS 107 credit/liquidity risk) for transferred-asset disclosures.
  6. Check MSME payables disclosure (MSMED Act s.22) for payment delays.

Illustration (₹ crore)

A capital goods company reports CFO of ₹520 crore. Notes reveal receivables of ₹300 crore sold without recourse at year-end (₹120 crore prior year), and acceptances under a supplier finance programme of ₹260 crore included in trade payables (₹150 crore prior year), with tenor extended from 60 to 150 days.

Adjustment Amount
Reported CFO 520
Less increase in derecognised receivables (300 − 120) (180)
Less increase in SCF payables treated as debt (260 − 150) (110)
Adjusted CFO 230
Adjusted net debt increases by 560 (300 + 260)

The company's CFO/EBITDA conversion drops by more than half once the arrangements are reversed.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Recurring, stable programmes: if the factored balance is constant, CFO is not distorted in that year, but net debt and NWC still differ from peers; adjust balances even if flows are unaffected.
  • Quarter-end window dressing: higher factoring just before 31 March and 30 September; compare with average balances where disclosed.
  • TReDS on the supplier side: the buyer's payable becomes payable to the financier; legally it is still the same obligation — judge by terms.
  • Section 43B(h): delayed payment to MSMEs beyond the MSMED Act limit disallows the deduction until paid; companies may shift to SCF to comply, creating a new debt-like liability.
  • Credit insurance and partial recourse: continuing involvement under Ind AS 109 leads to partial recognition; read the transferred-assets note carefully.

Key takeaways

  • Non-recourse factoring and bill discounting without recourse move collections into CFO early; with-recourse arrangements stay as debt with financing cash flows.
  • Supply chain finance can lengthen payables and inflate CFO; if terms are substantially different, it is debt.
  • The Ind AS 7.44A reconciliation reveals non-cash debt changes and confirms where discounted bills sit.
  • Adjust both CFO (for changes in programme balances) and net debt/NWC (for the balances themselves).
  • Track the IAS 7/IFRS 7 supplier finance disclosures and their Ind AS notification status.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the arrangement [L1]

For each arrangement, state whether the receivable/payable is derecognised or reclassified and where the cash flows appear: (a) export bills discounted with recourse; (b) TReDS sale of an invoice without recourse; (c) reverse factoring where the bank pays suppliers on day 30 and the buyer pays the bank on day 90 at the same invoice value; (d) reverse factoring where the buyer pays the bank on day 240 with an interest charge of 9% p.a.

Q2. Adjust CFO and net debt [L2]

An auto component company reports: CFO ₹880 crore; EBITDA ₹1,250 crore; net debt ₹1,100 crore. Receivables derecognised under non-recourse factoring: ₹410 crore at 31 March 2025, ₹240 crore at 31 March 2024. Dealer financing guaranteed by the company: ₹320 crore (contingent liability). Supplier finance payables (tenor 180 days, interest-bearing) in trade payables: ₹190 crore (prior year ₹60 crore). Compute adjusted CFO, adjusted CFO/EBITDA and adjusted net debt, stating which items you include and why.

💡 Hint: Decide separately for each item whether it affects the flow (change in balance) or only the stock (balance).

Q3. Reading the 44A table [L2]

A company's Ind AS 7.44A table shows non-current borrowings: opening ₹2,000 crore, net cash flows +₹150 crore, acquisition of subsidiary +₹400 crore, forex +₹60 crore, closing ₹2,610 crore. Lease liabilities: opening ₹900 crore, cash flows −₹210 crore, new leases +₹350 crore, interest accretion +₹80 crore, closing ₹1,120 crore. Explain what each non-cash movement means for FCF analysis and for the net debt bridge.

Q4. Case: sudden DSO improvement [L3]

A listed EPC company's DSO falls from 145 to 95 days in one year while revenue grows 18% and CFO turns from negative to ₹600 crore. Management attributes it to "improved collections". Design a forensic review: data to request, notes to read, peer comparisons, and how you would present the adjusted metrics to an investment committee.
