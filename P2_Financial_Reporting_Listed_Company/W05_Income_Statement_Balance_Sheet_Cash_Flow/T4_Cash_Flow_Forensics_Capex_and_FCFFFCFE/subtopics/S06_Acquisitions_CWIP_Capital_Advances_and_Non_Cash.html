Subtopic note

Acquisitions, CWIP, Capital Advances and Non-Cash Capex

Scope: Acquisitions, capital advances and CWIP movements; supplier credit and capex creditors as non-cash capex

Why it matters

"Purchase of property, plant and equipment" in the investing section is the cash paid for capex in the year — not the economic investment made. Capex financed by suppliers, assets acquired through business combinations, advances paid for machinery not yet delivered and work-in-progress parked in CWIP all make the cash line diverge from the true investment. A company can show low capex (and high FCF) by stretching capital creditors or by buying growth through acquisitions shown on a separate line. Analysts reconstructing capex from the balance sheet — the standard approach in a 3-statement model — must reconcile these items, and auditors check them because Ind AS 7.43 requires non-cash investing and financing transactions to be excluded from the cash flow statement and disclosed elsewhere.

Core notes

Gross block roll-forward as the anchor

The PPE note (Schedule III requires a reconciliation of gross and net carrying amounts) gives:

\[ \text{Closing gross block} = \text{Opening} + \text{Additions} + \text{Acquired through business combinations} - \text{Disposals} \pm \text{Other adjustments (forex, reclassifications)} \]

Additions include capitalisation transferred from CWIP. Economic capex for the year is better captured as:

\[ \text{Accrual capex} = \text{Additions to PPE and intangibles (excl. ROU)} + \Delta\text{CWIP} + \Delta\text{Intangibles under development} \]

and cash capex as:

\[ \text{Cash capex} = \text{Accrual capex} + \Delta\text{Capital advances} - \Delta\text{Capital creditors} - \text{Non-cash additions (e.g. capitalised interest if shown separately, ARO additions)} \]

CWIP movements

CWIP holds costs of assets under construction, including borrowing costs capitalised under Ind AS 23 and directly attributable pre-operative costs. Only the increase in CWIP adds to accrual capex in the year; a capitalisation simply moves cost from CWIP to gross block (no new investment). Schedule III (2021 amendment) requires CWIP ageing (less than 1 year, 1–2, 2–3, more than 3 years) and a completion schedule for projects overdue or over budget — use it to judge whether CWIP is genuine growth or stalled (see t3).

Capital advances

Advances paid to suppliers for capital goods are shown under "Other non-current assets" per Schedule III. The cash has left the business and is reported within "Purchase of PPE" in the investing section by most companies. A spike in capital advances shows that future capacity has been committed; a persistent balance that does not convert into CWIP or gross block can signal related-party leakage or stalled projects.

Capex creditors and supplier credit

Capital creditors (payables for capital goods) sit inside "Other financial liabilities" in Schedule III. When a supplier delivers a machine on 180-day credit, the asset is added to gross block but no cash flows in the year.

Transaction Accounting Cash flow statement
Machine received on credit Dr PPE / Cr Capital creditors Nothing (non-cash; disclose per Ind AS 7.43)
Payment next year Dr Capital creditors / Cr Bank Investing outflow (purchase of PPE)
Buyer's credit / LC usance arranged by a bank, supplier paid by bank Dr Capital creditors / Cr Borrowings (or acceptances) No investing outflow; later repayment is financing

The last case is the most dangerous for FCF analysis: the capex never appears in investing — the cash leaves through financing as debt repayment. Many Indian infrastructure, power and metal companies use buyer's credit and supplier's credit. Reconstruct capex from the gross block and adjust.

Acquisitions

Ind AS 7.39–42A: aggregate cash flows from obtaining control of subsidiaries are presented separately in investing, net of cash acquired. Acquired PPE, working capital and debt are not separate lines. For analysis:

  • FCF "before acquisitions" is reported FCF; FCF "after acquisitions" deducts the acquisition outflow. Serial acquirers should be judged on the latter.
  • Deferred and contingent consideration paid later appears in investing (or financing if it is in substance financing) — track it.
  • Changes in ownership without loss of control (buying out NCI) are financing (Ind AS 7.42A), not investing — a subtle way growth spend avoids "investing" totals.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Item Amount
Additions to PPE (excl. ROU) per note 820
CWIP: opening 300, closing 460 Δ +160
Capital advances: opening 40, closing 95 Δ +55
Capital creditors: opening 70, closing 150 Δ +80
Buyer's credit raised for imported equipment 120
Reported "Purchase of PPE" in investing 835

Accrual capex = 820 + 160 = 980. Cash capex implied = 980 + 55 − 80 − 120 = 835, agreeing with the reported figure. FCF computed with ₹835 crore understates economic investment by ₹145 crore; ₹120 crore of it will surface as debt repayment in financing.

Model implementation

In the model's fixed asset schedule, forecast accrual capex and model capital creditors as days of capex (or a percentage), so that cash capex = accrual capex − Δ capital creditors. Keep capital creditors out of operating NWC; otherwise ΔNWC and CFO are distorted.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capital creditors inside trade payables: some companies do not separate them; look at the "other financial liabilities" note and the Ind AS 7.43 non-cash disclosure.
  • Government grants for capex (Ind AS 20): if deducted from asset cost or shown as deferred income, the grant receipt may appear in investing or financing; net capex comparisons need consistency.
  • Asset retirement obligations: capitalised decommissioning cost is non-cash.
  • Capex via SPVs or JVs (equity-accounted): investments in JVs are investing but not "capex"; group-level capacity may be built off-balance sheet.
  • Slump sales and asset purchases treated as asset acquisitions (not business combinations) are capex.
  • Forex capitalisation under the Para 46A option (Ind AS 101 transition exemption for long-term foreign currency monetary items) can add non-cash amounts to gross block.

Key takeaways

  • Reported purchase of PPE is cash capex, not economic investment; reconstruct accrual capex from the gross block and CWIP.
  • Capital advances increase cash capex ahead of assets; capital creditors and buyer's credit defer or reroute it.
  • Buyer's credit can push capex entirely into financing — always reconcile gross block to investing outflows.
  • Acquisitions and NCI buyouts are growth investments outside the capex line; show FCF before and after them.
  • Keep capital creditors out of operating NWC in the model.

Practice Questions

Q1. Reconcile capex [L2]

PPE note (₹ crore): additions 1,150 (including ₹40 crore capitalised borrowing cost and ₹25 crore ARO), assets acquired through business combination 210. CWIP opening 520, closing 410. Capital advances opening 90, closing 60. Capital creditors opening 130, closing 220. Reported purchase of PPE ₹1,020 crore. Compute accrual capex and implied cash capex, and identify any unexplained difference.

💡 Hint: Exclude the business-combination additions and decide whether capitalised interest is shown inside or outside purchase of PPE.

Q2. Journal entries for buyer's credit [L1]

Pass journal entries for: (a) import of a machine for USD 5 million when the rate is ₹83/USD under a bank buyer's credit arrangement where the bank pays the overseas supplier directly; (b) repayment of the buyer's credit a year later at ₹85/USD. State the cash flow classification of each step.

Q3. Serial acquirer FCF [L2]

A specialty pharma company reports five-year cumulative CFO ₹4,800 crore, purchase of PPE ₹1,900 crore, acquisition of subsidiaries net of cash ₹2,300 crore and acquisition of NCI ₹600 crore. Compute FCF on three definitions and argue which one should drive valuation.

Q4. Judgement: stalled capital advances [L3]

A mid-cap company's capital advances have stayed at ₹250–270 crore for four years, paid to an entity whose director is a relative of the promoter. CWIP ageing shows ₹180 crore more than 3 years old. Outline your analytical and audit concerns, the disclosures you would check (Schedule III, Ind AS 24, Companies Act s.188) and how you would treat the balances in invested capital and FCF.
