Subtopic note

Ind AS 116 Lease Flows: How CFO and FCF Get Inflated

Scope: Ind AS 116 effect: lease principal and interest in financing, inflating CFO and FCF unless adjusted

Why it matters

For Indian retailers, quick-service restaurants, multiplexes, airlines and hospital chains, lease rentals are often the largest fixed cost after employees. Before 1 April 2019 these rentals were an operating expense under Ind AS 17 and reduced CFO. Under Ind AS 116, the same cash leaves the company through the financing section. A retailer such as Trent or Avenue Supermarts did not generate more cash after FY2019-20, yet reported CFO jumped. If you compute FCF as CFO minus capex, you will overstate free cash flow by the entire rent bill, overstate CFO/EBITDA conversion and, in a DCF, double count value unless lease liabilities are treated as debt. This is the single largest presentation distortion in Indian consumer cash flow analysis.

Core notes

Accounting recap

For a lessee, Ind AS 116 recognises a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a lease liability at the present value of lease payments (Ind AS 116.26). Subsequently:

  • ROU asset is depreciated (P&L: depreciation, below EBITDA).
  • Lease liability accrues interest (P&L: finance cost, below EBITDA).
  • Each payment reduces the liability (principal) and settles accrued interest.

Exemptions (Ind AS 116.5): short-term leases (≤ 12 months) and low-value assets may be expensed. Variable lease payments not based on an index or rate — e.g. revenue-share rent in malls — are expensed in the P&L (Ind AS 116.38(b)) and stay in operating cash flows.

Cash flow classification (Ind AS 116.50)

Payment component Ind AS 116 classification Pre-Ind AS 116 (Ind AS 17 operating lease)
Principal portion of lease liability Financing Operating (as rent)
Interest portion Per Ind AS 7 policy for interest paid — financing for non-financial entities Operating
Short-term, low-value and variable payments Operating Operating

For an Indian non-financial entity, both principal and interest move to financing. Under IFRS, an entity that chose to classify interest paid as operating would keep the interest in CFO, so the inflation is smaller — another reason Indian CFO is not comparable with IFRS peers.

Quantifying the distortion

\[ \text{CFO}_{\text{lease-adjusted}} = \text{CFO}_{\text{reported}} - \text{Lease principal paid} - \text{Lease interest paid} \]

\[ \text{FCF}_{\text{lease-adjusted}} = \text{CFO}_{\text{lease-adjusted}} - \text{Capex} \]

Both amounts are disclosed in the financing section (often as "Payment of lease liabilities — principal" and "Interest on lease liabilities") and in the lease note (total cash outflow for leases, Ind AS 116.53(g)).

Illustration (₹ crore)

A fashion retailer, FY2024-25:

Item Reported Lease-adjusted
EBITDA (post-Ind AS 116) 1,900 1,900
Lease payments: principal — (420)
Lease payments: interest — (210)
Rent-adjusted EBITDA — 1,270
Reported CFO 1,520 1,520
Less lease principal and interest — (630)
CFO comparable with pre-2019 / rent-adjusted basis 1,520 890
Capex (stores, fit-outs) (600) (600)
FCF 920 290

CFO/EBITDA conversion looks like 80% on a reported basis (1,520 ÷ 1,900) and 70% on a consistent rent-adjusted basis (890 ÷ 1,270). Free cash flow falls by more than two-thirds.

Two consistent valuation treatments

There are only two internally consistent ways to treat leases in an FCFF/EV framework:

Approach FCFF EV-to-equity bridge EBITDA multiple
A. Leases as debt (post-Ind AS 116) Exclude lease payments from FCFF; capex excludes new ROU additions but FCFF must still reflect new leases as a financing-like investment Deduct lease liabilities as debt EV including leases ÷ post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA
B. Leases as operating cost (pre-Ind AS 116) Deduct lease payments in FCFF Do not deduct lease liabilities EV excluding leases ÷ rent-adjusted EBITDA

Most Indian sell-side analysts use approach B for retailers because the discount rate (WACC) is estimated without lease debt and because new store leases are a recurring operating requirement. The error is mixing them — deducting lease liabilities in the bridge and deducting lease payments in FCFF (double count), or doing neither (value overstated).

A subtle issue in approach A: new leases signed during the year create ROU assets without cash (a non-cash investing and financing transaction under Ind AS 7.43). If FCFF ignores them, a growing retailer appears to grow with no investment. Practitioners either treat ROU additions as capex or prefer approach B.

Model implementation

In the historical model keep three lease lines separate: ROU additions (non-cash), lease principal paid, lease interest paid. In Excel:

=CFO_Reported - Lease_Principal - Lease_Interest

and a check that principal + interest agrees with the total cash outflow disclosed in the lease note (minus short-term and variable payments, which are already in CFO).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Revenue-share and variable rents remain in operating expenses and CFO, so retailers with more revenue-share malls appear to have lower EBITDA and lower CFO "inflation"; normalise the mix before comparing.
  • Rent concessions (COVID-era practical expedient) were recognised in P&L as negative variable lease payments; FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 CFO and EBITDA include these one-offs.
  • Lease modifications and terminations generate gains in "other income" without cash; reverse them in CFO and normalised EBITDA.
  • Security deposits paid to landlords are financial assets at amortised cost; their outflows are often shown in investing or working capital. Decide consistently whether they are part of store capex.
  • Sale-and-leaseback (Ind AS 116.98–103) produces large investing inflows and a lease liability; treat the proceeds as financing in substance when analysing FCF.
  • Airlines with aircraft on lease have both principal and maintenance reserves; maintenance reserves stay in operating, distorting the comparison further.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 116 moves lease principal and interest into financing for Indian non-financial entities, inflating CFO and CFO-minus-capex FCF by the full lease payment.
  • Lease-adjusted CFO = reported CFO − lease principal − lease interest; disclosed in financing and the lease note.
  • Use one consistent valuation treatment: leases as debt (post-116 EBITDA, deduct lease liabilities) or leases as operating cost (rent-adjusted EBITDA, deduct lease payments).
  • New ROU additions are non-cash under Ind AS 7.43 but are an economic investment in store growth.
  • Variable rents, concessions, modifications and deposits need separate handling.

Practice Questions

Q1. Restate CFO and FCF [L2]

A QSR chain reports FY2024-25 CFO ₹610 crore, capex ₹280 crore, payment of lease liabilities (principal) ₹190 crore and interest on lease liabilities ₹95 crore. Variable revenue-share rent of ₹40 crore is in other expenses. EBITDA post-Ind AS 116 is ₹760 crore. Compute reported and lease-adjusted CFO/EBITDA conversion and FCF.

💡 Hint: Revenue-share rent is already inside both EBITDA and CFO; only the capitalised lease cash flows need adjusting.

Q2. Double count in a DCF [L2]

An analyst values a retailer by discounting FCFF computed as CFO − lease payments − capex, then deducts borrowings of ₹300 crore and lease liabilities of ₹2,400 crore to reach equity value. Explain the error, quantify the direction of the bias and prescribe the correct treatment under each consistent approach.

Q3. Pre- and post-transition comparison [L2]

Using the following data for a multiplex operator, build a 4-year CFO series on a consistent basis. FY2018-19 (Ind AS 17): CFO ₹420 crore, rent expense ₹380 crore. FY2019-20 to FY2021-22 (Ind AS 116): CFO ₹710, ₹180 and ₹520 crore; lease principal ₹300, ₹110, ₹270 crore; lease interest ₹200, ₹190, ₹205 crore; rent concessions credited to P&L ₹0, ₹160, ₹70 crore.

Q4. Interview: Is Ind AS 116 cash-neutral? [L1]

"Ind AS 116 has no impact on cash, so it should not change FCF." Critically evaluate this statement in under 200 words.

Q5. Model-build: lease block [L3]

Design the lease schedule in a 3-statement model for Trent Ltd that supports both valuation approaches through a single switch cell. Specify the inputs (store additions, average lease tenure, incremental borrowing rate, rent per store), the outputs that change with the switch and the integrity checks.
