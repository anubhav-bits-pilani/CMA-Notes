Subtopic note

Ind AS 7 Classification of Interest and Dividends

Scope: Ind AS 7 classification: interest paid financing, interest/dividend received investing for non-financial entities

Why it matters

CFO is the headline "cash earnings" number used in credit rating reports, CFO/EBITDA conversion screens and FCF definitions. Under IAS 7 a company may choose where interest and dividends sit; under Ind AS that choice has been removed for non-financial entities. The result is that an Indian company's CFO is structurally higher than a US GAAP peer's and than many IFRS peers' because interest paid is not deducted. If you compare Titan's CFO with a US jeweller's, or build FCFF from reported CFO without understanding this, you will mis-measure leverage capacity and valuation. In audit, misclassification between sections is one of the most common cash flow statement observations in NFRA inspection reports.

Core notes

The Ind AS carve-out

IAS 7.33 allows interest paid, interest received and dividends received to be classified as operating, or as financing/investing, applied consistently. India's Ind AS 7 modifies this:

Item Financial entity (banks, NBFCs, insurers) Non-financial entity (Ind AS 7.33)
Interest paid Operating Financing
Interest received Operating Investing
Dividends received Operating Investing
Dividends paid (Ind AS 7.34) Financing Financing
Taxes on income (Ind AS 7.35) Operating unless specifically identified Operating unless specifically identified

Thus, for a non-financial company there is no choice. The rationale is that interest paid is a cost of obtaining financial resources and interest/dividends received are returns on investments.

What "interest paid" includes

Ind AS 7.32 requires the total interest paid during the period to be disclosed, whether expensed or capitalised under Ind AS 23. Hence:

  • Interest on borrowings expensed → financing.
  • Interest capitalised into qualifying assets → still interest paid; in practice most Indian companies show it within financing (some show it inside capex in investing — check the policy note).
  • Interest component of lease payments under Ind AS 116.50(b) → financing (because the company applies the Ind AS 7 requirement for interest paid).
  • Unwinding of discount on provisions (decommissioning) is non-cash; it is added back in CFO and does not appear as interest paid.
  • Interest on income-tax delays (section 234B/234C) is usually treated as tax-related or finance cost depending on policy; Indian companies frequently include it in finance cost. Check the notes before reclassifying.

Mechanics in the indirect method

Because finance cost is deducted in arriving at PBT, the indirect-method reconciliation adds back finance cost in the operating section and the actual interest paid appears in financing. Similarly, interest and dividend income are deducted in the operating section and the cash amounts received appear in investing. The amounts added/deducted in CFO are P&L amounts; the amounts in financing/investing are cash amounts — the difference is the movement in interest accrued (payable or receivable).

Illustration (₹ crore)

A retailer reports finance cost of ₹180 crore (lease interest ₹130 crore, borrowings ₹50 crore), interest income on fixed deposits ₹40 crore and dividend from a JV ₹10 crore. Interest accrued on borrowings rose from ₹4 crore to ₹7 crore; interest accrued on deposits fell from ₹6 crore to ₹3 crore.

Section Line Amount
Operating Add back finance cost +180
Operating Less interest income −40
Operating Less dividend income −10
Financing Interest paid on borrowings (50 − 3 increase in accrual) −47
Financing Interest on lease liabilities −130
Investing Interest received (40 + 3 decrease in accrual) +43
Investing Dividend received from JV +10

Under US GAAP (ASC 230), the same company would deduct ₹177 crore of interest paid (lease interest for finance leases; operating lease cost entirely) within operating, and include interest and dividends received in operating. CFO would be lower by roughly ₹180 crore minus ₹53 crore received.

Making CFO comparable

For peer comparison and for credit metrics, analysts typically build an adjusted CFO after interest:

\[ \text{CFO}_{\text{after interest}} = \text{CFO}_{\text{Ind AS}} - \text{Interest paid} + \text{Interest received} + \text{Dividends received} \]

Rating agencies in India (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE) typically compute "cash accruals" or "funds from operations" after interest, so the Ind AS CFO is not their starting number. Decide which definition you use, document it, and apply it to every year and company.

Presentation and disclosure points

  • Ind AS 7.31: cash flows from interest and dividends received and paid shall each be disclosed separately.
  • Accounting policy note should state the classification; for financial entities, the policy explicitly treats them as operating.
  • Companies that are partly financial (e.g. a group with an NBFC subsidiary) classify at the consolidated level using the group's predominant nature; read the consolidated policy note carefully.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Holding companies and CICs: core investment companies registered with RBI are financial entities; their dividend income is operating. A non-registered holding company with mainly investment income may still be "non-financial" — check the policy.
  • Interest received on customer financing (e.g. auto dealers, consumer durables with EMI schemes) — if financing is part of the operating model, management may argue it is operating revenue; under Ind AS 7.33 for a non-financial entity it is still investing unless it is revenue from operations. Read the revenue note.
  • Interest on trade payables / supplier credit — interest on extended supplier credit (e.g. usance LCs) is interest paid and goes to financing, which can flatter CFO relative to peers that pay cash on time.
  • Dividends from subsidiaries in standalone statements — investing; at consolidated level they are eliminated entirely, and only dividends from associates/JVs remain as investing inflows.
  • Capitalised interest — a company in a heavy capex phase may appear to have low "interest paid" in financing if capitalised interest is shown in investing; normalise when comparing.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 7.33 removes the IAS 7 choice: non-financial entities classify interest paid as financing, interest and dividends received as investing.
  • Financial entities classify all three as operating; never compare an NBFC's CFO with a corporate's without restating.
  • Indian CFO is structurally higher than US GAAP CFO; build "CFO after interest" for comparability.
  • The P&L amounts are reversed in operating; the cash amounts (adjusted for accrual movements) appear in financing/investing.
  • Total interest paid includes capitalised interest and lease interest; disclosure is required under Ind AS 7.32.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classification drill [L1]

State the Ind AS 7 section for each item for (i) a cement company and (ii) an NBFC: interest paid on NCDs ₹65 crore; interest received on ICDs ₹12 crore; dividend received from listed shares ₹3 crore; dividend paid to shareholders ₹90 crore; interest on lease liabilities ₹8 crore.

Q2. Cash interest from accruals [L2]

A company's P&L shows finance cost ₹96 crore, which includes ₹14 crore unwinding of discount on decommissioning provision and ₹22 crore lease interest. Interest accrued but not due on borrowings was ₹9 crore at the start and ₹15 crore at the end of the year. Interest of ₹18 crore was capitalised to CWIP during the year and is not in finance cost. Compute the total interest paid to be disclosed and show where each component appears in the cash flow statement.

💡 Hint: Separate non-cash unwinding from cash interest, and remember Ind AS 7.32 includes capitalised interest.

Q3. Restating to a US GAAP basis [L2]

Assume a jewellery retailer's reported CFO is ₹3,200 crore, interest paid in financing ₹420 crore (including lease interest ₹150 crore), interest received in investing ₹160 crore and dividends received ₹5 crore. Restate CFO to a basis comparable with a US GAAP retailer that has only operating leases, stating every assumption about lease principal and interest.

Q4. Judgement: group with an NBFC arm [L3]

A consumer durables group consolidates a captive NBFC that provides EMI financing to customers. In the consolidated cash flow statement, management classifies NBFC loan disbursements as investing and NBFC interest received as investing. Evaluate this policy under Ind AS 7, propose an alternative presentation and explain the impact on group CFO/EBITDA conversion.
