Subtopic note

Reconstructing Indirect-Method CFO from PBT to Taxes Paid

Scope: Indirect-method CFO reconstruction: PBT → non-cash items → working-capital changes → taxes paid

Why it matters

Almost every Indian listed company presents its cash flow statement using the indirect method permitted by Ind AS 7.18(b). The reported CFO line is a single number, but analysts, lenders and valuation teams need to know why it differs from profit. Reconstructing CFO from the face of the P&L and two balance sheets tells you whether the company's reported statement is internally consistent, which items management has chosen to classify where, and whether working capital is quietly absorbing profits. In a 3-statement model the cash flow statement is not "input" at all; it is derived from P&L and balance sheet movements, so the reconstruction logic you learn here is exactly the logic of the model's cash flow tab (Week 14). In audit, a CFO that cannot be reconstructed from the notes is a signal of misclassification, netting or non-cash transactions shown as cash.

Core notes

The four-block structure

Ind AS 7 does not prescribe a template for the indirect method, but Schedule III Division II practice and Ind AS 7.20 produce a near-universal layout:

Block Content Sign logic
1. Starting point Profit before tax (most Indian companies) — some start from PAT Accrual profit
2. Non-cash and non-operating adjustments D&A, impairment, ECL allowance, unrealised forex, share-based payment, fair value gains/losses, profit/loss on sale of PPE/investments, finance cost, interest income, dividend income, share of associates/JVs Reverse anything that is not operating cash, or that belongs in investing/financing
3. Working-capital changes Δ trade receivables, inventories, other financial and non-financial assets, trade payables, other liabilities, provisions, contract balances Increase in operating asset = cash outflow; increase in operating liability = cash inflow
4. Taxes Income taxes paid (net of refunds) Actual cash, not P&L tax

The result is "Net cash generated from operating activities". Ind AS 7.35 requires taxes on income to be classified as operating unless specifically identified with investing or financing activities.

Block 1: why PBT and not PAT

Starting from PBT keeps tax out of the accrual chain. The P&L tax charge (current + deferred) is replaced by the cash tax actually paid in Block 4. If a company starts from PAT, it must add back total tax expense and then deduct tax paid — the answer is identical, but the reconstruction has one extra line.

Block 2: non-cash and reclassification adjustments

Two different kinds of adjustment sit in this block and should be mentally separated:

  • Genuinely non-cash items: depreciation and amortisation (including ROU depreciation under Ind AS 116), impairment of PPE/goodwill, ECL allowance on receivables (Ind AS 109), inventory write-downs (although many companies leave these inside the inventory movement), ESOP cost (Ind AS 102), unrealised exchange differences, fair value changes on FVTPL instruments, provisions for warranties and employee benefits created but not paid (if not routed through working capital).
  • Reclassifications to other sections: finance cost (added back because interest paid is shown in financing per Ind AS 7.33), interest income and dividend income (deducted because received amounts go to investing), profit or loss on sale of PPE and investments (the full proceeds go to investing), share of profit of equity-accounted investees (dividends received go to investing).

A useful test: after Block 2, the remaining number should approximate operating profit before working capital changes, which is close to EBITDA adjusted for non-cash items such as ESOP cost and ECL.

Block 3: working-capital changes

The movement is computed from opening and closing balance sheets, but three corrections are needed before using raw balance sheet differences:

  1. Non-cash movements in the same line: allowance for ECL (if Block 2 added it back, use gross receivables or avoid double counting), inventory write-downs, unrealised forex on receivables/payables.
  2. Non-operating items hiding in operating lines: capital creditors within "other financial liabilities", interest accrued on borrowings, unpaid dividends, capital advances within "other non-current assets", GST input credit on capital goods. These belong to investing/financing.
  3. Acquisitions and disposals of subsidiaries: working capital acquired through a business combination is part of the investing outflow (Ind AS 7.39), not a working-capital change, so consolidated balance sheet differences overstate the operating movement.

Block 4: taxes paid

Taxes paid is derived as:

\[ \text{Tax paid} = \text{Current tax expense} + \text{Opening current tax liability (net)} - \text{Closing current tax liability (net)} \]

where the net liability is current tax liabilities minus current and non-current tax assets (advance tax, TDS receivable and refunds due). Deferred tax never enters the cash computation. Tax on OCI items (e.g. on remeasurement of defined benefit plans) affects the current tax account and must be included if it is current tax.

If reported CFO were, say, ₹760 crore, the ₹50 crore gap must be explained — typical causes are capital creditors parked in "other liabilities", a working capital acquisition, or customer advances reclassified.

Excel implementation

Build the reconstruction on a separate tab that pulls the P&L, notes and balance sheet columns, and always place a check line against the reported CFO:

=ROUND(Reconstructed_CFO - Reported_CFO, 0)

Flag any non-zero variance with conditional formatting, and document each reconciling item in a notes column. A movement formula such as =-(BS!E25-BS!D25) for an asset line keeps signs consistent: increase in an asset is negative cash.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Starting from PAT vs PBT across peers — when standardising, always restate to a PBT start so that the tax block is comparable.
  • Exceptional items — a cash VRS payout sits in CFO; an impairment does not. Read the exceptional items note to split cash from non-cash before deciding what is "normalised" CFO.
  • Customer advances and gold schemes (jewellers) — inflows under customer schemes are reported in working capital, boosting CFO. Whether this is operating cash or quasi-debt is a judgement (see t3 on debt-like items).
  • Netting of tax refunds — a large refund of earlier years' tax inflates current-year CFO; disclose it as a non-recurring item in the bridge.
  • Discontinued operations — Ind AS 105.33(c) requires separate disclosure of net cash flows of discontinued operations; companies that present them within the main statement need an extra reconciliation line.
  • Banks and NBFCs — loans and deposits are operating; the reconstruction for financial entities is fundamentally different and should not be mixed with corporate peers.

Key takeaways

  • Indirect-method CFO = PBT + non-cash items ± reclassifications to investing/financing ± working-capital changes − taxes paid.
  • Separate genuinely non-cash adjustments from reclassifications (finance cost, interest/dividend income, disposal gains); both sit in the same block but for different reasons.
  • Raw balance sheet movements need cleaning for capex creditors, accrued interest, acquisitions and non-cash movements before they become working-capital changes.
  • Taxes paid is derived from current tax expense and the movement in net current tax balances; deferred tax is irrelevant.
  • Always reconcile reconstructed CFO to reported CFO and explain every residual.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify reconciliation items [L1]

For an Indian manufacturing company preparing Ind AS financial statements, state in which block of the indirect-method CFO each of the following appears, and the sign: (a) ROU asset depreciation ₹42 crore; (b) dividend income from a subsidiary ₹15 crore; (c) loss on sale of a factory building ₹6 crore; (d) increase in contract liabilities ₹28 crore; (e) unrealised forex loss on an ECB ₹11 crore; (f) deferred tax credit ₹9 crore.

Q2. Reconstruct CFO from statements [L2]

Data (₹ crore) for FY2024-25: PBT 540; depreciation 125; amortisation 15; finance cost 70; interest income 22; dividend from mutual funds 4; profit on sale of investments 13; ESOP cost 9. Balance sheet (opening → closing): trade receivables 410 → 470; inventories 620 → 690; trade payables 380 → 450; other current liabilities 150 → 195 (closing includes capital creditors of 35; opening had 10); current tax liabilities 18 → 25; advance tax and TDS receivable 40 → 52. Current tax expense 150; deferred tax 12. Reconstruct CFO and list the assumptions you made.

💡 Hint: Strip the capital creditors out of the other current liabilities movement before using it, and derive tax paid from the net current tax position.

Q3. Explaining a CFO gap [L2]

Your reconstruction of a listed FMCG company's CFO gives ₹1,240 crore, but the reported figure is ₹1,318 crore. The notes show: working capital of ₹52 crore came with an acquired subsidiary; income tax refunds of ₹19 crore for AY2021-22 were received; a bill discounting facility of ₹30 crore was outstanding at year-end against trade receivables. Explain how each item could create or close the gap and what further information you would request.

Q4. Model-build: CFO tab design [L3]

Specify the input links, the sign conventions, the cleaning adjustments for non-operating items within operating balance sheet lines, the check row against reported CFO, and how you would document unexplained residuals.
