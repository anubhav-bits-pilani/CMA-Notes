Subtopic note

Estimating Maintenance vs Growth Capex

Scope: Maintenance vs growth capex estimation: depreciation proxy, management guidance, capacity additions, per-store capex

Why it matters

Free cash flow available to owners in a steady state is CFO minus the capex needed merely to sustain current earnings — maintenance capex. Growth capex is a discretionary investment that should be valued by its own returns. Ind AS does not require this split, and Indian companies rarely disclose it in the financial statements; it comes from investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, capacity data and unit economics. How you estimate it drives sustainable FCF yield, the terminal-year reinvestment assumption in a DCF, and judgement calls such as whether a company "could" pay higher dividends. In FP&A, the same split governs capital budgets: maintenance capex is usually pre-approved, while growth capex competes for capital on NPV/IRR (Week 21).

Core notes

Definitions

  • Maintenance (sustaining) capex: expenditure required to keep existing capacity, service levels and competitive position — replacement of machinery, store refurbishments, IT refresh, statutory/safety upgrades.
  • Growth (expansion) capex: expenditure that adds capacity, new stores, new plants, new products or geographies, or step-changes efficiency beyond the current run rate.
  • Total capex in the cash flow statement = purchase of PPE and intangibles (including CWIP and capital advances), net of disposal proceeds if you choose a "net capex" definition.

Method 1: depreciation proxy

\[ \text{Maintenance capex} \approx \text{D\&A (excluding ROU depreciation and acquired-intangible amortisation)} \]

Logic: depreciation spreads the historical cost of assets that need replacing. Limitations:

  • In an inflationary environment, historical-cost depreciation understates replacement cost (Indian construction and equipment costs have risen faster than the useful lives imply).
  • Long-lived asset bases (cement, power) depreciate over 25–40 years while technology-heavy assets need faster replacement — depreciation can over- or understate.
  • Exclude ROU depreciation (lease cost, handled via lease payments) and amortisation of acquired brands or customer relationships (no replacement spend).
  • Companies that changed useful lives (Schedule II permits component accounting and different lives with justification) create a break in the proxy.

Method 2: management guidance

Many Indian companies give a guidance split in calls ("of the ₹1,000 crore capex, about ₹150 crore is maintenance"). Use it, but test it: maintenance guidance should be consistent with depreciation trends and with capex observed in years when no expansion was undertaken. Management incentives favour labelling more capex as growth.

Method 3: capacity additions

For manufacturers, reconcile capex to capacity:

\[ \text{Growth capex} \approx \Delta\text{Capacity} \times \text{Capex per unit of capacity} \]

\[ \text{Maintenance capex} = \text{Total capex} - \text{Growth capex} \]

Example: a cement company added 4 MTPA at an industry replacement cost of about ₹700–800 crore per MTPA (brownfield lower than greenfield). Total capex of ₹3,500 crore with 4 MTPA added at ₹750 crore/MTPA implies growth capex of ₹3,000 crore and maintenance of ₹500 crore. Cross-check against tonnes produced: maintenance of ₹150–250 per tonne is a common sector rule of thumb — state such benchmarks as estimates, not facts.

Method 4: per-store capex (retail)

For Titan, Trent, DMart, QSR chains:

\[ \text{Growth capex} = \text{New stores (or new sq. ft.)} \times \text{Capex per store (or per sq. ft.)} \]

\[ \text{Maintenance capex} = \text{Store base} \times \text{Refurbishment cost} \div \text{Refurbishment cycle} \]

Illustration (₹ crore): Trent-like retailer opens 120 stores at ₹12 crore fit-out each (leased premises), refurbishes existing 900 stores on a 7-year cycle at ₹3.5 crore each:

Component Calculation Amount
Growth capex 120 × 12 1,440
Maintenance capex 900 × 3.5 ÷ 7 450
Total implied capex 1,890
Reported capex 1,960
Unexplained (IT, warehouses, CWIP) 70

Owned-store models (DMart buys land and buildings) have much higher growth capex per store and lower lease payments; compare per sq. ft. capex including the capitalised value of leases to make them comparable.

Triangulation and capex intensity

Use at least two methods and present a range. Capex intensity (\( \text{Capex} \div \text{Revenue} \)) by type helps: maintenance capex intensity is usually stable across years, while growth intensity is lumpy. A maintenance estimate that swings with the expansion cycle is probably contaminated by growth spend.

Using the split

\[ \text{Sustainable FCF} = \text{CFO (lease-adjusted)} - \text{Maintenance capex} \]

\[ \text{Growth FCF} = \text{Sustainable FCF} - \text{Growth capex} \]

In the DCF terminal year, reinvestment should be linked to growth via \( \text{Reinvestment rate} = g \div \text{ROIC} \), not set equal to maintenance capex alone (Week 15).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capex that is both: replacing an old machine with a larger, more efficient one — split by the capacity increment.
  • Regulatory capex (pollution control, BIS norms, EV transition) is maintenance in substance: it adds no revenue but is necessary to continue operating.
  • Capitalised software and R&D (Ind AS 38 development costs): treat recurring platform spend as maintenance; a new platform build may be growth.
  • Store closures and relocations: relocation capex replaces lost capacity and is closer to maintenance.
  • Acquisitions are not capex in the cash flow statement (they are separate investing lines) but are growth investment economically; see s06.
  • Underinvestment: a company running maintenance capex below depreciation for years flatters FCF; watch asset age (gross block ÷ annual depreciation, accumulated depreciation ÷ gross block).

Key takeaways

  • Maintenance capex sustains current earnings; growth capex buys new earnings and should be judged on returns.
  • Depreciation is a quick but biased proxy — adjust for inflation, asset lives, ROU and acquired intangibles.
  • Management guidance must be tested against depreciation and no-expansion years.
  • Capacity-based and per-store unit economics give the most defensible estimates for manufacturers and retailers.
  • Present a range from at least two methods and link terminal reinvestment to growth and ROIC.

Practice Questions

Q1. Per-store split [L2]

A jewellery retailer had 600 stores at the start of FY2025 and 680 at the end. New-store capex (fit-out, excluding inventory) is ₹6 crore per store; stores are refurbished every 6 years at ₹1.8 crore each. Reported capex was ₹690 crore, D&A excluding ROU ₹210 crore. Estimate maintenance and growth capex under the depreciation proxy and the per-store method, and comment on the gap.

💡 Hint: Use the opening store base for the refurbishment estimate and the net store additions for growth.

Q2. Capacity-based estimate [L2]

A cement company's capacity rose from 32 MTPA to 38 MTPA during FY2025 through brownfield expansion costing an estimated ₹650 crore per MTPA. Reported capex was ₹4,600 crore including ₹300 crore of WHRS (waste heat recovery) and ₹200 crore of land for a future plant. D&A was ₹1,100 crore. Estimate maintenance capex using two methods and explain how you would classify WHRS and land.

Q3. Testing management guidance [L3]

Management of an FMCG company guides that maintenance capex is "about 1% of revenue". Over FY2021–FY2025 revenue grew from ₹12,000 crore to ₹18,000 crore; capex was ₹350, ₹420, ₹900, ₹1,100 and ₹500 crore; D&A excluding ROU was ₹280, ₹300, ₹330, ₹380 and ₹430 crore. Assess the credibility of the guidance and state what additional data you would request.

Q4. Interview: why it matters for valuation [L1]

Explain to a portfolio manager in under 150 words why splitting maintenance and growth capex changes the FCF yield and the terminal value of a fast-growing retailer.
