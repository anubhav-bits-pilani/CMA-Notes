Subtopic note

The CFO-to-EBITDA Bridge

Scope: CFO-to-EBITDA bridge: EBITDA − ΔNWC − taxes paid ± provisions, other non-cash items = CFO

Why it matters

EBITDA is the metric managements guide on, lenders write covenants on and multiples are quoted on. CFO is the cash the business actually produced. The bridge between them is where earnings quality is tested: a company whose EBITDA grows 20% a year while CFO stagnates is either investing in working capital, recognising revenue ahead of cash, capitalising costs or classifying items aggressively. The bridge is a standard exhibit in quality of earnings (QoE) reports, credit appraisal notes and equity research initiations, and it is a required deliverable in this week's Titan vs Trent exercise. It also enforces the week's cross-cutting rule that P&L normalisation must reconcile to cash.

Core notes

The bridge identity

Starting from EBITDA instead of PBT, the indirect method becomes:

\[ \text{CFO} = \text{EBITDA} - \Delta\text{NWC} - \text{Taxes paid} \pm \Delta\text{Provisions} \pm \text{Other non-cash items} \pm \text{Other operating items} \]

where:

  • \( \text{EBITDA} \) is reported EBITDA under your documented definition (typically PBT + finance cost + D&A − other income, or before exceptional items).
  • \( \Delta\text{NWC} \) is the increase in operating net working capital (an increase reduces CFO).
  • \( \text{Taxes paid} \) is income tax paid net of refunds, from the cash flow statement.
  • \( \Delta\text{Provisions} \) captures provisions charged to EBITDA but not yet paid (warranty, gratuity, leave encashment, onerous contracts) — an increase adds to CFO.
  • \( \text{Other non-cash items} \) include ESOP cost, ECL allowance, inventory write-downs, unrealised forex within EBITDA.
  • \( \text{Other operating items} \) include operating cash items that sit outside EBITDA: e.g. cash exceptional items (VRS paid), other income received in cash if classified operating.

Each residual must be explained: other income included in CFO, interest received on operating deposits, reclassifications between sections, or acquisition effects.

Why EBITDA and CFO differ in Indian statements

Because Ind AS 7 puts interest paid in financing and Ind AS 116 removes rent from EBITDA and from CFO, a post-116 EBITDA and an Ind AS CFO are on the same "pre-interest, pre-lease" basis. The bridge therefore does not need an interest line. If you use rent-adjusted EBITDA, you must use lease-adjusted CFO (deducting principal and interest) to keep the bridge coherent.

The story: EBITDA grew 19% CAGR but CFO grew 10% CAGR because working capital absorbed a rising share of EBITDA. The analyst's next step is to decompose ΔNWC into receivable days, inventory days and payable days (see t3 and Week 6 efficiency ratios).

Building it in Excel / Power BI

Use a long-format table with columns Company, FY, Bridge_Line, Amount, Source_Note so that the same bridge renders for Titan and Trent and multiple years. In Excel, with named input cells SelCompany and SelFY, a check formula:

=SUMIFS(Bridge[Amount],Bridge[Company],SelCompany,Bridge[FY],SelFY) - XLOOKUP(1,(CF[Company]=SelCompany)*(CF[FY]=SelFY)*(CF[Line]="Net cash from operating activities"),CF[Amount])

In Power BI, a waterfall visual on the bridge table with a DAX measure:

Bridge Amount = SUM ( Bridge[Amount] )

sliced by company and FY gives the standard investor-presentation waterfall.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Definition of EBITDA: if your EBITDA excludes other income but some other income is operating cash in CFO (e.g. scrap sales classified as other income, export incentives), you will get a residual; reclassify consistently.
  • Customer advance schemes: jewellers' customer schemes and gold-on-lease arrangements can make ΔNWC positive. Show them as a separate bridge line so readers can judge sustainability.
  • Tax refunds and disputed tax deposits: payments under protest are often shown in taxes paid; a refund wave from settled litigation inflates CFO in one year.
  • Acquired working capital: in consolidated statements, the NWC of acquired subsidiaries is in investing; a balance-sheet-derived ΔNWC will overstate the outflow.
  • Factoring and supply chain finance: ΔNWC can be engineered by derecognising receivables or stretching payables (see s07); show the gross position as a memo line.
  • Single-year noise: festive-season timing (Diwali falling in October vs November) shifts receivables and inventory across the 31 March or 30 September cut-off; prefer cumulative multi-year ratios.

Key takeaways

  • CFO = EBITDA − ΔNWC − taxes paid ± provision movements ± other non-cash and operating items; any residual must be explained.
  • Post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA pairs with Ind AS CFO; rent-adjusted EBITDA pairs with lease-adjusted CFO.
  • The bridge separates growth-driven working-capital investment from earnings-quality problems.
  • Build it in long format so it scales across companies, years and visuals.
  • Read cumulative 3–5 year conversion, not a single year.

List the hypotheses you would test, the notes you would read, and the bridge lines that would confirm or reject each hypothesis.
