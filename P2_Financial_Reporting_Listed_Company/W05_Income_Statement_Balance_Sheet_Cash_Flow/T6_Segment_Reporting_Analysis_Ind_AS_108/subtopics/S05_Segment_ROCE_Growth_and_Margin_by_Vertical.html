Subtopic note

Segment ROCE, Growth and Margin by Vertical

Scope: Segment ROCE = Segment result ÷ (Segment assets − Segment liabilities); growth and margin by vertical

Why it matters

Consolidated ROCE averages businesses with very different economics. A group whose jewellery business earns a high ROCE on large inventories and whose eyecare business earns a low ROCE on store capex will see its overall return move with mix alone. Segment ROCE, growth and margin by vertical tell you where value is being created, which businesses deserve capital, and which a sum-of-the-parts valuation should rate highly. Fund managers often ask "what is the ROCE of the core business?" — the answer comes from segment data, adjusted carefully.

Core notes

Definition

\[ \text{Segment ROCE} = \frac{\text{Segment result}}{\text{Segment assets} - \text{Segment liabilities}} \]

where segment result is the CODM profit measure (usually EBIT before unallocable items and finance cost in Indian filings), and segment assets minus segment liabilities is segment capital employed. Prefer average capital employed (opening + closing ÷ 2) for growing segments:

\[ \text{Segment ROCE}_t = \frac{\text{Result}_t}{\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{CE}_{t-1} + \text{CE}_t)} \]

A post-tax version (segment ROIC) applies the marginal rate (25.168%) to the result: \( \text{Result} \times (1 - t) \).

DuPont decomposition by segment

\[ \text{Segment ROCE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Result}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Capital employed}}}_{\text{capital turnover}} \]

Jewellery retail typically has thin margins but high turnover only if inventory is funded by gold metal loans and customer schemes (which sit in liabilities); watches have higher margins but more fixed assets per rupee of sales. Decomposition shows why returns differ.

Growth and margin by vertical

Metric Formula Use
External revenue growth \( \text{Rev}_t \div \text{Rev}_{t-1} - 1 \) or CAGR (t1 c03) Market share, demand
Result margin \( \text{Result} \div \text{Revenue} \) Profitability trend
Incremental margin \( \Delta\text{Result} \div \Delta\text{Revenue} \) Operating leverage
Contribution to group growth \( \Delta\text{Rev}_{\text{seg}} \div \Delta\text{Rev}_{\text{group}} \) Growth source
Incremental ROCE \( \Delta\text{Result} \div \Delta\text{CE} \) Returns on new capital
Capex/D&A Segment capex ÷ segment D&A Investment phase

Consistency adjustments before comparing

  1. Leases: if ROU assets are in segment assets but lease liabilities are unallocable, either deduct lease liabilities from segment capital employed (and add back lease interest only if it is not in the result) or remove ROU assets and use a rent-adjusted result.
  2. Gold metal loans and customer advances (jewellers): if treated as segment liabilities they reduce capital employed and boost ROCE; if they are debt-like (t3), add them back.
  3. Goodwill: acquisitive segments carry goodwill; report ROCE with and without it.
  4. Corporate costs and unallocable assets: fully loaded ROCE allocates both (s03).
  5. Restatements: re-pull prior years if segment definitions changed (topic pitfalls).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative capital employed (segments with large customer advances) gives meaningless ROCE; use result ÷ revenue or result per store instead.
  • Start-up segments have negative ROCE by design; evaluate on unit economics and cohort maturity.
  • Seasonal segments: year-end capital employed may be unrepresentative (inventory build before festive season); use averages of half-yearly balance sheets where segment assets are disclosed in Reg 33 (s06).
  • Inter-segment pricing shifts profit between segments; adjust if a captive segment is priced above market.
  • Segment result after allocated exceptional items: normalise first.
  • Currency effects on international segments: compute constant-currency growth where possible.

Key takeaways

  • Segment ROCE = segment result ÷ (segment assets − segment liabilities), preferably on average capital employed.
  • Decompose into margin and capital turnover to understand why segments differ.
  • Growth, incremental margin, contribution to group growth and incremental ROCE together show where value is created.
  • Make leases, debt-like liabilities, goodwill and corporate costs consistent before comparing segments or peers.
  • Negative capital employed and start-up segments need alternative metrics.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute segment ROCE [L1]

A segment reports result ₹640 crore, assets ₹4,100 crore (opening ₹3,700 crore) and liabilities ₹1,300 crore (opening ₹1,100 crore), revenue ₹5,600 crore. Compute ROCE on closing and average capital employed and decompose it into margin and turnover.

Q2. Lease consistency [L2]

A retail segment reports result ₹900 crore (after ROU depreciation ₹260 crore, before lease interest ₹140 crore, which is in finance cost), segment assets ₹6,200 crore including ROU assets ₹2,100 crore, segment liabilities ₹1,500 crore (lease liabilities ₹2,300 crore are unallocable). Compute ROCE on (a) reported basis, (b) leases as debt basis with lease liabilities deducted from capital employed and lease interest deducted from the result, and (c) rent-adjusted basis using rent of ₹420 crore.

💡 Hint: In each basis, the numerator and denominator must treat leases the same way.

Q3. Incremental analysis [L2]

Segment X: revenue ₹2,000 → ₹2,900 crore, result ₹300 → ₹350 crore, capital employed ₹1,200 → ₹2,100 crore over two years. Segment Y: revenue ₹1,500 → ₹1,700 crore, result ₹180 → ₹240 crore, capital employed ₹900 → ₹950 crore. Compute growth, incremental margin and incremental ROCE, and advise where the next ₹500 crore of capex should go.

Q4. Case: jewellery segment ROCE [L3]

A listed jeweller's segment liabilities include gold metal loans of ₹4,500 crore and customer scheme advances of ₹2,800 crore, giving segment ROCE of 48%. A peer funds inventory with bank borrowings (unallocable). Recompute a comparable ROCE given segment result ₹4,000 crore and segment capital employed ₹8,400 crore, and discuss which treatment is more appropriate for valuation.
