Subtopic note

Entity-Wide Disclosures: Products, Geography and Major Customers

Scope: Entity-wide disclosures: revenue by product/service, by geography (India vs outside India), major customers

Why it matters

Segment data follows the CODM's structure, which can be coarse — many Indian companies report a single segment. Ind AS 108.31–34 therefore require entity-wide disclosures even from single-segment entities: revenue by products and services, revenue and non-current assets by geography, and reliance on major customers. These give the analyst product mix, export dependence, currency exposure, concentration risk and a cross-check on segment data. For Indian companies, the India vs outside-India split also matters for tax, transfer pricing, exchange-rate sensitivity and the "China plus one" or export-incentive narratives common in chemicals, pharma, auto components and electronics manufacturing.

Core notes

Scope (Ind AS 108.31)

Entity-wide disclosures apply to all entities within the scope of Ind AS 108, including those with a single reportable segment. Information already provided in the segment disclosures need not be repeated. Amounts are based on the financial information used to produce the entity's financial statements (i.e. Ind AS measures, not CODM measures).

Products and services (Ind AS 108.32)

Revenue from external customers for each product and service, or each group of similar products and services, unless the necessary information is not available and the cost to develop it would be excessive (then that fact is disclosed). Complements Ind AS 115.114 disaggregation of revenue, which requires disaggregation into categories depicting how economic factors affect revenue and cash flows — by type of good, geography, market, contract type, timing of transfer (point in time vs over time) and sales channel.

Analytical use: product mix shifts explain margin changes (t1 margin compression analysis), and the revenue disaggregation note often shows more detail than the segment note.

Geography (Ind AS 108.33)

Unless information is unavailable and too costly to develop:

  • (a) revenue from external customers attributed to the entity's country of domicile (India) and to all foreign countries in total; if revenue from an individual foreign country is material, disclose separately; and the basis for attributing revenue to countries (customer location, shipping destination, entity location);
  • (b) non-current assets other than financial instruments, deferred tax assets, post-employment benefit assets and rights under insurance contracts, located in India and in all foreign countries in total, with material individual countries separately.

Indian annual reports commonly present "Within India" and "Outside India" columns; Reg 33 segment formats may present geographical segments when the CODM manages by geography (e.g. IT services companies by North America, Europe, India, rest of world).

Analytical uses:

  • Export share drives sensitivity to USD/INR and to export incentives (RoDTEP, PLI — see t2 s05).
  • Non-current assets outside India indicate where capacity and acquisitions sit — relevant for consolidation (t5) and repatriation risk.
  • Revenue attributed by customer location vs billing entity differ — read the basis.

Major customers (Ind AS 108.34, see c03)

If revenue from a single external customer amounts to 10% or more of entity revenue, disclose that fact, the total revenue from each such customer, and the segment(s) reporting it. The identity of the customer need not be disclosed. A group of entities under common control is treated as a single customer; a government and entities known to be under its control are treated as a single customer, with judgement on the extent of economic integration (Ind AS 108.34).

A specialty chemicals company (single reportable segment):

of which USA 1,400 1,100
Non-current assets — India 3,800 4,400
Non-current assets — outside India 200 210

Observations: export share fell from 58% to 54%, driven by the US; the major customer's revenue fell 26%, likely explaining most of the US decline; capacity remains in India (export-oriented). An analyst would model US demand and customer concentration explicitly and check whether the customer contract is take-or-pay.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • "Not available and excessive cost" exemption is rarely credible for listed companies with ERPs; challenge it.
  • Attribution basis changes (e.g. from billing entity to customer location) break the trend; restate.
  • Group customers: sales to several subsidiaries of one multinational must be aggregated for the 10% test.
  • Government as customer: PSU customers (railways, defence, oil marketing companies) — whether each is treated separately depends on economic integration; defence and EPC companies often disclose "Government of India" concentration.
  • Ind AS 115 vs Ind AS 108 geography: disaggregation may use a different basis; reconcile.
  • Gross vs net revenue: for principal vs agent judgements (Ind AS 115.B34–B38), entity-wide revenue follows the Ind AS 115 conclusion — a change from gross to net reporting shrinks revenue without economic change.

Key takeaways

  • Entity-wide disclosures apply even to single-segment entities and use Ind AS (not CODM) measures.
  • Products/services revenue complements the Ind AS 115 disaggregation note for mix analysis.
  • Geography requires India vs outside India revenue and non-current assets, with material countries separately.
  • Major-customer disclosure flags concentration at 10% of revenue; customer identity is not required.
  • Check attribution bases and their changes before building trends.

Practice Questions

Q1. Which disclosures apply? [L1]

A single-segment pharma formulations company exports 70% of revenue (US 45%, Europe 15%, others 10%) and has manufacturing in India and one US plant. Its largest distributor accounts for 14% of revenue. List each entity-wide disclosure required under Ind AS 108.32–34 and what it would show.

Q2. Concentration analysis [L2]

A contract manufacturer reports revenue of ₹5,600 crore. Three subsidiaries of one global brand owner buy ₹380 crore, ₹290 crore and ₹160 crore; an Indian PSU oil marketing company buys ₹420 crore and another PSU OMC ₹300 crore. Determine which customers must be disclosed under Ind AS 108.34 and discuss the PSU judgement.

💡 Hint: Aggregate entities under common control before applying the 10% test.

Q3. Export sensitivity [L2]

Using the illustration data, estimate the impact on FY2025 revenue in ₹ of a 3% INR appreciation against the USD, assuming all outside-India revenue is USD-denominated, and explain how hedging disclosures under Ind AS 107 would refine the estimate.

Q4. Case: mix shift [L3]

A consumer company's Ind AS 115 disaggregation shows revenue by product: Premium ₹2,000 → ₹2,900 crore, Mass ₹5,500 → ₹5,400 crore, Exports ₹900 → ₹600 crore over two years, while consolidated EBITDA margin rose from 14% to 16%. Assuming premium margins of 24%, mass 11% and exports 9% (constant), decompose the margin change into mix and rate effects and draw conclusions.
