Subtopic note

Segment Reconciliations and Unallocable Items

Scope: Reconciliations of segment totals to entity totals; unallocable corporate items and treasury income

Why it matters

Segment figures are measured on the CODM's basis, so they rarely add up to reported consolidated numbers. The reconciliation required by Ind AS 108.28 is where the gap is explained — and where a lot of analytical value hides. Large "unallocable expenses" can make every segment look more profitable than the company is; "unallocable income" often contains treasury income from surplus cash, which should not be credited to operating segments; unallocable assets often include cash and investments that inflate capital employed if wrongly spread across segments. A clean segment analysis starts by mapping each reconciling line.

Core notes

Required reconciliations (Ind AS 108.28)

An entity reconciles:

  • (a) total reportable segments' revenues to the entity's revenue;
  • (b) total reportable segments' measure of profit or loss to the entity's profit before tax and discontinued operations (or after tax if taxes are allocated);
  • (c) total reportable segments' assets to entity assets (if segment assets are reported);
  • (d) total segment liabilities to entity liabilities (if reported);
  • (e) totals of every other material item disclosed to the corresponding entity amount.

All material reconciling items are separately identified and described — e.g. the effect of different accounting policies between segment and entity measures.

Typical Indian reconciliation structure (P&L)

Line Content
Total segment results Sum of reportable segment results (+ "others")
Less: Finance cost Interest on borrowings, lease interest (if not in segments)
Less: Unallocable expenditure Corporate office, brand building not allocated, CSR, ESOP cost, corporate IT
Add: Unallocable income Interest on deposits, dividend, MF gains (treasury income), rental income, profit on sale of investments
Less/Add: Exceptional items If not allocated to segments
Profit before tax Agrees with P&L

Typical Indian reconciliation structure (balance sheet)

Segment assets Unallocable assets Total assets
PPE, ROU, inventory, receivables of segments Cash and bank, current and non-current investments, tax assets, DTA, corporate PPE, loans to group entities Agrees with balance sheet
Segment liabilities Unallocable liabilities Total liabilities
Trade payables, contract liabilities, operating provisions Borrowings, lease liabilities (often), tax liabilities, DTL, corporate provisions Agrees with balance sheet

Analytical treatment of reconciling items

  1. Treasury income (interest, dividends, FVTPL gains): never allocate to segments; exclude from operating EBIT entirely and value the corresponding surplus cash separately (consistent with t2 and t3).
  2. Unallocable corporate costs: these are real operating costs. For segment comparison with peers, either allocate them (by revenue, capital employed or headcount) to arrive at fully loaded segment margins, or analyse segments "before corporate costs" and capitalise corporate costs separately in SOTP (as a negative value, e.g. at a multiple of after-tax holdco costs).
  3. Unallocable assets: separate operating ones (corporate office, shared IT) from financial ones (cash, investments). Only the former belong in total operating capital employed.
  4. Inter-segment eliminations in revenue and the corresponding unrealised profit adjustments.
  5. Measurement differences: e.g. CODM reviews segments on a pre-Ind AS 116 basis or before ECL — the reconciliation shows the Ind AS adjustment line.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Item FY2025
Segment results: A 1,800; B 650; C (90) 2,360
Less finance cost (240)
Less unallocable expenditure (310)
Add unallocable income (of which treasury ₹170 crore) 210
PBT 2,020

Segment result margin overall (on revenue ₹18,000 crore) = 2,360 ÷ 18,000 = 13.1%. Fully loaded operating margin = (2,360 − 310 + 40 non-treasury unallocable income) ÷ 18,000 = 11.6%. The 1.5 percentage-point gap is the cost of the corporate centre. If unallocable assets of ₹4,100 crore include ₹3,300 crore of cash and investments, only ₹800 crore belongs in operating capital employed.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Changing the unallocable perimeter: a company that moves costs from segments to unallocable improves reported segment margins without any change in the business — compare unallocable expenditure as a % of revenue over time.
  • Negative unallocable expenditure (net of income) can indicate treasury income netted against costs; split it.
  • Exceptional items in segments — some companies include impairments in segment results; normalise before comparing years.
  • Lease liabilities in unallocable while ROU assets sit in segment assets creates an asymmetric capital employed; for consistency, deduct lease liabilities or exclude ROU assets (and use rent-adjusted results).
  • Discontinued operations: excluded from segments; reconciliation is to PBT from continuing operations.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 108.28 requires reconciliation of segment revenue, profit, assets, liabilities and other material items to entity totals, with reconciling items described.
  • Treasury income in unallocable income is non-operating and must not be credited to segments.
  • Unallocable corporate costs are real; analyse segments both before and after them.
  • Unallocable assets mix operating corporate assets with cash and investments; split them before computing ROCE.
  • Track the unallocable perimeter over time to detect cosmetic segment margin improvement.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the reconciliation [L1]

Segment results (₹ crore): Consumer 1,200; Industrial 480; Others (60). Finance cost 150; unallocable expenses 210; interest on deposits 95; dividend from mutual funds 15; profit on sale of corporate land 70 (exceptional, not allocated). Prepare the reconciliation to PBT and compute PBT.

Q2. Fully loaded margins [L2]

Using Q1 data and segment revenues Consumer ₹8,000 crore, Industrial ₹4,800 crore, Others ₹700 crore, allocate unallocable expenses by revenue and compute fully loaded segment margins. Then repeat using segment capital employed of ₹3,000 crore, ₹4,200 crore and ₹300 crore as the allocation key, and comment on the sensitivity.

💡 Hint: Treasury income and the exceptional gain stay out of segment margins under both keys.

Q3. Capital employed from the reconciliation [L2]

Total assets ₹22,000 crore: segment assets ₹15,500 crore; unallocable assets ₹6,500 crore comprising cash ₹1,200 crore, investments ₹3,800 crore, tax assets ₹600 crore, corporate PPE ₹900 crore. Total liabilities (excluding equity) ₹9,000 crore: segment liabilities ₹4,200 crore; unallocable ₹4,800 crore comprising borrowings ₹3,000 crore, lease liabilities ₹1,100 crore, tax liabilities ₹700 crore. Compute total operating capital employed and explain the treatment of each unallocable item.

Q4. Judgement: rising unallocable costs [L3]

Over four years a company's segment result margin rose from 14% to 17% while consolidated EBIT margin stayed at 12%. Unallocable expenditure rose from 1.5% to 4.8% of revenue. Outline the questions for management, the disclosures to check, and how you would present segment trends to an investment committee.
