Subtopic note

Segment Revenue, Result, Assets, Liabilities, Capex and D&A

Scope: Segment revenue (external vs inter-segment), segment result, segment assets and liabilities, capex and D&A

Why it matters

Once segments are identified, the analyst works with the numbers disclosed for each: revenue, result (profit), assets, liabilities, capex and depreciation. These are the raw inputs for segment growth, margin, asset turnover, ROCE and capex intensity — and for building segment-level drivers in an operating model. Each item has its own definitional traps: inter-segment revenue inflates gross totals, "segment result" in India is often EBIT before unallocable items rather than EBITDA, and segment assets may exclude large shared assets. Reading the definitions correctly is the difference between a meaningful segment ROCE and a misleading one.

Core notes

What must be disclosed (Ind AS 108.21–24)

For each reportable segment, an entity reports a measure of profit or loss (Ind AS 108.23), and a measure of total assets and liabilities if such amounts are regularly provided to the CODM. The following are disclosed if included in the profit measure reviewed by the CODM or otherwise regularly provided:

Item (Ind AS 108.23) Item (Ind AS 108.24, assets-related)
(a) revenue from external customers (a) investment in associates and JVs accounted by equity method
(b) revenue from transactions with other operating segments (b) additions to non-current assets (other than financial instruments, deferred tax assets, net defined benefit assets and rights under insurance contracts) — i.e. capex
(c) interest revenue
(d) interest expense
(e) depreciation and amortisation
(f) material items of income and expense disclosed under Ind AS 1.97
(g) share of profit of associates and JVs
(h) income tax expense or income
(i) material non-cash items other than D&A

Interest revenue and expense are reported separately unless most of the segment's revenues are from interest and the CODM relies primarily on net interest revenue (Ind AS 108.23, final paragraph).

External vs inter-segment revenue

  • External revenue is sales to customers outside the entity — sums to consolidated revenue from operations (subject to reconciling items).
  • Inter-segment revenue is sales between segments (e.g. a captive packaging segment selling to the FMCG segment), usually at transfer prices set internally; the basis of accounting for inter-segment transactions must be disclosed (Ind AS 108.27(a)).

Analytical rule: compute growth and market share on external revenue; compute segment margins on total segment revenue only if the result includes the inter-segment margin. Inter-segment pricing above market inflates the supplying segment's margin and depresses the buyer's.

Segment result

Ind AS 108 does not define the profit measure — it is whatever the CODM uses. In Indian practice (and the SEBI Reg 33 format, s06), "segment results" are typically profit before interest and tax, before unallocable corporate expenses and unallocable income, i.e. close to segment EBIT. Some companies report EBITDA or contribution. Always read the basis note (Ind AS 108.27) and check whether:

  • D&A is included (EBIT) or excluded (EBITDA);
  • Ind AS 116 lease depreciation and interest are in the segment result (lease interest often sits in finance cost, below segment results);
  • exceptional items are allocated;
  • corporate costs (brand, IT, head office) are allocated, partly allocated or held as unallocable.

Segment assets and liabilities

Segment assets typically include PPE, ROU assets, intangibles, goodwill, inventories, receivables and other operating assets employed by the segment. Segment liabilities typically include trade payables, contract liabilities, provisions and other operating liabilities. Excluded (usually unallocable): cash and bank, investments, tax assets and liabilities, borrowings, and often lease liabilities (as financing). The difference makes segment capital employed close to an operating invested capital measure (t3).

Capex and D&A

Additions to non-current assets by segment (Ind AS 108.24(b)) and D&A by segment (Ind AS 108.23(e)) allow segment-level capex intensity and maintenance capex estimates (t4). Watch whether ROU asset additions are included in "additions to non-current assets" — some companies include them, which inflates capex for retail-heavy segments.

Excel/Power BI layout

Store segment data in long format: Company, FY, Segment, Metric, Amount, Basis_Note. A measure such as

Segment ROCE = DIVIDE ( [Segment Result], [Segment Assets] - [Segment Liabilities] )

in Power BI allows slicing by segment and year; flag years with restated definitions.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Segment assets not reviewed by CODM: since Ind AS 108 requires assets/liabilities only if regularly provided, some companies disclose none — segment ROCE is then impossible; use proxies (e.g. capex and D&A).
  • Shared assets: a common manufacturing plant may be allocated on an arbitrary key. Amounts allocated must be allocated on a reasonable basis (Ind AS 108.25), and asymmetrical allocations — e.g. depreciation charged to a segment without the related assets being allocated to it — are permitted but must be disclosed (Ind AS 108.27(f)). Treat such segments' ROCE with caution.
  • Goodwill allocation: goodwill from acquisitions is allocated to segments for impairment testing (Ind AS 36); segment assets may or may not include it — check.
  • Inter-segment eliminations for revenue must be shown in the reconciliation (s03).
  • Tax by segment is rarely disclosed in India; use the marginal rate for segment NOPAT.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 108.23–24 list the per-segment items disclosed when reviewed by the CODM: revenue split, interest, D&A, material items, share of associates, tax, non-cash items, capex and equity-accounted investments.
  • Use external revenue for growth and market share; inter-segment pricing can distort margins.
  • Segment result in Indian filings is usually EBIT before unallocable items — read the basis note.
  • Segment assets and liabilities typically exclude cash, investments, tax and borrowings, approximating operating capital employed.
  • Segment capex and D&A support capex intensity and maintenance capex analysis by vertical.

Practice Questions

Q1. External vs inter-segment [L1]

A company reports segment revenue: Chemicals ₹3,400 crore (including ₹600 crore sold to the Paints segment), Paints ₹5,200 crore (all external). Compute external revenue, total segment revenue and the inter-segment elimination, and state which figure you would use for each segment's growth rate.

Q2. Segment metrics [L2]

Segment data (₹ crore): revenue 6,000; segment result 720 (after D&A 210); segment assets 5,400 (including goodwill 1,200 and ROU assets 600); segment liabilities 1,300 (excluding lease liabilities of 650); capex 480 (including ROU additions 150). Compute segment EBIT margin, EBITDA margin, ROCE (with and without goodwill) and capex intensity with and without ROU additions.

💡 Hint: Decide consistently whether lease assets and liabilities are both in or both out of capital employed.

Q3. Reading the basis note [L2]

A company's segment note says: "Segment results are profit before finance cost, exceptional items, unallocable expenditure (net of unallocable income) and tax. Lease liabilities and related interest are unallocable." List the adjustments needed to compare its segment results with a peer whose segment result is EBITDA after allocated corporate costs.

Q4. Model-build: segment driver tab [L3]

Design an Excel tab that converts five years of disclosed segment data for Titan into drivers for a forecast (external revenue growth, result margin, capital turnover, capex/D&A by segment), including checks to the segment reconciliation and flags for restated years.
