Subtopic note

Identifying Operating and Reportable Segments

Scope: Identifying operating segments via the CODM's internal reporting; aggregation criteria and quantitative thresholds

Why it matters

Segment disclosures are the only audited, standardised view of a diversified company's business lines. Titan's jewellery, watches and eyecare businesses have very different margins, capital intensity and growth; a single consolidated margin hides all of that. How a company defines its segments determines what the analyst can see: a company that aggregates two dissimilar businesses into one reportable segment, or labels a loss-making unit as "Others", can mask problems. Understanding how segments are identified — and how much discretion exists — tells you how much to trust segment data and what questions to ask management. In FP&A, the same "management approach" underpins how internal MIS is structured (Week 20).

Core notes

The management approach

Ind AS 108 adopts the "through the eyes of management" approach: segments are defined by how the chief operating decision maker (CODM) reviews the business, not by an external template. The core principle (Ind AS 108.1) is to disclose information enabling users to evaluate the nature and financial effects of business activities and the economic environments in which the entity operates.

Chief operating decision maker (Ind AS 108.7): a function, not a title — the person or group that allocates resources to and assesses the performance of operating segments. In Indian companies it is commonly the MD & CEO, the executive committee or the board. Identification matters: the CODM's reporting package defines segments and, under Ind AS 108.25, the measures disclosed.

Operating segment definition (Ind AS 108.5)

A component of an entity:

  1. that engages in business activities from which it may earn revenues and incur expenses (including transactions with other components of the same entity);
  2. whose operating results are regularly reviewed by the CODM to make decisions about resource allocation and assess performance; and
  3. for which discrete financial information is available.

Start-up operations can be operating segments before earning revenue. Corporate headquarters and some functional departments (e.g. treasury, corporate HR) are not operating segments if they earn only incidental revenue (Ind AS 108.6). Post-employment benefit plans are not operating segments.

Where the CODM reviews multiple overlapping sets of information (e.g. by product and by geography — a matrix structure), the entity determines segments by reference to the core principle (Ind AS 108.10); segment managers are another indicator (Ind AS 108.9).

Aggregation criteria (Ind AS 108.12)

Two or more operating segments may be aggregated into a single operating segment if aggregation is consistent with the core principle, the segments have similar economic characteristics (e.g. similar long-term average gross margins) and are similar in each of:

  • (a) the nature of products and services;
  • (b) the nature of production processes;
  • (c) type or class of customer;
  • (d) methods used to distribute products or provide services; and
  • (e) if applicable, the nature of the regulatory environment (e.g. banking, insurance, public utilities).

Ind AS 108.22(aa) requires disclosure of the judgements made in applying the aggregation criteria, including a brief description of the segments aggregated and the economic indicators assessed.

Quantitative thresholds (Ind AS 108.13, see c01)

An operating segment is reportable if any of:

  • reported revenue (external + inter-segment) is 10% or more of combined revenue of all operating segments;
  • absolute reported profit or loss is 10% or more of the greater, in absolute amount, of combined profit of all profit-making segments and combined loss of all loss-making segments;
  • assets are 10% or more of combined assets of all operating segments.

Additional rules:

  • Segments below thresholds may still be reported if management believes the information is useful (Ind AS 108.13).
  • Below-threshold segments may be combined only if they share a majority of the aggregation criteria (Ind AS 108.14).
  • If reportable segments' external revenue is below 75% of entity revenue, add more segments until 75% is reached (Ind AS 108.15, see c02).
  • Remaining business activities are combined in an "all other segments" category, separately from reconciling items (Ind AS 108.16).
  • A segment reportable in the prior year continues to be reported if management judges it of continuing significance (Ind AS 108.17); a newly reportable segment requires restated comparatives unless impracticable (Ind AS 108.18).
  • Practical limit: consider whether more than ten reportable segments become too detailed (Ind AS 108.19).

Illustration (₹ crore, hypothetical figures for a Titan-like group)

Jewellery 46,000 5,200 22,000
Watches & wearables 4,000 420 2,600
Eyecare 800 (30) 600
Fragrances 250 (40) 180

Revenue threshold 5,120; profit threshold 562 (greater of 5,620 and 130); asset threshold 2,568. Jewellery is reportable on all tests; watches qualify on assets (2,600 ≥ 2,568) though not on revenue or profit. Eyecare, fragrances and sarees fall below all tests — they may be combined in "others" or reported voluntarily. External revenue check: jewellery + watches (assuming negligible inter-segment revenue) = 50,000 ÷ 51,200 = 97.7%, above 75%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • CODM receives too much detail: if the CODM reviews store-level or brand-level data, the entity must decide which level is used for resource allocation — regulators challenge overly aggregated segments.
  • Single-segment claims: many Indian companies report "one segment" (e.g. a single product line); test this against investor presentations and the CODM package described in the notes — inconsistency is a red flag.
  • Aggregation of dissimilar margins: aggregating a high-margin and low-margin business conceals mix effects; ESMA and SEC enforcement focuses on this.
  • Reorganisation: a change in internal structure changes segments; comparatives must be restated (Ind AS 108.29) unless impracticable, with disclosure (Ind AS 108.30).
  • Vertically integrated businesses: a captive component supplier can be an operating segment even with mostly inter-segment revenue.

Key takeaways

  • Operating segments follow the CODM's internal reporting; the CODM is a function, not a title.
  • An operating segment needs business activities, regular CODM review and discrete financial information.
  • Aggregation requires similar economic characteristics and similarity in all five listed criteria, with judgements disclosed under para 22(aa).
  • Reportable segments are identified by 10% revenue, profit/loss and asset tests, topped up to cover 75% of external revenue.
  • Changes in segment structure require restated comparatives — check before building trend series.

Practice Questions

Q1. CODM and operating segments [L1]

A diversified Indian company's executive committee (MD, CFO, two business heads) receives monthly P&L by business (cement, paints, chemicals) and by region (North, South, Export). Business heads are accountable for P&L; regional heads for sales only. Identify the CODM and the operating segments, giving reasons.

Q2. Apply quantitative thresholds [L2]

Operating segments (₹ crore): A revenue 7,200, profit 900, assets 6,000; B revenue 1,100, loss (350), assets 900; C revenue 950, profit 60, assets 1,500; D revenue 600, profit 40, assets 300; E revenue 400, loss (20), assets 250. Inter-segment revenue is included only in A (₹500 crore). Determine reportable segments and test the 75% external revenue requirement.

💡 Hint: The profit threshold uses the greater of total profits and total absolute losses, and the 75% test uses external revenue only.

Q3. Aggregation judgement [L3]

A consumer company proposes to aggregate its premium watches business (gross margin 55%, sold through brand stores) with its smart wearables business (gross margin 25%, sold mainly online and imported from contract manufacturers) into one "Watches & Wearables" segment. Evaluate the proposal against Ind AS 108.12 and the disclosure required.

Q4. Interview: why the management approach? [L1]

Explain in under 150 words why Ind AS 108 uses the management approach rather than a standardised segment definition, and one disadvantage for analysts.
