Subtopic note

Reg 33 Quarterly Segment Format and Quarterly-Annual Consistency

Scope: SEBI Reg 33 quarterly results segment format; consistency between quarterly and annual segment data

Why it matters

Annual Ind AS 108 notes come once a year; listed companies' segment data is published every quarter with the Reg 33 results. For tracking business verticals — Titan's jewellery growth in the festive quarter, a conglomerate's chemicals margin through a commodity cycle — the quarterly segment table is the primary dataset. But quarterly segment data has its own format, a limited-review (not audit) assurance level and occasional inconsistencies with the annual note. An analyst building a segment time series must know the format, reconcile quarters to the year, and spot restatements.

Core notes

The Reg 33 segment format

Regulation 33 of SEBI (LODR) Regulations, read with SEBI's prescribed formats (now consolidated in the LODR master circular), requires listed entities that have reportable segments to publish segment information along with the quarterly financial results, in a format broadly as follows (standalone and consolidated):

Section Lines
1. Segment revenue Revenue of each segment; less inter-segment revenue; revenue from operations (agrees with results)
2. Segment results Profit/(loss) before tax and interest from each segment; less finance cost; less other unallocable expenditure net of unallocable income; profit before tax
3. Segment assets Assets of each segment; unallocated assets; total assets
4. Segment liabilities Liabilities of each segment; unallocated liabilities; total liabilities

Columns: current quarter, preceding quarter, corresponding quarter of previous year, year-to-date current and prior year, and previous full year (audited). Segment assets and liabilities are reported at quarter-end, giving quarterly capital employed by segment — more frequent than many other disclosures, and useful for seasonal businesses.

Segment identification follows Ind AS 108 (the entity's reportable segments); companies with a single reportable segment state so and do not present the table.

Assurance and timing

  • Quarterly results (and the segment table) are subject to limited review by the statutory auditor; annual results are audited.
  • Filing within 45 days of quarter-end (60 days for annual), as covered in t5 s07.
  • Q4 figures are derived as balancing figures between audited annual and published nine-month figures — Q4 segment data inherits year-end true-ups.

Consistency checks between quarterly and annual data

  1. Sum of quarters = annual: \( Q1 + Q2 + Q3 + Q4 = \text{FY} \) for revenue and results, by segment, using the quarters as originally published. Since Q4 is a balancing figure, any reclassification made after earlier quarters (or a restatement) is silently absorbed into Q4 — an unusual Q4 swing by segment is the symptom.
  2. Annual results vs annual report note: the Reg 33 annual segment table should match the Ind AS 108 note in the audited financial statements; differences may arise from events after the reporting period or late adjustments.
  3. Restated comparatives: when segments are redefined mid-year (e.g. a business transferred from "Others" to a new segment), earlier quarters in the new filing are restated; your database must replace, not append, old figures.
  4. Assets and liabilities: quarter-end segment assets should roll into the annual note's balance; large Q4 reclassifications between segment and unallocable assets are a flag.
  5. Basis consistency: quarterly results sometimes use different allocation keys for corporate costs than the annual note — read footnotes.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Segment revenue Q1 Q2 Q3 Q4 (derived) Reg 33 annual (sum) Annual report Ind AS 108 note
Jewellery 9,800 10,100 15,300 10,600 45,800 45,800
Watches 950 1,000 1,150 900 4,000 4,050
Others 260 270 330 260 1,120 1,070

Because Q4 is derived, the quarters always sum to the Reg 33 annual figure — so the meaningful test is against the audited annual report note and against the quarters as originally published (a Q1 restated in a later filing will differ from the Q1 in your database). Here the ₹50 crore mismatch between Watches and Others suggests a small business (e.g. wearables accessories) was moved from Others to Watches when the annual report was finalised. The analyst should take the latest restated figures (in the next year's quarterly filings' comparatives) and rebuild the quarterly series on the new basis.

Building a quarterly segment database

Use Power Query to load each quarter's segment table (from NSE/BSE XBRL filings or PDFs) into a long table: Company, Period_End, Basis (Standalone/Consolidated), Segment, Line, Amount, Filing_Date. Keep the latest filing for each period:

= Table.Group(Source, {"Company","Period_End","Basis","Segment","Line"},
    {{"Latest", each Table.Max(_, "Filing_Date"), type record}})

Then expand the record. In Excel, with named input cells SelSegment and AsAtDate (latest quarter-end), an LTM segment revenue formula:

=SUMIFS(Seg[Amount],Seg[Segment],SelSegment,Seg[Line],"Revenue",Seg[Period_End],">"&EDATE(AsAtDate,-12),Seg[Period_End],"<="&AsAtDate)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Standalone vs consolidated segment tables: both are filed; use consolidated for group analysis but note that subsidiaries may form or join segments (e.g. CaratLane within jewellery at consolidated level).
  • Seasonality: compare YoY quarters (Q3 FY25 vs Q3 FY24), not sequential quarters, for festive-driven segments.
  • Mid-year acquisitions: a segment's quarterly growth includes inorganic revenue from the acquisition date; separate it using management commentary.
  • Ind AS 34 interim measurement: some costs (bonuses, annual discounts) are estimated quarterly and trued up in Q4, distorting Q4 segment margins.
  • XBRL tagging errors: vendor data sometimes mislabels segments; cross-check against PDFs for key periods.

Key takeaways

  • Reg 33 segment tables present revenue, results, assets and liabilities by segment each quarter, reconciled to revenue, PBT and total assets/liabilities.
  • Quarterly data is limited-reviewed; annual data is audited; Q4 is a derived balancing figure.
  • Check that quarters sum to the year and that annual Reg 33 data matches the Ind AS 108 note.
  • Replace earlier figures with restated comparatives when segments are redefined.
  • Build the database in long format keeping the latest filing per period; compute LTM by segment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Format recall [L1]

List the four sections of the Reg 33 segment table and the reconciling lines in each, and state which columns (periods) are presented in a Q3 filing.

Q2. Derive Q4 and test consistency [L2]

A company's published segment results (₹ crore) for Segment A: Q1 210, Q2 225, 9M 690; annual audited 960. Segment B: Q1 80, Q2 95, 9M 260; annual 330. The annual report Ind AS 108 note shows Segment A ₹940 crore and Segment B ₹350 crore. Derive Q3 and Q4 for both segments and explain possible reasons for the annual report difference.

💡 Hint: Q3 comes from 9M minus H1; then compare the Reg 33 annual with the annual report note.

Q3. Power Query design [L3]

Design a Power Query workflow to build a 20-quarter consolidated segment database for Titan from exchange filings, handling restated comparatives, segment renaming and standalone vs consolidated tables. Specify the steps, the keys and the validation checks against annual Ind AS 108 notes.

Q4. Seasonality judgement [L2]

A jewellery segment's Q3 revenue is 33% of annual revenue and its Q3 result margin is 13% against 10% for the year. Explain why sequential Q3-to-Q4 comparisons mislead, how you would compute LTM margin, and how quarter-end segment assets help analyse inventory build.
