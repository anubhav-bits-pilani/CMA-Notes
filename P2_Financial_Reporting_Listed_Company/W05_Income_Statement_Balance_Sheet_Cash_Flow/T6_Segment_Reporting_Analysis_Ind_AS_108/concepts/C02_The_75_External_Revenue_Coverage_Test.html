Key concept

The 75% External Revenue Coverage Test

Concept: Ind AS 108 para 15: reportable segments must cover at least 75% of entity external revenue

Statement

\[ \frac{\sum_{i \in \text{Reportable}} \text{External revenue}_i}{\text{Entity revenue}} \geq 75\% \]

If the total external revenue of reportable segments identified under the 10% tests is less than 75% of the entity's revenue, additional operating segments are identified as reportable (even if they do not meet the 10% criteria) until at least 75% of entity revenue is included in reportable segments (Ind AS 108.15).

Explanation

The 10% tests can leave a large part of revenue in "all other segments" when a company has many medium-sized businesses. The 75% rule guarantees that most external revenue is covered by separately reported segments.

Key points:

  • The numerator uses external revenue only — inter-segment sales do not count, unlike the 10% revenue test.
  • The denominator is the entity's revenue (as in the financial statements).
  • Management chooses which additional segments to report; logically, the largest remaining segments.
  • Remaining activities are disclosed as "all other segments" with a description of their sources of revenue (Ind AS 108.16).
  • A practical ceiling: if the number of reportable segments exceeds ten, consider whether information becomes too detailed (Ind AS 108.19) — not a hard limit.

Analytical relevance: the 75% test tells you the minimum coverage you can expect. If "others" is still large relative to revenue (approaching 25%), ask which businesses sit there — often new ventures or loss-making units (for Titan, items like fragrances, women's ethnic wear and accessories have been reported within "Others").

Illustration

Entity revenue ₹10,000 crore. Reportable segments after 10% tests: A (external revenue ₹5,200 crore) and B (₹1,500 crore). Coverage = 6,700 ÷ 10,000 = 67% < 75%.

Remaining operating segments: C ₹800 crore, D ₹700 crore, E ₹600 crore, F ₹400 crore, G ₹300 crore (all below 10%).

Step Added Cumulative external revenue Coverage
Start A, B 6,700 67%
Add C C 7,500 75% — requirement met

C becomes reportable; D–G go to "all other segments" (₹2,000 crore, 20%), unless management voluntarily reports more.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the coverage test [L1]

Entity revenue ₹24,000 crore. Reportable segments by 10% tests: X external ₹12,500 crore, Y external ₹3,800 crore. Other operating segments: P ₹1,900 crore, Q ₹1,700 crore, R ₹1,600 crore, S ₹1,500 crore, T ₹1,000 crore. Determine the additional reportable segments needed.

Q2. Inter-segment trap [L2]

A segment has total revenue of ₹3,000 crore (₹1,200 crore inter-segment) in an entity with revenue ₹12,000 crore and combined segment revenue ₹14,500 crore. Explain how it is treated under the 10% revenue test and in the 75% coverage calculation.

💡 Hint: The two tests use different revenue definitions.

Q3. Analyst follow-up [L2]

A company's "all other segments" has grown from 9% to 23% of revenue in three years while reportable segments still cover 77%. Draft three questions you would ask management and the disclosures you would check.
