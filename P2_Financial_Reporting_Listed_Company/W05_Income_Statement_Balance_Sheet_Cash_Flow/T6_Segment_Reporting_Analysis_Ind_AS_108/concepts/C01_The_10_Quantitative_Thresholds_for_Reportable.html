Key concept

The 10% Quantitative Thresholds for Reportable Segments

Concept: Ind AS 108 para 13: reportable if 10% or more of combined revenue, absolute profit/loss, or assets

Statement

An operating segment is a reportable segment if it meets any one of the following (Ind AS 108.13):

\[ \frac{R_i}{\sum_j R_j} \geq 10\% \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{\lvert P_i \rvert}{\max\left(\sum_{P_j > 0} P_j,\; \left\lvert \sum_{P_j < 0} P_j \right\rvert\right)} \geq 10\% \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{A_i}{\sum_j A_j} \geq 10\% \]

where \( R_i \) is segment \( i \)'s reported revenue including both external and inter-segment sales, \( P_i \) is its reported profit or loss, and \( A_i \) its assets; sums run over all operating segments (not only reportable ones).

Explanation

The thresholds stop entities from burying significant businesses in "others". Key mechanics:

  • Revenue test uses total revenue (external + inter-segment), so a captive segment with mostly inter-segment sales can be reportable.
  • Profit test compares the absolute value of each segment's result with the larger of (i) total profits of profit-making segments and (ii) the absolute total of losses of loss-making segments. This makes a large loss-making segment reportable even if total group profit is small.
  • Asset test uses segment assets as reviewed by the CODM.
  • Meeting any test is enough; voluntary reporting below thresholds is allowed if useful.
  • Further steps: aggregate small segments only if they share a majority of the aggregation criteria (Ind AS 108.14), then apply the 75% external revenue top-up (Ind AS 108.15, c02), and carry forward segments of continuing significance (Ind AS 108.17).

Why it matters analytically: if a business you consider important is not separately reported, check whether it falls below all three tests or has been aggregated — and ask management about the CODM package.

Connections

Applies s01 (segment identification) and leads to c02 (75% test). Uses segment data defined in s02. Relevant to the topic pitfall on segment re-definitions, because crossing a threshold creates a newly reportable segment with restated comparatives (Ind AS 108.18).

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the tests [L1]

Segments (₹ crore): A revenue 3,000, profit 420, assets 2,500; B revenue 280, loss (90), assets 150; C revenue 410, profit 30, assets 900; D revenue 250, profit 10, assets 120. Identify reportable segments.

Q2. Loss-making segment [L2]

A group has five segments with profits of ₹500, ₹200 and ₹100 crore and losses of ₹(600) and ₹(40) crore. Revenue and assets of the loss-making ₹(40) crore segment are 4% and 6% of totals. Determine the profit threshold and whether each segment passes the profit test.

💡 Hint: Compare total profits with the absolute value of total losses and take the greater.

Q3. Captive segment [L2]

A vertically integrated textile company's spinning segment sells 85% of its output to the group's garments segment. Its external revenue is 3% of entity revenue, but total revenue including inter-segment sales is 16% of combined segment revenue. Is it reportable, and what does an analyst gain from its disclosure?
