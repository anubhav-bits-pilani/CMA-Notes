Key concept

Segment Measures Follow the CODM Basis

Concept: Segment measures follow CODM's basis, not necessarily Ind AS measurement; read the basis note

Statement

The amount of each segment item reported is the measure reported to the CODM for the purposes of allocating resources and assessing performance (Ind AS 108.25). Adjustments and eliminations made in preparing the entity's financial statements, and allocations of revenues, expenses and gains or losses, are included in segment profit only if they are included in the measure used by the CODM. If the CODM uses more than one measure, the one most consistent with Ind AS measurement is reported (Ind AS 108.26). The entity explains the measurement basis (Ind AS 108.27).

Explanation

Ind AS 108 is a disclosure standard: it does not require segment data to be measured under Ind AS. The CODM may use EBITDA, contribution, EBIT before corporate costs, figures on a pre-Ind AS 116 basis, constant currency, or management accounts with different inventory costing. Ind AS 108.27 requires disclosure of at least:

  • (a) the basis of accounting for inter-segment transactions;
  • (b)–(d) the nature of differences between segment profit, assets and liabilities and the entity's measures (e.g. accounting policies, allocation of centrally incurred costs);
  • (e) changes from prior periods in measurement methods and their effect;
  • (f) the nature and effect of asymmetrical allocations (e.g. depreciation allocated without the related assets).

The reconciliation (c04) quantifies the differences.

Why it matters: two companies' "segment EBIT" may not be comparable — one may include ROU depreciation and allocated corporate costs, another may exclude both. Within a single company, a change in measurement basis breaks the trend. An analyst must read the basis note before computing margins or ROCE, and restate where necessary.

India-specific observations: many companies define segment result as "profit before interest, exceptional items, unallocable items and tax"; some IT services companies report segment results before depreciation and unallocable costs; banks report segments per RBI's prescribed segments (Treasury, Corporate/Wholesale, Retail, Other banking), which follows regulatory rather than CODM-specific definitions.

Illustration

Two retailers report segment results for their fashion segments (₹ crore):

Item Company A (CODM: EBITDA pre-Ind AS 116, before corporate costs) Company B (CODM: EBIT post-Ind AS 116, after allocated corporate costs)
Revenue 10,000 10,000
Reported segment result 1,400 700
Reported margin 14.0% 7.0%

Restating A to B's basis: less D&A on owned assets ₹250 crore, add back rent ₹600 crore and deduct ROU depreciation ₹480 crore, less allocated corporate costs ₹200 crore → result ₹1,070 crore, margin 10.7%. The apparent 7-point gap shrinks to 3.7 points — still a real difference, but half as large.

Practice Questions

Q1. Read the basis note [L1]

List five items a basis-of-measurement note under Ind AS 108.27 should tell you, and for each, one way it could make segment margins non-comparable across companies.

Q2. Restate to a common basis [L2]

Company X segment result ₹850 crore is "EBITDA before corporate costs, on a pre-Ind AS 116 basis". Company Y segment result ₹520 crore is "EBIT after allocated corporate costs, post-Ind AS 116". X data: owned-asset D&A ₹180 crore, rent ₹310 crore, ROU depreciation would be ₹260 crore, corporate costs ₹140 crore allocable by revenue share of 70%. Restate X to Y's basis.

💡 Hint: Move step by step: D&A, then the lease swap, then corporate costs.

Q3. Change in measurement [L2]

A company changes its segment profit measure from EBIT to EBITDA in FY2025 and restates only FY2024. Explain the disclosure required under Ind AS 108.27(e) and Ind AS 108.29–30 and how you would rebuild a five-year segment margin series.
