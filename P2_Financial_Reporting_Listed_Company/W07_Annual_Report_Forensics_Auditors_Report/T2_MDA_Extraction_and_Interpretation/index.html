Roadmap topic: Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A): Extracting industry outlook, raw material inflation commentary, capacity expansion plans, and risk mitigation disclosures

Overview

The Management Discussion and Analysis is management's own account of the year: why revenue and margins moved, how the industry is evolving, what capacity is coming, what could go wrong and how it is being managed. Under LODR Reg 34(2)(e) and Reg 34(3) read with Schedule V Part B it is a mandatory part of every listed company's annual report, with prescribed contents including explanations for key ratio changes of 25% or more and for any change in return on net worth. It is not audited, but the auditor reads it under SA 720 for material inconsistency.

For the target roles the MD&A has two uses. In FP&A, equity research and valuation, it is the starting point for forecast drivers — industry growth, volume and realisation, raw material pass-through, capex and commissioning timelines, margin bands. In forensic and credit work, it is a set of claims to be tested: does the raw material story explain the gross margin? Does the commissioning timeline match the CWIP ageing schedule? Are the risks in the MD&A the same ones the auditor flagged as KAMs and the Risk Management Committee oversees? The analytical discipline is to treat every MD&A sentence as a hypothesis and every number in the financial statements, quarterly results, earnings calls and BRSR as evidence.

The learning sequence: start with what Schedule V Part B requires and how to read each element (s01) and the legal status of the MD&A (c01); then the ratio-change and RONW explanation requirements, with the 25% rule (s02, c02); then the two most forecast-relevant narratives — raw material inflation versus gross margin and pass-through lag (s03), and capacity expansion versus funding and CWIP ageing (s04); then risk disclosures, the RMC under Reg 21 and BRSR/BRSR Core metrics (s05, c04); and finally triangulation of the MD&A with earnings calls, investor presentations and the board's report under s.134 (s06), culminating in guidance credibility scoring (c03). The output is an MD&A claims register and guidance tracker that feeds both the week's forensic memo and the forecast models built from Week 13 onwards.

Related concepts to study

SEC Item 303 and IFRS Practice Statement 1

SEC Item 303 MD&A (US) vs Indian MD&A depth; IFRS Practice Statement 1 Management Commentary

US MD&A under Item 303 of Regulation S-K is considerably deeper than the Indian Schedule V Part B version. It requires discussion of liquidity and capital resources (including material cash requirements from known contractual obligations), results of operations including known trends or uncertainties reasonably likely to have a material effect, and, since the 2020 amendments, critical accounting estimates with their sensitivity. It is subject to SEC review comment letters, which creates enforcement pressure for specificity. IFRS Practice Statement 1 Management Commentary (2010) is a non-mandatory framework; the IASB consulted on a comprehensive revision (Exposure Draft 2021) emphasising information on long-term prospects, intangible resources and key matters — check whether the revision has been finalised. Comparing an Indian company's MD&A with a US-listed peer's 10-K MD&A quickly shows the gaps (liquidity discussion, critical estimates) you need to fill from notes and calls.

Outlook statements as forecast inputs

Outlook statements as assumption inputs → Weeks 13 and 17 (forecast drivers)

Outlook statements in the MD&A and on calls become explicit driver assumptions in the operating model built in Week 13 (revenue = volume × realisation, capacity utilisation, margin bands, capex and commissioning) and in the scenario and sensitivity framework of Week 17. The discipline is to record each outlook statement with source and date, convert vague language into numeric bands, adjust for the management team's historical bias (c03), and document in the assumptions sheet why the model's base case differs from guidance. A model whose base case simply copies guidance inherits management optimism; one that ignores guidance discards the best available information about plans. The guidance tracker built in this topic is the bridge.

Board's report and corporate governance report as companions

Directors' report, corporate governance report (Schedule V Part C) as companion sources

The board's report under s.134(3) and Rule 8 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules provides statutory facts that test MD&A narrative: explanations of audit qualifications (s.134(3)(f)), loans and guarantees under s.186, related party contracts in AOC-2, material changes after year-end, risk management policy, IFC adequacy, significant regulatory orders, and IBC proceedings. The corporate governance report under Schedule V Part C covers board composition, committee meetings and attendance (audit committee, NRC, stakeholder relationship committee, RMC), director remuneration, materially significant RPTs, non-compliances and penalties in the last three years, total fees paid to the statutory auditor and network entities on a consolidated basis, details of material subsidiaries, and the certificate that no director is debarred. Together they test whether MD&A claims about governance and controls are backed by board practice.

Common pitfalls

Copying narrative without testing

Copying management narrative into analysis without testing against numbers

The mistake is to paste MD&A statements ("margins impacted by raw material inflation", "capacity on track for commissioning") into a research note or credit memo as findings. It happens because the MD&A is well written, time is short, and the statements sound authoritative. The correct treatment is to treat each claim as a hypothesis and test it: gross margin versus commodity indices and peers (s03), commissioning claims versus CWIP ageing (s04), ratio explanations versus DuPont and quarterly data (s02). In review, check that every narrative statement in the memo is paired with a number from the financial statements or a third-party source, and that untested claims are labelled "management's view".

Missing tone changes and dropped disclosures

Missing changes in tone or dropped disclosures year-over-year

Analysts read the current MD&A in isolation, so they miss that last year's quantified margin guidance has become "we remain focused on profitable growth", that a previously discussed export market is no longer mentioned, or that a risk listed for three years has vanished just as it materialised. These changes are invisible without comparison. The correct practice is a side-by-side comparison of at least two years, ideally with a text comparison tool or Power Query word counts by section, and a log of added, removed and reworded statements. In review, check that the memo contains a "changes from last year" section and that dropped disclosures have been followed up on the earnings call.

Segment contradictions with Ind AS 108

Ignoring segment-level contradictions between MD&A and Ind AS 108 notes

The MD&A may describe a segment as "the growth engine" while the Ind AS 108 note shows its segment result falling, or MD&A product-wise figures may not reconcile with reported segment revenue. Analysts miss this because the two sections are far apart in the annual report and use different groupings. The correct treatment is to reconcile MD&A segment commentary to the operating segment note (revenue, result, assets, capex by segment), and to check whether segment definitions changed (Ind AS 108 requires restatement of comparatives when segments change). In review, look for a segment reconciliation table in the workpapers and flag any unexplained change in segment composition.

Practice Questions

Q1. Integrated MD&A test [L3]

A listed specialty chemicals company's FY 2024-25 MD&A states: (i) "industry demand recovered in H2; we grew volumes 14%"; (ii) "gross margin was impacted by raw material inflation of 9%"; (iii) "our ₹900 crore Gujarat expansion will commission in Q1 FY 2025-26, funded from internal accruals"; (iv) "our risk management framework is robust". Data (₹ crore unless stated): revenue 3,640 (prior year 3,380); cost of materials consumed plus change in inventories 2,180 (prior 1,860); CFO 290; dividends 70; capex 610; net debt up 380; CWIP 840, of which 520 is 1–2 years old and 160 is 2–3 years old; IFC opinion qualified on inventory costing; KAM on inventory valuation; RMC met twice, 200 days apart. Test each of the four statements against the data, quantify discrepancies, and draft a 250-word findings section with page-reference placeholders.

💡 Hint: Decompose revenue growth into volume and realisation before judging the raw material explanation.

Q2. Ratio-change disclosure audit [L2]

From the following, compute the seven Schedule V Part B ratios for two years, identify which require explanation, and draft model explanations based on the data: revenue 2,400 / 2,950; operating profit (EBIT) 312 / 295; finance cost 40 / 82; PAT 205 / 160; average receivables 330 / 520; average inventory (at COGS) 250 / 410; COGS 1,500 / 1,900; current assets 900 / 1,150; current liabilities 640 / 980; total debt 420 / 900; equity 1,400 / 1,520. Also compute RONW (using average equity; opening equity for year 1 was ₹1,260 crore) and explain its change using DuPont.

Q3. Triangulation and guidance credibility [L3]

Over eight quarters a company guided EBITDA margin of 21–23% each quarter and delivered 21.4, 20.8, 20.1, 21.9, 19.6, 20.2, 19.9, 20.5. Its MD&A for the latest year says "margins remained within our guided range", the investor presentation shows a chart of "adjusted EBITDA margin" (excluding ₹ 30–45 crore per quarter of "one-time" costs) within range, and the board's report is silent. Quarterly revenue is about ₹1,500 crore. Compute the credibility statistics, recompute adjusted margins, and evaluate whether the MD&A statement is a material inconsistency the auditor should have considered under SA 720.

Q4. Year-on-year disclosure change log [L2]

Design a Power Query-based process to compare two years of MD&A text by section (industry, outlook, risks, segment, internal controls), producing counts of new, removed and changed sentences and a keyword tone index. Specify the steps (load, split, normalise, merge, compare) and the outputs you would show in a Power BI page.

Q5. Capex, ESG and risk integration [L3]

A cement company's MD&A announces 12 MTPA of new capacity at ₹7,200 crore over three years. BRSR Core shows Scope 1 intensity of 0.60 tCO2e per tonne and production of 40 million tonnes; the MD&A risk section does not mention carbon pricing; the RMC charter lists ESG risks. EBITDA is ₹6,500 crore and net debt ₹4,000 crore. Build a funding-mix table assuming 50% internal accruals and 50% debt, a carbon-cost sensitivity at ₹1,000 and ₹2,500 per tonne on 30% of emissions, and write a credit committee paragraph on whether the MD&A's risk disclosure is adequate.
