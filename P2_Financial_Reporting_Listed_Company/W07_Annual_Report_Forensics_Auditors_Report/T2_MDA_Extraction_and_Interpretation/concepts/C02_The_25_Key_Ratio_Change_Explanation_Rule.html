Key concept

The 25% Key Ratio Change Explanation Rule

Concept: Schedule V Part B requires explanations for 25% or more change in key ratios vs previous year

Statement

LODR Schedule V Part B requires the MD&A to give details of significant changes — a change of 25% or more compared with the immediately previous financial year — in key financial ratios, along with detailed explanations. The named ratios are debtors turnover, inventory turnover, interest coverage ratio, current ratio, debt equity ratio, operating profit margin (%) and net profit margin (%), or sector-specific equivalent ratios.

The trigger is a relative change:

\[ \text{Change \%} = \frac{R_t - R_{t-1}}{\left\lvert R_{t-1} \right\rvert} \times 100, \qquad \text{explanation required if } \left\lvert \text{Change \%} \right\rvert \geq 25 \]

where \( R_t \) is the ratio for the current year and \( R_{t-1} \) for the previous year. For margins expressed in %, the change is relative (a margin falling from 12% to 9% is a 25% change), not a percentage-point change.

Explanation

The rule forces management to explain big moves, but leaves definitions to the company. Analysts should note four practical issues:

  • Relative versus absolute. Some companies compute percentage-point changes for margins, under-reporting triggers. A fall from 12.0% to 9.5% is 2.5 pp but a 20.8% relative change; from 8% to 6% is 2 pp but 25% relative and triggers disclosure.
  • Direction. Improvements also require explanation; companies sometimes explain only adverse moves.
  • Negative or near-zero bases. Where the previous ratio is negative (a net loss) or near zero, relative change is unstable; good practice is to state the values and explain qualitatively.
  • Overlap with Schedule III. The Schedule III ratio note requires explanations for changes of more than 25% for eleven ratios in the audited notes; the MD&A rule is "25% or more". Explanations should be consistent between the two.

The quality test is whether the explanation names a driver that can be verified (for example, "receivables increased due to a ₹180 crore milestone billing to a state utility in March, collected in April") rather than restating the change ("due to increase in receivables").

Four ratios require explanation. If management explains the debt-equity rise as "borrowings for the new plant", the analyst checks CWIP additions and capex in the cash flow; if inventory turnover's decline is attributed to "strategic stocking ahead of price increases", the analyst checks inventory composition (raw material versus finished goods) and the subsequent quarter's inventory days.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute triggers [L1]

Compute relative changes and identify triggers: interest coverage 3.2x → 2.5x; current ratio 0.92 → 1.18; operating margin 6.4% → 4.9%; debt-equity 1.10 → 0.80; net profit margin (–)2.0% → 1.5%. Explain how you would handle the last item.

Q2. Evaluate explanations [L2]

Rate each explanation as verifiable or boilerplate and propose a better version: (a) "Inventory turnover declined due to higher inventory"; (b) "Interest coverage declined due to increase in finance cost on borrowings of ₹600 crore drawn in Q3 for the Dahej expansion, which is under CWIP and will commission in Q2 FY 2025-26"; (c) "Net profit margin declined due to various factors".

💡 Hint: A verifiable explanation names an amount, a cause and a document where it can be checked.

Q3. Percentage-point manipulation [L2]

A company reports its operating profit margin fell "2.9 percentage points, below the 25% threshold" from 11.4% to 8.5%. Assess whether the disclosure is compliant and draft the comment you would include in a disclosure-quality review.
