Key concept

MD&A as a Mandatory Part of the Annual Report under Reg 34

Concept: LODR Reg 34(3) read with Schedule V Part B: MD&A forms part of annual report

Statement

A listed entity with listed specified securities must include a Management Discussion and Analysis in its annual report. LODR Reg 34(2)(e) requires the annual report to contain the MD&A, either as part of the directors' report or as an addition to it; Reg 34(3) requires the annual report to contain the disclosures specified in Schedule V; and Schedule V Part B specifies the MD&A's content (industry structure and developments, opportunities and threats, segment or product performance, outlook, risks and concerns, internal control systems, discussion of financial performance with respect to operational performance, human resources developments, key ratio changes of 25% or more, change in return on net worth, and disclosure of any departure from accounting standards).

Explanation

Three consequences follow from the MD&A being a mandated part of the annual report rather than voluntary commentary:

  1. Liability and accuracy. As part of the annual report approved by the board, MD&A statements carry board accountability. Misleading statements can attract action under the SEBI Act and LODR (Reg 4 principles of disclosure require information to be adequate, accurate, explicit, timely and presented in a simple language) and under the SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations where they mislead investors.
  2. Auditor's reading under SA 720 (Revised). The MD&A is "other information" in the annual report. The auditor reads it and considers whether there is a material inconsistency with the financial statements or the auditor's knowledge obtained in the audit, and reports in the "Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor's Report Thereon" section. The auditor does not audit the MD&A, but a material uncorrected misstatement of other information must be reported. Where the annual report is not available by the date of the auditor's report, the auditor reports accordingly and reads it later.
  3. Timing and dissemination. Reg 34(1) requires the annual report to be submitted to the stock exchanges and published on the website not later than the day of commencement of dispatch to shareholders. The MD&A is therefore public, dated and comparable year-on-year.

The MD&A sits alongside other Schedule V parts: Part A (related party disclosures in the annual report), Part C (corporate governance report), Part D (CEO declaration on code of conduct), Part E (compliance certificate on corporate governance) and Part F (demat suspense account). Reading Part B without Part C misses board and committee information that tests MD&A claims about governance and controls.

The section is to be prepared "within the limits set by the listed entity's competitive position", which permits withholding competitively sensitive detail but not omitting material risks.

Illustration

A listed auto ancillary's annual report places a two-page "Management Discussion" within the directors' report covering industry, outlook and risks, but omits the ratio change table and RONW explanation. Debtors turnover fell 31% and interest coverage fell 44%. The omission is a non-compliance with Schedule V Part B, which an analyst logs as a disclosure-quality finding and which the auditor should consider under SA 720 only insofar as stated information is inconsistent, not for omission of required content. The analyst should compute the ratios from Schedule III disclosures and seek explanations on the earnings call.

Practice Questions

Q1. Location and status [L1]

Explain the difference in legal status and assurance between (a) the MD&A in the annual report, (b) the investor presentation filed under Reg 30, and (c) the notes to the audited financial statements, with reference to Reg 34, SA 720 and the Companies Act.

Q2. Material inconsistency under SA 720 [L2]

The MD&A states "net debt reduced to ₹420 crore", while the balance sheet shows borrowings ₹1,180 crore, lease liabilities ₹210 crore and cash and current investments ₹560 crore. Evaluate whether this is a material inconsistency the auditor should address under SA 720, and how the auditor's report would reflect it if management refuses to correct it.

💡 Hint: Consider whether a definitional difference can explain the gap.

Q3. Compliance review [L2]

Prepare a checklist (one line per Schedule V Part B element) and use it to assess an annual report that contains industry overview, outlook, risks and HR, but no segment discussion, no internal controls discussion, and a ratio table without explanations. State the findings and their severity for a governance score.
