Key concept

BRSR Applicability: Top 1,000 Listed Entities under Reg 34(2)(f)

Concept: BRSR mandatory for top 1,000 listed entities by market cap (Reg 34(2)(f))

Statement

LODR Reg 34(2)(f) requires the annual report of the top 1,000 listed entities based on market capitalisation to include a Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) describing initiatives taken from an environmental, social and governance perspective, in the format specified by SEBI. BRSR became mandatory from FY 2022-23 (voluntary for FY 2021-22) under SEBI circular of 10 May 2021, replacing the Business Responsibility Report. The BRSR Core subset of KPIs (SEBI circular of 12 July 2023) is subject to assurance or assessment on a phased basis by market-cap rank. Other listed entities may file BRSR voluntarily.

Explanation

Ranking basis. Market-cap-based applicability under LODR is driven by rankings prepared by the stock exchanges. Following the December 2024 LODR amendments, the ranking is based on the average market capitalisation over a six-month period (1 July to 31 December) each year, rather than the 31 March snapshot previously used; applicability continues under Reg 3(2) unless the entity falls outside the threshold for three consecutive years. These mechanics are date-sensitive; verify the current text of Reg 3(2) and the latest exchange list before concluding applicability.

Structure. BRSR has Section A (general disclosures), Section B (management and process disclosures) and Section C (principle-wise performance under the nine NGRBC principles, with essential and leadership indicators). BRSR Core covers nine attributes: GHG footprint, water footprint, energy footprint, embracing circularity, employee wellbeing and safety, gender diversity, inclusive development, fairness in engaging with customers and suppliers, and openness of business.

Assurance glide path. The 2023 circular set reasonable assurance of BRSR Core for the top 150 from FY 2023-24, top 250 from FY 2024-25, top 500 from FY 2025-26 and top 1,000 from FY 2026-27. In March 2025 SEBI changed the requirement to "assessment or assurance" and deferred and relaxed value-chain disclosures. The BRSR is also linked to Reg 21 (the RMC's remit includes sustainability/ESG risk) and to the corporate governance report.

Analytical relevance. For the analyst, BRSR provides (i) ESG metrics for carbon and resource scenarios, (ii) governance cross-checks — the openness attribute discloses related-party share of purchases, sales, loans and investments, and the fairness attribute discloses days payable — and (iii) a sign of disclosure maturity. Companies just inside the top 1,000 often produce minimal BRSRs; large caps with assured BRSR Core are more reliable.

Illustration

Company X is ranked 940th in the exchange list effective for FY 2024-25 and 1,060th for FY 2025-26. Under the three-consecutive-year rule (as amended), BRSR continues to apply in FY 2025-26 because the entity has not yet been outside the top 1,000 for three consecutive years. Its BRSR Core shows related-party purchases at 38% of total purchases, while the Ind AS 24 note shows purchases from related parties of ₹520 crore against cost of materials of ₹1,600 crore (32.5%). The 5.5 pp difference may be explained by inclusion of capital goods or services purchases in BRSR; the analyst requests the definition and logs the item.

Practice Questions

Q1. Applicability [L1]

Explain, with reference to Reg 34(2)(f) and the market-cap ranking rules, whether BRSR applies to (a) a company ranked 980th in each of the last two lists, (b) a company newly listed in October 2024 that enters the top 1,000 in the December 2024 list, and (c) a high value debt listed entity without listed equity. State any assumptions about the current rules.

💡 Hint: Check whether Reg 34(2)(f) is framed around listed specified securities.

Q2. Openness metrics cross-check [L2]

BRSR Core shows loans and advances to related parties at 62% of total loans and advances and investments in related parties at 85% of total investments. The balance sheet shows loans ₹410 crore and investments ₹1,250 crore. Compute the implied related-party amounts and explain which notes and CARO clauses you would reconcile them to.

Q3. Assurance quality [L2]

Two peer companies' BRSR Core disclosures are, respectively, under "reasonable assurance by an audit firm under SSAE 3000" and "assessment by an empanelled ESG assessor". Explain how you would weigh the two in an ESG risk comparison and what you would read in each provider's report.
