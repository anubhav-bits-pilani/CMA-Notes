Subtopic note

Key Ratio Change Disclosures and Return on Net Worth Explanations

Scope: Key ratio change disclosures (25% or more) and return on net worth explanations

Why it matters

Since FY 2018-19 the MD&A of every listed company must explain significant (25% or more) changes in key financial ratios and any change in return on net worth. Since FY 2021-22 Schedule III also requires a ratio table with explanations for changes above 25%. These are the two places where management is forced to give a reason for a ratio move, and they are useful precisely because the explanations can be tested: a debtors turnover decline "due to higher sales in the last month" can be checked against quarterly revenue, and an RONW decline "due to capital raised for expansion" can be checked against CWIP and the timing of the equity raise. Weak or recycled explanations are a signal about disclosure quality.

Core notes

LODR Schedule V Part B requirements

Schedule V Part B requires:

  1. Details of significant changes (change of 25% or more as compared to the immediately previous financial year) in key financial ratios, along with detailed explanations, including: - Debtors turnover - Inventory turnover - Interest coverage ratio - Current ratio - Debt equity ratio - Operating profit margin (%) - Net profit margin (%) - or sector-specific equivalent ratios, as applicable.
  2. Details of any change in return on net worth as compared to the immediately previous financial year, along with a detailed explanation. Note there is no 25% threshold for RONW: any change needs explanation.

SEBI does not prescribe formulas, so companies define them. Disclosure of the definitions is good practice; their absence makes peer comparison unreliable.

Schedule III (Division II) ratio disclosures

The 2021 amendments to Schedule III added, as additional regulatory information, eleven ratios with numerator, denominator, current and previous year values, % variance, and explanation for any change of more than 25%:

Schedule III ratio Typical definition used in practice
Current ratio Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Debt-equity ratio Total debt ÷ Shareholders' equity
Debt service coverage ratio Earnings available for debt service ÷ (Interest + lease payments + principal repayments)
Return on equity Net profit after tax (less preference dividend) ÷ Average shareholders' equity
Inventory turnover COGS or revenue ÷ Average inventory
Trade receivables turnover Revenue ÷ Average trade receivables
Trade payables turnover Purchases (or COGS) ÷ Average trade payables
Net capital turnover Revenue ÷ Working capital
Net profit ratio Net profit ÷ Revenue
Return on capital employed EBIT ÷ Capital employed (tangible net worth + total debt + deferred tax liability)
Return on investment Income from investments ÷ Average investments

The ICAI Guidance Note on Division II of Schedule III gives indicative definitions. The Schedule III disclosure is in the notes (audited); the LODR disclosure is in the MD&A (unaudited but covered by SA 720 reading for material inconsistency). They overlap on current ratio, debt-equity, inventory and receivables turnover and net profit margin, so the explanations should be consistent.

Return on net worth

\[ \text{RONW} = \frac{\text{Profit after tax attributable to equity holders}}{\text{Average net worth}} \]

where net worth is defined in s.2(57) of the Companies Act as paid-up share capital plus reserves created out of profits and securities premium, less accumulated losses, deferred expenditure and miscellaneous expenditure not written off, excluding reserves created out of revaluation, write-back of depreciation and amalgamation. In practice many companies use total equity; note the definition and adjust if OCI-heavy reserves (for example, equity instruments at FVOCI) distort it.

A defensible explanation decomposes the change (DuPont):

\[ \text{RONW} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average total assets}}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Average total assets}}{\text{Average net worth}}}_{\text{equity multiplier}} \]

Worked illustration

A specialty chemicals company (₹ crore):

Item FY 2023-24 FY 2024-25
Revenue 4,200 4,410
EBIT 714 529
Finance cost 42 78
PAT 504 336
Average total assets 5,600 6,700
Average net worth 3,500 3,900
Average trade receivables 690 980

RONW fell from 14.4% to 8.6%. DuPont: net margin 12.0% → 7.6%; asset turnover 0.750 → 0.658; equity multiplier 1.60 → 1.72. Margin explains most of the fall. Interest coverage (EBIT ÷ finance cost) fell from 17.0x to 6.8x (–60%) and debtors turnover from 6.09x to 4.50x (–26%), so both breach the 25% threshold and need explanation. If the MD&A attributes the RONW decline only to "capacity commissioned towards year-end", that is incomplete: the receivables build (DSO up from about 60 to about 81 days) and margin compression also need explanation.

Testing explanations

Explanation offered Test
"Higher sales in Q4" for receivables turnover decline Compare Q4 revenue share with prior year (quarterly results, Reg 33)
"Increase in borrowings for capex" for debt-equity rise Check CWIP and capex in cash flow; check interest capitalised under Ind AS 23
"Lower realisations" for margin decline Compare with commodity indices and peer margins
"Strategic inventory build-up" Inventory days by category (raw material, WIP, FG) and inventory note
"One-time items" Exceptional items line and its notes

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Definition changes year-on-year can manufacture or hide a 25% change. Recompute using constant definitions.
  • Low base effects: a ratio moving from 0.04 to 0.06 is a 50% change; companies may explain trivial moves at length and important ones briefly. Apply materiality.
  • Standalone versus consolidated: MD&A ratio tables are frequently standalone while the narrative is consolidated.
  • Sector-specific substitutes (for example, gross NPA and NIM for NBFCs, EBITDA per tonne for cement) are permitted; check that substitution is not used to avoid disclosing an adverse standard ratio.
  • Ind AS 116 effects on debt-equity and interest coverage (lease liabilities included or not) must be applied consistently.
  • RONW boosted by buybacks or losses in OCI reducing equity is a quality-of-return issue, not operating improvement.

Key takeaways

  • Schedule V Part B requires explanations for 25% or more changes in seven named ratios (or sector equivalents) and for any change in RONW.
  • Schedule III separately requires eleven ratios with explanations for changes over 25%, in the audited notes.
  • Recompute ratios on consistent definitions and reconcile the two disclosures.
  • Explain RONW changes with a DuPont decomposition; test management's explanation against it.
  • Weak, generic or recycled explanations are a disclosure-quality signal.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify disclosure triggers [L1]

From the following ratios (previous year, current year) identify which require MD&A explanation and which require Schedule III explanation: current ratio 1.42, 1.08; debt-equity 0.55, 0.71; interest coverage 6.2x, 4.9x; inventory turnover 5.8x, 4.1x; net profit margin 8.4%, 8.1%; RONW 15.1%, 14.6%; ROCE 18.0%, 13.2%.

💡 Hint: RONW has no threshold under Schedule V Part B.

Q2. DuPont attribution [L2]

Using these figures (₹ crore), decompose the change in RONW into margin, turnover and leverage effects using sequential substitution in the order margin → turnover → leverage: FY 2023-24 revenue 2,500, PAT 250, average assets 3,125, average net worth 1,875; FY 2024-25 revenue 3,100, PAT 248, average assets 4,100, average net worth 2,050.

Q3. Test a management explanation [L2]

An MD&A explains a 32% fall in debtors turnover as "due to extended credit offered to government customers". The segment note shows government revenue at 18% of total in both years, total revenue up 6%, and trade receivables up 41%, with the receivables ageing note showing amounts over 180 days rising from ₹42 crore to ₹156 crore. Evaluate the explanation and state what you would write in a forensic memo.

Q4. Build the ratio check in Excel [L3]

Describe an Excel block that recomputes all seven Schedule V Part B ratios and the eleven Schedule III ratios from a standard Schedule III input sheet for two years, computes % changes, flags changes of 25% or more, compares with the company-reported ratio values, and highlights differences above 5%. Specify the formulas for the flag and the reported-versus-recomputed variance.
