Subtopic note

Risk Disclosures, the Risk Management Committee and BRSR Core

Scope: Risk mitigation disclosures; Risk Management Committee (Reg 21) and BRSR/BRSR Core ESG metrics

Why it matters

Risk disclosures tell you what management believes can go wrong; the Risk Management Committee (RMC) disclosures tell you whether the board actually oversees those risks; and the Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR), especially the assured BRSR Core metrics, gives quantitative ESG data that lenders, global investors and supply-chain customers increasingly price. In a forensic review the risk sections are tested for specificity, consistency with KAMs and contingent liabilities, and whether risks that later materialised had been disclosed. For FP&A and modelling, quantified risk sensitivities become scenario inputs, and ESG metrics (emissions intensity, energy and water use) feed carbon cost and capex scenarios.

Core notes

Risk and mitigation disclosures in the MD&A

Schedule V Part B requires "risks and concerns" in the MD&A. Section 134(3)(n) requires the board's report to include a statement indicating development and implementation of a risk management policy, including identification of elements of risk which in the board's opinion may threaten the existence of the company. Section 177(4)(vii) makes evaluation of internal financial controls and risk management systems a function of the audit committee.

A high-quality risk disclosure has five features:

  1. Prioritisation — top risks ranked by likelihood and impact, not an alphabetical list.
  2. Quantification — sensitivities (for example, "a 1% change in USD/INR affects EBITDA by ₹42 crore; 60% of next-twelve-month exposure is hedged").
  3. Specific mitigation — named actions (hedging policy, dual sourcing, price escalation clauses, insurance), not "the company monitors the situation".
  4. Change commentary — which risks increased or decreased and why.
  5. Consistency — alignment with KAMs, contingent liabilities, Ind AS 107 financial risk disclosures (credit, liquidity, market risk) and the RMC's stated focus.

Risk Management Committee (LODR Reg 21)

Requirement Rule
Applicability Top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation, and high value debt listed entities
Composition Minimum three members, majority being members of the board, including at least one independent director; for entities with outstanding SR equity shares, at least two-thirds independent directors
Chairperson A member of the board; senior executives of the entity may be members
Meetings At least twice a year
Quorum Two members or one-third of members, whichever is higher, including at least one board member in attendance
Gap between meetings Not more than 210 consecutive days (amended in 2023; earlier 180 days)
Powers Seek information from any employee, obtain outside legal or professional advice

Role (Schedule II Part D, Part C): formulate a detailed risk management policy including a framework for identification of internal and external risks, specifically financial, operational, sectoral, sustainability (particularly ESG), information and cyber security risks; measures for risk mitigation including systems and processes for internal control; a business continuity plan; ensure appropriate methodology, processes and systems to monitor and evaluate risks; monitor and oversee implementation of the policy; periodically review the policy, at least once in two years; keep the board informed; and review the appointment, removal and terms of the Chief Risk Officer, if any.

The corporate governance report (Schedule V Part C) discloses RMC composition, meetings and attendance. Analytical tests: did the RMC meet only the minimum twice? Did members attend? Is a CRO appointed? Are the RMC's stated focus areas consistent with the MD&A's top risks and the auditor's KAMs?

BRSR and BRSR Core

BRSR was introduced by SEBI circular of 10 May 2021, replacing the Business Responsibility Report, and is mandatory under Reg 34(2)(f) for the top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation from FY 2022-23. It is structured around the nine principles of the National Guidelines on Responsible Business Conduct (NGRBC), with three sections:

  • Section A: general disclosures (operations, employees, CSR, transparency and grievance mechanisms).
  • Section B: management and process disclosures (policies, governance, oversight).
  • Section C: principle-wise performance, with essential (mandatory) and leadership (voluntary) indicators.

BRSR Core (SEBI circular of 12 July 2023) is a subset of key performance indicators with assurance requirements, covering nine attributes:

Attribute Example KPIs
GHG footprint Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions; intensity per rupee of turnover
Water footprint Water consumption and withdrawal; intensity
Energy footprint Total energy consumed; share from renewable sources; intensity
Embracing circularity Waste generated by category; waste recovered or recycled
Employee wellbeing and safety Spending on wellbeing as % of total costs; LTIFR; fatalities
Gender diversity Wages paid to females as % of total wages; POSH complaints
Inclusive development Input material sourced from MSMEs/small producers and from within India; job creation in smaller towns
Fairness in engaging with customers and suppliers Data breaches; days of accounts payable
Open-ness of business Concentration of purchases and sales with trading houses, dealers and related parties; loans, advances and investments with related parties

The original glide path for reasonable assurance was top 150 listed entities from FY 2023-24, top 250 from FY 2024-25, top 500 from FY 2025-26 and top 1,000 from FY 2026-27. SEBI's March 2025 circular, following the Expert Committee's recommendations, replaced "reasonable assurance" with "assessment or assurance" and relaxed value-chain ESG disclosures (made voluntary for FY 2025-26 for the top 250, with assessment or assurance voluntary from FY 2026-27). This area changes frequently; verify the current circular before relying on dates.

Using BRSR in financial analysis

  • Openness attribute is a governance goldmine: concentration of sales through dealers and related parties, and related-party loans and investments as % of totals, cross-check the related party note.
  • Days payable (fairness attribute) can be reconciled with the balance sheet and with MSME payment disclosures (and the s.43B(h) Income-tax Act 45-day rule for MSME payments).
  • Emissions intensity feeds a carbon-cost scenario: \( \text{Carbon cost} = \text{Scope 1 emissions (tCO}_2\text{e)} \times \text{Assumed carbon price} \), relevant given India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme and the EU CBAM for exporters of steel, aluminium and cement.
  • Safety metrics (LTIFR, fatalities) are leading indicators of operational and reputational risk in heavy industry.

Illustration

A steel company's MD&A lists "raw material volatility" and "regulatory risk" generically. Its RMC met twice, 205 days apart, with one independent director. BRSR Core shows Scope 1 emissions of 21.4 million tCO2e with EBITDA of ₹9,600 crore and 18% of sales to EU customers. A carbon-cost scenario at ₹2,500 per tonne on the EU-linked share (assuming CBAM effectively prices that share) yields \( 21.4 \times 0.18 \times 2{,}500 \) ≈ ₹9,630 million, i.e. about ₹963 crore, or 10% of EBITDA — a risk the MD&A does not quantify. The finding: risk disclosure lacks specificity relative to a material, measurable exposure.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Market-cap thresholds move with annual rankings; an entity dropping out of the top 1,000 may still have obligations for a period under Reg 3(2). Verify current applicability rules.
  • Assurance versus assessment: an "assessment" by an assessor differs in scope from ICAI-standard assurance (SSAE 3000); read the provider's report for scope and exceptions.
  • Boundary differences: BRSR may be standalone while the financial statements are consolidated; intensity ratios then mix bases.
  • Greenwashing risk: leadership indicators are voluntary and unassured; do not rely on them without corroboration.
  • Cyber risk is now an explicit RMC area; incidents disclosed under Reg 30 (cyber security incidents) should be reflected in risk disclosures.

Key takeaways

  • Good risk disclosures are prioritised, quantified, specific on mitigation and consistent with KAMs and Ind AS 107.
  • Reg 21 RMC: top 1,000 listed entities, minimum three members, twice-yearly meetings, 210-day maximum gap, policy review every two years.
  • BRSR is mandatory for the top 1,000 under Reg 34(2)(f); BRSR Core's nine attributes carry assessment or assurance on a glide path that has been revised.
  • BRSR openness and fairness attributes provide governance cross-checks on related parties and payables.
  • Turn emissions and other ESG metrics into explicit scenario inputs where economically material.

Practice Questions

Q1. Reg 21 compliance check [L1]

A top-500 listed company's RMC has four members: the MD (chair), the CFO, the CRO and one independent director. It met on 12 May 2024 and 20 December 2024 (FY 2024-25). Evaluate compliance with Reg 21 composition, meeting frequency and gap requirements.

💡 Hint: Count how many members are directors.

Q2. Score a risk disclosure [L2]

Design a five-criterion scoring rubric (0–2 points each) for MD&A risk disclosures and apply it to this excerpt: "The company is exposed to foreign exchange risk, commodity price risk, interest rate risk and competition. The company has a robust risk management framework and monitors these risks regularly." State the score and the specific improvements you would expect.

Q3. BRSR Core cross-check [L2]

BRSR Core reports that 34% of sales are through dealers and distributors, 11% to related parties, and that loans and advances to related parties are 46% of total loans and advances. The Ind AS 24 note shows sales to related parties of ₹420 crore against revenue of ₹6,100 crore, and loans to related parties of ₹185 crore against total loans and advances of ₹310 crore. Reconcile the two disclosures and list the possible explanations for any difference.

Q4. Carbon scenario [L3]

A cement company with clinker capacity of 30 MTPA reports Scope 1 emissions of 0.62 tCO2e per tonne of cement and production of 34 million tonnes, EBITDA ₹5,800 crore. Build a sensitivity table of carbon cost as % of EBITDA at carbon prices of ₹500, ₹1,500 and ₹3,000 per tonne and coverage shares of 25%, 50% and 100% of emissions, and explain how you would present this in a credit memo.
