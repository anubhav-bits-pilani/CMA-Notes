Subtopic note

Capacity Expansion Plans: Capex Guidance, Funding and CWIP Ageing

Scope: Capacity expansion plans: capex guidance, funding mix, commissioning timelines vs CWIP ageing

Why it matters

Capacity expansion is where MD&A narrative most directly drives valuation. A cement company announcing 10 million tonnes of new capacity, a specialty chemicals company guiding ₹2,000 crore capex, or a PLI beneficiary promising commissioning "by Q3" all feed volume, capex and depreciation lines in a forecast. The risk is that announced plans slip, overrun, or are funded in ways that strain the balance sheet. Since the 2021 Schedule III amendments, CWIP ageing schedules let you test commissioning claims against what has actually been sitting in CWIP, and the cash flow statement shows how the capex was really funded. A credible capex story is consistent across MD&A, investor presentations, CWIP ageing, borrowing notes and the use-of-proceeds statements.

Core notes

Capex guidance: what to capture

For each announced project, record in a capex tracker:

Field Source
Project description, location, capacity (units, MTPA, MW) MD&A, investor presentation, Reg 30 board approval disclosure
Total estimated cost and phasing Board approval, earnings call
Expected commissioning date (original and revised) MD&A, presentations over time
Cumulative spend to date CWIP note, cash flow (purchase of PPE), capital advances
Funding plan (internal accruals, debt, equity, grants/subsidies) MD&A, borrowing note, offer documents
Expected asset turnover and payback Presentations, calls
Status of approvals (environmental clearance, land, power linkage) MD&A, Reg 30 updates

Reg 30 read with Schedule III Part B requires disclosure of capacity addition or product launch and of the commencement of commercial production; these filings give you dated milestones to compare with guidance.

Funding mix

Typical sources and what to verify:

  • Internal accruals: compare cumulative CFO less dividends and maintenance capex with growth capex. If accruals are claimed but net debt rises, the claim is false.
  • Term debt: project loans, NCDs, ECBs. Check sanction versus drawdown in the borrowings note, repayment schedule versus project cash flow ramp-up, and interest capitalised under Ind AS 23.
  • Equity: QIP, rights issue, preferential allotment, IPO. Under LODR Reg 32, listed entities file a quarterly statement of deviation or variation in the use of issue proceeds, reviewed by the audit committee; where ICDR requires a monitoring agency, its report is filed quarterly. Proceeds parked in deposits for long periods suggest delayed execution.
  • Grants and incentives: state capital subsidies and PLI incentives (Ind AS 20 grant accounting, often deducted from asset cost or shown as deferred income).
  • Short-term funds: CARO 3(ix)(d) reports short-term funds used for long-term purposes, a sign the funding plan failed.

\[ \text{Funding gap} = \text{Capex}_{t} - (\text{CFO}_{t} - \text{Dividends}_{t} - \text{Interest paid}_{t}) - \text{Equity raised}_{t} \]

A positive gap must be financed by debt or working capital; compare with the change in net debt.

Commissioning timelines versus CWIP ageing

Schedule III (Division II) requires a CWIP ageing schedule split into projects in progress and projects temporarily suspended, with buckets: less than 1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years, more than 3 years. For CWIP whose completion is overdue or has exceeded its cost compared to its original plan, a CWIP completion schedule must show, project-wise, the amounts expected to be completed in less than 1 year, 1–2, 2–3 and more than 3 years. Equivalent schedules apply to intangible assets under development.

Tests to perform:

  1. Guidance versus ageing: a project guided to commission "this year" while its spend sits in the 2–3 year bucket has already slipped.
  2. Completion schedule versus MD&A: the completion schedule's expected year should match the MD&A's timeline; differences need explanation.
  3. Overrun: compare current estimated cost (presentations) with board-approved cost; Schedule III requires overrun projects to appear in the completion schedule.
  4. Suspended projects: spend under "temporarily suspended" means no active development; Ind AS 23.20 requires suspension of borrowing cost capitalisation during extended periods without active development, and an impairment review under Ind AS 36 may be needed.
  5. Capitalisation timing: under Ind AS 16.20, cost recognition stops when the asset is in the location and condition necessary to operate as intended. A plant that has started trial production but remains in CWIP may be hiding depreciation and interest (see the earnings quality topic). Proceeds from selling items produced before intended use go to profit or loss, not cost (Ind AS 16.20A).

Illustration

A specialty chemicals company's FY 2023-24 MD&A guided commissioning of a ₹1,200 crore multipurpose plant by March 2025, funded 60% by internal accruals and 40% by debt. At 31 March 2025: CWIP ₹1,050 crore, of which ₹680 crore in the 1–2 year bucket and ₹220 crore in 2–3 years; completion schedule shows ₹1,050 crore expected in "less than 1 year"; current estimated cost ₹1,450 crore. FY 2024-25 CFO ₹310 crore, dividends ₹60 crore, capex ₹520 crore; net debt up ₹390 crore. Findings: commissioning has slipped by at least a year; cost overrun 21%; funding gap was financed largely by debt (debt share far above 40%); and borrowing costs capitalised need checking. The forecast should push commissioning to FY 2025-26 H2, ramp utilisation over two years, and model the higher debt.

Model implementation

In a three-statement model, link a capex schedule by project: spend phasing → CWIP roll-forward (opening + additions − capitalisation = closing) → capitalisation date → depreciation start → capacity and volume ramp. Flag: CWIP closing balance older than two years with no capitalisation; capex funded share by source versus guidance.

=IF(AND(Project_CWIP>0, YEARFRAC(First_Spend_Date, BS_Date)>2, Capitalised_This_Year=0), "AGEING FLAG", "")

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Brownfield versus greenfield: debottlenecking capex is commissioned in phases and may be capitalised piecemeal; greenfield projects have longer gestation and higher slippage risk.
  • Capital advances to vendors are presented under other non-current assets, not CWIP, and are outside the ageing schedule; large, ageing capital advances can be a parking place for funds (see related party topic).
  • Regulatory delays (environmental clearance, land acquisition) are legitimate, but repeated revisions without disclosure of the cause are not.
  • PLI timelines are tied to incentive eligibility; slippage can cost incentives, which should be reflected in forecasts.
  • Group-level capex in subsidiaries appears only in consolidated CWIP; standalone analysis misses it.

Key takeaways

  • Build a project-wise capex tracker from MD&A, presentations and Reg 30 filings, updating original versus revised dates and costs.
  • Verify the funding mix against cash flow, net debt change, Reg 32 use-of-proceeds statements and CARO 3(ix)(d).
  • Use the Schedule III CWIP ageing and completion schedules to test commissioning claims and cost overruns.
  • Check capitalisation timing (Ind AS 16.20) and borrowing cost capitalisation (Ind AS 23) for projects that appear complete.
  • Translate slippage and overruns directly into the model's capitalisation, depreciation and volume ramp.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ageing versus guidance [L1]

A company guided in May 2024 that its new line would commission in Q2 FY 2024-25. The 31 March 2025 CWIP ageing shows the project's ₹340 crore as: less than 1 year ₹60 crore, 1–2 years ₹190 crore, 2–3 years ₹90 crore; the completion schedule shows "1–2 years". State what each data point implies about the timeline.

Q2. Funding mix test [L2]

Over FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 a company guided ₹1,800 crore capex funded "largely from internal accruals". Combined data (₹ crore): CFO 1,120; dividends 180; interest paid 140; capex 1,760; QIP proceeds 0; net debt increased 960. Compute the funding gap and the actual funding mix, and draft one sentence for an analyst note on the credibility of the guidance.

💡 Hint: Interest paid is a committed outflow before discretionary capex.

Q3. Capitalisation timing [L2]

A cement company began trial runs of a new clinker line in November 2024 and sold 0.4 million tonnes of clinker produced during trials for ₹180 crore before declaring commercial operation on 1 May 2025. The plant cost of ₹2,100 crore remained in CWIP at 31 March 2025. Explain the accounting for trial-run sale proceeds and costs under Ind AS 16 as amended, whether the plant should have been capitalised before 31 March 2025, and estimate the depreciation and interest effect if the plant was ready for intended use on 1 January 2025 (useful life 30 years, straight line, nil residual; borrowing cost capitalised at ₹14 crore per month).

Q4. Multi-source triangulation [L3]

Design a two-year capex triangulation table for a listed company covering MD&A guidance, investor presentation figures, Reg 30 capacity commissioning announcements, CWIP ageing, capital advances, Reg 32 use-of-proceeds statement and CARO 3(ix)(c)/(d). Describe the columns, the consistency checks and how you would grade inconsistencies.
