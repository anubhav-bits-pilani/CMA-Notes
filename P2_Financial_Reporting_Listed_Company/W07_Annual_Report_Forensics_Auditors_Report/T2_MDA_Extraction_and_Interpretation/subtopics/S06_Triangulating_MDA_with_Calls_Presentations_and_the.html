Subtopic note

Triangulating MD&A with Calls, Presentations and the Board's Report

Scope: Triangulation with earnings call transcripts, investor presentations, directors' report (s.134)

Why it matters

The annual MD&A is a once-a-year, legally vetted document. Earnings calls and investor presentations are quarterly, more specific and less vetted; the board's report under s.134 is statutory and carries specific declarations. Each source has different incentives: presentations sell, calls answer analyst pressure, the MD&A balances legal caution with narrative, and the board's report must disclose specified facts. Triangulating them exposes inconsistencies (a capex number that differs by ₹500 crore between documents, a risk discussed on the call but absent from the MD&A), tests guidance credibility, and gives the most specific forecast inputs. Analysts who rely on one source inherit its bias.

Core notes

The sources and what each contributes

Source Legal basis Strengths Weaknesses
MD&A LODR Reg 34(2)(e), Schedule V Part B Mandatory coverage; annual; read by auditor under SA 720 for material inconsistency Often generic; annual lag
Earnings call transcript LODR Reg 30, Schedule III Part A Para A item 15; Reg 46(2) Specific guidance, Q&A pressure, management tone Unaudited; selective; verbal hedging
Investor presentation Reg 30 Schedule III Part A item 15 (presentations to analysts/institutional investors) KPIs, segment data, capex tables, operating metrics Promotional; definitions change
Board's report Companies Act s.134(3), Rule 8 of Companies (Accounts) Rules Statutory facts: loans/guarantees, RPTs (AOC-2), material changes after year-end, risk policy, IFC, regulatory orders Formulaic; often cross-refers
Quarterly results LODR Reg 33 Audited or reviewed numbers; segment results Limited narrative

Under Reg 30 read with Schedule III Part A Para A item 15, the schedule of analyst or institutional investor meets and the presentations made must be disclosed to stock exchanges; audio or video recordings of post-earnings or quarterly calls must be made available on the website and to the exchanges within 24 hours or before the next trading day, whichever is earlier, and transcripts within five working days of the call.

A triangulation workflow

  1. Build a claims register: for each quantitative claim (volume growth, margin, capex, capacity, debt target, working capital days), capture source, date, page or timestamp, exact quote, and value.
  2. Match across sources: the same metric from MD&A, presentation and call in the same quarter. Differences in definitions (for example, EBITDA with or without other income; net debt with or without lease liabilities) must be resolved before comparing.
  3. Test against reported numbers: Reg 33 quarterly results and annual financial statements.
  4. Score consistency: consistent, explained difference, unexplained difference, contradiction.
  5. Track tone and language: words such as "robust", "headwinds", "transitory", and the frequency of hedging phrases. A shift from specific numbers to qualitative language is a signal.
  6. Log omissions: topics raised by analysts on the call but absent from the MD&A (for example, a customer loss or a pricing dispute).

Tools

Transcripts can be loaded into Excel or Power Query as text; a simple keyword count by quarter (for example, "delay", "pressure", "one-off") tracks tone. In Power BI, a claims register table with source, metric, period and value supports a matrix of claim versus actual by quarter.

= Table.AddColumn(Transcripts, "DelayMentions", each List.Count(Text.PositionOf(Text.Lower([Text]), "delay", Occurrence.All)))

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Selective disclosure concerns: information given on calls but not in filings may raise SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations fair disclosure issues; transcripts are therefore part of the public record and quotable.
  • Definitions drift: presentations often move from "EBITDA" to "adjusted EBITDA"; reconcile to the P&L every time.
  • Consolidated versus standalone: presentations are usually consolidated; the board's report may discuss standalone.
  • Translation and paraphrase: transcripts are edited for clarity; for contentious points, check the audio.
  • Silence as information: a company that stops hosting calls or stops giving a metric it previously guided is signalling something.

Key takeaways

  • Each source has different legal status and incentives; triangulate rather than rely on one.
  • Reg 30 requires disclosure of analyst meet presentations, call recordings within 24 hours and transcripts within five working days.
  • The board's report under s.134(3) and Rule 8(5) supplies statutory facts that test MD&A narrative.
  • Use a claims register with source references, reconcile definitions, and score consistency.
  • Omissions and tone shifts across sources are as informative as numerical contradictions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Source mapping [L1]

For each of the following, identify the primary and a secondary source where you would expect to find it: (a) material changes after year-end affecting financial position; (b) quarterly volume guidance; (c) details of loans given under s.186; (d) management's explanation of an audit qualification; (e) energy consumption from renewable sources.

Q2. Reconcile EBITDA definitions [L2]

An investor presentation reports "EBITDA ₹1,210 crore, margin 19.8%" for FY 2024-25. The P&L shows revenue from operations ₹6,110 crore, other income ₹145 crore, total expenses ₹5,320 crore including finance cost ₹180 crore and depreciation ₹260 crore, and exceptional loss ₹40 crore shown below. Compute reported EBITDA with and without other income, identify which definition the presentation likely uses, and comment on the margin denominator.

💡 Hint: Check whether the margin uses revenue from operations or total income.

Q3. Claims register exercise [L2]

Using the most recent four quarterly calls and the latest annual report of an NSE-listed company of your choice, build a claims register with at least ten quantitative claims, each with source, date and page or timestamp, and classify each as consistent, explained difference, unexplained difference or contradiction against reported numbers.

Q4. Omission analysis [L3]

On the Q4 FY 2024-25 call, management of a listed pharma company disclosed that an import alert at a plant affects products contributing 9% of US revenue. The annual report released two months later contains no mention in the MD&A, but the board's report under Rule 8(5)(vii) states "no significant orders impacting going concern". Evaluate whether the omission is defensible, which disclosure requirements are relevant (including Reg 30), and how you would grade it in a governance assessment.
