Subtopic note

Schedule V Part B: What an Indian MD&A Must Contain

Scope: LODR Schedule V Part B MD&A contents: industry structure, opportunities/threats, segment performance, outlook, risks, internal controls

Why it matters

The Management Discussion and Analysis is the only mandatory part of an Indian annual report where management explains, in narrative form, why the numbers moved and what it expects next. For a forecaster it is the source of driver assumptions (volume growth, realisation, capacity, cost inflation); for a credit analyst it is where management commits to a view of industry risks; for a forensic reviewer it is the baseline against which later outcomes are tested. Indian MD&As vary from rigorous to promotional; knowing the mandatory content lets you identify what has been omitted as well as what has been said.

Core notes

Legal basis

LODR Reg 34(2)(e) requires the annual report of a listed entity with listed specified securities to contain the MD&A, either as part of the directors' report or in addition to it, and Reg 34(3) requires the annual report to contain the disclosures in Schedule V. Schedule V Part B prescribes the MD&A content. The MD&A must be within the limits set by the company's competitive position.

The mandatory elements

Item in Schedule V Part B What management should cover How an analyst uses it
Industry structure and developments Market size, growth, competitive structure, regulation, demand drivers Top-down market model inputs; check against industry data (CMIE, trade bodies, CRISIL)
Opportunities and threats Demand tailwinds, policy changes (PLI, import duties), new entrants, technology Scenario design; identify threats management later blames
Segment-wise or product-wise performance Revenue, margin and volume commentary by segment Reconcile to Ind AS 108 note; build segment model
Outlook Directional guidance on demand, margins, capex Forecast driver baseline; later used for credibility scoring
Risks and concerns Key business, financial, regulatory and ESG risks Risk register; compare with RMC disclosures and KAMs
Internal control systems and their adequacy Description of control environment, internal audit Compare with IFC opinion and CARO 3(xiv)
Discussion on financial performance with respect to operational performance Bridge from operating metrics to financial results Volume–price–mix analysis; margin bridge
Material developments in human resources / industrial relations, including number of people employed Headcount, attrition, wage settlements, strikes Employee cost per head; capacity utilisation
Details of significant changes (25% or more) in key financial ratios with explanations Debtors turnover, inventory turnover, interest coverage, current ratio, debt-equity, operating profit margin, net profit margin, or sector-specific equivalents Verify computation; test explanations (s02)
Change in return on net worth with explanation RONW movement versus previous year DuPont decomposition (s02)

Schedule V Part B also requires disclosure of accounting treatment: where, in the preparation of financial statements, a treatment different from that prescribed in an accounting standard has been followed, the fact must be disclosed in the MD&A with management's explanation of why it believes the alternative treatment is more representative of the underlying transactions. Under Ind AS this is rare (most departures would draw an audit modification), so any such disclosure deserves immediate attention.

Schedule V Part B applies to listed entities; banks have additional disclosure requirements under RBI directions, and some content (for example, segment commentary) overlaps with the corporate governance report (Part C) and the directors' report (s.134).

Reading each element critically

Industry structure. Look for quantification (market size in ₹ crore or tonnes, share, capacity utilisation of the industry). Purely descriptive text taken from an industry report adds little; note the source and date. A change of source year-on-year can hide a change in market definition that flatters market share.

Opportunities and threats. The most useful test is retrospective: did last year's "threats" materialise, and did management say so? Threats that disappear without comment, or new threats that appear only after results disappoint, indicate narrative management.

Segment performance. The MD&A segments must be consistent with Ind AS 108 operating segments (the chief operating decision maker's view). Product-wise commentary that cuts across reportable segments is permitted, but check that sub-segment numbers add up to the reported segment.

Outlook. Indian MD&As are often vague ("we remain cautiously optimistic"). Where numbers appear (volume growth, EBITDA margin band, capex), record them in a guidance tracker; earnings calls usually carry more specific guidance.

Risks and concerns. A good disclosure ranks risks, quantifies sensitivity (for example, "₹1/kg change in PVC resin price affects EBITDA by ₹18 crore") and names the mitigation. A list of twenty generic risks without prioritisation is a weak disclosure.

Internal controls. Compare the adjectives used ("robust", "commensurate") with the IFC opinion, CARO 3(xiv) on internal audit, and any audit committee commentary. A strong MD&A statement alongside a qualified IFC opinion is a contradiction to log.

Illustration

A paints company's MD&A states: "Industry grew ~7% in volume; we grew 11% in volume and 6% in value due to a shift to economy emulsions; raw material index fell 4%; we expect high single-digit volume growth and EBITDA margin of 18–20%." The segment note shows decorative paints revenue up 6% to ₹9,400 crore and segment result up 3%. The analyst records: volume 11%, value 6% implying realisation/mix decline of about 4.5% (\( \frac{1.06}{1.11} - 1 \)); segment margin decline despite raw material deflation, which implies higher advertising or dealer incentives; guidance band 18–20% to test next year.

Building an MD&A extraction template

In Excel, maintain a sheet with one row per year and columns for each Schedule V Part B element: quantitative claims (number, unit, page), qualitative claims (text snippet), the corresponding financial statement number, and a "tested?" flag. Use structured references so that a Power BI matrix can compare claims and outcomes across years and peers.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Integrated annual reports may scatter MD&A content across "value creation" and "capitals" sections; the MD&A heading may be short while substance sits elsewhere. Map it before concluding a disclosure is missing.
  • Consolidated versus standalone. MD&A often discusses the consolidated business while ratio disclosures are standalone; check which basis each statement refers to.
  • Holding companies may give little segment commentary; read subsidiaries' annual reports where material.
  • Competitive-harm defence. Management may withhold detail citing competitive position; that is permitted but should not extend to omitting known material risks.
  • Forward-looking statements disclaimers do not reduce the analyst's need to test outlook statements against outcomes.

Key takeaways

  • MD&A is mandatory under Reg 34(2)(e) and Reg 34(3), with contents in Schedule V Part B.
  • Required elements: industry structure, opportunities and threats, segment performance, outlook, risks, internal controls, financial versus operational performance, HR, ratio changes of 25% or more, and change in RONW.
  • Any disclosed departure from an accounting standard must be explained in the MD&A and is a priority item.
  • Reconcile segment commentary with Ind AS 108 and control statements with the IFC opinion.
  • Convert quantitative outlook statements into a tracker for later credibility testing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mandatory content checklist [L1]

List each element of Schedule V Part B and, for a company you follow, identify the page number where each appears in its latest annual report. Mark any element that is missing or covered only in boilerplate.

Q2. Volume–value decomposition [L2]

An FMCG company's MD&A states: "Revenue grew 9.2% to ₹14,860 crore on underlying volume growth of 4%." Gross margin moved from 51.4% to 53.1%, and the company states it took price increases of about 3% in H1 and price cuts of about 1% in Q4. Decompose revenue growth into volume and price/mix, and evaluate whether the gross margin movement is consistent with the stated pricing actions, assuming raw material costs per unit fell 2%.

💡 Hint: Use multiplicative decomposition: \( (1 + g) = (1 + v)(1 + p) \).

Q3. Control statement contradiction [L2]

The MD&A of a listed auto component company states that "internal control systems are commensurate with the size and nature of business and operating effectively", while the IFC opinion is qualified for controls over inventory costing and CARO 3(xiv) states internal audit reports were not available for two plants. Draft the paragraph you would include in a governance assessment.

Q4. Departure from an accounting standard [L3]

An MD&A discloses that the company has continued to recognise revenue from long-term maintenance contracts on billing rather than on an Ind AS 115 basis "as this is more representative for customers", and the auditor's report contains an EoM on this point. Evaluate whether the auditor's reporting appears appropriate, what you would expect in the basis for opinion paragraph, and how you would adjust revenue in a model if annual billing is ₹260 crore against an estimated Ind AS 115 revenue of ₹215 crore.
