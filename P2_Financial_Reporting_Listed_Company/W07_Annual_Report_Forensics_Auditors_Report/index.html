Refresh before starting

SA reporting structure and CARO clause map

SA 700/701/705/706/570/720 reporting structure and CARO 2020 clause map from CMA audit

Recall the report order for a listed company: Opinion → Basis for Opinion → Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern (SA 570) → Emphasis of Matter (SA 706) → Key Audit Matters (SA 701) → Other Information (SA 720: auditor reads the annual report for material inconsistencies) → management and auditor responsibilities → Other Matter (SA 706) → Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements (s.143(3), Rule 11, s.197(16)), with Annexure A (CARO) and Annexure B (IFC). SA 705 matrix: misstatement material-not-pervasive = qualified, pervasive = adverse; scope limitation material-not-pervasive = qualified, pervasive = disclaimer.

CARO 2020 para 3 clause map (21 clauses): (i) PPE and intangibles, title deeds; (ii) inventory verification and working capital returns; (iii) investments, guarantees, loans and advances; (iv) s.185/186 compliance; (v) deposits; (vi) cost records; (vii) statutory dues, undisputed and disputed; (viii) unrecorded income surrendered in tax assessments; (ix) defaults, wilful defaulter, diversion, short-term for long-term, funding of group obligations, pledge of group securities; (x) IPO/FPO and preferential allotment proceeds; (xi) fraud and whistle-blower complaints; (xii) Nidhi; (xiii) RPT compliance with s.177 and s.188; (xiv) internal audit; (xv) non-cash transactions with directors; (xvi) RBI registration and CICs; (xvii) cash losses; (xviii) auditor resignation; (xix) capacity to meet liabilities within one year; (xx) CSR unspent amounts; (xxi) CARO remarks of group companies in consolidated statements.

Ind AS 115, 37, 24 and 109 recognition rules

Ind AS 115, 37, 24 and 109 (financial guarantees, ECL) recognition rules

Ind AS 115: five steps (contract, performance obligations, transaction price including constrained variable consideration, allocation on relative stand-alone selling prices, recognition when or as control transfers); over-time criteria in 115.35; bill-and-hold B79–B82; principal versus agent B34–B38; Indian addition 115.126AA (reconciliation of contract price to revenue). Ind AS 37: provision when present obligation, probable outflow and reliable estimate (37.14); contingent liability disclosed unless outflow remote (37.86); contingent assets disclosed only when inflow probable; provisions reviewed each reporting date (37.59) with movement table (37.84). Ind AS 24: related party definition (persons and close family members with control, joint control, significant influence or KMP status; group entities, associates, JVs, post-employment plans, entities controlled by such persons, and entities providing KMP services); disclosure of KMP compensation (24.17) and transactions and balances (24.18). Ind AS 109: financial guarantee contracts initially at fair value; subsequently at the higher of the ECL allowance and the amount initially recognised less cumulative income recognised under Ind AS 115 (109.4.2.1(c)); ECL general three-stage approach (12-month ECL for stage 1, lifetime for stages 2 and 3), simplified lifetime approach for trade receivables and contract assets (109.5.5.15).

LODR regulations and Schedule V

LODR Reg 23, 30, 33, 34 and Schedule V basics

Reg 23: all RPTs need prior approval of the audit committee (only independent directors vote); omnibus approval valid for one year; material RPT = individually or cumulatively in a year exceeding ₹1,000 crore or 10% of annual consolidated turnover, whichever is lower (5% for brand usage/royalty under Reg 23(1A)), requiring shareholder approval with related parties not voting in favour; half-yearly RPT disclosure filed with results. Reg 30: disclosure of material events — Schedule III Part A deemed material (including change in auditor and KMP, auditor resignation reasons, analyst meet schedules and call recordings/transcripts, defaults, fraud), Part B subject to materiality (threshold is the lower of 2% of turnover, 2% of net worth, or 5% of three-year average absolute PAT); generally within 24 hours (30 minutes for board-meeting outcomes; 12 hours for events originating within the entity). Reg 33: quarterly results within 45 days of quarter-end and annual audited results within 60 days; limited review; half-yearly balance sheet and cash flow; Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications for modified opinions. Reg 34: annual report contents (audited and consolidated statements, cash flow, directors' report, MD&A, BRSR for top 1,000). Schedule V: Part A RPT disclosures, Part B MD&A, Part C corporate governance report, Part D CEO declaration, Part E compliance certificate, Part F demat suspense account.

Cross-cutting concepts

Triangulation across documents

Triangulation: auditor's report, notes, MD&A, CARO and quarterly results must tell one consistent story

No single document is reliable on its own: the auditor's opinion is a pass/fail judgement at a materiality level, the notes are management's disclosures, the MD&A is narrative, CARO is a set of facts at the standalone level, and quarterly results show timing. Triangulation means testing each claim against at least two other sources. Examples: a KAM on receivable recoverability should match a rising receivables ageing bucket in the notes, an ECL movement, an MD&A explanation for a debtors-turnover change and perhaps a CARO 3(ii)(b) stock-statement mismatch; a contingent liability note should reconcile to CARO 3(vii)(b) disputed dues and to Rule 11(a) litigation statements; an MD&A commissioning claim should agree with the CWIP ageing and completion schedules and Reg 30 commercial-production announcements; a Q4 revenue spike in Reg 33 results should be visible in year-end receivables. When sources disagree, the inconsistency itself is a finding — log it with references and ask which document is wrong. Build a cross-reference matrix (rows = findings, columns = documents) in the workbook so gaps are visible.

Governance signals precede accounting signals

Governance risk often precedes accounting risk; RPT growth, pledges and auditor exits are early signals

In most Indian collapses the governance indicators moved before the accounting numbers broke: related party transactions growing faster than revenue, ICDs to promoter entities, rising promoter pledges or non-disposal undertakings (NDUs) as promoters borrowed against shares, mid-term auditor resignations, CFO or company secretary exits, delayed results, and boards dominated by long-tenured "independent" directors. Accounting red flags (DSO creep, falling CFO/PAT, ageing CWIP) often follow because stressed promoters need reported profit to sustain share prices that collateralise their borrowings. The practical rule: when governance signals cluster, lower your threshold for accounting red flags and apply more scrutiny to related-party and capital-advance balances. Sources include Reg 31 shareholding patterns and SAST Reg 31 encumbrance disclosures (pledge trends), Reg 23 half-yearly RPT disclosures, Reg 30 filings (auditor and KMP changes), the corporate governance report (attendance, tenure) and proxy-advisor reports.

Severity grading of findings

Severity grading of findings: quantum vs net worth/EBITDA, recurrence, management response

A memo listing thirty findings without grading is unusable. Grade each finding on three axes. Quantum: express the potential impact as a % of net worth and of EBITDA (and of PAT where relevant); a working scale is low below 2% of net worth or 5% of EBITDA, medium at 2–5% of net worth or 5–20% of EBITDA, and high above 5% of net worth or 20% of EBITDA — adjust for leverage, since the same quantum is more serious for a company at 4x net debt/EBITDA. Recurrence: a finding repeated for two or more years, or growing, moves up one grade; a one-off with a clear resolution moves down. Management response: a specific, verifiable response (provision made, reconciliation published, policy changed) moves down; a generic or absent response, or a response contradicted by later events, moves up. Combine into Low/Medium/High/Critical, with "Critical" reserved for findings that threaten solvency, indicate likely misstatement or fraud, or involve governance failure (auditor disclaimer, fund diversion). Document the grading logic in the workbook so another analyst would reach the same grade.

Exercise components across two years

Exercise: build a KAM log, contingent liability table (with probability view), RPT outflow table and M-score for 2 years

The four schedules are the core of the forensic workbook and each covers FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 so that change, not just level, is visible. The KAM log (topic 1) records each KAM with note references, quantum, key assumptions, auditor procedures and status (new/continuing/dropped). The contingent liability table (topic 4) lists each claim, guarantee and commitment with forum, stage, amount including interest and penalty, pre-deposit, and an assessed loss probability, producing a probability-weighted exposure expressed against net worth and EBITDA. The RPT outflow table (topic 5) captures purchases, royalties, management fees, ICDs and loans given, guarantees and capital advances to related parties, as % of revenue and EBITDA, tested against Reg 23 materiality and s.186(7) interest requirements. The M-score (topic 6) computes the eight Beneish variables for both years with defensible Indian proxies and threshold flags at −1.78 and −2.22. Each schedule feeds a single red-flag summary sheet.

Referencing every finding

Cite annual report page/note references for every finding

A forensic finding without a reference is an opinion. Every row in every schedule and every sentence in the memo must cite the source: document (annual report FY 2024-25, Q3 results, earnings call transcript), page number, note number and, where useful, the paragraph or table line (for example, "AR FY25 p. 214, Note 38(b)"; "Q4 FY25 call transcript p. 9"). References allow a reviewer to verify the number quickly, prevent transcription errors from propagating, and are essential when findings are challenged by management or investor relations. In the workbook, keep a "Sources" sheet listing each document with its date and URL, and a reference column in every schedule; in Power BI or a dashboard, display the reference as a tooltip. In the memo, use footnote-style references so the one-page limit is respected.

Hands-on exercise brief

Exercise: Conduct a forensic annual report review of an Indian mid-cap/large-cap company: extract all KAMs, contingent liabilities, related-party outflows, and compute Beneish M-Score.

Company selection and sources

Choose one NSE-listed non-financial company with market capitalisation above ₹5,000 crore (banks, NBFCs and insurers are excluded because the M-score and Z-score do not apply). Prefer a company with at least one of: a KAM on revenue or receivables, contingent liabilities above 10% of net worth, or related party transactions above 5% of revenue. Collect: annual reports for FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 (standalone and consolidated), Reg 33 quarterly results for the last eight quarters, two most recent earnings call transcripts and investor presentations, Reg 30 filings for the last two years (auditor/KMP changes, credit ratings), shareholding patterns (promoter pledge), and the BRSR.

  1. Cover: company, period, analyst, version, overall severity, summary of top five findings.
  2. Sources: document register with dates and URLs; reference codes used elsewhere.
  3. Inputs: Schedule III balance sheet, P&L and cash flow for two years (and a third year where needed for averages), in ₹ crore, with sign conventions documented.

  6. CL_Table: every contingent liability, guarantee and commitment, with interest and penalty separately, pre-deposits, forum, probability band and weighted exposure; reconciliation to CARO 3(vii)(b).

Deliverables

An Excel workbook with the tabs above (inputs in blue, formulas in black, checks returning TRUE/FALSE with a master check cell), and a one-page forensic memo (maximum 600 words plus a findings table) with a severity rating for each finding, an overall rating, and recommended actions (questions for management, model adjustments, monitoring triggers).
