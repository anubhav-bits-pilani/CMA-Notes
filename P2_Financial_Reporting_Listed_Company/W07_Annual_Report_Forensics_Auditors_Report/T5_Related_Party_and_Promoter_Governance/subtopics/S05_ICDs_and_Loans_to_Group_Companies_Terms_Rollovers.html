Subtopic note

ICDs and Loans to Group Companies: Terms, Rollovers and ECL

Scope: ICDs and loans to group companies: interest-free/below-market terms, rollovers, write-offs, ECL provisions on related-party loans

Why it matters

Loans and inter-corporate deposits to group and promoter companies are the most direct route for cash to leave a listed company. In the Coffee Day case, cash moved from subsidiaries to a promoter entity through intra-group transfers that were later found to be unrecoverable; in the 2025 SEBI order against Gensol, term loans raised for EV purchases were found to have been routed to promoter-related entities. The accounting tells you a lot before the fraud is found: interest-free or below-market terms create day-one fair value differences under Ind AS 109; repeated rollovers signal that the borrower cannot repay; and ECL provisions or write-offs eventually crystallise the loss.

Core notes

Accounting for below-market and interest-free loans (Ind AS 109)

A loan is a financial asset measured at fair value on initial recognition (Ind AS 109.5.1.1). If it carries a below-market rate, the transaction price is not fair value; fair value is the present value of contractual cash flows discounted at the market rate for a similar instrument (Ind AS 109.B5.1.1). The difference reflects the substance of the relationship:

Lender → borrower Debit for the day-one difference
Parent → subsidiary Investment in subsidiary (deemed capital contribution)
Subsidiary → parent Equity (deemed distribution) in the subsidiary's books
Company → promoter entity or fellow subsidiary Equity (distribution to the common controlling party) or P&L, per policy; it is not an asset

Subsequently the loan is measured at amortised cost with interest income at the effective (market) rate, so P&L shows "notional interest income" that will never be received in cash beyond the contractual coupon. Repayable-on- demand loans have fair value approximately equal to the face amount, which is why groups often document loans as "repayable on demand" — avoiding any day-one charge while the loan is in practice never demanded.

Illustration: a listed company gives a ₹100 crore, 3-year interest-free ICD to a promoter-owned company on 1 April 2024. Market rate for the borrower: 12%.

  • Fair value = \( 100 \div 1.12^{3} = 71.18 \); day-one difference ₹28.82 crore.
  • Entry: Dr Loan ₹71.18 crore, Dr Equity (deemed distribution) ₹28.82 crore, Cr Bank ₹100 crore.
  • FY 2024-25 interest income at 12% = ₹8.54 crore (non-cash), loan carrying ₹79.72 crore.

The deemed-distribution debit to equity shows the value transfer directly. Where companies instead keep the loan at face value without fair valuing, they understate the transfer and later surprise with a large ECL.

Separately, s.186(7) prohibits loans at interest below the G-sec yield closest to tenor (c03) — an interest-free ICD to a body corporate breaches the Companies Act unless the lender falls within the s.186(11) exemptions (banks, NBFCs in the ordinary course, infrastructure companies and others specified).

Rollovers and modifications

When a loan is extended or renegotiated, Ind AS 109.5.4.3 requires assessing modification: if not substantial, the gross carrying amount is recalculated at the original EIR and a modification gain/loss recognised; if substantial (judged qualitatively, often supported by analogy to the 10% test used for liabilities), the old loan is derecognised and a new one recognised at fair value. More important analytically: a rollover is evidence of significant increase in credit risk if the borrower could not repay — moving the loan to Stage 2 (lifetime ECL) or Stage 3 (credit-impaired).

Forensic indicators:

  • ICDs disclosed as current year after year (always "repayable within 12 months", always rolled).
  • Interest accrued but not received, capitalised into principal.
  • Repayment received just before year-end and re-lent shortly after (check quarterly balances in Reg 23(9) half-yearly disclosures and CARO 3(iii)(c)–(f)).
  • Loans to entities with negative net worth (check MCA filings of the borrower).
  • "Evergreening" through a third party: loan to Company X, which lends to the promoter entity.

ECL on related-party loans

Ind AS 109's general approach applies: 12-month ECL at Stage 1; lifetime ECL at Stage 2 (significant increase in credit risk) and Stage 3 (credit-impaired, interest on net carrying amount). For intra-group loans repayable on demand, ECL is based on whether the borrower has sufficient accessible liquid assets to repay if demanded; if not, expected recovery strategies (e.g. sale of assets over time) determine loss.

Companies often argue nil ECL for related-party loans "as the borrower is a group company supported by the promoter". That support must be evidenced (guarantee, collateral with realisable value). Ind AS 24.18(c)–(d) requires disclosure of ECL provisions on related-party balances and amounts written off during the period — a key line to extract.

Write-offs are recognised when there is no reasonable expectation of recovery (Ind AS 109.5.4.4). A write-off of a related-party loan after years of nil ECL indicates prior misstatement or a sudden event; check whether the write-off was presented as an exceptional item, which analysts tend to exclude from normalised earnings (they should not exclude the cash loss from valuation).

CARO cross-checks

  • 3(iii)(a)–(f): aggregate loans/advances/guarantees/security to subsidiaries, JVs, associates and others; whether terms are prejudicial; whether schedule of repayment stipulated and regular; overdue amounts above 90 days; loans renewed or extended or fresh loans granted to settle overdues of existing loans; loans repayable on demand or without specifying terms, with amounts and percentage to promoters and related parties.
  • 3(iv): compliance with s.185 and s.186.
  • 3(ix)(e)/(f): whether funds were taken from any entity to meet obligations of subsidiaries/associates/JVs, and loans raised on pledge of securities held in them.

Ratios

\[ \text{Related-party loan intensity} = \frac{\text{Loans and ICDs to related parties (gross)}}{\text{Net worth}} \]

\[ \text{Yield gap} = r_{\text{market}} - r_{\text{contract}}, \qquad \text{Recovery ratio} = \frac{\text{Repayments received in year}}{\text{Opening loans to related parties}} \]

A recovery ratio near zero with growing balances and nil ECL is the classic warning sign.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Loans to wholly-owned subsidiaries for capex are normal; the question is subsidiary viability, not the RPT label.
  • Advances for supplies or capital advances to related parties function as loans when no supply follows; CARO 3(iii) covers "advances in the nature of loans".
  • Security deposits (e.g. for leased premises from promoters) at amounts far above market rent are disguised loans.
  • Optionally convertible instruments issued by group companies may avoid ECL as FVTPL investments but carry the same risk.
  • Consolidation elimination: loans to subsidiaries vanish on consolidation; loans to promoter entities do not.

Key takeaways

  • Below-market and interest-free loans are recognised at fair value; the day-one difference is an investment for subsidiaries and a deemed distribution for upstream/sister entities.
  • Repayable-on-demand labels avoid day-one charges; check whether demand ever happens.
  • Rollovers, capitalised interest and year-end repayments signal credit deterioration and window dressing.
  • ECL on related-party loans must be evidence-based; nil ECL with poor borrower health is a red flag.
  • Reconcile with Ind AS 24.18, CARO 3(iii), 3(iv), 3(ix)(e)–(f) and s.186(7).

Practice Questions

Q1. Interest-free ICD accounting [L2]

On 1 April 2024, a listed company lends ₹250 crore for 4 years, interest-free, to a fellow subsidiary controlled by the promoter. The borrower's market rate is 11%. Pass the journal entries for initial recognition and FY 2024-25 interest, and state the s.186(7) position if the 4-year G-sec yield is 7.0%.

💡 Hint: The debit for the day-one difference depends on the direction of the relationship.

Q2. Rollover and staging [L2]

A company's ICD of ₹80 crore to a promoter-group company has been rolled over annually since FY 2020-21, with interest of ₹9 crore per year capitalised. The borrower's latest MCA filing shows negative net worth of ₹30 crore. Classify the ICD under the ECL stages, describe how you would estimate lifetime ECL, and draft the CARO 3(iii) reporting you would expect.

Q3. Recovery ratio analysis [L1]

Opening loans to related parties ₹600 crore; new loans in the year ₹350 crore; repayments ₹40 crore (all in the last week of March); closing ₹910 crore; ECL nil; net worth ₹2,500 crore. Compute related-party loan intensity and recovery ratio, and list three follow-up questions.

Q4. Case analysis [L3]

Using the publicly reported facts of the Gensol Engineering 2025 SEBI interim order (term loans for EV purchases allegedly diverted to related entities), describe which annual report disclosures, CARO clauses and ratios in this note could have flagged the issue before the order, and how you would have graded the severity.
