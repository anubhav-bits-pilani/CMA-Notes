Subtopic note

Ind AS 24 Related Party Identification and KMP Compensation

Scope: Ind AS 24 related party identification: promoter group, KMP and relatives, entities with significant influence; KMP compensation disclosure

Why it matters

Every related-party analysis starts with the list of related parties — and the list is where most leakage hides. If an entity controlled by the promoter's brother-in-law is not on the Ind AS 24 list, none of its purchases, loans or royalties appear in the related-party note, Reg 23 approvals or CARO cross-checks. Indian groups are typically promoter-controlled with layered holding companies, trusts and family members holding shares, so identification is a substantive judgement, not a formality. KMP compensation disclosure is the second element: it is the cleanest measure of what the promoter family extracts as salary and commission relative to minority dividends.

Core notes

The definition

A related party is a person or entity related to the reporting entity (Ind AS 24.9).

(a) A person or a close member of that person's family is related if that person:

  1. has control or joint control over the reporting entity;
  2. has significant influence over the reporting entity; or
  3. is a member of the key management personnel (KMP) of the reporting entity or of its parent.

(b) An entity is related if any of the following applies:

  1. it and the reporting entity are members of the same group (parent, subsidiaries and fellow subsidiaries);
  2. one is an associate or joint venture of the other (or of a member of a group of which the other is a member);
  3. both are joint ventures of the same third party;
  4. one is a JV of a third entity and the other is an associate of that third entity;
  5. it is a post-employment benefit plan for employees of the reporting entity or a related entity;
  6. it is controlled or jointly controlled by a person identified in (a);
  7. a person identified in (a)(1) has significant influence over it or is KMP of it (or of its parent);
  8. it, or any member of its group, provides KMP services to the reporting entity or its parent.

Close members of the family are those who may be expected to influence, or be influenced by, that person, and include children, spouse or domestic partner, children of the spouse, and dependants of the person or spouse (Ind AS 24.9). Ind AS 24 (unlike IAS 24 as originally issued) also lists brother, sister, father and mother in the Indian carve-in, so the family circle is wider than under IFRS.

KMP are those having authority and responsibility for planning, directing and controlling the activities of the entity, directly or indirectly, including any director (executive or otherwise) (Ind AS 24.9).

Other definitions to reconcile:

  • Companies Act s.2(76) "related party" — directors and relatives, KMP, firms and private companies where a director or relative is partner/member/director, public companies where a director holds more than 2% with relatives, holding/subsidiary/associate companies and others. It governs s.188 approvals.
  • SEBI LODR Reg 2(1)(zb) — related party as per Companies Act or Ind AS, and since April 2023 any person or entity in the promoter/promoter group holding 10% or more (20% earlier) is deemed a related party, as is any person holding 10% or more in the listed entity directly or beneficially.

The union of the three lists is the analytical universe. A forensic reviewer builds it from the Ind AS 24 note, the shareholding pattern (promoter group names), MBP-1 interest disclosures where available, directors' other directorships (MCA21 master data), and the Form AOC-2 annexure to the Board's report.

Disclosure requirements

  • Relationships between a parent and subsidiaries are disclosed irrespective of transactions; name the parent and ultimate controlling party (Ind AS 24.13).
  • For each category of related party, disclose the nature of the relationship and information about transactions and outstanding balances, including commitments: amount of transactions, balances and terms (secured/unsecured, nature of consideration), guarantees given or received, ECL provisions on balances and amounts written off (Ind AS 24.18).
  • Categories: parent, entities with joint control or significant influence, subsidiaries, associates, JVs, KMP, and other related parties (Ind AS 24.19).
  • Arm's length statement only if it can be substantiated (Ind AS 24.23).
  • Government-related entity exemption (Ind AS 24.25–26) — relevant for PSUs.

KMP compensation

Disclose KMP compensation in total and for each of (Ind AS 24.17): short-term employee benefits, post-employment benefits, other long-term benefits, termination benefits and share-based payment. The Companies Act adds: s.197 managerial remuneration ceiling of 11% of net profit (computed under s.198) for all directors, 5% for one managing/whole-time director or manager and 10% for all of them, with excess subject to shareholder special resolution; Schedule V for inadequate profits; and s.197(12) requires disclosure of the ratio of each director's remuneration to median employee remuneration in the Board's report.

Illustration: a listed textile company with consolidated PAT ₹180 crore pays the promoter chairman ₹14 crore (₹6 crore salary plus ₹8 crore commission at 4% of net profit) and his son as WTD ₹6 crore. Promoter KMP compensation ₹20 crore = 11.1% of PAT. Dividends paid: ₹45 crore, of which minorities (45% holding) received ₹20.25 crore. The promoter family takes ₹20 crore + ₹24.75 crore = ₹44.75 crore against minorities' ₹20.25 crore — a 69:31 split of cash returned despite a 55:45 ownership split.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Shadow promoters: entities controlled by former employees or associates of the promoter that transact heavily with the company but are not formally related. Look for common addresses, common directors and auditors, and vendors incorporated shortly before contracts.
  • Relatives beyond close family (cousins, in-laws) fall outside Ind AS 24 but may be inside s.2(77) or the promoter group definition.
  • Significant influence via contracts: a dominant supplier or distributor with board nomination rights.
  • Changes in the list year on year — entities dropping off just before large transactions.
  • Group-level KMP: KMP employed and paid by the parent but managing the listed subsidiary — disclose amounts charged as management fee (Ind AS 24.17A).
  • Commission on profits for non-executive promoter directors can be large and is easy to miss within "sitting fees and commission".

Key takeaways

  • The related-party universe is the union of Ind AS 24, Companies Act s.2(76) and LODR Reg 2(1)(zb); build it before analysing transactions.
  • Ind AS 24's family definition is wider than IAS 24, covering parents and siblings.
  • Disclosures cover nature, amounts, balances, terms, guarantees and ECL on related-party balances.
  • KMP compensation by category plus s.197 limits and the median pay ratio show promoter extraction.
  • Compare promoter cash take (remuneration plus dividends) with minorities' dividend share.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify related parties [L1]

Mr A holds 48% of Zeta Ltd (listed) and is chairman. Identify whether each is a related party of Zeta under Ind AS 24 and/or Companies Act s.2(76): (a) Mr A's brother's wholly-owned company; (b) a firm where Mr A's wife is a partner; (c) a PE fund holding 12% with one board seat; (d) Zeta's gratuity trust; (e) a company where Zeta's CFO is an independent director; (f) Mr A's cousin's proprietorship that supplies packaging.

💡 Hint: Test each against the person-based limbs first, then the entity-based limbs.

Q2. KMP compensation and minority cash [L2]

A listed company has net profit under s.198 of ₹250 crore. The promoter MD receives salary ₹5 crore, commission 3% of net profit, and perquisites ₹0.8 crore; two promoter WTDs receive ₹4 crore each. Promoters hold 60%; dividend paid is ₹60 crore. Test compliance with s.197 ceilings and compute the promoter vs minority split of total cash returned.

Q3. Building the universe [L3]

Describe the step-by-step process you would follow to build a complete related-party universe for an NSE-listed mid-cap with 40 subsidiaries and a promoter family of six members, naming the documents and public databases at each step and how you would detect undisclosed related vendors.

Q4. Interview: why disclose if arm's length? [L1]

Explain in under 120 words why Ind AS 24 requires disclosure even when transactions are at arm's length, citing the rationale in the standard.
