Subtopic note

Promoter Pledges, Encumbrance, NDUs and Shareholding Patterns

Scope: Promoter pledge and encumbrance trends (SAST Reg 31), NDUs, quarterly shareholding pattern under LODR Reg 31

Why it matters

When promoters borrow against their shares, the listed company's governance becomes hostage to its share price. A fall triggers margin calls; if promoters cannot top up, lenders invoke the pledge and sell, depressing the price further and possibly shifting control. Zee/Essel (2019), DHFL, the ADAG companies and Coffee Day all showed high or rising promoter encumbrance well before their crises. Pledging also hints at promoter-level cash needs — the same need that drives royalties, ICDs and related-party leakage. The data is public, frequent and free: SAST Reg 31 event disclosures and the quarterly shareholding pattern under LODR Reg 31.

Core notes

What counts as encumbrance

Under SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011, Reg 28(3) defines encumbrance to include a pledge, lien, negative lien, non-disposal undertaking (NDU), or any covenant, transaction, condition or arrangement in the nature of encumbrance, by whatever name called, whether executed directly or indirectly. The breadth is deliberate: lenders to promoter entities often take NDUs (the promoter undertakes not to sell or encumber the shares) or negative liens instead of a formal pledge, which historically escaped disclosure.

Form Mechanism Risk to minorities
Pledge (depository-recorded) Lender can invoke and sell on default Direct overhang, forced selling
NDU Promoter agrees not to sell/encumber; often paired with a power of attorney Same economic constraint, less visible
Negative lien Promoter agrees not to create further charges Restricts promoter flexibility
Margin pledge to broker Shares pledged for trading margin (MTF) Short-term liquidity pressure
Pledge by promoter entity for the listed company's own loans Lender comfort for company borrowing Lower conflict, still price-sensitive

SAST Reg 31 disclosures

  • Event disclosure (Reg 31(1)–(2)): the promoter must disclose creation, invocation or release of encumbrance on shares of the listed company to the company and the stock exchanges within seven working days.
  • Reasons for encumbrance: where the combined encumbrance of the promoter and persons acting in concert equals or exceeds 50% of their shareholding or 20% of the company's total share capital, detailed reasons must be disclosed (proviso to Reg 31(1), inserted 2019).
  • Annual declaration (Reg 31(4)): the promoter declares that no encumbrance has been made directly or indirectly other than those already disclosed.
  • Disclosure format captures the type of encumbrance (pledge/lien/NDU/others), the lender, purpose, and the security cover/collateral details.

Certain pledges recorded in the depository system for margin obligations in favour of stock brokers/clearing members have been subject to separate SEBI clarifications on disclosure; check the current circular position rather than assuming they are reported in the same way.

LODR Reg 31 shareholding pattern

Listed entities file the shareholding pattern with stock exchanges quarterly within 21 days of the end of each quarter (and before listing, and within 10 days of capital restructuring changes of 2% or more) in the SEBI format. The promoter table (Table II) shows, for each promoter and promoter group entity, shares held, shares pledged or otherwise encumbered (number and as % of shares held), and since later format revisions, shares under NDU and other encumbrances. Public shareholding tables show FPI, MF, insurance and retail holdings — useful for spotting institutions exiting as pledges rise.

Metrics (c05)

\[ \text{Pledge ratio (on promoter holding)} = \frac{\text{Encumbered promoter shares}}{\text{Promoter shares held}} \]

\[ \text{Pledge ratio (on capital)} = \frac{\text{Encumbered promoter shares}}{\text{Total shares outstanding}} \]

\[ \text{Unencumbered promoter stake} = \text{Promoter holding \%} \times (1 - \text{Pledge ratio on promoter holding}) \]

The last number tells you how much control survives full invocation. A promoter with 40% holding and 80% pledged has only an 8% unencumbered stake.

Margin-call trigger price (approximate): if loan \( L \) is secured by \( N \) pledged shares and the lender requires security cover \( c \) (e.g. 2.0x), the share price at which a top-up is needed is

\[ P^{*} = \frac{c \times L}{N} \]

Illustration — trend analysis

Quarter Promoter holding Encumbered (% of promoter holding) Encumbered (% of capital) Share price (₹)
Mar-23 52.0% 18% 9.4% 640
Mar-24 50.5% 41% 20.7% 510
Sep-24 48.0% 62% 29.8% 390
Mar-25 44.0% 78% 34.3% 280

Pattern: encumbrance rising, holding falling (partial invocations or sales), price falling — the classic spiral. Reg 31 reasons disclosure was triggered from Mar-24 (above 20% of capital). If loans total ₹1,200 crore against 9 crore pledged shares with 2.0x cover, \( P^{*} = 2.0 \times 1{,}200 \div 9 \approx 267 \), i.e. about ₹267 — close to the Mar-25 price.

Linking pledges to company analysis

  • Purpose of pledge (from Reg 31 disclosures): funding the listed company (less concerning), funding other promoter ventures, or personal/holding-company debt refinancing (most concerning).
  • Cross-check with related-party flows: rising pledges plus rising ICDs or royalty to promoter entities suggest the listed company is supporting promoter debt.
  • Rating actions on promoter holding companies (if rated) and on the listed entity often cite pledge levels.
  • Lender identity: NBFCs and mutual funds (via structured debt) are more likely to invoke quickly than banks.
  • Corporate actions: preferential allotments or warrants to promoters at the same time as high pledges deserve scrutiny over funding sources.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • NDUs not labelled as pledge: the shareholding pattern may show "0% pledged" while Reg 31 filings show NDUs; read both, and treat NDUs as encumbrance (see topic pitfall).
  • Encumbrance at holding-company level: the promoter pledges shares of the unlisted holdco that owns the listed shares — not captured by Reg 31 of the listed entity. Check the holdco's charge filings (MCA CHG forms).
  • Invocation without sale: lenders may invoke and hold, becoming significant shareholders and triggering SAST thresholds.
  • Release and re-pledge around quarter-ends to report lower encumbrance at the shareholding pattern date — compare event disclosures through the quarter.
  • Financial institutions' exemptions from open-offer obligations on invocation exist; that changes takeover dynamics, not the risk.

Key takeaways

  • Encumbrance under SAST Reg 28(3) includes pledges, liens, negative liens, NDUs and any similar arrangement.
  • Reg 31 requires event disclosure within seven working days, reasons above 50% of holding or 20% of capital, and an annual declaration.
  • LODR Reg 31 quarterly shareholding patterns (within 21 days) show encumbrance by promoter entity.
  • Compute pledge ratios on promoter holding and on capital, unencumbered stake and approximate trigger price.
  • Read pledges together with related-party outflows and lender identity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pledge ratios and reasons disclosure [L1]

A company has 50 crore shares. Promoters hold 27 crore shares; 15 crore are pledged and 3 crore are under NDU. Compute the pledge ratio on promoter holding and on total capital (with and without NDUs), the unencumbered stake, and state whether the Reg 31 reasons disclosure is triggered.

Q2. Trigger price [L2]

A promoter entity has borrowed ₹900 crore from NBFCs against 6 crore pledged shares, with a required cover of 2.5x and a top-up requirement within 3 days. The current share price is ₹420. Compute the trigger price and the additional shares needed to restore cover if the price falls 20%, and discuss the implications for minority shareholders.

💡 Hint: Rearrange the cover condition to solve for shares needed at the new price.

Q3. Reconciling sources [L2]

The March 2025 shareholding pattern shows promoter encumbrance at 12% of promoter holding. During the quarter, Reg 31 filings show creation of pledges on 8% of promoter holding on 10 January, release of 6% on 28 March, and re-creation of 6% on 3 April. Explain what you infer and how you would present the encumbrance in a governance assessment.

Q4. Governance memo [L3]

A mid-cap's promoter holding fell from 60% to 46% over eight quarters, with encumbrance rising from 10% to 70% of promoter holding, while loans to promoter-group entities rose from ₹100 crore to ₹650 crore (net worth ₹2,000 crore) and the statutory auditor resigned. Write a one-page governance red-flag memo with evidence references and a severity rating.
