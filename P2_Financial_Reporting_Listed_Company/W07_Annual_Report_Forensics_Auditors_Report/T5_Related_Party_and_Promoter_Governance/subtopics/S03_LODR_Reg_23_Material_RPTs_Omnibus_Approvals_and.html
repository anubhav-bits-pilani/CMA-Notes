Subtopic note

LODR Reg 23 Material RPTs, Omnibus Approvals and Voting Bar

Scope: LODR Reg 23 material RPT approvals, omnibus audit committee approvals (Reg 18), related parties barred from voting in favour

Why it matters

For listed companies, SEBI LODR Reg 23 is a far stricter gate than Companies Act s.188: it has no ordinary-course exemption from audit committee approval, it brings subsidiaries' transactions within scope, it treats transactions with unrelated parties that benefit a related party as RPTs, and it bars all related parties from voting on material RPT resolutions. Shareholder votes on material RPTs are where minority investors and proxy advisers push back — rejected RPT resolutions at companies such as Zee, Vedanta and several MNC subsidiaries over royalty increases show the mechanism working. For a forensic reviewer, the audit committee's approval record, the shareholder voting results (Reg 44) and the half-yearly RPT disclosures (Reg 23(9)) are the evidence trail.

Core notes

Definitions that widen the scope

  • Related party (Reg 2(1)(zb)): as defined under the Companies Act or Ind AS, plus any person or entity in the promoter/promoter group holding 10% or more, and any person holding 10% or more directly or beneficially (from 1 April 2023).
  • Related party transaction (Reg 2(1)(zc)): a transfer of resources, services or obligations between (a) the listed entity or any subsidiary and a related party of the listed entity or any subsidiary, or (b) the listed entity or any subsidiary and any other person where the purpose and effect is to benefit a related party of the listed entity or any subsidiary — regardless of whether a price is charged. Specified exclusions include issue of securities by preferential issue/rights/bonus, uniform dividend payments, and certain retail banking services on uniform terms.

Approval architecture

Layer Requirement Reference
Policy Board-approved policy on materiality and dealing with RPTs, including clear thresholds for "material modifications"; reviewed at least once every three years Reg 23(1)
Audit committee Prior approval of all RPTs and subsequent material modifications; only independent directors on the committee vote Reg 23(2), Reg 18(3) with Schedule II Part C
Subsidiary RPTs Prior audit committee approval of the listed entity where a subsidiary is party (and listed entity is not) and the value exceeds the threshold specified (10% of the subsidiary's standalone turnover, subject to the later amendment applying the lower of that and the Reg 23(1) materiality threshold) Proviso to Reg 23(2)
Shareholders Prior approval by resolution for material RPTs and material modifications; no related party shall vote to approve, whether or not it is party to the particular transaction Reg 23(4)
Disclosure Half-yearly RPT disclosure to stock exchanges in prescribed format on the date of publishing results; published on website Reg 23(9)

Material RPT (Reg 23(1)): a transaction, individually or taken together with previous transactions in a financial year, exceeding ₹1,000 crore or 10% of the annual consolidated turnover per the last audited financial statements, whichever is lower (c01). Brand/royalty payments have a separate 5% test (c02).

Exemptions (Reg 23(5)): transactions between a holding company and its wholly-owned subsidiary whose accounts are consolidated and placed before shareholders; between two wholly-owned subsidiaries of the listed holding company; and specified transactions of public sector entities.

Omnibus approvals by the audit committee

Under Reg 23(3), the audit committee may grant omnibus approval for RPTs that are repetitive in nature, subject to:

  • criteria laid down by the committee consistent with the RPT policy, and satisfaction of the need for omnibus approval and that it is in the interest of the entity;
  • specifying the name of the related parties, nature, period, maximum value per transaction, aggregate value, indicative base price/current contracted price and formula for variation;
  • where the need cannot be foreseen and details are not available, omnibus approval for transactions not exceeding ₹1 crore per transaction;
  • quarterly review by the committee of transactions entered into under the omnibus approval;
  • validity of not more than one year (fresh approval needed each year).

Shareholder approval of material RPTs given at an AGM is valid until the next AGM (not exceeding 15 months); approval at other general meetings is valid for one year (SEBI master circular).

SEBI has also prescribed Industry Standards on minimum information to be placed before the audit committee and shareholders for RPT approval (effective 2025), covering the justification, comparable pricing, valuation reports, and for loans/ICDs the source of funds, interest rate, tenure, security and end-use. These standards are your checklist when reading an explanatory statement to a notice.

The voting bar in practice

Reg 23(4) prohibits every related party — not just the counterparty — from voting in favour. The practical effect: the resolution is decided by public (non-related) shareholders. Promoters may vote against, but this is rare. When reading the voting results filed under Reg 44:

  1. Identify the votes cast by public institutions and public non-institutions separately.
  2. Compute the percentage "for" among institutional votes — high institutional dissent is a governance signal even when the resolution passes on retail votes.
  3. Compare with proxy adviser recommendations (IiAS, InGovern, SES).

Illustration

Listed auto company, consolidated turnover FY 2023-24 ₹18,000 crore; materiality threshold = lower of ₹1,000 crore and ₹1,800 crore = ₹1,000 crore. For FY 2024-25 it proposes purchases of components from a promoter-group company of ₹1,150 crore and an ICD of ₹200 crore to a promoter-group NBFC.

  • Component purchases: material (above ₹1,000 crore) → audit committee prior approval (independent directors only), shareholder approval with related parties barred from voting, explanatory statement per industry standards.
  • ICD ₹200 crore: below the materiality threshold on its own, so audit committee approval suffices — but if the same NBFC also received ₹850 crore of other transactions during the year, aggregation with the same related party is required for the materiality test.
  • Both also need Companies Act compliance: s.188 for purchases (exemption possible if ordinary course and arm's length) and s.185/186 for the ICD.

Reading the evidence

  • Corporate governance report (Schedule V Part C) and the RPT policy on the website.
  • Notice of AGM/postal ballot explanatory statements for material RPTs.
  • Reg 44 voting results; Reg 23(9) half-yearly RPT disclosures (often more granular than the annual note).
  • Secretarial audit report (Reg 24A) for non-compliance observations.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Splitting a large contract across multiple related entities or periods to stay below the threshold; Reg 23 aggregates transactions with the same related party during the year, and policies should catch economic splits.
  • Material modification defined loosely in policy (e.g. only above 50% variation) weakens oversight.
  • Ratification: amendments in 2024–25 allow limited ratification of small RPTs by the audit committee within a specified window; frequent ratification instead of prior approval is a control weakness.
  • Approval is not proof of arm's length: committees often rely on management-prepared benchmarking (see the pitfall in the topic overview).
  • Subsidiary-level RPTs with the listed parent's promoter entities are within scope; many companies miss these.

Key takeaways

  • Reg 23 covers transactions of the listed entity and its subsidiaries, and transactions with others that benefit a related party.
  • Audit committee prior approval is required for all RPTs; only independent directors vote.
  • Material RPTs (lower of ₹1,000 crore or 10% of consolidated turnover) need shareholder approval with all related parties barred from voting in favour.
  • Omnibus approvals are for repetitive RPTs with defined caps, quarterly review and one-year validity; ₹1 crore per transaction for unforeseen items.
  • Use explanatory statements, Reg 44 voting results and Reg 23(9) half-yearly disclosures as evidence.

Practice Questions

Q1. Materiality and approvals [L2]

A listed FMCG company's consolidated turnover for FY 2023-24 was ₹6,500 crore. In FY 2024-25 it plans: (a) sale of products to a promoter-owned distributor ₹420 crore; (b) purchase of packaging from the same distributor's sister company ₹280 crore; (c) a ₹5 crore one-off consultancy contract with a director's firm; (d) transactions with its wholly-owned subsidiary ₹900 crore. Determine which transactions are material, which approvals each needs and who may vote.

💡 Hint: Compute the threshold first and check whether aggregation across entities is relevant.

Q2. Omnibus approval design [L1]

Draft the key parameters of an omnibus approval for monthly raw material purchases from a promoter-group company expected at ₹15–20 crore per month, consistent with Reg 23(3).

Q3. Voting result analysis [L2]

A material RPT resolution (royalty increase) was put to shareholders. Promoters hold 55% and did not vote. Public institutions holding 25% all voted: 40% of their shares for, 60% against. Public non-institutions holding 20% had a 30% turnout, voting 95% for. Compute the overall percentage of votes cast in favour, state whether an ordinary resolution passes, and comment on what the institutional split signals.

Q4. Subsidiary transaction caught [L3]

A listed parent's step-down subsidiary in Singapore lends USD 50 million to an entity owned by the promoter's son. The parent's audit committee was not informed. Analyse the Reg 2(1)(zc) and Reg 23 implications, the disclosure consequences, and what the statutory auditor and secretarial auditor might report.
