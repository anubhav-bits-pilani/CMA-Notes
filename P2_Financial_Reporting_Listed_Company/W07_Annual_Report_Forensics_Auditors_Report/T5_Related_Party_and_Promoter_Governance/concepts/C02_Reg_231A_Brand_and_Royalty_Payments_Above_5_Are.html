Key concept

Reg 23(1A): Brand and Royalty Payments Above 5% Are Material

Concept: Reg 23(1A): brand usage/royalty payments material if exceeding 5% of annual consolidated turnover (individually or aggregate)

Statement

Under SEBI LODR Reg 23(1A), a transaction involving payments made to a related party with respect to brand usage or royalty is considered material if the transaction(s), individually or taken together with previous transactions during a financial year, exceed 5% of the annual consolidated turnover of the listed entity as per its last audited financial statements:

\[ \text{Material if} \quad \sum_{j \in \text{FY}} R_j > 0.05 \times \text{Turnover}_{\text{cons}, t-1} \]

where \( R_j \) is each brand usage or royalty payment to the related party in the year. Material royalty arrangements require prior shareholder approval with related parties not voting in favour (Reg 23(4)).

Explanation

Reg 23(1A) was inserted by SEBI in 2019 (effective 1 July 2019) after repeated controversies over MNC subsidiaries increasing royalty rates to foreign parents without minority approval. The logic: royalty is a pure transfer to the promoter and the general materiality threshold (then 10% of turnover) would rarely be reached by royalties, so a lower threshold was set.

Points to note:

  • Turnover, not profit, is the base. A 4.5% royalty in a company with 12% PBT margin consumes over a quarter of pre-royalty profits but stays below the 5% test. The rule therefore catches only very high royalties.
  • Interaction with the general threshold: the general Reg 23(1) test (lower of ₹1,000 crore and 10%) also applies. For large companies, a royalty above ₹1,000 crore is material under Reg 23(1) even if below 5% of turnover. For example, at consolidated turnover of ₹40,000 crore, a 3% royalty of ₹1,200 crore is material under Reg 23(1).
  • Aggregation of all brand and royalty payments to the same related party (technical know-how, trademark licence, brand fee) in the year.
  • Rate increases are material modifications if the policy so defines; investor votes on royalty increases have been contested.
  • The audit committee must still approve all royalty payments under Reg 23(2).

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply both tests [L1]

For a listed company with last audited consolidated turnover of ₹22,000 crore, determine whether shareholder approval is needed for FY 2025-26 royalty payments to the parent at rates of 3.0%, 4.6% and 5.2% on expected sales of ₹24,000 crore.

Q2. Profit-based view [L2]

A company has revenue ₹10,000 crore, PBT before royalty ₹1,500 crore and pays royalty of 4.8% to its promoter. Compute royalty as % of PBT before royalty and explain why the 5% turnover test may be a weak safeguard for minorities in low-margin businesses.

💡 Hint: Compare the royalty amount with pre-royalty profit rather than revenue.

Q3. Splitting arrangements [L2]

A promoter entity charges a listed company 2.5% of sales as trademark licence fee and 2.0% as "technical services fee" under separate agreements, plus a ₹50 crore annual "brand promotion contribution". Turnover is ₹8,000 crore. Evaluate the Reg 23(1A) position and the aggregation argument.
