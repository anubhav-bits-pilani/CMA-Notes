Key concept

RPT Intensity: Related-Party Outflows to Revenue and EBITDA

Concept: RPT intensity = Related-party outflows (purchases, royalty, loans given) ÷ Revenue or ÷ EBITDA

Statement

\[ \text{RPT intensity}_{\text{Rev}} = \frac{\text{RPO}_t}{\text{Revenue}_t}, \qquad \text{RPT intensity}_{\text{EBITDA}} = \frac{\text{RPO}_t}{\text{EBITDA}_t} \]

where related-party outflows \( \text{RPO}_t \) for year \( t \) are the sum of purchases of goods and services from related parties, royalty and brand fees, management and consultancy fees, rent, loans and ICDs given (gross or net of repayments, stated consistently), guarantees given (shown separately), capital advances and asset purchases from related parties, and CSR contributions to related trusts:

\[ \text{RPO}_t = \text{Purch}_t + \text{Roy}_t + \text{Fees}_t + \text{Rent}_t + \text{Loans}_t + \text{Assets}_t + \text{CSR}_t \]

Transactions with wholly-owned subsidiaries are usually excluded in consolidated analysis (they eliminate) but included in standalone analysis of a holding company.

Explanation

The ratio scales the size of the promoter's economic relationship with the company. Revenue-based intensity is comparable across companies; EBITDA-based intensity shows how much of the company's earning power passes through related hands — a 40% RPT/EBITDA ratio means related parties touch outflows equal to 40% of operating profit.

Design choices that must be explicit:

  • Gross vs net: purchases from a related supplier are a real operating cost (not a leak) if priced at arm's length; the intensity measures exposure, not loss. Pair it with pricing tests (s07) to estimate leakage.
  • Flows vs balances: use flows for intensity; use balances (loans outstanding, receivables from related parties) divided by net worth for stock exposure.
  • Separate buckets: operating (purchases, royalty, fees, rent), financing (loans, ICDs, guarantees) and capital (asset purchases, capital advances). The financing and capital buckets are where most frauds occurred.
  • Trend: three to five years; rising intensity without a business rationale (new supply chain integration) is a governance signal.
  • Source: Ind AS 24 note, Reg 23(9) half-yearly disclosures, AOC-2, CARO 3(iii); state the page/note reference.

Thresholds are judgemental. Many governance screens flag RPT outflows above 10% of revenue or 25% of EBITDA, and any financing outflows to promoter entities, for deeper review.

Illustration

Mid-cap textile company (₹ crore):

Item FY23 FY24 FY25
Purchases from related parties 380 420 510
Royalty and fees 30 45 70
Rent to promoter trust 12 12 15
ICDs given to promoter entities (gross) 0 90 240
Asset purchase from promoter 0 0 110
RPO 422 567 945
Revenue 4,000 4,300 4,500
EBITDA 560 520 480
RPO ÷ Revenue 10.6% 13.2% 21.0%
RPO ÷ EBITDA 75% 109% 197%
Financing + capital RPO ÷ EBITDA 0% 17% 73%

Operating RPTs grew roughly with revenue, but financing and capital outflows jumped from nil to 73% of EBITDA while EBITDA fell — a high-severity finding.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute intensity [L1]

From the following FY 2024-25 data (₹ crore) — revenue 8,000; EBITDA 1,200; related-party purchases 640; royalty 160; management fees 40; loans to promoter entities 300 given, 100 repaid; capital advances to related EPC contractor 220 — compute RPT intensity on revenue and EBITDA using gross and net loans, and the financing-plus-capital share.

Q2. Bucket and interpret [L2]

Explain why two companies with identical RPT intensity of 15% of revenue can have very different governance risk. Construct two numerical examples to illustrate.

💡 Hint: Vary the mix between operating, financing and capital buckets.

Q3. Excel build [L3]

Design an Excel sheet that pulls related-party transactions from a pasted Ind AS 24 note table (party, nature, amount, year), classifies each into operating/financing/capital buckets using a mapping table, and computes RPT intensity by bucket for three years. Write the key formula for the bucket totals.
