Key concept

Reg 23 Material RPT Threshold: ₹1,000 Crore or 10% of Turnover

Concept: Reg 23 material RPT = exceeding ₹1,000 crore or 10% of annual consolidated turnover, whichever is lower

Statement

Under SEBI LODR Reg 23(1), a transaction with a related party is material if the transaction(s) to be entered into individually or taken together with previous transactions during a financial year exceed:

\[ T = \min\left(1{,}000,\; 0.10 \times \text{Turnover}_{\text{cons}, t-1}\right) \quad \text{(in rupees crore)} \]

where \( \text{Turnover}_{\text{cons}, t-1} \) is annual consolidated turnover per the last audited financial statements of the listed entity. A transaction is material if

\[ \sum_{j \in \text{FY}} V_j > T \]

with \( V_j \) the value of each transaction with the same related party during the financial year (including the proposed one). Material RPTs and their material modifications require prior shareholder approval, with no related party voting to approve (Reg 23(4)).

Explanation

The threshold was revised with effect from 1 April 2022. Previously, materiality was 10% of consolidated turnover alone; the ₹1,000 crore absolute cap ensures large companies cannot enter multi-thousand-crore RPTs without a minority vote. For a company with turnover above ₹10,000 crore, the absolute cap binds; below that, the 10% test binds.

Key mechanics:

  • Aggregation is per related party over the financial year. Transactions of different types with the same related party (sales, purchases, loans) are aggregated under most companies' policies and SEBI's approach; splitting across entities of the same promoter group may be caught by the policy's aggregation rules and by Reg 2(1)(zc).
  • Subsidiary transactions count: RPTs of subsidiaries with related parties of the listed entity or subsidiary are within Reg 23's scope.
  • Loans and guarantees: value is the principal or guaranteed amount (not interest only).
  • Modification: increases beyond the policy's materiality of modification (e.g. 20% variation) need fresh approval.
  • Exemptions (Reg 23(5)): holding company and wholly-owned subsidiary transactions where accounts are consolidated, and transactions between two wholly-owned subsidiaries.
  • Brand/royalty: separate 5% test under Reg 23(1A) (c02).

Audit committee approval is required for all RPTs regardless of materiality; materiality only decides whether shareholders must also approve.

Practice Questions

Q1. Threshold computation [L1]

Compute the Reg 23 materiality threshold for companies with consolidated turnover (last audited) of ₹2,400 crore, ₹9,500 crore, ₹10,000 crore and ₹75,000 crore.

Q2. Aggregation test [L2]

A listed company with consolidated turnover of ₹7,800 crore (FY 2023-24) has the following FY 2024-25 transactions with the same promoter-group company: purchases ₹420 crore (approved by audit committee in April), a ₹200 crore ICD proposed in October, and a ₹150 crore guarantee proposed in January. Determine at which point shareholder approval became necessary and what the explanatory statement must cover.

💡 Hint: Keep a running cumulative total against the threshold.

Q3. Policy design judgement [L3]

A company's RPT policy defines "material modification" as any change above 50% of the approved value. Explain why this weakens Reg 23, and propose a better definition with reasons.
