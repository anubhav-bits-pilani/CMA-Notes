Subtopic note

Rising CWIP, Capital Advances and Intangibles Under Development

Scope: Rising CWIP, capital advances and intangibles under development; Schedule III ageing review

Why it matters

Capital work-in-progress, capital advances and intangible assets under development are balance sheet holding areas where expenditure waits before being depreciated or amortised. While it waits, it generates no depreciation, attracts capitalised interest, and is not tested for impairment as actively as operating assets. That makes these lines the natural parking place for both legitimate long-gestation projects and for costs that should have been expensed, assets already in use but not capitalised, or cash routed to vendors that is not coming back as assets. Several Indian frauds and stressed cases involved large, ageing capital advances to related or obscure vendors. Since FY 2021-22, Schedule III ageing schedules make these balances testable.

Core notes

Where each item sits

Item Schedule III presentation Accounting basis
Capital work-in-progress Separate line under non-current assets Ind AS 16: cost of PPE under construction, not depreciated
Intangible assets under development Separate line under non-current assets Ind AS 38: development-phase cost meeting Ind AS 38.57
Capital advances Other non-current assets (separately disclosed) Advances paid for acquisition of PPE; not financial assets; not in CWIP
Capital creditors Other financial liabilities Unpaid amounts for capital goods
Capital commitments Note (contracts remaining to be executed on capital account, not provided for) Schedule III commitments disclosure

Schedule III ageing requirements (Division II, 2021 amendments)

CWIP ageing schedule: amounts in CWIP for a period of less than 1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years and more than 3 years, separately for projects in progress and projects temporarily suspended.

CWIP completion schedule: for CWIP whose completion is overdue or has exceeded its cost compared with its original plan, project-wise amounts "to be completed in" less than 1 year, 1–2, 2–3 and more than 3 years. Details of projects whose activity has been suspended are given separately.

Intangible assets under development: equivalent ageing and completion schedules.

Capital advances are not covered by an ageing schedule, which is why they require separate scrutiny; the Ind AS 24 note, CARO 3(iii) (loans and advances in the nature of loans) and the BRSR openness attribute help.

Analytical metrics

\[ \text{CWIP to gross block} = \frac{\text{CWIP}}{\text{Gross block of PPE}} \]

\[ \text{CWIP capitalisation rate} = \frac{\text{Transfers from CWIP to PPE during the year}}{\text{Opening CWIP} + \text{Additions to CWIP}} \]

\[ \text{Aged CWIP share} = \frac{\text{CWIP older than 2 years}}{\text{Total CWIP}} \]

\[ \text{Capital advances to capex} = \frac{\text{Capital advances (closing)}}{\text{Capex for the year}} \]

Rule-of-thumb flags (calibrate by industry): CWIP above 25–30% of gross block for a company not in a disclosed major expansion; aged CWIP share above 30%; capital advances above 25% of annual capex or rising for two years without corresponding CWIP additions; intangibles under development rising while revenue from new products is flat.

Review procedure

  1. Roll-forward: opening CWIP + additions (including capitalised interest and pre-operative costs) − capitalised to PPE − impairment/write-off = closing CWIP. Missing reconciliations are a disclosure gap.
  2. Ageing versus guidance: compare project-wise ageing with MD&A commissioning claims (see the MD&A topic).
  3. Suspended projects: check that borrowing cost capitalisation has stopped (Ind AS 23.20) and that impairment indicators under Ind AS 36.12 have been considered.
  4. Capitalisation timing: look for evidence the asset is in use — production volumes, capacity statements, trial-run sales, Reg 30 "commencement of commercial production" announcements — while the cost remains in CWIP.
  5. Capital advances: identify counterparties (related parties, newly incorporated vendors), ageing (from notes or annual comparison), and whether advances convert into CWIP or are refunded; check CARO 3(iii) for advances in the nature of loans.
  6. Intangibles under development: test the Ind AS 38.57 criteria and the period since commencement; products not launched in two or three years need impairment testing annually (Ind AS 36.10(a) requires annual impairment testing of intangibles not yet available for use).

Illustration

A listed mid-cap pharma (₹ crore):

Item FY 2022-23 FY 2023-24 FY 2024-25
Gross block (PPE) 2,400 2,520 2,610
CWIP 380 640 910
of which more than 2 years 40 150 410
Capital advances 90 210 360
Intangibles under development 120 260 430
Capex (cash flow) 410 520 560
Revenue 3,100 3,250 3,380

CWIP to gross block rose from 16% to 35%; aged share from 11% to 45%; capital advances to capex from 22% to 64%; intangibles under development more than tripled while revenue grew 9% in two years. Gross block grew by only ₹210 crore over two years despite capex of ₹1,080 crore in those two years — almost all spending is still "in progress". Possible explanations: a genuine long-gestation greenfield plant awaiting USFDA approval (verify with MD&A and regulatory filings), or capitalisation of costs that should be expensed, advances to vendors that are not delivering, and depreciation being avoided. The forensic memo should quantify depreciation that would arise if, say, ₹410 crore aged CWIP were capitalised over 15 years (about ₹27 crore a year) plus interest that would be expensed.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Regulated approvals (pharma plant inspections, environmental clearance for mining) legitimately extend CWIP ageing; the question is whether impairment and borrowing-cost suspension were considered.
  • Capital advances for land in states with slow conversion processes can age legitimately, but advances to related-party land aggregators deserve scrutiny.
  • CWIP in consolidated statements may sit in subsidiaries funded by parent loans; the standalone balance sheet shows a loan, not CWIP.
  • Reclassification between capital advances, CWIP and "other non-current assets" across years can mask ageing; track the combined total.
  • Write-offs of CWIP or advances in a "kitchen sink" year after a CEO change often confirm earlier concerns; check exceptional items.
  • Ind AS 36 for CWIP: CWIP is part of a cash-generating unit; impairment testing may be avoided if the CGU has headroom, but standalone stalled projects may need separate assessment.

Key takeaways

  • CWIP, capital advances and intangibles under development generate no depreciation and attract capitalised interest; rising balances inflate profit.
  • Use Schedule III ageing and completion schedules to test commissioning claims and identify stalled or overrun projects.
  • Capital advances have no ageing schedule; identify counterparties and test conversion into assets.
  • Compute CWIP to gross block, capitalisation rate, aged share and advances to capex across years and peers.
  • Intangibles not yet available for use require annual impairment testing under Ind AS 36.10(a).
  • Quantify hidden depreciation and interest from assets that appear ready for use.

Practice Questions

Q1. CWIP roll-forward [L1]

Opening CWIP ₹520 crore; additions ₹610 crore (including capitalised borrowing costs ₹45 crore); closing CWIP ₹930 crore; impairment of a suspended project ₹30 crore. Compute the amount capitalised to PPE during the year and the CWIP capitalisation rate.

Q2. Aged CWIP and hidden depreciation [L2]

A company's CWIP ageing shows ₹1,140 crore, of which ₹620 crore is older than 2 years and ₹180 crore is in "temporarily suspended" projects. Capitalised borrowing costs for the year were ₹74 crore, of which ₹22 crore relates to suspended projects. The company's average depreciation rate is 6.5%. Estimate the PAT effect (tax rate 25.17%) if the suspended projects' interest is expensed and if ₹300 crore of the aged CWIP is found to have been ready for use from the start of the year.

💡 Hint: Treat each adjustment separately before combining.

Q3. Capital advances investigation [L2]

Capital advances rose from ₹60 crore to ₹290 crore over two years. The Ind AS 24 note shows ₹170 crore paid as "advance for purchase of machinery" to an entity in which a promoter's relative is a director; CARO 3(iii) is reported "not applicable". Explain which provisions and disclosures (Ind AS 24, s.188, LODR Reg 23, CARO 3(iii) and 3(xiii)) you would examine, and the evidence that would show the advance is genuine.

Q4. Intangibles under development [L3]

An auto component company has intangible assets under development of ₹340 crore relating to EV powertrain platforms, of which ₹210 crore has been under development for more than three years; no EV product revenue has been reported. Prepare an analyst challenge list referring to Ind AS 38.57 and Ind AS 36.10(a), and describe how you would treat this balance in a DCF and in an ROCE comparison with peers.
