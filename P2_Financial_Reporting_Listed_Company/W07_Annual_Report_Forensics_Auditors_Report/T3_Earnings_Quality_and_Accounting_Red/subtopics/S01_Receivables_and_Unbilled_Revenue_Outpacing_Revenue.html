Subtopic note

Receivables and Unbilled Revenue Outpacing Revenue: DSO Creep

Scope: Receivables and unbilled revenue growth vs revenue; DSO creep

Why it matters

Revenue that is not collected is the most common route to overstated earnings, from outright fictitious sales to aggressive cut-off, extended credit to push volume, and premature recognition on long-term contracts. Receivables and unbilled revenue (contract assets) growing faster than revenue is the first quantitative flag in almost every earnings-quality screen, including the DSRI variable of the Beneish M-score. For FP&A and modelling, DSO assumptions drive the working capital forecast, and a creeping DSO that is extrapolated rather than questioned overstates cash flow. In credit, rising receivables in a company with flat revenue is a liquidity warning before it is an accounting one.

Core notes

Definitions and Schedule III mapping

Item Schedule III location Ind AS basis
Trade receivables (billed) Current/non-current financial assets; ageing schedule split into undisputed/disputed and considered good / significant increase in credit risk / credit impaired Ind AS 109 (ECL), Ind AS 115.108 (receivable is an unconditional right to consideration)
Unbilled revenue / contract assets Usually other current financial assets ("unbilled revenue") or other current assets ("contract assets") Ind AS 115.105–107: contract asset is a right to consideration conditional on something other than passage of time
Contract liabilities (advances, deferred revenue) Other current liabilities Ind AS 115.106
Allowance for ECL Deducted from receivables; movement disclosed Ind AS 109.5.5.15 simplified approach for trade receivables

The 2021 Schedule III amendment requires a trade receivables ageing schedule (less than 6 months, 6 months–1 year, 1–2 years, 2–3 years, more than 3 years, from due date of payment or, if none, date of transaction), separately for undisputed and disputed receivables. Unbilled dues are shown separately.

Core metrics

\[ \text{DSO} = \frac{\text{Average (or closing) trade receivables}}{\text{Revenue from operations}} \times 365 \]

\[ \text{Receivable growth gap} = g_{\text{receivables}} - g_{\text{revenue}} \]

\[ \text{DSO incl. unbilled} = \frac{\text{Trade receivables} + \text{Unbilled revenue / contract assets}}{\text{Revenue from operations}} \times 365 \]

Use gross receivables (before ECL) as well as net, because a rising ECL allowance can mask a rising gross balance, and a falling allowance can flatter net DSO. For seasonal businesses or those with lumpy Q4 billing, compare closing receivables to Q4 revenue annualised as well as to annual revenue.

What DSO creep can mean

Pattern Possible explanation Test
Receivables up faster than revenue, Q4 revenue spike Channel stuffing or aggressive cut-off Q4 share of annual revenue versus prior years; Q1 next-year returns and credit notes
Unbilled revenue rising faster than billed Percentage-of-completion estimates running ahead of milestones (IT services, EPC) Unbilled ageing; contract asset reconciliation; KAM on estimates
Rising 1–3 year ageing, low ECL Under-provisioning ECL rate on aged buckets versus peers; write-offs
Receivables from related parties rising Circular or non-arm's-length sales Ind AS 24 note; BRSR openness attribute
DSO up across the industry Customer stress or industry credit terms (government payment delays) Peer DSO; sector commentary
Customer concentration rising Bargaining power shift Ind AS 108.34 major customer disclosure
Factoring or bill discounting reduced Mechanical increase in DSO Borrowings note; derecognition policy under Ind AS 109

The reverse is also a flag: DSO falling sharply while revenue grows, combined with rising short-term borrowings, may indicate receivable factoring or supply-chain finance that removes receivables from the balance sheet (if derecognised under Ind AS 109.3.2.3–3.2.6) and shifts cash from financing to operating activities.

Illustration

An EPC company (₹ crore):

Item FY 2022-23 FY 2023-24 FY 2024-25
Revenue 4,800 5,300 5,750
Trade receivables (net) 1,180 1,410 1,720
ECL allowance 95 92 88
Unbilled revenue 620 840 1,160
Receivables over 2 years 140 210 330

Revenue grew 10% and 8.5%; net receivables grew 19.5% and 22.0%; unbilled revenue grew 35.5% and 38.1%. Closing DSO (net, billed) rose from 90 to 97 to 109 days; including unbilled from 137 to 155 to 183 days. The ECL allowance fell while the over-two-year bucket more than doubled, so the ECL coverage on that bucket fell from 68% to 27%. Interpretation: revenue recognised on progress is not being converted to billing, collections are slowing, and provisioning has not kept pace. The model should not assume DSO mean-reversion without evidence; the credit view should treat a share of aged receivables as impaired.

Excel implementation

DSO_gross: =(Receivables_Net + ECL_Allowance) / Revenue * 365
Growth_gap: =(Receivables_t / Receivables_t1 - 1) - (Revenue_t / Revenue_t1 - 1)
Flag: =IF(AND(Growth_gap > 0.10, DSO_t - DSO_t1 > 10), "DSO CREEP", "")

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Business model shifts (from cash-and-carry retail to B2B, or adding government customers) legitimately raise DSO; verify with segment data rather than flag mechanically (see the topic pitfall on growth-phase working capital).
  • Acquisitions add receivables at the balance sheet date but only part-year revenue; use pro forma revenue or exclude the acquiree.
  • GST and billing timing: receivables include GST while revenue excludes it; DSO is overstated by roughly the GST rate unless adjusted. Keep consistent across years.
  • Retention money in EPC contracts is contractually deferred; separate it from overdue receivables.
  • Contract assets reclassification between unbilled and receivables can move DSO without economic change; track the combined measure.
  • Government receivables (power distribution companies, state agencies) may be collectible but slow; time value and ECL still apply.

Key takeaways

  • Compare receivable and unbilled growth with revenue growth every year; a persistent positive gap is the first earnings-quality flag.
  • Compute DSO on net, gross and including-unbilled bases, and on Q4 annualised revenue for seasonal businesses.
  • Read the Schedule III ageing schedule and the ECL movement together; falling coverage on rising aged buckets is a red flag.
  • Falling DSO with rising borrowings may indicate factoring or supply-chain finance.
  • Explain the creep (business model, customers, contracts) before calling it manipulation, but do not extrapolate it into forecasts without evidence.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute DSO measures [L1]

Revenue ₹2,600 crore (prior ₹2,350 crore); trade receivables net ₹610 crore (prior ₹470 crore); ECL allowance ₹45 crore (prior ₹52 crore); unbilled revenue ₹180 crore (prior ₹120 crore). Compute closing DSO on net, gross and including-unbilled bases for both years, and the receivable growth gap.

Q2. Q4 concentration test [L2]

A consumer durables company's quarterly revenue (₹ crore) in FY 2024-25 was 1,820, 1,640, 2,010 and 2,930 (prior year Q4 2,280 out of annual 7,600). Closing receivables rose from ₹820 crore to ₹1,310 crore. Compute Q4 share of revenue for both years, closing DSO on annual and on Q4-annualised revenue, and explain whether the pattern is more consistent with seasonality or with channel stuffing, listing the additional evidence you would seek.

💡 Hint: Q1 of the following year's revenue and credit notes are the natural follow-up evidence.

Q3. ECL coverage analysis [L2]

Receivables ageing (₹ crore) for two years — less than 6 months: 900 / 940; 6–12 months: 120 / 210; 1–2 years: 60 / 150; more than 2 years: 40 / 110. ECL allowance: 75 / 82. Compute ECL as a % of total and of receivables over 1 year for both years, and draft a comment for an audit committee briefing.

Q4. Factoring and DSO [L3]

A company's DSO fell from 84 to 52 days in a year in which revenue grew 18% and CFO rose from ₹140 crore to ₹410 crore. The borrowings note is unchanged, but a new note discloses "receivables of ₹380 crore derecognised under a non-recourse arrangement with a bank; the company continues to collect cash as servicer". Explain the Ind AS 109 derecognition tests, how this affects CFO and DSO comparability, and how you would adjust both for analysis.
