Subtopic note

Other Income, Provision Reversals, RP Sales and Auditor/CFO Turnover

Scope: Other income share rising; provision reversals; related party sales; frequent auditor/CFO changes

Why it matters

Not all profit comes from operations, and not all operating profit is repeatable. A rising share of other income, releases of provisions made in earlier years, sales to related parties at undisclosed terms, and a revolving door in the CFO's chair or the auditor's office are softer signals than DSO or CFO/PAT, but they often explain how reported profit was achieved when the operating business was weak. Several Indian cases combined these: treasury income propping up a declining core business, "liabilities no longer required written back" in the year of a target, sales to promoter-linked distributors, and finance heads resigning in quick succession before a forensic audit. For valuation, these items belong outside the core earnings that are capitalised; for credit, they do not service debt sustainably.

Core notes

Other income

Schedule III requires other income to be shown separately from revenue from operations, with note disclosure of interest income, dividend income and other non-operating income (net of directly attributable expenses), and net gain or loss on sale of investments. Under Ind AS, other income also often includes fair value gains on FVTPL investments, foreign exchange gains, rental income, government grants (if presented as income), unwinding of discount on financial assets, and liabilities or provisions written back.

\[ \text{Other income share} = \frac{\text{Other income}}{\text{Profit before tax}} \]

\[ \text{Core PBT} = \text{PBT} - \text{Other income} + \text{Finance cost on borrowings funding non-operating assets (if identifiable)} \]

Flags: other income above 20–25% of PBT for a non-investment company, rising for consecutive years; interest income on loans to group companies that is accrued but not received (check the cash flow statement for interest received versus interest income); fair value gains on unlisted investments (Level 3) that drive PBT; and "miscellaneous income" without breakdown.

Provision reversals

Provisions under Ind AS 37 must be reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted to the current best estimate (Ind AS 37.59); a provision no longer required is reversed. ECL allowances under Ind AS 109 are remeasured each period, and reversals go to profit or loss. Legitimate reversals follow resolution of uncertainty; aggressive ones are "cookie jar" releases — reserves built in good years (or a "big bath" year) and released to meet targets.

Use the Ind AS 37.84 provisions movement table (opening, additions, utilisation, unused amounts reversed, unwinding of discount, closing) to identify reversals that the P&L face does not show.

Related party sales

Sales to related parties (subsidiaries not consolidated in standalone statements, associates, promoter-owned distributors or trading entities) can inflate revenue through circular transactions, non-arm's-length pricing or sales that are never collected. Indicators:

  • RPT sales growing faster than total revenue; RPT receivables outstanding longer than third-party receivables.
  • Year-end spikes in RPT sales, reversed or credited in the next period.
  • Related parties as major customers in the Ind AS 108.34 disclosure.
  • BRSR Core openness metrics: share of sales to dealers, distributors and related parties.
  • Sales to entities whose own financial statements (MCA filings) show losses and minimal capital.

Standalone revenue that includes sales to subsidiaries is eliminated on consolidation; a large gap between standalone and consolidated revenue growth is a quick test. Approvals under s.188 and LODR Reg 23 and the Form AOC-2 disclosure in the board's report should agree with the Ind AS 24 note.

Frequent auditor and CFO changes

The CFO is a key managerial personnel under s.2(51) and s.203; changes are disclosed under LODR Reg 30 (Schedule III Part A Para A item 7, change in KMP) and Reg 26A requires a CFO vacancy to be filled within three months. Auditor changes are covered in the auditor's report topic (s.139, s.140, the SEBI October 2019 circular).

Signals: two or more CFO changes in three years; a CFO resigning shortly before results or the audit report; resignation letters citing "personal reasons" followed by delayed results; an interim CFO from the promoter family; the head of internal audit or company secretary leaving at the same time; auditor resignation within the same window. Research on accounting restatements consistently associates CFO turnover with subsequent restatements, and Indian enforcement cases frequently show finance-leadership exits before the issue became public.

Illustration

A listed mid-cap engineering company (₹ crore):

Item FY 2022-23 FY 2023-24 FY 2024-25
PBT 210 230 245
Other income 28 54 92
of which interest on ICDs to group companies 12 31 58
Interest received (cash flow) 14 15 17
Provisions written back 4 9 31
Sales to related parties 180 290 470
Revenue 2,400 2,580 2,760
CFO changes 0 1 2

Other income share rose from 13% to 38% of PBT; core PBT (excluding other income) fell from ₹182 crore to ₹153 crore; interest on group ICDs is largely accrued but not received; provisions written back are 13% of PBT; related party sales rose from 7.5% to 17% of revenue; and two CFOs left in the latest year. Each item alone has an explanation; together they show that headline PBT growth of 17% over two years conceals a 16% fall in core profit and growing dependence on group entities.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cash-rich companies (IT services, some FMCG) legitimately earn large treasury income; assess the yield and whether treasury is managed conservatively, and separate it in valuation (cash valued separately).
  • Holding companies and investment companies have dividend and interest as core income; these ratios do not apply in the same way.
  • Large one-off reversals after a legal win are genuine; disclose and exclude from normalised earnings rather than penalise.
  • Related party sales in vertically integrated groups (for example, a captive distribution subsidiary) are structural; focus on pricing and collection terms.
  • CFO changes on promotion or group transfer are benign; check the successor's background and whether the change coincides with other signals.

Key takeaways

  • Separate other income from core profit and track its share of PBT; check accrued versus received interest on group loans.
  • Use the Ind AS 37.84 movement table to identify provision reversals hidden inside expense lines.
  • Track related party sales as a share of revenue and their collection, reconciling Ind AS 24, AOC-2 and Reg 23 approvals.
  • Treat clustered CFO, auditor, CS and internal audit exits as a governance red flag requiring follow-up.
  • Combine soft signals: each may be explicable, but together they often reveal declining core earnings.

Practice Questions

Q1. Core profit computation [L1]

PBT ₹480 crore includes other income of ₹136 crore (interest on fixed deposits ₹42 crore, fair value gain on unlisted Level 3 investment ₹58 crore, liabilities written back ₹21 crore, forex gain ₹15 crore). Compute other income share and core PBT, and state which components you would exclude from normalised earnings for a DCF and why.

Q2. Provision movement analysis [L2]

The warranty provision movement (₹ crore) shows: opening 120; additions 45; utilised 38; unused amounts reversed 41; closing 86. Revenue grew 12% and the warranty period is unchanged. Compute the reversal as a % of PBT of ₹310 crore, explain what the movement suggests about the estimate, and list the evidence that would justify the reversal.

💡 Hint: Compare additions with prior-year additions scaled for revenue growth.

Q3. Related party sales test [L2]

Standalone revenue grew 24% while consolidated revenue grew 9%; standalone sales to two wholly owned distribution subsidiaries rose from ₹310 crore to ₹690 crore; inventory at these subsidiaries rose from ₹140 crore to ₹420 crore. Explain what is happening, its effect on standalone versus consolidated profit, and what you would say in a red-flag memo.

Q4. Governance signal cluster [L3]

Within 14 months a listed company's CFO resigned (personal reasons), the interim CFO (a promoter's son-in-law) was appointed, the company secretary left, and the statutory auditor completed its term and was replaced by a smaller firm. Over the same period other income rose to 31% of PBT and related party sales doubled. Draft a 200-word governance risk paragraph with a severity rating and three specific information requests.
