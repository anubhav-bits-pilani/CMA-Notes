Key concept

Depreciation Method and Useful-Life Changes as Changes in Estimate

Concept: Ind AS 16 depreciation method/life change is change in estimate (Ind AS 8, prospective)

Statement

Under Ind AS 16, the residual value and useful life of an asset are reviewed at least at each financial year-end (Ind AS 16.51), and the depreciation method is reviewed at least at each financial year-end (Ind AS 16.61). If expectations differ from previous estimates, or the expected pattern of consumption of economic benefits has changed significantly, the change is accounted for as a change in accounting estimate under Ind AS 8, applied prospectively: it affects the period of change and future periods, with no restatement of prior periods (Ind AS 8.36–38). Ind AS 8.39 requires disclosure of the nature and amount of the change that affects the current period or is expected to affect future periods (unless estimating the future effect is impracticable).

For straight-line depreciation after a change at the start of year \( t \):

\[ \text{Depreciation}_t = \frac{\text{Carrying amount}_{t-1} - \text{Revised residual value}}{\text{Revised remaining useful life}} \]

Explanation

The classification matters because a change in estimate is not a change in accounting policy: there is no retrospective restatement, no third balance sheet (Ind AS 1.40A), and the effect falls entirely into the current and future P&L. That makes the treatment simple — and also makes life extensions an easy lever for raising profit, since the benefit appears immediately while the cost (lower depreciation over a longer period) is spread into the future.

The definition of accounting estimates inserted into Ind AS 8 by the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules 2023, effective from 1 April 2023 — "monetary amounts in financial statements that are subject to measurement uncertainty" — confirms that a change in a measurement technique or input used to develop an estimate is a change in estimate unless it results from the correction of a prior-period error. A depreciation method change (WDV to SLM) is therefore a change in estimate, not policy, under Ind AS. Under pre-2016 Indian GAAP (AS 6, Depreciation Accounting), a method change required retrospective recomputation with the cumulative difference taken to the P&L in the year of change; AS 10 (Revised) later aligned with the estimate approach. Analysts comparing long histories of Indian companies need to know this.

Companies Act Schedule II provides indicative useful lives; deviation requires disclosure of justification supported by technical advice. Changes must reflect genuine reassessment (technical evaluation, refurbishment, change in usage) — not profit targets. An error (for example, applying an obviously wrong life from inception) is corrected retrospectively under Ind AS 8.42, not prospectively.

Illustration

A power company's turbine: cost ₹900 crore, acquired 1 April 2019, life 25 years, residual value nil, straight line. Carrying amount at 31 March 2024: \( 900 - 5 \times 36 = 720 \) crore. On 1 April 2024, after a life-extension study, total life is revised to 35 years (remaining 30 years) and residual value to ₹45 crore. FY 2024-25 depreciation: \( \frac{720 - 45}{30} = 22.5 \) crore, versus 36 crore previously. Pre-tax profit increases by ₹13.5 crore in FY 2024-25 and in each remaining year; no restatement of FY 2019-20 to FY 2023-24. Ind AS 8.39 disclosure should state the ₹13.5 crore current-year effect and the expected effect on future periods.

Connections

In forecasting, a life change should be applied to the remaining depreciable base in the fixed asset schedule, not to gross block on the old rate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Prospective computation [L1]

Plant cost ₹480 crore, acquired 1 April 2021, life 12 years, residual ₹24 crore, straight line. On 1 April 2024 the remaining life is revised to 12 years (total 15) and residual to ₹12 crore. Compute FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 depreciation and the FY 2024-25 pre-tax effect.

Q2. Estimate or error? [L2]

In FY 2024-25 a company discovers that a class of moulds with a physical life of 3 years had been depreciated over 15 years since FY 2020-21, based on a misreading of Schedule II. Management proposes to revise the remaining life prospectively. Evaluate this treatment under Ind AS 8 and describe the correct accounting and disclosure.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the original estimate was a reasonable one based on information available at the time.

Q3. Forensic quantification [L2]

A listed telecom infrastructure company extended tower useful lives from 20 to 30 years in a year when EBITDA fell 6%. Depreciation fell from ₹1,240 crore to ₹890 crore, PAT rose from ₹410 crore to ₹520 crore, and the notes quantify the change as ₹360 crore. Compute PAT excluding the change (tax rate 25.17%), and draft the paragraph for an earnings-quality memo with a severity grade.
