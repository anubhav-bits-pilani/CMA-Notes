Key concept

Bill-and-Hold Criteria and Principal versus Agent Guidance

Concept: Ind AS 115 B79–B82 bill-and-hold criteria; B34–B38 principal vs agent

Statement

Bill-and-hold (Ind AS 115 B79–B82). In a bill-and-hold arrangement the entity bills the customer but retains physical possession. Revenue is recognised when the customer obtains control (Ind AS 115.38 indicators), and for the customer to have obtained control in a bill-and-hold arrangement all of the following criteria must be met (B81):

  1. the reason for the bill-and-hold arrangement is substantive (for example, the customer has requested it);
  2. the product is identified separately as belonging to the customer;
  3. the product is currently ready for physical transfer to the customer; and
  4. the entity cannot have the ability to use the product or to direct it to another customer.

If revenue is recognised, the entity considers whether it has remaining performance obligations (for example, custodial services) to which part of the transaction price is allocated (B82).

Principal versus agent (Ind AS 115 B34–B38). When another party is involved in providing goods or services, the entity determines whether it is a principal (it controls the specified good or service before transfer and recognises revenue at the gross amount) or an agent (it arranges for the other party to provide it and recognises revenue at the fee or commission, net). The assessment is made for each specified good or service. Indicators that the entity controls the specified good or service (B37) include: primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, and discretion in establishing the price.

Explanation

Both rules prevent the same problem from different directions: bill-and-hold prevents revenue being recognised too early (before control passes), and principal-versus-agent prevents revenue being recognised too large (gross instead of net). Neither necessarily changes profit over the life of the transaction, but both distort the metrics that drive valuation and covenants: revenue growth, receivables, margins and revenue multiples.

For bill-and-hold, the burden of evidence is high because the goods remain with the seller. Auditors look for a customer-initiated written request, segregation and tagging, inspection or acceptance, and evidence that the goods were not later redirected. For principal-versus-agent, the core question is control (B35): the indicators are evidence, not a checklist, and an entity can be primarily responsible for fulfilment yet still be an agent if it never controls the item (for example, a marketplace). The 2016 IFRS 15 clarifications, incorporated in Ind AS 115, removed "credit risk" and "commission-based consideration" from the indicators.

Illustration

Bill-and-hold: A defence electronics company bills ₹120 crore on 30 March for radar units held at its plant because the Ministry's storage facility is under construction; the units are tested, tagged with contract numbers, stored separately and cannot be used for other orders under the contract. The four criteria appear met; part of the price is allocated to six months' storage and maintenance (say ₹1.8 crore), deferred and recognised over the storage period.

Principal versus agent: A travel company sells holiday packages for ₹900 crore, of which ₹700 crore is hotel and airline components where it does not pre-purchase inventory, cannot set prices beyond a markup cap, and the suppliers bear service responsibility. If assessed as agent on these components with an average margin of 10%, revenue on them is ₹70 crore, not ₹700 crore, and total revenue falls from ₹900 crore to ₹270 crore, tripling the reported margin percentage while profit is unchanged.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply B81 [L2]

A steel trader bills ₹40 crore of coils on 31 March 2025 to a customer who has not requested delay; the coils remain in a common bay with other stock and the trader's dispatch plan shows them being sent to a different customer in April. Evaluate each B81 criterion and conclude on revenue recognition at 31 March.

Q2. Gross versus net decision [L2]

An IT services company resells third-party cloud subscriptions: it negotiates bulk pricing with the hyperscaler, sets its own price to the customer within a band, provides first-level support, but the hyperscaler delivers the service and is responsible for uptime under a direct agreement with the end customer. Annual resale billing is ₹650 crore with a 9% margin. Analyse control and the B37 indicators, conclude on presentation, and quantify the effect on revenue and EBITDA margin if total revenue is ₹4,200 crore and EBITDA ₹760 crore.

💡 Hint: Identify the specified service first; then ask who controls it before it reaches the customer.

Q3. Interview: why does gross-up matter if profit is unchanged? [L1]

Give three reasons why an analyst should care about principal-versus-agent classification even though it does not change profit.
