Key concept

Cash Flow Accrual Ratio

Concept: Accrual ratio (CF) = (PAT − CFO − CFI) ÷ Average net operating assets

Statement

\[ \text{Accrual ratio}^{\text{CF}}_t = \frac{\text{PAT}_t - (\text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t)}{\left(\text{NOA}_t + \text{NOA}_{t-1}\right) / 2} \]

where \( \text{PAT}_t \) is profit after tax, \( \text{CFO}_t \) is net cash from operating activities, \( \text{CFI}_t \) is net cash from investing activities (normally negative, so subtracting it adds back investment outflows), and NOA is net operating assets as defined in c01. Written with the sign made explicit, the numerator is \( \text{PAT} - \text{CFO} - \text{CFI} \).

Explanation

The cash flow version measures accruals directly from the cash flow statement: profit that was not matched by cash generated from operating and investing activities combined. Including CFI captures expenditure capitalised into PPE, CWIP and intangibles — costs that never pass through CFO. Two companies with identical CFO/PAT can therefore have very different CF accrual ratios if one is capitalising heavily.

In theory the BS and CF versions should be close, since both measure the change in NOA; differences arise from non-cash acquisitions, foreign exchange translation, reclassifications, and how investments are classified. A large gap between the two is itself a reason to check the reconciliation.

Indian adjustments needed before computing:

  • Treasury flows in CFI: purchases and sales of mutual funds and fixed deposits sit in CFI under Ind AS 7. Because NOA excludes cash and short-term investments, treasury movements must be removed from CFI, otherwise a company that parks cash in liquid funds appears to have large negative CFI and a misleadingly low (or negative) accrual ratio.
  • Interest and dividends received are in CFI under Ind AS 7.33 for non-financial entities; they are returns on non-operating assets and are best excluded from CFI (and the corresponding income excluded from PAT, or both left in consistently).
  • Acquisitions of subsidiaries (net of cash acquired) are in CFI and are operating investments; include them but flag the year.
  • Loans to group companies are typically in CFI; include them — they are a classic route for cash leakage that this measure catches.

Interpretation mirrors the BS ratio: higher is worse for earnings quality; compare with history and peers; a high ratio in a capex phase needs to be judged against expected returns on the new assets.

Illustration

FY 2024-25 for a listed consumer company (₹ crore): PAT 640; CFO 410; CFI (–)980, comprising capex (–)520, purchase of liquid mutual funds (–)300, interest received +40, loan to promoter group company (–)200. Average NOA 3,200.

Unadjusted: \( \frac{640 - 410 - (-980)}{3{,}200} = \frac{1{,}210}{3{,}200} = 37.8\% \). Adjusted for treasury (remove the –300 mutual fund purchase and +40 interest received): CFI becomes (–)720; ratio = \( \frac{640 - 410 + 720}{3{,}200} = \frac{950}{3{,}200} = 29.7\% \). Removing the treasury distortion lowers the ratio, but 29.7% is still high, driven by capex and the ₹200 crore group loan; the group loan alone contributes 6.3 percentage points.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both ratios [L2]

Data (₹ crore): PAT 380; CFO 260; CFI (–)540 (capex –410, purchase of fixed deposits over 3 months –160, interest received +30); NOA opening 2,600, closing 3,050. Compute the CF accrual ratio unadjusted and adjusted for treasury items, and the BS accrual ratio. Explain any difference between the adjusted CF and BS ratios.

💡 Hint: Fixed deposits are cash-like for NOA purposes even though Ind AS 7 puts them in CFI.

Q2. Same CFO/PAT, different quality [L2]

Company P and Company Q each report PAT ₹500 crore and CFO ₹450 crore. P's CFI is (–)150 (maintenance capex); Q's CFI is (–)620, including ₹280 crore of capitalised development cost and ₹190 crore of capital advances to a new vendor. Both have average NOA of ₹4,000 crore. Compute CF accrual ratios and explain why CFO/PAT alone is insufficient.

Q3. Excel build [L1]

Write Excel formulas (using named ranges) to compute the adjusted CF accrual ratio for five years, with treasury CFI items held in a separate input row, and a flag when the ratio exceeds both 15% and the company's own five-year median.
