Key concept

Balance Sheet Accrual Ratio

Concept: Accrual ratio (BS) = ΔNet operating assets ÷ Average net operating assets

Statement

\[ \text{Accrual ratio}^{\text{BS}}_t = \frac{\text{NOA}_t - \text{NOA}_{t-1}}{\left(\text{NOA}_t + \text{NOA}_{t-1}\right) / 2} \]

\[ \text{NOA} = \underbrace{(\text{Total assets} - \text{Cash and short-term investments})}_{\text{Operating assets}} - \underbrace{(\text{Total liabilities} - \text{Total debt})}_{\text{Operating liabilities}} \]

where \( \text{NOA}_t \) is net operating assets at the end of year \( t \); total debt includes short-term and long-term borrowings, current maturities and (consistently) lease liabilities; and cash and short-term investments include cash, bank balances, liquid mutual funds and other current investments.

Explanation

The balance sheet approach measures accruals as the growth in net operating assets: if profit exceeds cash generated from operating and investing activities, the difference must show up as growth in operating assets (receivables, inventory, PPE, CWIP, capitalised costs) or a fall in operating liabilities. Scaling by average NOA makes the measure comparable across company sizes and removes the effect of financing choices (debt and cash are excluded). This version, popularised in the CFA Institute's financial reporting quality curriculum, captures both working-capital accruals and long-term operating accruals (capex and capitalised costs), unlike the older working-capital-only measures.

Interpretation: higher ratios mean a larger accrual component in earnings and, empirically (Sloan 1996 and subsequent work), lower earnings persistence. There is no universal threshold; compare against the company's own history and its peers. Sustained ratios above about 10–15% for a mature business, or a sharp rise relative to peers, warrant investigation. A high ratio can be legitimate for a company in a capex phase or acquiring businesses, so pair it with revenue growth: NOA growing much faster than revenue is the concern.

Indian implementation issues:

  • Investments: classify strategic investments in subsidiaries and associates (standalone) as operating or non-operating consistently; treasury investments (mutual funds, bonds) are excluded as cash-like.
  • Lease liabilities: either include in debt (and ROU assets in operating assets) or exclude both; mixing inflates or deflates NOA.
  • Deferred tax and provisions are operating liabilities by default.
  • Loans to related parties are often presented as financial assets; include them in operating assets (they are not cash), which captures cash leakage into group entities.
  • Acquisitions step up NOA; use a pro forma prior-year NOA or flag the year.

Illustration

A listed auto component company (₹ crore):

Item FY 2023-24 FY 2024-25
Total assets 6,200 7,450
Cash and current investments 540 410
Total liabilities 3,300 4,250
Total debt (incl. lease liabilities) 1,450 2,150

NOA FY 2023-24 = (6,200 − 540) − (3,300 − 1,450) = 5,660 − 1,850 = 3,810. NOA FY 2024-25 = (7,450 − 410) − (4,250 − 2,150) = 7,040 − 2,100 = 4,940. Accrual ratio = \( \frac{4{,}940 - 3{,}810}{(4{,}940 + 3{,}810)/2} = \frac{1{,}130}{4{,}375} = 25.8\% \). If revenue grew 8%, NOA growth of 30% is far ahead of the business, and the analyst should break down which operating assets grew (receivables, CWIP, capital advances, loans to group companies).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the BS accrual ratio [L1]

Compute NOA and the balance sheet accrual ratio for FY 2024-25 (₹ crore): total assets 3,100 and 3,680; cash and liquid investments 260 and 190; total liabilities 1,700 and 2,060; total debt 820 and 1,140 (prior year, current year).

Q2. Classification sensitivity [L2]

A company has loans to promoter group entities of ₹420 crore (prior ₹180 crore) presented as current financial assets and liquid mutual funds of ₹300 crore (prior ₹600 crore). Other data: operating assets excluding these ₹2,800 crore (prior ₹2,500 crore); operating liabilities ₹900 crore (prior ₹850 crore). Compute the accrual ratio treating the group loans (a) as operating assets and (b) as cash-like, and explain which treatment is analytically correct.

💡 Hint: Ask whether the loans are available to repay debt tomorrow.

Q3. Interpretation [L2]

Two peers have BS accrual ratios of 22% and 6%. The first has revenue growth of 25% and a new plant commissioned mid-year; the second has revenue growth of 3%. Explain why the raw ratio may mislead and propose an adjusted comparison.
