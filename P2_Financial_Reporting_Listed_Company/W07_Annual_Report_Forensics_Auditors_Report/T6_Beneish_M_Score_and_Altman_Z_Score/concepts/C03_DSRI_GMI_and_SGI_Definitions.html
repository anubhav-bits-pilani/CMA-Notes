Key concept

DSRI, GMI and SGI Definitions

Concept: DSRI = (Rec_t ÷ Sales_t) ÷ (Rec_t−1 ÷ Sales_t−1); GMI = GM_t−1 ÷ GM_t; SGI = Sales_t ÷ Sales_t−1

Statement

\[ \text{DSRI} = \frac{\text{Rec}_t / \text{Sales}_t}{\text{Rec}_{t-1} / \text{Sales}_{t-1}}, \qquad \text{GMI} = \frac{\text{GM}_{t-1}}{\text{GM}_t}, \qquad \text{SGI} = \frac{\text{Sales}_t}{\text{Sales}_{t-1}} \]

with

\[ \text{GM}_t = \frac{\text{Sales}_t - \text{COGS}_t}{\text{Sales}_t} \]

where \( \text{Rec}_t \) is trade receivables at the end of year \( t \) (current plus non-current; optionally plus contract assets/unbilled revenue, consistently), \( \text{Sales}_t \) is revenue from operations for year \( t \), and \( \text{COGS}_t \) is cost of goods sold (Indian proxy: materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories, plus separable production costs).

Explanation

DSRI compares receivables-to-sales (equivalently, days' sales in receivables) across two years. A value above 1 means receivables have grown faster than revenue — consistent with revenue recognised before cash is collectible, fictitious sales, channel stuffing on extended credit, or a genuine shift in customer mix or credit terms. It has a coefficient of 0.920, making it one of the most influential variables.

GMI is inverted (prior over current) so that a value above 1 signals deterioration in gross margin. Beneish argued that firms with worsening prospects are more likely to manipulate. Note that a GMI above 1 is a motive indicator, not evidence; margin pressure from raw material inflation (Week 7 topic 2 s03) raises GMI without any manipulation.

SGI captures growth. Growth itself is not manipulation, but high-growth firms face capital-market pressure to sustain growth and have weaker controls relative to scale; when growth slows, incentives to manipulate rise.

Conditions and computation notes:

  • Use year-end receivables, not averages, as Beneish did.
  • Revenue should be net of GST and exclude other income.
  • If \( \text{GM}_t \le 0 \), GMI is undefined or misleading; cap or set to a stated value.
  • In acquisition years, use pro forma or restated comparatives for both Sales and Rec.

Illustration

Listed EPC company (₹ crore):

Item FY24 FY25
Revenue from operations 3,200 4,000
Trade receivables 800 1,150
Contract assets (unbilled) 400 850
COGS proxy 2,560 3,280
  • DSRI (trade receivables only) = (1,150/4,000) ÷ (800/3,200) = 0.2875 ÷ 0.25 = 1.150.
  • DSRI (incl. contract assets) = (2,000/4,000) ÷ (1,200/3,200) = 0.50 ÷ 0.375 = 1.333.
  • GM: FY24 = 0.200; FY25 = 0.180; GMI = 1.111.
  • SGI = 1.250.

The contract-asset-inclusive DSRI adds \( 0.920 \times (1.333 - 1.150) = 0.168 \) to M. Days of revenue in receivables plus contract assets rose from 137 to 183 days.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the three indices [L1]

Revenue ₹1,800 crore (FY24) and ₹2,250 crore (FY25); trade receivables ₹270 crore and ₹495 crore; COGS ₹1,170 crore and ₹1,530 crore. Compute DSRI, GMI and SGI and the combined contribution of the three to M.

Q2. Business model or red flag? [L2]

A listed pharma company shows DSRI 1.45 as it shifted from domestic trade sales (30-day credit) to US generics sales through distributors (90-day credit, with chargebacks and rebates). Explain how you would distinguish a business-model shift from aggressive recognition, and which Ind AS 115 disclosures you would read.

💡 Hint: Segment and geographic revenue mix, contract liability and refund liability movements are useful.

Q3. GMI with negative margins [L2]

A company's gross margin moved from 4% to −2% during a commodity shock. Explain why GMI breaks down and propose a defensible treatment in the model.
