Key concept

M-Score Cut-offs: −1.78 and −2.22

Concept: Cut-offs: M above −1.78 (Beneish 1999) flags likely manipulation; −2.22 widely used as conservative screen

Statement

\[ \text{Flag}(M) = \begin{cases} \text{Likely manipulator} & M > -1.78 \\ \text{Watch / grey zone} & -2.22 < M \le -1.78 \\ \text{Unlikely manipulator} & M \le -2.22 \end{cases} \]

The −1.78 cut-off comes from Beneish (1999) for the 8-variable model and corresponds to the threshold that minimised expected misclassification costs at the relative error costs he considered plausible for investors (a missed manipulator being far costlier than a false alarm). The −2.22 cut-off is widely used by practitioners as a more conservative screen (it flags more companies); it is commonly associated with later practitioner literature and the 5-variable variant, so state the source you rely on.

Explanation

A cut-off on a probit score trades off two errors:

  • Type I — failing to flag a manipulator (costly for investors and lenders).
  • Type II — flagging a non-manipulator (costly in analyst time and reputational harm).

Lowering the cut-off from −1.78 to −2.22 catches more manipulators but at the price of many more false positives. Beneish reported that at his preferred cut-off the model identified roughly half of manipulators in the holdout sample while misclassifying a modest fraction of non-manipulators; the exact rates depend on the assumed cost ratio.

How to use the cut-offs in practice:

  1. Treat −1.78 as a flag requiring investigation, not a conclusion.
  2. Treat −2.22 to −1.78 as a watch list, especially if the trend is rising.
  3. Below −2.22, do not conclude the accounts are clean — the model misses manipulation types such as fictitious cash and related-party leakage (s05).
  4. Look at change over time: a jump of +0.7 over two years is often more informative than the level.
  5. Compare with sector peers in the same year (growth cycles move whole sectors).

Illustration

Five-year M-scores for an NSE-listed infrastructure company:

FY M Flag Comment
FY21 −2.65 Unlikely
FY22 −2.40 Unlikely SGI 1.25 post-COVID recovery
FY23 −2.10 Watch DSRI 1.30, unbilled revenue up
FY24 −1.85 Watch TATA 0.05; CFO/PAT 0.4
FY25 −1.60 Likely manipulator DSRI 1.25, AQI 1.35 (capital advances to related EPC)

The trend crosses −2.22 two years before crossing −1.78. A screen using only −1.78 would have flagged in FY25; the conservative screen in FY23. The qualitative drivers (unbilled revenue, CFO/PAT, related-party capital advances) are what the memo should lead with.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify a portfolio [L1]

Classify the following M-scores under both cut-offs and state how many companies each screen flags: −3.10, −2.30, −2.20, −1.95, −1.79, −1.77, −1.20, −0.40.

Q2. Choosing a cut-off [L2]

You run a credit early-warning screen for a bank's mid-corporate book of 400 borrowers with limited analyst capacity (20 deep-dives per quarter). Explain how you would choose between −1.78 and −2.22, and how you would prioritise within the flagged list.

💡 Hint: Combine the level, the change in score and the size of exposure.

Q3. Interview: what does −1.78 mean? [L1]

In under 120 words, explain to an interviewer what an M-score of −1.60 does and does not tell you about a company.
