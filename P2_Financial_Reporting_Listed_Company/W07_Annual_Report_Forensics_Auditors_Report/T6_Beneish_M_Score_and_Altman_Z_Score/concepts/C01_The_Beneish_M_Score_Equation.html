Key concept

The Beneish M-Score Equation

Concept: M = −4.84 + 0.920 DSRI + 0.528 GMI + 0.404 AQI + 0.892 SGI + 0.115 DEPI − 0.172 SGAI + 4.679 TATA − 0.327 LVGI

Statement

\[ M = -4.84 + 0.920\,\text{DSRI} + 0.528\,\text{GMI} + 0.404\,\text{AQI} + 0.892\,\text{SGI} + 0.115\,\text{DEPI} - 0.172\,\text{SGAI} + 4.679\,\text{TATA} - 0.327\,\text{LVGI} \]

where DSRI is the days' sales in receivables index, GMI the gross margin index, AQI the asset quality index, SGI the sales growth index, DEPI the depreciation index, SGAI the SG&A expense index, TATA total accruals to total assets, and LVGI the leverage index — each computed for year \( t \) relative to \( t-1 \) (c03, c04). DEPI, SGAI and LVGI are:

\[ \text{DEPI} = \frac{\text{Dep}_{t-1} / (\text{Dep}_{t-1} + \text{PPE}_{t-1})}{\text{Dep}_{t} / (\text{Dep}_{t} + \text{PPE}_{t})}, \quad \text{SGAI} = \frac{\text{SGA}_t / \text{Sales}_t}{\text{SGA}_{t-1} / \text{Sales}_{t-1}}, \quad \text{LVGI} = \frac{(\text{CL}_t + \text{LTD}_t) / \text{TA}_t}{(\text{CL}_{t-1} + \text{LTD}_{t-1}) / \text{TA}_{t-1}} \]

\( M \) is a probit score: the estimated probability of manipulation is \( \Phi(M) \), where \( \Phi \) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function.

Explanation

Messod Beneish (1999, Financial Analysts Journal) estimated the model by weighted exogenous sample maximum likelihood probit on 74 US firms subject to SEC enforcement for earnings overstatement and about 2,300 non-manipulator controls matched by industry, for 1982–1992. The signs are intuitive for six variables: receivables growing faster than sales, deteriorating margins, rising soft assets, high growth, slowing depreciation and high accruals all raise M. Two coefficients were statistically insignificant in the original study and carry "unexpected" signs: SGAI (negative) and LVGI (negative). They are retained in the 8-variable model for completeness; a 5-variable version (DSRI, GMI, AQI, SGI, TATA) is also used.

Key properties:

  • A firm with all indices at 1 and TATA at 0 scores −2.48 (\( \Phi(-2.48) \approx 0.7\% \)).
  • TATA's large coefficient means accruals of 5% of total assets add 0.23 to M.
  • Because \( \Phi \) is non-linear, moving from −2.5 to −2.0 raises the implied probability from about 0.6% to 2.3%; from −1.78 to −1.0 raises it from about 3.8% to 16%. These are sample-conditional probabilities, not population probabilities.
