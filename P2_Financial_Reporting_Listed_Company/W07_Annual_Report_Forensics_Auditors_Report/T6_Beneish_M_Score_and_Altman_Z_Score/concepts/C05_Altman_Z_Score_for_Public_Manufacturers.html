Key concept

Altman Z-Score for Public Manufacturers

Concept: Z = 1.2X1 + 1.4X2 + 3.3X3 + 0.6X4 + 1.0X5 (X1 WC/TA, X2 RE/TA, X3 EBIT/TA, X4 MV equity/TL, X5 Sales/TA); safe above 2.99, distress below 1.81

Statement

\[ Z = 1.2X_1 + 1.4X_2 + 3.3X_3 + 0.6X_4 + 1.0X_5 \]

\[ X_1 = \frac{\text{WC}}{\text{TA}}, \quad X_2 = \frac{\text{RE}}{\text{TA}}, \quad X_3 = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{TA}}, \quad X_4 = \frac{\text{MVE}}{\text{TL}}, \quad X_5 = \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{TA}} \]

where WC is working capital (current assets − current liabilities), TA total assets, RE retained earnings, EBIT earnings before interest and tax, MVE market value of equity (shares × price), TL total liabilities, and Sales revenue from operations. Ratios are decimals. Zones:

\[ Z > 2.99 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{safe}; \qquad 1.81 \le Z \le 2.99 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{grey}; \qquad Z < 1.81 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{distress} \]

Explanation

Edward Altman (1968, Journal of Finance) used multiple discriminant analysis on 66 US publicly traded manufacturing firms (33 that filed for bankruptcy 1946–1965 and 33 matched survivors). The model classified the original sample with high accuracy one year before failure, with accuracy falling for two or more years ahead.

Interpretation of each ratio:

  • X1 liquidity: shrinking working capital relative to assets precedes distress.
  • X2 cumulative profitability: young or historically loss-making firms have low RE; it also reflects dividend policy and leverage history.
  • X3 earning power: the most heavily weighted (3.3); asset values ultimately depend on earning power.
  • X4 market cushion: how far equity value can fall before liabilities exceed assets. Uses market value, so it embeds investor expectations — and market noise.
  • X5 asset turnover: industry-sensitive, which is why Z'' drops it.

Indian application rules:

  • Use for listed manufacturers with liquid shares; cross-check with Z'' (c06).
  • RE: use the retained earnings component of other equity (statement of changes in equity), not total other equity (which includes securities premium, capital reserves and OCI reserves). Including general reserve is defensible because it arose from profit transfers; state the choice.
  • EBIT: PBT before exceptional items + finance costs.
  • MVE: shares outstanding × NSE closing price on 31 March.
  • TL: total non-current + current liabilities, including lease liabilities and deferred tax liabilities.

Illustration

Listed auto-component manufacturer, 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): current assets 1,400; current liabilities 1,000; retained earnings 900; total assets 3,500; EBIT 280; market cap 2,100; total liabilities 1,900; revenue 3,850.

Ratio Value Weighted
X1 = 400/3,500 0.114 0.137
X2 = 900/3,500 0.257 0.360
X3 = 280/3,500 0.080 0.264
X4 = 2,100/1,900 1.105 0.663
X5 = 3,850/3,500 1.100 1.100
Z 2.52

Grey zone. Using total other equity of ₹1,500 crore (retained earnings ₹900 crore plus ₹600 crore securities premium) for X2 would raise Z to about 2.76 — overstating safety (topic pitfall).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute Z [L1]

A listed steel company (₹ crore): current assets 8,000; current liabilities 9,500; retained earnings 6,000; total assets 40,000; EBIT 2,400; market cap 18,000; total liabilities 26,000; revenue 44,000. Compute Z and state the zone.

Q2. Market value sensitivity [L2]

For the illustration company, compute the share-price fall that would push Z below 1.81, holding all else constant. Comment on whether that signal would reflect fundamentals.

💡 Hint: Solve for the market value of equity that gives the threshold, then compare with the current market cap.

Q3. Wrong variant [L2]

An analyst computes Z = 4.6 for an NSE-listed IT services company with sales/TA of 1.6 and market cap/TL of 6.0. Explain why the result is not meaningful and what you would compute instead.
