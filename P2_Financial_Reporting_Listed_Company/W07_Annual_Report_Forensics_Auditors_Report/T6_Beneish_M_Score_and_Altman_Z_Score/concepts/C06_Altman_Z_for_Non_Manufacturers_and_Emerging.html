Key concept

Altman Z'' for Non-Manufacturers and Emerging Markets

Concept: Z'' = 6.56X1 + 3.26X2 + 6.72X3 + 1.05X4 (X4 = book equity/TL); safe above 2.60, distress below 1.10

Statement

\[ Z'' = 6.56X_1 + 3.26X_2 + 6.72X_3 + 1.05X_4 \]

with \( X_1 = \text{WC}/\text{TA} \), \( X_2 = \text{RE}/\text{TA} \), \( X_3 = \text{EBIT}/\text{TA} \) as in the original model, and

\[ X_4 = \frac{\text{Book value of equity}}{\text{Total liabilities}} \]

Zones:

\[ Z'' > 2.60 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{safe}; \qquad 1.10 \le Z'' \le 2.60 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{grey}; \qquad Z'' < 1.10 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{distress} \]

The emerging-market score (EMS) variant adds a constant: \( \text{EMS} = 3.25 + Z'' \), with zones shifted to about 5.85 (safe) and 4.35 (distress), and mapped to bond-rating equivalents.

Explanation

Altman (1995, with Hartzell and Peck for emerging-market corporates) re-estimated the model without X5 (sales/total assets) because asset turnover varies widely by industry — retailers turn assets several times a year, utilities and infrastructure firms less than once — and because it is sensitive to country-specific factors. Book equity replaces market equity for broader applicability (private firms, illiquid markets). The coefficients are larger than in Z because the remaining ratios must carry the discrimination.

When to use Z'' in India:

  • Non-manufacturers: IT and ITeS, retail, hospitality, healthcare services, telecom, media, logistics, infrastructure and construction.
  • Any company where cross-sector or cross-country comparison is needed.
  • Listed companies with illiquid or volatile share prices (book X4 is stable).
  • Not for banks, NBFCs, insurers (s05).

Interpretation cautions:

  • X1 has a coefficient of 6.56 — negative working capital (FMCG, quick-service restaurants, airlines with advance ticket sales) depresses Z'' heavily even for strong firms.
  • X3 at 6.72 makes Z'' highly sensitive to EBIT; a cyclical trough can push a sound company into the grey zone.
  • Book equity can be inflated by revaluation or capitalised costs, overstating X4; it can also be depressed by large buybacks or Ind AS 116 transition adjustments to retained earnings.
  • Never compare a Z'' value with Z cut-offs (2.99/1.81), and never mix EMS (with 3.25) with plain Z'' thresholds.

Illustration

Listed hotel company, 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): current assets 900; current liabilities 1,100; retained earnings 400; total assets 6,000; EBIT 540; book equity 2,800; total liabilities 3,200.

Ratio Value Weighted
X1 = −200/6,000 −0.033 −0.219
X2 = 400/6,000 0.067 0.217
X3 = 540/6,000 0.090 0.605
X4 = 2,800/3,200 0.875 0.919
Z'' 1.52

Grey zone (EMS = 4.77, also grey). Low retained earnings (after pandemic losses) and negative working capital drive the result; improving EBIT is the main lever. Applying the original Z with market cap ₹12,000 crore and sales/TA of 0.45 would give a very different and less meaningful picture.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute Z'' and EMS [L1]

A listed logistics company (₹ crore): current assets 1,100; current liabilities 950; retained earnings 650; total assets 3,800; EBIT 310; book equity 1,700; total liabilities 2,100. Compute Z'' and EMS, and state the zone under each.

Q2. Negative working capital business [L2]

A listed quick-service restaurant chain has X1 = −0.08, X2 = 0.05, X3 = 0.12 and X4 = 0.60. Compute Z'' and explain whether the grey or distress result reflects true insolvency risk, citing business-model features.

💡 Hint: Consider how supplier credit and cash sales fund the working capital.

Q3. Choosing between Z and Z'' [L2]

For a listed cement company and a listed IT services company, compute (with your own reasonable assumptions stated) both Z and Z'', and explain why the gap between the two scores is much larger for the IT company.
