Subtopic note

Indian Proxies: SG&A, COGS, Lease Liabilities and Ind AS Adjustments

Scope: Indian proxies: SG&A = employee benefits + other expenses (excl. power/freight if separable); LTD incl. lease liabilities consistently

Why it matters

Beneish and Altman were estimated on US filings, where the income statement shows cost of goods sold and SG&A directly. Schedule III Division II uses a nature-of-expense format: cost of materials consumed, purchases of stock-in-trade, changes in inventories, employee benefits expense, finance costs, depreciation and amortisation, and other expenses. Ind AS 116 (effective 1 April 2019) moved most operating leases onto the balance sheet, changing leverage, depreciation and EBIT. Unless you define proxies and apply them identically in years t and t−1, the indices measure your mapping choices rather than the company. Defensible proxies are also what an interviewer or reviewer will probe first.

Core notes

SG&A proxy

Base proxy: SG&A = Employee benefits expense + Other expenses.

Rationale: in a nature-of-expense format, employee costs and other expenses together contain the selling, general and administrative costs that US companies report as SG&A. But they also contain manufacturing costs, so refine:

Component of Other expenses (from the note) Treatment in SGA Reason
Power and fuel Exclude (move to COGS) if separable Manufacturing cost; volatile with energy prices
Freight and forwarding / outward freight Exclude (or move to COGS) if separable Volume-driven distribution cost; often large for cement, steel, FMCG
Stores, spares, packing materials consumed Exclude (move to COGS) Production cost
Sub-contracting / job work charges Exclude (move to COGS) Production cost
Advertisement and sales promotion Include Classic selling expense
Rent, rates and taxes, insurance, repairs (non-plant), travel, legal and professional, communication Include Administrative
CSR expenditure Include or exclude consistently Statutory, not efficiency-related; many analysts exclude
Provision for doubtful debts / ECL, bad debts written off Exclude (or show separately) Credit cost; mixing with SGA mutes DSRI signal
Exchange loss, loss on sale of assets, impairment in other expenses Exclude Non-operating or non-recurring

Employee benefits expense includes factory wages. Where the company discloses manufacturing vs other employee costs (rare), exclude factory wages; otherwise include all, and note that SGAI will be affected by production labour intensity. The key requirement is consistency: the same rule in t and t−1.

COGS proxy (for GMI)

COGS = Cost of materials consumed + Purchases of stock-in-trade + Changes in inventories of finished goods, WIP and stock-in-trade, plus any items moved out of other expenses (power and fuel, freight inward, stores and spares, job work). If you move them out of SGA, you must move them into COGS — otherwise you lose costs. For service companies (IT, consulting), there is no materials line; use employee benefits for delivery staff plus sub-contracting as COGS, or set GMI to 1 and document it.

Long-term debt (LTD) including lease liabilities

LVGI uses current liabilities plus long-term debt over total assets. Under Ind AS 116:

  • Non-current lease liabilities are presented separately from borrowings (Schedule III requires lease liabilities as a separate line under financial liabilities).
  • Current lease liabilities are within current liabilities.

Rule: LTD = Non-current borrowings + Non-current lease liabilities, in both years. Since current lease liabilities are already in CL, this treats all lease obligations as debt. Mixing (including lease liabilities in t but not in t−1, or across a transition year) creates a spurious LVGI jump. For FY 2019-20 vs FY 2018-19 (Ind AS 116 transition under the modified retrospective approach, without restated comparatives), either exclude leases in both years or add back pro forma lease liabilities to the earlier year.

Consistency with ROU assets and depreciation

If leases are in LTD, the corresponding ROU assets should be:

  • in PPE for AQI (otherwise ROU assets are counted as "soft assets" and AQI rises);
  • in PPE for DEPI, with ROU depreciation included in Dep (the Schedule III depreciation line usually includes ROU depreciation; the PPE/ROU notes give the split).

A common error is to include ROU assets in PPE but use only PPE depreciation, which lowers the depreciation rate and raises DEPI artificially (see topic pitfall).

Other Indian proxies

Model input Proxy choice Comment
Receivables Trade receivables (current + non-current), optionally + contract assets/unbilled revenue Include contract assets for IT/EPC to capture unbilled build-up
CWIP and capital advances Either in PPE or in "soft assets"; state choice Including CWIP in soft assets makes AQI sensitive to project build-out (Week 7 topic 3 s04)
Intangible assets under development Soft assets Captures capitalised development cost
Income from continuing operations PAT from continuing operations Use profit before OCI
EBIT (Altman X3) PBT before exceptional items + finance costs Finance costs include interest on lease liabilities — consistent with treating leases as debt
Retained earnings (Altman X2) Retained earnings (+ general reserve, stated) Not total other equity
Market value of equity NSE closing price × shares at 31 March Use free-float-unadjusted market cap

Illustration — effect of proxy choice

Cement company, FY24/FY25 (₹ crore): revenue 10,000/11,000; employee 600/650; other expenses 3,800/4,400, of which power and fuel 1,900/2,300 and freight 1,200/1,350.

  • Base proxy SGA = 4,400 / 5,050 → SGAI = (5,050/11,000) ÷ (4,400/10,000) = 0.4591 ÷ 0.44 = 1.043.
  • Refined SGA (excluding power, freight) = 1,300 / 1,400 → SGAI = (1,400/11,000) ÷ (1,300/10,000) = 0.1273 ÷ 0.13 = 0.979.

The base proxy shows rising SG&A intensity (driven by an energy price spike), the refined proxy shows improving efficiency. With a coefficient of −0.172 the M-score effect is small (about 0.011), but in GMI — if power and freight are moved into COGS — the effect is larger because GMI's coefficient is 0.528.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Companies that report "manufacturing and other expenses" as one line with no split: use the base proxy and disclose the limitation.
  • Function-of-expense format is not allowed under Schedule III for the face of P&L, but some companies give segment or functional notes in MD&A; use them only if available for both years.
  • Capitalised employee costs (in CWIP or intangibles) reduce employee benefits expense; add back if material and disclosed, to avoid SGAI understating costs.
  • Lease liabilities for short-term or low-value leases are not recognised; their rent sits in other expenses — fine, as long as consistent.
  • Restated comparatives must be used if reclassifications occurred (Schedule III regrouping notes).

Key takeaways

  • SGA proxy = employee benefits + other expenses, excluding power and fuel, freight and other production-linked costs where separable; move exclusions into COGS.
  • COGS proxy = materials + purchases + inventory change (+ moved items); for services, set GMI carefully.
  • LTD = non-current borrowings + non-current lease liabilities in both years; current lease liabilities stay in CL.
  • Treat ROU assets and ROU depreciation consistently with lease liabilities in AQI and DEPI.
  • Document every proxy and apply it identically in t and t−1; test alternatives in sensitivity (s06).

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the proxies [L2]

An FMCG company's other expenses note shows (FY24/FY25, ₹ crore): power and fuel 120/140; freight 300/340; advertisement 650/780; rent 90/100; repairs (plant) 60/65; legal and professional 40/70; CSR 25/28; ECL provision 5/30; exchange loss 0/15; miscellaneous 110/120. Employee benefits are 820/900 and revenue 14,000/15,500. Compute SGAI under the base proxy and under a refined proxy, stating each inclusion/exclusion.

💡 Hint: Decide where each excluded item goes (COGS or outside both) before computing.

Q2. Lease consistency check [L1]

A retailer reports non-current borrowings ₹200 crore, non-current lease liabilities ₹1,800 crore, current lease liabilities ₹300 crore, ROU assets ₹1,900 crore and PPE (excluding ROU) ₹700 crore. Explain how each enters LVGI, AQI and DEPI under a consistent treatment and what goes wrong if ROU depreciation is omitted from DEPI.

Q3. Transition year problem [L2]

A hotel company adopted Ind AS 116 on 1 April 2019 using the modified retrospective approach, recognising lease liabilities of ₹900 crore with no restatement of FY 2018-19. Total assets were ₹4,000 crore (FY19) and ₹4,950 crore (FY20); CL plus borrowings were ₹1,600 crore and ₹1,700 crore excluding leases. Compute LVGI including leases only in FY20, and propose a consistent alternative.

Q4. Reviewer's challenge [L3]

A junior analyst's M-score sheet for an IT services company sets GMI using "revenue minus employee benefits" as gross margin and includes all other expenses in SGA. Critique the choice, propose an alternative mapping, and describe the sensitivity test you would run before presenting the score.
