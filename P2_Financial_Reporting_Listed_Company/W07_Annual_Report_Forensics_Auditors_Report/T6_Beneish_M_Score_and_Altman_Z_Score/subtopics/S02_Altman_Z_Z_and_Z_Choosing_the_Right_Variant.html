Subtopic note

Altman Z, Z' and Z'': Choosing the Right Variant

Scope: Altman Z (public manufacturers), Z' (private firms, book equity) and Z'' (non-manufacturers/emerging markets)

Why it matters

The Altman Z-score is a discriminant model of bankruptcy risk built from five accounting and market ratios. Credit analysts, auditors assessing going concern (SA 570 indicators), lenders' early-warning systems and equity screens all use it. But there are three variants built for different populations, with different coefficients and different cut-offs, and using the wrong one is the most common error in Indian applications — particularly using the original manufacturing Z for IT services, retail, real estate or other asset-light firms, where the asset-turnover term (X5) dominates and produces misleading results.

Core notes

The ratios

Ratio Definition What it measures
X1 Working capital ÷ Total assets Short-term liquidity relative to size
X2 Retained earnings ÷ Total assets Cumulative profitability and age; leverage history
X3 EBIT ÷ Total assets Operating productivity of assets
X4 Market value of equity ÷ Total liabilities (Z); Book value of equity ÷ Total liabilities (Z', Z'') Cushion before liabilities exceed asset value
X5 Sales ÷ Total assets Asset turnover (Z and Z' only)

Original Z (1968): public manufacturing companies

\[ Z = 1.2X_1 + 1.4X_2 + 3.3X_3 + 0.6X_4 + 1.0X_5 \]

Estimated on 66 US manufacturing firms (33 bankrupt, 33 non-bankrupt) from 1946–1965. Zones: safe above 2.99, grey 1.81–2.99, distress below 1.81. X4 uses market capitalisation; X5's coefficient is often printed as 0.999. Ratios are entered as decimals (0.15, not 15%).

Z' (1983): private manufacturing firms

\[ Z' = 0.717X_1 + 0.847X_2 + 3.107X_3 + 0.420X_4 + 0.998X_5 \]

X4 is replaced by book value of equity ÷ total liabilities, because private firms have no market price. Zones: safe above 2.90, grey 1.23–2.90, distress below 1.23. Use for unlisted Indian companies (e.g. a borrower in a bank's SME/mid-corporate book, an acquisition target), or for listed firms where market value is distorted (illiquid, operator-driven prices).

Z'' (1995): non-manufacturers and emerging markets

\[ Z'' = 6.56X_1 + 3.26X_2 + 6.72X_3 + 1.05X_4 \]

X5 is dropped to remove the industry effect of asset turnover (retailers have high turnover, utilities low), and X4 uses book equity ÷ total liabilities. Zones: safe above 2.60, grey 1.10–2.60, distress below 1.10. Altman, Hartzell and Peck's emerging-market version adds a constant of 3.25 (EM score \( = 3.25 + Z'' \)) so that the score can be mapped to bond-rating equivalents (around 5.85 or above for investment grade-like profiles, below about 4.35 for distress); when the constant is used, the cut-offs shift accordingly — never mix the two.

Choosing the variant for Indian companies

Company type Recommended Reason
Listed manufacturer with liquid shares (auto components, cement, chemicals) Z, cross-check with Z'' Original population match
Unlisted manufacturer, or listed with illiquid/manipulated price Z' Book equity avoids market noise
Services, IT, retail, hospitality, telecom, infrastructure, real estate Z'' Removes asset-turnover bias
Any Indian company for cross-country comparison Z'' (with or without 3.25 constant, stated) Designed for emerging markets
Banks, NBFCs, insurers, HFCs None Balance-sheet structure invalidates ratios (s05)

Indian data mapping

Input Schedule III source Notes
Working capital Total current assets − Total current liabilities Includes current maturities of debt and current lease liabilities in CL
Retained earnings Retained earnings component of Other equity (statement of changes in equity) Not total Other equity (see topic related item); include general reserve if it arose from profit transfers, stated as a choice
EBIT Profit before exceptional items and tax + Finance costs Decide treatment of other income: include for consistency with total assets including investments, or exclude and also consider excluding investments
Market value of equity Shares outstanding × closing NSE price at balance-sheet date Use 31 March price, or the average of the last month if volatile
Book equity Equity attributable to owners (share capital + other equity) Exclude NCI or include consistently with total liabilities
Total liabilities Total non-current + total current liabilities Includes lease liabilities, deferred tax liabilities, provisions
Sales Revenue from operations Net of GST
Total assets Total assets

Worked illustration

A listed Indian specialty chemical company, FY 2024-25 (₹ crore): current assets 1,800; current liabilities 1,200; retained earnings 1,500; total assets 5,000; PBT before exceptional items 450; finance costs 150; market cap 6,000; book equity 2,600; total liabilities 2,400; revenue 4,500.

X1 = 600/5,000 = 0.12; X2 = 0.30; X3 = 600/5,000 = 0.12; X4 (market) = 2.50; X4 (book) = 1.083; X5 = 0.90.

\[ Z = 1.2(0.12) + 1.4(0.30) + 3.3(0.12) + 0.6(2.50) + 1.0(0.90) = 0.144 + 0.420 + 0.396 + 1.500 + 0.900 = 3.36 \]

\[ Z'' = 6.56(0.12) + 3.26(0.30) + 6.72(0.12) + 1.05(1.083) = 0.787 + 0.978 + 0.806 + 1.137 = 3.71 \]

Both are in the safe zone. Note that X4 contributes 1.50 of the Z score — a 40% fall in the share price would lower Z by 0.60 without any change in fundamentals, which is why Z'' (book equity) is the more stable cross-check.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative retained earnings (accumulated losses) make X2 negative — correct and informative; do not floor at zero.
  • Negative working capital is normal for FMCG and quick-service retail with supplier-funded models; Z'' penalises X1 heavily (coefficient 6.56), so interpret against business model.
  • Holding companies: standalone total assets are mostly investments; ratios are meaningless — use consolidated.
  • Capital-intensive projects under construction (large CWIP, no revenue yet) score low on X3 and X5 by design.
  • Real estate developers carry inventory as current assets for years; X1 looks strong while liquidity is weak.
  • Market cap spikes in small caps inflate X4; Z' or Z'' is safer.

Key takeaways

  • Z (1968) for public manufacturers uses market equity and sales/assets; safe above 2.99, distress below 1.81.
  • Z' (1983) replaces market with book equity for private firms; safe above 2.90, distress below 1.23.
  • Z'' (1995) drops X5 for non-manufacturers and emerging markets; safe above 2.60, distress below 1.10; the 3.25 constant version uses different cut-offs.
  • Use the retained earnings component, not total other equity, for X2.
  • Pick the variant by business model and data quality; never apply any version to banks or NBFCs.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute all three variants [L2]

An unlisted auto-component maker (₹ crore): current assets 620; current liabilities 540; retained earnings 210; total assets 1,400; EBIT 120; book equity 480; total liabilities 920; revenue 1,750. Its listed peer trades at 1.8 times book. Compute Z' and Z'', and Z using the peer multiple as a proxy for market value. State the zone under each and which you would report.

💡 Hint: Match each score to its own cut-offs; do not compare Z'' against Z thresholds.

Q2. Variant selection [L1]

Select the appropriate Altman variant (or none) for: (a) an NSE-listed IT services company; (b) a listed cement company; (c) a private dairy cooperative's processing subsidiary; (d) a listed housing finance company; (e) a listed hotel chain; (f) a listed real estate developer. Give one-line reasons.

Q3. Market sensitivity [L2]

For the chemical company in the illustration, compute Z if the share price falls 50% and if total liabilities rise by ₹1,000 crore funded by debt used for capex (total assets rise equally). Explain which change is more informative about distress risk.

Q4. Going concern linkage [L3]

An auditor's going concern assessment under SA 570 cites recurring losses, negative working capital and debt covenant breaches. Explain how you would use Z'' alongside SA 570 indicators in a credit memo, and why a safe-zone Z'' would not override the auditor's material uncertainty paragraph.
