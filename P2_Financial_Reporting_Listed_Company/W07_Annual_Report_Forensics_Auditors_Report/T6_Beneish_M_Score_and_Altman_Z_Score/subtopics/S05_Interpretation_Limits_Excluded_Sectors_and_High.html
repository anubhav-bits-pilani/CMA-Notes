Subtopic note

Interpretation Limits: Excluded Sectors and High-Growth False Positives

Scope: Interpretation limits: exclude banks/NBFCs/insurers; high-growth firms trigger false positives via SGI and AQI

Why it matters

Both models are statistical classifiers estimated on specific samples. Applied outside those samples, they produce confident-looking numbers with no meaning. The two biggest misuse patterns in India are scoring financial companies (banks, NBFCs, HFCs, insurers — a large share of NSE market cap) and reading a high M-score for a fast-growing company as evidence of manipulation. A forensic memo that ignores these limits loses credibility immediately; one that acknowledges them and explains drivers is persuasive.

Core notes

Why financial companies are excluded

Beneish: the original estimation excluded financial firms. For a bank or NBFC:

  • "Sales" is interest income; "receivables" are the loan book — DSRI becomes a loan-growth-to-income index, not a revenue-quality signal.
  • There is no gross margin in the trading sense; GMI is meaningless.
  • Current/non-current classification is not presented by banks (Schedule III Division III for NBFCs uses a liquidity order format), so AQI cannot be computed.
  • CFO for lenders includes loan disbursements and deposit flows; TATA is dominated by balance sheet growth.
  • Leverage of 8–12x is the business model; LVGI changes reflect funding mix.

Altman: Z was estimated on manufacturers; Z'' on non-manufacturers but still non-financial firms. Working capital is undefined for banks; equity/liabilities of 0.1 is normal; EBIT/TA of 2% is healthy for a bank. Every financial company would score "distress".

What to use instead for financials: CAMELS-style metrics — capital adequacy (CRAR, CET1), asset quality (GNPA, NNPA, Stage 2/3 under Ind AS 109 for NBFCs, restructured book, write-offs), management, earnings (NIM, credit cost, RoA), liquidity (LCR, ALM gaps, CP dependence), and for forensic signals: evergreening indicators, related-party lending, divergence in NPA reported by RBI inspection (formerly disclosed by banks), and auditor KAMs on ECL. Insurers: solvency ratio, combined ratio, reserving triangles, embedded value. Also exclude holding companies (CICs), real estate investment trusts and highly regulated utilities from mechanical interpretation.

High-growth false positives

The M-score rewards stability. Growth mechanically raises the score:

  1. SGI directly: sales growth of 50% gives SGI = 1.5, adding \( 0.892 \times 0.5 = 0.446 \) to M versus a no-growth firm. Beneish included SGI because growth firms face pressure to sustain growth — growth is a motive, not evidence.
  2. AQI: growth companies investing in capacity, acquisitions (goodwill), software development (intangibles under development) and CWIP raise "soft" assets relative to total assets. Acquisitive companies see AQI jump in the acquisition year.
  3. DSRI: when growth comes from new customers on longer terms (government, export, B2B), receivable days rise.
  4. TATA: working capital build in growth phases makes CFO lag PAT (Week 7 topic 3 s02).
  5. LVGI: debt-funded expansion raises leverage.

A company growing at 40% with capex and working-capital build can score above −1.78 with no manipulation at all.

Illustration — growth false positive

Two companies, same accruals, different growth:

Variable Coefficient Stable Co Growth Co Contribution difference
DSRI 0.920 1.00 1.15 +0.138
GMI 0.528 1.00 0.98 −0.011
AQI 0.404 1.00 1.40 +0.162
SGI 0.892 1.05 1.55 +0.446
DEPI 0.115 1.00 1.00 0
SGAI −0.172 1.00 0.92 +0.014
TATA 4.679 0.02 0.05 +0.140
LVGI −0.327 1.00 1.10 −0.033
M −2.34 −1.49 +0.86

Stable Co: \( -2.48 + 0.892 \times 0.05 + 4.679 \times 0.02 \approx -2.34 \). Growth Co crosses −1.78 purely through growth-linked variables. The analyst's job is to decompose: SGI and AQI explained by a documented acquisition and capacity build is benign; DSRI and TATA unexplained by business-model change are the parts to investigate.

Other interpretation limits

  • Sample and era: Beneish used 74 manipulators from 1982–1992 US enforcement cases; Altman Z used 1946–1965 manufacturers. Indian accounting (Ind AS), sector mix and capital structure differ.
  • Detection vs prediction: M estimates the probability that reported earnings are manipulated, conditional on the sample's base rate; the base rate of manipulation in the population is much lower, so false positives dominate even at good accuracy.
  • Big-bath years: losses and write-offs (impairments) can push TATA negative and AQI down, producing low M exactly when earnings have been managed downward.
  • Manipulation that the model cannot see: fictitious cash (Satyam-type), off-balance-sheet guarantees, related-party leakage (Week 7 topic 5) and round-tripping with matching cash flows leave the eight variables unchanged.
  • Altman and market sentiment: X4 market value moves with markets; Z can rise in a bubble and fall in a crash.
  • Ind AS specifics: Ind AS 116 leases, Ind AS 115 contract assets and fair value through OCI change the inputs relative to US GAAP of the estimation era.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Diversified groups with an NBFC subsidiary: consolidated scores mix lending and operating businesses; use standalone or segment data for the operating company.
  • Gold-loan or vehicle-finance arms of manufacturers (captive finance) distort receivables; exclude the finance segment if separable.
  • Turnaround companies have SGI and GMI improving and TATA negative (provisions being reversed) — low M despite provision-release earnings management (Week 7 topic 3 s06).
  • Cyclical commodity firms see GMI and SGI swing with prices; compare with peers in the same year.
  • New-age platform companies with negative EBIT score deep distress on Z'' while holding large IPO cash; read X1 and cash runway instead.

Key takeaways

  • Do not apply Beneish or Altman to banks, NBFCs, HFCs or insurers; use CAMELS-type and ECL-based metrics.
  • Growth mechanically raises SGI, AQI, DSRI, TATA and LVGI; a no-change firm scores about −2.48 and a 50% grower adds about 0.45 from SGI alone.
  • Decompose the score: separate growth-explained drivers from unexplained ones.
  • Models miss fictitious cash, off-balance-sheet risk and related-party leakage.
  • Always compare with sector peers in the same year and state the limits in the memo.

Practice Questions

Q1. Exclusion decisions [L1]

State whether you would compute M-score and Z'' for: (a) an NSE-listed private bank; (b) a two-wheeler manufacturer with a captive finance subsidiary consolidated; (c) a listed microfinance NBFC; (d) a listed general insurer; (e) a listed hospital chain; (f) a core investment company. For excluded cases, name two alternative metrics.

Q2. Decompose a growth company's score [L2]

A listed EMS (electronics manufacturing services) company grew revenue 70% in FY 2024-25, acquired a PCB maker (goodwill ₹400 crore on total assets of ₹3,000 crore), and reported DSRI 1.10, GMI 1.02, AQI 1.55, SGI 1.70, DEPI 0.98, SGAI 0.90, TATA 0.04, LVGI 1.15. Compute M, compute what M would be with SGI and AQI set to 1, and write the interpretation.

💡 Hint: Isolate the contribution of the growth-linked variables by recomputing with those set to neutral.

Q3. Model blind spots [L2]

Explain why the M-score would likely not have flagged Satyam's fictitious cash in the years before 2009, and which non-model checks would have.

Q4. Memo paragraph [L3]

Write a 200-word paragraph for a credit memo on a fast-growing listed specialty chemical company with M = −1.65 and Z'' = 2.4, explaining what the scores do and do not show, and which follow-up diligence items you recommend.
