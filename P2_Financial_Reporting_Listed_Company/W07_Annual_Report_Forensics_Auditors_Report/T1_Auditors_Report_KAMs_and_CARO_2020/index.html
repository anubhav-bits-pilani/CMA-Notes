Roadmap topic: Independent Auditor's Report & CARO 2020: Analyzing Emphasis of Matter (EoM), Key Audit Matters (KAMs), qualified opinions, and statutory dues/loan default clauses

Overview

The independent auditor's report of an Indian listed company is not one opinion but a bundle of them: an opinion on the financial statements under SA 700 and SA 705, attention paragraphs under SA 701, SA 706 and SA 570, a separate opinion on internal financial controls under s.143(3)(i), statutory statements under s.143(3) and Rule 11 (including the audit trail statement under Rule 11(g)), and a fact-based annexure under CARO 2020. Each piece answers a different question. The opinion asks whether the numbers are fairly stated; KAMs say where judgement is concentrated; EoM, Other Matter and going concern sections point to what you must not overlook; IFC tells you whether the process producing the numbers is reliable; CARO tells you whether the company pays its taxes and lenders, keeps its bank stock statements honest, and can meet its liabilities over the next year.

For the roles this curriculum targets — credit analyst, equity research associate, FP&A or financial reporting analyst, transaction diligence — the auditor's report is the cheapest and most authoritative risk screen available. Your CMA audit background gives you the vocabulary; the shift here is from writing these reports to reading them adversarially: quantifying every exception against net worth and EBITDA, mapping each to a note, tracking changes year-on-year and across standalone and consolidated reports, and triangulating against the auditor's own behaviour (resignations, rotation, NFRA findings).

The learning sequence is: first master opinion types and the basis paragraph (s01), because everything else is read relative to the opinion; then KAMs (s02) and the non-modifying paragraphs (s03), with the core distinction that KAMs and EoMs do not modify the opinion (c05); then the India-specific statutory layer of s.143(3), IFC and Rule 11 (s04); then the CARO clauses most relevant to liquidity and lender relationships (s05) with the detailed concept notes on stock statements (c01), disputed dues (c02), borrowings and diversion (c03), and group-level CARO remarks (c04); and finally the auditor's own conduct — changes, resignations and regulatory findings (s06). The output skill is the KAM and CARO log that feeds the week's forensic exercise and the Saturday risk memo.

Related concepts to study

Audit trail under Rule 3(1) from 1 April 2023

Companies (Accounts) Rules Rule 3(1) audit trail (edit log) mandatory from 1 Apr 2023

The proviso to Rule 3(1) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014 requires every company that uses accounting software to use only software that records an audit trail of each and every transaction, creates an edit log of each change with the date of change, and does not allow the audit trail to be disabled, for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2023. Books must be retained for eight years (s.128(5)), and electronic books must have daily backups on servers physically located in India (Rule 3(5)). The auditor reports compliance under Rule 11(g). For an analyst the relevance is evidential: without an edit log, back-dated entries, post-closing changes and deleted invoices cannot be traced. A Rule 11(g) exception is graded by scope (database versus application level, modules affected, period not covered, preservation) and by whether other red flags exist.

PCAOB CAMs and ISA 701 compared with SA 701

US PCAOB AS 3101 Critical Audit Matters vs SA 701 KAMs; ISA 701 equivalent

ICAI's SA 701 is substantively aligned with IAASB's ISA 701, so KAMs in Indian, UK and other ISA-based reports are comparable. The US equivalent is the Critical Audit Matter under PCAOB AS 3101, effective for large accelerated filers for fiscal years ending on or after 30 June 2019 and for other filers from 15 December 2020 (emerging growth companies are exempt). A CAM is a matter communicated or required to be communicated to the audit committee that relates to accounts or disclosures material to the financial statements and involved especially challenging, subjective or complex auditor judgement. CAMs are thus narrower (material accounts only) and more procedure-focused than KAMs. Indian ADR issuers and US-listed peers give a useful cross-check: compare the CAMs of a US peer with KAMs of the Indian company in the same industry.

SEBI October 2019 auditor resignation circular

SEBI auditor resignation circular (Oct 2019): reasons disclosure and completion of limited review

SEBI circular CIR/CFD/CMD1/114/2019 of 18 October 2019, now within the LODR Master Circular, requires a resigning auditor of a listed entity or its material subsidiary to complete the limited review or audit for the quarter (within 45 days of quarter-end) or for that and the next quarter (after 45 days), and to complete the full-year audit if the first three quarters' reviews are signed. Concerns must be raised with the audit committee, whose views are disclosed, and the auditor provides reasons in a prescribed format, which the company files under Reg 30 within 24 hours. This removed the earlier practice of auditors exiting quietly before a difficult report and made the reasons themselves a disclosure item. Detailed mechanics are in s06.

Common pitfalls

Reading only the main opinion

Ignoring the IFC opinion and CARO annexure while only reading the main opinion

The mistake is to stop reading at "give a true and fair view" and treat the report as clean. People do it because the main opinion appears first, the annexures run to fifteen pages of standardised language, and most data vendors capture only the opinion type. The correct practice is to read Annexure B (IFC) and Annexure A (CARO) every year and record every exception — material weaknesses, Rule 11(g) audit trail gaps, 3(ii)(b) mismatches, 3(vii) arrears, 3(ix) defaults and diversion, 3(xvii) cash losses, 3(xix) liability capacity. In review, test whether the analyst's KAM/CARO log has a row for each annexure and whether "no exceptions" has been positively confirmed rather than assumed.

Treating EoM as a qualification and missing 3(xxi)

Treating EoM as a qualification, or missing qualifications in subsidiaries' CARO reports (3(xxi))

The first half of the mistake is overreaction: an EoM on litigation is reported as "auditor qualified the accounts", inflating severity and sometimes triggering wrong covenant conclusions. The second half is underreaction: the consolidated report's clause 3(xxi) lists subsidiaries with CARO defaults or cash losses, but the analyst reads only the standalone annexure. Both arise from not knowing the structure. Correct treatment: classify every paragraph by its heading and standard (c05), and always open the consolidated report's CARO paragraph. In review, check that the memo uses the exact terms ("qualified", "emphasis of matter") and that 3(xxi) components are cross-referenced to AOC-1 and to guarantees.

Not tracking KAMs year-on-year

Not tracking KAMs year-on-year; new or dropped KAMs are signals

Analysts often read only the current year's KAMs. A KAM that appears for the first time (recoverability of loans to group companies, impairment after an acquisition) marks a newly significant risk; a KAM that disappears after an auditor change, or while the underlying balance grows, may indicate a change in the auditor's assessment that deserves explanation; and an unchanged KAM whose underlying balance has doubled indicates growing exposure. The correct practice is a multi-year KAM log with status flags (new/continuing/dropped) and the underlying balance each year. In review, check that the log covers at least three years and both standalone and consolidated reports.

Practice Questions

Q1. Integrated auditor's report review [L3]

A listed mid-cap pharmaceutical company (FY ending 31 March 2025) has net worth ₹2,600 crore, EBITDA ₹540 crore and gross debt ₹1,350 crore. Its reports contain: (i) consolidated opinion qualified for non-provision of ₹96 crore receivable from a distributor in a sanctioned jurisdiction; (ii) KAMs on US price-erosion provisions (₹410 crore) and on impairment of an acquired US subsidiary's intangibles (₹780 crore, new this year); (iii) EoM on a USFDA warning letter at the main plant; (iv) IFC opinion qualified for controls over chargeback accruals; (v) Rule 11(g) exceptions stated for the US subsidiary's ERP and for the Indian ERP at database level; (vi) CARO 3(vii)(b) disputed income tax ₹212 crore at ITAT; (vii) 3(xxi) listing an Indian subsidiary with a 3(xvii) cash loss remark. Prepare a findings table with columns: item, standard/clause, modification or attention, quantum, % of net worth, % of EBITDA, linked note, severity (low/medium/high), and follow-up. Then explain which items are inconsistent with each other or need correction in how they are described.

💡 Hint: One listed item refers to a component outside the scope of Indian statutory reporting.

Q2. Two-year CARO and KAM change analysis [L2]

For a listed textile company: FY 2023-24 report — unmodified, KAMs on inventory valuation and deferred tax assets, CARO clean except 3(vii)(b) ₹28 crore. FY 2024-25 report — unmodified with MURGC section, KAMs on inventory valuation and recoverability of trade receivables (deferred tax KAM dropped after a ₹64 crore DTA write-off), CARO 3(ii)(b) mismatches in three quarters, 3(ix)(a) interest delays of up to 60 days, 3(xvii) cash loss ₹31 crore, 3(xix) negative. Net worth fell from ₹520 crore to ₹410 crore. Write a 300-word year-on-year change commentary for a credit committee and assign an overall severity.

Q3. Auditor resignation and subsequent report [L3]

On 20 June 2025 the auditor of a listed small-cap resigned citing "inability to verify ₹184 crore of capital advances to vendors and non-receipt of board minutes of a material subsidiary". The company's audit committee states it "does not agree with the auditor's observations". The FY 2024-25 audit report, issued before resignation, has a disclaimer of opinion on the consolidated statements and a qualified opinion on standalone. Net worth ₹640 crore. (a) Explain which reports the auditor was obliged to issue under the 2019 circular. (b) Explain why the consolidated report has no KAM section. (c) Draft the risk paragraph for an equity memo with a severity rating and recommended action.

Q4. Model integration of CARO findings [L2]

Design the auditor's report sheet of a forensic Excel workbook: list the input fields to capture from the main report, KAMs, IFC annexure, Rule 11 paragraph and CARO annexure for two years; specify three automated calculations (for example, quantum as % of net worth and EBITDA, CARO 3(vii)(b) versus contingent liability reconciliation, new/dropped KAM flags); and describe how the sheet feeds the red-flag summary on the dashboard.

Q5. Interview case: clean report, weak company [L3]

An interviewer gives you a company with an unmodified opinion, no EoM, three routine KAMs and a clean IFC opinion, but CARO 3(ix)(d) reports ₹420 crore short-term funds used for long-term purposes, 3(ix)(e) reports ₹160 crore raised for a subsidiary's obligations, and the consolidated 3(xxi) lists two subsidiaries with 3(xix) remarks. EBITDA is ₹300 crore. Explain how you would present this company's risk profile and what you would check next in the notes and quarterly results.
