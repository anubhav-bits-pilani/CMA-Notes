Subtopic note

CARO 2020 Clauses on Working Capital, Dues, Defaults and Solvency

Scope: CARO 2020 para 3 clauses: (ii) inventory/working-capital returns, (vii) statutory dues, (ix) loan defaults, (xvii) cash losses, (xix) liability meeting capacity

Why it matters

The Companies (Auditor's Report) Order 2020 (CARO 2020) turns the auditor into a reporter of specific facts rather than an opinion-giver. Five clauses matter most to credit and equity analysts because they speak directly to liquidity and lender relationships: stock-statement mismatches with banks (ii), unpaid and disputed statutory dues (vii), loan defaults and fund diversion (ix), cash losses (xvii), and the auditor's view on whether liabilities due within a year can be met (xix). They are reported in Annexure A to the auditor's report, which many readers never open.

Core notes

Applicability

CARO 2020 was notified by MCA on 25 February 2020 under s.143(11) and applies from FY 2021-22. It applies to every company including foreign companies, except banking companies, insurance companies, s.8 companies, OPCs, small companies, and private companies that are not subsidiaries or holding companies of public companies and have paid-up capital plus reserves not exceeding ₹1 crore, borrowings not exceeding ₹1 crore at any time during the year, and total revenue not exceeding ₹10 crore. It applies to standalone financial statements; on consolidated statements only clause 3(xxi) applies. ICAI's Guidance Note on CARO 2020 (revised edition 2022) is authoritative for interpretation.

The auditor must give reasons for any unfavourable or qualified answer (para 4).

Clause 3(ii): inventory and working capital returns

  • 3(ii)(a): whether physical verification of inventory has been conducted at reasonable intervals by management, whether coverage and procedure are appropriate, and whether discrepancies of 10% or more in aggregate for each class of inventory were noticed and properly dealt with in the books.
  • 3(ii)(b): whether, during any point of time of the year, the company has been sanctioned working capital limits in excess of ₹5 crore in aggregate from banks or financial institutions on the basis of security of current assets; and whether the quarterly returns or statements filed with them are in agreement with the books; if not, give details.

Schedule III (Division II, additional regulatory information) separately requires the company to disclose whether quarterly returns agree with books and, if not, a summary of reconciliation and reasons for material discrepancies. Differences commonly arise from provisional versus audited figures, inclusion of ineligible receivables (over 90 days, related-party), and valuation of inventory at selling price in stock statements. A large or recurring mismatch suggests drawing power was overstated, which banks treat as a serious irregularity.

Clause 3(vii): statutory dues

  • 3(vii)(a): whether the company is regular in depositing undisputed statutory dues including GST, provident fund, ESI, income tax, sales tax, service tax, customs duty, excise duty, VAT, cess and other statutory dues; and the extent of arrears outstanding at the last day of the year for more than six months from the date they became payable.
  • 3(vii)(b): where dues referred to in (a) have not been deposited on account of any dispute, the amounts involved and the forum where the dispute is pending. A deposit made under protest is not a non-deposit to that extent.

Undisputed PF or TDS arrears are a liquidity distress signal: companies generally pay statutory dues before trade creditors because of personal liability and prosecution risk (for example, s.276B of the Income-tax Act for TDS). Disputed dues in 3(vii)(b) should reconcile to the contingent liability note.

Clause 3(ix): borrowings

Sub-clause Matter reported
(a) Default in repayment of loans or other borrowings or in payment of interest to any lender; period and amount of default
(b) Whether declared a wilful defaulter by any bank, financial institution or other lender
(c) Whether term loans were applied for the purpose for which obtained; if not, amount diverted and purpose
(d) Whether funds raised on short-term basis have been utilised for long-term purposes; nature and amount
(e) Whether funds were taken from any entity or person on account of or to pay the obligations of subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures; details
(f) Whether loans were raised on the pledge of securities held in subsidiaries, joint ventures or associates; details and any default

Clause (d) is tested by comparing short-term sources (short-term borrowings, current maturities, trade payables used as funding) with short-term applications; a negative net working capital funding capex is the classic finding.

Clause 3(xvii): cash losses

Whether the company has incurred cash losses in the financial year and in the immediately preceding financial year, and the amounts. ICAI guidance treats cash loss as net loss adjusted for non-cash items (depreciation, amortisation, impairment, deferred tax, non-cash provisions), broadly loss before depreciation and non-cash charges. Two consecutive cash losses mean the business is consuming cash at the operating level before capex.

Clause 3(xix): capacity to meet liabilities

On the basis of financial ratios, ageing and expected dates of realisation of financial assets and payment of financial liabilities, other information accompanying the financial statements, the auditor's knowledge of the board's plans, whether the auditor is of the opinion that no material uncertainty exists as on the date of the audit report that the company is capable of meeting liabilities existing at the balance sheet date as and when they fall due within one year from the balance sheet date. The standard wording adds that this is not an assurance about future viability.

Clause 3(xix) is not the same as SA 570: going concern looks at the entity as a whole for twelve months, while 3(xix) is a liability-by-liability capacity test. An adverse 3(xix) answer without a MURGC section is a contradiction worth flagging.

Illustration

A mid-cap textile company (sanctioned limits ₹180 crore) reports under 3(ii)(b) that Q3 stock statement receivables were ₹236 crore versus books ₹189 crore; under 3(vii)(a) PF dues of ₹4.1 crore outstanding beyond six months; and under 3(ix)(d) short-term funds of ₹62 crore used for long-term purposes. Net worth is ₹410 crore and EBITDA ₹58 crore. The drawing power overstatement (₹47 crore, 25% of books) plus PF arrears is a liquidity-stress pattern; 3(ix)(d) at 107% of EBITDA shows the capex was funded by working capital lines, so a renewal refusal would trigger default.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Sanction above ₹5 crore at any point in the year triggers 3(ii)(b) even if limits were reduced by year-end.
  • Non-fund-based limits (LCs, bank guarantees) secured on current assets are generally included when assessing the ₹5 crore threshold under ICAI guidance; check the borrowings note.
  • Disputed dues paid under protest are excluded from 3(vii)(b) to the extent paid, so 3(vii)(b) can be lower than the contingent liability figure; the difference should be the pre-deposit.
  • Interest and penalty components: 3(vii)(b) amounts often show demand amounts including interest, while contingent liability notes sometimes show tax only; reconcile.
  • Defaults cured before year-end are still reportable under 3(ix)(a) if they occurred during the year, depending on ICAI guidance on "continuing default"; the auditor normally reports the period of delay.
  • 3(xix) carve-outs like "subject to the promoter infusing ₹200 crore" convert a nominally positive answer into a conditional one; treat as negative for credit.

Key takeaways

  • CARO applies to standalone statements; only 3(xxi) applies to consolidated statements.
  • 3(ii)(b) mismatches between bank stock statements and books are drawing power red flags.
  • 3(vii)(a) undisputed arrears over six months signal liquidity stress; 3(vii)(b) must reconcile to contingent liabilities.
  • 3(ix) covers defaults, wilful defaulter status, diversion and short-term-for-long-term funding.
  • 3(xvii) flags two consecutive cash losses; 3(xix) is a one-year liability-capacity test distinct from SA 570.
  • Quantify every adverse CARO answer against net worth and EBITDA, and map it to a note.

Practice Questions

Q1. Threshold test for 3(ii)(b) [L1]

A company had a cash credit limit of ₹3.5 crore and a bank guarantee limit of ₹2 crore secured by hypothecation of stock and receivables until 30 September 2024, after which the guarantee limit was cancelled. State whether 3(ii)(b) reporting applies for FY 2024-25 and what the auditor must compare.

Q2. Reconcile 3(vii)(b) with contingent liabilities [L2]

CARO 3(vii)(b) lists: income tax ₹84 crore pending before CIT(A) (of which ₹16.8 crore paid under protest is excluded), GST ₹37 crore before the Appellate Authority, customs ₹9 crore before CESTAT. The contingent liability note shows "Income tax demands ₹96 crore; indirect tax demands ₹41 crore". Prepare a reconciliation, list the likely reconciling items and state which differences would concern you.

💡 Hint: Think about pre-deposits, interest computed to date, and matters where the company is the appellant versus the department.

Q3. Short-term funds used for long-term purposes [L2]

From the following balances (₹ crore) at 31 March 2025 compute the amount that CARO 3(ix)(d) would likely report: current assets 1,420 (including cash 80); current liabilities 1,910 (including short-term borrowings 640 and current maturities of long-term debt 210); non-current assets 2,900; equity 1,150; long-term borrowings 1,260.

Q4. Cash loss and 3(xix) [L2]

A company reports net loss of ₹48 crore after depreciation ₹31 crore, impairment ₹12 crore and deferred tax credit ₹9 crore in FY 2024-25; FY 2023-24 net loss ₹22 crore after depreciation ₹28 crore. Compute cash loss/profit for both years and state whether 3(xvii) is triggered. Then explain what additional data you would need to form a view equivalent to the auditor's 3(xix) assessment.

Q5. Case: lender red flags [L3]

A listed mid-cap's CARO annexure reports a 3(ii)(b) mismatch in all four quarters (stock statements higher by 18–31%), no 3(ix)(a) default, an unqualified 3(xix), and 3(ix)(e) funds of ₹140 crore raised to meet obligations of a Singapore subsidiary. Write a 250-word credit view explaining how these findings interact and what you would request from the company.
