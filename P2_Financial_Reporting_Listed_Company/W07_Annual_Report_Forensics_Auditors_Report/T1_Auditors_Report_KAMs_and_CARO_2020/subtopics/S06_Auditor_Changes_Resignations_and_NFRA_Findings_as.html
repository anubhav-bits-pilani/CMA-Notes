Subtopic note

Auditor Changes, Resignations and NFRA Findings as Risk Signals

Scope: Auditor changes and resignations: LODR Reg 30 disclosures, NFRA inspection findings and orders

Why it matters

An auditor who resigns mid-term is telling the market something management may not. Indian experience (Manpasand Beverages in 2017, Vakrangee in 2018, Reliance Home Finance in 2019) shows that mid-term resignations frequently preceded qualifications, forensic audits or regulatory action. Since October 2019 SEBI has forced the reasons into the open, and since 2018 NFRA has published audit quality inspection reports and disciplinary orders. A forensic review must read auditor change disclosures alongside the auditor's report: rotation is routine, a resignation citing "non-receipt of information" is not.

Core notes

Companies Act framework

Provision Rule
s.139(1) Auditor appointed at AGM for a term of five years; ADT-1 filed
s.139(2) read with Rule 6 Rotation for listed and specified companies: an individual for one term of five years; a firm for two terms of five consecutive years; cooling-off of five years
s.140(1) Removal before expiry of term requires special resolution and prior Central Government approval, after giving the auditor an opportunity to be heard
s.140(2) A resigning auditor must file ADT-3 with the company and the Registrar within 30 days, stating reasons and other facts relevant to the resignation
s.140(3) Penalty for non-filing
s.140(5) NCLT may direct change of auditor if satisfied the auditor acted fraudulently or abetted fraud
s.144 Prohibited non-audit services

ADT-3 reasons are publicly visible on the MCA portal. A generic "pre-occupation" reason in ADT-3 that conflicts with a stock exchange disclosure is itself a finding.

LODR Reg 30 disclosures

Reg 30 read with Schedule III Part A Para A item 7 deems change in auditor a material event to be disclosed within 24 hours. Item 7A requires that, in case of resignation of the auditor, the detailed reasons for resignation as given by the auditor be disclosed by the listed entity to the stock exchanges as soon as possible, but not later than 24 hours of receipt of such reasons from the auditor.

SEBI circular of 18 October 2019 (CIR/CFD/CMD1/114/2019)

Now consolidated in the SEBI Master Circular for compliance with LODR, the circular imposes conditions on resignations:

  • Completion of limited review/audit:
  • Resignation within 45 days from the end of a quarter: the auditor must issue the limited review or audit report for that quarter before resigning.
  • Resignation after 45 days from the end of a quarter: the auditor must issue the report for that quarter as well as for the next quarter.
  • If the auditor has signed the limited review for the first three quarters of a financial year, the auditor must, before resigning, issue the limited review for the last quarter and the audit report for the full financial year.
  • Concerns to audit committee: where the auditor proposes to resign, all concerns with respect to the proposed resignation, with relevant documents, must be brought to the notice of the audit committee. If the auditor has not received information or explanations from management, the auditor should report this to the audit committee. The audit committee must deliberate and communicate its views to management and the auditor, and these views are disclosed.
  • Information from the auditor: the resigning auditor provides the listed entity with information in a specified format (reasons, whether there was inability to obtain information, whether the inability was pervasive, and the auditor's perspective), which the listed entity discloses.
  • Inability to obtain information: the auditor is not to resign merely because management withholds information; the auditor must issue a report that discloses the inability and its effect (typically a disclaimer or qualification).
  • The conditions also apply to auditors of material subsidiaries.

These requirements are written into the auditor's terms of appointment. The practical effect is that a resignation can no longer be used to escape signing a difficult report.

Reading resignation disclosures

Stated reason Signal
Completion of term or mandatory rotation Routine; check new auditor's first-year KAMs and qualifications
Fee or "commercial considerations" mid-term Mild; check timing versus results
"Pre-occupation" or "other engagements" mid-term Weak explanation; check for delayed results or limited review modifications
Non-receipt of information, unresponsive management, unreconciled balances Severe; expect disclaimer, forensic audit, lender action
Concerns over related-party transactions, use of funds, or genuineness of documents Severe; governance red flag
Conflict with new regulatory requirement (independence breach) Neutral to mild if documented

Also track: downgrade from a large network firm to a small firm; the auditor of a material subsidiary resigning while the parent's auditor stays; multiple auditor changes within three years; secretarial auditor or internal auditor resignations (Reg 24A secretarial audit report); and Reg 36(5) disclosures of proposed audit fees and material change in fee in the AGM notice.

NFRA: role, inspections and orders

The National Financial Reporting Authority was constituted under s.132 in October 2018. Under the NFRA Rules 2018 it covers listed companies; unlisted public companies with paid-up capital of at least ₹500 crore, annual turnover of at least ₹1,000 crore, or aggregate outstanding loans, debentures and deposits of at least ₹500 crore; insurance companies, banking companies, electricity companies and other specified entities. Its functions:

  • Recommend accounting and auditing standards (s.132(2)(a)) and monitor compliance (s.132(2)(b)).
  • Audit Quality Inspections of audit firms, published as inspection reports covering firm-wide quality control and individual engagements, with findings such as inadequate evidence on revenue, related parties, going concern and ECL.
  • Financial Reporting Quality Reviews of companies' financial statements, identifying non-compliance with Ind AS and Schedule III.
  • Investigate professional or other misconduct (s.132(4)) and impose penalties: for individuals not less than ₹1 lakh up to five times the fees received; for firms not less than ₹10 lakh up to ten times the fees; and debarment from six months up to ten years.

NFRA orders have related to audits of IL&FS group entities, DHFL, Reliance group finance companies and several smaller listed companies, typically for failures in auditing related-party loans, going concern, and ECL. Some orders and show-cause proceedings have been litigated; the Delhi High Court in 2025 upheld NFRA's disciplinary powers while setting aside certain show-cause notices on procedural grounds. Treat the legal position as date-sensitive.

Using NFRA output analytically

  • Search NFRA orders and inspection reports for the company and its auditor; an order against the engagement partner of a company you cover is a direct quality signal on past reported numbers.
  • Inspection findings against a firm in a specific area (for example, revenue testing) raise the bar for relying on that firm's KAM responses in the same area.
  • Financial Reporting Quality Review findings on a sector (for example, ECL on related-party loans in NBFC-linked groups) indicate where peer companies may have the same issue.

Illustration

A small-cap logistics company announces on 14 May 2025 that its auditor has resigned, citing "non-provision of bank statements for ₹212 crore of balances and confirmations for ₹340 crore of related-party advances". Net worth is ₹780 crore. Under the 2019 circular, because resignation came within 45 days after the March quarter, the auditor must still issue the audit report for FY 2024-25. Expect a disclaimer or qualification covering ₹552 crore (71% of net worth). For an equity analyst this is a sell-or-suspend-coverage trigger until the audit report and audit committee views are published.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Resignation after signing, citing future concerns, still needs the audit committee's views disclosed; read both documents.
  • Joint auditors: one joint auditor resigning while the other continues is a strong signal of disagreement.
  • Rotation that coincides with a new KAM or a qualification in the first year of the new auditor suggests the predecessor's acceptance of management positions; compare the predecessor's final report.
  • Group-level patterns: simultaneous resignations across several listed group companies (as seen in some promoter groups) indicate a group-wide information problem.
  • Auditor's Reg 30 letter versus ADT-3 reasons can differ in emphasis; the more specific document governs your view.

Key takeaways

  • Rotation under s.139(2) is routine; mid-term resignation under s.140(2) needs reasons in ADT-3 and under Reg 30 item 7A within 24 hours.
  • The October 2019 SEBI circular requires the resigning auditor to complete the quarter's review or the year's audit and to route concerns through the audit committee.
  • Reasons citing information access, related parties or genuineness of documents are severe red flags.
  • NFRA inspections, quality reviews and orders are evidence on the reliability of past audits.
  • Track auditor, secretarial auditor and material subsidiary auditor changes over five years.
  • Always pair a resignation with the next audit report and the audit committee's views.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the 45-day rule [L1]

An auditor of a listed company with FY ending 31 March resigns on (a) 10 August 2025, (b) 2 October 2025, and (c) 5 March 2026 after having signed limited reviews for Q1–Q3 of FY 2025-26. For each date state which reports the auditor must issue before the resignation takes effect.

Q2. Grade three resignation disclosures [L2]

Grade the severity (low, medium, high) of each and state the follow-up evidence you would seek: (a) "Our firm's network policy prohibits continuing as auditor following the appointment of a director related to a partner"; (b) "We have not received satisfactory responses to queries on ₹96 crore advances to suppliers that are also shareholders of promoter entities"; (c) "Due to increased audit fee expectations not accepted by the company". Net worth is ₹1,100 crore.

Q3. Interpret an NFRA order [L2]

NFRA has imposed a penalty and a five-year debarment on the engagement partner who audited a listed housing finance company for FY 2018-19, citing failure to evaluate ECL on loans to entities linked to promoters and failure to report fraud under s.143(12). Explain how you would use this order in (a) a credit assessment of the company's current bonds, and (b) evaluating restated comparatives.

Q4. Five-year auditor history table [L3]

Design an Excel table (columns, data sources, flags) to track the auditor history of a company and its material subsidiaries over five years, including statutory auditor, secretarial auditor and internal auditor, with automated flags for mid-term exits, downgrade in firm size, and coincidence with modified opinions. Describe at least four formulas or rules you would use.

💡 Hint: A COUNTIFS on change events within a rolling three-year window is a useful starting flag.

Q5. Interview: resignation as signal [L1]

In 120 words, explain to an interviewer why the 2019 SEBI circular reduced, but did not eliminate, the information value of an auditor resignation.
