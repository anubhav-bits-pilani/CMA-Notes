Subtopic note

Audit Opinion Types under SA 700 and SA 705 and the Basis Paragraph

Scope: Opinion types under SA 700 (Revised) and SA 705 (Revised): unmodified, qualified, adverse, disclaimer; basis-for-opinion paragraph

Why it matters

The opinion paragraph is the single sentence that lenders, rating agencies and equity analysts read first, but the useful information sits in the basis paragraph that follows it. A credit analyst who sees "except for" must immediately quantify the excepted matter against net worth and EBITDA; an equity analyst must decide whether the matter is a one-off disagreement (for example, an unprovided receivable) or a scope limitation that undermines every number in the filing. In FP&A and modelling work, a modified opinion changes which numbers you can trust as the base year of a forecast. SEBI LODR Reg 33(3)(d) also requires a listed entity with a modified opinion to file a Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications with its annual results, so the quantified impact is usually available if you know where to look.

Core notes

Structure of the auditor's report (SA 700 Revised)

SA 700 (Revised), Forming an Opinion and Reporting on Financial Statements, applies to audits of financial statements for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2018. For a listed company the report is ordered as follows:

Order Section Governing SA
1 Opinion (first section, with heading "Opinion") SA 700 / SA 705
2 Basis for Opinion SA 700 / SA 705
3 Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern (if applicable) SA 570 (Revised)
4 Emphasis of Matter (placement may vary) SA 706 (Revised)
5 Key Audit Matters SA 701
6 Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor's Report Thereon SA 720 (Revised)
7 Responsibilities of Management and Those Charged with Governance SA 700
8 Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit SA 700
9 Other Matter (if applicable) SA 706
10 Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements: s.143(3), Rule 11, s.197(16); Annexure A (CARO 2020); Annexure B (IFC, s.143(3)(i)) Companies Act 2013

The report closes with signature, membership number, firm registration number, place, date and UDIN.

The decision tree for modification (SA 705 Revised)

SA 705 (Revised) frames the choice on two axes: the nature of the matter (misstatement versus inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence) and the auditor's judgement about the pervasiveness of its effects.

Nature of matter Material but not pervasive Material and pervasive
Financial statements are materially misstated Qualified opinion (SA 705.7(a)) Adverse opinion (SA 705.8)
Unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence (scope limitation) Qualified opinion (SA 705.7(b)) Disclaimer of opinion (SA 705.9)

Pervasive effects are those that, in the auditor's judgement, are not confined to specific elements, accounts or items; or, if confined, represent a substantial proportion of the financial statements; or, for disclosures, are fundamental to users' understanding. Pervasiveness is a judgement, not a numeric threshold, but in practice an excepted matter above roughly a fifth to a quarter of net worth, or one that wipes out reported profit, starts to test it.

Wording signatures that identify each type at a glance:

  • Unmodified: "the aforesaid standalone financial statements give a true and fair view in conformity with the Ind AS…"
  • Qualified: "except for the effects (or possible effects) of the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section…"
  • Adverse: "because of the significance of the matter… the financial statements do not give a true and fair view…"
  • Disclaimer: "we do not express an opinion… because of the significance of the matter… we have not been able to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to provide a basis for an audit opinion." In a disclaimer the Basis section becomes "Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion", and the auditor does not include a Key Audit Matters section and does not report on other information under SA 720.

SA 705 also requires withdrawal-or-disclaim analysis when management imposes a scope limitation after acceptance: if possible effects could be material and pervasive, the auditor withdraws where practicable and permitted by law; where withdrawal is not possible (Indian statutory audits generally cannot simply be abandoned), the auditor disclaims.

The Basis for Opinion paragraph

Under SA 700 (Revised) the Basis for Opinion section states that the audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under s.143(10); refers to the Auditor's Responsibilities section; confirms independence under the ICAI Code of Ethics and the Companies Act and fulfilment of other ethical responsibilities; and states whether the auditor believes the evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the opinion.

When the opinion is modified the section is retitled "Basis for Qualified/Adverse/Disclaimer of Opinion" and must describe the matter giving rise to the modification. SA 705 requires the auditor, where practicable, to quantify the financial effect of a material misstatement relating to specific amounts (including effect on profit, tax and equity). If quantification is not practicable, the auditor must say so. For narrative-disclosure misstatements the auditor explains how disclosures are misstated; for omitted disclosures the auditor includes the omitted information where practicable.

Where the quantified impact flows

Source What you get
Basis for Qualified Opinion Auditor's description and quantification
Board's report, s.134(3)(f) Board's explanation or comments on every qualification, reservation or adverse remark
LODR Reg 33(3)(d) Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications Audited figures "as reported" and "adjusted for qualification", plus management's views, signed by CEO, CFO, audit committee chair and auditor
CARO annexure and IFC annexure Whether the same matter recurs as a control failure or statutory default

Illustration

A mid-cap engineering company reports net worth of ₹1,200 crore, EBITDA of ₹260 crore and PAT of ₹95 crore. The auditor qualifies: trade receivables of ₹140 crore from a state utility are overdue beyond three years and no expected credit loss allowance has been recognised; the auditor estimates the required allowance at ₹110 crore before tax. Analytical adjustment at 25.17% tax: post-tax hit about ₹82 crore, i.e. 6.9% of net worth and 86% of PAT. The matter is confined to one balance, so "except for" (not adverse) is defensible, but the earnings base for a forecast should be adjusted and the receivable treated as impaired in valuation.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Recurring qualifications. A qualification repeated for three years with the board's explanation "management is confident of recovery" is a stronger red flag than a fresh one; it signals that management has accepted a modified report rather than correct the accounts.
  • Qualification in consolidated only. A matter in a subsidiary can qualify the consolidated report but not the standalone; analysts reading only standalone figures miss it. The component auditor's qualification is often reproduced in the parent auditor's report under "Other Matter" or in the basis paragraph.
  • Disclaimer on consolidated, unmodified on standalone. This typically arises where component information was unavailable (for example, an overseas subsidiary in insolvency). It is not a clean bill of health for the group.
  • "Except for" scope limitations on opening balances (first-year audits after an auditor change) can be technical, but check whether the outgoing auditor's file was accessible.
  • Modified opinion with a KAM section. KAMs still appear in a qualified or adverse report, but the matter giving rise to the modification is reported in the basis section, not repeated as a KAM.
  • Interim results. Limited review reports under SA 2410/SRE 2410 issue conclusions, not opinions; a "modified conclusion" in Q3 often foreshadows an annual qualification.

Key takeaways

  • Two axes decide the opinion: misstatement versus scope limitation, and material versus pervasive.
  • "Except for" is qualified; "do not give a true and fair view" is adverse; "do not express" is a disclaimer.
  • The basis paragraph, the board's s.134(3)(f) explanation and the Reg 33(3)(d) impact statement together give the quantum.
  • Always quantify the excepted matter against net worth, EBITDA and PAT before grading severity.
  • A disclaimer removes KAM reporting and means no number in the financial statements has been opined upon.
  • Track qualifications across years and across standalone versus consolidated reports.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the opinion [L1]

For each fact pattern state the opinion type and the SA 705 basis: (a) inventory of ₹45 crore at a third-party warehouse could not be physically verified or confirmed; total inventory is ₹300 crore and net worth ₹900 crore; (b) the company has not consolidated a 60% subsidiary whose assets are 55% of group assets; (c) the books of the company were seized by an investigating agency and the auditor could access only the trial balance; (d) a ₹12 crore warranty provision is understated by ₹3 crore against PAT of ₹220 crore.

Q2. Quantify a qualification for a credit memo [L2]

A listed textile company (FY ending 31 March 2025) reports net worth ₹640 crore, EBITDA ₹118 crore, PAT ₹41 crore and net debt ₹410 crore. The auditor qualifies for (i) non-recognition of interest of ₹26 crore on a loan in default, and (ii) a ₹38 crore investment in an associate under liquidation carried at cost. Tax rate 25.17%; assume the interest is tax-deductible but the investment write-down is not. Compute adjusted PAT, adjusted net worth and adjusted net debt/EBITDA, and state whether you would treat the opinion as effectively closer to adverse.

💡 Hint: Interest in default is both a P&L hit and an addition to debt; the investment loss affects equity but not EBITDA.

Q3. Read the Reg 33 impact statement [L2]

A company's Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications shows "Impact not quantifiable; management believes the matter is sub judice" for a ₹180 crore claim. The auditor's basis paragraph states that management has not provided its legal assessment. Explain what this combination tells you about the nature of the modification and what further evidence you would seek from the annual report.

Q4. Interview: why does a disclaimer drop KAMs? [L1]

In an interview you are asked why a disclaimer of opinion report contains no Key Audit Matters section and no SA 720 statement on other information. Give a two-paragraph answer referring to the purpose of each section.

Q5. Standalone versus consolidated divergence [L3]

A holding company's standalone report is unmodified, but the consolidated report contains a qualified opinion relating to a step-down subsidiary's unreconciled intercompany balance of ₹74 crore, and the component auditor's report is referred to under Other Matter. Consolidated net worth is ₹2,050 crore. Draft a 150-word paragraph for an equity research note explaining the risk, the evidence you would examine, and the severity grade you would assign.
