Subtopic note

Reading SA 701 Key Audit Matters and Linking Them to the Notes

Scope: SA 701 KAMs (mandatory for listed entities): reading auditor response and linking to notes

Why it matters

Key Audit Matters are the only place in an Indian annual report where the auditor tells you, in their own words, which numbers kept them awake. For an analyst they are a free map of where management judgement is concentrated: revenue cut-off, impairment of goodwill, ECL on receivables, uncertain tax positions, inventory valuation, capitalisation of development cost. Each KAM points to a note, and the note tells you the size of the exposure and the assumptions. In a forensic review the KAM log is usually the first schedule built, because it tells you which notes to read in depth and which assumptions to stress in a model.

Core notes

Applicability and definition

SA 701, Communicating Key Audit Matters in the Independent Auditor's Report, applies to audits of complete sets of general purpose financial statements of listed entities, and wherever the auditor otherwise decides, or is required by law or regulation, to communicate KAMs. It became effective in India for audits of financial statements for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2018. Unlisted companies can include KAMs voluntarily, but few do.

Key audit matters are those matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements of the current period. They are selected from matters communicated with those charged with governance (the audit committee).

How the auditor selects KAMs (SA 701 para 9–10)

The auditor first identifies matters that required significant auditor attention, taking into account:

  1. Areas of higher assessed risk of material misstatement, or significant risks identified under SA 315.
  2. Significant auditor judgements relating to areas involving significant management judgement, including accounting estimates with high estimation uncertainty.
  3. The effect on the audit of significant events or transactions during the period.

From these, the auditor determines which were of most significance; those become KAMs. The number is a judgement; Indian large-cap reports typically carry two to five.

What each KAM must contain (SA 701 para 13)

Each KAM is described under an appropriate sub-heading and must include a reference to the related disclosure in the financial statements (if any), and address:

  • Why the matter was considered to be one of most significance (the "Key audit matter" column in the common two-column table); and
  • How the matter was addressed in the audit (the "How our audit addressed the key audit matter" column).

The auditor's response typically lists: understanding and testing of design and operating effectiveness of controls; substantive procedures (confirmations, cut-off testing, recomputation); use of auditor's experts (valuation, actuarial, tax, IT); evaluation of management's experts; sensitivity analysis on key assumptions; and evaluation of disclosures. Indian reports rarely state the auditor's conclusion on each KAM, because a KAM is not a separate opinion on an element.

Reading the auditor's response analytically

Treat the response column as evidence of where the auditor found the risk, not as boilerplate. Useful signals:

Signal in the response What it suggests
"Involved our internal valuation specialists to evaluate discount rate and terminal growth" Impairment headroom is likely thin; check the sensitivity disclosure (Ind AS 36.134(f))
"Tested journal entries posted after period-end / manual revenue entries" Cut-off risk; compare Q4 revenue with Q1 next year
"Obtained legal opinions from management's external counsel" Litigation judgement; reconcile to contingent liability note
"Circularised balance confirmations; performed alternative procedures for non-responses" Receivable or related-party balance existence risk
"Evaluated management's assessment of indicators of control transfer" Ind AS 115 timing judgement (bill-and-hold, installation, acceptance)
"Assessed IT general controls and application controls over revenue" High-volume systems revenue; link to audit trail reporting under Rule 11(g)

Linking KAMs to notes: the KAM log

A practical KAM log has one row per KAM per year:

Column Content
Year / Standalone or Consolidated FY 2024-25, Consolidated
KAM title Impairment of goodwill – Europe CGU
Note reference and page Note 5 and Note 46, pp. 212, 268
Carrying amount ₹1,840 crore
Amount as % of net worth / EBITDA 22% / 1.9x
Key assumptions disclosed WACC 10.8%, terminal growth 2.5%, headroom ₹95 crore
Auditor procedures (summary) Valuation specialist; sensitivity; past forecast accuracy
New, continuing or dropped Continuing (since FY 2022-23)
Analyst action Stress WACC +100 bp in DCF; check H1 trading

In Excel, keep the log as a structured table so that a pivot by "KAM category" (revenue, impairment, tax, provisions, IT) and by status (new/dropped) can be built; Power BI can show KAM counts across peers for a sector screen.

Illustration

A consumer durables company's KAM states: "Revenue of ₹8,420 crore includes ₹612 crore of sales in the last fortnight of March; we tested a sample of dispatches against delivery evidence and evaluated rebate accruals of ₹318 crore." The linked notes show trade receivables rising from ₹1,050 crore to ₹1,540 crore (DSO from 52 to 67 days) and a rebate liability up 34% against revenue growth of 11%. The KAM did not modify the opinion, but it directs the analyst to test channel stuffing and variable consideration, topics developed in the earnings quality topic.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • KAM versus modification. A KAM is not a substitute for a modified opinion (SA 701 para 11 and SA 705). If the matter is misstated, it belongs in the basis for qualification, not in a KAM.
  • KAM versus going concern. A material uncertainty related to going concern is by nature a KAM, but it is reported in its own SA 570 section; the KAM section only cross-refers to it.
  • No KAMs to report. Permitted, but in a listed entity this is rare; the auditor must state that there are no KAMs to communicate. Treat as unusual and check audit committee minutes or tenure.
  • Not communicating a KAM (SA 701 para 14) is allowed only where law or regulation precludes public disclosure, or in extremely rare cases where adverse consequences would reasonably be expected to outweigh public interest benefits. Practically never invoked in India.
  • Boilerplate drift. Identical KAM text for five years with only the numbers updated suggests the auditor is not re-evaluating; still log it, but weigh newly added procedures more heavily.
  • Standalone versus consolidated KAMs differ. Goodwill and component-level KAMs often appear only in the consolidated report; read both.
  • Year-on-year movement. New KAMs (for example, "recoverability of loans to group entities") and dropped KAMs (for example, a revenue KAM disappearing after a change of auditor) are signals in their own right.

Key takeaways

  • SA 701 KAMs are mandatory for listed entities; they are drawn from matters discussed with the audit committee.
  • Each KAM must explain why it was significant, how it was addressed, and refer to the related note.
  • The response column reveals the nature of the risk: specialists, cut-off tests and confirmations each point to different exposures.
  • Build a KAM log with note references, quantum versus net worth and EBITDA, assumptions and status (new/continuing/dropped).
  • KAMs never modify the opinion and never replace a qualification or the SA 570 section.
  • Use KAMs to decide which assumptions to stress in the valuation or credit model.

Practice Questions

Q1. Map a KAM to its note [L1]

A KAM titled "Recoverability of minimum alternate tax credit and deferred tax assets" refers to Note 38. List the specific disclosures you would extract from Note 38 and from the tax reconciliation to quantify the exposure, and the one forecast assumption that most affects recoverability.

Q2. Build a two-year KAM log [L2]

A cement company's consolidated auditor's report for FY 2023-24 contained KAMs on (i) capitalisation of a new clinker line, (ii) recoverability of incentives receivable from state governments of ₹640 crore, and (iii) litigation with the Competition Commission. FY 2024-25 contains (ii) at ₹910 crore and a new KAM on (iv) impairment of an acquired subsidiary with goodwill of ₹1,150 crore. Net worth is ₹9,800 crore and EBITDA ₹2,150 crore in FY 2024-25. Set out the KAM log in table form with all required columns, mark status for each KAM, and rank them by analytical priority.

💡 Hint: A KAM that grows faster than the balance sheet and one that is newly added after an acquisition usually rank above a dropped one.

Q3. Interpret auditor procedures [L2]

The "how addressed" column for a revenue KAM of an IT services company reads: "We tested controls over estimation of efforts to complete fixed-price contracts, performed retrospective review of prior-year estimates, and tested a sample of contracts where revenue recognised exceeded billing by more than 20%." Explain what risk the auditor was addressing, which balance sheet line you would examine, and what ratio you would compute.

Q4. Judgement: a dropped KAM [L3]

After an audit firm rotation, a pharmaceutical company's KAM on "chargebacks and rebate provisions in the US business" disappears, while US revenue grows 28% and the provision grows 4%. Prepare four questions you would put to investor relations and explain how the answers would change your view of earnings quality.

Q5. Interview: KAM versus EoM [L1]

Explain to a non-accountant fund manager, in under 150 words, the difference between a KAM and an Emphasis of Matter paragraph and why neither changes the auditor's opinion.
