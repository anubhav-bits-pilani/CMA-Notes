Subtopic note

Section 143(3) Reporting, IFC Opinion and Rule 11 Audit Trail

Scope: s.143(3) report items incl. IFC over financial reporting (s.143(3)(i)) and Rule 11 items (audit trail under Rule 11(g))

Why it matters

The "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements" is where Indian auditors make statements that have no equivalent in ISA-based reports: whether proper books were kept, whether directors are disqualified, whether internal financial controls work, whether the accounting software kept an untampered edit log, and whether funds were routed through intermediaries. Analysts skip this section because it looks like boilerplate. In forensic work it is often where the first crack appears: a qualified IFC opinion over revenue or inventory, a Rule 11(g) exception that the audit trail was disabled for the ERP database, or a comment under s.143(3)(f) about matters having an adverse effect on the company's functioning.

Core notes

Section 143(3) items

Section 143(3) of the Companies Act 2013 requires the auditor's report to state:

Clause Matter reported
(a) Whether the auditor sought and obtained all information and explanations necessary for the audit; if not, details and effect
(b) Whether proper books of account as required by law have been kept, and proper returns from branches not visited
(c) Whether branch auditor's report (s.143(8)) has been sent and how it has been dealt with
(d) Whether the balance sheet and P&L dealt with in the report agree with the books and returns
(e) Whether the financial statements comply with the accounting standards (Ind AS under s.133)
(f) Observations or comments on financial transactions or matters which have any adverse effect on the functioning of the company
(g) Whether any director is disqualified from being appointed under s.164(2)
(h) Any qualification, reservation or adverse remark relating to the maintenance of accounts and other matters connected therewith
(i) Whether the company has adequate internal financial controls with reference to financial statements in place and the operating effectiveness of such controls
(j) Such other matters as may be prescribed (Rule 11 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules 2014)

Section 197(16) additionally requires the auditor to state whether remuneration paid to directors complies with s.197, and to give details of any excess. Watch (f) and (h): Indian auditors use (f) sparingly, so any comment there (for example, "the matter described in the Basis for Qualified Opinion section, in our opinion, may have an adverse effect on the functioning of the Company") is significant. Clause (h) often reports a qualification under Rule 11(g) as a matter connected with maintenance of accounts, because s.128 and Rule 3(1) require books to be kept with an audit trail.

Section 143(3)(i): IFC over financial reporting

The IFC report sits as Annexure B. It is governed by the ICAI Guidance Note on Audit of Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting, which is broadly modelled on COSO 2013 and PCAOB AS 2201 concepts. Key points:

  • Applicability: all companies, with an MCA exemption (notification of 13 June 2017) for OPCs, small companies, and private companies with turnover below ₹50 crore as per latest audited financial statements or aggregate borrowings from banks, financial institutions or body corporates below ₹25 crore at any point during the year, provided they are not in default of filing financial statements (s.137) or annual returns (s.92).
  • Opinion types mirror SA 705: unmodified, qualified ("except for the effects of the material weakness described… the company has maintained, in all material respects, adequate internal financial controls…"), adverse, or disclaimer.
  • Material weakness: a deficiency or combination of deficiencies such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of the annual or interim financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely basis.
  • Consistency: a qualified IFC opinion can coexist with an unmodified financial statements opinion if the auditor performed additional substantive work. The reverse (qualified financial statements, clean IFC) needs explanation because a misstatement usually implies a control failure.
  • Directors' side: s.134(5)(e) requires listed company directors to state that they have laid down IFC and these are adequate and operating effectively; Rule 8(5)(viii) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules requires the board's report to give details of the adequacy of IFC.

Rule 11 items (s.143(3)(j))

Rule 11 clause Matter reported
(a) Whether the company has disclosed the impact, if any, of pending litigations on its financial position
(b) Whether provision has been made for material foreseeable losses, if any, on long-term contracts including derivative contracts
(c) Whether there has been any delay in transferring amounts required to be transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund
(d) Omitted (formerly specified bank note disclosures for the 2016 demonetisation period)
(e) Management representations, and auditor's comment, that no funds have been advanced, loaned or invested (from borrowed funds, share premium or any other source) to intermediaries with the understanding that the intermediary will lend or invest in other persons identified by the company (ultimate beneficiaries) or provide guarantees on their behalf; and correspondingly that no funds have been received from funding parties on such terms; and whether anything has come to notice that causes the auditor to believe these representations contain material misstatement
(f) Whether dividend declared or paid during the year complies with s.123
(g) Whether the company has used accounting software for maintaining books which has a feature of recording audit trail (edit log) facility, whether it has been operated throughout the year for all transactions recorded in the software, whether the audit trail feature has been tampered with, and whether the audit trail has been preserved by the company as per statutory requirements for record retention

The Rule 11(g) requirement was operationalised alongside Rule 3(1) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, which mandates audit-trail-enabled software for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2023, so FY 2023-24 was the first year of substantive reporting. ICAI's Implementation Guide on Reporting under Rule 11(g) (revised 2024) is the reference.

What Rule 11(g) exceptions look like

Typical modifications in FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 reports:

  • "The audit trail feature was not enabled at the database level for direct changes" (common with SAP/Oracle where application-level logging exists but direct database changes by privileged users are not logged).
  • "Audit trail was enabled from 15 August 2023" (not operated throughout the year).
  • "In the absence of a SOC 1 Type 2 report from the service organisation, we are unable to comment" (payroll or outsourced accounting).
  • "Audit trail of prior year has not been preserved" (relevant from FY 2024-25, the first year in which preservation could be tested against the previous year's log).

Severity depends on scope: a database-level gap with strong application-level controls is a hygiene issue; audit trail disabled for the general ledger in a company with other red flags is a serious governance signal, because it removes evidence of back-dated or altered entries.

Illustration

A listed chemicals company's Annexure B carries a qualified IFC opinion: "material weakness in controls over approval and recording of credit notes to dealers, which could result in revenue being recorded at amounts higher than entitled". The Rule 11(g) paragraph adds that the audit trail was not enabled for the dealer-incentive module. Revenue is ₹3,800 crore; dealer incentives ₹290 crore. The financial statements opinion is unmodified. Analytically, a 10% under-accrual of incentives would cut EBITDA of ₹520 crore by ₹29 crore (5.6%); the combination of weak controls and no edit log means management's estimate cannot be independently verified, so the analyst should apply a valuation haircut or seek dealer-level evidence.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • IFC exemption misapplied. Private companies exceeding the ₹25 crore borrowing threshold at any point during the year lose the exemption even if year-end borrowings are lower.
  • Consolidated IFC. The IFC report on consolidated statements covers only the holding company and Indian subsidiaries, associates and JVs incorporated in India; foreign components are outside s.143(3)(i). The parent auditor relies on component auditors' IFC reports.
  • Rule 11(e) "ultimate beneficiary" representations are management representations; the auditor only reports whether anything has come to notice contrary to them. A clean statement is not assurance that no layering exists (the Gensol and Coffee Day patterns involved routing through vendors and group entities).
  • Rule 11(a) and contingent liabilities. The auditor's statement that pending litigations have been disclosed refers to the contingent liability note; inconsistencies with CARO 3(vii)(b) disputed dues are worth testing.
  • Section 143(12) fraud reporting (ADT-4 to the Central Government for frauds of ₹1 crore or more) surfaces through CARO 3(xi)(b), not s.143(3), but it belongs in the same review.

Key takeaways

  • Section 143(3) has ten items; (f), (h) and (i) are the analytically important ones.
  • The IFC opinion is a separate opinion with its own modification types and its own material weakness concept.
  • Rule 11 covers litigations, foreseeable losses, IEPF, intermediary funding representations, dividend compliance and the audit trail.
  • Rule 11(g) audit trail reporting has bitten since FY 2023-24; read the exception's scope (database versus application, period, preservation).
  • A clean financial statements opinion with a qualified IFC opinion is a control red flag, not a contradiction.
  • Always map IFC and Rule 11 exceptions to the affected notes and to related CARO clauses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Sort the reporting items [L1]

State the reporting provision (s.143(3) clause, s.197(16) or Rule 11 clause) under which each matter would appear: (a) a director was disqualified under s.164(2); (b) dividend declared exceeded what s.123 permits; (c) delay of seven months in transferring ₹0.4 crore to IEPF; (d) managerial remuneration exceeded limits by ₹3.2 crore; (e) the ERP edit log was disabled for three months.

Q2. Assess an IFC qualification [L2]

A listed logistics company with revenue ₹5,600 crore and EBITDA ₹740 crore has a qualified IFC opinion citing a material weakness in fuel expense accruals (annual fuel cost ₹1,950 crore) and in vendor master changes. Its financial statements opinion is unmodified. Estimate the EBITDA sensitivity if fuel accruals are misstated by 2% and 5%, and explain how you would grade severity and what further disclosures you would examine.

Q3. Rule 11(g) exception severity [L2]

Compare the severity of the following Rule 11(g) exceptions in FY 2024-25 reports and rank them, giving reasons: (a) audit trail not enabled at the database level for SAP HANA for direct data changes; (b) audit trail not preserved for FY 2023-24; (c) audit trail enabled only from 1 October 2024 for the inventory module in a company whose inventory is 38% of total assets; (d) no SOC report for the payroll service provider.

💡 Hint: Consider which balances could be altered without trace, and for how long.

Q4. Exemption analysis [L1]

A private company had turnover of ₹46 crore in its latest audited financial statements; borrowings were ₹18 crore on 31 March 2025 but peaked at ₹31 crore in November 2024. It filed its annual return late in FY 2023-24. Determine whether the IFC reporting exemption applies for FY 2024-25 and explain each condition.

Q5. Draft a review comment [L3]

You are reviewing a junior analyst's red-flag memo on a mid-cap NBFC-linked group company. The memo says "Auditor's report clean – no issues". The Annexure B IFC opinion is qualified for controls over related-party loan approvals, s.143(3)(f) refers to the qualification, and the Rule 11(e) paragraph states that funds of ₹220 crore were advanced to an entity that on-lent to promoter group companies. Write a 200-word review comment explaining what was missed and how it should be presented.
