Subtopic note

Emphasis of Matter, Other Matter and Going Concern Uncertainty

Scope: SA 706 EoM and Other Matter; SA 570 Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section

Why it matters

Emphasis of Matter (EoM), Other Matter (OM) and the Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern (MURGC) section are the three places where an auditor draws attention to something without necessarily modifying the opinion. Analysts routinely misread them in both directions: some treat any EoM as a qualification and dump the stock; others ignore a MURGC paragraph because the opinion is "clean". For a credit analyst the MURGC section is the most important paragraph in the report after a qualification: it tells you management and the auditor agree there is significant doubt about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, and it typically precedes restructuring, one-time settlements or IBC proceedings.

Core notes

SA 706 (Revised): Emphasis of Matter

An Emphasis of Matter paragraph refers to a matter appropriately presented or disclosed in the financial statements that, in the auditor's judgement, is of such importance that it is fundamental to users' understanding of the financial statements. Conditions (SA 706 para 8):

  1. The auditor would not be required to modify the opinion under SA 705 as a result of the matter; and
  2. Where SA 701 applies, the matter has not been determined to be a key audit matter.

The paragraph must be headed "Emphasis of Matter", include a clear reference to the matter and to where the relevant disclosures can be found, and state that the auditor's opinion is not modified in respect of the matter.

Typical Indian EoM subjects: an uncertainty relating to the future outcome of exceptional litigation or regulatory action (for example, an SFIO investigation or SEBI show-cause notice); a significant subsequent event; a major catastrophe affecting operations; restatement of prior-period figures; a scheme of arrangement approved by NCLT accounted from the appointed date as per the scheme; and early application of a new standard with pervasive effect. EoM is meant to be rare; overuse dilutes its effect.

SA 706 (Revised): Other Matter

An Other Matter paragraph refers to a matter other than those presented or disclosed in the financial statements that, in the auditor's judgement, is relevant to users' understanding of the audit, the auditor's responsibilities or the auditor's report, and is not prohibited by law. The heading is "Other Matter". Common Indian uses:

  • Reliance on the reports of other (component or branch) auditors: "We did not audit the financial statements of eleven subsidiaries whose financial statements reflect total assets of ₹3,420 crore…; our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter."
  • Unaudited component financial information certified by management, included in the consolidated statements.
  • Comparative figures audited by a predecessor auditor.
  • Restriction on distribution or use of the report.

For an analyst the OM paragraph on unaudited components is often more informative than an EoM: it tells you what share of group assets, revenue and cash flows the signing auditor did not examine.

SA 570 (Revised): Going concern reporting

Management must assess going concern under Ind AS 1.25–26, covering at least twelve months from the end of the reporting period. The auditor evaluates that assessment. Reporting outcomes:

Situation Auditor's reporting
Going concern basis appropriate, no material uncertainty No GC paragraph; auditor's responsibilities section notes the conclusion obligation
Going concern basis appropriate, material uncertainty exists, adequately disclosed Unmodified opinion plus a separate section headed "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern", drawing attention to the note and stating the opinion is not modified
Material uncertainty exists, not adequately disclosed Qualified or adverse opinion; basis section states a material uncertainty exists and is not adequately disclosed
Going concern basis inappropriate but used by management Adverse opinion
Multiple significant uncertainties (rare) Disclaimer may be considered
Events identified but no material uncertainty after mitigating plans ("close call") No MURGC section, but the auditor evaluates adequacy of disclosure of the events; often reported as a KAM

The MURGC section replaced the older Indian practice of reporting going concern doubts as an EoM. If you see "we draw attention to Note X… indicate that a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt…", the paragraph is under SA 570 even if the report is old-style.

Going concern indicators to cross-check

Indicator (SA 570 examples) Where to verify
Net liability or net current liability position Balance sheet; Schedule III current ratio disclosure
Fixed-term borrowings approaching maturity without realistic prospects of renewal Borrowings note, maturity analysis (Ind AS 107.39)
Indications of withdrawal of financial support by creditors; default CARO 3(ix)(a), Schedule III wilful defaulter disclosure
Negative operating cash flows Cash flow statement
Substantial operating losses; cash losses CARO 3(xvii)
Arrears or discontinuance of dividends Directors' report
Inability to pay creditors on due dates MSME dues note, CARO 3(xix)
Loss of key management, licence, major market or customer MD&A, Reg 30 disclosures
Pending legal or regulatory proceedings that may not be satisfied Contingent liabilities note

Illustration

A listed power developer has current liabilities of ₹6,250 crore against current assets of ₹1,980 crore, has defaulted on ₹1,140 crore of principal and interest, and has negative CFO for two years. Note 47 discloses a resolution plan under the RBI Prudential Framework (June 2019) not yet implemented. The auditor includes a MURGC section referring to Note 47, plus an EoM on a ₹420 crore arbitration award under challenge. For credit purposes the MURGC is decisive: the opinion is unmodified, but the analyst should model a restructuring case, check CARO 3(ix) and 3(xix), and treat the arbitration EoM as an upside or downside scenario rather than a base case.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • EoM versus KAM overlap. A matter cannot be both; if it was a KAM the auditor highlights it in the KAM section. An EoM in a listed company report therefore signals something fundamental that the auditor did not regard as a judgement-heavy audit area, often a disclosed external event.
  • EoM on a matter that is really a scope limitation. Some EoMs read like disguised qualifications ("management is in the process of reconciling…"). If disclosure is not adequate, SA 706 should not be used; flag the possibility that the opinion should have been modified.
  • MURGC plus qualification. Possible where the GC uncertainty is adequately disclosed but another unrelated matter is misstated.
  • Going concern for a holding company of distressed subsidiaries. The standalone may have no MURGC because the parent holds liquid investments, while the consolidated carries it; read both.
  • Subsequent events. Events after the balance sheet date (Ind AS 10.14–16) that indicate going concern is inappropriate require the financial statements to move off the going concern basis; the auditor must consider these up to the report date.
  • Banks' perspective. An unmodified report with MURGC is commonly treated by lenders as a trigger for early warning signals under RBI frameworks.

Key takeaways

  • EoM and OM never modify the opinion; the paragraph must say so explicitly.
  • EoM refers to something disclosed in the financial statements; OM refers to something outside them (often reliance on other auditors).
  • An EoM cannot be used for a matter that is a KAM or that requires modification.
  • MURGC is a separate SA 570 section; inadequate disclosure converts it into a qualified or adverse opinion.
  • Read OM paragraphs for the share of group numbers not audited by the signing auditor.
  • Verify every going concern indicator against the balance sheet, cash flow, borrowings note and CARO clauses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify the paragraph type [L1]

For each of the following, state whether it should be an EoM, OM, MURGC section, KAM or a modification: (a) the company's factory was destroyed by fire after year-end and the loss is disclosed in Note 52; (b) the auditor did not audit two foreign subsidiaries contributing 18% of consolidated revenue; (c) net current liabilities of ₹860 crore, loan defaults and a disclosed but unapproved restructuring; (d) a ₹95 crore receivable the auditor believes is irrecoverable and is unprovided.

Q2. Going concern indicator scan [L2]

From the following data for a listed hospitality company for FY 2024-25, prepare a going concern indicator table and state whether you would expect a MURGC section: total equity ₹(–)210 crore; current assets ₹340 crore; current liabilities ₹1,180 crore including ₹720 crore of term loan instalments overdue; CFO ₹(–)45 crore; promoter has given an undertaking for financial support of ₹150 crore; asset sale of a hotel for ₹500 crore signed but awaiting regulatory approval.

💡 Hint: Mitigating plans count only if they are feasible and likely to be effective within the assessment period.

Q3. Read an Other Matter paragraph [L2]

An Other Matter paragraph states that subsidiaries with total assets of ₹4,600 crore, revenue of ₹2,950 crore and net cash outflows of ₹210 crore were audited by other auditors, and that two joint ventures with a share of loss of ₹38 crore are based on unaudited management accounts. Consolidated total assets are ₹14,200 crore and revenue ₹9,700 crore. Compute the share of assets and revenue not audited by the principal auditor and explain how you would factor this into an equity risk memo.

Q4. Disclosure inadequacy [L3]

Management's going concern note says only "The company has obtained support from its lenders and the financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis." The auditor's report contains an EoM paragraph referring to this note. Lenders have in fact recalled ₹1,300 crore of loans after year-end. Evaluate whether the reporting is appropriate under SA 570 and SA 706 and what opinion type should have been considered.
