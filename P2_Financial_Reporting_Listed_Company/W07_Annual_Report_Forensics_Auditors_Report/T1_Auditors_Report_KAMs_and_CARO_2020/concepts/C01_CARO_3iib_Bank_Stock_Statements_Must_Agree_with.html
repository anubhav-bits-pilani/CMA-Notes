Key concept

CARO 3(ii)(b): Bank Stock Statements Must Agree with Books

Concept: CARO 3(ii)(b): quarterly returns to banks for working capital limits above ₹5 crore must agree with books

Statement

Where, at any point of time during the year, a company has been sanctioned working capital limits in excess of ₹5 crore in aggregate from banks or financial institutions on the basis of security of current assets, the auditor reports under CARO 2020 para 3(ii)(b) whether the quarterly returns or statements filed by the company with such banks or financial institutions are in agreement with the books of account, and if not, gives details.

The analytical measure of a mismatch is:

\[ \text{Overstatement \%} = \frac{\text{Value per stock statement} - \text{Value per books}}{\text{Value per books}} \times 100 \]

where "value" is computed separately for inventory, trade receivables and (if included) other current assets, for the same quarter-end date.

Explanation

Working capital lenders compute drawing power from monthly or quarterly stock and book-debt statements: eligible inventory and receivables less a margin (typically 25% on stock, 25–40% on receivables), excluding receivables older than 90 or 180 days and related-party receivables. If the statements show higher current assets than the books, the company can draw more than the security justifies.

The clause has three elements: the ₹5 crore sanction threshold (aggregate, at any point in the year, fund-based and, per ICAI guidance, generally non-fund-based limits secured on current assets); the quarterly frequency (monthly statements are tested at quarter-ends); and agreement with books, meaning the books as finally audited for the year-end quarter and the unaudited books for interim quarters.

Schedule III (Division II, additional regulatory information) requires the company itself to disclose whether quarterly returns agree with books and, if not, a summary of the reconciliation and reasons for material discrepancies. The auditor's CARO answer and the company's note must be consistent.

Legitimate differences arise from provisional figures filed within 15–20 days of quarter-end before closing entries (provisions, ECL, cut-off), valuation basis differences, and inclusion of goods in transit. Illegitimate differences arise from inclusion of fictitious or ineligible receivables, inflated inventory quantities or valuation at selling price. Persistent one-directional overstatement across all four quarters is the pattern that matters.

Illustration

A steel trader has a cash credit limit of ₹120 crore. Statements filed versus books (₹ crore):

Quarter Inventory: statement Inventory: books Receivables: statement Receivables: books
Q1 142 131 96 88
Q2 155 129 104 85
Q3 161 124 118 83
Q4 139 136 91 90

Q3 inventory is overstated by \( \frac{161 - 124}{124} \times 100 = 29.8\% \) and receivables by 42.2%. The Q4 gap almost closes because the year-end is audited. The rising interim gap, closing only at the audited quarter, is a classic drawing power manipulation pattern; with a 25% margin, Q3 excess drawing power is roughly ₹54 crore.

Connections

Links to s05 (CARO clause map), c03 (3(ix) defaults and diversion, since excess drawing power often funds long-term uses), the week 5–6 DSO and working capital analysis, and the inventory and receivable red flags in the earnings quality topic. In credit modelling, the reconciled (books) figures should drive any borrowing base projection. Banks' early warning signal frameworks under RBI fraud risk management directions treat repeated stock statement discrepancies as a red flag for account classification.

Practice Questions

Q1. Threshold and scope [L1]

A company had sanctioned limits from two banks: cash credit ₹2.8 crore and working capital demand loan ₹1.9 crore, both secured on current assets, plus an unsecured overdraft of ₹1 crore. Determine whether CARO 3(ii)(b) reporting is triggered and explain your reasoning.

Q2. Compute drawing power excess [L2]

For a quarter-end, the stock statement shows inventory ₹210 crore and receivables ₹165 crore (of which ₹22 crore over 90 days); books show inventory ₹184 crore and receivables ₹151 crore (of which ₹31 crore over 90 days). Margins are 25% on inventory and 30% on receivables under 90 days; receivables over 90 days are ineligible. Sanctioned limit ₹250 crore, outstanding ₹238 crore. Compute drawing power per statement and per books and the excess drawing, and comment on the CARO and Schedule III disclosures you would expect.

💡 Hint: Drawing power is the lower of sanctioned limit and eligible security less margin.

Q3. Judgement: provisional figures defence [L2]

Management's Schedule III note explains a 17% receivables mismatch in Q2 as "statements filed on provisional basis before ECL and credit note adjustments". List three tests you would perform on the annual report and quarterly results to evaluate this explanation.
