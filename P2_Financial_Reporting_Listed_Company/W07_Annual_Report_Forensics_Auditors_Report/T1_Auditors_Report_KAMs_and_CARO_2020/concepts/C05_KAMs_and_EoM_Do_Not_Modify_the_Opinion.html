Key concept

KAMs and EoM Do Not Modify the Opinion; Qualifications Do

Concept: SA 701 KAMs are not modifications; EoM does not modify opinion; qualification does

Statement

The auditor's opinion is modified only under SA 705 (Revised): qualified, adverse or disclaimer. Three other report elements draw attention to matters but leave the opinion unmodified:

Element Standard Modifies opinion? Refers to
Key Audit Matter SA 701 No Matters of most significance in the audit, usually judgement-heavy areas
Emphasis of Matter SA 706 (Revised) No (paragraph must say so) A matter appropriately presented or disclosed that is fundamental to understanding
Other Matter SA 706 (Revised) No A matter not presented or disclosed, relevant to understanding the audit or report
Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern SA 570 (Revised) No, if adequately disclosed Significant doubt on going concern
Qualified / adverse / disclaimer SA 705 (Revised) Yes Material misstatement or inability to obtain evidence

Explanation

The logic is that a modification expresses disagreement or inability: the auditor either believes the financial statements are materially misstated or could not obtain sufficient appropriate evidence. KAMs, EoMs and OMs express communication: the auditor agrees the statements are fairly presented but wants users to focus on something.

Consequences of the distinction:

  • SA 701 para 11 (and SA 705): communicating a KAM is not a substitute for expressing a modified opinion when required. A matter giving rise to a modification is by nature a KAM, but it is reported in the Basis for Qualified/Adverse Opinion section, and the KAM section only refers to it.
  • SA 706 para 8: an EoM can be used only where the matter would not require modification and, where SA 701 applies, the matter is not a KAM.
  • Regulatory treatment: SEBI LODR Reg 33(3)(d) requires a Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications only for modified opinions; an EoM or KAM does not trigger it. Section 134(3)(f) requires the board to explain every qualification, reservation or adverse remark; boards often also respond to EoMs, but this is not a modification.
  • Lender and covenant documents frequently define an event of default as a "qualified audit opinion"; an EoM or KAM does not breach such covenants, while a qualification may.

Why analysts confuse them: EoMs often address alarming subjects (litigation, regulatory investigations, going concern-like uncertainties), and KAMs describe risks in language that sounds negative. The fix is mechanical — read the opinion heading and the first sentence. "Opinion" with no prefix is unmodified; "Qualified Opinion", "Adverse Opinion" or "Disclaimer of Opinion" is modified.

Severity grading still uses all elements: an unmodified opinion with a MURGC section and an EoM on an SFIO investigation can be riskier for a lender than a qualified opinion on a ₹10 crore inventory count in a ₹5,000 crore company.

Illustration

Three reports for FY 2024-25:

Company Opinion heading Other elements Modified? Analyst severity
A (FMCG, net worth ₹4,200 crore) Opinion KAM: trade promotion accruals ₹380 crore No Low–medium
B (Realty, net worth ₹1,100 crore) Opinion EoM: ED attachment of land worth ₹260 crore; MURGC No High
C (Auto ancillary, net worth ₹900 crore) Qualified Opinion Basis: unprovided ECL ₹14 crore Yes Low–medium (1.6% of net worth)

Company C will file a Reg 33(3)(d) impact statement; B will not, yet B carries the greatest credit risk.

Practice Questions

Q1. Label each paragraph [L1]

Classify each excerpt as modification or not, and name the standard: (a) "We draw attention to Note 41 describing the uncertainty related to the outcome of the arbitration… Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter"; (b) "Except for the possible effects of the matter described…"; (c) "Revenue recognition on long-term contracts — refer Note 2.4 and Note 30"; (d) "We did not audit the financial information of three branches…".

Q2. Covenant consequence [L2]

A loan agreement defines an event of default as "the borrower's auditor issuing a qualified, adverse or disclaimer opinion". The FY 2024-25 report contains an EoM on a ₹450 crore tax demand and a KAM on goodwill impairment of ₹1,200 crore; net worth is ₹3,000 crore. Advise whether the covenant is breached and how you would still reflect the matters in a credit rating rationale.

Q3. Interview: rank the risk [L2]

Rank companies A, B and C in the illustration above for an equity investor rather than a lender, and justify why the ranking may differ from the credit view.
