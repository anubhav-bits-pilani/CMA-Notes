Key concept

CARO 3(ix): Defaults, Wilful Defaulter Status and Fund Diversion

Concept: CARO 3(ix): default in repayment, wilful defaulter status, diversion of term loans, short-term funds used long-term

Statement

CARO 2020 para 3(ix) requires the auditor to report:

  • (a) whether the company has defaulted in repayment of loans or other borrowings or in payment of interest thereon to any lender, and if so the period and amount of default (lender-wise);
  • (b) whether the company has been declared a wilful defaulter by any bank, financial institution or other lender;
  • (c) whether term loans were applied for the purpose for which they were obtained, and if not, the amount diverted and the purpose;
  • (d) whether funds raised on a short-term basis have been utilised for long-term purposes, and the nature and amount;
  • (e) whether funds were taken from any entity or person to meet obligations of subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures;
  • (f) whether loans were raised on the pledge of securities held in subsidiaries, joint ventures or associates, with details of any default.

The usual analytical test for (d) is:

\[ \text{ST funds used for LT} = \max\left(0,\; (\text{CL} - \text{CA}_{\text{adj}})\right) \]

where \( \text{CL} \) is current liabilities (including short-term borrowings and current maturities) and \( \text{CA}_{\text{adj}} \) is current assets after excluding non-realisable items, subject to the auditor's analysis of sources and applications.

Explanation

Default (a) covers any lender, not only banks, and both principal and interest; even a few days' delay is reportable with the period. A default reported here should be consistent with the borrowings classification under Ind AS 1.69–76: a breach of a material covenant on or before year-end that makes a long-term loan repayable on demand requires current classification, except that under the Indian carve-out in Ind AS 1.74 the liability stays non-current if the lender agrees, after the reporting period but before the financial statements are approved, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach (IFRS requires the waiver by the reporting date). Check whether the company relied on that carve-out.

Wilful defaulter (b) is a lender's formal declaration under the RBI Master Directions on Treatment of Wilful Defaulters and Large Defaulters (July 2024), which apply to borrowers with outstanding of ₹25 lakh and above who have the capacity to pay but do not, divert funds, siphon funds or dispose of security without lender knowledge. Schedule III separately requires disclosure if the company is a declared wilful defaulter. Consequences include a bar on fresh institutional finance and on promoters joining other boards, and IBC s.29A ineligibility as a resolution applicant.

Diversion (c) under RBI usage includes using term loan proceeds for purposes other than sanctioned, routing funds to group or related entities, and investing in other companies or assets. Short-term for long-term (d) is a funding mismatch rather than necessarily misconduct, but it signals refinancing risk. (e) and (f) capture holding companies borrowing to support subsidiaries, and promoter-style financing by pledging subsidiary shares.

Illustration

A listed infrastructure developer's CARO annexure reports: (a) delays of 31–89 days on interest of ₹46 crore to two banks and principal of ₹120 crore to an NBFC; (b) not a wilful defaulter; (c) ₹85 crore of a ₹400 crore term loan sanctioned for a road project was temporarily used for working capital; (d) ₹210 crore short-term funds used for long-term purposes; (e) ₹150 crore borrowed to meet a subsidiary's debt servicing. Net worth ₹1,900 crore, EBITDA ₹340 crore, gross debt ₹2,600 crore. Total flagged amounts (excluding (a) duplication) ₹445 crore are 23% of net worth and 1.3x EBITDA. The pattern of defaults plus diversion plus subsidiary support points to group-level cash stress; a credit analyst would treat this as at least an SMA-type risk and look for SA 570 commentary.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the findings [L1]

State which sub-clause of 3(ix) applies to each: (a) a ₹60 crore commercial paper rolled over to fund a plant expansion; (b) term loan proceeds of ₹40 crore lent to a promoter group company; (c) loan against pledge of shares of a listed subsidiary; (d) principal instalment paid 12 days late; (e) borrowing from an NBFC to repay a joint venture's bank loan guaranteed by the company.

Q2. Compute and interpret 3(ix)(d) [L2]

Balances at 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): current assets 860 (including ₹70 crore of advances to a related party unlikely to be realised within 12 months); current liabilities 1,140 (short-term borrowings 520, current maturities 140, trade payables 390, other 90). Long-term sources: equity 980, long-term debt 1,020. Non-current assets 2,280. Compute the likely 3(ix)(d) amount and explain the refinancing risk with reference to Ind AS 1 classification.

💡 Hint: Adjust current assets for items that are current in form but not realisable in the operating cycle.

Q3. Judgement: wilful defaulter disclosure absent [L3]

News reports state that a public sector bank has classified a listed company as a wilful defaulter in February 2025, but the company has challenged the classification before the High Court and the FY 2024-25 CARO 3(ix)(b) answer reads "not declared a wilful defaulter". Discuss whether the CARO answer is defensible, what Schedule III disclosure you would expect, and how you would treat the matter in a credit memo.
