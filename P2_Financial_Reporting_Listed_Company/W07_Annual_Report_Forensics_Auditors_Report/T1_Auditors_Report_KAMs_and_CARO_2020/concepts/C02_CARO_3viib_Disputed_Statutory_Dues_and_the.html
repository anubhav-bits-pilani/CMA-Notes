Key concept

CARO 3(vii)(b): Disputed Statutory Dues and the Contingency Cross-Check

Concept: CARO 3(vii)(b): disputed statutory dues not deposited, with forum; cross-check to contingent liabilities

Statement

Under CARO 2020 para 3(vii)(b), where statutory dues referred to in 3(vii)(a) (GST, provident fund, ESI, income tax, sales tax, service tax, customs, excise, VAT, cess and other statutory dues) have not been deposited on account of any dispute, the auditor states the amounts involved, the name of the statute, nature of dues, period to which the amount relates, and the forum where the dispute is pending. Amounts deposited under protest are excluded to that extent.

The analytical cross-check is:

\[ \text{CL}_{\text{tax}} \approx \text{CARO}_{\text{vii(b)}} + \text{Pre-deposits} + \text{Interest/penalty differences} + \text{Matters not yet demanded} \]

where \( \text{CL}_{\text{tax}} \) is the tax component of the contingent liabilities note and \( \text{CARO}_{\text{vii(b)}} \) is the total of disputed dues not deposited.

Explanation

The clause gives a forum-wise view of tax disputes that the contingent liability note (Ind AS 37.86) usually aggregates into one or two lines. The forum matters because it indicates how far the dispute has travelled and the realistic timeline:

Forum (direct tax) Forum (indirect tax) Stage implication
Assessing Officer / Dispute Resolution Panel Adjudicating authority Early; high variance
CIT(Appeals) Appellate Authority (Commissioner Appeals) First appeal
ITAT GSTAT / CESTAT Final fact-finding forum
High Court High Court Question of law
Supreme Court Supreme Court Final

Matters lost at successive forums (for example, a demand confirmed by CIT(A) and ITAT now at High Court) warrant a higher loss probability in analytical adjustments. Differences between the CARO list and the contingent liability note arise for legitimate reasons: pre-deposits (generally 20% of the disputed demand for a stay pending CIT(A) under the CBDT office memoranda of 2016 and 2017; 10% of disputed tax for a GST first appeal under s.107(6) of the CGST Act and a further 10% before GSTAT under s.112(8), reduced from 20% by the Finance Act 2024, both subject to monetary caps; 7.5% before Commissioner (Appeals) and 10% before CESTAT for customs and excise), interest computed to date, matters where the company is the appellant on a refund and the department in appeal, and amounts that management has provided for (provided amounts are neither contingent liabilities nor necessarily excluded from CARO if not deposited).

Gaps that should concern you: a CARO list materially larger than the contingent liability note (suggesting the note omits matters), forum entries showing the company has lost at ITAT/CESTAT but no provision has been made, and large amounts at the "adjudicating authority" stage that are omitted from the contingent liability note on the ground that no formal demand exists.

Illustration

CARO 3(vii)(b) for FY 2024-25 shows (₹ crore): income tax 140 at ITAT, 62 at CIT(A); GST 48 at Appellate Authority; customs 11 at CESTAT; total 261. The contingent liability note shows: income tax 228, indirect tax 67, total 295. Pre-deposits disclosed in other non-current assets: income tax 22 (paid under protest), GST 4.8, customs 0.8. Reconciliation: 261 + 27.6 = 288.6, leaving ₹6.4 crore unexplained, likely interest to date. If net worth is ₹2,400 crore, disputed dues are 12.3% of net worth; the ITAT-stage ₹140 crore deserves specific probability assessment because a loss at ITAT is a finding of fact.

Practice Questions

Q1. Forum-wise probability view [L2]

CARO 3(vii)(b) reports: income tax ₹75 crore at High Court (company lost at ITAT), ₹38 crore at CIT(A); GST ₹26 crore at adjudication; excise ₹9 crore at Supreme Court (company won at CESTAT, department appealed). Assign a defensible loss probability to each and compute the probability-weighted exposure, explaining the reasoning for each probability.

💡 Hint: Who won at the last fact-finding forum is a strong anchor.

Q2. Reconciliation gap [L2]

The contingent liability note shows indirect tax claims of ₹54 crore, but CARO 3(vii)(b) shows GST and customs disputes of ₹83 crore not deposited. No pre-deposits are disclosed. Explain three possible reasons and what each would imply for the accuracy of the financial statements.

Q3. Interview question [L1]

Explain why an amount paid under protest drops out of CARO 3(vii)(b) but may still appear in the contingent liabilities note, and where the paid amount sits on the balance sheet.
