Key concept

CARO 3(xxi): Group Company CARO Qualifications in Consolidated Reports

Concept: CARO 3(xxi): qualifications in CARO reports of group companies included in consolidated statements

Statement

CARO 2020 para 3(xxi) applies only to the auditor's report on consolidated financial statements. The auditor reports whether there have been any qualifications or adverse remarks by the respective auditors in the CARO reports of the companies included in the consolidated financial statements; if yes, the auditor indicates the details of the companies and the paragraph numbers of the CARO report containing the qualifications or adverse remarks.

A useful analytical summary is the share of the group touched by such remarks:

\[ \text{Group exposure \%} = \frac{\sum \text{Net worth (or revenue) of components with adverse CARO remarks}}{\text{Consolidated net worth (or revenue)}} \times 100 \]

Explanation

CARO is a standalone-level order; it is not applied to consolidated numbers clause by clause. Clause 3(xxi) is the bridge: it tells the reader of the consolidated report that a subsidiary, associate or joint venture has its own CARO problems — defaults (3(ix)), unpaid statutory dues (3(vii)), cash losses (3(xvii)), fraud (3(xi)), liability capacity doubts (3(xix)) — which may not surface in the parent's standalone annexure.

Scope points from the ICAI Guidance Note on CARO 2020:

  • Only components that are companies to which CARO applies are covered: Indian subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures. Foreign subsidiaries are not covered; neither are exempt classes (banking and insurance companies, s.8 companies, OPCs, small companies).
  • "Qualifications or adverse remarks" means unfavourable or qualified answers in the component's CARO report, not every factual statement.
  • If a component's CARO report has not been issued by the date of the consolidated report, the parent auditor states this.
  • The parent auditor reports what the component auditors have reported; it does not re-perform their work.

Analytically, the clause matters most for holding structures where operating risk sits in subsidiaries: infrastructure groups with project SPVs, real estate groups with development subsidiaries, and conglomerates where a listed parent funds unlisted step-downs. A parent with clean standalone CARO and a long 3(xxi) list is a strong signal that the risk has been pushed down the structure. Because foreign subsidiaries are excluded, overseas acquisitions financed through SPVs remain a blind spot; use the Other Matter paragraph and Form AOC-1 for those.

Illustration

A listed holding company's consolidated report includes under 3(xxi):

Component Relationship CARO clauses with adverse remarks
Alpha Roads Pvt Ltd Subsidiary 3(ix)(a), 3(xvii), 3(xix)
Beta Realty Ltd Subsidiary 3(vii)(a), 3(iii)(c)
Gamma Power Pvt Ltd Joint venture 3(ix)(d)

AOC-1 shows Alpha net worth ₹(–)140 crore, revenue ₹410 crore; Beta net worth ₹620 crore, revenue ₹290 crore. Consolidated revenue ₹6,800 crore. The revenue share of subsidiaries with adverse remarks is \( \frac{410 + 290}{6{,}800} \times 100 = 10.3\% \), but Alpha's 3(xix) remark plus the parent's corporate guarantee to Alpha's lenders makes the parent's contingent exposure the real issue, to be read with the guarantee note.

Practice Questions

Q1. Scope of 3(xxi) [L1]

A group consists of: a listed parent; an Indian private subsidiary with revenue ₹8 crore, paid-up capital plus reserves ₹0.6 crore and no borrowings; a Singapore subsidiary; an Indian insurance JV; and an Indian associate with ₹90 crore revenue. State which components' CARO reports would feed 3(xxi) and why.

Q2. Build a component risk table [L2]

Clause 3(xxi) lists four subsidiaries with remarks under 3(vii)(a), 3(ix)(a), 3(xvii) and 3(xi)(a) respectively. AOC-1 data (₹ crore) — net worth: 210, (–)35, 48, 390; revenue: 520, 140, 75, 1,050. Consolidated net worth ₹3,600 crore and revenue ₹9,200 crore. Prepare a table with severity grades and compute the group exposure percentages on both bases.

💡 Hint: A fraud remark under 3(xi) deserves a high grade regardless of size.

Q3. Judgement: clean parent, troubled subsidiaries [L3]

Explain to a portfolio manager why a listed parent with an unmodified standalone report and clean standalone CARO annexure may still carry high credit risk, using 3(xxi) and the guarantee note as evidence.
