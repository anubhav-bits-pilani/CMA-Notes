Key concept

Contingent Liabilities as a Percentage of Net Worth

Concept: Contingent liabilities % net worth = Total contingent liabilities ÷ Net worth; trend over 3–5 years

Statement

\[ \text{CL\%NW}_t = \frac{\text{CL}_t}{\text{NW}_t} \times 100 \]

where \( \text{CL}_t \) is total contingent liabilities at year-end \( t \) (claims not acknowledged as debt, guarantees, and other money contingently liable, as disclosed under Schedule III) and \( \text{NW}_t \) is net worth (equity share capital plus other equity, excluding NCI in consolidated analysis unless stated). The ratio is read as a trend over 3–5 years, alongside:

\[ \text{CL growth}_t = \frac{\text{CL}_t - \text{CL}_{t-1}}{\text{CL}_{t-1}}, \qquad \text{CL}/\text{EBITDA}_t = \frac{\text{CL}_t}{\text{EBITDA}_t} \]

Net worth should be read consistently with s.2(57) of the Companies Act 2013 where used for regulatory comparisons (paid-up capital plus reserves created out of profits and securities premium, less accumulated losses, deferred expenditure and miscellaneous expenditure not written off, excluding revaluation reserves and amalgamation reserves).

Explanation

The ratio converts a list of disputes into a measure of how much equity cushion would be consumed if every contingent liability crystallised — a stress test, not an expected loss. It is useful because:

  • It scales exposures across companies of different size and is a standard line in credit appraisal notes and rating rationales.
  • The trend matters more than the level: a ratio rising from 10% to 35% over four years indicates either escalating disputes or a policy of keeping probable items off balance sheet.
  • The composition matters: guarantees for healthy subsidiaries are different from tax demands with adverse precedent.

Practical rules for building it:

  1. Use gross amounts (add back deposits paid under protest if the note is net) and record whether interest and penalty are included.
  2. Compute separately for standalone and consolidated; standalone includes subsidiary guarantees.
  3. Exclude capital commitments (they are not contingent liabilities) — show them in a separate ratio to capex guidance.
  4. Pair with CL/EBITDA for credit work: a 30% CL%NW in a low-ROE company can equal several years' EBITDA.
  5. Thresholds are heuristic; many analysts treat above 25–30% of net worth, or a sustained rising trend, as a finding requiring probability analysis (c05).

Limitations: net worth itself can be inflated (revaluation, capitalised costs), so the ratio understates risk for companies with weak equity quality; and the ratio treats a frivolous ₹1,000 crore demand like a probable one.

Illustration

Mid-cap chemical company (standalone, ₹ crore):

FY Claims not acknowledged Guarantees Other Total CL Net worth CL%NW EBITDA CL/EBITDA
FY21 120 200 10 330 2,000 16.5% 450 0.7x
FY22 150 260 12 422 2,150 19.6% 480 0.9x
FY23 210 420 15 645 2,250 28.7% 400 1.6x
FY24 280 600 15 895 2,300 38.9% 380 2.4x
FY25 330 760 18 1,108 2,380 46.6% 360 3.1x

Guarantees drive most of the rise, consistent with subsidiaries borrowing on the parent's credit while standalone EBITDA falls — the finding is graded high severity and leads into look-through leverage (s03).

Practice Questions

Q1. Build and interpret the trend [L2]

Using the following standalone data (₹ crore) for FY21–FY25 — total contingent liabilities 400, 460, 520, 900, 1,250; net worth 3,000, 3,200, 3,350, 3,400, 3,300; EBITDA 700, 720, 650, 600, 520 — compute CL%NW and CL/EBITDA each year, and write three sentences of interpretation.

Q2. Gross-up before computing [L1]

A company reports claims not acknowledged as debt of ₹240 crore "net of ₹60 crore paid under protest and excluding interest". Estimated interest on demands is ₹70 crore. Net worth is ₹1,500 crore. Compute the reported and gross CL%NW and explain which you would present in a credit memo.

💡 Hint: Deposits reduce cash exposure, not the loss exposure.

Q3. Model build in Excel [L3]

Specify an Excel layout (rows, columns, named inputs, check cells) that computes CL%NW and CL/EBITDA for five years for both standalone and consolidated statements, flags years where the ratio exceeds 25% or rises by more than 10 percentage points, and records the annual report page reference for each input. Write the flag formula.
