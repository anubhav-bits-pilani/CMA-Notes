Key concept

Ind AS 37 Para 14: Three Conditions for a Provision

Concept: Ind AS 37 para 14: provision if present obligation, probable outflow and reliable estimate

Statement

A provision shall be recognised when all three conditions are met (Ind AS 37.14):

  1. the entity has a present obligation (legal or constructive) as a result of a past event;
  2. it is probable that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation; and
  3. a reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation.

If any condition fails, no provision is recognised. Expressed as a decision rule with \( P_{\text{obl}} \) the probability that a present obligation exists and \( P_{\text{out}} \) the probability of outflow given the obligation:

\[ \text{Recognise} \iff P_{\text{obl}} > 0.5 \;\wedge\; P_{\text{out}} > 0.5 \;\wedge\; \text{reliable estimate exists} \]

Explanation

Present obligation. The past event must be an obligating event — one that leaves the entity no realistic alternative to settling (Ind AS 37.17). A legal obligation arises from contract, legislation or operation of law; a constructive obligation arises from an established pattern of past practice, published policy or a sufficiently specific current statement that creates a valid expectation (Ind AS 37.10). Obligations must exist independently of future actions — a requirement to fit smoke filters by a future date is not an obligation until the entity operates without them (Ind AS 37.19 and the illustrative examples). Where it is unclear whether an obligation exists (lawsuits), the "more likely than not" test is applied considering all evidence, including events after the reporting period (Ind AS 37.15–16).

Probable outflow. Probable means more likely than not (Ind AS 37.23). For a class of similar obligations, probability is assessed for the class (Ind AS 37.24), so a warranty provision can exist even if each claim is individually unlikely.

Reliable estimate. Estimates are inherent to provisions; only in extremely rare cases is no reliable estimate possible, and then the item is a contingent liability (Ind AS 37.25–26).

The three conditions are cumulative and are applied at each reporting date (Ind AS 37.59). The rule is deliberately symmetric with derecognition: once outflow is no longer probable, the provision is reversed.

Indian context: under the pre-Ind AS framework (AS 29), constructive obligations were not recognised in the same way, and AS 29 did not permit discounting (other than for decommissioning under the 2018 revision). Ind AS companies therefore recognise more provisions (e.g. constructive obligations for refunds and restoration) than AS companies.

Illustration

A two-wheeler manufacturer announces on 20 March 2025, via a press release and dealer circular, a free replacement of a brake component for 2 lakh vehicles sold in FY 2024-25. Expected cost: ₹1,800 per vehicle, take-up 80%.

  • Present obligation: constructive — the public announcement created a valid expectation (past event: sale of defective vehicles plus announcement).
  • Probable outflow: yes, assessed for the population.
  • Reliable estimate: 2,00,000 × 80% × ₹1,800 = ₹28.8 crore.

Recognise ₹28.8 crore. Had the board only discussed a recall without communicating it by 31 March, no constructive obligation would exist yet; the item would be disclosed as a non-adjusting event if announced after year-end.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the three conditions [L1]

For each item at 31 March 2025, state which para 14 condition is met or failed: (a) a board decision on 15 March to close a plant, not yet communicated; (b) a refinery's legal obligation to restore land at the end of a 25-year lease; (c) expected operating losses of ₹50 crore in FY 2025-26 for a loss-making division; (d) a government notification requiring installation of emission control equipment by 2027.

Q2. Constructive obligation from practice [L2]

An NSE-listed retailer has for five years refunded customers for returns within 60 days although its stated policy is 30 days. Sales in March 2025 were ₹300 crore, of which historically 4% are returned between days 31 and 60, at a margin of 35%. Determine whether para 14 requires recognition, and explain whether the measurement falls under Ind AS 37 or Ind AS 115.

💡 Hint: Consider whether refund rights affect the transaction price before reaching for Ind AS 37.

Q3. Interview: provision vs accrual [L1]

Explain in under 120 words how a provision differs from trade payables and accruals under Ind AS 37.11, and why this matters for working-capital analysis.
