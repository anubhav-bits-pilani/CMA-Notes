Key concept

Ind AS 37 Para 86: Disclose Contingent Liabilities Unless Remote

Concept: Ind AS 37 para 86: disclose contingent liabilities unless outflow is remote

Statement

Unless the possibility of any outflow in settlement is remote, an entity shall disclose for each class of contingent liability at the end of the reporting period a brief description of its nature and, where practicable (Ind AS 37.86):

  1. an estimate of its financial effect, measured under Ind AS 37.36–52;
  2. an indication of the uncertainties relating to the amount or timing of any outflow; and
  3. the possibility of any reimbursement.

The resulting probability ladder for a matter with outflow probability \( p \):

\[ \text{Treatment}(p) = \begin{cases} \text{Provision} & p > 0.5 \\ \text{Disclose as contingent liability} & \text{remote} < p \le 0.5 \\ \text{No disclosure} & p \text{ remote} \end{cases} \]

Ind AS does not define "remote" numerically; practice treats it as a slight chance (often read as below roughly 5–10%), but it is a qualitative judgement.

Explanation

Contingent liabilities are never recognised (Ind AS 37.27) but must be continually assessed; if an outflow becomes probable, a provision is recognised in the period of change (Ind AS 37.30). Para 86 ensures users see the "possible but not probable" pool. Supporting rules:

  • Grouping: classes are formed by considering whether items are sufficiently similar for a single statement (Ind AS 37.87). Tax demands, civil suits and guarantees are usually separate classes.
  • Linked items: where a provision and a contingent liability arise from the same circumstances, disclose in a way that shows the link (Ind AS 37.88) — e.g. principal provided, penalty contingent.
  • Impracticability: if any information is not disclosed because it is not practicable, that fact must be stated (Ind AS 37.91).
  • Seriously prejudicial: in extremely rare cases, disclosure of some or all information can be withheld, with the general nature of the dispute and the reason disclosed (Ind AS 37.92).
  • Joint and several liability: the portion expected to be met by other parties is a contingent liability (Ind AS 37.29) — relevant to consortium contracts and JV guarantees.

Schedule III adds the "claims not acknowledged as debt / guarantees / other" structure (s02), and SEBI LODR Reg 30 adds event-based disclosure of material litigation during the year. Auditors assess remoteness through legal confirmations (SA 501) and management representations (SA 580).

The analytical weakness of para 86 is that the financial effect is usually the demanded amount, not a probability-weighted estimate — so the note overstates expected loss for frivolous demands and understates it where management has kept likely losses out of provisions. Hence c05.

Illustration

A cement company's FY 2024-25 contingent liabilities note:

Class Amount (₹ cr) Narrative Assessment
Income tax 140 "Appeals pending; management believes demands are not sustainable" Meets 86(a); 86(b) weak
Competition Commission penalty 380 "Stay granted by NCLAT; 10% deposited" Linked deposit; uncertainty described
Mining royalty dispute Not quantified "Not practicable to estimate" Needs 37.91 statement; challenge it
Consumer complaints — Omitted as remote Acceptable if small and frivolous

The CCI penalty, upheld at NCLAT for peers, may in fact be probable; a reviewer would test whether it belongs in provisions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draft a para 86 disclosure [L2]

A listed auto-component maker has (a) a ₹55 crore customs demand on classification, CESTAT ruled against a peer on the same issue; (b) a ₹10 crore labour claim; (c) a ₹90 crore claim by a customer for warranty recall costs, partly insured (insurer has accepted ₹40 crore). Draft the contingent liability note, including the elements required by Ind AS 37.86(a)–(c) and 37.88, and state which items you would challenge as possibly probable.

💡 Hint: Adverse precedent on an identical issue bears directly on probability.

Q2. Remote or not? [L1]

Give three fact patterns where a large claim could legitimately be assessed as remote and omitted, and three indicators that should prevent a remote assessment.

Q3. Prejudicial exemption misuse [L3]

A company invokes Ind AS 37.92 for a ₹600 crore arbitration claim, disclosing only "a commercial dispute with a customer". The arbitration is publicly reported in the press with amounts. Evaluate whether the exemption is appropriate and what the auditor's report might be expected to say.
