Key concept

Probability-Weighted Contingent Liability Exposure

Concept: Probability-weighted exposure = Σ (Disputed amount × assessed loss probability) for analytical adjustments

Statement

\[ E = \sum_{i=1}^{n} D_i \times p_i \]

where \( D_i \) is the disputed amount of matter \( i \) (gross of deposits, including interest and penalty where demanded or accruing), \( p_i \) is the analyst's assessed probability of loss for matter \( i \), and \( n \) is the number of matters (or classes). A refinement adjusts for partial loss and timing:

\[ E_{\text{PV}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{D_i \times p_i \times s_i}{(1 + r)^{T_i}} - \text{Deposits}_i \]

with \( s_i \) the expected severity (share of the demand payable if lost), \( T_i \) years to resolution, \( r \) a pre-tax discount rate, and deposits already paid subtracted from the cash outflow (but not from the loss).

Explanation

Ind AS 37 forces a binary choice — provide or disclose — and the disclosed number is usually the full demand. For valuation and credit analysis, neither the full number nor zero is right. The probability-weighted exposure is an analytical adjustment, not an accounting measure; it sits in the EV bridge as a debt-like item or in adjusted net debt for covenant headroom.

How to assess \( p_i \) with defensible evidence:

Evidence Indicative probability band
Company has won the identical issue at ITAT/CESTAT/HC in its own earlier years 10–25%
Mixed precedent, first-level appeal pending 30–50%
Adverse ruling at first appeal; second appeal pending 50–70%
Adverse Supreme Court or jurisdictional HC precedent for peers on same issue 70–90%
Demand under fraud provisions (s.74 GST) with documentary allegations Raise severity, not just probability

Bands are heuristics for analysis and should be stated as assumptions. Use the auditor's KAM narrative, CARO 3(vii)(b) forum data, Reg 30 announcements and legal databases to support each one.

Conditions and cautions:

  • Do not double count: subtract amounts already provided (they are in reported liabilities).
  • Interest continues to accrue; for long-dated disputes include expected interest to resolution.
  • Correlated matters (same issue across years) should share one probability — they win or lose together, so the exposure is lumpy and a scenario view is better than an average.
  • Tax effect: GST and customs losses are deductible business expenses for income tax; income-tax demands are not. Use post-tax amounts for equity value.

Illustration

Listed pharma company, 31 March 2025 (₹ crore):

Matter \( D_i \) Provided \( p_i \) \( D_i \times p_i \)
Income-tax TP (AY 2016-17 to 2021-22, ITAT ruled for company in 3 years) 320 0 15% 48.0
GST ITC disputes FY18–FY20, s.73 60 0 40% 24.0
NPPA drug price overcharging demand, Supreme Court precedent adverse 150 20 75% 112.5
Guarantee for associate, associate stressed 200 0 30% 60.0
Civil suits 25 0 10% 2.5
Total 755 20 247.0

Less amounts already provided ₹20 crore (NPPA): incremental exposure ≈ ₹227 crore pre-tax. Against net worth of ₹3,000 crore, the adjustment is 7.6% — far smaller than the 25% gross CL%NW, but the NPPA item alone is a likely under-provision worth flagging. Because the TP matters are correlated, a downside scenario "TP lost on all years" (₹320 crore) is shown separately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute weighted exposure [L2]

A company discloses: income tax ₹180 crore (ITAT won on identical issue twice), customs ₹90 crore (CESTAT lost, appeal to SC pending), GST ₹40 crore (s.74 fraud allegations), guarantee ₹300 crore for a subsidiary with interest cover 0.9x, civil suits ₹15 crore. Provisions held: customs ₹10 crore. Assign defensible probabilities, compute the probability-weighted exposure net of provisions, and express it as % of net worth of ₹2,200 crore.

💡 Hint: State your evidence for each probability and remember to remove what is already provided.

Q2. Discounting and deposits [L2]

For a single ₹100 crore income-tax demand with 60% loss probability, expected severity 80%, resolution in 4 years, discount rate 10% and ₹20 crore already deposited, compute \( E_{\text{PV}} \) and explain why the deposit is treated differently in the loss and cash views.

Q3. Correlated disputes [L3]

Explain, using a company with the same s.80-IA issue disputed across six assessment years totalling ₹420 crore, why a simple probability-weighted sum can mislead, and design a two-scenario presentation for an equity research note.
