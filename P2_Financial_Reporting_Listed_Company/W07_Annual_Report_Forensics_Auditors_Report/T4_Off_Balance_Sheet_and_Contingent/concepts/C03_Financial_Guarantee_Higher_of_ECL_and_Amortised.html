Key concept

Financial Guarantee: Higher of ECL and Amortised Amount

Concept: Financial guarantee (Ind AS 109): initial fair value; subsequently higher of ECL allowance and amortised amount

Statement

An issuer of a financial guarantee contract measures it initially at fair value (Ind AS 109.5.1.1) and subsequently at the higher of (Ind AS 109.4.2.1(c)):

\[ \text{FGC}_t = \max\left( \text{ECL}_t,\; \text{FV}_0 - \sum_{k=1}^{t} \text{Income}_k \right) \]

where \( \text{FGC}_t \) is the carrying amount at date \( t \); \( \text{ECL}_t \) is the loss allowance determined under Section 5.5 (12-month or lifetime); \( \text{FV}_0 \) is the initial fair value; and \( \text{Income}_k \) is the guarantee income recognised in period \( k \) under Ind AS 115 principles (usually straight-line over the guarantee term).

A typical fair value estimate for an un-priced intra-group guarantee:

\[ \text{FV}_0 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{(r_{\text{without}} - r_{\text{with}}) \times L_k}{(1 + r_{\text{without}})^{k}} \]

with \( L_k \) the loan outstanding in year \( k \), \( r_{\text{without}} \) the beneficiary's stand-alone rate and \( r_{\text{with}} \) the guaranteed rate.

Explanation

The "higher of" rule ensures the liability never falls below the expected loss. While the debtor is healthy, the amortised premium dominates and the guarantor books small non-cash income. When the debtor deteriorates, ECL rises above the amortised amount and the liability jumps, with the increase charged to P&L.

Key conditions and exceptions:

  • If the guarantee was issued in an arm's length transaction to an unrelated party, \( \text{FV}_0 \) is the premium received, absent evidence to the contrary.
  • Free intra-group guarantees: the debit for \( \text{FV}_0 \) is an investment in the subsidiary (capital contribution) in standalone accounts; for upstream or sister-company guarantees, an equity distribution or expense.
  • ECL staging: the date of initial recognition for impairment is the date the guarantee becomes irrevocable (Ind AS 109.5.5.6). Stage 1 uses 12-month ECL; significant increase in credit risk triggers lifetime ECL.
  • Cash shortfalls for a guarantee are expected payments to the holder less recoveries from the debtor (Ind AS 109.B5.5.32).
  • An entity that previously asserted it regards such contracts as insurance may elect Ind AS 117 (previously Ind AS 104) — rare for corporates.
  • Ind AS 107 requires disclosure of the maximum exposure (full amount callable), which is the number that matters analytically.

Illustration

Parent guarantees a ₹200 crore, 4-year bullet loan to its subsidiary on 1 April 2023. Rate without guarantee 11%, with guarantee 9%. Differential ₹4 crore p.a.; 4-year annuity factor at 11% ≈ 3.102, so the initial fair value is about ₹12.4 crore, amortised at ₹3.1 crore p.a.

Date Amortised amount (₹ cr) ECL (₹ cr) Carrying amount (₹ cr) P&L effect (₹ cr)
31 Mar 2024 9.3 1.0 (stage 1) 9.3 +3.1 income
31 Mar 2025 6.2 24.0 (stage 2, lifetime) 24.0 +3.1 income, −17.8 ECL charge (net −14.7)

Note that standalone EBITDA would show ₹3.1 crore guarantee "income" both years if presented in other income — a forensic adjustment candidate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute carrying amounts [L2]

On 1 April 2024 a company guarantees a ₹150 crore, 3-year loan of its JV for an arm's length fee of 1% p.a. received annually in arrears, present valued at 10% (initial fair value equal to the PV of fees). At 31 March 2025, 12-month ECL is ₹0.6 crore; at 31 March 2026, the JV breaches covenants and lifetime ECL is ₹18 crore. Compute the carrying amount and P&L effect at both dates.

💡 Hint: Build the amortised-amount column first, then compare with ECL each year.

Q2. Debit side of the entry [L1]

State the debit entry on initial recognition of a free guarantee given by (a) a parent for a wholly owned subsidiary; (b) a subsidiary for its parent's loan; (c) a company for a promoter-owned private company. Explain the reasoning for each.

Q3. Analyst view [L2]

Explain why a guarantee liability of ₹15 crore can coexist with a disclosed maximum exposure of ₹1,500 crore, and how you would adjust the guarantor's net debt in a credit memo.
