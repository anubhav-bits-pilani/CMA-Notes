Disputed Direct Tax Demands, Appendix C and Vivad se Vishwas 2024

Scope: Disputed direct tax demands: Ind AS 12 Appendix C uncertain tax treatments; stay deposit, Vivad se Vishwas 2024

Why it matters

Income-tax demands are typically the largest line in "claims against the company not acknowledged as debt" for Indian companies — transfer pricing adjustments, s.14A disallowances, s.80-IA/10AA claims, depreciation on goodwill, MAT credits, and characterisation of payments (royalty vs business income). Because these are income taxes, the recognition question is answered by Ind AS 12 Appendix C, not Ind AS 37. The cash side — pre-deposits to obtain stay of demand, adjustments of refunds against demands, and settlement schemes such as the Direct Tax Vivad se Vishwas Scheme, 2024 — affects cash flow and the balance sheet in ways analysts often misread.

Core notes

Scope: Ind AS 12 vs Ind AS 37

Appendix C to Ind AS 12 (Uncertainty over Income Tax Treatments, effective for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2019) applies to the determination of taxable profit, tax bases, unused losses, credits and rates when there is uncertainty. It does not apply to taxes outside Ind AS 12 (GST, customs) — those stay in Ind AS 37 (s05). Interest and penalties on income taxes are not specifically addressed; the entity chooses, as an accounting policy applied consistently, whether to treat them under Ind AS 12 (if they are in substance income taxes) or Ind AS 37, consistent with the IFRIC agenda decision of 2018 on interest and penalties.

The Appendix C model

  1. Unit of account: consider each uncertain tax treatment separately or together with others, whichever better predicts resolution.
  2. Detection risk is ignored: assume the tax authority will examine amounts it has a right to examine with full knowledge of all relevant information.
  3. Probable acceptance test: if it is probable (more likely than not) that the authority will accept the treatment, accounting follows the tax return.
  4. If not probable, reflect the uncertainty using whichever method better predicts resolution: - the most likely amount — suitable for binary outcomes (allowed or disallowed), or - the expected value — suitable for a range of outcomes (transfer pricing margin disputes).
  5. Reassess when facts change — new assessment orders, appellate rulings on the same issue, a change in jurisdictional High Court precedent, or expiry of the limitation period.

Appendix C uses the same "probable" threshold as Ind AS 37 but measures through current/deferred tax, so the charge hits the tax line, not "other expenses" — which matters for EBITDA comparisons.

The demand lifecycle in India

Stage Forum Typical cash position Accounting signal
Assessment order u/s 143(3)/144C AO / DRP (TP cases) Notice of demand u/s 156 Reassess Appendix C
First appeal CIT(A) / NFAC Stay on payment of 20% of disputed demand (CBDT OM, 2016 as revised 2017) Deposit is an asset if recovery probable
Second appeal ITAT Stay from ITAT, often conditional Favourable ITAT order lowers probability
Question of law High Court, Supreme Court Department appeals subject to monetary limits (CBDT Circular 5/2024: ₹60 lakh ITAT, ₹2 crore HC, ₹5 crore SC) Low-value department appeals may be withdrawn

Stay deposit accounting: tax paid under protest or adjusted against refunds is not an expense. It is recognised as a current tax asset / "balances with government authorities" if the entity expects to recover it, and it reduces the cash outflow exposure of the contingent liability, not the exposure to loss. Many notes show contingent liabilities net of such deposits; the analyst should gross up to see total exposure and separately note the cash already locked up.

Illustration

A listed IT company has a TP adjustment of ₹180 crore (tax effect ₹62 crore plus interest u/s 234B/234C of ₹25 crore) for AY 2020-21. It paid 20% of the disputed tax, ₹12.4 crore, to get a stay. Counsel's view: the DRP applied a comparables set the ITAT rejected for this company in two prior years, so acceptance by the authority on final resolution is probable.

  • Appendix C: probable acceptance → no current tax liability recognised; disclose ₹62 crore (and interest if policy covers it) under contingent liabilities.
  • Deposit: ₹12.4 crore as non-current tax asset.

If instead the ITAT had ruled against the company on the same issue for AY 2019-20, acceptance would not be probable. Using expected value with outcomes ₹0 (30%), ₹30 crore (40%) and ₹62 crore (30%), the liability would be ₹30.6 crore, recognised in current tax with interest per policy.

Direct Tax Vivad se Vishwas Scheme, 2024 (DTVSV 2024)

Enacted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 and effective from 1 October 2024, DTVSV 2024 allows settlement of income-tax appeals, writs and SLPs pending as on 22 July 2024 (including DRP objections and revision applications). The settlement amount depends on the type of dispute and timing (figures as enacted; verify against the rules and any extension notifications):

Dispute type Declaration filed by 31 Dec 2024 Filed after 31 Dec 2024
Disputed tax — appeal filed after 31 Jan 2020 ("new appellant") 100% of disputed tax 110%
Disputed tax — appeal pending on or before 31 Jan 2020 ("old appellant") 110% 120%
Disputed interest, penalty or fee only 25% of disputed amount 30%

Half rates apply where the appeal is filed by the department or the issue is already decided in the assessee's favour at an appellate level. Interest and penalty are waived on settlement of disputed tax. Exclusions include search cases with specified conditions, prosecution cases and certain undisclosed foreign income.

Accounting on opting in: the decision to settle converts an uncertain tax position into a known liability. The settlement amount less any amount already provided is charged to current tax (prior-year tax) when the entity has decided to settle and the outcome is no longer uncertain — typically when the declaration is filed. Pre-deposits are adjusted against the settlement amount. Analysts should treat the charge as a non-recurring prior-period tax item, but should ask why the matter was disclosed as contingent (implying probable success) and then settled at 100% of tax.

Reading the note

  • Separate income-tax demands from other claims and check whether interest is included.
  • Track AY-wise recurrence: the same issue every year implies a structural tax position, not a one-off dispute.
  • Compare the effective tax rate with 25.17% (s.115BAA) or the applicable regime; a persistently low ETR plus rising income-tax contingent liabilities suggests aggressive positions not provided for.
  • Check deferred tax: unused tax credits (MAT) or losses challenged by the department affect DTA recoverability.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Favourable ITAT ruling, department appeal to HC: probability of acceptance usually remains probable; but if the HC has ruled against another taxpayer on the same issue, reassess.
  • Penalty proceedings u/s 270A are often initiated but kept in abeyance; companies omit them from contingent liabilities. Ask whether they are "remote" or merely unquantified.
  • Refunds withheld u/s 245 against disputed demands effectively collect the demand; cash flow from operations understates if refunds are blocked.
  • Group-wide issues: in consolidation, a subsidiary's uncertain tax position must be assessed under the parent's policy.
  • Settlement under DTVSV as evidence: opting in after years of disclosing "not probable" outflow weakens the credibility of other contingent tax disclosures.

Key takeaways

  • Income-tax uncertainties fall under Ind AS 12 Appendix C: assume full examination, test probable acceptance, measure by most likely amount or expected value.
  • Interest and penalties follow a consistently applied policy choice (Ind AS 12 or Ind AS 37).
  • Stay deposits (typically 20% of disputed demand) are assets, not expenses; gross up net-of-deposit disclosures.
  • DTVSV 2024 converts uncertain positions into fixed settlements; record as current tax of earlier years.
  • Recurring AY-wise demands on the same issue signal structural risk; reconcile with ETR and CARO 3(vii)(b).

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply Appendix C [L2]

A company claimed ₹90 crore deduction u/s 80-IA for a power project in AY 2021-22 (tax effect at 34.94%). The AO disallowed it; CIT(A) upheld the disallowance; the appeal is before ITAT. The jurisdictional High Court allowed a similar claim for another taxpayer in 2023. Assess whether a liability should be recognised under Appendix C at 31 March 2025, and state which measurement method fits.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the outcome is binary and what the precedent implies for "probable acceptance".

Q2. Stay deposit and disclosure [L1]

Disputed income-tax demand: tax ₹150 crore, interest ₹48 crore. The company paid ₹30 crore for stay. Draft the journal entry for the deposit and the contingent liability disclosure line under both "gross" and "net" formats.

Q3. Vivad se Vishwas decision [L2]

A company has three disputes pending as on 22 July 2024: (a) disputed tax ₹40 crore, own appeal filed in 2022 before CIT(A); (b) disputed tax ₹25 crore, appeal by the department before HC after the company won at ITAT; (c) penalty of ₹12 crore only, own appeal before ITAT. Using the DTVSV 2024 rates for declarations filed by 31 December 2024, compute the settlement amount for each and discuss the accounting impact for FY 2024-25 if all three are settled.

Q4. ETR and contingent tax screen [L3]

A mid-cap manufacturer under s.115BAA reports PBT of ₹800 crore and tax expense of ₹150 crore for FY 2024-25. Income-tax contingent liabilities rose from ₹120 crore to ₹260 crore, with the same issue (s.37 disallowance of promotional expenses) for five consecutive years. Write an analyst's assessment and list the adjustments you would consider in a valuation.
