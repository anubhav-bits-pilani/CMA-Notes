Subtopic note

Corporate Guarantees and Ind AS 109 Financial Guarantee Accounting

Scope: Corporate guarantees for subsidiaries/related parties; Ind AS 109 financial guarantee accounting

Why it matters

Indian groups routinely borrow at the subsidiary or SPV level with a parent (or promoter company) guarantee. The guaranteed debt does not sit on the guarantor's standalone balance sheet, yet the guarantor's creditors are exposed to it. Collapses such as IL&FS, the Essel group (letters of comfort) and several infra and power groups showed that standalone leverage was meaningless without the guarantee book. Ind AS 109 brought guarantees partly on balance sheet at fair value, and Ind AS 107 requires the maximum exposure to be disclosed. Credit analysts add guarantees to debt for stressed beneficiaries; equity analysts use them to test whether the "holding company discount" is warranted.

Core notes

Definition

A financial guarantee contract requires the issuer to make specified payments to reimburse the holder for a loss it incurs because a specified debtor fails to make payment when due under a debt instrument (Ind AS 109, Appendix A). Corporate guarantees to banks for a subsidiary's term loan or working capital facility meet this definition. Performance guarantees (completion of a project) do not — they are Ind AS 37 or Ind AS 115 items. Letters of comfort meet it only if they create an enforceable obligation to pay.

Accounting by the issuer (guarantor)

Initial recognition: at fair value (Ind AS 109.5.1.1). If the guarantee is issued to an unrelated party in a stand-alone arm's length transaction, fair value equals the premium received. For intra-group guarantees given free of charge, fair value must be estimated, commonly as:

  • the present value of the interest differential (rate the subsidiary would pay without the guarantee less the rate achieved with it), or
  • the present value of expected loss (probability of default × loss given default × exposure), or
  • market guarantee commission rates (bank guarantee charges of about 0.5%–2% p.a.).

Subsequent measurement: at the higher of (i) the loss allowance under the expected credit loss model of Section 5.5, and (ii) the amount initially recognised less cumulative income recognised per Ind AS 115 (Ind AS 109.4.2.1(c)). The amortisation of the initial amount is recognised as income (guarantee commission) over the guarantee period, usually straight-line.

Other side of the initial entry, depending on who benefits:

Beneficiary Debit in guarantor's standalone books
Subsidiary, no fee charged Investment in subsidiary (deemed capital contribution)
Subsidiary, fee charged below fair value Cash/receivable for fee; investment for shortfall
Parent/promoter entity (upstream) Equity (deemed distribution), not an asset
Third party, arm's length fee Cash/receivable

Illustration

Parent Ltd guarantees a ₹500 crore, 5-year term loan to its wholly-owned subsidiary on 1 April 2024 at no charge. Without the guarantee the subsidiary would pay 11%; with it, 9.5%. Interest differential = ₹7.5 crore p.a. Using a discount rate of 11% for 5 years (annuity factor about 3.696), fair value ≈ ₹27.7 crore.

Date Entry Dr (₹ cr) Cr (₹ cr)
1 Apr 2024 Investment in subsidiary 27.7
Financial guarantee liability 27.7
31 Mar 2025 Financial guarantee liability 5.5
Other income — guarantee commission 5.5

At 31 March 2025 the carrying amount is ₹22.2 crore. If the subsidiary's credit deteriorates and lifetime ECL on the guarantee is ₹60 crore (stage 2/3 exposure), the liability is raised to ₹60 crore with ₹37.8 crore charged to P&L. Note the asymmetry: the parent's "income" from guarantees is non-cash and the eventual loss is front-loaded only when credit deteriorates.

In the subsidiary's books, if it has not paid for the guarantee, Ind AS practice does not require recognising the benefit separately; the loan is recognised at its fair value using the guaranteed rate. In consolidated statements the guarantee eliminates.

ECL for guarantees

The ECL model treats the guarantee as a loan commitment-like exposure: the date the entity becomes party to the irrevocable commitment is the date of initial recognition for impairment purposes (Ind AS 109.5.5.6). Stage 1 uses 12-month ECL; a significant increase in the debtor's credit risk moves it to lifetime ECL. Cash shortfalls are the expected payments to reimburse the holder less amounts expected to be recovered from the debtor (Ind AS 109.B5.5.32).

Disclosure

  • Ind AS 107: maximum exposure to credit risk — for a financial guarantee, the maximum amount the entity would have to pay if called, which may be significantly greater than the liability recognised (Ind AS 107.B10(c)).
  • Ind AS 24: guarantees given or received with related parties.
  • Schedule III: guarantees under contingent liabilities (to the extent not provided for).
  • Companies Act s.186(4): disclose particulars of loans, guarantees and securities given and their purpose in the financial statements; s.185/186 restrict guarantees to directors' interested entities and set limits (60% of paid-up capital, free reserves and securities premium, or 100% of free reserves and securities premium, whichever is more, unless a special resolution is passed).
  • CARO 3(iii): auditor reports aggregate guarantees given during the year and outstanding, to subsidiaries, JVs, associates and others, and whether terms are prejudicial.

Analytical treatment

  1. List every guarantee by beneficiary, amount guaranteed and outstanding loan covered.
  2. Assess beneficiary health: interest cover, net debt/EBITDA, CARO 3(ix) for the subsidiary, rating.
  3. Classify: healthy and self-servicing (no adjustment, monitor), stressed (add outstanding covered debt to the guarantor's adjusted debt or apply a probability weight), already called/invoked (treat fully as debt).
  4. Compute guarantees as % of standalone net worth and the "look-through" leverage.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Guarantee amount vs outstanding: notes often show the sanctioned limit guaranteed, not the drawn balance. Use outstanding for exposure, limit for maximum potential.
  • Cross-guarantees in consortium lending and guarantees from multiple group entities for the same loan — exposure is joint and several; do not divide.
  • Guarantees for promoter-owned entities outside the listed group are value transfers; they also require Reg 23 approval and are a governance red flag (Week 7 topic 5).
  • Security instead of guarantee: a pledge of shares or mortgage of assets for a related party's loan is not a financial guarantee in form but has the same economic exposure; CARO 3(iii) covers "security".
  • Zero carrying amount: many companies carry guarantees at nil, arguing the fair value is immaterial. With stressed beneficiaries this is hard to defend under the ECL test.
  • Invoked guarantees become financial liabilities (payable to the bank) and should move out of contingent liabilities.

Key takeaways

  • Financial guarantees are recognised at fair value and then at the higher of ECL and unamortised initial amount.
  • Free intra-group guarantees are a deemed capital contribution (investment) to a subsidiary, or a deemed distribution if upstream.
  • The balance-sheet liability is tiny relative to the exposure; Ind AS 107 maximum exposure and Schedule III contingent liabilities give the real number.
  • Standalone analysis must look through guarantees to the beneficiary's ability to service debt.
  • Cross-check against CARO 3(iii), s.186(4) disclosure and Reg 23 approvals.

Practice Questions

Q1. Initial and subsequent measurement [L2]

On 1 April 2024, Alpha Ltd guarantees a ₹300 crore, 4-year loan to its subsidiary Beta at no charge. Beta's stand-alone borrowing rate would be 12%; the guaranteed rate is 10%. At 31 March 2025, Beta has been downgraded and the 12-month ECL on the guarantee is ₹4 crore, lifetime ECL ₹35 crore. Compute the initial fair value using the interest differential method (discount at 12%), and pass all journal entries for FY 2024-25 in Alpha's standalone books.

💡 Hint: Decide the ECL stage first, then compare the ECL with the amortised initial amount.

Q2. Upstream guarantee [L1]

A listed company guarantees, without fee, a ₹200 crore loan taken by its promoter's private holding company. Explain the debit side of the initial entry, the governance approvals required, and how CARO would report it.

Q3. Look-through leverage [L2]

Standalone Gamma Ltd: net debt ₹800 crore, EBITDA ₹400 crore, net worth ₹2,400 crore. Guarantees outstanding: ₹600 crore for Sub A (interest cover 4.0x), ₹450 crore for Sub B (interest cover 0.8x, CARO 3(ix) default reported), ₹150 crore for an associate (rated BBB). Build a look-through adjusted leverage table with your probability assumptions and state guarantees as % of net worth.

Q4. Analyst judgement on nil carrying amount [L3]

A power holding company carries ₹2,800 crore of guarantees for SPVs at nil value, stating "fair value is not material". Three SPVs have regulatory disputes and delayed DISCOM receivables. Write a memo paragraph assessing the policy against Ind AS 109.4.2.1(c) and recommending analytical adjustments.
