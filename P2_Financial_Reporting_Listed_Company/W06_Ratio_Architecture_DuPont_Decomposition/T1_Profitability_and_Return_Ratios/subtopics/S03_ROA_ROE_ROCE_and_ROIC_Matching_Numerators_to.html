Subtopic note

ROA, ROE, ROCE and ROIC: Matching Numerators to Capital Bases

Scope: ROA, ROE (owners' PAT on owners' equity), ROCE (pre-tax), ROIC (post-tax, operating)

Why it matters

Return ratios answer the investor's question — "how much does this business earn on the money tied up in it?" — but each ratio answers it for a different provider of capital. ROE is the shareholders' view, ROCE the all-capital pre-tax view that Indian bankers and brokers favour, ROIC the operating post-tax view that drives valuation, and ROA the asset-intensity view used for banks and for leverage-neutral comparison. Mismatching numerator and denominator (equity profit over total capital, pre-tax profit over post-tax hurdle) is the most common error in pitch books and IC memos. The governing principle is consistency of claimants: the profit in the numerator must belong to exactly the capital providers whose capital is in the denominator.

Core notes

The claimant-matching principle

Ratio Numerator Belongs to Denominator Typical Indian usage
ROA PAT (or NOPAT) Shareholders (or all capital) Average total assets Banks/NBFCs; leverage-neutral screens
ROE PAT attributable to owners of the parent Equity shareholders of parent Average equity attributable to owners Equity research, Schedule III
ROCE EBIT (pre-tax, pre-interest) All capital providers, pre-tax Average capital employed (equity + debt, or total assets − current liabilities) Broker notes, bank appraisal, Schedule III
ROIC NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − t) All capital, post-tax, operating only Average invested capital (operating NWC + net operating fixed assets) Valuation, EVA, ROIC vs WACC

ROA

\[ \text{ROA} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Average total assets}} \]

Mixing claimants is built into the conventional ROA: PAT is after interest (an equity number), while total assets are funded by debt and equity. A leveraged company is penalised for its interest cost but not credited for the debt funding. The leverage-neutral variant uses NOPAT (or PAT + interest × (1 − t)) in the numerator. For banks ROA (PAT ÷ average total assets) is standard because assets are the earning base and leverage is regulated.

ROE

\[ \text{ROE} = \frac{\text{PAT attributable to owners of the parent}}{\text{Average equity attributable to owners of the parent}} \]

  • Under Ind AS 1.81B the consolidated P&L allocates profit between owners of the parent and non-controlling interests; under Ind AS 110 NCI is presented within equity but separately from parent equity. Use the owners' line on both sides.
  • If preference shares classified as equity exist, deduct preference dividend from the numerator and preference capital from the denominator (Ind AS 33 logic). Compulsorily convertible instruments classified as equity remain in equity.
  • Equity includes other equity: retained earnings, securities premium, OCI reserves (FVOCI equity reserve, cash flow hedge reserve). Large OCI reserves can make ROE volatile without any change in operating profit — consider an "ROE ex-OCI reserves" variant for companies with big FVOCI stakes.

ROCE (pre-tax)

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Average capital employed}} \]

Two common definitions of capital employed:

  1. Financing side: Total equity (incl. NCI) + total borrowings (+ lease liabilities, if EBIT is post-Ind AS 116) (+ deferred tax liability in the ICAI Guidance Note variant).
  2. Asset side: Total assets − current liabilities (non-interest bearing). Cleaner if short-term borrowings are added back as capital.

ICAI's Guidance Note on Division II illustrates capital employed as tangible net worth + total debt + deferred tax liability; companies may adopt their own definition but must disclose numerator and denominator (Schedule III). Because both sides include NCI and all lenders, the numerator must be pre-NCI, pre-interest. Whether EBIT includes other income should mirror whether cash and investments are in capital employed: include both or exclude both (see s04).

ROIC (post-tax, operating)

\[ \text{ROIC} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Average invested capital}}, \qquad \text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT}_{\text{op}} \times (1 - t) \]

  • EBIT\(_{\text{op}}\) excludes other income, exceptional items and share of associates; it may add back lease interest if leases are treated as debt.
  • t is an operating tax rate: the statutory rate (25.168% for a s.115BAA company: 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess) or the effective rate adjusted for the tax shield on interest and tax on other income. Do not use the reported effective tax rate blindly when it is distorted by deferred tax remeasurement or MAT write-offs.
  • Invested capital is built operationally (operating NWC + net PPE + ROU + operating intangibles ± goodwill) and reconciled to the financing side (equity + debt + leases − surplus cash − non-operating assets), as constructed in Week 5 (w5_t3).

Illustration (illustrative, ₹ crore, averages)

Item ₹ crore
EBIT (excl. other income) 1,600
Other income 250
Finance cost 60
PAT (owners 1,300; NCI 40) 1,340
Average total assets 10,500
Average owners' equity 7,600; NCI 200
Average borrowings + leases 600
Average surplus cash and liquid MFs 2,400
Tax rate 25.168%
  • ROA = 1,340 ÷ 10,500 = 12.8%
  • ROE = 1,300 ÷ 7,600 = 17.1%
  • ROCE (incl. cash, EBIT + other income) = 1,850 ÷ 8,400 = 22.0%
  • ROCE (ex-cash, EBIT only) = 1,600 ÷ 6,000 = 26.7%
  • ROIC = 1,600 × 0.74832 ÷ 6,000 = 1,197 ÷ 6,000 = 20.0%

The gap between ROE (17.1%) and ROIC (20.0%) is not "negative leverage": the equity base carries ₹2,400 crore of low-yielding cash. This is the Asian Paints/IT-major pattern.

Why ROCE is pre-tax and ROIC post-tax

ROCE is popular in India because it is easy to compute from the face of the statements and is unaffected by tax regime changes (s.115BAA migration, tax holidays under s.80-IA/10AA). It is suitable for ranking operating efficiency across companies with different tax positions. ROIC is the right input for value creation because the hurdle rate (WACC) is post-tax. The two are linked:

\[ \text{ROIC} \approx \text{ROCE}_{\text{ex-cash}} \times (1 - t) \]

when capital employed (ex-cash) and invested capital are defined identically.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative or tiny equity. After large accumulated losses or buybacks, ROE explodes or turns meaningless; use ROIC.
  • Holding companies. ROE on standalone accounts of a holding company reflects dividend income, not operations; use consolidated.
  • Revaluation reserves. A company on the Ind AS 16 revaluation model, or with deemed cost at fair value on transition (Ind AS 101), has a higher asset base and lower returns than a historical-cost peer.
  • Capital work-in-progress. Large CWIP depresses ROCE until commissioning; show ROCE ex-CWIP as a supplementary metric.
  • Goodwill. ROIC excluding goodwill measures organic operating returns; including goodwill measures returns on acquisition price paid. Show both.
  • Tax rate on NOPAT for loss-making years. Use the marginal statutory rate unless losses are unrecognised and will never be used.

Key takeaways

  • Match claimants: numerator profit must belong to the providers of the denominator capital.
  • ROE uses owners' PAT over owners' equity; strip NCI from both.
  • ROCE is pre-tax on all capital; define capital employed and disclose it.
  • ROIC is post-tax, operating, on invested capital — the only return comparable with WACC.
  • Cash treatment must match: other income in the numerator only if cash is in the denominator.
  • Report ex-cash and ex-goodwill variants for cash-rich and acquisitive companies.

Practice Questions

Q1. Four returns from one set of accounts [L1]

An NSE-listed engineering company reports (₹ crore, FY2024-25 consolidated): EBIT (excl. other income) 2,400; other income 180; finance cost 320; tax 560; PAT attributable to owners 1,560; PAT attributable to NCI 140. Opening/closing: total assets 18,000/20,000; owners' equity 9,500/10,500; NCI 900/1,000; borrowings 3,800/4,200; lease liabilities 300/340; cash and liquid investments 1,100/1,300. Tax rate 25.168%. Compute ROA (PAT and NOPAT variants), ROE, ROCE (two definitions) and ROIC.

Q2. Claimant mismatch [L2]

A sell-side note computes "ROCE" as consolidated PAT ÷ (owners' equity + borrowings) and reports 14%. Explain every inconsistency in this definition and show how each would bias the ratio for a company with high NCI and high debt.

💡 Hint: Check separately for interest, tax and NCI whether the numerator and the denominator refer to the same claimants.

Q3. Tax regime and the ROCE/ROIC choice [L2]

Company P pays tax at 25.168% under s.115BAA. Company Q enjoys a SEZ deduction under s.10AA and has an effective tax rate of 12%. Both have pre-tax ROCE of 24%. Explain which ratio you would use to rank operating efficiency and which to assess value creation against a 12% WACC, and why the answers differ.

Q4. Holding company trap [L3]

A diversified group's listed holding company reports a standalone ROE of 31% driven by a large special dividend from a subsidiary; consolidated ROE is 11%. Write a short note for a portfolio manager explaining which number reflects economic performance, and list the disclosures you would examine to confirm.
