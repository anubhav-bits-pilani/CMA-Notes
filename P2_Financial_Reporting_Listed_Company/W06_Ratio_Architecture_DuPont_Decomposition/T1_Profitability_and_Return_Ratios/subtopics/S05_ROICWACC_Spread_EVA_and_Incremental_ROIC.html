Subtopic note

ROIC–WACC Spread, EVA and Incremental ROIC

Scope: ROIC vs WACC spread, EVA and value creation; incremental ROIC on new capital

Why it matters

A company creates value only when the return on the capital it deploys exceeds the return investors could earn elsewhere at the same risk. Growth with ROIC below WACC destroys value — the faster the growth, the faster the destruction. This is the single most important link between ratio analysis (this week) and valuation (DCF in Week 15–16): in a DCF, terminal value is driven by the long-run spread and reinvestment rate, and an FP&A team evaluating capex, acquisitions or a new product line is implicitly forecasting incremental ROIC. Indian context adds specifics: high nominal WACC (G-sec yields around 6.5–7.5% plus an equity risk premium), a 25.168% concessional tax regime that changes the debt tax shield, and a market that pays very high multiples for the handful of companies with sustained 30%+ ROIC (paints, FMCG, IT, select financials).

Core notes

The spread

\[ \text{Spread} = \text{ROIC} - \text{WACC} \]

Both terms must be post-tax, nominal, and on the same capital base:

  • ROIC = NOPAT ÷ average (or opening) invested capital, NOPAT = EBIT\(_{\text{op}}\) × (1 − t).
  • WACC = \(w_E k_E + w_D k_D (1 - t)\) using market-value weights; the tax shield on debt is inside WACC, so NOPAT must not also deduct interest.

If ROIC is computed excluding surplus cash, WACC should be the operating (unlevered-asset) WACC, not one blended with the very low return on cash. Pre-tax ROCE must never be compared with post-tax WACC (see the topic pitfalls).

Economic Value Added (EVA)

Stern Stewart's EVA restates residual income for the whole firm:

\[ \text{EVA}_t = (\text{ROIC}_t - \text{WACC}) \times \text{IC}_{t-1} = \text{NOPAT}_t - \text{WACC} \times \text{IC}_{t-1} \]

where \(\text{IC}_{t-1}\) is opening invested capital (or average, consistently). EVA is a ₹ amount: positive EVA means the business earned more than the capital charge. Stern Stewart listed over 150 accounting adjustments (capitalising R&D and advertising, adding back goodwill amortisation, LIFO reserves, provisions). Practitioners in India typically use 4–6 adjustments:

  1. Add back acquired-intangible amortisation and goodwill impairments (keep cumulative amounts in IC).
  2. Treat lease liabilities as debt and ROU assets as IC (post-Ind AS 116 this is automatic).
  3. Remove surplus cash and non-operating investments from IC and their income from NOPAT.
  4. Remove exceptional items (net of tax) from NOPAT.
  5. Capitalise R&D for pharma or tech if comparing with peers who capitalise development costs (Ind AS 38.57).
  6. Use cash operating taxes rather than book tax if deferred tax is large and persistent.

Market Value Added (MVA) is the market's capitalisation of expected future EVA:

\[ \text{MVA} = \text{Enterprise value} - \text{Invested capital} = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \frac{\text{EVA}_t}{(1 + \text{WACC})^t} \]

Value creation and growth

The value-driver formula (McKinsey) connects growth \(g\), ROIC and WACC:

\[ \text{Value} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_{t+1} \times \left(1 - \frac{g}{\text{RONIC}}\right)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

where RONIC is the return on new invested capital and \(g/\text{RONIC}\) is the reinvestment rate. When RONIC = WACC, the value is \(\text{NOPAT}_{t+1} / \text{WACC}\) irrespective of growth — growth adds nothing. When RONIC < WACC, faster growth lowers value.

Incremental ROIC

Average ROIC reflects the whole historical capital base, including legacy plants bought cheaply decades ago. Decisions are about new capital, so measure the return on what has been added:

\[ \text{Incremental ROIC} = \frac{\Delta \text{NOPAT}}{\Delta \text{IC}} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_{t} - \text{NOPAT}_{t-n}}{\text{IC}_{t-1} - \text{IC}_{t-n-1}} \]

Use a multi-year window (3–5 years) and lag the capital by a year because capacity takes time to generate profit. Single-year incremental ROIC is noise: a ₹50 crore NOPAT rise on a ₹5 crore capital change gives a meaningless 1,000%.

Illustration (illustrative paint company, ₹ crore)

FY NOPAT Opening IC ROIC on opening IC WACC EVA
FY2022-23 900 3,600 25.0% 12.0% 468
FY2023-24 1,050 4,000 26.3% 12.0% 570
FY2024-25 1,080 5,200 20.8% 12.0% 456

Incremental ROIC over the window (FY2022-23 to FY2024-25): (1,080 − 900) ÷ (5,200 − 3,600) = 180 ÷ 1,600 = 11.3%. Average ROIC is still a healthy 20.8%, but the last ₹1,600 crore of capital (a new plant plus a jump in receivables as the company defends share against a new entrant) is earning less than WACC. EVA has fallen even though NOPAT has grown. This is exactly the diagnostic a board needs before approving the next capex tranche.

Where WACC comes from

The full build — Indian 10-year G-sec as risk-free rate, equity risk premium (commonly 6–8% for India), beta from NSE regressions or bottom-up relevered peers, cost of debt from ratings/actual borrowing, post-tax at 25.168% for a s.115BAA company — is in Week 15. For this week, use a given WACC and focus on computing the spread consistently.

FP&A and modelling use

  • Capex appraisal: require projects to show IRR above WACC and a path to incremental ROIC above WACC by year 3 of operation.
  • Segment scorecards: compute EVA by segment using segment assets (Ind AS 108) and an allocated capital charge.
  • Incentive design: some Indian groups link management bonuses to EVA improvement rather than PAT growth.
  • DCF sanity check: if your forecast implies RONIC of 40% forever with 12% growth, the terminal value is probably overstated; fade RONIC towards WACC.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative or near-zero ΔIC: incremental ROIC is undefined or explosive; report "n.m." and analyse asset-light growth through capital turnover instead.
  • Acquisitions: include goodwill in IC for capital-allocation judgement; acquired businesses usually show low incremental ROIC initially.
  • Inflation: in high-inflation years, historical-cost IC is understated and ROIC overstated relative to replacement-cost returns.
  • Cyclicals (cement, metals): judge the spread over a full cycle, not at a peak.
  • WACC uncertainty: a 100 bps change in WACC can flip a thin spread; present a sensitivity.
  • Excess cash: including cash in IC dilutes ROIC towards the post-tax treasury yield (~5%), which is below WACC by construction — not a sign of value destruction by operations, but a capital allocation question.

Key takeaways

  • Value is created only when ROIC exceeds WACC on a consistent post-tax, operating basis.
  • EVA converts the spread into rupees: NOPAT − WACC × IC.
  • MVA is the present value of expected future EVA; growth adds value only if RONIC exceeds WACC.
  • Incremental ROIC on new capital, over a lagged multi-year window, is the decision-relevant measure.
  • Use a handful of documented EVA adjustments rather than an unreplicable long list.

Practice Questions

Q1. EVA build [L1]

A cement company reports EBIT (excl. other income and exceptional items) of ₹2,600 crore, tax rate 25.168%, opening invested capital ₹19,000 crore and WACC 11.5%. Compute ROIC, the spread and EVA. State whether the company created value in the year.

Q2. Incremental ROIC over a window [L2]

NOPAT (₹ crore): FY2020-21 410; FY2021-22 470; FY2022-23 520; FY2023-24 610; FY2024-25 640. Invested capital at 31 March (₹ crore): 2020 2,900; 2021 3,050; 2022 3,600; 2023 4,400; 2024 5,100; 2025 5,600. Compute average ROIC for FY2024-25, three-year and four-year lagged incremental ROIC, and comment on capital allocation against a WACC of 12%.

💡 Hint: Pair each NOPAT change with the invested capital change one year earlier.

Q3. Growth that destroys value [L2]

Using the value-driver formula, compute the value of a business with next-year NOPAT of ₹500 crore and WACC of 12% at growth rates of 4% and 8%, first with RONIC of 20% and then with RONIC of 10%. Explain the pattern in one paragraph suitable for a board presentation.

Q4. Designing an EVA scorecard [L3]

A diversified Indian group with paints, chemicals and a real-estate subsidiary wants an EVA-based bonus scheme for business heads. Draft the design: NOPAT definition, IC definition (cash, goodwill, leases, land bank), capital charge by business, treatment of growth capex in its first two years, and three governance safeguards against gaming.

Q5. Interview: high ROIC, falling EVA [L2]

"A company's ROIC fell from 28% to 22% but EVA rose. How is that possible, and is it good news?" Prepare a numerical example and a short answer.
