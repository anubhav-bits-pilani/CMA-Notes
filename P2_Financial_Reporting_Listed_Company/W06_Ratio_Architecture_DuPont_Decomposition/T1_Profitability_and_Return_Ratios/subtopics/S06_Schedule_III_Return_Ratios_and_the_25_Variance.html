Subtopic note

Schedule III Return Ratios and the 25% Variance Explanation

Scope: Schedule III mandated ratios (ROE, ROCE, net profit ratio, return on investment) and 25% variance explanations

Why it matters

The 2021 amendments to Schedule III (effective for financial years beginning on or after 1 April 2021) added an "Additional Regulatory Information" section that requires every company — listed or unlisted — to disclose eleven ratios in its financial statements, explain what goes into each numerator and denominator, and explain any ratio that moved by more than 25% against the previous year. For an analyst these are a free, audited data point, but company-defined and therefore not comparable without checking definitions. For a financial-reporting professional they are a recurring year-end deliverable that auditors review and that regulators (NFRA, ROC) can question. Four of the eleven are return/profitability ratios and fall in this topic; the liquidity and turnover ratios are covered in t4 and t5.

Core notes

The eleven ratios (Schedule III Division II, Additional Regulatory Information)

1 Current ratio t4
2 Debt-equity ratio t4
3 Debt service coverage ratio t4
4 Return on equity ratio t1
5 Inventory turnover ratio t3/t5
6 Trade receivables turnover ratio t3
7 Trade payables turnover ratio t3
8 Net capital turnover ratio t5
9 Net profit ratio t1

The requirement has three parts: (a) disclose the ratios for the current and previous year, (b) explain the items included in the numerator and denominator, and (c) explain any change of more than 25% compared with the preceding year. The ratios form part of the notes to the financial statements and are therefore covered by the audit opinion. The prevailing interpretation (supported by ICAI's Guidance Note on Division II) is that Additional Regulatory Information applies to standalone financial statements and is not required in consolidated financial statements; verify against the current Guidance Note for the reporting year.

Formulas illustrated in the ICAI Guidance Note

Schedule III does not prescribe formulas. ICAI's Guidance Note on Division II – Ind AS Schedule III illustrates commonly used definitions; companies may use others but must state them.

Ratio Illustrative numerator Illustrative denominator
Return on equity Net profit after taxes − preference dividend (if any) Average shareholders' equity
Net profit ratio Net profit Net sales (revenue from operations; total sales less sales returns)
Return on capital employed Earnings before interest and taxes Capital employed = tangible net worth + total debt + deferred tax liability
Return on investment Income generated from invested funds Average invested funds in treasury investments

Return on investment is the least understood. It is not ROIC. It measures the return on the company's treasury investments (mutual funds, bonds, fixed deposits, equity investments held for return). The Guidance Note illustrates a time-weighted formula:

\[ \text{ROI} = \frac{MV_{T1} - MV_{T0} - \sum_t C_t}{MV_{T0} + \sum_t \left(W_t \times C_t\right)} \]

where \(MV_{T0}\) and \(MV_{T1}\) are the market values at the start and end of the period, \(C_t\) is the net cash flow (investment or withdrawal) at time \(t\), and \(W_t\) is the weight equal to the proportion of the period remaining after the flow, i.e., \(W_t = (T - t) / T\). A simpler variant (treasury income ÷ average treasury investments) is also seen; whichever is used must be stated. Companies with multiple treasury categories sometimes disclose ROI separately for mutual funds, fixed deposits and equity investments.

Writing the 25% variance explanation

The explanation must be specific and causal. Weak practice — "due to increase in profit" — restates the arithmetic. Good practice identifies the driver and, where useful, quantifies it.

Ratio FY2023-24 FY2024-25 Variance Explanation (illustrative)
Return on equity 24.1% 16.8% −30.3% Lower PAT due to impairment of investment in subsidiary (₹210 crore, exceptional item) and increase in average equity following QIP of ₹1,500 crore in December 2024
Net profit ratio 11.2% 7.9% −29.5% Exceptional impairment; gross margin compression from TiO2 cost increase partly offset by price increases
ROCE 27.5% 19.4% −29.5% Capitalisation of new plant (₹1,100 crore) in Q4 with limited contribution to EBIT in the year; QIP proceeds held in liquid funds pending deployment
ROI 7.4% 7.1% −4.1% Not applicable (variance below 25%)

Note the variance is measured as a relative change in the ratio (\((16.8 - 24.1) / 24.1 = -30.3\%\)), not in percentage points. A move from 10% to 13% is a 30% change and needs an explanation even though it is only 300 bps.

Computing the ratios from the financial statements

  1. Pull standalone numbers (P&L, balance sheet, the two balance sheet dates and the prior opening balance for averages).
  2. Apply the company's stated definitions — or, for your own company, the definitions in the accounting manual approved by the CFO and reviewed by auditors.
  3. Compute current and previous year ratios on the same definition; if the definition changes, restate the comparative and disclose.
  4. Compute relative variance; for every ratio with absolute relative variance above 25%, draft a driver-based explanation.
  5. Tie out: numerators and denominators should reconcile to line items; auditors will ask for the workings.

A simple Excel flag:

=IF(ABS(CurrRatio/PrevRatio-1)>0.25,"Explain","No explanation required")

Using Schedule III ratios as an analyst

  • They are standalone, so for groups with significant subsidiaries (Asian Paints, Berger with international subsidiaries) they differ from consolidated ratios used in equity research.
  • Definitions differ across peers — some ROCE definitions include cash and other income, some don't; some use closing capital employed. Always read the definition column before using the number.
  • The variance explanation is a free, auditor-reviewed management commentary — useful for spotting one-offs (impairments, tax remeasurement, land sale gains).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative base year: when the prior-year ratio is negative or near zero, a relative variance is mathematically meaningless; explain anyway and state the change in absolute terms.
  • Loss-making companies: ROE and net profit ratio are negative; still disclose and explain.
  • Exceptional items: decide whether "net profit" is before or after exceptional items and disclose; most companies use reported PAT.
  • Total debt and leases: whether lease liabilities are "debt" in ROCE and debt-equity is a company choice; disclose it.
  • No treasury investments: ROI may be "not applicable"; say so rather than omitting it.
  • Restatements: if comparatives are restated under Ind AS 8, compute the comparative ratio on restated figures.
  • Rounding at 25%: a variance of 24.9% on unrounded figures that rounds to 25% does not need an explanation, but many companies explain borderline cases voluntarily.

Key takeaways

  • Schedule III requires eleven ratios, with numerator/denominator disclosure and explanations for changes above 25%.
  • The four return ratios are ROE, net profit ratio, ROCE and return on investment (treasury return, not ROIC).
  • Formulas are company-defined; the ICAI Guidance Note provides illustrative definitions.
  • The 25% test is on relative change in the ratio, not percentage-point change.
  • Explanations should name causal drivers, ideally quantified.
  • Schedule III ratios are standalone and not directly comparable across peers without reading definitions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and flag [L1]

Standalone data (₹ crore): FY2023-24 — revenue from operations 6,400, PAT 640, EBIT 900, average equity 3,100, capital employed (tangible net worth + debt + DTL) 3,900. FY2024-25 — revenue 6,900, PAT 560, EBIT 820, average equity 3,600, capital employed 4,700. Compute ROE, net profit ratio and ROCE for both years, compute the relative variance, and identify which ratios require an explanation.

Q2. Time-weighted return on investment [L2]

A company's mutual fund portfolio had a market value of ₹500 crore on 1 April 2024. It invested a further ₹200 crore on 1 October 2024 and redeemed ₹100 crore on 1 January 2025. Market value on 31 March 2025 was ₹640 crore. Compute ROI using the time-weighted formula illustrated in the ICAI Guidance Note and compare it with a simple "income ÷ average balance" approach.

💡 Hint: Net flows enter with a negative sign in the numerator and are weighted by the fraction of the year remaining in the denominator.

Q3. Draft variance explanations [L2]

Your company's ROE fell from 21% to 14% and ROCE from 26% to 18%. Causes identified by the controllership team: a ₹900 crore rights issue in October; a deferred tax charge of ₹65 crore on remeasurement after electing s.115BAA and writing off MAT credit; a new plant capitalised in February. Draft the two Schedule III explanations in audit-ready language.

Q4. Analyst use of company-defined ratios [L3]

Two listed paint companies disclose ROCE of 31% and 24% in their standalone Schedule III notes. One defines capital employed as total equity + total borrowings; the other as total equity + borrowings + lease liabilities − cash and current investments, with EBIT excluding other income. Explain why the disclosed numbers are not comparable, what you would recompute, and in which direction each number is likely to move.
