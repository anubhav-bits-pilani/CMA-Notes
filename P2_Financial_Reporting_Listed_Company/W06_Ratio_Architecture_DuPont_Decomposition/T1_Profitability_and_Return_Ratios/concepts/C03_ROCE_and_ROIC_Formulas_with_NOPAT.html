Key concept

ROCE and ROIC Formulas with NOPAT

Concept: ROCE = EBIT ÷ Average capital employed; ROIC = NOPAT ÷ Average invested capital, NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − t)

Statement

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\overline{\text{CE}}}, \qquad \text{ROIC} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\overline{\text{IC}}}, \qquad \text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT}_{\text{op}} \times (1 - t) \]

where:

  • \(\text{EBIT}\) = earnings before interest (finance costs) and tax; state whether other income and exceptional items are included.
  • \(\text{EBIT}_{\text{op}}\) = operating EBIT, excluding other income (treasury income), exceptional items and share of associates/JVs.
  • \(\overline{\text{CE}}\) = average capital employed = total equity (incl. NCI) + total debt (± lease liabilities) (± DTL in the ICAI variant), or equivalently total assets − non-interest-bearing current liabilities.
  • \(\overline{\text{IC}}\) = average invested capital = operating NWC + net PPE + ROU assets + operating intangibles (± goodwill), equal to equity + debt + leases − surplus cash − non-operating assets.
  • \(t\) = operating tax rate (statutory 25.168% for a s.115BAA company; 34.944% under the old regime with 12% surcharge; or a normalised effective rate).

Explanation

ROCE is the pre-tax, all-capital return. Because it ignores tax, it is useful for ranking operating performance across companies with different tax regimes (old regime, s.115BAA, SEZ units) and is easy to compute from published accounts — which is why Indian brokers, bankers and Schedule III use it.

ROIC is the post-tax, operating return. NOPAT is the profit an unlevered version of the business would earn: tax is computed on EBIT as if there were no interest, so the tax shield on debt is excluded from NOPAT and instead captured in the after-tax cost of debt within WACC. That is why ROIC — not ROCE — is the right comparator for WACC.

Symmetry conditions:

  1. If surplus cash is excluded from IC/CE, exclude treasury income from EBIT.
  2. If lease liabilities are in CE/IC, use EBIT after ROU depreciation but before lease interest (post-Ind AS 116).
  3. If goodwill is in IC, the ratio measures returns on acquisition price; excluding it measures returns on tangible operating capital.
  4. NCI's capital is in CE and IC; NOPAT is before NCI share — no NCI adjustment is needed for ROCE/ROIC.

Tax rate choice: the reported effective tax rate may be distorted by deferred tax remeasurement on s.115BAA migration, MAT credit write-off, tax on other income or capital gains taxed at different rates. A defensible NOPAT uses the statutory marginal rate or a "cash operating tax" computed as reported tax + tax shield on interest − tax on non-operating income.

Illustration

Illustrative (₹ crore): EBIT\(_{\text{op}}\) 2,000; other income 300; average equity (incl. NCI) 9,000; average debt 1,500; average lease liabilities 500; average surplus cash and liquid MFs 3,000; tax 25.168%.

Measure Numerator Denominator Result
ROCE (all capital, incl. cash and other income) 2,300 11,000 20.9%
ROCE (operating, ex-cash) 2,000 8,000 25.0%
ROIC (operating, ex-cash) 2,000 × 0.74832 = 1,497 8,000 18.7%

The relationship \(\text{ROIC} = \text{ROCE}_{\text{op}} \times (1 - t)\) holds exactly because the operating denominators are identical: 25.0% × 0.74832 = 18.7%.

Practice Questions

Q1. ROCE and ROIC from a summary balance sheet [L1]

Average balances (₹ crore): total equity 7,400 (incl. NCI 400); borrowings 2,200; lease liabilities 600; cash and current investments 1,800 (operating cash 200). EBIT before other income ₹1,900 crore; other income ₹140 crore; tax rate 25.168%. Compute inclusive ROCE, operating ROCE and ROIC.

Q2. Which tax rate for NOPAT [L2]

A company reports PBT ₹1,000 crore and tax expense ₹420 crore, including a one-time ₹95 crore MAT credit write-off on electing s.115BAA. Finance cost is ₹120 crore and other income ₹80 crore. Compute NOPAT using (a) the reported effective rate, (b) the statutory 25.168% rate, and (c) a cash operating tax approach, and argue which belongs in ROIC.

💡 Hint: Remove the one-off first, then think about what tax the operating business would have paid with no debt and no treasury income.

Q3. Interview: ROCE vs ROIC [L1]

"Why do Indian broker reports quote ROCE when valuation theory uses ROIC?" Answer in under 120 words, mentioning taxation, data availability and the hurdle-rate comparison.
