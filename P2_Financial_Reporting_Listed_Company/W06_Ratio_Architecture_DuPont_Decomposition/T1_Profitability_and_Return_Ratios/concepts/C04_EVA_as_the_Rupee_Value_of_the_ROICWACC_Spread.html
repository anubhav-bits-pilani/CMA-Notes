Key concept

EVA as the Rupee Value of the ROIC–WACC Spread

Concept: EVA = (ROIC − WACC) × Invested capital

Statement

\[ \text{EVA}_t = (\text{ROIC}_t - \text{WACC}) \times \text{IC} = \text{NOPAT}_t - \text{WACC} \times \text{IC} \]

where:

  • \(\text{NOPAT}_t\) = net operating profit after tax for year \(t\) = \(\text{EBIT}_{\text{op}} \times (1 - t)\), after any documented EVA adjustments.
  • \(\text{IC}\) = invested capital — conventionally opening (\(\text{IC}_{t-1}\)) or average invested capital; the same base must be used in ROIC.
  • \(\text{WACC}\) = post-tax weighted average cost of capital = \(w_E k_E + w_D k_D (1 - t)\), at target or market-value weights.
  • \(\text{WACC} \times \text{IC}\) = the capital charge.

Explanation

EVA (a Stern Stewart trademark) is a residual income measure for the whole enterprise. Accounting profit charges only for debt (interest); EVA charges for all capital, including equity, at its opportunity cost. A company can report rising PAT and still destroy value if it raises capital faster than it raises NOPAT.

Why it matters for valuation: the present value of all future EVA equals Market Value Added (enterprise value − invested capital). A firm trading at an EV/IC multiple well above 1 is one the market expects to keep earning positive EVA. In India, the market's premium for paints, FMCG and IT majors reflects expectations of sustained, large positive EVA.

Conditions for a meaningful EVA:

  1. ROIC and WACC on the same post-tax, operating basis — never pre-tax ROCE against post-tax WACC.
  2. Surplus cash excluded from IC (its return and cost belong to treasury) unless the analysis is explicitly "all capital".
  3. A small set of documented adjustments: add back acquired-intangible amortisation and cumulative goodwill impairments; treat leases as debt; strip exceptional items; consider capitalising R&D for pharma.
  4. A WACC that reflects the business's risk, not the group's — for multi-segment companies, use segment WACCs.

Limitations: EVA is a single-period measure and penalises long-gestation investments (capital is charged from day one while NOPAT ramps up); it is sensitive to historical-cost accounting (old plants make ROIC look high); and the WACC estimate is uncertain by ±1–2 percentage points.

Illustration

Illustrative paint company (₹ crore), WACC 12.5%:

Year NOPAT Opening IC ROIC Capital charge EVA
FY2023-24 1,400 4,800 29.2% 600 800
FY2024-25 1,460 6,200 23.5% 775 685

NOPAT grew 4.3%, but IC grew 29% (new capacity plus higher receivables as dealer credit was extended to defend share). EVA fell by ₹115 crore. The ROIC is still well above WACC, so the business still creates value — just less per rupee of capital, and the new capital itself is earning below WACC so far (incremental ROIC = 60 ÷ 1,400 = 4.3%).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute EVA and MVA intuition [L1]

A company has opening invested capital of ₹8,000 crore, EBIT (operating) of ₹1,700 crore, tax rate 25.168% and WACC of 11%. Compute NOPAT, ROIC, capital charge and EVA. If EVA is expected to remain constant forever, estimate MVA and enterprise value.

Q2. EVA with adjustments [L2]

Reported EBIT ₹900 crore includes: amortisation of acquired brands ₹60 crore; an exceptional impairment of ₹110 crore shown above EBIT by the company; lease rent ₹0 (all leases under Ind AS 116, lease interest ₹45 crore is in finance cost). Opening IC as reported ₹5,400 crore excludes ₹350 crore of goodwill impaired in an earlier year. Tax 25.168%, WACC 12%. Compute EVA before and after adjustments and explain each adjustment.

💡 Hint: Decide for each item whether it changes NOPAT, IC or both, then apply the capital charge to the adjusted IC.

Q3. Gestation penalty [L3]

A cement company commissions a ₹3,000 crore plant on 1 April 2024 that will reach full NOPAT of ₹420 crore by year 3 (₹90 crore in year 1, ₹260 crore in year 2). WACC is 11.5%. Show the EVA contribution of the plant for three years and propose how a board should evaluate management during the ramp-up without abandoning EVA.
