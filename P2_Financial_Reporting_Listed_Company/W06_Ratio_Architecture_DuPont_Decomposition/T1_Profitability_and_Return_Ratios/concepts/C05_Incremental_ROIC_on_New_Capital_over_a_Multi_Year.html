Key concept

Incremental ROIC on New Capital over a Multi-Year Window

Concept: Incremental ROIC = ΔNOPAT ÷ ΔInvested capital (multi-year window)

Statement

\[ \text{Incremental ROIC}_{t, n} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_t - \text{NOPAT}_{t-n}}{\text{IC}_{t-1} - \text{IC}_{t-n-1}} \]

where:

  • \(n\) = window length in years (typically 3 to 5).
  • \(\text{NOPAT}_t\) = operating profit after tax in year \(t\), on a consistent definition across the window.
  • \(\text{IC}_{t-1}\) = invested capital at the end of year \(t-1\) (i.e., the opening capital of year \(t\)); the one-year lag recognises that capital deployed in a year earns from the following year.
  • The window is chosen so that numerator and denominator cover the same span of capital deployment.

Explanation

Average ROIC mixes old and new capital. An Indian company with plants built in the 1990s at historical cost will show high average ROIC even if every new rupee earns below WACC. Incremental ROIC — also called return on new invested capital (RONIC) in forecasting — isolates the return on capital added. It is the right measure for:

  • judging management's capital allocation track record (capex, acquisitions, working-capital build);
  • setting the RONIC assumption in a DCF's value-driver terminal value;
  • deciding whether a growth plan creates value (RONIC > WACC) or destroys it.

Why multi-year and lagged: single-year changes are dominated by noise — a cyclical upturn can raise NOPAT with no new capital (infinite incremental ROIC), while a capex year can add capital with no profit yet (near-zero incremental ROIC). Three-to-five-year windows with a one-year lag smooth both effects. For long-gestation industries (cement, chemicals) a two-year lag may be justified.

Interpretation cautions:

  • ΔIC ≤ 0 or very small: the ratio is undefined or explosive; report "n.m." and describe the business as self-funding or asset-light.
  • Cyclical NOPAT: choose windows from similar points in the cycle (trough-to-trough), or normalise NOPAT.
  • Acquisitions: include goodwill in IC; otherwise acquisitive growth looks free.
  • Inflation and pricing: price-led NOPAT growth with no capital is "real" value but may not persist; separate volume-led and price-led increments where possible.

Illustration

Illustrative paint company (₹ crore):

FY ending 31 March 2021 2022 2023 2024 2025
NOPAT 760 820 930 1,120 1,150
Closing IC 3,200 3,500 3,900 4,800 6,000

Three-year lagged window ending FY2024-25: \(\Delta\text{NOPAT} = 1{,}150 - 820 = 330\); \(\Delta\text{IC} = \text{IC}_{2024} - \text{IC}_{2021} = 4{,}800 - 3{,}200 = 1{,}600\). Incremental ROIC = 20.6%.

Average ROIC for FY2024-25 on opening IC = 1,150 ÷ 4,800 = 24.0%. The gap shows new capital earning less than the legacy base — but still above a 12% WACC. The ₹1,200 crore added in FY2024-25 has not yet entered the window; next year's figure will reveal whether it earns its keep.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute windows [L1]

Using the illustration's data, compute the two-year and four-year lagged incremental ROIC ending FY2024-25 and compare them with the three-year figure. Comment on the sensitivity to window length.

💡 Hint: Keep the one-year lag between NOPAT and IC endpoints for every window.

Q2. Cyclical distortion [L2]

A cement company's NOPAT (₹ crore) was 1,800 in FY2020-21 (cyclical peak), 1,100 in FY2022-23 (trough) and 1,900 in FY2024-25; closing IC was 14,000 (Mar 2020), 15,500 (Mar 2022) and 19,000 (Mar 2024). Compute peak-to-peak and trough-to-peak incremental ROIC and explain which is the better guide to capital allocation quality.

Q3. Model assumption check [L3]

A DCF you are reviewing assumes NOPAT growth of 14% a year for 10 years with reinvestment of only 25% of NOPAT. Compute the implied RONIC and compare it with the company's five-year historical incremental ROIC of 18%. Draft review comments for the model owner.
