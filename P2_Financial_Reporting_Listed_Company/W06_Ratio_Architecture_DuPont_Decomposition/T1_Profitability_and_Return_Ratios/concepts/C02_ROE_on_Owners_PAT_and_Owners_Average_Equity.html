Key concept

ROE on Owners' PAT and Owners' Average Equity

Concept: ROE = PAT attributable to owners ÷ Average equity attributable to owners

Statement

\[ \text{ROE}_t = \frac{\text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t}{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(E^{\text{owners}}_{t-1} + E^{\text{owners}}_{t}\right)} \]

where:

  • \(\text{PAT}^{\text{owners}}_t\) = profit for the year attributable to owners of the parent (Ind AS 1.81B(a)), less dividends on preference shares classified as equity, if any.
  • \(E^{\text{owners}}\) = equity attributable to owners of the parent = equity share capital + other equity (retained earnings, securities premium, general reserve, OCI reserves, other reserves); excludes non-controlling interests (presented separately within equity under Ind AS 110).
  • \(t-1\) and \(t\) denote the 31 March balance sheet dates bracketing the year.

Explanation

ROE is the return earned for the parent's equity shareholders on the book capital they have provided and retained. Both sides must belong to the same claimant: consolidated PAT includes the NCI share, and consolidated total equity includes NCI, so using either unadjusted with the other overstates or understates ROE.

Why average equity: profit accrues through the year on a capital base that changes with retained earnings, dividends, buybacks and issues. A simple average approximates the year's capital; for large equity events (QIP, rights issue, buyback, large special dividend) use a time-weighted average (topic pitfall). Closing equity understates ROE for growing companies and overstates it after a buyback.

Conditions and exceptions:

  • Negative or very small equity: ROE is not meaningful; report "n.m.".
  • OCI volatility: FVOCI equity reserves move with share prices of strategic holdings; consider ROE on equity excluding OCI reserves for comparability.
  • Treasury shares / ESOP trusts: shares held by an ESOP trust consolidated into the company are deducted from equity; follow the reported presentation.
  • Compulsorily convertible preference shares classified as equity: keep in equity; if presented as financial liability (Ind AS 32), their dividend is a finance cost and they are excluded from equity.
  • Schedule III: the mandated ROE (standalone) is "net profit after taxes − preference dividend ÷ average shareholders' equity" in the ICAI illustration; the company must disclose its definition and explain changes above 25%.

Illustration

Illustrative consolidated data (₹ crore):

Item 31 Mar 2024 31 Mar 2025
Equity attributable to owners 14,800 16,600
Non-controlling interests 1,200 1,450

FY2024-25 PAT: total 3,050, owners 2,800, NCI 250.

  • Correct ROE = 2,800 ÷ 15,700 = 17.8%.
  • Total PAT ÷ total equity = 3,050 ÷ 17,025 = 17.9% (close here, but only by coincidence — if NCI subsidiaries were more or less profitable than the group the gap would widen).
  • Total PAT ÷ owners' equity = 3,050 ÷ 15,700 = 19.4% — overstated mismatch.
  • Owners' PAT ÷ closing owners' equity = 2,800 ÷ 16,600 = 16.9% — understated for a growing company.

Practice Questions

Q1. NCI-correct ROE [L1]

Consolidated data (₹ crore): PAT 1,920 of which NCI 310; owners' equity 9,200 opening and 10,400 closing; NCI 2,100 opening and 2,300 closing. Compute ROE correctly and under the two common mismatched definitions, and explain the direction of each error.

Q2. Buyback year [L2]

A company with opening owners' equity of ₹12,000 crore completes a ₹4,000 crore buyback on 1 May 2024, pays dividends of ₹1,000 crore on 1 August 2024 and earns PAT of ₹2,700 crore evenly through FY2024-25. Compute ROE on closing, simple average and time-weighted average equity, and state which you would report in an equity research note.

💡 Hint: Build the closing equity first, then weight each movement by the months it was outstanding.

Q3. OCI-heavy balance sheet [L2]

A holding-cum-operating company carries an FVOCI reserve of ₹6,000 crore on strategic listed stakes within owners' equity of ₹15,000 crore (both averages). PAT attributable to owners is ₹1,500 crore. Compute ROE with and without the FVOCI reserve, and discuss which better reflects management's operating performance.
