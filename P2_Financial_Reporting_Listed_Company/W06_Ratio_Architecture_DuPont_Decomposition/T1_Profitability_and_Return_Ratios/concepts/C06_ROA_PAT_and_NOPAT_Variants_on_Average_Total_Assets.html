Key concept

ROA: PAT and NOPAT Variants on Average Total Assets

Concept: ROA = PAT ÷ Average total assets (or NOPAT ÷ assets for leverage-neutral view)

Statement

\[ \text{ROA}_{\text{PAT}} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\overline{\text{TA}}}, \qquad \text{ROA}_{\text{NOPAT}} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\overline{\text{TA}}} \;\text{ or }\; \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Finance cost} \times (1 - t)}{\overline{\text{TA}}} \]

where:

  • \(\text{PAT}\) = profit for the year (consolidated total, including NCI, because total assets include assets funded by NCI).
  • \(\overline{\text{TA}}\) = average total assets = \((\text{TA}_{t-1} + \text{TA}_t)/2\).
  • \(\text{NOPAT}\) = \(\text{EBIT}_{\text{op}} \times (1 - t)\); the alternative numerator adds back after-tax interest to PAT (retaining after-tax other income).
  • \(t\) = tax rate (statutory or normalised).

Explanation

ROA measures how much profit the whole asset base generates. The PAT version carries a claimant mismatch: the numerator is after interest (the equity holders' profit), while the denominator includes assets funded by lenders. So two companies with identical operations but different leverage show different ROA — the more leveraged one lower, because its interest has been deducted but the debt-funded assets are still in the base.

The leverage-neutral version fixes this by adding back after-tax interest (or using NOPAT), giving a return on assets independent of financing. It is the "return on assets" that, multiplied by leverage, links to ROE in the Penman-style decomposition covered in topic t2.

Where each is used:

  • Banks and NBFCs: PAT ÷ average total assets is the standard (RBI data, analyst reports), because interest is an operating cost and leverage is a regulated business variable.
  • Industrial companies: NOPAT-based ROA or ROIC is more informative; PAT-based ROA appears in the 3-step DuPont as net margin × asset turnover.
  • Cash-rich companies: total assets include large treasury balances; ROA understates operating productivity — consider operating assets only.

Exceptions:

  • Total assets include ROU assets post-Ind AS 116; lease-heavy companies show lower ROA than owners of their premises after the transition.
  • Goodwill and revaluation (Ind AS 16 revaluation model, Ind AS 101 deemed cost) inflate the base.
  • Large non-operating assets (investment property, strategic stakes) belong outside an operating ROA.

Illustration

Two illustrative companies with identical operations (₹ crore):

Item Company L (levered) Company U (unlevered)
EBIT 1,000 1,000
Finance cost 200 0
PBT 800 1,000
Tax at 25.168% 201 252
PAT 599 748
Average total assets 6,000 6,000
ROA (PAT) 10.0% 12.5%
PAT + interest × (1 − t) 599 + 150 = 749 748
ROA (leverage-neutral) 12.5% 12.5%

PAT-based ROA penalises L for its financing choice; the leverage-neutral version shows the operations are equally productive. Company L's ROE may still be higher if its equity base is smaller — the subject of DuPont analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two ROA variants [L1]

A company reports PAT ₹640 crore, finance cost ₹180 crore, other income ₹60 crore, tax rate 25.168%; total assets ₹7,200 crore opening and ₹8,000 crore closing. Compute PAT-based ROA, leverage-neutral ROA (PAT + after-tax interest) and NOPAT-based ROA (excluding other income).

Q2. Bank vs manufacturer [L2]

Explain why a ROA of 1.8% is considered strong for an Indian private bank while 1.8% would be poor for a paint company. Refer to leverage, the nature of interest expense and regulatory capital.

💡 Hint: Link ROA to ROE through the equity multiplier for each business.

Q3. Lease transition effect [L2]

A retailer with total assets of ₹4,000 crore recognises ROU assets of ₹2,500 crore on adopting Ind AS 116. Its EBIT rises by ₹90 crore and finance cost by ₹220 crore. Assuming a 25.168% tax rate and no other changes, compute PAT-based and leverage-neutral ROA before and after the transition, and comment on comparability with a peer that owns its stores.
