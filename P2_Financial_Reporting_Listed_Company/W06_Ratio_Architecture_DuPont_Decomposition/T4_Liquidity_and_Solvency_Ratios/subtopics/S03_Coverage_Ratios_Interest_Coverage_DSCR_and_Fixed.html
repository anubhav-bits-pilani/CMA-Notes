Subtopic note

Coverage Ratios: Interest Coverage, DSCR and Fixed-Charge Cover

Scope: Coverage: interest coverage (EBIT and EBITDA basis), DSCR, fixed-charge coverage incl. lease payments

Why it matters

Leverage ratios tell you the stock of obligations; coverage ratios tell you whether the flow of earnings or cash can service them. Every Indian term-loan sanction letter carries a minimum DSCR (commonly 1.20–1.50x) and many carry an interest coverage floor. Rating agencies weight interest coverage and DSCR heavily in their financial-risk assessment. Schedule III requires DSCR disclosure with 25% variance explanations. In a 3-statement model the coverage tests are the covenant checks on the debt schedule; in FP&A they decide how much incremental capex can be debt-funded without triggering a breach.

Core notes

Interest coverage — EBIT basis

\[ \text{Interest coverage}_{\text{EBIT}} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Finance costs}} \]

EBIT is earnings before finance costs and tax. Decisions:

  • Other income: include or exclude? For a net-cash company, treasury income in other income inflates EBIT relative to a tiny finance cost. Lenders generally include recurring other income; analysts often exclude it to focus on operating cover. State the definition (cross-link: t2 interest-burden pitfall).
  • Exceptional items: exclude from EBIT for an underlying ratio.
  • Finance costs: the Schedule III line includes interest on borrowings, interest on lease liabilities under Ind AS 116, unwinding of discount on provisions and other borrowing costs, and certain exchange differences regarded as an adjustment to interest cost. Capitalised interest (Ind AS 23) is not in the P&L finance cost but is a real cash outflow — rating agencies add it back to the denominator.

Interest coverage — EBITDA basis

\[ \text{Interest coverage}_{\text{EBITDA}} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Finance costs}} \]

EBITDA cover is closer to cash cover (D&A is non-cash) and is preferred for capital-intensive businesses with heavy depreciation (cement, telecom, power). It overstates cover where maintenance capex is large, because depreciation is a proxy for capital that must be reinvested. A useful intermediate is (EBITDA − maintenance capex) ÷ interest.

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

Lender (Indian bank) basis:

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{D\&A} + \text{Interest on term debt} + \text{Other non-cash charges}}{\text{Interest on term debt} + \text{Principal repaid in the year}} \]

  • The numerator is cash accruals available for debt service; interest is added back because it appears in the denominator. Banks compute average DSCR over the loan tenor in project appraisal and minimum DSCR for each year.
  • Some banks include working-capital interest in both numerator and denominator; others exclude it as being serviced from the operating cycle. Principal excludes prepayments funded by refinancing.
  • Cash-flow (analyst/project finance) basis: CFADS ÷ (interest + scheduled principal), where CFADS = EBITDA − tax paid − change in working capital − maintenance capex. This is the Week 21 project-finance definition.

Fixed-charge coverage including lease payments

\[ \text{FCCR} = \frac{\text{EBIT} + \text{Lease charges}}{\text{Finance costs} + \text{Lease charges}} \]

Pre-Ind AS 116 this was the way to put lessee and owner companies on a common footing: add back rent to EBIT and add rent to fixed charges. Post-116, rent is replaced by depreciation on the ROU asset (above EBIT) and interest on the lease liability (in finance costs); the principal portion of lease payments sits in financing cash flow. A post-116 fixed-charge cover therefore uses:

\[ \text{FCCR}_{\text{post-116}} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Finance costs (incl. lease interest)} + \text{Lease principal repaid}} \]

or, for a covenant definition, (EBITDA − capex − tax) ÷ (interest + scheduled principal + lease payments). Short-term and low-value leases expensed under the Ind AS 116 exemption and variable lease payments still sit in operating expenses and should be added to both sides for full comparability.

Worked illustration

Retail chain FY2024-25 (₹ crore): EBITDA (post-116) 600; D&A 340 (of which ROU depreciation 210); other income 20; finance costs 150 (of which lease interest 95); PAT 100; lease principal repaid 190; term-loan principal repaid 60; variable rent expensed 30.

Ratio Computation Result
EBIT 600 − 340 260
Interest cover (EBIT, ex-other income) 260 ÷ 150 1.73x
Interest cover (EBITDA) 600 ÷ 150 4.00x
DSCR (bank basis, term debt only) (100 + 340 + 55) ÷ (55 + 60) 4.30x
FCCR post-116 (600 + 30) ÷ (150 + 190 + 30) 1.70x

The DSCR computed on term debt alone looks comfortable, but once lease obligations are treated as debt service the cover falls to 1.70x — the economically relevant number for a retailer.

Excel implementation

ICR_EBIT    = (EBITDA - DA + IF(Incl_OI, Other_Income, 0)) / Finance_Cost
DSCR_Bank   = (PAT + DA + TermInt + NonCash) / (TermInt + Principal)
FCCR_116    = (EBITDA + VarRent) / (Finance_Cost + Lease_Principal + VarRent)
Covenant_OK = IF(DSCR_Bank >= Min_DSCR, "PASS", "BREACH")

In a model, flag the minimum year DSCR with =MIN(DSCR_Row) and conditionally format any year below the covenant.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bullet repayments make DSCR spike downward in the maturity year; lenders usually assume refinancing, so report DSCR both with and without the bullet.
  • Moratorium years: DSCR is infinite or meaningless during principal moratorium; use interest cover.
  • Negative EBIT: coverage below zero should be shown as "NM" with commentary, not ranked.
  • Capitalised interest during a capex cycle understates P&L finance cost and flatters coverage — add back from the Ind AS 23 disclosure.
  • Foreign exchange on borrowings: the portion of exchange differences treated as borrowing cost under Ind AS 23 may swing finance costs; strip it for an underlying ratio.
  • Holding companies: standalone DSCR may depend on dividends from subsidiaries; check upstreaming restrictions.

Key takeaways

  • EBIT cover measures earnings cover; EBITDA cover approximates cash cover but ignores reinvestment needs.
  • Bank DSCR = cash accruals plus interest over interest plus principal; specify which debt is in scope.
  • Add back capitalised interest to finance costs when comparing companies in a capex cycle.
  • Post-Ind AS 116, lease principal must be added to debt service to compare lessees with owners.
  • Report minimum-year DSCR, not only average, and show "NM" for negative or moratorium years.

Practice Questions

Q1. Interest cover with other income [L1]

A net-cash FMCG company reports EBITDA ₹2,800 crore, D&A ₹300 crore, other income (treasury) ₹450 crore and finance costs ₹40 crore (₹30 crore lease interest). Compute EBIT-basis interest cover with and without other income and EBITDA-basis cover, and state which you would present in a peer table and why.

Q2. Bank DSCR over a loan tenor [L2]

A ₹200 crore term loan at 9.5% is repayable in eight equal annual instalments from FY2025-26. Projected PAT (₹ crore) is 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 58, 62, 66 and depreciation is constant at ₹28 crore. Interest is on opening balances. Set out the DSCR computation for each year and identify the minimum and average DSCR.

💡 Hint: Build the debt schedule first; interest falls as principal is repaid.

Q3. Fixed-charge cover for two retailers [L2]

Retailer A owns its stores; Retailer B leases all of them. Both have EBITDA of ₹500 crore on a pre-116 basis (rent of B ₹180 crore already deducted). Post-116, B's EBITDA is ₹680 crore, lease interest ₹70 crore, ROU depreciation ₹130 crore, lease principal ₹110 crore. A has term-loan interest ₹40 crore and principal ₹60 crore; B has no other debt. Compute a comparable fixed-charge coverage for both.

Q4. Covenant test in a model [L3]

Specify an Excel covenant-test block for a 5-year forecast with: minimum DSCR 1.30x, maximum net debt/EBITDA 3.0x and minimum interest cover 3.0x. Describe inputs, formulas, the flag logic, and how you would show the headroom (in ₹ EBITDA) before each covenant is breached.

Q5. Capitalised interest judgement [L2]

A cement company capitalised ₹180 crore of interest on a new plant in FY2024-25, while P&L finance costs were ₹90 crore and EBIT ₹1,100 crore. Compute interest cover with and without capitalised interest and explain which a rating agency would use.
