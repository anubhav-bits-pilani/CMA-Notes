Subtopic note

Ind AS 116 Effects on Leverage, Coverage and Covenant Definitions

Scope: Ind AS 116 impact on leverage and coverage; covenant definitions (frozen GAAP vs current GAAP)

Why it matters

Ind AS 116 (effective for Indian companies from 1 April 2019) moved almost all lessee leases onto the balance sheet. For retailers, airlines, quick-service restaurants, hospitals and telecom tower users, the change added lease liabilities equal to several times reported borrowings and lifted EBITDA by the amount of rent. Every leverage and coverage ratio shifted — some up, some down — without any change in economics. Analysts comparing FY2018-19 with FY2019-20 onward, comparing Ind AS filers with US GAAP filers (where operating lease cost remains a single straight-line expense in the income statement), or testing a loan covenant drafted before 2019, must know exactly which direction each ratio moves and how the loan agreement defines its terms.

Core notes

Mechanics recap

For a lessee (Ind AS 116.22–26): at commencement, recognise a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a lease liability measured at the present value of unpaid lease payments discounted at the rate implicit in the lease or, if not readily determinable, the lessee's incremental borrowing rate. Subsequently: depreciation on the ROU asset (straight line over the lease term, typically) and interest on the liability (effective interest, front-loaded). Exemptions: short-term leases (12 months or less) and low-value assets may be expensed (Ind AS 116.5). Variable payments not linked to an index or rate (e.g. revenue-share rent in malls) stay in operating expenses.

Cash flow statement: principal portion of lease payments → financing activities; interest portion → financing or operating per the entity's interest policy (Ind AS 7); short-term, low-value and variable payments → operating.

Direction of impact on each ratio

Ratio Effect of Ind AS 116 Reason
EBITDA Up Rent removed from opex; replaced by depreciation and interest below EBITDA
EBIT Slightly up Rent replaced by ROU depreciation (lower than rent since interest is excluded)
PAT (early years) Down Front-loaded interest makes depreciation + interest exceed rent early in the lease
Debt / D/E Up (if leases included) Lease liability added to debt
Net debt/EBITDA Ambiguous Both numerator and denominator rise; for lease-heavy firms usually up
Interest coverage (EBIT) Usually down Finance cost rises by lease interest proportionally more than EBIT
EBITDA/interest Ambiguous EBITDA rises by rent, interest rises by lease interest only
Current ratio Down Current portion of lease liability added to CL; ROU is non-current
Total asset turnover / ROA Down ROU assets added to total assets
Operating cash flow Up Principal moves to financing

Worked illustration

A retailer pre-116: EBITDA ₹300 crore, rent ₹150 crore already deducted, D&A ₹60 crore, finance cost ₹20 crore, borrowings ₹200 crore. Post-116: lease liability ₹900 crore, ROU depreciation ₹110 crore, lease interest ₹80 crore.

Metric Pre-116 Post-116
EBITDA 300 450
EBIT 240 280
Finance cost 20 100
Debt 200 1,100
Debt/EBITDA 0.67x 2.44x
EBIT/interest 12.0x 2.8x

Same business; the leverage ratio quadruples and coverage collapses. This is the pitfall in the topic: excluding lease liabilities from debt while using post-116 EBITDA (₹200 ÷ ₹450 = 0.44x) understates leverage even below the pre-116 figure.

Consistent pairing rule

Pick one basis and apply both sides:

  • Post-116 (lease-inclusive): debt includes lease liabilities; EBITDA post-116; finance cost includes lease interest. Rating agencies in India generally follow this, sometimes with sector-specific adjustments.
  • Pre-116 (lease-exclusive) / "EBITDAR-less-rent": debt excludes leases; EBITDA after deducting cash rent (EBITDA − lease principal − lease interest, approximating rent). Management guidance of retailers and QSR operators usually quotes "pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA" for store economics.
  • Capitalised-rent (legacy): debt + 8 × annual rent (the old agency convention) ÷ EBITDAR.

Covenant definitions: frozen GAAP vs current GAAP

A loan agreement defines "Indebtedness", "EBITDA", "Finance Costs" and "Net Worth" by reference to accounting standards. Two drafting approaches:

  • Frozen GAAP: ratios are computed under accounting principles as at the date of the agreement. A loan signed in 2017 under Ind AS 17 continues to be tested on an operating-lease basis; the borrower must provide a reconciliation from current financial statements back to the frozen basis. Protects both parties from accounting-driven breaches but imposes a dual-reporting burden.
  • Current (floating) GAAP: ratios use standards as amended from time to time. Ind AS 116 adoption could trigger an artificial breach of a debt/EBITDA or D/E covenant. Well-drafted agreements contain an "accounting change" clause requiring the parties to negotiate in good faith to restore the original economic intent, often with an explicit exclusion such as "Indebtedness shall exclude lease liabilities arising from leases that would have been operating leases under Ind AS 17".

Practical implications for an FP&A or treasury team:

  1. Maintain a covenant workbook that reproduces each definition literally — including carve-outs for leases, exceptional items, and other income.
  2. Reconcile reported ratios to covenant ratios each quarter; auditors will review the compliance certificate when assessing classification under Ind AS 1 (see s05).
  3. In a 3-statement model, compute both the reported ratio and the covenant ratio; the model's breach flag must use the covenant ratio.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Lease term judgement (renewal options reasonably certain) changes the liability size — two retailers with similar store portfolios can report very different lease liabilities.
  • Incremental borrowing rate differences: a higher IBR lowers the liability and raises the interest share.
  • Rent concessions and lease modifications can produce gains in P&L that distort EBITDA in a single year.
  • Revenue-share leases (variable rent) remain off balance sheet; a mall-anchored retailer may look less levered than a fixed-rent peer.
  • Lessor side: Ind AS 116 lessor accounting is largely unchanged (finance vs operating lease), so asymmetry arises in intra-group leases on consolidation.
  • Sale and leaseback transactions can reduce reported borrowings while creating lease liabilities — check whether the covenant definition captures them.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 116 raises EBITDA and debt, lowers EBIT interest cover and the current ratio, and depresses asset turnover.
  • Always pair lease-inclusive debt with post-116 EBITDA, or lease-exclusive debt with pre-116 EBITDA.
  • Frozen GAAP tests covenants under the standards at signing; current GAAP floats and needs an accounting-change clause.
  • Keep a covenant workbook that reconciles reported ratios to the literal covenant definitions every quarter.
  • Lease-term, IBR and variable-rent choices create comparability gaps even among Ind AS filers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ratio directions under Ind AS 116 [L1]

An airline adopts a new fleet of leased aircraft. Without computing, state the direction of change (up, down, ambiguous) in EBITDA, EBIT interest cover, net debt/EBITDA, current ratio, ROA and operating cash flow, with a one-line reason for each.

Q2. Restating to a pre-116 basis [L2]

A QSR chain reports (₹ crore) post-116 EBITDA 820, finance costs 210 (lease interest 170), borrowings 150, lease liabilities 1,900, cash 260, lease payments in the cash flow statement 420 (principal 250, interest 170), and variable rent expensed 40. Compute net debt/EBITDA and EBIT interest cover (D&A 450) on both post-116 and pre-116 bases.

💡 Hint: Pre-116 EBITDA deducts total cash lease payments that were capitalised.

Q3. Frozen GAAP covenant reconciliation [L3]

A ₹500 crore term loan signed in March 2018 tests "Total Debt ÷ EBITDA ≤ 2.5x" under frozen GAAP. FY2024-25 reported figures: borrowings 640, lease liabilities 380, EBITDA (post-116) 520, of which ₹110 crore relates to lease rentals that would have been operating lease expense under Ind AS 17. Prepare the compliance-certificate reconciliation and conclude on compliance under both frozen and current GAAP readings.

Q4. Drafting an accounting-change clause [L2]

You are the borrower's treasury lead negotiating a new facility in 2025. Draft the key points of an accounting-change clause and a lease carve-out definition that would protect the company from a future change in lease or revenue accounting.

Q5. Comparability across GAAPs [L2]

An Indian retailer (Ind AS) is benchmarked against a US-listed peer (US GAAP, ASC 842). Explain how operating lease presentation differs in the US peer's income statement and which adjustments you would make to compare EBITDA margin and net debt/EBITDA.
