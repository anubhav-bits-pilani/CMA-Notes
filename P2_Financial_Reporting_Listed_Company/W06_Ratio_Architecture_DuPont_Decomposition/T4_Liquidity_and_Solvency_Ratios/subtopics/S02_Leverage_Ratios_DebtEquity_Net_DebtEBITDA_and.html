Subtopic note

Leverage Ratios: Debt/Equity, Net Debt/EBITDA and TOL/TNW

Scope: Leverage: debt/equity, net debt/EBITDA, TOL/TNW (bank appraisal metric)

Why it matters

Leverage ratios answer "how much of the business is funded by creditors, and how quickly could earnings repay them?" Three audiences use three different ratios. Equity analysts and rating agencies anchor on net debt/EBITDA because it scales debt to cash-generating capacity and is the language of loan covenants and LBO models. Statutory reporting under Schedule III requires the debt-equity ratio, with explanations for variances above 25%. Indian bank credit appraisal notes still lead with TOL/TNW (total outside liabilities to tangible net worth), a balance-sheet ratio that captures trade creditors and other non-debt obligations the other two ignore. An FP&A analyst preparing a board pack or a lender presentation must be fluent in all three and know which definitions the counterparty uses.

Core notes

Debt/equity

\[ \text{Debt/Equity} = \frac{\text{Total borrowings (non-current + current, incl. CMLTD)}}{\text{Total equity}} \]

Decisions to document:

  • Debt: long-term borrowings, short-term borrowings, CMLTD, and — under the post-Ind AS 116 convention — lease liabilities. Many companies' Schedule III disclosures exclude lease liabilities; analysts usually include them. Include preference shares classified as financial liabilities under Ind AS 32 (redeemable preference shares), and compound instrument liability components.
  • Equity: equity attributable to owners or total equity including NCI. For a consolidated group, total equity is consistent with consolidated debt (which includes subsidiaries' borrowings). State the choice.
  • Book vs market: book D/E is the reporting and covenant metric; market D/E (debt ÷ market capitalisation) is the WACC-weighting metric (Week 15).

A net debt/equity (gearing) variant deducts cash and liquid investments from debt; for cash-rich companies it turns negative.

Net debt/EBITDA

\[ \text{Net debt/EBITDA} = \frac{\text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities} - \text{Cash} - \text{Liquid investments}}{\text{EBITDA}} \]

  • Numerator at the reporting date (closing) — lenders care about debt outstanding now.
  • Denominator trailing twelve months (LTM) EBITDA. Use the normalised EBITDA from Week 5: exclude one-offs, and be consistent about other income (usually excluded) and Ind AS 116 (post-116 EBITDA pairs with debt including leases; pre-116 EBITDA pairs with debt excluding leases).
  • Interpretation bands (broad, sector-dependent): below 1x conservative; 1–3x moderate; above 3.5–4x stretched for most industrials; infrastructure and utilities tolerate 5–6x because of contracted cash flows.
  • Net-cash companies produce negative ratios. A ratio of −2.0x is not "better" than −0.5x in any meaningful ranking — it describes surplus cash, which is an allocation question (buybacks, dividends, M&A), not a solvency strength scale. Report net-cash companies separately (see topic pitfalls).

TOL/TNW — the bank appraisal metric

\[ \text{TOL/TNW} = \frac{\text{Total outside liabilities}}{\text{Tangible net worth}} \]

  • Total outside liabilities (TOL) = all liabilities other than owners' funds: borrowings (term and working capital), trade payables, other financial liabilities, provisions, other current/non-current liabilities, and usually deferred tax liability. Banks may treat quasi-equity (unsecured promoter loans subordinated to bank debt) as part of net worth rather than TOL — a frequent negotiation point in credit appraisal.
  • Tangible net worth (TNW) = equity share capital + reserves (free reserves; revaluation reserves are typically excluded) − intangible assets (goodwill, brands, software) − deferred revenue expenditure, miscellaneous expenditure not written off, and accumulated losses. Some banks also deduct investments in and advances to group companies.
  • Norms: Indian banks commonly look for TOL/TNW below 3x for manufacturing and up to 4–5x for trading businesses; specific thresholds are bank-policy and sector-dependent. Because TOL includes trade payables, it captures the risk of creditor-funded growth that D/E misses.

Worked illustration

Specialty chemicals company, 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): equity share capital 50, other equity 650 (of which revaluation reserve 40); goodwill and intangibles 110; term loans 260 (incl. CMLTD 60); working-capital loans 140; lease liabilities 30; trade payables 220; other liabilities and provisions 90; DTL 30. Cash and liquid funds 70. LTM EBITDA (post-116, normalised) 240.

Ratio Computation Result
D/E (excl. leases) (260 + 140) ÷ 700 0.57x
D/E (incl. leases) 430 ÷ 700 0.61x
Net debt/EBITDA (430 − 70) ÷ 240 1.50x
TNW 700 − 40 − 110 550
TOL 430 + 220 + 90 + 30 770
TOL/TNW 770 ÷ 550 1.40x

Note the TOL/TNW is materially worse than D/E would suggest because trade payables and intangibles matter to a bank but not to the debt-equity computation.

Link to the leverage arithmetic in DuPont

The equity multiplier (Assets ÷ Equity) used in t2 equals 1 + Total liabilities ÷ Equity — conceptually closer to TOL/Equity than to D/E. A company can have low D/E and a high equity multiplier if it is funded by suppliers and customer advances (FMCG, jewellers).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cash trapped or earmarked: exclude restricted cash, margin money and cash in subsidiaries subject to exchange controls from net debt.
  • Seasonality: year-end debt for a festive-season business may be unrepresentative; use average or peak-quarter debt (half-yearly balance sheets under Reg 33) as a sensitivity.
  • Acquisitions mid-year: closing debt includes acquisition funding while LTM EBITDA includes only a part year of the target — compute pro forma LTM EBITDA.
  • Perpetual bonds / hybrids: classification as equity under Ind AS 32 versus agency treatment as part-debt.
  • Guarantees and off-balance-sheet obligations: corporate guarantees for group borrowings, supplier finance and bills discounted are excluded from reported debt; rating agencies may add them back.
  • Negative TNW: companies with large accumulated losses or goodwill can have negative TNW; the ratio becomes meaningless and should be reported as "NM".

Key takeaways

  • D/E is the Schedule III and covenant balance-sheet ratio; define debt (leases, preference shares) explicitly.
  • Net debt/EBITDA scales closing net debt to LTM normalised EBITDA; match lease treatment in both terms.
  • Negative net debt/EBITDA identifies net-cash companies — rank them separately, not as "best".
  • TOL/TNW captures all outside liabilities against tangible equity and is the core Indian bank appraisal metric.
  • Quasi-equity, revaluation reserves and intangibles drive most TOL/TNW disagreements between borrower and bank.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three leverage ratios from one balance sheet [L1]

From the following (₹ crore) compute D/E (with and without leases), net debt/EBITDA and TOL/TNW: share capital 100; other equity 900 (including revaluation reserve 120); goodwill 80; software 20; NCD 400; term loan 300 (CMLTD 75); cash credit 150; lease liabilities 120; trade payables 380; provisions 60; other liabilities 140; DTL 50; cash 90; liquid mutual funds 110; LTM EBITDA (post-116) 520.

Q2. Quasi-equity negotiation [L2]

A promoter has lent ₹60 crore unsecured to the company. TNW excluding this loan is ₹200 crore and TOL including it is ₹720 crore. The bank's norm is TOL/TNW not above 3.0x. Compute TOL/TNW with and without treating the loan as quasi-equity and list the conditions a bank would typically require (subordination, no interest servicing, etc.) before allowing the treatment.

💡 Hint: Moving the loan changes both the numerator and the denominator.

Q3. Net-cash peer ranking [L2]

Five paint companies have net debt/EBITDA of −1.8x, −0.6x, 0.4x, 1.2x and 2.9x. A junior analyst ranks them in ascending order as "least to most levered". Critique the ranking and propose an alternative presentation that separates capital-allocation and solvency questions.

Q4. Pro forma leverage after acquisition [L3]

On 1 January 2025 a company borrows ₹1,200 crore to acquire a target with annual EBITDA of ₹240 crore. FY2024-25 reported EBITDA (including the target for three months) is ₹900 crore and closing net debt ₹2,700 crore. Compute reported and pro forma net debt/EBITDA and explain which a lender's covenant would normally use.

Q5. Interview — D/E versus TOL/TNW [L1]

Explain to a CFO why a jewellery retailer with D/E of 0.3x was flagged by its bank for a TOL/TNW of 4.2x.
