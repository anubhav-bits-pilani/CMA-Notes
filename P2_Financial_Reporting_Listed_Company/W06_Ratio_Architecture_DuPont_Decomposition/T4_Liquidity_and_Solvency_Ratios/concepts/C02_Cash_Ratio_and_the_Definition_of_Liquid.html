Key concept

Cash Ratio and the Definition of Liquid Investments

Concept: Cash ratio = (Cash + Cash equivalents + Liquid investments) ÷ CL

Statement

\[ \text{Cash ratio} = \frac{C + CE + LI}{CL} \]

where \(C\) = cash on hand and balances with banks in current accounts, \(CE\) = cash equivalents as defined in Ind AS 7.6–7.7 (short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible to known amounts of cash with insignificant risk of value change — normally maturity of three months or less from the date of acquisition), \(LI\) = other liquid investments that can be converted to cash within days without material loss (current investments in liquid/overnight/money-market mutual funds, unencumbered bank deposits with residual maturity under twelve months shown in "Other bank balances"), and \(CL\) = total current liabilities at the reporting date.

Explanation

The cash ratio is the most conservative liquidity measure: it assumes neither inventory nor receivables can be realised and asks whether cash-like resources alone meet current obligations. It is the right lens in stress scenarios — a sudden collapse in collections, a channel destocking, or a counterparty run on a company with large short-term borrowings or commercial paper outstanding.

What to exclude:

  • Earmarked balances: unpaid dividend accounts, balances held as margin money against bank guarantees or letters of credit, deposits under lien, escrow for buyback or open offers, and amounts held in trust (e.g. customer deposits in a regulated business).
  • Illiquid or risky investments: equity shares held as strategic investments, credit-risk or long-duration debt funds, inter-corporate deposits to group companies.
  • Trapped cash: balances in jurisdictions with exchange controls or held in subsidiaries where dividends are restricted.

Interpretation: there is no universal benchmark. Cash-rich consumer companies (Asian Paints, Pidilite) and IT majors carry high cash ratios; capital-goods and infrastructure companies run low cash ratios backed by committed undrawn limits. Rating agencies therefore pair cash with unutilised sanctioned lines when grading liquidity (Strong/Adequate/Stretched/Poor). A very high cash ratio also raises a capital-allocation question — excess cash depresses ROE and ROCE (t1 pitfall) — so it is not an unqualified positive.

Illustration

IT services company, 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): cash and bank 1,200 (including an unpaid dividend account of 15); deposits with original maturity under three months 800; liquid mutual funds 2,500; bank deposits with residual maturity of seven months 1,500 (of which ₹200 crore is margin money for guarantees); investment in a credit-risk fund 300. Current liabilities 4,000.

  • Qualifying cash and liquid resources = (1,200 − 15) + 800 + 2,500 + (1,500 − 200) = 5,785
  • Cash ratio = 5,785 ÷ 4,000 = 1.45x

Had the analyst included the unpaid dividend balance, the lien-marked deposits and the credit-risk fund, the numerator would be 6,300 and the ratio 1.58x — a material overstatement in a stress test.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the balances [L1]

Classify each of the following as included or excluded from the cash ratio numerator, with a reason: (a) 91-day treasury bill bought 20 days before year-end, (b) 12-month FD with five months remaining, (c) margin money FD against an import LC, (d) overnight fund units, (e) unpaid dividend account, (f) ICD to a group company repayable on demand, (g) cash in a subsidiary in a country with repatriation limits.

Q2. Cash ratio with undrawn lines [L2]

A capital-goods company has cash and liquid investments of ₹350 crore, current liabilities ₹2,100 crore and undrawn committed working-capital limits of ₹600 crore. Compute the cash ratio and an "available liquidity" ratio including undrawn lines, and explain how a rating agency would view the difference.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the undrawn lines are committed or callable at the bank's discretion.

Q3. Excess cash judgement [L2]

A paint company's cash ratio is 1.9x versus a peer median of 0.4x. Discuss two implications for ROE/ROCE analysis and one for valuation.
