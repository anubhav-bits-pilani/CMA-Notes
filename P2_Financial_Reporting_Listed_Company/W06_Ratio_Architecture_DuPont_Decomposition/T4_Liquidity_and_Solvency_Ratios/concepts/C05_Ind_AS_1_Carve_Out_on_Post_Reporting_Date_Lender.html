Key concept

Ind AS 1 Carve-Out on Post-Reporting-Date Lender Waivers

Concept: Ind AS 1 carve-out: lender waiver after reporting date but before approval of FS avoids current classification on breach

Statement

Under Ind AS 1.74, where there is a breach of a material provision of a long-term loan arrangement on or before the end of the reporting period with the effect that the liability becomes payable on demand on the reporting date, the entity does not classify the liability as current if the lender agreed, after the reporting period and before the approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach.

Timeline condition (with \(T_0\) = reporting date, \(T_W\) = waiver date, \(T_A\) = date of board approval of the financial statements for issue):

\[ T_0 < T_W \le T_A \;\Rightarrow\; \text{non-current (Ind AS)}, \qquad \text{current (IAS 1)} \]

If \(T_W \le T_0\), the liability is non-current under both frameworks (provided the waiver removes the demand right for twelve months); if \(T_W > T_A\), it is current under both.

Explanation

IAS 1.74 treats a waiver obtained after the reporting date as a non-adjusting event: at the reporting date the entity had no right to defer settlement, so the liability is current, with disclosure of the subsequent waiver. The ICAI and MCA carved this out for India because Indian lenders' credit committees routinely process waivers after the financial year-end, and mechanical current classification would have shown widespread, artificial working-capital deficits, triggering cross-defaults and qualified going-concern conclusions. The carve-out is explained in the "Comparison with IAS 1" appendix to Ind AS 1.

Conditions to verify:

  • The waiver must be from the lender (or debenture trustee with requisite holder approval), unconditional or with conditions within the entity's control, and cover the specific breach.
  • It must remove the right to demand repayment as a consequence of the breach — a mere extension of time to submit documents may not suffice.
  • Ind AS 1.75 grace periods: a grace period agreed by the reporting date that ends at least twelve months after it also supports non-current classification.
  • Disclosure: the breach, the waiver and its date; liquidity risk under Ind AS 107; going-concern considerations.

The IASB's 2020 and 2022 amendments to IAS 1 (right to defer must exist at the reporting date and be affected only by covenants tested on or before that date; disclosures for covenant-linked non-current liabilities) widen the IFRS/Ind AS gap further unless and until MCA notifies equivalent changes — check status at each reporting date.

Illustration

A listed company breaches its interest cover covenant on a ₹500 crore term loan at 31 March 2025 (₹100 crore due within twelve months). The bank's waiver letter is dated 28 April 2025; the board approves the financial statements on 14 May 2025. Current assets ₹1,200 crore; other current liabilities ₹800 crore.

Framework Classification of ₹500 crore Current ratio
Ind AS (carve-out applies) ₹100 crore current, ₹400 crore non-current 1,200 ÷ 900 = 1.33x
IFRS (IAS 1) Entire ₹500 crore current 1,200 ÷ 1,300 = 0.92x

Had the waiver been dated 20 May 2025, Ind AS would also require current classification.

Practice Questions

Q1. Date-based classification [L1]

For a breach at 31 March 2025 and board approval on 22 May 2025, classify the loan under Ind AS and IFRS for waiver dates of 15 March, 10 April, 22 May and 30 May 2025.

Q2. Conditional waiver [L2]

A bank's waiver dated 5 May 2025 (board approval 18 May 2025) is "subject to the promoter infusing ₹75 crore of equity by 30 June 2025". Discuss whether the carve-out applies and what additional evidence the auditor should obtain.

💡 Hint: Consider whether fulfilment of the condition is within the entity's control.

Q3. Comparability for an analyst [L2]

You are comparing an Indian borrower (Ind AS) with a Singapore-listed peer (IFRS), both of which breached covenants and obtained waivers in April. Explain how their current ratios and reported liquidity positions differ and what restatement you would make for a like-for-like comparison.
