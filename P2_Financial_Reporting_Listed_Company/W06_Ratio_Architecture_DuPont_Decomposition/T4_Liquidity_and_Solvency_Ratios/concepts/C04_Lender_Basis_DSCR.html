Key concept

Lender-Basis DSCR

Concept: DSCR (lender basis) = (PAT + D&A + Interest + other non-cash) ÷ (Interest + Principal repayments)

Statement

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{PAT + DA + I + NC}{I + P} \]

where \(PAT\) = profit after tax for the period, \(DA\) = depreciation and amortisation, \(I\) = interest on the debt in scope (term loans, NCDs; and lease interest where lease payments are treated as debt service), \(NC\) = other non-cash charges added back (e.g., loss on sale of PPE, impairment, unrealised exchange loss, provision for diminution, share-based payment expense), and \(P\) = scheduled principal repayments falling due in the period (term-loan instalments, NCD redemptions, lease principal where in scope). Revolving working-capital borrowings are usually excluded from \(P\).

Explanation

The numerator is "cash accruals plus interest" — the cash generated before servicing debt. Interest is added back because it is part of the denominator; PAT already has interest deducted. The ratio answers whether one year's cash accruals can meet one year's debt obligations. A DSCR of 1.0x means every rupee of accrual goes to lenders; banks in India typically require a minimum DSCR of 1.20–1.25x and an average DSCR over the tenor of 1.30–1.50x for term loans, with sector variations.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Scope of debt: specify which loans are included. Indian bank appraisal usually covers term debt only; Schedule III (ICAI Guidance Note) DSCR includes lease payments in debt service.
  • Tax: the lender formula uses PAT, so tax is already deducted; the cash-flow (project finance) variant uses CFADS = EBITDA − cash tax − ΔNWC − maintenance capex, which is more rigorous because it charges working capital and capex.
  • Non-cash income (e.g., fair value gains, deferred tax credit) should be deducted, not just non-cash charges added.
  • Bullets and refinancing: a bullet repayment refinanced from new debt crushes DSCR in one year; covenants often define principal as "scheduled amortisation excluding amounts refinanced".
  • Average vs minimum: average DSCR over the tenor hides weak years; lenders test each year and look at the minimum.

Illustration

Auto-component maker FY2024-25 (₹ crore): PAT 120, D&A 85, interest on term loans 40, interest on cash credit 15, loss on sale of PPE 5, unrealised FX gain 8, term-loan principal due 110, lease interest 6, lease principal 14.

Bank basis (term debt only, working-capital interest excluded):

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{120 + 85 + 40 + 5 - 8}{40 + 110} = \frac{242}{150} = 1.61\text{x} \]

Schedule III style (including lease payments):

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{120 + 85 + 40 + 6 + 5 - 8}{40 + 6 + 110 + 14} = \frac{248}{170} = 1.46\text{x} \]

The definitional choice moves the ratio by 0.15x — enough to straddle a 1.50x covenant.

Practice Questions

Q1. Year-by-year DSCR [L2]

A ₹150 crore term loan at 10% is repayable in five equal annual instalments starting FY2025-26, with interest on opening balances. Projected PAT is ₹22, 28, 33, 38, 44 crore and depreciation ₹18 crore each year. Compute DSCR for each year and the average and minimum DSCR.

Q2. Non-cash adjustments [L1]

Identify which of the following are added to, deducted from, or ignored in the DSCR numerator: deferred tax expense, fair value gain on investments, impairment of goodwill, ESOP expense, profit on sale of land, provision for warranty (cash outflow expected next year).

💡 Hint: Ask whether each item changes cash available for debt service in the period.

Q3. Covenant drafting [L3]

A borrower expects a ₹200 crore bullet NCD maturity in FY2026-27 to be refinanced. Propose a DSCR covenant definition that avoids an accounting-driven breach while protecting the lender, and compute the effect using PAT ₹90 crore, D&A ₹60 crore, interest ₹45 crore and other scheduled principal ₹70 crore.
