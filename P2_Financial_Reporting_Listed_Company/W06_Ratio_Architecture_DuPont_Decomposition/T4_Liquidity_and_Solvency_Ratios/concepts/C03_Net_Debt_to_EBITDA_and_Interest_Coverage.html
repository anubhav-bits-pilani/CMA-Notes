Key concept

Net Debt to EBITDA and Interest Coverage

Concept: Net debt/EBITDA = (Borrowings − Cash − Liquid investments) ÷ EBITDA; Interest coverage = EBIT ÷ Finance cost

Statement

\[ \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{EBITDA}} = \frac{B - C - LI}{\text{EBITDA}_{\text{LTM}}} \qquad \text{Interest coverage} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{FC} \]

where \(B\) = total borrowings at the reporting date (non-current, current and current maturities; add lease liabilities when EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116), \(C\) = cash and cash equivalents (unrestricted), \(LI\) = liquid investments (liquid/overnight funds, unencumbered short deposits), \(\text{EBITDA}_{\text{LTM}}\) = last twelve months earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (normalised for one-offs, other income policy stated), \(\text{EBIT}\) = earnings before finance costs and tax for the same period, and \(FC\) = finance costs as presented in the statement of profit and loss (optionally plus capitalised interest).

Explanation

Net debt/EBITDA approximates the number of years of current operating cash generation needed to repay net debt, ignoring tax, capex and working capital. It is the anchor leverage metric in loan covenants, LBO structuring and rating rationales (where it appears as "Total debt/OPBDITA"). Interest coverage measures how many times operating profit covers the annual interest bill — a flow measure of debt affordability.

Consistency conditions:

  • Lease pairing: lease liabilities in debt ⇔ post-116 EBITDA and lease interest in finance cost. Excluding leases from debt while using post-116 EBITDA understates leverage (topic pitfall).
  • Time alignment: closing net debt against LTM EBITDA; for acquisitions use pro forma LTM EBITDA.
  • Other income: exclude from EBITDA for an operating view; if included in EBIT for coverage, say so — for net-cash companies treasury income can make coverage look infinite.
  • Negative values: a negative net debt/EBITDA means net cash; it is not a "better" leverage reading to be ranked against positive values. Negative EBIT gives a coverage that should be shown as "NM".
  • EBITDA basis coverage (EBITDA ÷ finance cost) is used by rating agencies; EBIT basis is stricter because it charges depreciation as a proxy for reinvestment.

Illustration

Cement company FY2024-25 (₹ crore): long-term borrowings 3,800, short-term borrowings 700, lease liabilities 300, cash 450, liquid funds 650, normalised EBITDA (post-116) 2,000, D&A 750, other income 120 (excluded), finance costs 380, interest capitalised 90.

  • Net debt = 3,800 + 700 + 300 − 450 − 650 = 3,700
  • Net debt/EBITDA = 3,700 ÷ 2,000 = 1.85x
  • EBIT = 2,000 − 750 = 1,250; interest coverage = 1,250 ÷ 380 = 3.29x
  • Including capitalised interest: 1,250 ÷ 470 = 2.66x
  • EBITDA coverage = 2,000 ÷ 380 = 5.26x

The spread between 5.26x and 2.66x shows why the definition must be stated before comparing against a covenant or a rating threshold.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both ratios [L1]

From the following (₹ crore) compute net debt/EBITDA and EBIT interest cover: NCDs 1,500; term loans 900; CP 400; lease liabilities 250; cash 300; liquid funds 700; deposits under lien 100; EBITDA (post-116, normalised) 1,400; D&A 420; finance costs 290.

Q2. Lease mismatch [L2]

An analyst reports net debt/EBITDA of 0.9x for a retailer using borrowings net of cash (₹540 crore) and post-116 EBITDA (₹600 crore). Lease liabilities are ₹1,350 crore and rent that would have been expensed pre-116 is ₹230 crore. Compute the ratio on both consistent bases and explain the error.

💡 Hint: Either add leases to debt or deduct rent from EBITDA — not neither.

Q3. Net-cash company coverage [L2]

A net-cash IT company has EBIT ₹9,000 crore including other income of ₹1,400 crore, and finance costs of ₹120 crore (all lease interest). Compute coverage with and without other income and argue whether coverage is a meaningful credit metric for this company.
