Key concept

Annual Review of Useful Life and Residual Value

Concept: Ind AS 16 para 51: useful life and residual value reviewed at least annually; changes are prospective (Ind AS 8)

Statement

Ind AS 16.51: the residual value and the useful life of an asset shall be reviewed at least at each financial year-end and, if expectations differ from previous estimates, the change(s) shall be accounted for as a change in an accounting estimate in accordance with Ind AS 8. Ind AS 16.61 applies the same to the depreciation method.

Under Ind AS 8.36–38, a change in estimate is recognised prospectively — in the period of change and future periods — with no restatement of comparatives. For straight-line depreciation after a change at the start of year \(t\):

\[ D_{\text{new}} = \frac{NBV_{t} - RV_{\text{new}}}{L_{\text{rem,new}}} \]

where \(NBV_t\) = net book value at the date of change, \(RV_{\text{new}}\) = revised residual value, and \(L_{\text{rem,new}}\) = revised remaining useful life in years.

Explanation

Useful life and residual value are estimates; the standard requires them to track reality. Triggers for revision include technical assessments, changes in maintenance practice, technological obsolescence (e.g., IT hardware), regulatory changes (emission norms for cement kilns or vehicles), and changes in intended use. Schedule II to the Companies Act provides indicative lives; a company using different lives must disclose the justification supported by technical advice (s06).

Prospective treatment means past depreciation is not reversed. The full effect of a life extension appears as lower depreciation from the year of change, raising EBIT, PAT, net block and ROCE immediately. Disclosure: Ind AS 8.39 requires the nature and amount of the change in estimate affecting the current period and, where practicable, future periods.

Analytical implications:

  • A life extension in a weak year can be an earnings-management lever — compare depreciation as a percentage of average gross block across years and peers.
  • Peer comparisons of EBIT margin and ROCE should adjust to common lives where differences are material.
  • Distinguish a change in estimate (prospective) from correction of an error (retrospective restatement under Ind AS 8.42) — e.g., depreciation never charged on a commissioned asset is an error, not an estimate change.

Illustration

A paint company's process plant: cost ₹1,200 crore, residual value nil, original life 15 years, straight-line. At 1 April 2024 the plant is five years old (NBV ₹800 crore). Technical review extends the total life to 20 years (remaining 15 years) with residual value ₹50 crore.

  • Old annual depreciation = 1,200 ÷ 15 = ₹80 crore
  • New annual depreciation = (800 − 50) ÷ 15 = ₹50 crore
  • FY2024-25 EBIT rises by ₹30 crore; with EBIT of ₹1,000 crore before the change and average capital employed of ₹5,000 crore, ROCE moves from 20.0% to about 20.6% (ignoring the small increase in capital employed)
  • No restatement of FY2023-24 comparatives; disclose the ₹30 crore effect.

Practice Questions

Q1. Revised depreciation [L1]

A machine costing ₹90 crore (residual value ₹5 crore, life 10 years, straight-line) has been used for four years. At the start of year 5, the remaining life is revised to 3 years and residual value to nil. Compute the revised annual depreciation and the effect on year 5 profit.

Q2. Estimate change or error? [L2]

Classify each as a change in estimate or a prior-period error and state the accounting: (a) extending server lives from 6 to 7 years after a technical study; (b) discovering that a plant commissioned two years ago was never transferred from CWIP; (c) changing from WDV to straight-line to reflect consumption pattern; (d) correcting a formula error that applied a 30-year life to plant and machinery.

💡 Hint: Ask whether new information or a misuse of available information drove the change.

Q3. Peer adjustment [L3]

Peer A depreciates plant over 15 years; Peer B extended its lives to 25 years in FY2023-24, cutting depreciation by ₹220 crore a year. Both report EBIT of about ₹1,500 crore. Explain how you would adjust B to A's basis for EBIT margin and ROCE comparison, and what evidence would make you accept B's longer lives as economically valid.
