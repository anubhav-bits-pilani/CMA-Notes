Key concept

Net Fixed Asset Turnover Formula and Disclosure of Scope

Concept: Net fixed asset turnover = Revenue ÷ Average net PPE (state whether CWIP/ROU included)

Statement

\[ \text{NFAT} = \frac{R}{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(F_{\text{open}} + F_{\text{close}}\right)} \]

where \(R\) = revenue from operations for the year (net of GST, excluding other income), and \(F\) = net fixed assets at the opening and closing balance-sheet dates, defined as net PPE (gross block less accumulated depreciation and impairment) plus, where stated, capital work-in-progress (CWIP) and/or right-of-use (ROU) assets. The definition must be explicitly labelled, e.g. "NFAT (PPE + ROU, ex-CWIP)".

Explanation

NFAT measures revenue generated per rupee of net long-lived operating assets — a proxy for capital intensity and plant productivity. Its reciprocal (net PPE ÷ revenue) is the capital intensity used to forecast capex in models.

Why the scope statement is part of the formula:

  • CWIP represents capital committed but not yet earning. Including it depresses NFAT during a capex cycle; excluding it ignores capital the shareholders have already funded. Present both in capex-heavy sectors (cement, paints during expansion).
  • ROU assets (Ind AS 116) are economically similar to owned assets for retailers and QSR chains; including them makes lease-heavy and owner-operated peers comparable. For time series spanning FY2018-19, exclude ROU or restate.
  • Intangibles and goodwill are usually excluded from NFAT but included in an invested-capital turnover.
  • Average vs closing: average balances align revenue (a flow) with the asset base available during the year; closing balances overstate the base for growing companies and understate turnover.

Net PPE falls with accumulated depreciation, so NFAT rises as assets age even with flat productivity. Pair NFAT with gross block turnover and asset age (c04).

Illustration

Value retailer FY2024-25 (₹ crore, opening/closing): revenue 9,600; net PPE 1,100/1,300; CWIP 80/120; ROU assets 2,200/2,600.

  • NFAT (PPE only) = 9,600 ÷ 1,200 = 8.0x
  • NFAT (PPE + CWIP) = 9,600 ÷ 1,300 = 7.4x
  • NFAT (PPE + ROU) = 9,600 ÷ 3,600 = 2.7x
  • NFAT (PPE + CWIP + ROU) = 9,600 ÷ 3,700 = 2.6x

An unlabelled "8.0x" compared against an owner-operated department store's 2.5x would suggest a three-fold productivity advantage that largely reflects lease accounting (topic pitfall).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and label [L1]

A paint company reports (₹ crore, opening/closing): revenue 11,200; net PPE 3,800/4,600; CWIP 900/400; ROU 250/300. Compute NFAT under four labelled definitions and state which is most appropriate for a year in which a large plant was capitalised from CWIP.

Q2. Average versus closing [L2]

A company's net PPE grew from ₹800 crore to ₹1,400 crore during the year after a plant commissioned on 1 October; revenue was ₹3,300 crore. Compute NFAT on closing, simple average and time-weighted average bases (assume the ₹600 crore addition was capitalised entirely on 1 October and ignore depreciation) and explain which is most faithful.

💡 Hint: The time-weighted base counts the new plant for six months.

Q3. Interview — capital intensity driver [L2]

Explain how you would use historical NFAT to forecast capex in a five-year model for a cement company, and two reasons the historical ratio may be a poor guide.
