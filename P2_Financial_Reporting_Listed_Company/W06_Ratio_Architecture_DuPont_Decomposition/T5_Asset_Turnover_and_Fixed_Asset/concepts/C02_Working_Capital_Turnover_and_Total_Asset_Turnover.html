Key concept

Working Capital Turnover and Total Asset Turnover Formulas

Concept: Working capital turnover = Revenue ÷ Average NWC; Total asset turnover = Revenue ÷ Average total assets

Statement

\[ \text{WCT} = \frac{R}{\overline{NWC}} \qquad \text{TAT} = \frac{R}{\overline{TA}} \]

where \(R\) = revenue from operations, \(\overline{NWC}\) = average of opening and closing operating net working capital (trade receivables + inventories + other operating current assets − trade payables − other operating current liabilities; excluding cash, investments, borrowings, lease liabilities and tax balances), and \(\overline{TA}\) = average of opening and closing total assets as per the balance sheet.

Explanation

Working capital turnover shows how much revenue each rupee of operating working capital supports. It is the reciprocal of NWC as a percentage of revenue (t3 c04): a WCT of 6x means NWC of 16.7% of revenue, or about 61 days of revenue. Higher is generally better (less capital tied up), but very high WCT driven by stretched payables or factoring may not be sustainable. When NWC is near zero or negative, WCT explodes or turns negative and should be reported as "NM", with NWC % of revenue used instead.

Total asset turnover is the asset-efficiency leg of DuPont ROE:

\[ \text{ROE} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{R} \times \frac{R}{\overline{TA}} \times \frac{\overline{TA}}{\overline{E}} \]

For the product to equal ROE, TAT and the equity multiplier must use the same average total assets (t2 pitfall). Because total assets include cash, goodwill and investments, TAT for cash-rich or acquisitive companies understates operating efficiency; analysts compute operating asset turnover (excluding excess cash and non-operating investments) alongside.

Note the distinction from the Schedule III net capital turnover ratio (s02), which divides revenue by total working capital (CA − CL, including cash and current borrowings) and is not comparable with analyst WCT.

Illustration

Asian Paints-style company, FY2024-25 (₹ crore, opening/closing): revenue 34,000; receivables 3,500/3,900; inventories 6,000/6,400; other operating CA 900/1,000; trade payables 4,200/4,600; other operating CL 2,300/2,500; total assets 29,000/31,000; cash and treasury investments within total assets 5,000/5,600.

  • Opening NWC = 3,500 + 6,000 + 900 − 4,200 − 2,300 = 3,900; closing NWC = 3,900 + 6,400 + 1,000 − 4,600 − 2,500 = 4,200; average NWC = 4,050

  • WCT = 34,000 ÷ 4,050 = 8.4x (NWC ≈ 11.9% of revenue)

  • TAT = 34,000 ÷ 30,000 = 1.13x
  • Operating asset turnover = 34,000 ÷ (30,000 − 5,300) = 1.38x

The 0.25x gap between TAT and operating asset turnover is entirely treasury assets — relevant when comparing with a less cash-rich peer such as Kansai Nerolac.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both ratios [L1]

From the following (₹ crore, opening/closing) compute WCT, NWC % of revenue and TAT: revenue 6,400; receivables 720/860; inventories 1,050/1,210; other operating CA 140/160; payables 880/960; other operating CL 230/270; total assets 5,600/6,200.

Q2. DuPont consistency [L2]

An analyst computes TAT on closing total assets (₹6,200 crore) but the equity multiplier on average assets (₹5,900 crore) and average equity (₹3,400 crore), with PAT ₹560 crore and revenue ₹6,400 crore. Show that the product of the three components does not equal ROE on average equity and explain the fix.

💡 Hint: Use the same asset base in the turnover and leverage terms so it cancels.

Q3. Negative NWC interpretation [L2]

A QSR chain's NWC moves from −₹120 crore to −₹180 crore while revenue grows from ₹4,000 crore to ₹4,800 crore. Explain why WCT is not meaningful, compute NWC % of revenue for both years, and interpret the change.
