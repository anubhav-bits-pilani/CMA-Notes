Key concept

Capex-to-Depreciation Ratio as a Net Investment Signal

Concept: Capex/Depreciation ratio = Capex ÷ D&A; greater than 1 signals net investment

Statement

\[ \text{Capex/D\&A} = \frac{K}{D} \]

where \(K\) = capital expenditure for the period — purchase of PPE and intangible assets (including movements in CWIP and capital advances) from investing cash flows, optionally net of disposal proceeds and optionally including ROU asset additions for an "economic capex" view — and \(D\) = depreciation and amortisation expense for the same period (with ROU depreciation included or excluded consistently with \(K\)). A ratio above 1 means the company is adding to its net fixed asset base; below 1, the base is shrinking in book terms.

Explanation

Depreciation allocates the historical cost of existing assets; capex adds new ones. If capex exceeds depreciation, net PPE grows — the company is investing for growth or capacity. The ratio is therefore a leading indicator: high values foreshadow higher depreciation, lower near-term turnover and FCF, and (if investment succeeds) higher future revenue.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Inflation: depreciation is on historical cost, so replacing an asset costs more than its accumulated depreciation. A ratio modestly above 1 (about 1.1–1.3x) can be pure maintenance in an inflationary economy.
  • Asset-light shift (s05): a ratio below 1 may reflect a deliberate move to franchising or outsourcing, not under-investment.
  • Lumpiness: use a three- or five-year cumulative ratio (\(\sum K \div \sum D\)) to smooth project timing.
  • Inorganic growth: acquisitions add PPE without appearing in capex — supplement with PPE acquired through business combinations.
  • Useful-life changes alter \(D\) and the ratio without any change in spending.
  • ROU consistency: for retailers, cash capex/D&A including ROU depreciation understates investment unless ROU additions are also included.

Illustration

Three-year data for Berger-style paint company (₹ crore):

Year Capex D&A Capex/D&A
FY2022-23 620 250 2.48x
FY2023-24 540 290 1.86x
FY2024-25 300 330 0.91x
Cumulative 1,460 870 1.68x

The single-year 0.91x suggests under-investment, but the cumulative 1.68x shows a completed expansion phase now in digestion; rising D&A from new plants also mechanically lowers the latest ratio.

Practice Questions

Q1. Single-year versus cumulative [L1]

Capex (₹ crore) for five years: 400, 1,100, 1,600, 500, 350; D&A: 300, 320, 380, 470, 520. Compute annual and five-year cumulative capex/D&A and describe the investment phase.

Q2. Economic capex for a retailer [L2]

A retailer reports cash capex ₹420 crore, D&A ₹610 crore (of which ROU depreciation ₹380 crore) and ROU additions ₹700 crore. Compute capex/D&A on a cash basis, on an owned-asset basis (excluding ROU) and on an economic basis (including ROU), and interpret.

💡 Hint: Match the numerator and denominator scope in each version.

Q3. Terminal value consistency [L3]

In a DCF, a cement company's terminal year shows capex/D&A of 0.8x with 5% perpetual growth. Explain why this is internally inconsistent and how you would correct it.
