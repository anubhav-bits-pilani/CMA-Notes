Subtopic note

Capex-to-Depreciation, Asset Age and the Replacement Cycle

Scope: Capex-to-depreciation as expansion indicator; asset age and replacement cycle

Why it matters

Turnover ratios describe how productively today's assets are used; capex-to-depreciation and asset age describe where the asset base is heading. A company spending well above depreciation is building capacity — margins and turnover may dip now and rise later. A company spending below depreciation is harvesting — free cash flow looks excellent but the asset base is ageing and a catch-up capex wave is coming. For equity analysts this is the bridge between reported free cash flow and sustainable free cash flow; for lenders it signals funding needs; for FP&A it frames the split between maintenance and growth capex in the annual budget; and in the 3-statement model it is the sanity check on the capex and depreciation forecast (Week 14).

Core notes

Capex-to-depreciation ratio

\[ \text{Capex/Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Capex}}{\text{D\&A}} \]

  • Capex: purchase of PPE and intangibles from the cash flow statement (investing activities), net of proceeds from disposals if you want net capex. Add capital advances paid if material. ROU additions are non-cash and not in investing cash flow — include them in an "economic capex" variant for lease-heavy companies.
  • D&A: depreciation and amortisation expense from the P&L; decide whether ROU depreciation is included (consistent with whether ROU additions are in capex).

Interpretation:

Ratio Reading
Well above 1 (e.g., 2–4x) Expansion phase: new plants, capacity additions, acquisitions of assets
Around 1–1.3x Steady state with modest growth (inflation means replacement cost exceeds historical depreciation)
Below 1 Under-investment / harvesting; or asset-light shift; or post-capex digestion

Because depreciation is based on historical cost while replacement occurs at current prices, a mature company in an inflationary environment needs capex somewhat above depreciation just to stand still. In India, with WPI inflation of 3–6% for capital goods, a ratio of about 1.1–1.3x is often "maintenance plus normal growth".

Maintenance vs growth capex

Companies rarely disclose the split; analysts estimate:

  • Depreciation proxy: maintenance capex ≈ D&A (crude; understates under inflation).
  • Capacity method: growth capex = cost of new capacity announced (e.g., ₹ crore per MTPA for cement, ₹ crore per KL for paints); maintenance = total − growth.
  • Management guidance: earnings calls of Indian cement and paint companies often state maintenance capex per tonne or per year.

Sustainable FCF = CFO − maintenance capex; growth capex is a discretionary investment whose return should be tested with incremental ROIC (t1 c05).

Asset age

\[ \text{Average asset age (years)} \approx \frac{\text{Accumulated depreciation}}{\text{Annual depreciation}} \]

\[ \text{Average useful life} \approx \frac{\text{Gross block}}{\text{Annual depreciation}} \qquad \text{Remaining life} \approx \frac{\text{Net block}}{\text{Annual depreciation}} \]

Asset age ÷ useful life gives the proportion of life consumed. Exclude freehold land (not depreciated) and CWIP from gross and net block for these computations. Under straight-line depreciation (common in India for most assets under Schedule II), the proxies are reasonable; under WDV they overstate age.

Replacement cycle

When proportion of life consumed exceeds roughly 60–70%, a replacement or modernisation wave is likely within the forecast horizon. Combine with capex/D&A: an old asset base and a ratio below 1 is a classic setup for a capex surprise. Conversely, a young asset base after a large capex programme implies a few years of low capex and rising FCF, plus rising depreciation that compresses EBIT margin even as EBITDA expands.

Worked illustration

Cement company FY2024-25 (₹ crore, excluding land): gross block 18,000; accumulated depreciation 9,900; depreciation 900; capex 2,700; disposals proceeds 60.

Metric Computation Result
Gross capex / D&A 2,700 ÷ 900 3.0x
Net capex / D&A 2,640 ÷ 900 2.93x
Average asset age 9,900 ÷ 900 11.0 years
Average useful life 18,000 ÷ 900 20.0 years
Remaining life 8,100 ÷ 900 9.0 years
Life consumed 11 ÷ 20 55%

Interpretation: a heavy expansion phase (3x) on a mid-life asset base. Management guidance of maintenance capex of ₹150 per tonne on 40 MTPA (₹600 crore) implies growth capex of about ₹2,100 crore.

Excel implementation

Capex_to_DA   = Capex / DA
Asset_Age     = (AccDep - AccDep_Land) / Dep
Life_Consumed = Asset_Age / ((GrossBlock - Land) / Dep)
Maint_Capex   = Capacity_t * Maint_per_t / 1e7
Growth_Capex  = Capex - Maint_Capex

(₹ per tonne × tonnes ÷ 10^7 converts to ₹ crore.)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Acquisitions: buying a company adds assets through business combination, not capex — the ratio misses inorganic expansion. Add acquisition consideration allocated to PPE for a complete view.
  • ROU additions: a retailer opening 200 leased stores shows low cash capex; include ROU additions for economic capex.
  • Lumpy capex: one-year ratios swing; use three-year cumulative capex ÷ cumulative D&A.
  • Change in useful lives (s06, c05) changes depreciation and thus the ratio without any change in spending.
  • Deemed cost at Ind AS transition reset accumulated depreciation to zero for companies that elected previous GAAP carrying amount as deemed cost — the asset-age proxy then understates true age; check the Ind AS 101 disclosure in the transition-year accounts.
  • Impairment reduces net block but may be included in accumulated depreciation and impairment in the note — separate it.

Key takeaways

  • Capex/D&A above 1 signals net investment; around 1.1–1.3x is often steady state under inflation.
  • Split maintenance and growth capex to compute sustainable FCF and to test incremental ROIC.
  • Asset age ≈ accumulated depreciation ÷ annual depreciation; compare with useful life to gauge replacement need.
  • Old assets plus low capex/D&A foreshadow a capex wave; young assets after expansion foreshadow FCF recovery.
  • Adjust for acquisitions, ROU additions, deemed cost resets and lumpy years.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ratios for two cement peers [L1]

Peer A: gross block ₹22,000 crore (land ₹2,000 crore), accumulated depreciation ₹12,000 crore, depreciation ₹1,000 crore, capex ₹1,100 crore. Peer B: gross block ₹9,000 crore (land ₹600 crore), accumulated depreciation ₹2,100 crore, depreciation ₹420 crore, capex ₹1,700 crore. Compute capex/D&A, asset age, useful life and life consumed for both, and characterise each company's investment phase.

Q2. Sustainable FCF [L2]

A paint company reports CFO ₹1,800 crore and capex ₹1,300 crore. Depreciation is ₹420 crore. Management guides that a new 2 lakh KL plant costing ₹900 crore accounts for most of the year's capex. Estimate maintenance capex by two methods and compute reported and sustainable FCF under each.

💡 Hint: One method uses depreciation; the other subtracts identified growth capex from total capex.

Q3. Harvesting red flag [L3]

An auto-component company has had capex/D&A of 0.6–0.7x for four years, life consumed of 78%, and rising FCF conversion that management highlights on earnings calls. Prepare three questions you would ask management and explain how you would adjust the five-year forecast in a DCF.

Q4. Interview — ROU additions [L1]

Explain why capex/D&A for a QSR chain can be misleading after Ind AS 116 and how you would construct an "economic capex" measure.
