Subtopic note

Working Capital Turnover, Total Asset Turnover and Net Capital Turnover

Scope: Working capital turnover and total asset turnover; Schedule III net capital turnover

Why it matters

Fixed asset turnover (s01) covers only long-lived capital. Most businesses also tie up large sums in inventory and receivables, and total asset turnover is the precise leg that sits in DuPont ROE (t2) — every 0.1x change in total asset turnover moves ROE proportionally. Working capital turnover connects this topic to the cash conversion cycle (t3): it is the revenue-scaled mirror of NWC days. Schedule III adds a statutory version — the net capital turnover ratio — which every company must disclose with a 25% variance explanation, but whose definition differs materially from the operating NWC an analyst would use. Knowing the three ratios and how they reconcile prevents mixing incompatible numbers in a peer table.

Core notes

Working capital turnover

\[ \text{Working capital turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue from operations}}{\text{Average NWC}} \]

The analyst definition uses operating (non-cash) net working capital:

\[ \text{NWC} = \text{Trade receivables} + \text{Inventories} + \text{Other operating current assets} - \text{Trade payables} - \text{Other operating current liabilities} \]

Exclude cash, current investments, current borrowings, CMLTD, current lease liabilities, dividends payable and current tax balances (financing and tax items). Include contract assets, unbilled revenue, customer advances and contract liabilities, as these are operating (t3 s03).

Relationship to t3: NWC % of revenue is the reciprocal of working capital turnover, and NWC days = 365 ÷ WC turnover. A WC turnover of 8x equals NWC of 12.5% of revenue, or about 46 days.

Interpretation issue: when NWC is small or negative (FMCG, QSR, e-commerce), turnover becomes huge or negative and is not meaningful. Report NWC % of revenue instead for these companies.

Total asset turnover

\[ \text{Total asset turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue from operations}}{\text{Average total assets}} \]

Total assets include cash, investments, goodwill, ROU assets, deferred tax assets and everything else on the balance sheet. This is the DuPont definition (t2 c01, c02) and must use the same average-balance convention as the equity multiplier so the product of components equals ROE. Analytically useful variants:

  • Operating asset turnover = Revenue ÷ Average (total assets − cash − non-operating investments) — strips excess cash for cash-rich companies (Asian Paints, IT majors), mirroring the t1 ROCE adjustment.
  • Invested capital turnover = Revenue ÷ Average invested capital — the turnover leg of ROIC (ROIC = NOPAT margin × invested capital turnover).

Schedule III net capital turnover ratio

Introduced by the 2021 Schedule III amendment among the eleven mandatory ratios. The ICAI Guidance Note suggests:

\[ \text{Net capital turnover} = \frac{\text{Net sales}}{\text{Working capital}} \]

with net sales = revenue from operations (sales less returns), and working capital = current assets − current liabilities. Companies disclose their own definition; practice varies between closing and average working capital, and between total revenue from operations and only sale of products.

Crucial difference from the analyst ratio: Schedule III working capital is total CA − CL, so it includes cash, current investments and current borrowings, CMLTD and lease liabilities. For a cash-rich company it is large and the ratio low; for a company with high short-term debt it is small and the ratio high; for negative working capital it is negative. The 25% variance requirement means a CMLTD reclassification can trigger a mandatory explanation even with no operational change.

Worked illustration

Kansai Nerolac-style company, FY2024-25 (₹ crore, averages of opening and closing):

Item Average
Revenue from operations 7,800
Trade receivables 1,050
Inventories 1,500
Other operating current assets 200
Trade payables 1,100
Other operating current liabilities 250
Cash and current investments 900
Current borrowings and lease liabilities 150
Total assets 7,600
Ratio Computation Result
Operating NWC 1,050 + 1,500 + 200 − 1,100 − 250 1,400
WC turnover (analyst) 7,800 ÷ 1,400 5.57x
NWC % of revenue 1,400 ÷ 7,800 17.9%
Schedule III working capital 1,400 + 900 − 150 2,150
Net capital turnover (Sch III) 7,800 ÷ 2,150 3.63x
Total asset turnover 7,800 ÷ 7,600 1.03x
Operating asset turnover 7,800 ÷ (7,600 − 900) 1.16x

The analyst and statutory working-capital ratios differ by over 50% because of cash and borrowings — never place them in the same column.

Excel implementation

Op_NWC     = TR + INV + OthOpCA - TP - OthOpCL
WCT        = Revenue / AVERAGE(Op_NWC_Open, Op_NWC_Close)
NCT_SchIII = Revenue / (Total_CA - Total_CL)
TAT        = Revenue / AVERAGE(TA_Open, TA_Close)
WCT_Disp   = IF(AVERAGE(Op_NWC_Open, Op_NWC_Close) <= 0, "NM", WCT)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Seasonality: year-end NWC for festive-season businesses (paints peak in Q3 for Diwali) can differ sharply from the annual average; use half-yearly data under Reg 33 where possible.
  • Growth companies: closing balances overstate the base; averages smooth it (t3 pitfall).
  • Negative NWC: report as "NM" and use NWC % of revenue.
  • Factoring and supplier finance reduce NWC without operational change (t3 s05); adjust if material.
  • Revenue gross vs net: agents reporting net revenue under Ind AS 115 (marketplaces) show low revenue and low turnover against a principal reporting gross.
  • Schedule III definition drift: a company moving from closing to average working capital must disclose the change; variance explanations otherwise mislead.

Key takeaways

  • Analyst WC turnover uses operating NWC (no cash, no debt); it is the reciprocal of NWC % of revenue.
  • Total asset turnover is the DuPont leg; use averages consistent with the equity multiplier.
  • Operating asset turnover strips excess cash — essential for cash-rich peers.
  • Schedule III net capital turnover uses total CA − CL, including cash and current borrowings; it is not comparable to the analyst ratio.
  • Small or negative NWC makes turnover meaningless; switch to NWC % of revenue.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three turnover ratios [L1]

Compute operating WC turnover, Schedule III net capital turnover and total asset turnover from the following (₹ crore, averages): revenue 5,200; receivables 640; inventory 880; other operating CA 120; payables 720; other operating CL 180; cash and liquid funds 410; short-term borrowings 260; CMLTD 90; total assets 4,900.

Q2. CMLTD-driven variance [L2]

A company's operating NWC is unchanged at ₹900 crore in both years, revenue is flat at ₹6,000 crore, and cash is ₹300 crore in both years. In FY2024-25, ₹350 crore of a term loan becomes due within twelve months. Compute the Schedule III net capital turnover for both years (current borrowings were ₹100 crore in FY2023-24), compute the variance and draft the disclosure explanation.

💡 Hint: Schedule III working capital includes current maturities of long-term borrowings in current liabilities.

Q3. Negative working capital company [L2]

An FMCG company has average operating NWC of −₹450 crore on revenue of ₹15,000 crore. Explain why working capital turnover should not be reported, what metric replaces it, and how a move to −₹600 crore should be interpreted.

Q4. Turnover bridge in DuPont [L3]

For a paint company, total asset turnover fell from 1.25x to 1.05x over three years. Build a bridge decomposing the change into fixed-asset, working-capital and cash/investment components using the identity that total assets = fixed assets + NWC-related assets + cash + other, and describe the Excel layout you would use.
