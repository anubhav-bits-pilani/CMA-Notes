Subtopic note

Net Fixed Asset Turnover, CWIP, ROU Assets and Gross Block

Scope: Net fixed asset turnover with/without CWIP and ROU assets; gross block turnover

Why it matters

Fixed asset turnover measures how many rupees of revenue a company extracts from each rupee invested in plant, property and equipment. It is the asset-intensity driver behind the asset-turnover leg of DuPont (t2) and the capital-turnover leg of ROCE/ROIC (t1). In paints, cement, chemicals and auto components it separates operational productivity from pricing; in retail and QSR it is distorted by Ind AS 116; in a capex cycle it is distorted by capital work-in-progress (CWIP). In modelling, the inverse of fixed asset turnover (capex intensity per rupee of revenue) is a common driver for forecasting the PPE schedule. Before any peer comparison, the analyst must decide — and disclose — what is in the denominator.

Core notes

Base definition

\[ \text{Net fixed asset turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue from operations}}{\text{Average net PPE}} \]

Net PPE = gross block less accumulated depreciation and impairment, per the Ind AS 16 note. Use revenue from operations (excluding other income) net of GST, and average of opening and closing balances so that mid-year additions are partially reflected (the average-balance convention from t1).

Denominator choices

Variant Denominator When to use
Net PPE only Net PPE (owned) Productivity of operating, commissioned assets
Net PPE + CWIP Net PPE + capital work-in-progress Total capital committed; required for ROCE consistency
Net PPE + ROU Net PPE + right-of-use assets Lease-heavy peers (retail, QSR, hospitals) on a comparable footing
Net PPE + CWIP + ROU + intangibles All long-lived operating assets "Net operating fixed assets" for ROIC-style analysis
Gross block Gross PPE at cost Neutralises depreciation-age differences

CWIP is not yet producing revenue, so including it depresses turnover during a capex cycle — a timing distortion, not a productivity decline. Excluding it overstates productivity of capital actually committed. The practical answer is to compute both and flag the CWIP-to-net-PPE ratio. Schedule III requires a CWIP ageing schedule (less than 1 year, 1–2, 2–3, more than 3 years) and, for projects overdue or over budget, a completion schedule — use it to judge when CWIP will start earning.

ROU assets under Ind AS 116 represent leased stores, warehouses, offices and vehicles. A retailer that leases every store shows a tiny net PPE (only fit-outs) and a spectacular turnover if ROU is excluded. Including ROU puts it on the same footing as a peer that owns stores. For pre-FY2019-20 comparisons, the ROU-excluded figure is the only consistent series.

Gross block turnover

\[ \text{Gross block turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average gross block}} \]

Net PPE falls every year as depreciation accumulates, so an old, fully depreciated plant shows a high net turnover even with no productivity improvement. Gross block (cost) removes this age effect and is the better measure of physical capacity productivity. Caveats: under Ind AS 101 many companies elected deemed cost at transition (1 April 2015 or 2016), resetting gross block to the previous GAAP carrying amount — so gross block of older companies may understate historical cost; revaluation (s06) inflates gross block.

Worked illustration

Two paint companies, FY2024-25 (₹ crore):

Item Company X (new plant) Company Y (mature)
Revenue 9,000 9,000
Average gross block 6,000 7,500
Average accumulated depreciation 1,000 4,500
Average net PPE 5,000 3,000
Average CWIP 1,500 100
Average ROU assets 300 600
Ratio X Y
Net FAT (PPE only) 1.80x 3.00x
Net FAT (PPE + CWIP) 1.38x 2.90x
Net FAT (PPE + CWIP + ROU) 1.32x 2.43x
Gross block turnover 1.50x 1.20x

On net PPE, Y looks 67% more productive. On gross block, X generates more revenue per rupee of original investment — Y's apparent advantage is largely its older, depreciated asset base (topic pitfall).

Excel implementation

Avg_NetPPE   = AVERAGE(NetPPE_Open, NetPPE_Close)
NFAT_Core    = Revenue / Avg_NetPPE
NFAT_CWIP    = Revenue / (Avg_NetPPE + AVERAGE(CWIP_Open, CWIP_Close))
NFAT_ROU     = Revenue / (Avg_NetPPE + AVERAGE(ROU_Open, ROU_Close))
GBT          = Revenue / AVERAGE(Gross_Open, Gross_Close)
CWIP_Flag    = IF(AVERAGE(CWIP_Open, CWIP_Close) / Avg_NetPPE > 0.2, "Capex cycle", "")

Use a switch cell (CWIP in/out, ROU in/out) so the ratio engine can toggle definitions across peers.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capitalisation date: a plant commissioned on 25 March inflates closing PPE with almost no revenue; an average balance only partly solves this — consider a time-weighted or quarterly average using Reg 33 half-yearly balance sheets.
  • Capital advances sit in other non-current assets, not CWIP; for completeness of committed capital, add them to the CWIP-inclusive variant.
  • Investment property (Ind AS 40) and assets held for sale (Ind AS 105) should be excluded from operating PPE.
  • Consolidation and acquisitions: acquired PPE at fair value (Ind AS 103) inflates the base relative to organically built assets.
  • Freehold land never depreciates and can be a large part of gross block for companies with urban legacy sites; consider excluding land for productivity analysis.
  • Revenue mix: traded goods (purchased stock-in-trade) need no plant; a rising trading share flatters FAT.

Key takeaways

  • Fixed asset turnover = revenue ÷ average fixed assets; always state whether CWIP, ROU and intangibles are in.
  • CWIP-inclusive turnover dips in capex cycles — a timing effect; read the Schedule III CWIP ageing.
  • Include ROU assets for lease-heavy peers; exclude for pre-2019 time series consistency.
  • Gross block turnover neutralises asset age; net turnover rewards old, depreciated plants.
  • Deemed cost elections, revaluation and acquisitions distort gross block comparability.

Practice Questions

Q1. Four definitions [L1]

From the following (₹ crore, opening/closing) compute net FAT (PPE only), net FAT incl. CWIP, net FAT incl. CWIP and ROU, and gross block turnover: revenue 4,800; gross block 3,200/3,900; accumulated depreciation 1,100/1,300; CWIP 400/250; ROU 150/180.

Q2. Capex-cycle distortion [L2]

A cement company's net FAT incl. CWIP fell from 1.6x to 1.1x over two years while capacity rose from 20 MTPA to 30 MTPA; the new 10 MTPA was commissioned in February 2025. Using the CWIP ageing note excerpt — CWIP ₹3,500 crore, of which ₹2,900 crore less than one year and ₹600 crore 1–2 years — explain how you would present the trend to an investment committee.

💡 Hint: Separate the denominator effect of uncommissioned capital from the revenue ramp-up of the new capacity.

Q3. Retailer versus manufacturer [L2]

A value retailer reports revenue ₹12,000 crore, average net PPE ₹1,500 crore and average ROU ₹3,000 crore. An apparel manufacturer reports revenue ₹6,000 crore, average net PPE ₹2,400 crore and ROU ₹100 crore. Compute FAT with and without ROU and explain which comparison is meaningful.

Q4. Ratio engine toggle [L3]

Specify an Excel ratio-engine design that computes fixed asset turnover for six peers under user-selected definitions (CWIP, ROU, intangibles, land) with a single switch panel. Describe the input mapping from Schedule III notes, the formula structure, and two checks that ensure every peer uses the same definition.
