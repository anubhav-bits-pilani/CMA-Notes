Subtopic note

Unbilled Revenue, Contract Assets and Customer Advances in Days

Scope: Unbilled revenue and contract assets in DSO for IT services; customer advances for real estate/jewellery

Why it matters

For many Indian businesses the trade receivables line tells only part of the collection story. IT services companies recognise revenue ahead of invoicing — "unbilled revenue" can be 25–40% of total receivables for the large IT majors — so billed-only DSO flatters collection. At the other extreme, real-estate developers and jewellers collect cash before delivering — booking advances, gold savings schemes — creating contract liabilities that fund working capital and can push CCC negative. Ind AS 115 governs how these balances are classified, and the classification determines which line an analyst picks up. A days analysis that ignores them misreads the business model.

Core notes

Ind AS 115 classification

  • Receivable: an entity's right to consideration that is unconditional — only the passage of time is required before payment is due (Ind AS 115.108). An unbilled amount can be a receivable if the right is unconditional and only invoicing is pending.
  • Contract asset: the right to consideration in exchange for goods or services transferred, when that right is conditioned on something other than the passage of time (e.g., future performance, milestone acceptance) (Ind AS 115.107).
  • Contract liability: the obligation to transfer goods or services for which the entity has received consideration (or consideration is due) from the customer (Ind AS 115.106) — customer advances, deferred/unearned revenue, billing in excess of revenue.
  • Presentation: Ind AS 115.105 requires contract assets and contract liabilities to be presented in the balance sheet; Schedule III's trade receivables ageing schedule includes an "unbilled" column for unbilled receivables.

IT services: billed, unbilled and contract assets

Contract type Typical balance when work is done but not invoiced Classification
Time-and-material (T&M), billing monthly in arrears Unbilled revenue for hours worked Unbilled receivable (unconditional; only invoicing pending)
Fixed-price, milestone billing Revenue recognised on input/output method ahead of milestone Contract asset (conditional on milestone)
Fixed-price, billing in advance Invoice exceeds revenue recognised Contract liability (unearned revenue)
Annual licence or managed services billed annually in advance Deferred revenue Contract liability

Indian IT majors typically disclose: trade receivables (billed), unbilled receivables (within trade receivables or other financial assets, depending on presentation), contract assets (unbilled revenue on fixed-price contracts, often in "other current assets"), and unearned revenue (contract liabilities).

DSO variants:

\[ \text{DSO}_{\text{billed}} = \frac{\overline{\text{Billed TR}}}{R} \times 365, \qquad \text{DSO}_{\text{total}} = \frac{\overline{\text{Billed TR}} + \overline{\text{Unbilled}} + \overline{\text{Contract assets}}}{R} \times 365 \]

\[ \text{DSO}_{\text{net}} = \frac{\overline{\text{Billed TR}} + \overline{\text{Unbilled}} + \overline{\text{Contract assets}} - \overline{\text{Unearned revenue}}}{R} \times 365 \]

IT companies themselves report "DSO including unbilled" in investor presentations — often on quarterly annualised revenue and closing balances. Replicate their method when comparing with guidance, and use your consistent method for peers.

What drives unbilled days up: a shift to fixed-price contracts, large transformation deals with milestone billing, delays in client acceptance, and year-end timing. Rising unbilled with flat billed DSO is an early warning — revenue is being recognised that has not yet been accepted by the client.

Real estate: customer advances and negative CCC

Developers selling under-construction residential units collect instalments linked to construction stages (or under subvention schemes). Under Ind AS 115, most Indian residential contracts recognise revenue at a point in time (on handover/possession) because the criteria for over-time recognition (Ind AS 115.35) are typically not met for Indian buyer agreements. Consequences:

  • Collections accumulate as contract liabilities (advances from customers) for 3–5 years.
  • Inventory (land, construction WIP) builds up at cost.
  • DIO is enormous (years), DSO small, and contract liabilities fund a large share of inventory.

A days analysis should include customer advances as an offset:

\[ \text{CCC}_{\text{adj}} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} - \text{Days of advances}, \qquad \text{Days of advances} = \frac{\overline{\text{Contract liabilities}}}{R} \times 365 \]

RERA (Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016) requires 70% of buyer collections to be deposited in a designated project account and used for land and construction cost of that project — so advances are funding, but restricted funding.

Significant financing component (Ind AS 115.60–65): where advances are received well before transfer, the entity may need to accrue interest on the contract liability (increasing revenue and finance cost). The practical expedient for gaps of one year or less applies (Ind AS 115.63). Check the accounting policy; it affects margins and interest burden.

Jewellery: gold schemes and advances

Jewellers run customer advance schemes (monthly instalments with a bonus instalment, redeemed against jewellery). Balances appear as contract liabilities / advances from customers. Key points:

  • They fund inventory (gold is the largest asset), lowering net working capital.
  • Under the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014, advances received for goods are excluded from "deposits" only if appropriated against supply within 365 days; schemes structured otherwise risk being treated as deposits, with restrictions. Read the notes on scheme structure.
  • Gold metal loans (GML) from banks finance gold inventory; under Week 5 they are debt-like, not trade payables — exclude from DPO.
  • Old-gold exchange and studded jewellery change the inventory and payables profile.

Illustration (illustrative IT services company, ₹ crore, averages)

Item Amount
Revenue from operations 90,000
Billed trade receivables 15,500
Unbilled receivables (T&M) 4,200
Contract assets (fixed-price) 3,100
Unearned revenue 2,600
  • Billed DSO = 62.9 days.
  • Total DSO = (15,500 + 4,200 + 3,100) ÷ 90,000 × 365 = 92.5 days.
  • Net DSO = (22,800 − 2,600) ÷ 90,000 × 365 = 81.9 days.

The 30-day gap between billed and total DSO is the cash the company has earned but not yet invoiced.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Presentation varies: some IT companies show unbilled revenue in "other financial assets", others in trade receivables; read the note before pulling the line.
  • GST on unbilled: unbilled balances exclude GST (no invoice yet), so do not apply the GST gross-up to them (s02).
  • ECL on contract assets: impairment under Ind AS 109 applies to contract assets as well; large allowances signal disputes.
  • Real estate over-time recognition: some commercial or plotted-development contracts may qualify for over-time recognition, making contract assets more relevant than liabilities.
  • Jewellery gift vouchers and loyalty points: contract liabilities with breakage estimates (Ind AS 115.B44–B47).
  • Advances from distributors (paints, FMCG) — small but growing with cash-and-carry schemes; include as negative working capital.

Key takeaways

  • Under Ind AS 115, unconditional unbilled amounts are receivables; conditional ones are contract assets; advances are contract liabilities.
  • For IT services, compute billed, total and net DSO; rising unbilled days are an early warning.
  • Real-estate and jewellery CCC must net customer advances, which can make CCC negative.
  • RERA escrow and deposit rules restrict how advances can be used.
  • Always read the classification note before pulling balances.

Practice Questions

Q1. IT DSO variants [L1]

An IT services company reports (₹ crore, closing): FY2023-24 — billed receivables 6,200, unbilled receivables 1,900, contract assets 800, unearned revenue 900; FY2024-25 — billed 6,500, unbilled 2,600, contract assets 1,300, unearned revenue 850. Revenue FY2024-25 ₹40,000 crore. Compute billed, total and net DSO on average balances and comment on the trend in unbilled.

Q2. Classify the balances [L2]

Classify each of the following under Ind AS 115 for an IT services company at 31 March 2025, and state which DSO variant each enters: (a) March hours on T&M contracts invoiced on 5 April; (b) revenue on a fixed-price project recognised at 60% completion with the next invoice due on client acceptance of phase 2; (c) annual support fees billed on 1 January for calendar 2025; (d) an invoice raised on 20 March, due in 60 days.

Q3. Developer's negative cycle [L2]

A real-estate developer reports (₹ crore, averages): inventory 18,000; trade receivables 600; trade payables 1,400; customer advances 9,500; revenue (point-in-time) ₹7,000 crore; COGS ₹4,800 crore. Compute DIO, DSO, DPO, days of advances and adjusted CCC, and explain what happens to these metrics in a year when two large projects are handed over.

💡 Hint: Think about which balances fall and which flow spikes on handover.

Q4. Jeweller scheme review [L3]

A listed jeweller's customer advance scheme balances grew from ₹1,100 crore to ₹1,900 crore while revenue grew 15%. List the analytical checks (working capital, deposit-rules compliance, ageing of scheme balances, redemption rates) you would perform and explain how the growth affects CCC and the quality of cash flow.
