Subtopic note

NWC % of Revenue, Seasonality and Year-End Window Dressing

Scope: NWC as % of revenue; seasonal (festive quarter) vs year-end window dressing; quarterly half-yearly BS data under Reg 33

Why it matters

Days metrics describe each working-capital component; NWC as a percentage of revenue aggregates them into the number that drives the cash cost of growth. If NWC is 15% of revenue, every ₹100 crore of incremental revenue consumes ₹15 crore of cash before any capex. It is the standard working-capital driver in long-range plans and DCFs, and the quickest way to compare business models (negative-NWC FMCG vs NWC-heavy capital goods). But a single 31 March balance sheet is a snapshot — taken at the end of a quarter when Indian companies push collections, delay payments and clear MSE dues. Paint and consumer companies also have strong seasonality (the festive quarter around Diwali, monsoon slowdowns). Separating genuine seasonality from window dressing requires intra-year balance sheets, which SEBI LODR Regulation 33 provides half-yearly.

Core notes

Definition

\[ \text{NWC \% revenue} = \frac{\text{TR} + \text{Inv} - \text{TP} \pm \text{Other operating items}}{R} \]

Other operating items (from Week 5 w5_t3) include contract assets and unbilled receivables (+), contract liabilities and customer advances (−), operating provisions such as warranty and employee provisions (−), advances to suppliers (+), GST input credit receivable (+), GST and other statutory payables (−), and other operating current assets/liabilities. Exclude cash, investments, borrowings, current maturities of debt, lease liabilities, interest accrued, capex creditors, dividend payable and income-tax balances (or treat tax separately).

Two variants:

  • Trade NWC = TR + Inv − TP — the CCC-linked core.
  • Operating NWC = trade NWC ± all other operating items — the modelling driver.

Use average NWC for comparison with the year's revenue in analysis; use closing NWC as a % of revenue when calibrating a forecast driver that will be applied to closing balances.

Link to days

Trade NWC as a % of revenue can be rebuilt from days:

\[ \frac{\text{Trade NWC}}{R} = \frac{\text{DSO}}{365} + \frac{\text{DIO}}{365} \times \frac{\text{COGS}}{R} - \frac{\text{DPO}}{365} \times \frac{\text{Purchases}}{R} \]

so a fall in gross margin (higher COGS/R) raises NWC % even at constant days — an often-missed interaction.

Seasonality: paints and consumer businesses

Indian decorative paint demand peaks in the October–December quarter (Q3) around Diwali and the wedding season, and in the pre-monsoon months; it slows in the July–September quarter (Q2) during the monsoon. Consequences:

  • Inventory builds in Q2 ahead of festive demand (high DIO at 30 September).
  • Receivables peak after festive dispatches (December), then fall by March as collection drives and dealer schemes close.
  • 31 March is typically a low-NWC point for paint companies; 30 September is typically a high point.

So annual ratios on 31 March balances understate average working capital. A three-point average (31 March prior, 30 September, 31 March current) is more representative:

\[ \overline{\text{NWC}}_{3\text{pt}} = \frac{\text{NWC}_{\text{Mar}, t-1} + \text{NWC}_{\text{Sep}, t} + \text{NWC}_{\text{Mar}, t}}{3} \]

Reg 33 half-yearly data

SEBI LODR Regulation 33 requires listed entities to submit quarterly financial results within 45 days of quarter-end (60 days for the last quarter, with audited annual results). With the half-yearly results (30 September and 31 March), the entity must also disclose a statement of assets and liabilities and, since the 2019 amendment, a cash flow statement for the half-year. These give an extra balance-sheet point and an H1 cash flow — enough to detect year-end effects. Quarterly (June and December) balance sheets are not mandated; some companies disclose working-capital days in investor presentations quarterly.

Window dressing patterns at 31 March

Pattern Indicator
Collection push / channel stuffing DSO at March far below September; Q4 revenue spike with Q1 returns or discounts
Delaying supplier payments DPO at March above September; "other payables" spike
Year-end factoring / bill discounting Receivables fall in March, borrowings or factoring disclosures rise; reverses in April
MSE clearance for s.43B(h) MSE payables fall at March
Inventory push to dealers Inventory falls, receivables rise, with dealer-level stock rising (channel checks)
Advances from customers at year-end Contract liabilities spike in March

Diagnostic: compare March-to-September swings across years. A stable seasonal pattern is business seasonality; a widening gap, or a pattern unlike peers', suggests window dressing. Also compare H2 operating cash flow with H1 — if almost all annual CFO arrives in H2 and reverses in the next H1, investigate.

Illustration (illustrative paint company, ₹ crore)

Item 31 Mar 2024 30 Sep 2024 31 Mar 2025
Trade receivables 1,150 1,700 1,350
Inventories 1,950 2,600 2,150
Trade payables 1,550 1,900 1,750
Trade NWC 1,550 2,400 1,750

Revenue FY2024-25 ₹11,500 crore.

  • Closing-based NWC % = 1,750 ÷ 11,500 = 15.2%.
  • Two-point average = 1,650 ÷ 11,500 = 14.3%.
  • Three-point average = 1,900 ÷ 11,500 = 16.5%.

The three-point average shows ~₹250 crore more working capital deployed on average than the two-point average suggests. For cash forecasting (Week 18), the September peak matters more than either average: the company must fund ₹650 crore more NWC in September than in March.

Modelling use

  • Forecast driver: NWC % revenue (or components via days) applied to forecast revenue; ΔNWC flows to CFO.
  • Cash cost of growth: \(\Delta \text{NWC} = \text{NWC\%} \times \Delta R\).
  • Seasonal intra-year model: monthly or quarterly working-capital profile for revolver sizing.
=SUM(TradeRec,Inventory,ContractAssets,OtherOpCA)-SUM(TradePay,ContractLiab,OpProvisions,OtherOpCL)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative NWC businesses (FMCG, QSR, e-commerce marketplaces): growth releases cash; a slowdown consumes it — the reverse risk.
  • Revenue denominator: use GST-exclusive revenue from operations; balances are GST-inclusive (s02) — fine for driver use, adjust for cross-sector comparison.
  • Acquisitions: acquired NWC jumps without revenue for the full year; pro-forma.
  • Commodity price moves (TiO2, crude derivatives) change inventory value without volume change.
  • Half-yearly data are limited review, not audited, and some line items are aggregated — check that classification is consistent with annual statements.
  • Fiscal calendars: all three exercise companies have March year-ends, so seasonal timing is comparable; for non-March peers, align calendars.

Key takeaways

  • NWC % revenue converts working-capital efficiency into the cash cost of growth.
  • Define operating NWC precisely; exclude cash, debt, leases, capex creditors and tax.
  • Paint demand peaks in Q3 (festive) and dips in Q2 (monsoon); 31 March is a low-NWC point.
  • Use Reg 33 half-yearly balance sheets for three-point averages and to detect window dressing.
  • Compare March-vs-September swings over years and against peers to separate seasonality from window dressing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-point vs three-point NWC [L1]

Trade NWC (₹ crore): 31 March 2024 ₹820; 30 September 2024 ₹1,310; 31 March 2025 ₹900. Revenue FY2024-25 ₹6,400 crore. Compute NWC % revenue on closing, two-point and three-point bases, and the extra peak funding need at 30 September versus 31 March 2025.

Q2. Operating NWC build [L2]

From the following 31 March 2025 balances (₹ crore), compute trade NWC and operating NWC and their % of revenue of ₹15,000 crore: trade receivables 1,900; inventories 2,700; trade payables 2,300; contract liabilities 260; advances to suppliers 140; GST input credit receivable 180; statutory dues payable 210; employee provisions (current) 150; capex creditors 320; current maturities of long-term borrowings 400; current investments 1,200; interest accrued 25.

💡 Hint: Classify each item as operating, financing or investing before summing.

Q3. Window dressing test [L3]

A consumer durables company's DSO (on half-year annualised revenue) was: Sep-2022 58, Mar-2023 41, Sep-2023 61, Mar-2024 34, Sep-2024 66, Mar-2025 29. Its H1 CFO was negative in all three years and H2 CFO strongly positive. A peer shows Sep/Mar DSO of 48/42 consistently. Write a forensic note on whether this is seasonality or window dressing and list the disclosures you would check.

Q4. Margin interaction [L2]

A company keeps DSO 40, DIO 90 and DPO 75 days constant while its COGS/revenue rises from 55% to 62% due to input inflation (assume purchases equal COGS). Compute trade NWC % revenue before and after, and the cash impact on revenue of ₹10,000 crore.
