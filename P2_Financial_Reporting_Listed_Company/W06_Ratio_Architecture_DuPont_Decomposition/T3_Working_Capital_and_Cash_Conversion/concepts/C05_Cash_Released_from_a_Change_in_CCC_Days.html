Key concept

Cash Released from a Change in CCC Days

Concept: Cash released = ΔCCC days × Revenue (or COGS) ÷ 365

Statement

\[ \text{Cash released} \approx -\Delta \text{CCC} \times \frac{\text{Flow}}{365} \]

priced component by component:

\[ \text{Cash released} = -\left[\Delta\text{DSO} \times \frac{R}{365} + \Delta\text{DIO} \times \frac{\text{COGS}}{365} - \Delta\text{DPO} \times \frac{\text{Purchases or COGS}}{365}\right] \]

where:

  • \(\Delta\) = current-year days minus prior-year days (a negative ΔCCC releases cash).
  • \(R\) = revenue from operations; \(\text{COGS}\) and \(\text{Purchases}\) as used in the days calculations.
  • The flow used to price each component must be the one used to compute that component's days, and the current-year flow is used to isolate the "days" effect from the "growth" effect.

Explanation

Days are the language of operations; rupees are the language of the CFO and the treasurer. Converting a CCC improvement into cash shows its value — e.g., "a 5-day DSO reduction releases ₹150 crore, equal to 20% of annual free cash flow".

Why price components separately: one day of DSO is worth one day of revenue; one day of DIO or DPO is worth one day of COGS. For a company with a 40% material margin, a day of DSO is worth about 1.7 days of DIO. Multiplying total ΔCCC by revenue overstates the inventory and payables effects; multiplying by COGS understates the receivables effect. The concept's "Revenue (or COGS)" shorthand is acceptable only as a quick estimate when one component dominates.

Separating days effect from growth effect: the total change in working capital is

\[ \Delta \text{NWC} = \underbrace{\text{Days}_{t-1} \times \frac{\Delta \text{Flow}}{365}}_{\text{growth effect}} + \underbrace{\Delta\text{Days} \times \frac{\text{Flow}_t}{365}}_{\text{efficiency effect}} \]

Even with constant days, a growing company absorbs cash. The cash-released formula captures the efficiency effect only.

GST and balance basis: if days were computed on GST-inclusive balances over GST-exclusive flows (unadjusted), the rupee result is in GST-inclusive balance terms — the actual cash change. If days were GST-adjusted, multiply by \((1 + g)\) to get the actual cash effect on receivables and payables.

One-off vs recurring: cash released is a one-time balance-sheet release, not a recurring annual saving; its recurring value is the interest saved (cash released × cost of funding) and the reduced capital in ROIC.

Illustration

Illustrative FY2024-25 (₹ crore): revenue 12,000; COGS 7,000; purchases 7,200.

Component FY2023-24 days FY2024-25 days Δ days Priced on Cash effect (₹ crore)
DSO 44 39 −5 Revenue 12,000 +164 released
DIO 105 110 +5 COGS 7,000 −96 absorbed
DPO 85 80 −5 Purchases 7,200 −99 absorbed
CCC 64 69 +5 — −30 net absorbed

A naive "ΔCCC × revenue ÷ 365" would say ₹164 crore absorbed; "ΔCCC × COGS ÷ 365" would say ₹96 crore absorbed. The correct component-priced answer is about ₹30 crore absorbed. At a 7.5% pre-tax funding cost, the annual carrying cost is about ₹2 crore.

Practice Questions

Q1. Price a CCC change [L1]

Revenue ₹6,500 crore; COGS ₹3,900 crore; purchases ₹4,000 crore. DSO falls from 52 to 47 days, DIO rises from 70 to 74 days, DPO rises from 60 to 66 days. Compute the cash released or absorbed by each component and in total.

Q2. Growth vs efficiency [L2]

Revenue grew from ₹8,000 crore to ₹9,600 crore. DSO moved from 45 to 42 days (average balances). Split the change in average receivables into growth and efficiency effects and reconcile to the total change.

💡 Hint: Compute the prior-year days applied to the revenue change, then the days change applied to current revenue.

Q3. CFO presentation [L3]

Prepare a one-slide cash bridge for a CFO showing how a 6-day CCC improvement (DSO −4, DIO −3, DPO +1) translated into ₹ crore, separating the part that came from a new supplier finance programme (₹120 crore of DPO-equivalent) from organic improvement, for a company with revenue ₹15,000 crore and COGS ₹9,000 crore.
