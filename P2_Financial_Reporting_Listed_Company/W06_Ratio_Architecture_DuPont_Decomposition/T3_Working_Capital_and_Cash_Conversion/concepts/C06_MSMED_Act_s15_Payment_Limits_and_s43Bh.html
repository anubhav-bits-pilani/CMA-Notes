Key concept

MSMED Act s.15 Payment Limits and s.43B(h) Disallowance

Concept: MSMED Act s.15: pay within agreed term up to 45 days, else 15 days; s.43B(h) disallows unpaid amounts beyond limits

Statement

MSMED Act, 2006, s.15: where a supplier (a micro or small enterprise) supplies goods or renders services to a buyer, the buyer shall make payment on or before the date agreed in writing between them — which shall not exceed 45 days from the day of acceptance or deemed acceptance — or, where there is no written agreement, before the appointed day (the day following 15 days from acceptance or deemed acceptance).

Income-tax Act, 1961, s.43B(h) (inserted by Finance Act, 2023; from AY 2024-25): any sum payable by the assessee to a micro or small enterprise beyond the time limit specified in s.15 of the MSMED Act is allowed as a deduction only in the previous year in which it is actually paid.

Supporting provisions: s.16 MSMED (compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate on delayed payments); s.23 MSMED (such interest not deductible for income-tax); s.22 MSMED (disclosure of unpaid amounts and interest in the buyer's annual accounts).

Explanation

Acceptance and deemed acceptance. The clock starts on acceptance of goods/services; if the buyer raises no written objection within 15 days of delivery, the delivery date is the deemed acceptance date.

Who is protected. Micro and small enterprises (by Udyam classification); not medium enterprises. Traders' inclusion in the MSME definition (2021) was restricted to priority-sector lending, so they are generally outside the delayed-payment protection and s.43B(h). The buyer must verify supplier status.

Tax mechanics. At year-end, identify MSE dues outstanding beyond the s.15 limit; add them back in the tax computation; claim them in the year of payment. Under Ind AS 12, the timing difference creates a deferred tax asset, but current tax cash outflow rises in the year of disallowance. Interest under s.16 is an additional cost and is permanently non-deductible.

Accounting disclosure. Schedule III requires trade payables to show MSE dues separately, with ageing, plus the s.22 disclosures (principal and interest due and unpaid, interest paid with delays, interest accrued and unpaid).

Behavioural effect. From FY2023-24, companies paid MSE dues faster, re-negotiated terms to 45 days, onboarded TReDS, and in some cases moved purchases to medium/large suppliers — reducing MSE DPO and often blended DPO (s04).

Date sensitivity. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections; check where the equivalent provision sits for FY2026-27 onwards. MSME classification thresholds were revised from 1 April 2025.

Illustration

A company's MSE supplier delivers goods worth ₹50 lakh on 10 February 2025; written terms say 60 days; no objection is raised.

  • Deemed acceptance: 10 February 2025. The written term of 60 days exceeds the 45-day cap, so the effective limit is 45 days — payment due by 27 March 2025.
  • If paid on 15 April 2025, the ₹50 lakh is disallowed for FY2024-25 and allowed in FY2025-26.
  • Tax effect at 25.168%: ₹12.6 lakh higher current tax in FY2024-25, offset by a DTA reversing in FY2025-26.
  • s.16 interest: at a bank rate of, say, 6.5%, three times is 19.5% p.a., compounded monthly, for 19 days of delay — roughly ₹0.5 lakh, non-deductible.

Practice Questions

Q1. Due date and disallowance [L1]

An MSE supplier delivers services on 5 March 2025; there is no written agreement on credit terms; the buyer raises no objection. The invoice of ₹8 crore is paid on 10 April 2025. Determine the s.15 due date, whether s.43B(h) applies for FY2024-25, and the deferred tax consequence at 25.168%.

Q2. Supplier status [L2]

List the supplier categories for which s.43B(h) does and does not apply (micro manufacturer, small service provider, medium manufacturer, Udyam-registered wholesale trader, unregistered small manufacturer), and the documentation a buyer should hold at year-end.

💡 Hint: Protection depends on both the enterprise category and the nature of the registration.

Design a year-end close checklist for an FP&A/controllership team to quantify s.43B(h) exposure and Schedule III/s.22 disclosures, including data sources, cut-off for acceptance dates, interest computation and management review sign-offs.
