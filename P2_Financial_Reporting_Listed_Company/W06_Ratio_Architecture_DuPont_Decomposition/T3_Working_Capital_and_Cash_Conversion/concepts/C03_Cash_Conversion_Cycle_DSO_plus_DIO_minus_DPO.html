Key concept

Cash Conversion Cycle: DSO plus DIO minus DPO

Concept: CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO

Statement

\[ \text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} \]

where DSO, DIO and DPO are defined in concepts c01 and c02, computed on the same averaging convention and day count. The operating cycle is DSO + DIO; CCC is the operating cycle less the days of supplier financing.

Explanation

CCC measures the number of days between paying suppliers for inputs and collecting cash from customers — the period for which the company must finance its working capital from its own resources or from banks. A shorter (or negative) CCC means less capital tied up and lower funding needs.

Additive in days, not in rupees. DSO is on revenue while DIO and DPO are on COGS (or purchases), so the three day-counts have different rupee values per day. CCC is a time measure; to convert a CCC change into cash, price each component on its own denominator (concept c05).

Extended form for Indian business models:

\[ \text{CCC}_{\text{ext}} = \text{DSO}_{\text{total}} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} - \text{Days of customer advances} \]

for IT (unbilled and contract assets in DSO), real estate and jewellery (customer advances), and FMCG/QSR (negative CCC from cash sales and supplier credit).

Consistency rules (topic pitfalls): same averaging for all three components; DPO never on revenue; GST adjustments applied to both DSO and DPO or to neither; MSME, factoring and supplier-finance effects identified.

Interpretation benchmarks (indicative): FMCG and QSR negative to low; paints 30–70 days (dealer credit plus high inventory, offset by supplier credit); cement low (cash-and-carry trade sales, moderate inventory); capital goods and EPC very high (retentions, long project cycles); IT 60–100 days (no inventory, long DSO including unbilled).

Bank link: the operating cycle underpins the bank's working-capital assessment (Tandon/Nayak methods, drawing power) — a longer cycle means a larger working-capital gap to be financed.

Illustration

Illustrative paint companies, FY2024-25 (days, reported basis):

Metric Company A Company B Company C
DSO 28 42 55
DIO 95 110 85
DPO 80 75 70
Operating cycle 123 152 140
CCC 43 77 70

Company A's cycle is shortest despite high inventory, because dealers pay quickly and suppliers fund most inventory. Company C carries less inventory (an industrial-coatings mix with made-to-order supply) but gives longer credit to OEM customers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute CCC and operating cycle [L1]

Revenue ₹7,200 crore; COGS ₹4,300 crore; average trade receivables ₹890 crore; average inventory ₹1,250 crore; average trade payables ₹1,020 crore. Compute DSO, DIO, DPO (on COGS), operating cycle and CCC.

Q2. Extended CCC for a jeweller [L2]

A jeweller reports (₹ crore, averages): revenue 45,000; COGS 39,000; inventory 30,000; trade receivables 900; trade payables 1,200; customer advances (scheme balances) 4,500; gold metal loans 6,000. Compute standard and extended CCC, explain why gold metal loans are excluded from DPO, and comment on how the business funds its inventory.

💡 Hint: Express customer advances as days of revenue and keep debt-like items out of trade payables.
