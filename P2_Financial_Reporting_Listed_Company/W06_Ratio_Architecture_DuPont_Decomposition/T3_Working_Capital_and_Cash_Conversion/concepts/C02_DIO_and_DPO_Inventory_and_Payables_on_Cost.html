Key concept

DIO and DPO: Inventory and Payables on Cost Denominators

Concept: DIO = Average inventory ÷ COGS × 365; DPO = Average trade payables ÷ COGS (or purchases) × 365

Statement

\[ \text{DIO} = \frac{\overline{\text{Inv}}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365, \qquad \text{DPO} = \frac{\overline{\text{TP}}}{\text{COGS or Purchases}} \times 365 \]

where:

  • \(\overline{\text{Inv}}\) = average inventories (raw materials, WIP, finished goods, stock-in-trade, stores and spares, goods in transit), at cost or NRV per Ind AS 2.
  • \(\text{COGS}\) = materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories (Indian material COGS), or a full-cost COGS including conversion costs (state which).
  • \(\overline{\text{TP}}\) = average trade payables for goods and services (excluding capex creditors, statutory dues, employee dues, interest accrued).
  • \(\text{Purchases}\) = COGS + closing inventory − opening inventory (material purchases), optionally plus credit-purchased services that generate trade payables.

Explanation

Both inventory and payables are measured at cost, so both must be divided by cost flows, not revenue. Dividing by revenue understates days by the gross margin and makes the CCC inconsistent.

DIO nuances:

  • Indian material COGS excludes conversion costs while inventory is valued at absorption cost, so material-COGS DIO is overstated; full-cost COGS gives a lower, internationally comparable DIO.
  • Component DIO (raw-material days on materials consumed, finished-goods days on COGS) is more informative for operations.
  • Inventory write-downs to NRV lower DIO — a deceptively "better" ratio.

DPO nuances:

  • Purchases is the conceptually correct denominator: payables arise from purchases. When inventory is growing, purchases exceed COGS, and COGS-based DPO overstates days.
  • Trade payables include services (freight, advertising, contract labour, utilities in some cases). For service-heavy cost structures (cement freight and power, FMCG advertising), a broad purchases denominator including these costs avoids overstated DPO.
  • GST: payables include GST on purchases where ITC is available; purchases exclude it — divide by \((1 + g_p)\) for cross-company comparison (s02).
  • MSE payables are capped at 45 days (MSMED s.15; s.43B(h)) — decompose (s04).
  • Supplier finance can inflate DPO — strip extended-term balances (s05).

Interpretation: high DIO may reflect strategic stocking (ahead of price increases or the festive season), slow-moving stock, or a wide SKU range (paints: thousands of shade-pack combinations, partly mitigated by tinting machines). High DPO reflects bargaining power — or stress, if accompanied by rising overdue payables.

Illustration

Illustrative paint company (₹ crore): inventory ₹1,900 opening and ₹2,300 closing; material COGS ₹6,300 crore; conversion costs (power, stores, manufacturing wages, plant depreciation) ₹600 crore; trade payables ₹1,500 and ₹1,700.

  • Purchases = 6,300 + 2,300 − 1,900 = 6,700.
  • DIO (material COGS) = 2,100 ÷ 6,300 × 365 = 121.7 days; DIO (full-cost) = 2,100 ÷ 6,900 × 365 = 111.1 days.
  • DPO (COGS) = 1,600 ÷ 6,300 × 365 = 92.7 days; DPO (purchases) = 1,600 ÷ 6,700 × 365 = 87.2 days.

The choice of denominator moves each metric by 5–10 days.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute DIO and DPO [L1]

Materials consumed ₹3,800 crore; purchases of stock-in-trade ₹900 crore; changes in inventories ₹(150) crore (increase). Opening/closing inventories ₹1,100/1,250 crore; trade payables ₹950/1,050 crore. Compute DIO on COGS, DPO on COGS and DPO on purchases.

Q2. Revenue-denominator correction [L2]

A peer table shows "inventory days" 48 and "payable days" 40 computed on revenue for a company with material margin of 38%. Recompute both on COGS, and explain how the CCC changes.

💡 Hint: Revenue-based days equal COGS-based days multiplied by the COGS-to-revenue ratio.

Q3. Strategic inventory [L3]

In Q4 FY2024-25 a paint company built ₹400 crore of extra raw-material inventory ahead of an expected TiO2 price increase. Explain how this affects DIO, DPO (if the purchases are on 60-day credit), CCC and CFO for the year, and how you would present it in a CCC peer comparison.
