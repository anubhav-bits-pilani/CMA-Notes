Key concept

NWC as a Percentage of Revenue

Concept: NWC % revenue = (Trade receivables + Inventory − Trade payables ± other operating items) ÷ Revenue

Statement

\[ \text{NWC\%} = \frac{\text{TR} + \text{Inv} - \text{TP} + \text{OCA}_{\text{op}} - \text{OCL}_{\text{op}}}{R} \]

where:

  • \(\text{TR}\), \(\text{Inv}\), \(\text{TP}\) = trade receivables, inventories, trade payables (average for analysis; closing for forecast drivers).
  • \(\text{OCA}_{\text{op}}\) = other operating current assets: contract assets, unbilled receivables (if not in TR), advances to suppliers, GST input credit receivable, operating prepayments.
  • \(\text{OCL}_{\text{op}}\) = other operating current liabilities: contract liabilities and customer advances, operating provisions (warranty, current employee benefits), statutory dues payable, other operating payables.
  • \(R\) = revenue from operations (GST-exclusive).

Excluded: cash and cash equivalents, current investments, bank deposits, borrowings and current maturities, lease liabilities, interest accrued, capex creditors, dividends payable, and (usually) income-tax balances.

Explanation

NWC % revenue converts working-capital intensity into a single driver: the cash required per rupee of revenue. In a forecast:

\[ \Delta \text{NWC}_t = \text{NWC\%} \times R_t - \text{NWC\%}_{t-1} \times R_{t-1} \]

which, at a constant percentage, simplifies to \(\text{NWC\%} \times \Delta R\) — the cash cost of growth.

Why define "operating" carefully: including cash or short-term debt turns NWC into a liquidity measure and breaks the link to operations. Including capex creditors understates NWC in a capex year. The classification follows the Week 5 balance-sheet reclassification (operating vs financing vs non-operating).

Interaction with margins: trade NWC % revenue depends on days and on cost structure: \(\text{DIO} \times \text{COGS}/R\). Input inflation raises NWC % even with unchanged days.

Seasonality and timing: 31 March balances may not represent the year (s06). Use three-point averages with the Reg 33 half-yearly balance sheet for analysis, and test the peak-month requirement for funding.

Relation to Schedule III: the "net capital turnover ratio" (revenue ÷ average working capital) is broadly the reciprocal, though Schedule III working capital is typically total current assets − total current liabilities (including cash and short-term debt) — not the same as operating NWC.

Illustration

Illustrative FY2024-25 averages (₹ crore) for a paint company with revenue ₹11,500 crore:

Item ₹ crore
Trade receivables 1,250
Inventories 2,050
Trade payables (1,650)
Trade NWC 1,650
Advances to suppliers 120
GST input credit receivable 160
Contract liabilities (dealer advances) (90)
Statutory dues payable (230)
Operating provisions (140)
Operating NWC 1,470

Trade NWC % = 14.3%; operating NWC % = 12.8%. A 12% revenue growth plan (₹1,380 crore) consumes roughly ₹177 crore of working capital at 12.8%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both NWC measures [L1]

Average balances (₹ crore): trade receivables 640; inventories 910; trade payables 780; contract assets 60; customer advances 110; statutory dues 95; GST credit receivable 70; capex creditors 140; current investments 500. Revenue ₹5,200 crore. Compute trade and operating NWC % revenue, listing excluded items with reasons.

Q2. Cash cost of growth [L2]

A company with operating NWC of 18% of revenue plans revenue growth from ₹3,000 crore to ₹3,750 crore while improving NWC to 16%. Compute the change in NWC and the effect on CFO, and compare with the CFO effect if NWC % stayed at 18%.

💡 Hint: Apply each year's percentage to each year's revenue rather than the percentage to the revenue change.

Q3. Schedule III comparison [L2]

Explain why a company's Schedule III net capital turnover ratio may move in the opposite direction from its operating NWC % revenue in a year when it raised a large QIP held in liquid funds.
