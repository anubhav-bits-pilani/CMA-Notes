Subtopic note

Consolidated DuPont with Non-Controlling Interests

Scope: Consolidated DuPont with NCI: use owners' PAT and owners' equity; align asset base

Why it matters

Most Indian listed groups report consolidated financial statements with partly owned subsidiaries: paint majors with overseas subsidiaries and JVs, FMCG groups with listed subsidiaries, conglomerates with layered holdings. The ROE that matters to the parent's shareholders is owners' PAT over owners' equity. But consolidated revenue and total assets include 100% of every subsidiary, regardless of ownership. If the DuPont tree mixes consolidated totals with owners' figures carelessly, the product does not equal reported ROE — or worse, it equals ROE by accident while each component is mis-stated. Getting the NCI alignment right is a recurring interview question and a frequent error in sell-side models.

Core notes

The accounting background

  • Under Ind AS 110, the parent consolidates 100% of a subsidiary's assets, liabilities, income and expenses, and presents non-controlling interests within equity, separately from equity attributable to owners of the parent.
  • Ind AS 1.81B requires the statement of profit and loss to show profit (and total comprehensive income) attributable to (a) owners of the parent and (b) non-controlling interests.
  • Associates and joint ventures (Ind AS 28) are equity-accounted: one line in the balance sheet (investment) and one line in P&L (share of profit), with no revenue or asset gross-up.
  • EPS (Ind AS 33) is based on profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent.

The consistency problem

Consolidated revenue, EBIT and total assets are "100% figures" (belonging to all capital providers including NCI). Owners' PAT and owners' equity are "parent share" figures. The DuPont identity works as long as the cancelling terms are identical; the trick is to put the NCI adjustment in one explicit place.

Recommended structure: an explicit NCI factor

\[ \text{ROE}_{\text{owners}} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{PAT}^{\text{own}}}{\text{PAT}}}_{\text{NCI retention}} \times \frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{PBT}} \times \frac{\text{PBT}}{\text{EBIT}} \times \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Rev}} \times \frac{\text{Rev}}{\overline{\text{TA}}} \times \frac{\overline{\text{TA}}}{\overline{E}^{\text{own}}} \]

  • NCI retention = owners' share of consolidated PAT (e.g., 0.95 if NCI takes 5%).
  • Tax burden, interest burden, EBIT margin, asset turnover use 100% consolidated figures — operating economics of the whole group.
  • Financial leverage = average consolidated total assets ÷ average owners' equity. NCI equity is effectively treated as another source of funding, like liabilities: \(\overline{\text{TA}}/\overline{E}^{\text{own}} = 1 + (\overline{L} + \overline{\text{NCI}})/\overline{E}^{\text{own}}\).

The product equals owners' PAT ÷ average owners' equity exactly.

Alternative (fold into tax burden): use owners' PAT ÷ PBT as a combined "tax and minority burden". Shorter, but it hides whether the change came from tax or from the profit mix between wholly and partly owned subsidiaries. The explicit factor is preferable.

Associates and JVs

Share of profit of associates/JVs has no revenue or operating assets behind it in the consolidated statements (only the investment carrying amount sits in total assets). Options:

  1. Keep it in PBT, exclude from EBIT. Interest burden absorbs it (PBT ÷ EBIT rises). Simple but blurs interest burden.
  2. Separate "associate factor": (PBT including share of associates) ÷ (PBT excluding share of associates). Cleanest.
  3. Exclude from both and remove investment in associates from assets — a "core" DuPont; then reconcile to reported ROE.

Presentation of the share of associates varies in practice (some companies show it before "profit before tax", others after the tax line); read the P&L and apply your chosen treatment consistently.

The common mistakes

Mistake Result
Total PAT ÷ owners' equity Overstates ROE by the NCI share of profit
Owners' PAT ÷ total equity (incl. NCI) Understates ROE
Owners' PAT in margin, consolidated assets and total equity in leverage Product ≠ owners' ROE
Removing subsidiary assets proportionally ("proportionate consolidation") Not the reported basis; makes reconciliation impossible

Aligning the asset base

"Align the asset base" means:

  • Use consolidated total assets (100%) in both turnover and leverage — never a mix of standalone and consolidated, and never proportionately scaled assets.
  • Use the same averaging (two-point or three-point) in both.
  • If a subsidiary is acquired mid-year, its assets enter the average partially but its revenue only post-acquisition; flag the distortion or pro-forma.
  • For the Penman variant, separate operating and financial assets on the same consolidated basis.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • NCI losses: if NCI subsidiaries are loss-making, NCI retention exceeds 1 (owners' PAT above total PAT).
  • Negative NCI balances are possible under Ind AS 110 (losses attributed to NCI even if this results in a deficit balance).
  • Listed subsidiaries: when the parent owns, say, 51%, consider whether shareholders value the group via sum-of-the-parts instead; the consolidated DuPont still applies to the parent ROE.
  • NCI put options recognised as financial liabilities change equity and liabilities; follow the accounting presentation and note it.
  • Standalone vs consolidated choice: Schedule III ratios are standalone; equity research uses consolidated. Never mix the two in one tree.
  • Change in ownership without loss of control is an equity transaction (Ind AS 110), moving amounts between owners' equity and NCI with no P&L effect — it shifts the leverage factor.

Key takeaways

  • Consolidated ROE for the parent = owners' PAT ÷ average owners' equity.
  • Use 100% consolidated revenue, EBIT and total assets for operating factors; add an explicit NCI retention factor.
  • Leverage = consolidated total assets ÷ owners' equity, which treats NCI like a funding source.
  • Handle share of associates/JVs with a separate factor or exclude it consistently.
  • Never mix standalone and consolidated figures, or closing and average assets, in one tree.

Practice Questions

Q1. NCI-aligned tree [L2]

Consolidated FY2024-25 data (₹ crore): revenue 14,500; EBIT (excl. other income) 2,030; other income 120; finance cost 250; PBT 1,900; tax 480; PAT 1,420; NCI share 170. Average total assets 15,200; average owners' equity 8,300; average NCI 900. Build a six-factor DuPont with an explicit NCI retention factor and verify the product.

Q2. Find the error [L1]

An analyst's model shows: net margin = total PAT ÷ revenue; turnover = revenue ÷ average consolidated total assets; leverage = average consolidated total assets ÷ average total equity (incl. NCI). The product is compared with reported owners' ROE and differs. Explain which ratio should change and how, without changing the others.

💡 Hint: Find the single factor whose numerator or denominator must switch from "100%" to "owners" to restore the identity.

Q3. Associate-heavy group [L3]

A holding group's consolidated PBT of ₹5,000 crore includes ₹1,800 crore share of profit of equity-accounted associates; investment in associates is ₹14,000 crore of average total assets of ₹40,000 crore. Design a "core" DuPont that excludes associates and a bridge back to reported owners' ROE. Explain why the core tree is more useful for judging the operating subsidiaries.

Q4. Ownership change [L2]

During FY2024-25 a parent bought an additional 20% of a subsidiary from NCI for ₹600 crore (carrying amount of that NCI ₹350 crore). Explain the effect on owners' equity, NCI, the leverage factor and the NCI retention factor in the following year, assuming the subsidiary's profit is unchanged.
