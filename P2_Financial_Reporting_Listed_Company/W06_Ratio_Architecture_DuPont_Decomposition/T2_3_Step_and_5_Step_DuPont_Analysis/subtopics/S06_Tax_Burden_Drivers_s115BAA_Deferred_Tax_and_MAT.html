Subtopic note

Tax Burden Drivers: s.115BAA, Deferred Tax and MAT Credit

Scope: Tax burden drivers: s.115BAA migration, deferred tax remeasurement, MAT credit write-off

Why it matters

The tax burden (PAT ÷ PBT) is the DuPont component most likely to move for reasons unrelated to business performance. The Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 introduced s.115BAA, giving domestic companies an option to pay tax at 22% (25.168% with surcharge and cess) in exchange for giving up most deductions and exemptions and the MAT regime. Companies migrated in different years — some immediately in FY2019-20, others only after exhausting MAT credits or tax holidays — so peer tax burdens diverged and jumped in the migration year. The year of migration usually also carries a one-off deferred tax remeasurement and, for companies with unutilised MAT credit, a write-off. An analyst who credits a 400 bps ROE improvement to "efficiency" when it is a tax regime change commits one of this topic's named pitfalls.

Core notes

Rates (domestic companies, Income-tax Act, 1961)

Regime Base rate Surcharge Cess Effective rate
Normal provisions, total income > ₹10 crore 30% 12% 4% 34.944%
Normal provisions, turnover/gross receipts ≤ ₹400 crore in the reference year (rate per annual Finance Act), income > ₹10 crore 25% 12% 4% 29.120%
s.115BAA (any domestic company opting) 22% 10% (flat) 4% 25.168%
s.115BAB (new manufacturing companies; sunset for commencement by 31 March 2024) 15% 10% 4% 17.160%
MAT under s.115JB (not applicable to s.115BAA/115BAB companies) 15% of book profit 12% (if > ₹10 crore) 4% 17.472%

Rates are as per the Income-tax Act, 1961 and Finance Acts up to FY2025-26. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act for tax years beginning 1 April 2026 and renumbers the provisions; the concessional regime is carried forward, but verify the new section references and any rate changes for the period you analyse.

Key s.115BAA features:

  • Option exercised by filing the prescribed form on or before the due date of the return; once exercised for a year it cannot be withdrawn for that or subsequent years.
  • Total income computed without specified deductions/exemptions (e.g., s.10AA SEZ, s.80-IA, s.80-IB, additional depreciation under s.32(1)(iia), s.35(2AB) weighted deduction); carried-forward losses attributable to those deductions cannot be set off.
  • MAT (s.115JB) does not apply, and MAT credit carried forward cannot be utilised — which is why many companies deferred migration until MAT credit was consumed.

Three tax-burden drivers in the migration year

1. Current tax rate change. Current tax on taxable profit drops from ~34.944% (or MAT at 17.472% for MAT-paying companies) to 25.168%. For an old-regime full-rate taxpayer, tax burden rises from ~0.65 to ~0.75 — a ~15% rise in ROE with no operational change.

2. Deferred tax remeasurement. Ind AS 12 measures deferred tax at the rates expected to apply when the temporary differences reverse, based on rates enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date. When a company decides (or is expected) to opt for s.115BAA, it remeasures its DTA/DTL at 25.168%. ICAI's FAQ on the accounting implications of s.115BAA indicates the remeasurement effect is recognised in the period the company decides to opt — in P&L except to the extent it relates to items recognised in OCI or equity (Ind AS 12 backward tracing).

  • Net DTL company (typical manufacturer with accelerated tax depreciation): DTL falls → one-off deferred tax credit → tax burden temporarily above the new run-rate.
  • Net DTA company: DTA falls → one-off deferred tax charge → tax burden temporarily below run-rate.

3. MAT credit write-off. Unutilised MAT credit is recognised as a deferred tax asset (under Ind AS it is presented within deferred tax assets). On opting for s.115BAA it can never be utilised, so it is written off as a tax expense — a one-off charge that depresses the tax burden in the migration year. Companies with large MAT credits (e.g., power, infra, companies with s.80-IA holidays) often delayed opting until credits were utilised, creating a multi-year transition.

Illustration (illustrative, ₹ crore)

A manufacturer opts for s.115BAA in FY2024-25. PBT (both years) ₹1,000 crore; no permanent differences except as noted.

Item FY2023-24 (old regime) FY2024-25 (migration) FY2025-26 (run-rate)
Current tax 349 252 252
Deferred tax remeasurement (net DTL ₹400 crore at 34.944% → 25.168%) — (112) credit —
MAT credit write-off — 60 —
Total tax 349 200 252
PAT 651 800 748
Tax burden 0.651 0.800 0.748

DTL remeasurement: \(400 \times (1 - 25.168/34.944) = 400 \times 0.2798 \approx 112\).

Holding all else equal, ROE in FY2024-25 is 22.9% higher than FY2023-24 (0.800 ÷ 0.651 − 1); the sustainable improvement is 14.9% (0.748 ÷ 0.651 − 1). The remaining ~8 percentage points of the uplift is one-off. In the DuPont narrative, report: "Tax burden improved from 0.65 to 0.80, of which 0.10 reflects the lower statutory rate (recurring) and 0.05 net one-off items (DTL remeasurement credit ₹112 crore less MAT credit write-off ₹60 crore)."

Other tax-burden drivers to scan

  • Tax holidays expiring (SEZ s.10AA phase-outs) — rising effective rate.
  • Tax on other income at different rates — dividend income taxable in the hands of the company post-2020; long-term capital gains at special rates.
  • Non-deductible expenses: CSR spend under s.135 Companies Act is not deductible (Explanation 2 to s.37(1)); s.43B(h) MSME disallowances (temporary differences); penalties.
  • Prior-year tax adjustments ("tax relating to earlier years") — reversals of provisions after assessments.
  • Unrecognised DTA on losses of subsidiaries in consolidated accounts — group effective rate above statutory.
  • Labour Codes and other one-offs affecting PBT but not tax proportionally.
  • Uncertain tax treatments under Ind AS 12 Appendix C provisions.

Analytical practice

  1. Read the tax note's effective tax rate reconciliation (Ind AS 12.81(c)): it lists the statutory rate, the effect of rate changes, non-deductible items and prior-year adjustments.
  2. Build a normalised tax burden = 1 − statutory rate adjusted for recurring permanent differences only.
  3. Compute DuPont on reported and normalised tax burden; attribute ROE change on the normalised basis (s05).
  4. Disclose one-offs in the narrative with ₹ amounts.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Decision timing: if the board resolves to opt for s.115BAA from next year, the remeasurement may fall in the current year's accounts — tax burden moves before the rate does.
  • Partially migrated groups: in consolidated DuPont, some subsidiaries may be on the old regime; the group rate is a mix.
  • MAT credit partially written off: some companies recognised MAT credit only to the extent of probable utilisation; check the DTA note.
  • Loss-making entities: tax burden is not meaningful; use pre-tax analysis.
  • Foreign subsidiaries: different statutory rates (and Pillar Two top-up taxes where applicable) affect consolidated tax burden.
  • Interaction with WACC: the lower rate reduces the debt tax shield; post-tax cost of debt rises (Week 15).

Key takeaways

  • s.115BAA offers 25.168% all-in but removes most deductions, MAT, and the use of MAT credit; the option is irreversible.
  • The migration year contains three effects: lower current tax (recurring), deferred tax remeasurement (one-off, sign depends on net DTL/DTA), and MAT credit write-off (one-off charge).
  • Use the Ind AS 12 tax rate reconciliation to separate recurring from one-off effects.
  • Attribute ROE changes on a normalised tax burden; never call a tax-driven uplift "efficiency".
  • Statutory references are date-sensitive: the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026.

Practice Questions

Q1. Migration-year tax burden [L2]

A company with PBT of ₹2,400 crore in each of FY2023-24 and FY2024-25 was taxed at 34.944% in FY2023-24. In FY2024-25 it opts for s.115BAA; it has a net DTL of ₹650 crore (at 34.944%) before remeasurement and unutilised MAT credit of ₹140 crore. Compute the tax burden for both years and the run-rate tax burden, and quantify the one-off component.

Q2. Net DTA company [L2]

A company with a net DTA of ₹300 crore (measured at 34.944%, mainly on provisions and s.43B items) opts for s.115BAA. Compute the remeasurement effect and explain the direction of the effect on tax burden and on ROE in the migration year.

💡 Hint: Apply the ratio of new to old rates to the carrying amount, and consider which way the asset moves.

Q3. Reading an effective tax rate reconciliation [L2]

A tax note shows: PBT ₹1,500 crore; tax at statutory rate 25.168% = ₹377.5 crore; effect of non-deductible CSR ₹8 crore; income taxed at lower rates (LTCG) (₹6 crore); tax relating to earlier years (₹40 crore); unrecognised DTA on subsidiary losses ₹22 crore; total tax ₹361.5 crore. Compute reported and normalised tax burden and state which items you treat as recurring.

Q4. Case: the "efficiency" claim [L3]

A company's annual report claims "ROE improved from 17.2% to 20.8% reflecting our operational excellence programme". Its DuPont components show tax burden moving from 0.66 to 0.79, interest burden 0.93 to 0.94, EBIT margin 13.0% to 12.6%, asset turnover 1.22× to 1.20× and leverage unchanged. Write a 200-word analyst note evaluating the claim, including an attribution of the ROE change.
