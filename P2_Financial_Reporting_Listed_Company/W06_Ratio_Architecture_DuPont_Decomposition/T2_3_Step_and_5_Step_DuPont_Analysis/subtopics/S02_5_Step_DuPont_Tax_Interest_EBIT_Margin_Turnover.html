Subtopic note

5-Step DuPont: Tax, Interest, EBIT Margin, Turnover, Leverage

Scope: 5-step decomposition: tax burden, interest burden, EBIT margin, asset turnover, financial leverage

Why it matters

The 3-step tree lumps tax, interest and operating profitability together inside net margin. The 5-step (extended) DuPont splits net margin into three pieces so you can see whether ROE moved because the business got better at operating (EBIT margin), because financing costs changed (interest burden) or because the tax rate changed (tax burden). For Indian companies this matters acutely: the s.115BAA migration from 2019-20 moved effective tax rates by 5–10 percentage points for many companies; debt-funded capex cycles in cement and chemicals swing interest burden; and cash-rich paint and IT companies have interest burdens above 1 because other income exceeds finance cost. The week's exercise — 5-step DuPont for Asian Paints, Berger Paints and Kansai Nerolac — and the Saturday case both depend on getting this tree right.

Core notes

The identity

\[ \text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{PBT}}}_{\text{Tax burden}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{PBT}}{\text{EBIT}}}_{\text{Interest burden}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{EBIT margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\overline{\text{TA}}}}_{\text{Asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\overline{\text{TA}}}{\overline{E}}}_{\text{Financial leverage}} \]

The first three terms multiply to PAT ÷ Revenue (net margin), so the 5-step is a refinement of the 3-step, not a different model.

The five components

Component Formula Value range Reads as
Tax burden PAT ÷ PBT Usually 0.65–0.80 in India (1 − effective tax rate) Share of pre-tax profit kept after tax; higher is better
Interest burden PBT ÷ EBIT ≤ 1 if EBIT includes other income; can exceed 1 if it excludes it Share of operating profit left after financing; higher is better
EBIT margin EBIT ÷ Revenue Sector-specific Operating profitability
Asset turnover Revenue ÷ Average total assets Sector-specific Asset efficiency
Financial leverage (equity multiplier) Average total assets ÷ Average equity ≥ 1 Balance-sheet gearing (all liabilities)

Interpretation of burdens. "Burden" ratios are retention ratios: a tax burden of 0.75 means 25% of PBT goes in tax. A higher burden ratio is better for shareholders. Under s.115BAA the statutory all-in rate is 25.168%, so a company fully on the new regime with no permanent differences has a tax burden near 0.748; an old-regime company with 12% surcharge faces 34.944% and a burden near 0.65, partly offset by deductions and MAT dynamics.

Defining EBIT — the critical choice

Because PBT = EBIT − finance cost (+ other income if excluded from EBIT) ± exceptional items, the definition of EBIT determines the interest burden:

Convention EBIT definition Interest burden formula Effect for net-cash company
A. Reported PBT + finance costs (other income included) (EBIT − finance cost) ÷ EBIT Always ≤ 1
B. Operating PBT + finance costs − other income (EBIT − finance cost + other income) ÷ EBIT Can be > 1

Convention A is mathematically tidy but buries treasury income inside "operating" margin. Convention B keeps EBIT margin truly operating but pushes the treasury effect into the interest burden, which then reads as a "financial" benefit — economically accurate for a net-cash company. Subtopic s03 explores this in depth. Whichever you choose, apply it to every peer and every year and state it in the ratio dictionary.

Exceptional items must also be placed. Options: (i) use PBT before exceptional items and PAT before exceptional items (normalised DuPont), with a separate reconciliation to reported ROE; or (ii) include them and let the interest burden absorb them (distorting). Option (i) is preferred for diagnostics; reported ROE must still reconcile in a separate bridge.

=TaxBurden*InterestBurden*EBITMargin*AssetTurnover*FinLeverage-ROE

DuPont Check :=
[Tax Burden] * [Interest Burden] * [EBIT Margin] * [Asset Turnover] * [Financial Leverage]
    - [ROE]

A conditional format that turns the check cell red if its absolute value exceeds 0.0001 catches mixed averaging immediately.

Reading the 5-step tree

  • EBIT margin × asset turnover = EBIT ÷ average total assets = a pre-tax operating return on assets (close to, but not equal to, ROCE because the denominator is total assets, not capital employed).
  • Interest burden × financial leverage together show whether leverage helps: if borrowing raises the multiplier more than it lowers the interest burden, leverage is accretive to ROE.
  • Tax burden changes are usually policy- or one-off-driven (s06); strip them out before crediting management.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Loss years: PBT negative and PAT negative gives a positive tax burden above 1 or other odd values; the decomposition is not interpretable — report "n.m." and analyse EBIT margin and turnover only.
  • Tax credit years: deferred tax credit from remeasurement can make tax burden above 1.
  • Share of associates/JVs: in consolidated P&L it can appear above or below PBT depending on presentation; include it consistently (e.g., as an extra "associate" factor or within PBT) and do not include it in EBIT, because it has no revenue behind it.
  • Lease interest: post-Ind AS 116 finance costs include lease interest; for retailers this depresses the interest burden. Compare peers on the same lease basis.
  • Capitalised interest (Ind AS 23) during construction flatters the interest burden in capex years.
  • Financial leverage label: some texts use average assets ÷ average equity; others closing; keep averages throughout.

Key takeaways

  • 5-step DuPont splits net margin into tax burden × interest burden × EBIT margin.
  • Burden ratios are retention ratios — higher is better.
  • The EBIT definition (with or without other income) decides whether interest burden can exceed 1; choose, document and apply consistently.
  • Normalise exceptional items and reconcile to reported ROE separately.
  • Always include a product-equals-ROE check row.
  • EBIT margin × turnover is the operating engine; interest burden × leverage is the financing engine; tax burden is policy.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the 5-step tree [L1]

A cement company reports (₹ crore, FY2024-25): revenue from operations 16,000; EBIT (excluding other income) 2,240; other income 160; finance costs 520; no exceptional items; tax 470. Average total assets 22,000; average equity 12,500. Compute all five components under the operating convention, verify the product equals ROE, and then recompute under the reported convention.

Q2. Is leverage accretive? [L2]

Company M has EBIT margin 14%, asset turnover 1.1×, tax burden 0.75, interest burden 0.92 and leverage 1.6×. It plans to borrow ₹1,000 crore at 9.5% to fund an asset expansion that keeps turnover and EBIT margin unchanged. Current revenue is ₹8,800 crore, average total assets ₹8,000 crore, average equity ₹5,000 crore. Estimate the new interest burden, leverage and ROE, and explain whether the borrowing is accretive.

💡 Hint: Compute the new EBIT and interest in rupees first, then rebuild each ratio.

Q3. Normalised vs reported ROE bridge [L2]

A company's reported PAT of ₹980 crore includes an exceptional gain on sale of land of ₹240 crore (tax on it ₹55 crore). Revenue ₹7,500 crore; EBIT excluding other income and exceptional items ₹1,080 crore; other income ₹90 crore; finance costs ₹60 crore; tax on normal profit ₹278 crore; average total assets ₹6,200 crore; average equity ₹4,400 crore. Construct the normalised 5-step tree and a bridge from normalised ROE to reported ROE.

Q4. Interview: why five steps [L1]

"Why would you present a 5-step rather than a 3-step DuPont to the CFO of a company that just moved to the s.115BAA regime?" Answer in five bullet points.
