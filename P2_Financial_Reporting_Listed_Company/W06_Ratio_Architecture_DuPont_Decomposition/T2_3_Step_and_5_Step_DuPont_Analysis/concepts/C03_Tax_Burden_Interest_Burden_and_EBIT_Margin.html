Key concept

Tax Burden, Interest Burden and EBIT Margin

Concept: Tax burden = PAT ÷ PBT; Interest burden = PBT ÷ EBIT; EBIT margin = EBIT ÷ Revenue

Statement

\[ \text{TB} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{PBT}} = 1 - \text{ETR}, \qquad \text{IB} = \frac{\text{PBT}}{\text{EBIT}}, \qquad \text{EM} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{R} \]

\[ \text{TB} \times \text{IB} \times \text{EM} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{R} = \text{Net margin} \]

where:

  • \(\text{ETR}\) = effective tax rate = total tax expense (current + deferred) ÷ PBT.
  • \(\text{EBIT}\) = PBT + finance costs (reported convention, other income inside) or PBT + finance costs − other income (operating convention).
  • \(R\) = revenue from operations.

Explanation

The three factors split net margin into policy (tax), financing (interest) and operations (EBIT margin).

Tax burden. For a company on s.115BAA with few permanent differences, TB ≈ 1 − 0.25168 = 0.748. Under the old regime with 12% surcharge, TB ≈ 0.651 (34.944%), or higher if deductions apply. Deviations come from deferred tax remeasurement, MAT credit write-offs, tax on income at special rates, non-deductible items (CSR), prior-year adjustments and unrecognised DTA on losses. Read the Ind AS 12.81(c) reconciliation to separate recurring from one-off items.

Interest burden. Under the reported convention:

\[ \text{IB} = 1 - \frac{\text{Finance cost}}{\text{EBIT}} = 1 - \frac{1}{\text{Interest coverage}} \]

so IB is a transformation of interest coverage (topic t4). Under the operating convention:

\[ \text{IB} = 1 + \frac{\text{Other income} - \text{Finance cost}}{\text{EBIT}_{\text{op}}} \]

which exceeds 1 for a net lender. Exceptional items should be excluded from PBT for a normalised tree; if they are included, IB absorbs them.

EBIT margin. The operating profitability factor. Under the operating convention it excludes treasury income and is comparable across cash-rich and leveraged peers; under the reported convention it is inflated for cash-rich firms.

Higher is better for all three. A falling EBIT margin with rising TB (from a regime change) can leave net margin unchanged while the business weakens — the reason to decompose.

Peer 2 has the higher net margin despite the weaker operating margin — thanks to tax regime and treasury income. An analyst reading only net margin would rank them the wrong way on operating quality.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the burdens [L1]

PBT ₹2,150 crore; tax ₹545 crore; finance cost ₹95 crore; other income ₹245 crore; revenue ₹14,000 crore. Compute TB, IB and EM under both EBIT conventions and confirm that their product equals net margin in both cases.

Q2. From interest coverage to interest burden [L2]

A company's interest coverage (EBIT ÷ finance cost, reported convention) falls from 8.0× to 4.0× after a debt-funded acquisition. Compute the interest burden in both years and the percentage change in ROE attributable to this factor alone.

💡 Hint: Use the identity linking IB to interest coverage under the reported convention.

Q3. Normalising the tax burden [L2]

A company's ETR was 18.5% in FY2024-25 because of a ₹70 crore deferred tax credit on remeasurement after opting for s.115BAA; PBT was ₹1,100 crore. Compute the reported and normalised tax burdens and explain how each would appear in a 5-step DuPont presented to an investment committee.
