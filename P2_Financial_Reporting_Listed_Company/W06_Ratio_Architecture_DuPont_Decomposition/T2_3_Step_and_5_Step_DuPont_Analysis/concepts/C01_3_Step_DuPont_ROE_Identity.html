Key concept

3-Step DuPont ROE Identity

Concept: ROE (3-step) = (PAT ÷ Revenue) × (Revenue ÷ Average assets) × (Average assets ÷ Average equity)

Statement

\[ \text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{PAT}}{R}}_{\text{Net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{R}{\overline{A}}}_{\text{Asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\overline{A}}{\overline{E}}}_{\text{Equity multiplier}} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\overline{E}} \]

where:

  • \(\text{PAT}\) = profit after tax (owners' share in consolidated analysis, with an NCI factor if needed — see s04).
  • \(R\) = revenue from operations for the year.
  • \(\overline{A}\) = average total assets = \((A_{t-1} + A_t)/2\).
  • \(\overline{E}\) = average equity (owners' equity in consolidated analysis).

Explanation

The identity is algebraically trivial — revenue and average assets cancel — but analytically powerful because each factor has a distinct economic driver:

  • Net margin reflects pricing power and cost control, and also financing and tax (which the 5-step version separates).
  • Asset turnover reflects capital intensity and working-capital efficiency.
  • Equity multiplier reflects how much of the asset base is funded by liabilities of every kind (debt, leases, trade payables, provisions).

The first two together give ROA (PAT basis). So ROE = ROA × equity multiplier: leverage amplifies asset returns into equity returns.

Conditions for the identity to hold exactly: the same PAT, revenue, average assets and average equity must appear wherever the term recurs. Mixing closing assets in turnover with average assets in the multiplier breaks the cancellation; databases that mix conventions show a "DuPont ROE" that differs from reported ROE.

Limits: the 3-step tree cannot tell whether leverage is value-accretive (interest is already inside net margin), cannot separate tax effects, and is distorted by large cash balances (which lower turnover and raise net margin through other income).

Illustration

Illustrative FY2024-25 (₹ crore):

Item Value
Revenue from operations 12,600
PAT 1,512
Total assets 31 Mar 2024 / 2025 9,400 / 11,600
Equity 31 Mar 2024 / 2025 6,300 / 7,700

Net margin = 12.0%; asset turnover = 12,600 ÷ 10,500 = 1.20×; equity multiplier = 10,500 ÷ 7,000 = 1.50×. ROE = 12.0% × 1.20 × 1.50 = 21.6% = 1,512 ÷ 7,000. ✓

If an analyst instead used closing assets in turnover (12,600 ÷ 11,600 = 1.086×) but kept the average-based multiplier, the product would be 19.6% — wrong by 200 bps.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the identity [L1]

Revenue ₹3,200 crore; PAT ₹224 crore; total assets ₹2,700 crore (opening) and ₹3,100 crore (closing); equity ₹1,250 crore and ₹1,450 crore. Compute the three components and verify that the product equals ROE.

Q2. Reverse-engineer a peer [L2]

A peer has ROE of 18%, net margin of 6% and an equity multiplier of 2.2×. Compute its implied asset turnover. If its average total assets are ₹5,000 crore, compute its implied revenue and PAT, and comment on what kind of business model these numbers suggest.

💡 Hint: Rearrange the identity to solve for the missing factor first.

Q3. Leverage illusion [L2]

Explain, with a numerical example of your own, how a company can increase its equity multiplier and its ROE simultaneously while its ROA falls, and why the 3-step tree alone cannot tell you whether this is good for shareholders.
