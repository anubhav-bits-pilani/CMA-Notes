Key concept

5-Step DuPont ROE Identity

Concept: ROE (5-step) = (PAT ÷ PBT) × (PBT ÷ EBIT) × (EBIT ÷ Revenue) × (Revenue ÷ Assets) × (Assets ÷ Equity)

Statement

\[ \text{ROE} = \frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{PBT}} \times \frac{\text{PBT}}{\text{EBIT}} \times \frac{\text{EBIT}}{R} \times \frac{R}{\overline{A}} \times \frac{\overline{A}}{\overline{E}} \]

where:

  • \(\text{PAT}\) = profit after tax; \(\text{PBT}\) = profit before tax (state whether before or after exceptional items).
  • \(\text{EBIT}\) = earnings before interest and tax, defined either as PBT + finance costs (including other income) or PBT + finance costs − other income (operating EBIT); the choice must be stated.
  • \(R\) = revenue from operations.
  • \(\overline{A}\), \(\overline{E}\) = average total assets and average equity (same averages in both places).

Explanation

The 5-step (extended) DuPont splits the 3-step net margin into three factors whose product is PAT ÷ Revenue:

  • Tax burden (PAT ÷ PBT): share of pre-tax profit retained after tax — reflects the tax regime (s.115BAA at 25.168% vs old regime at up to 34.944%), deferred tax remeasurement and one-offs.
  • Interest burden (PBT ÷ EBIT): share of operating profit left after financing — below 1 for borrowers, possibly above 1 for net-cash companies if EBIT excludes other income.
  • EBIT margin (EBIT ÷ Revenue): operating profitability before financing and tax.

The two remaining factors — asset turnover and financial leverage — are the same as in the 3-step tree.

Why it is more informative: it separates the operating engine (EBIT margin × turnover = EBIT ÷ average assets) from financing (interest burden × leverage) and from tax policy (tax burden). It shows whether leverage is accretive: more debt raises leverage but lowers interest burden; ROE rises only if the first effect dominates, i.e., if the pre-tax return on assets exceeds the cost of borrowing.

Conditions: positive PBT and EBIT for interpretable burdens; consistent EBIT and exceptional-item conventions across peers and years; identical averages in turnover and leverage. "Assets" and "Equity" in the concept text mean average balances in practice — if closing balances are used, they must be closing in both places.

Illustration

Illustrative FY2024-25 data for a paint company (₹ crore): revenue 9,000; EBIT (operating, excl. other income) 1,350; other income 140; finance cost 90; PBT 1,400; tax 352; PAT 1,048; average total assets 7,200; average equity 4,800.

Factor Computation Value
Tax burden 1,048 ÷ 1,400 0.7486
Interest burden 1,400 ÷ 1,350 1.0370
EBIT margin 1,350 ÷ 9,000 15.00%
Asset turnover 9,000 ÷ 7,200 1.250×
Financial leverage 7,200 ÷ 4,800 1.500×
ROE Product 21.83%

Check: 1,048 ÷ 4,800 = 21.83%. ✓ The interest burden above 1 indicates other income (140) exceeds finance cost (90).

Practice Questions

Q1. Build and check [L1]

Revenue ₹5,600 crore; EBIT (including other income) ₹840 crore; finance cost ₹120 crore; tax ₹181 crore; average total assets ₹6,000 crore; average equity ₹3,000 crore. Compute the five factors, the product, and verify against PAT ÷ average equity.

Q2. Accretive leverage test [L2]

Using the illustration's company, assume it borrows an additional ₹1,200 crore (average) at 8.5% and invests in assets that earn the same EBIT ÷ average assets as the existing business. Compute the new five factors and ROE, and state the condition under which leverage is accretive in terms of EBIT ÷ assets versus the borrowing cost.

💡 Hint: Express interest burden in terms of EBIT ÷ assets, interest rate and debt ÷ assets.

Q3. Interview: burdens [L1]

"Is a higher interest burden ratio good or bad?" Answer in under 80 words and explain how it can exceed 1.
