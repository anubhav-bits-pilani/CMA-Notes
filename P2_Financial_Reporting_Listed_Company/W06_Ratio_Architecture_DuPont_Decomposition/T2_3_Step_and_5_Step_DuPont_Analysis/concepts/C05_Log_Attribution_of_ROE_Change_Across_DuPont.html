Key concept

Log Attribution of ROE Change Across DuPont Factors

Concept: Log attribution: ln(ROE_t ÷ ROE_t−1) = Σ ln(component_t ÷ component_t−1)

Statement

For ROE expressed as a product of \(k\) positive components \(x_1, \ldots, x_k\):

\[ \ln\frac{\text{ROE}_t}{\text{ROE}_{t-1}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \ln\frac{x_{i,t}}{x_{i,t-1}} \]

The basis-point contribution of component \(i\) to the change in ROE is:

\[ \text{Effect}_i = \frac{\ln(x_{i,t} / x_{i,t-1})}{\ln(\text{ROE}_t / \text{ROE}_{t-1})} \times (\text{ROE}_t - \text{ROE}_{t-1}) \]

where \(t\) is the current year, \(t-1\) the prior year, and the components are the 3-step or 5-step DuPont factors (or extended trees with NCI and treasury factors).

Explanation

Because DuPont is multiplicative, changes in factors interact: a 10% fall in margin and a 10% rise in turnover do not offset exactly in arithmetic terms. Taking logarithms turns the product into a sum, so each factor's log-change is an additive, order-independent contribution to the log-change in ROE. Scaling those contributions to the arithmetic ΔROE (the second formula) gives basis-point effects that sum exactly to ΔROE.

Advantages over sequential substitution:

  • No dependence on the order in which factors are substituted.
  • Symmetric treatment of interaction effects.
  • Log-changes add across years, so multi-year bridges are simply the sum of annual log effects.

Conditions and limitations:

  • Every component and both ROEs must be strictly positive. Losses, negative equity, or an interest burden that changes sign make the method unusable — fall back on sequential substitution or Shapley averaging.
  • When ROE barely changes, \(\ln(\text{ROE}_t / \text{ROE}_{t-1})\) is near zero and the scaled effects become very large and offsetting. Present component log-changes (approximately percentage changes) instead of basis points.
  • Definitions must be identical in both years; restate for changes such as Ind AS 116 adoption or a new EBIT convention.
  • Strip one-offs (e.g., s.115BAA deferred tax remeasurement) before attribution if the purpose is to explain recurring performance.

For small changes, \(\ln(1 + g) \approx g\), so log effects approximate percentage changes in each factor; the sum of percentage changes approximates the percentage change in ROE.

Illustration

Illustrative 3-step components:

Factor FY2023-24 FY2024-25 ln ratio Share Effect (bps)
Net margin 12.0% 11.0% −0.08701 1.3221 −212
Asset turnover 1.40× 1.30× −0.07411 1.1261 −181
Equity multiplier 1.50× 1.65× 0.09531 −1.4483 +233
ROE 25.20% 23.60% −0.06581 1.0000 −160

Calculation: ROE FY2023-24 = 0.12 × 1.40 × 1.50 = 25.20%; FY2024-25 = 0.11 × 1.30 × 1.65 = 23.595%; ΔROE ≈ −160 bps. Shares are the log ratios divided by −0.06581. Higher leverage offset most of the operating decline — a less comforting story than the small headline change suggests.

Practice Questions

Q1. Five-factor log attribution [L2]

FY2023-24: TB 0.70, IB 0.95, EBIT margin 15.0%, turnover 1.20×, leverage 1.60×. FY2024-25: TB 0.75, IB 0.97, EBIT margin 14.0%, turnover 1.25×, leverage 1.55×. Compute both ROEs and allocate the change in basis points to each factor using the log method. Confirm the effects sum to ΔROE.

Q2. Multi-year chaining [L2]

ROE components for three years are given: net margin 10%, 11%, 12%; turnover 1.5×, 1.4×, 1.35×; multiplier 1.8×, 1.8×, 1.9×. Compute the log contribution of each factor for each year-on-year change and for the two-year change, and show that the annual log contributions sum to the two-year contribution.

💡 Hint: Work with log ratios first and convert to basis points only at the end.

Q3. Near-zero change [L3]

ROE moved from 18.00% to 18.05% while net margin fell 8% and turnover rose 8.6%. Explain what happens to the basis-point log attribution in this case and recommend how to present the analysis to a CFO.
