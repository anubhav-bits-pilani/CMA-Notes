Roadmap topic: Sector-Specific KPIs: Retail (SSSG, revenue/sq.ft), IT Services (utilization, attrition, deal TCV), Manufacturing (capacity utilization, EBITDA/ton)

Overview

Generic ratios — margins, turnover, ROE, CCC — tell you what happened to a company's returns; sector KPIs tell you why. A retailer's EBITDA margin moves because same-store sales, revenue per square foot or the store mix changed; an IT company's EBIT margin moves because of utilisation, attrition-driven wage inflation, currency and deal flow; a cement company's ROCE moves because of utilisation, realisation per tonne and the cost of power, fuel and freight. Equity research, credit analysis, FP&A budgeting and investor relations in India all operate on these KPIs: management guides on them, analysts forecast them, and boards review them monthly. For the Week 6 exercise on Asian Paints, Berger and Kansai Nerolac, the paints KPIs (volume vs value growth, dealer network, tinting machines, A&P) explain much of what the 5-step DuPont shows.

The topic moves sector by sector, then to sourcing. Retail (s01) covers SSSG/LFL, revenue and EBITDA per square foot, store additions, trading area and pre-Ind AS 116 store economics. Jewellery (s02), a special case of retail, adds gold price pass-through, the studded ratio, franchise versus owned stores, gold metal loans and Ind AS 109 hedging. IT services (s03) covers constant-currency growth, utilisation, LTM attrition, TCV and large-deal TCV, client buckets and revenue per FTE. Manufacturing (s04) covers capacity utilisation, volume growth, realisation, EBITDA and cost per tonne. Paints and FMCG (s05) covers the volume–price–mix split, price hikes, dealer networks and tinting machines, and A&P intensity. Finally, s06 maps where each KPI is found — investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, Reg 33 results and RHP KPI sections — and how reliable each source is.

The concepts pin down the definitions that most often cause comparability errors: SSSG and the comparable-store base (c01), revenue per sq.ft and EBITDA per tonne (c02), IT and manufacturing utilisation (c03), constant-currency growth (c04), and SEBI ICDR's KPI governance requirements for offer documents (c05). The overarching discipline is the same as the rest of the week's ratio architecture: every KPI has a written definition, unit, period and source, and is reconciled to audited figures before it enters a model or a peer table.

Related concepts to study

Infosys and Wipro 20-F filings under IFRS

Infosys and Wipro also file 20-F under IFRS (IASB); compare with Ind AS reported KPIs

Infosys and Wipro have ADRs listed on the NYSE and file annual reports on Form 20-F with the US SEC, with financial statements prepared under IFRS as issued by the IASB, alongside their Ind AS statements for Indian reporting. Differences between Ind AS and IFRS figures are usually small for these companies but can arise from Ind AS carve-outs and differing transition elections; segment and KPI disclosures (revenue by geography, client metrics, attrition, utilisation) are broadly aligned but presented with different emphasis. The 20-F also contains risk factors, critical accounting estimates and the remaining performance obligation disclosure in US-style detail. Comparing the two is a useful exercise in reconciling KPIs to audited numbers and in spotting definition differences between filings.

KPI-driven revenue builds, dashboards and MIS

KPI-driven revenue builds → Week 13; KPI dashboards in Power BI → Week 12; MIS → Week 20

The KPIs in this topic become model drivers in Week 13: retail revenue = mature-store base × (1 + SSSG) + new stores × area × ramp-adjusted revenue per sq.ft; IT revenue = headcount × utilisation × billing rate × CC growth assumptions × currency; cement revenue = capacity × utilisation × realisation. In Week 12 you will build Power BI dashboards with DAX measures for SSSG, utilisation and EBITDA per tonne, using a KPI definition table and a fact table of reported values. Week 20 MIS design applies the same KPIs internally — monthly variance analysis by store cohort, plant or client bucket — with governance over definitions mirroring the ICDR certification discipline.

Ind AS 115: TCV versus revenue and RPO

Ind AS 115 for TCV vs recognized revenue and remaining performance obligations

TCV is a management bookings metric: the total value of contracts signed, often including optional renewals, volume estimates and termination-for-convenience components. Revenue under Ind AS 115 is recognised as performance obligations are satisfied — over time for most IT services (input methods such as efforts or costs incurred for fixed-price contracts; time-and-materials as billed). Ind AS 115 requires disclosure of the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations (RPO) and when it is expected to be recognised, with practical expedients (e.g., contracts of one year or less, or where revenue equals the right to invoice). RPO is therefore the audited, contract-enforceable backlog, typically smaller than cumulative TCV; reconciling the two indicates how much of announced TCV is firm.

Common pitfalls

Comparing SSSG across different comparable-store definitions

Comparing SSSG across retailers with different comparable-store definitions.

Retailers define the comparable-store base differently — 12 versus 24 months of trading, inclusion or exclusion of refurbished and relocated stores, attribution of online sales, and whether franchise stores count. A 24-month rule excludes ramping stores and lowers SSSG; a 12-month rule includes them and raises it. Analysts make the mistake because SSSG is presented as a single headline number in investor decks with definitions buried in footnotes. Correct treatment: record each company's definition verbatim in the KPI log, compare trends within a company rather than levels across companies, and supplement with revenue per sq.ft on average area and cohort disclosures. In review, check every peer table for a definition column and footnote; if absent, the comparison is unreliable.

Treating TCV as revenue

Treating TCV as revenue rather than multi-year bookings with renewals and terminations.

A US$2 billion TCV announcement does not mean US$2 billion of revenue: it is spread over three to seven years, often includes renewals of existing revenue, may include optional extensions, and can be descoped or terminated for convenience. People make this mistake because large-deal announcements receive press coverage framed as "order wins". Correct treatment: convert TCV into annual contract value (TCV ÷ tenor), separate net new from renewal, apply ramp-up and attrition assumptions, and cross-check against the Ind AS 115 RPO disclosure and book-to-bill. In review, any model where revenue growth jumps in line with headline TCV should be challenged for tenor, renewal share and ramp timing.

Ignoring volume versus price in paints and FMCG growth

Ignoring volume vs price split in paint/FMCG growth.

Value growth of 8% can mean 8% volume with flat prices (healthy demand and share gains) or 0% volume with 8% price hikes (inflation pass-through with no underlying growth) — or, as recently in decorative paints, high-single-digit volume with negative value growth (price cuts and down-trading under competitive pressure). Analysts ignore the split because only value is in the audited statements. Correct treatment: decompose value growth into volume, price and mix using management disclosures (investor presentations, calls), track the raw material index against price actions, and link the result to the DuPont margin component. In review, any growth commentary without a volume figure — or any forecast that extrapolates value growth without a price assumption — should be flagged.

Practice Questions

Q1. Paint peer KPI dashboard [L3]

For three paint companies (FY2024-25 vs FY2023-24): Company A — decorative value growth −2%, volume growth +5%, A&P 4.8% of revenue (prior 4.1%), retail touchpoints 1.68 lakh (+8%), tinting machines 1.1 lakh (+10%); Company B — value +4%, volume +8%, A&P 3.9% (prior 3.6%), touchpoints 0.55 lakh (+12%); Company C — value +1%, volume +3%, A&P 3.2% (prior 3.0%), industrial share 45%. Raw material index down 3%. (a) Compute the implied price/mix effect for each. (b) Explain the likely effect on gross margin and EBIT margin. (c) Link your conclusions to each company's 5-step DuPont components. (d) List the source documents and page types you would use to verify each KPI.

💡 Hint: Use the multiplicative identity between value, volume and price/mix.

Q2. IT services quarter review [L3]

An IT company reports for the quarter: USD revenue US$4,100 million (prior quarter US$4,020 million), CC growth 1.4%, utilisation excluding trainees 85.2% (prior 83.6%), LTM attrition 13.1% (prior 12.4%), large-deal TCV US$3.2 billion of which 60% renewals with average tenor five years, US$100 million+ clients 40 (prior 39), headcount 3,21,000 (prior 3,24,000). (a) Compute the cross-currency effect, annualised revenue per FTE and large-deal book-to-bill. (b) Estimate the annualised net-new revenue implied by TCV. (c) Write a five-point assessment of growth quality and margin outlook. (d) Explain how you would reconcile TCV with the RPO disclosure in the annual report.

Q3. Retailer versus jeweller [L2]

Retailer R: stores 450 → 520, area 8.0 → 9.6 million sq.ft, revenue ₹18,700 crore, comparable-store revenue ₹15,400 crore vs ₹14,300 crore, post-116 EBITDA ₹2,600 crore, capitalised rent ₹900 crore. Jeweller J: revenue ₹22,000 crore (+24%), gold price +21%, studded ratio 26% (prior 24%), 60% of stores franchised, GML funding 55% of inventory. (a) Compute R's SSSG, revenue per sq.ft and pre-116 EBITDA margin. (b) Estimate J's approximate grammage growth. (c) Explain why EBITDA margin is a poor basis for comparing R and J, and propose better KPIs for each.

Q4. Cement EBITDA per tonne and utilisation [L2]

A cement company: capacity 60 MTPA on 1 April 2024, plus 9 MTPA commissioned on 1 November 2024; volume 49 MT; net sales ₹26,950 crore; total operating costs ₹22,540 crore including ₹300 crore of one-off plant start-up costs; state incentives of ₹240 crore included in revenue. Compute effective utilisation, realisation per tonne (reported and ex-incentives), and EBITDA per tonne (reported and adjusted), and explain which figures you would use to compare with a peer that has no incentives.
