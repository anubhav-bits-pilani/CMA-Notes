Key concept

Revenue per Sq.ft and EBITDA per Ton

Concept: Revenue per sq.ft = Revenue ÷ Average trading area; EBITDA/ton = EBITDA ÷ Sales volume (tonnes)

Statement

\[ \text{Revenue per sq.ft} = \frac{R_{\text{retail}}}{\tfrac{1}{2}\left(A_{\text{open}} + A_{\text{close}}\right)} \qquad \text{EBITDA per tonne} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{V} \]

where \(R_{\text{retail}}\) = revenue from retail operations for the period (net of GST, excluding online-only and wholesale revenue where separable), \(A\) = trading (selling) area in square feet at the opening and closing dates (or a monthly weighted average), \(\text{EBITDA}\) = operating EBITDA for the period excluding other income and one-offs (state treatment of incentives), and \(V\) = sales volume in tonnes for the same period (state whether clinker and traded volumes are included).

Explanation

Both KPIs convert a rupee figure into a unit-productivity measure, making companies of different sizes, and periods with different prices, comparable.

Revenue per sq.ft measures space productivity: how much sales each square foot of selling space generates. Average area is essential — a retailer adding 25% area late in the year would otherwise see an artificial drop. Weighted average area (months open × area ÷ 12) is more accurate still. Trading area excludes back-of-house and storage. Benchmarks vary by format: hypermarkets and value grocery (high throughput, low margin) report much higher revenue per sq.ft than department stores or premium apparel. Read alongside SSSG (c01): falling revenue per sq.ft with healthy SSSG usually means new, immature stores are diluting the average.

EBITDA per tonne measures unit profitability in commodity manufacturing (cement, steel, aluminium). It equals realisation per tonne minus cost per tonne (s04) and is insensitive to the price-driven percentage-margin swings that make EBITDA margin unreliable for such companies. Strip state incentives (often recognised as other operating revenue) and other income for comparability; use the same volume definition as the denominator of realisation.

Both KPIs are unaudited in most cases (area and volume come from investor presentations), so reconcile: revenue per sq.ft × average area should reproduce retail revenue; EBITDA per tonne × volume should reproduce EBITDA.

Illustration

Retail: area 9.8 → 11.6 million sq.ft; retail revenue ₹31,030 crore. Average area 10.7 million sq.ft; revenue per sq.ft = ₹31,030 crore ÷ 10.7 million = ₹29,000. Using closing area (₹26,750) would understate productivity by 8%.

Cement: EBITDA ₹4,320 crore including state incentives of ₹270 crore; sales volume 36 MT (including 1.5 MT clinker sales). EBITDA per tonne (reported) = ₹4,320 crore ÷ 36 MT = ₹1,200. Excluding incentives = ₹4,050 crore ÷ 36 MT = ₹1,125. Excluding clinker from volume as well requires removing clinker EBITDA — the denominator and numerator must be adjusted together.

Practice Questions

Q1. Average versus closing area [L1]

A value retailer's trading area rose from 4.6 to 6.0 million sq.ft, with most new stores opened in Q4. Revenue was ₹14,840 crore. Compute revenue per sq.ft using closing area, simple average area and a weighted average area of 4.9 million sq.ft, and state which you would use.

Q2. Adjusted EBITDA per tonne [L2]

A cement company reports EBITDA of ₹2,880 crore on 24 MT, including ₹180 crore of incentives, ₹60 crore of other income included by management in "operating EBITDA" and a ₹45 crore one-off provision reversal. Compute reported and adjusted EBITDA per tonne.

💡 Hint: Remove items that are not recurring operating earnings from the numerator only.

Q3. Cross-check with NFAT [L2]

A retailer reports revenue per sq.ft of ₹32,000 on 8 million sq.ft average area, but its NFAT on owned PPE is 11x while a peer with revenue per sq.ft of ₹34,000 shows NFAT of 3x. Explain what likely causes the difference.
