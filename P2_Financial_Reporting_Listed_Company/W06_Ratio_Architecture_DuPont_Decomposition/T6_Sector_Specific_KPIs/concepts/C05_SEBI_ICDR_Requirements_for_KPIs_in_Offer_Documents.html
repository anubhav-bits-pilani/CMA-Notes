Key concept

SEBI ICDR Requirements for KPIs in Offer Documents

Concept: SEBI ICDR requires KPIs in offer documents to be audit-committee approved, certified, and disclosed post-listing

Statement

Under the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018, as amended in November 2022 (with subsequent clarifications), an issuer's offer document (DRHP/RHP) must, in the "Basis for Offer Price" section:

  1. Disclose all key performance indicators that have been disclosed to its investors at any point in the three years preceding the offer document (e.g., to pre-IPO investors or in prior communications).
  2. Have the KPIs approved by the audit committee of the issuer.
  3. Have the KPIs certified by the statutory auditor or by a chartered accountant (or firm) holding a valid peer-review certificate.
  4. Provide definitions, an explanation of how each KPI is relevant to assessing performance, and a comparison with listed industry peers where available.
  5. Continue to disclose the KPIs after listing — in the annual report — for at least one year after listing or until utilisation of the issue proceeds is complete, whichever is later (the obligation as stated in the regulations should be verified against the latest amendment).

The offer document also discloses pricing based on KPIs: the weighted average cost of acquisition of shares in recent transactions compared with the offer price, linked to the KPIs.

Explanation

Before 2022, new-age IPOs (internet platforms, D2C brands, fintechs) often marketed themselves on bespoke operating metrics — gross merchandise value, monthly transacting users, take rate, contribution margin — that were neither defined consistently nor verified. Pre-IPO investors had been shown different KPIs from those in the prospectus. SEBI's amendment aims to ensure that (a) every KPI used to justify the offer price is consistently defined, (b) a governance body (audit committee) owns it, (c) an independent professional has checked it against underlying records, and (d) investors can track it after listing, preventing a metric from disappearing once it turns adverse.

For analysts and FP&A professionals:

  • The RHP KPI section is the most reliable historical KPI dataset for a newly listed company — certified and defined.
  • Post-listing presentations should use the same definitions; divergences warrant questions.
  • In a pre-IPO company, FP&A must build KPI computation processes that can withstand certification: documented definitions, system-generated data, reconciliation to audited financials, and change-control over definitions.
  • KPIs remain unaudited management measures (certification is not an audit opinion on the financial statements); the ICAI has issued guidance on such certification engagements.

Illustration

A D2C beauty brand's RHP discloses: revenue from operations, gross margin, EBITDA and EBITDA margin, repeat customer rate, number of SKUs, marketplace vs own-website share, and average order value — each with a definition ("Repeat customer rate = customers with two or more orders in the year ÷ total transacting customers"), three years of data, a statement that the audit committee approved them, and a certificate from a peer-reviewed CA firm. After listing, the first annual report includes the same KPIs. If the company's investor presentation later switches "repeat customer rate" to a 24-month window, an analyst should restate or flag the series and ask why.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compliance checklist [L1]

List the steps an issuer's CFO office must complete to include a KPI such as "revenue per active store" in an RHP under the ICDR requirements, from definition to post-listing disclosure.

Q2. Definition drift after listing [L2]

A recently listed quick-commerce company reported "average order value" in its RHP on a GST-inclusive basis; after listing, its investor presentation reports AOV net of GST and discounts. Explain the analytical problem, the governance concern under the ICDR framework, and how you would restate the series.

💡 Hint: Consider which definition was certified and approved by the audit committee.

Q3. FP&A readiness [L3]

You are the FP&A lead of a pre-IPO jewellery retailer that has shared same-store sales growth, studded ratio and revenue per sq.ft with private-equity investors over three years using spreadsheets. Design a KPI governance framework (data sources, definition register, reconciliation, sign-offs) that would support certification and post-listing disclosure.
