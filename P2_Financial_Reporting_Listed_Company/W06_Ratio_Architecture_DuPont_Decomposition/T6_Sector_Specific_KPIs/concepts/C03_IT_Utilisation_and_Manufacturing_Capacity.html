Key concept

IT Utilisation and Manufacturing Capacity Utilisation

Concept: Utilization (IT) = Billed effort ÷ Available effort (incl./excl. trainees); Capacity utilization = Output ÷ Installed capacity

Statement

\[ U_{\text{IT}} = \frac{E_{\text{billed}}}{E_{\text{available}}} \qquad U_{\text{mfg}} = \frac{Q}{K} \]

where \(E_{\text{billed}}\) = person-months (or hours) billed to clients in the period; \(E_{\text{available}}\) = total person-months available from delivery employees in the period — including trainees (lower figure) or excluding trainees (higher figure), with the basis stated; \(Q\) = production (or sales) volume in the period; \(K\) = installed capacity for the same period, time-weighted for additions (effective capacity).

Explanation

Both ratios measure how much of the available productive capacity is generating revenue, and both drive operating leverage — costs are largely fixed with respect to capacity (salaries in IT, plant fixed costs in manufacturing).

IT utilisation: delivery staff on the bench (between projects, in training, or awaiting visas) are paid but not billed. Including trainees shows the true cost burden of a fresher-heavy hiring cycle; excluding them shows the deployability of the productive workforce. Some companies measure "offshore" and "onsite" separately. Very high utilisation (above about 85% excluding trainees) leaves no buffer to take on new deals quickly; low utilisation signals over-hiring or weak demand. A one percentage point change in utilisation shifts EBIT margin by roughly 20–40 bps for large Indian IT firms, per management commentary.

Capacity utilisation in manufacturing: use effective capacity when plants commission mid-year; distinguish clinker vs cement capacity, or crude-steel vs finished-steel capacity. Industry-wide utilisation influences pricing: Indian cement pricing typically firms when industry utilisation rises into the high 70s percent and weakens when new capacity pushes it down. Company utilisation above industry suggests market share gains or a favourable regional footprint.

Illustration

IT firm, quarter: delivery headcount 1,20,000 including 8,000 trainees; billed person-months 2,88,000; each employee provides 3 person-months per quarter.

  • Available incl. trainees = 1,20,000 × 3 = 3,60,000 → \(U\) = 80.0%
  • Available excl. trainees = 1,12,000 × 3 = 3,36,000 → \(U\) = 85.7%

Cement company: capacity 50 MTPA from 1 April, plus 8 MTPA commissioned on 1 January; FY volume 41 MT.

  • Effective capacity = 50 + 8 × 3 ÷ 12 = 52 MTPA → \(U\) = 78.8%
  • Nameplate closing capacity 58 MTPA → \(U\) = 70.7% (understates the operating rate)

Practice Questions

Q1. IT utilisation both ways [L1]

A firm has 85,000 delivery employees of whom 6,500 are trainees; billed hours in a month are 1,04,00,000 and each employee has 168 available hours. Compute utilisation including and excluding trainees.

Q2. Effective capacity [L1]

A steel company's crude-steel capacity was 18 MTPA on 1 April 2024; 5 MTPA was commissioned on 1 September 2024. Production was 17.2 MT. Compute utilisation on effective and closing capacity.

💡 Hint: Time-weight the new capacity by months in operation.

Q3. Margin sensitivity [L2]

An IT firm's EBIT margin is 21% at 84% utilisation (excluding trainees). Revenue is US$2,000 million a quarter with employee costs at 55% of revenue. Estimate the EBIT margin at 87% utilisation if billing rates and headcount are unchanged, and explain the assumption that makes the estimate an upper bound.
