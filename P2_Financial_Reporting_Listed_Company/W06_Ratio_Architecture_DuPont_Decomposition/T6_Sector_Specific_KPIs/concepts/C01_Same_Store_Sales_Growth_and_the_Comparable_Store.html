Key concept

Same-Store Sales Growth and the Comparable-Store Base

Concept: SSSG = (Revenue of comparable stores_t ÷ Revenue of same stores_t−1) − 1; define comparable-store base

Statement

\[ \text{SSSG}_t = \frac{\sum_{i \in S} R_{i,t}}{\sum_{i \in S} R_{i,t-1}} - 1 \]

where \(S\) = the comparable-store set — stores that traded throughout both period \(t\) and period \(t-1\) and meet the maturity rule (e.g., open for at least 12 months before the start of period \(t-1\), or before the start of period \(t\), depending on the company's definition); \(R_{i,t}\) = revenue of store \(i\) in period \(t\) on a consistent measure (net of GST, same treatment of online-fulfilled sales and concessions). The same set \(S\) is used in both numerator and denominator.

Explanation

SSSG isolates organic productivity of the existing estate from growth by expansion. Because new stores ramp up for 12–24 months, including them would overstate underlying growth; because closed stores are excluded, SSSG is survivorship-biased.

Elements of the definition that must be stated:

  • Maturity threshold (12, 18 or 24 months) and whether measured from opening date to the start of the comparison period.
  • Treatment of refurbished, relocated, resized or temporarily closed stores (e.g., excluded for the period of closure, or included on a per-day basis).
  • Channel boundaries: whether click-and-collect and ship-from-store revenue is attributed to stores.
  • Calendar alignment: same number of trading days; festival timing (Diwali, Eid, wedding season) can shift quarterly comparisons — some companies present calendar-adjusted figures.
  • Revenue basis: gross of GST or net; franchise stores at retail or wholesale value.

Analytically, SSSG decomposes as (1 + footfall growth) × (1 + conversion change) × (1 + average bill value growth) − 1; and as (1 + volume) × (1 + price/mix) − 1. In inflationary periods, nominal SSSG of 5% may be negative in volume.

Illustration

A retailer's store cohorts (₹ crore):

Cohort Stores Opened FY2023-24 revenue FY2024-25 revenue
Mature 150 Before April 2022 3,000 3,180
2022-23 cohort 30 During FY2022-23 420 510
2023-24 cohort 40 During FY2023-24 210 640
Closed in FY2024-25 10 Before April 2022 120 30
  • Strict rule (open at least 12 months before the start of the comparative year, i.e., before April 2022): mature cohort only → 3,180 ÷ 3,000 − 1 = 6.0%.
  • Full-comparative-year rule (open before April 2023, so trading throughout both years): mature + 2022-23 cohort → 3,690 ÷ 3,420 − 1 = 7.9%.
  • Total revenue growth (all stores) → 4,360 ÷ 3,750 − 1 = 16.3%.

The same retailer can report 6.0% or 7.9% SSSG — the comparable-store base must be stated before comparing peers (topic pitfall).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute SSSG under two rules [L1]

Using cohorts: mature 220 stores (₹5,500 crore → ₹5,830 crore), FY2022-23 cohort 35 stores (₹560 crore → ₹670 crore), FY2023-24 cohort 50 stores (₹260 crore → ₹900 crore), closed 8 stores (₹90 crore → ₹15 crore), compute SSSG under a 24-month and a 12-month comparable rule, and total revenue growth.

Q2. Volume versus price [L2]

A grocery retailer reports SSSG of 8.5%; bill counts in comparable stores rose 2.0% and food inflation was 6.0%. Estimate the implied real change in average basket size and interpret.

💡 Hint: Decompose SSSG multiplicatively into bills, inflation and real basket.

Q3. Definition change [L2]

A company changes its SSSG definition from a 24-month to a 12-month maturity rule mid-year. Explain how this affects the reported trend and what restatement you would request for your model.
