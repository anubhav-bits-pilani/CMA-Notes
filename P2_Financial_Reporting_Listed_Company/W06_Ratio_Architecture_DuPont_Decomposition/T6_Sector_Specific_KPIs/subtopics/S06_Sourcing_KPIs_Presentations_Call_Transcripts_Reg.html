Subtopic note

Sourcing KPIs: Presentations, Call Transcripts, Reg 33 and RHPs

Scope: Source documents: investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, Reg 33 results, RHP KPI sections

Why it matters

Sector KPIs are mostly not in the audited financial statements. Same-store sales growth, utilisation, TCV, EBITDA per tonne and tinting-machine counts live in investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, fact sheets and offer documents. An analyst building the Week 6 peer comparison or a sell-side model must know where each number comes from, how reliable it is, how definitions drift, and how to reconcile KPIs to the audited numbers. FP&A professionals preparing these documents for their own companies must understand the regulatory obligations — SEBI LODR on disclosure of presentations and transcripts, and SEBI ICDR on KPIs in offer documents.

Core notes

Hierarchy of sources and reliability

Source Regulatory basis Assurance Typical KPI content
Annual report (audited FS, notes) Companies Act 2013; Ind AS Audited Segment data, Schedule III ratios, RPO (Ind AS 115), capacity (sometimes in MD&A)
Quarterly/half-yearly results SEBI LODR Reg 33 Limited review (quarterly), audit (annual) P&L, segment results, half-yearly balance sheet and cash flow
Investor presentation LODR Reg 30 and Reg 46 (website dissemination) None Volume, realisation, SSSG, utilisation, TCV, stores, dealers
Earnings call transcript/recording LODR Reg 46 and Schedule III Part A (audio/video and transcripts to be disseminated within prescribed timelines) None Management commentary, guidance, sensitivities, KPI definitions
Offer documents (DRHP/RHP) SEBI ICDR Regulations KPIs certified by statutory auditor or chartered accountant; approved by audit committee Historical KPIs with definitions and reconciliation
Annual report MD&A / BRSR LODR Reg 34 Limited (BRSR core assurance for top listed entities) Ratios with explanations, operational metrics, ESG KPIs

Reg 33 results

Listed entities file quarterly financial results within 45 days of quarter-end (60 days for the annual audited results), with a statement of assets and liabilities and a cash flow statement for the half-year (H1) and annual periods. Quarterly results are subject to limited review by the statutory auditor. For KPI work:

  • Half-yearly balance sheets (30 September) allow average-balance computations and seasonal analysis of working capital (t3 s06).
  • Segment results under Ind AS 108 appear quarterly.
  • Debt-listed entities add the Reg 52(4) ratios (t4 s06).

Investor presentations

The richest KPI source: sector operating metrics, store counts, capacity, volume growth, client metrics. Working practices:

  • Build a KPI log in Excel or Power Query: KPI name, definition text (verbatim), unit, period, value, source document, page number, date accessed.
  • Watch for definition changes (footnotes such as "SSSG now computed on stores operating 12 months" or "volume growth excludes putty") — restate history or break the series.
  • Selective disclosure risk: companies may stop disclosing a KPI when it turns adverse; note when a metric disappears.

Earnings call transcripts

Provide context, guidance and the "why" behind KPIs: management quantifies sensitivities (rupee, utilisation), explains one-offs and sometimes reveals unpublished numbers in Q&A. Use them to capture:

  • Guidance ranges (revenue growth, EBITDA margin bands, capex plans).
  • KPI definitions clarified by management.
  • Qualitative signals (competitive intensity, pricing actions, demand trends).

Because transcripts are unaudited and forward-looking statements carry safe-harbour language, triangulate with reported numbers.

RHP KPI sections (SEBI ICDR)

SEBI's 2022 amendments to the ICDR Regulations require the "Basis for Offer Price" section to disclose KPIs that have been disclosed to investors at any time in the three years before the offer document, approved by the audit committee, certified by the statutory auditor or a peer-reviewed chartered accountant, with explanations of how they help assess performance, comparison with listed peers, and their tracking after listing (c05). The RHP also includes a "Management's Discussion and Analysis" with KPI definitions. For recently listed companies the RHP is often the cleanest single source of three-year KPI history with formal definitions.

Reconciliation discipline

Before using any KPI in a ratio or model:

  1. Tie to audited numbers where possible: revenue per sq.ft × average area should reproduce reported retail revenue; realisation × volume should approximate segment revenue; revenue per FTE × headcount should reproduce revenue.
  2. Check units (crore vs million, MT vs tonnes, litres vs kg, USD vs INR).
  3. Check period (quarterly vs LTM vs annualised).
  4. Document adjustments in the KPI log.

Worked illustration

A paint company's investor presentation states decorative volume growth of 10% for FY2024-25; the transcript clarifies that volume includes putty by weight and that value growth was 2%; the audited segment note shows decorative revenue of ₹18,600 crore versus ₹18,235 crore in FY2023-24 (growth 2.0%). The KPI log records volume growth (10%, includes putty), value growth (2.0%, tied to audited segment revenue) and the implied price/mix effect (1.02 ÷ 1.10 − 1 = −7.3%).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Pre-IPO KPIs vs post-listing: compare post-listing presentations with RHP definitions; divergences should be explained under ICDR tracking obligations.
  • Consolidated vs standalone KPIs in presentations; align with the financial statements used for ratios.
  • Non-GAAP measures (adjusted EBITDA, "pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA"): require a reconciliation to Ind AS figures; SEBI has emphasised fair presentation of such measures.
  • Analyst meet presentations may contain KPIs absent from quarterly decks — check the stock exchange filings under Reg 30 for the complete set.
  • Data vendors (Capitaline, ProwessIQ, Bloomberg) may standardise definitions differently from the company.

Key takeaways

  • Most sector KPIs sit outside audited statements — in presentations, transcripts and offer documents.
  • Reg 33 results give quarterly P&L and half-yearly balance sheets; investor presentations and transcripts give operating KPIs and context.
  • RHP KPI sections are audit-committee approved and certified, and must be tracked after listing.
  • Maintain a KPI log with verbatim definitions, units, periods and page references.
  • Reconcile KPIs to audited figures and flag definition changes before using them in models.

Practice Questions

Q1. Map KPIs to sources [L1]

For each KPI, name the most reliable source document and its regulatory basis: (a) SSSG of a listed apparel retailer; (b) remaining performance obligations of an IT company; (c) EBITDA per tonne of a cement company; (d) half-yearly trade receivables; (e) KPIs of a company listed six months ago.

Q2. Build a KPI log [L2]

Design the column structure and validation rules for a Power Query–fed KPI log covering six paint and cement companies over twelve quarters, including how definition changes and restated history are captured.

💡 Hint: Separate the "definition" dimension from the "value" fact table.

Q3. Reconciliation test [L2]

An IT company's fact sheet reports revenue per FTE of US$58,400 (annualised from the quarter), average headcount of 2,45,000 and quarterly revenue of US$3,620 million. Test whether the figures reconcile and list possible reasons for any difference.

Q4. Disappearing KPI [L3]

A retailer that reported SSSG every quarter for three years stops disclosing it after a quarter of −4% SSSG, replacing it with "revenue per sq.ft growth". Discuss the analytical implications, how you would estimate SSSG from other disclosures, and the governance questions you would raise on the earnings call.
