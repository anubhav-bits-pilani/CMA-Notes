Subtopic note

Jewellery KPIs: Gold Pass-Through, Studded Ratio, GML and Hedging

Scope: Jewellery: gold price pass-through, studded ratio, franchise vs owned stores, gold metal loan cost, hedging (Ind AS 109)

Why it matters

Indian jewellery retail (Titan's Tanishq, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold, PC Jeweller, Thangamayil, P N Gadgil) is a gold-price business with a retail wrapper. Revenue moves with the gold price almost one-for-one, gross margin percentages compress when gold rises even if rupee margins hold, inventory is the dominant asset, and the funding of that inventory — gold metal loans versus rupee working capital — and its hedging drive both profitability and earnings volatility. Analysts who read jewellery revenue growth as "volume" or gross margin compression as "pricing pressure" without separating the gold effect will mis-diagnose the business. The KPIs below are the ones these companies report on earnings calls and that FP&A teams in the sector budget on.

Core notes

Gold price pass-through

Plain gold jewellery is priced as gold value at the day's rate + making charges + GST (3% on jewellery; 5% on making charges for job-work). The gold component is passed through to the customer at the prevailing price, so:

  • Revenue growth ≈ (1 + volume growth in grams) × (1 + gold price change) × (1 + mix/making-charge change) − 1. A 20% gold price rise can deliver 15–20% revenue growth with flat or falling grammage.
  • Gross margin % compresses when gold rises: making charges and studded margins are partly fixed per gram or per piece, while the pass-through denominator rises. Track gross margin per gram or absolute gross profit.
  • Inventory gains/losses: if inventory is unhedged and bought earlier at lower prices, rising gold creates a one-off margin gain (and a loss when prices fall). Companies that hedge or use gold metal loans neutralise this.
  • Customer behaviour: sharp price rises shift demand to lighter-weight pieces, lower caratage (18K, 14K, and from 2025 the BIS hallmarking of 9K) and exchange of old gold.

KPIs: grammage (volume) growth, buyer growth, average ticket size, old gold exchange share of sales, gold price change during the period.

Studded ratio

\[ \text{Studded ratio} = \frac{\text{Revenue from studded (diamond/gemstone) jewellery}}{\text{Total jewellery revenue}} \]

Studded jewellery carries far higher gross margins (often 30–40%+) than plain gold (roughly 8–15%), because pricing is not tied to a transparent commodity price. A rising studded ratio lifts gross margin; Tanishq's mid-to-high 20s% studded share versus regional players in low teens explains much of the margin gap. Note that "studded ratio" may be reported excluding gold coins and bullion sales.

Franchise vs owned stores

Jewellers increasingly expand through franchises (FOFO) — Kalyan's "asset-light" expansion and Tanishq's large franchise share:

Aspect Owned (COCO) Franchise (FOFO)
Inventory Brand's balance sheet Franchisee funds inventory (sometimes gold on loan from brand)
Revenue Retail value Wholesale sale to franchisee; lower revenue per gram
Gross margin % Higher Lower (franchisee margin)
Capital employed High (inventory, fit-out, ROU) Low → higher ROCE
Risks Inventory price risk, theft Franchisee credit, brand control

Analytical point: reported revenue, gross margin and inventory days are not comparable across jewellers with different franchise mixes without segmenting (t5 s05).

Gold metal loan (GML) cost

A gold metal loan is a bank loan denominated in grams of gold, borrowed from nominated banks (under RBI's GML scheme), typically for up to 180 days, with interest charged as a percentage (often 2–5% p.a.) on the gold value. The jeweller uses the gold to manufacture and sell; repayment is made by buying gold at the then-prevailing price (or by converting into a rupee purchase). Effects:

  • Natural hedge: inventory (asset) and GML (liability) both move with gold — price risk is neutralised.
  • Lower cost: GML interest is well below rupee working-capital rates (8–10%).
  • Accounting: GML is a financial liability; it is generally measured at fair value through profit or loss (or with an embedded derivative separated, depending on terms), so fair value changes offset inventory value changes when hedge accounting is applied (below). Interest is a finance cost.
  • KPI: share of inventory funded by GML, GML cost %, weighted average cost of inventory funding.

Hedging under Ind AS 109

Jewellers hedge gold price risk on inventory (fair value hedge) or on forecast purchases (cash flow hedge) using gold futures/forwards on MCX or the GML itself as the hedging instrument.

  • Fair value hedge (Ind AS 109.6.5.8): change in fair value of the hedging instrument goes to P&L; the hedged item (inventory, normally at lower of cost and NRV) is adjusted for fair value changes attributable to the hedged risk, also through P&L — so gains and losses offset.
  • Cash flow hedge (Ind AS 109.6.5.11): effective portion of hedging instrument changes goes to OCI (cash flow hedge reserve) and is reclassified to inventory cost or P&L when the hedged purchase affects profit.
  • Requirements: formal designation and documentation at inception, economic relationship, credit risk not dominating, and hedge ratio consistent with risk management (Ind AS 109.6.4.1). Ineffectiveness is recognised in P&L.
  • Without hedge accounting, fair value changes on futures hit P&L while inventory is at cost — producing artificial earnings volatility.

Worked illustration

A jeweller's FY2023-24 revenue was ₹10,000 crore: plain gold ₹7,600 crore at 11% gross margin (₹836 crore, mostly making charges, which are fixed per gram) and studded ₹2,400 crore at 36% (₹864 crore). Gross margin 17.0%.

In FY2024-25 the gold price rises 22%, plain-gold grammage falls 4%, and the studded ratio rises to 27% with studded margin unchanged at 36%.

Item FY2023-24 FY2024-25 Working
Plain gold revenue 7,600 8,901 7,600 × 0.96 × 1.22
Plain gold gross profit 836 803 Making charges move with grams: 836 × 0.96
Total revenue 10,000 12,193 Plain = 73% of total
Studded revenue 2,400 3,292 27% of total
Studded gross profit 864 1,185 36%
Gross profit 1,700 1,988 +16.9%
Gross margin 17.0% 16.3% Compression despite richer mix

Revenue grew 21.9% with falling plain-gold volumes; gross profit grew 16.9%; gross margin percentage fell 70 bps purely because the gold pass-through inflated the denominator.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bullion and coin sales are low margin and inflate revenue; strip them for margin analysis.
  • Gold on lease to franchisees stays on the brand's balance sheet as inventory; check notes.
  • Customer gold schemes (monthly instalment schemes) create customer advances (contract liabilities) and are regulated as deposits under the Companies Act depending on structure — a working-capital source.
  • Import duty changes (e.g., the July 2024 Budget cut in gold customs duty from 15% to 6%) cause one-off inventory losses for those holding unhedged, duty-paid inventory.
  • Hedge accounting discontinuation releases reserves to P&L; read the hedge reserve note.
  • Old-gold exchange is a non-cash purchase that reduces cash intake from sales.

Key takeaways

  • Jewellery revenue = grams × gold price × mix; separate volume from gold price before calling growth.
  • Gross margin % falls mechanically when gold rises; watch gross profit per gram and the studded ratio.
  • Franchise-heavy jewellers show lower revenue per gram and gross margin but higher ROCE.
  • Gold metal loans provide cheap, naturally hedged inventory funding.
  • Ind AS 109 hedge accounting (fair value or cash flow hedges) is needed to avoid artificial P&L volatility.

Practice Questions

Q1. Decomposing jewellery growth [L1]

A jeweller's revenue rose from ₹12,000 crore to ₹14,900 crore. Average gold price rose 19%; studded share rose from 22% to 24%. Estimate grammage growth assuming studded pricing moved with gold and making charges were unchanged as a percentage, and comment on the quality of growth.

Q2. GML versus rupee funding [L2]

A jeweller holds 2,000 kg of gold inventory at ₹7,200 per gram. Option A: fund with cash credit at 9.25% p.a. Option B: fund 70% via GML at 3.0% p.a. and the rest via cash credit. Compute annual funding cost under each and the P&L effect of a 10% fall in gold price under each, assuming no other hedging.

💡 Hint: Under GML, the liability falls with the gold price, offsetting the inventory write-down.

Q3. Hedge accounting choice [L3]

A listed jeweller sells MCX gold futures to hedge 60% of its inventory. Explain the P&L and balance-sheet effects of a 12% gold price rise under (a) no hedge accounting, (b) fair value hedge accounting, and (c) cash flow hedge accounting of forecast purchases, citing the relevant Ind AS 109 requirements and the documentation needed.

Q4. Franchise mix comparison [L2]

Jeweller X has 70% owned stores and Jeweller Y 70% franchise stores. Both report revenue of ₹20,000 crore. Explain how you would expect their gross margin, inventory days, ROCE and DSO to differ, and what segment disclosures you would request to compare them.
