Subtopic note

Paints and FMCG KPIs: Volume vs Value, Distribution and A&P

Scope: Paints/FMCG: volume vs value growth, price hikes, dealer network/tinting machines, A&P spend % of revenue

Why it matters

In decorative paints and FMCG, headline revenue growth combines volume, price and mix — and investors value them very differently. Volume growth reflects underlying demand and market share; price hikes pass through input inflation (crude derivatives for paints, palm oil and milk for FMCG) and may not be repeatable; mix reflects premiumisation or down-trading. Distribution reach — dealers, tinting machines, outlets — is the moat in Indian paints, and advertising and promotion (A&P) spend is the lever companies pull to defend share. With the entry of Birla Opus (Grasim) and JSW Paints, the Week 6 exercise peers (Asian Paints, Berger, Kansai Nerolac) have faced price competition and elevated dealer incentives, making these KPIs central to diagnosing their margin and ROE trends.

Core notes

Volume vs value growth

\[ 1 + g_{\text{value}} = (1 + g_{\text{volume}}) \times (1 + g_{\text{price}}) \times (1 + g_{\text{mix}}) \]

  • Volume in paints: litres/kilolitres or tonnes of decorative paint; in FMCG: tonnes or units ("underlying volume growth", UVG, as reported by Hindustan Unilever).
  • Price: realised price change from list price hikes/cuts, net of discounts and dealer schemes.
  • Mix: shift between premium emulsions, economy distempers, putty, waterproofing; or across pack sizes in FMCG. Paints companies note that a mix shift toward putty and economy products (heavier, lower-priced per litre) can produce "volume growth ahead of value growth".

Indian paint companies commonly disclose decorative volume growth (often in a range or as a percentage) and value growth; the gap is price plus mix. In FY2024-25, several players reported positive volume growth with flat or negative value growth — price cuts and down-trading amid competitive intensity — a warning sign for margins despite healthy-looking volumes.

Price hikes and input costs

Paint raw materials: titanium dioxide (TiO2), monomers (vinyl acetate, acrylics), solvents and other crude-linked derivatives — roughly 55–60% of revenue as material cost for decorative players. Companies take price hikes with a lag of one to two quarters after input inflation; the gross margin trajectory reflects the timing gap between input costs and pricing. Track the raw material index (management commentary) against cumulative price changes.

Interpretation of a price hike: is it a pass-through (protects absolute gross profit, gross margin % may still dip), a margin expansion (pricing power), or a correction (price cuts in deflation or under competitive pressure)?

Dealer network and tinting machines

Decorative paints are sold through dealers who tint colours on site using company-supplied tinting machines (dispensers). KPIs:

  • Number of dealers/retail touchpoints (Asian Paints reports well over 1.5 lakh retail touchpoints; peers report smaller networks).
  • Tinting machines installed: an indicator of colour-capability reach and capital deployed in the channel — machines are typically company-owned PPE placed with dealers, adding to fixed assets.
  • Depots and service centres, distribution reach into tier-3/4 towns.
  • Dealer incentives and schemes: sit in discounts (net against revenue under Ind AS 115 as consideration payable to a customer) or in other expenses depending on nature — affects comparability of gross margin.

A new entrant's aggressive dealer acquisition (high incentives, tinting machine placement) is visible in incumbents' rising discounts and A&P.

A&P spend as % of revenue

\[ \text{A\&P \%} = \frac{\text{Advertisement and sales promotion expense}}{\text{Revenue from operations}} \]

Typically disclosed within "other expenses" in the notes. Indian FMCG majors spend roughly 8–12% of revenue; paint companies lower single digits to mid-single digits. Caveats: some promotional spend (trade schemes, free goods) is netted against revenue under Ind AS 115, so reported A&P understates total commercial investment; classification varies across companies. A rising A&P ratio with falling volume growth suggests share defence under competition.

Worked illustration

Decorative paints segment, FY2024-25 vs FY2023-24: value growth −1.5%; reported volume growth +7.0%; management says mix deteriorated by 3% (toward economy and putty).

\[ 1 + g_{\text{price}} = \frac{1 - 0.015}{1.07 \times 0.97} = 0.949 \]

Implied price/discount effect ≈ −5.1%: substantial net price reduction (price cuts plus higher dealer discounts). If raw material costs fell only 2%, gross margin will compress — consistent with a ROE decline driven by margin in the DuPont decomposition (t2).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Volume definitions: some companies report volume including putty and waterproofing by weight, others in litres — not comparable across peers.
  • Industrial vs decorative: Kansai Nerolac's higher industrial (auto) share means auto cycles affect growth; segment before comparing with Asian Paints.
  • Channel inventory: pre-price-hike dealer stocking inflates the quarter before a hike and depresses the next.
  • International operations and adjacent businesses (home décor, kitchens, adhesives) muddy consolidated growth.
  • FMCG UVG vs reported volume: HUL's UVG excludes certain categories; read definitions.
  • GST: revenue net of GST; price hikes quoted as MRP increases include GST.

Key takeaways

  • Value growth = volume × price × mix; decompose before judging growth quality.
  • Price hikes may be pass-through, margin expansion or a reversal — read alongside the raw material index.
  • Dealer network and tinting machines are the moat in Indian decorative paints and visible PPE in the channel.
  • A&P % of revenue understates total promotion where trade schemes are netted against revenue.
  • Positive volume with negative value growth under competition foreshadows margin pressure.

Practice Questions

Q1. Decompose growth [L1]

A paint company reports decorative value growth of 9.5% with volume growth of 11% and management states price hikes of 2%. Compute the implied mix effect and interpret it.

Q2. Price lag and gross margin [L2]

Raw material cost is 58% of revenue at the start of the year. Input prices rise 10% in Q1; the company raises prices 3% in Q2 and another 4% in Q3. Assuming flat volumes, compute the gross margin each quarter (Q1–Q4) and explain the shape of the trajectory.

💡 Hint: Index revenue and raw material cost per unit to 100 at the start.

Q3. Competitive entry diagnostics [L3]

A new entrant claims 6% decorative market share within 18 months. For an incumbent, list the KPIs you would track in each quarterly result and earnings call (with expected direction if the incumbent is defending share) across volume, price, dealer count, tinting machines, discounts and A&P, and explain how each flows into the 5-step DuPont.

Q4. A&P comparability [L2]

FMCG Company A reports A&P of 10.5% of revenue and nets trade schemes of 4% against revenue; Company B reports A&P of 13% and classifies trade schemes within A&P. Restate both to a common basis and comment on relative brand investment.
