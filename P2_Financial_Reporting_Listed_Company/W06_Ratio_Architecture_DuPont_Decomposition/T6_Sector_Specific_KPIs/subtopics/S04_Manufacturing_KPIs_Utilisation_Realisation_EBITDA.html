Subtopic note

Manufacturing KPIs: Utilisation, Realisation, EBITDA and Cost per Ton

Scope: Manufacturing: capacity utilization, volume growth, realization/ton, EBITDA/ton, cost/ton (power, fuel, freight)

Why it matters

In commodity and semi-commodity manufacturing — cement, steel, aluminium, fertiliser, paper, and to an extent chemicals — margins as a percentage of revenue swing with commodity prices and say little about competitive position. Per-unit metrics do: an Indian cement company's EBITDA per tonne is the number every analyst, rating agency and investor tracks quarter by quarter, and cost per tonne (power and fuel, freight, raw material) is how FP&A teams set budgets and benchmark plants. Capacity utilisation and volume growth link the P&L to the fixed asset base (t5) and determine operating leverage. These KPIs come from investor presentations, earnings calls and, in cement, the industry data published monthly (e.g., the Office of the Economic Adviser's index of eight core industries, and company-reported dispatches).

Core notes

Capacity utilisation and volume growth

\[ \text{Capacity utilisation} = \frac{\text{Production (or sales volume)}}{\text{Installed (effective) capacity}} \]

Use effective capacity time-weighted for mid-year commissioning (t5 s04). In cement, distinguish clinker capacity utilisation from cement (grinding) capacity utilisation; blended cement (PPC, PSC) uses fly ash or slag, so cement output can exceed clinker output by a clinker factor (cement ÷ clinker, about 1.4–1.5x for blended-heavy players).

Volume growth = \(V_t \div V_{t-1} - 1\), with \(V\) = sales volume reported in million tonnes. Separate organic volume from acquired volume (cement M&A has been active: e.g., UltraTech–Kesoram, Adani–Penna/Sanghi/Orient). Compare company volume growth with industry growth to measure market share gain.

Realisation per tonne

\[ \text{Realisation per tonne} = \frac{\text{Net sales revenue (excluding GST and, where possible, other operating income)}}{\text{Sales volume (tonnes)}} \]

Realisation reflects price, product mix (premium cement, trade vs non-trade share), region and freight terms (some sales are on delivered basis, embedding freight in price). Trade (retail, dealer-driven) realisations exceed non-trade (institutional/infrastructure) by a meaningful margin.

Cost per tonne

Indian cement cost structure per tonne (indicative proportions):

Cost head Approx. share Drivers
Power and fuel 25–30% Pet coke and coal prices (imported vs domestic), kiln efficiency (kcal/kg clinker), captive power, waste heat recovery (WHR), renewable share
Freight and forwarding 25–30% Lead distance, rail vs road mix, diesel prices, plant–market proximity
Raw materials 15–20% Limestone royalty, fly ash, slag, gypsum
Employee cost 5–7% Fixed; benefits from utilisation
Other expenses 15–20% Packing, maintenance, selling

\[ \text{Cost per tonne}_{k} = \frac{\text{Cost head}_{k}}{\text{Sales volume}} \]

Management commentary often reports fuel cost per kcal (₹ per million kcal) and lead distance (km).

EBITDA per tonne

\[ \text{EBITDA per tonne} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Sales volume}} = \text{Realisation per tonne} - \text{Total cost per tonne} \]

The core profitability KPI for cement and steel. For Indian cement it has ranged roughly ₹700–1,400 per tonne across players and cycles. Use EBITDA excluding other income and one-offs (incentives under state investment promotion schemes can be large and time-bound; strip or show separately). Normalised EBITDA per tonne × capacity also feeds EV per tonne replacement-cost valuation.

Operating leverage

With a high fixed cost share, a rise in utilisation lowers fixed cost per tonne. If fixed costs are ₹600 crore on 20 MT volume (₹300 per tonne), rising to 24 MT cuts fixed cost per tonne to ₹250, adding ₹50 per tonne to EBITDA without any price change.

Worked illustration

Cement company FY2024-25: capacity 40 MTPA (effective), sales volume 31 MT; net sales ₹16,430 crore; power and fuel ₹3,720 crore; freight ₹3,875 crore; raw materials ₹2,480 crore; employee ₹930 crore; other expenses ₹2,790 crore.

KPI Computation Result
Utilisation 31 ÷ 40 77.5%
Realisation/t ₹16,430 crore ÷ 31 MT ₹5,300
Power and fuel/t 3,720 ÷ 31 ₹1,200
Freight/t 3,875 ÷ 31 ₹1,250
Raw material/t 2,480 ÷ 31 ₹800
Employee/t 930 ÷ 31 ₹300
Other/t 2,790 ÷ 31 ₹900
Total cost/t ₹4,450
EBITDA/t 5,300 − 4,450 ₹850
EBITDA 850 × 31 MT ₹2,635 crore

(₹ crore ÷ million tonnes × 10 = ₹ per tonne.)

Excel structure

Realisation_t = Net_Sales_Cr * 1e7 / (Volume_MT * 1e6)
Cost_t_k      = Cost_k_Cr * 1e7 / (Volume_MT * 1e6)
EBITDA_t      = Realisation_t - SUM(Cost_t_Range)
Check         = ABS(EBITDA_t * Volume_MT * 1e6 / 1e7 - EBITDA_Cr) < 0.5

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Volume definition: sales vs production; including/excluding clinker sales and traded cement.
  • Incentives (state GST refunds, capital subsidies) in other operating revenue inflate realisation; isolate.
  • Inventory changes: fuel bought at lower prices last quarter lags into costs; per-tonne cost may trail spot.
  • Consolidated vs standalone: subsidiaries with different regions distort averages.
  • Steel: use EBITDA per tonne of saleable steel; distinguish integrated vs secondary producers; iron ore and coking coal dominate costs.
  • Captive power and WHR: reduce power cost but require capex — compare cost per tonne together with capital per tonne (t5).

Key takeaways

  • Per-tonne KPIs neutralise commodity price swings that distort percentage margins.
  • Utilisation must use effective capacity; distinguish clinker and cement capacity.
  • Realisation reflects price, mix, region and freight terms; trade share matters.
  • Power and fuel and freight dominate Indian cement costs; track ₹/tonne and fuel ₹/million kcal.
  • EBITDA per tonne = realisation − cost per tonne; strip incentives and other income for comparability.

Practice Questions

Q1. Per-tonne build [L1]

A cement company sold 18.5 MT in FY2024-25 with net sales ₹10,360 crore, power and fuel ₹2,405 crore, freight ₹2,220 crore, raw materials ₹1,480 crore, employee cost ₹555 crore, other expenses ₹1,665 crore. Effective capacity was 25 MTPA. Compute utilisation, realisation, each cost per tonne and EBITDA per tonne.

Q2. Operating leverage [L2]

Fixed costs are ₹1,100 crore and variable cost ₹3,900 per tonne; realisation ₹5,400 per tonne. Compute EBITDA per tonne at 20 MT, 24 MT and 28 MT volumes and the percentage change in EBITDA for each 4 MT step.

💡 Hint: Fixed cost per tonne falls as volume rises.

Q3. Fuel cost sensitivity [L2]

A plant consumes 720 kcal per kg of clinker, with a clinker factor of 1.45 (tonnes of cement per tonne of clinker). Fuel cost rises from ₹1.60 to ₹1.95 per 1,000 kcal. Compute the increase in fuel cost per tonne of cement and the price increase per 50 kg bag needed to offset it.

Q4. Peer diagnosis [L3]

Two cement companies report EBITDA per tonne of ₹1,150 and ₹820. Company 1 has 70% trade share, 35% green power and lead distance 280 km; Company 2 has 55% trade share, 10% green power, lead distance 420 km. Structure a diagnosis of the gap by realisation and cost head, and list the investor-presentation data needed to quantify each driver.
