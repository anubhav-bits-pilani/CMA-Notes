Subtopic note

Retail KPIs: SSSG, Revenue per Sq.ft and Store Economics

Scope: Retail: SSSG/LFL, revenue per sq.ft, store additions, trading area, EBITDA per sq.ft, pre-Ind AS 116 store economics

Why it matters

A retailer's consolidated revenue growth mixes two very different things: more stores, and more sales per store. Investors pay for the second — it signals brand strength and operating leverage — while the first consumes capital and can mask deteriorating stores. Indian listed retailers (DMart/Avenue Supermarts, Trent, V-Mart, Shoppers Stop, Aditya Birla Fashion, Metro Brands, Devyani, Jubilant FoodWorks) report a common toolkit of KPIs in investor presentations: same-store sales growth, revenue and EBITDA per square foot, store additions and trading area, and "pre-Ind AS 116" store economics. FP&A teams in retail build budgets on exactly these drivers, and equity models forecast revenue as stores × area × throughput. Knowing the definitions — and how each company bends them — is essential to comparing peers.

Core notes

Same-store sales growth (SSSG) / like-for-like (LFL)

\[ \text{SSSG} = \frac{\text{Revenue of comparable stores}_t}{\text{Revenue of the same stores}_{t-1}} - 1 \]

The comparable-store base is the set of stores operating throughout both periods, usually defined as stores open for at least 12 (sometimes 18 or 24) months before the start of the comparison period. Definition choices that change the number:

  • Maturity threshold (12 vs 24 months): newer stores grow faster during ramp-up; a shorter threshold inflates SSSG.
  • Renovated, relocated or resized stores: excluded, included, or included on a per-sq.ft basis.
  • Closed stores: excluded from both periods (survivorship flatters SSSG).
  • Channel: whether online sales fulfilled by stores (omnichannel) are attributed to stores.
  • Calendar: festive shifts (Diwali in October vs November) distort quarterly SSSG; companies sometimes report "calendar-adjusted" growth.
  • Revenue measure: gross of GST or net; including or excluding concessionaire/shop-in-shop sales.

LFL is the UK term; Indian companies use SSSG, LFL growth and "like-to-like" interchangeably. Volume vs ticket decomposition: SSSG ≈ (1 + footfall growth) × (1 + conversion growth) × (1 + average transaction value growth) − 1.

Revenue per square foot and trading area

\[ \text{Revenue per sq.ft} = \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average trading (retail) area}} \]

Use trading or carpet area (selling space) rather than built-up area, and average area (opening + closing ÷ 2, or monthly weighted) to avoid penalising new stores opened late in the year. DMart reports revenue per sq.ft on retail business area; apparel retailers use weighted average area. Revenue per sq.ft measures space productivity and is the key metric for format comparison (hypermarket vs value fashion vs premium apparel).

Store additions: net stores added = gross openings − closures; report also area added (sq.ft), since a retailer shifting to larger formats can add fewer stores but more area. A falling revenue per sq.ft during rapid expansion may reflect new-store immaturity rather than decay in the mature base — cross-check with SSSG.

EBITDA per sq.ft

\[ \text{EBITDA per sq.ft} = \frac{\text{Store-level or company EBITDA}}{\text{Average trading area}} \]

Combines throughput and margin. Store-level EBITDA (four-wall EBITDA) excludes corporate overheads; company EBITDA includes them. State which.

Pre-Ind AS 116 store economics

Ind AS 116 removes rent from EBITDA, so post-116 EBITDA per sq.ft and EBITDA margin are inflated for leased stores and incomparable with pre-FY2019-20 history or with owned-store peers. Indian retailers therefore report "pre-Ind AS 116" figures:

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{pre-116}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{post-116}} - \text{Lease payments (rent) relating to capitalised leases} \]

Store unit economics used by management and investors:

Metric Definition
Capex per store / per sq.ft Fit-out, fixtures, IT, security deposit (sometimes excluded)
Store-level EBITDA margin (pre-116) (Store revenue − COGS − staff − rent − utilities − other store costs) ÷ revenue
Payback period Capex (+ initial inventory, net of payables) ÷ annual pre-116 store EBITDA
Store ROCE Pre-116 store EBIT ÷ store capital employed

Worked illustration

Value-fashion retailer FY2024-25: stores 320 opening, 380 closing (closures 10, openings 70); area 3.2 → 4.0 million sq.ft; revenue ₹5,400 crore, of which comparable stores ₹4,100 crore vs ₹3,800 crore last year; post-116 EBITDA ₹860 crore; capitalised rent ₹310 crore.

KPI Computation Result
SSSG 4,100 ÷ 3,800 − 1 7.9%
Average area (3.2 + 4.0) ÷ 2 3.6 mn sq.ft
Revenue per sq.ft ₹5,400 crore ÷ 3.6 mn ₹15,000
EBITDA per sq.ft (post-116) ₹860 crore ÷ 3.6 mn ₹2,389
Pre-116 EBITDA 860 − 310 ₹550 crore
Pre-116 EBITDA margin 550 ÷ 5,400 10.2%
EBITDA per sq.ft (pre-116) ₹550 crore ÷ 3.6 mn ₹1,528
Net store additions 70 − 10 60

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Franchise stores (FOFO): revenue recognised at wholesale; including them in area and revenue per sq.ft understates throughput (t5 s05).
  • E-commerce share: rising online sales lower revenue per sq.ft without any store deterioration.
  • Inflation: SSSG in nominal terms; in high-inflation periods real (volume) LFL may be flat.
  • Store closures: a retailer closing weak stores lifts reported revenue per sq.ft and SSSG of the remaining base.
  • Quarterly SSSG under Reg 33 results is often only in investor presentations, unaudited and definitional changes may be footnoted.
  • Lease modifications and rent concessions distort pre-116 reconstruction in specific years.

Key takeaways

  • SSSG isolates growth from existing stores; always read the comparable-store definition.
  • Revenue and EBITDA per sq.ft should use average trading area; state store-level vs company EBITDA.
  • Net store additions and area added describe expansion; cross-check with SSSG to separate new-store immaturity.
  • Pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA (post-116 EBITDA less capitalised rent) is the basis for store economics and history.
  • Store payback and store ROCE connect retail KPIs to capital allocation.

Practice Questions

Q1. SSSG under two definitions [L2]

A retailer had 200 stores at 1 April 2023. During FY2023-24 it opened 40 (20 in Q1, 20 in Q4) and closed 5. FY2024-25 revenue: 195 surviving original stores ₹3,900 crore (FY2023-24: ₹3,650 crore); Q1-opened stores ₹420 crore (FY2023-24: ₹300 crore for nine months); Q4-opened stores ₹380 crore (FY2023-24: ₹60 crore). Compute SSSG under a 12-month maturity rule (stores open for the full prior year) and a rule that includes all stores open at the start of FY2024-25.

💡 Hint: Decide for each cohort whether it was trading for the full comparative period.

Q2. Space productivity [L1]

A hypermarket chain's area rose from 15.2 to 17.8 million sq.ft and revenue from ₹49,000 crore to ₹57,800 crore. Prior-year opening area was 13.0 million sq.ft. Compute revenue per sq.ft on average area for both years and the growth rate.

Q3. Pre-116 store payback [L2]

A new 10,000 sq.ft store needs ₹4.5 crore capex and ₹1.5 crore of inventory net of payables. Expected annual revenue ₹18 crore, gross margin 34%, rent ₹1.4 crore, staff ₹1.2 crore, other store costs ₹1.0 crore. Compute pre-116 store EBITDA, margin, EBITDA per sq.ft and simple payback.

Q4. Peer comparison memo [L3]

Write a one-page memo outline comparing two apparel retailers where one reports SSSG on a 12-month base including online sales fulfilled by stores, and the other on a 24-month base excluding online. Specify the adjustments and supplementary KPIs you would use to reach a like-for-like view.
