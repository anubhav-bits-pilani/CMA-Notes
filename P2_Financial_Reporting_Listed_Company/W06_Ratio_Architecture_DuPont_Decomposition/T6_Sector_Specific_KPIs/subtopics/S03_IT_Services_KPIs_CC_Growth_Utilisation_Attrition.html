Subtopic note

IT Services KPIs: CC Growth, Utilisation, Attrition, TCV and Clients

Scope: IT services: constant-currency growth, utilization, LTM attrition, TCV/large-deal TCV, client buckets, revenue per FTE

Why it matters

Indian IT services companies (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro, Tech Mahindra, LTIMindtree) earn most revenue in US dollars, euros and pounds, sell people-hours and managed services, and win multi-year contracts. Their quarterly results under SEBI LODR Reg 33 are accompanied by fact sheets that carry a standard KPI set: constant-currency growth, utilisation, attrition, total contract value of deals won, client-size buckets and productivity per employee. Sell-side models, buy-side screens and the companies' own FP&A budgets are built on these drivers. They also explain margin moves: a 1 percentage point change in utilisation or a 1% rupee depreciation each moves EBIT margin by roughly 20–40 bps for the large players, according to management commentary.

Core notes

Constant-currency (CC) growth

\[ \text{CC growth} = \frac{\text{Revenue}_t \text{ at prior-period exchange rates}}{\text{Revenue}_{t-1}} - 1 \]

Two layers of currency effect:

  • Cross-currency (USD reporting): euro, pound and Australian dollar revenue translated to USD; a strong dollar lowers USD growth relative to CC.
  • INR translation: USD revenue translated to rupees for Ind AS reporting; rupee depreciation lifts INR growth.

Indian IT companies typically guide and report growth in CC terms (and USD), with INR growth as reported. Example: CC growth 4.0%, cross-currency impact −0.8% → USD growth 3.2%; rupee depreciation of 3% → INR growth about 6.3%.

Utilisation

\[ \text{Utilisation} = \frac{\text{Billed person-months (effort)}}{\text{Available person-months}} \]

  • Including trainees (fresher batches on the bench in training) gives a lower figure; excluding trainees measures deployability of productive staff. Infosys historically reports both.
  • Onsite vs offshore utilisation differ (onsite typically higher).
  • High utilisation (above about 85% excluding trainees) lifts margins but limits capacity to take new work and can raise attrition from burnout; low utilisation signals bench build-up ahead of demand or weak demand.

Attrition (LTM)

\[ \text{LTM attrition} = \frac{\text{Voluntary (or total) exits in the last twelve months}}{\text{Average headcount over the period}} \]

Definition differences: voluntary only vs total (including involuntary); IT services employees only vs total including BPM; average headcount computed over the twelve months vs opening-closing. Annualised quarterly attrition (quarterly exits × 4 ÷ average headcount) is more timely but volatile. FY2021-22's post-pandemic attrition spike (above 25% at several players) drove wage inflation and margin pressure in FY2022-23.

TCV and large-deal TCV

Total contract value (TCV) = total value of contracts signed during the period over their full term (including renewals for some companies). Large-deal TCV = TCV of deals above a threshold (e.g., US$50 million for Infosys, broadly similar thresholds elsewhere; check each company's definition). Distinctions:

  • Net new vs renewal TCV: renewals sustain revenue; net new drives growth.
  • Book-to-bill = TCV ÷ revenue for the period (or LTM); above 1 suggests a growing backlog.
  • TCV is not revenue: it may be recognised over three to seven years, can be terminated or descoped, is subject to volume variability, and often contains optional elements. Under Ind AS 115, the disclosed remaining performance obligation (RPO) is the audited, contractual measure of backlog (subject to practical expedients), whereas TCV is a management KPI (topic related item).

Client buckets

Number of clients by annual revenue: US$1 million+, US$5 million+, US$10 million+, US$50 million+, US$100 million+. Growth in larger buckets indicates mining of existing accounts; top-5/top-10 client concentration (% of revenue) indicates dependence risk. Revenue from top clients growing faster than the company indicates deepening relationships; the reverse may indicate vendor consolidation losses.

Revenue per FTE

\[ \text{Revenue per FTE} = \frac{\text{Revenue (annualised)}}{\text{Average headcount}} \]

Computed in USD for comparison (about US$50,000–60,000 per employee for large Indian IT firms). Rising revenue per FTE signals pricing, mix toward digital/consulting, automation or higher utilisation; falling indicates fresher-heavy pyramid or pricing pressure. Some companies report revenue per billable FTE (excluding support staff).

Worked illustration

Quarterly data for a large IT firm (US$ million): Q3 revenue 4,850; Q2 4,760; Q3 revenue at Q2 rates 4,885 (cross- currency headwind). Billed effort 1,64,000 person-months; available excl. trainees 1,95,000; trainees 9,000 person-months. Headcount opening 3,15,000, closing 3,18,000; LTM voluntary exits 40,500 on LTM average headcount 3,16,000. Large-deal TCV US$2,400 million.

KPI Computation Result
CC growth QoQ 4,885 ÷ 4,760 − 1 2.6%
USD growth QoQ 4,850 ÷ 4,760 − 1 1.9%
Utilisation excl. trainees 1,64,000 ÷ 1,95,000 84.1%
Utilisation incl. trainees 1,64,000 ÷ 2,04,000 80.4%
LTM attrition 40,500 ÷ 3,16,000 12.8%
Annualised revenue per FTE 4,850 × 4 ÷ 3,16,500 US$61,300
Large-deal book-to-bill 2,400 ÷ 4,850 0.49x

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Acquisitions inflate reported and CC growth; companies report organic CC growth separately — use it.
  • Pass-through revenue (third-party hardware/software resale) lowers revenue per FTE quality and margin.
  • Subcontractors do billed work but are excluded from headcount — revenue per FTE is overstated; check subcontracting cost as % of revenue.
  • Mega-deal renewals reported in TCV can be largely existing revenue; read the net-new disclosure.
  • Utilisation definitions differ (billed vs deployed); compare trends within a company more than levels across.
  • Hedging gains on forex forward contracts (Ind AS 109 cash flow hedges) sit in revenue or other income depending on policy — affecting reported vs CC growth reconciliation.

Key takeaways

  • CC growth strips cross-currency effects; USD and INR growth add translation effects.
  • Utilisation should be read incl./excl. trainees; it drives margin and capacity.
  • LTM attrition must be compared on consistent definitions (voluntary, IT-only, average headcount).
  • TCV is a booking KPI, not revenue; RPO under Ind AS 115 is the audited backlog measure.
  • Client buckets and revenue per FTE show account mining and productivity trends.

Practice Questions

Q1. CC versus reported growth [L1]

An IT company's revenue was US$2,400 million in FY2024-25 Q1 and US$2,470 million in Q2. At Q1 exchange rates Q2 revenue would have been US$2,455 million. The rupee moved from ₹83.4 to ₹83.9 per USD on average. Compute CC, USD and approximate INR QoQ growth and explain the direction of the cross-currency effect.

Q2. Utilisation and margin sensitivity [L2]

A firm has 2,00,000 delivery employees (10,000 trainees), billing rate US$28 per hour, 160 hours per month, cost per employee US$3,000 per month. Utilisation excluding trainees moves from 82% to 85%. Estimate the monthly revenue and EBIT impact, assuming no change in headcount or billing rates.

💡 Hint: Extra billed hours come from existing non-trainee staff, so cost is unchanged.

Q3. TCV interpretation [L2]

A company announces record TCV of US$4.2 billion in a quarter, of which US$3.1 billion is a seven-year renewal of its largest client (previous contract annual value US$400 million) and the rest net new deals of average tenor four years. Estimate the incremental annual revenue implied and explain why "record TCV" may not mean faster growth.

Q4. Client bucket analysis [L3]

Over two years the number of US$100 million+ clients rose from 18 to 22, while top-10 client share rose from 28% to 33% and overall CC growth was 3%. Discuss what this implies about account mining, concentration risk and the breadth of growth, and specify two further data points you would request.
