Key concept

Composite Score as a Weighted Sum of Normalized Scores

Concept: Composite score = Σ (Weight_i × Normalized score_i), with Σ weights = 1

Statement

\[ S_p = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \times n_{p,i}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i = 1, \qquad w_i \ge 0 \]

where \(S_p\) is the composite score of peer \(p\), \(w_i\) is the weight on metric \(i\), \(n_{p,i}\) is peer \(p\)'s direction-adjusted normalised score on metric \(i\) (z-score, rank score or percentile, the same method for all metrics), and \(M\) is the number of metrics.

Excel:

=SUMPRODUCT($C$20:$C$26, D20:D26)
=ROUND(SUM($C$20:$C$26),6)=1        ' weight check, feeds master check summary

Explanation

Why weights must sum to one. The composite then stays on the same scale as the inputs. A z-score composite is "average standard deviations above the peer mean", and a percentile composite stays in 0–1. This makes scores comparable across weight scenarios in sensitivity analysis (s03). Weights that sum to 1.2 silently inflate every score and break comparisons between scenarios.

Why non-negative. Direction is handled in normalisation (c03). A negative weight would reverse a metric's meaning a second time and hide the logic.

Properties:

  • With z-score inputs, the composite across the peer set also sums to zero. It ranks peers relative to each other, not against an absolute standard.
  • The composite is linear. A peer's lead can come from one huge score or many small ones. Show the contribution of each metric (\(w_i \times n_{p,i}\)) in a stacked bar or table so the reader sees why a peer wins.
  • Effective weight depends on score dispersion. Under rank scores every metric has the same spread, but under capped z-scores a metric with one outlier has less effective spread for the other peers. Nominal weight is not always actual influence.
  • Correlated metrics double-count. If ROIC and margin are 0.9-correlated across peers, a 25% + 20% weighting gives that dimension close to 45% influence.

Governance. Weights and their rationale belong on the definitions/assumptions sheet. They should be fixed before results are seen and versioned in the change log. Any change after the first draft is a documented sensitivity, not a silent edit.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and decompose a composite [L1]

Weights: CAGR 0.20, margin 0.15, CV 0.10, ROIC 0.25, CCC 0.15, leverage 0.10, CFO/EBITDA 0.05. Peer J's direction-adjusted z-scores are +0.8, −0.3, +0.2, +0.6, −1.1, −0.4 and +0.5. Compute the composite, list each metric's contribution and identify the two largest drivers.

Q2. Weight-sum error [L2]

A draft workbook's weights sum to 1.15 because a 15% CFO/EBITDA weight was added without rescaling. Peers' composites were +0.62, +0.10 and −0.72. Explain how this affects (a) the ranking, (b) comparison with an equal-weight scenario and (c) the narrative. Describe the check that would have caught it.

💡 Hint: Consider whether a uniform scaling of weights changes the order of peers, and whether the added weight was uniform.

Q3. Correlated metrics [L3]

Across six IT peers, the correlation between EBITDA margin and ROIC z-scores is 0.92. Your base weights are margin 20% and ROIC 25%. Propose two ways to correct the double-counting, show the revised weights under one approach, and explain how you would disclose the change.
