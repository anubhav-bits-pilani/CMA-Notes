Key concept

Margin Stability as the Coefficient of Variation

Concept: Margin stability = Standard deviation of annual EBITDA margins ÷ Mean margin (coefficient of variation)

Statement

\[ \text{CV} = \frac{s_m}{\bar{m}}, \qquad s_m = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{y=1}^{N} (m_y - \bar{m})^2}{N - 1}}, \qquad \bar{m} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{y=1}^{N} m_y \]

where \(m_y\) is the normalised EBITDA margin in year \(y\), \(N\) is the number of years (typically 3–5), \(s_m\) is the sample standard deviation and \(\bar{m}\) is the mean margin. Lower CV means more stable margins. It is a lower-is-better metric, so multiply by −1 when normalising (c03).

Excel:

=STDEV.S(C8:E8)/AVERAGE(C8:E8)

Explanation

Why scale by the mean. A 1-percentage-point standard deviation means different things for a 25% IT margin (very stable) and a 6% jewellery margin (quite volatile). Dividing by the mean makes stability comparable across peers with different margin levels. This is the same logic as relative risk in statistics.

What it captures. Pricing power, cost pass-through and operating leverage. IT majors typically show low CV because of contracted revenue and a flexible cost base (utilisation, subcontractors). Jewellers' margins move with gold-price volatility, hedging outcomes, studded mix shifts and customs-duty changes (for example the July 2024 cut in gold import duty created inventory losses for some players).

Conditions and cautions:

  • Few observations. With 3 years, \(N - 1 = 2\) and the estimate is very noisy. Use 5 years where possible, or show the min–max range alongside.
  • Trend vs volatility. A steadily rising margin (15%, 17%, 19%) has a non-zero CV even though it is predictable. Consider detrended volatility (standard deviation of residuals from a linear trend) or year-on-year changes, and explain in the narrative.
  • Low or negative mean. CV explodes when the mean approaches zero and is meaningless if it is negative. Fall back to the absolute standard deviation (in percentage points) and flag it.
  • Use normalised margins. One-off items (exceptional gains, inventory write-downs) inflate measured volatility. Strip them with the Week 5 rules, applied identically to all peers.
  • STDEV.S vs STDEV.P. Use one consistently. STDEV.S is conventional for a short sample.

Illustration

Peer FY23 FY24 FY25 Mean STDEV.S (pp) CV
IT Peer A 26.8% 27.0% 27.4% 27.07% 0.31 0.011
Jeweller J1 11.5% 10.6% 9.6% 10.57% 0.95 0.090
Jeweller J2 7.0% 8.1% 6.5% 7.20% 0.82 0.114

J2 has a lower absolute standard deviation than J1 (0.82 vs 0.95 pp) but a higher CV (0.114 vs 0.090), because its margin base is smaller. J1's volatility is mostly a steady decline, which is a trend and not noise. The narrative should say "J1's margin is compressing steadily" rather than "J1's margin is volatile".

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute CV for three peers [L1]

EBITDA margins FY21–FY25: Peer X 24.1%, 25.3%, 24.6%, 24.0%, 24.8%; Peer Y 18.0%, 21.5%, 19.2%, 22.4%, 20.1%; Peer Z 9.2%, 8.1%, 10.4%, 7.6%, 9.0%. Compute the mean, STDEV.S and CV for each, and rank stability.

Q2. Trend vs volatility [L2]

Peer M's margins were 14.0%, 15.5%, 17.0%, 18.5% and 20.0%. Peer N's were 17.0%, 18.6%, 15.8%, 18.4% and 17.2%. Compute CV for both. Then compute the standard deviation of year-on-year margin changes. Explain which measure better represents "stability" for each peer and what you would show in the report.

💡 Hint: Peer M's year-on-year changes are constant.

Q3. When CV breaks [L2]

A turnaround peer's EBITDA margins were −2.0%, 1.5% and 4.0%. Explain why CV is unsuitable here, propose an alternative stability measure and describe how you would treat this peer in a composite score so the matrix remains comparable.
