Key concept

Three-Year Revenue CAGR

Concept: 3-year revenue CAGR = (Revenue_t ÷ Revenue_t−3)^(1/3) − 1

Statement

\[ \text{CAGR}_{3} = \left(\frac{\text{Revenue}_t}{\text{Revenue}_{t-3}}\right)^{1/3} - 1 \]

where \(\text{Revenue}_t\) is revenue for the latest fiscal year (for example FY25), \(\text{Revenue}_{t-3}\) is revenue three fiscal years earlier (FY22), and the exponent \(1/3\) reflects three annual growth intervals (FY22→23, FY23→24, FY24→25). Note that four years of data are needed.

Excel:

=(F5/C5)^(1/3)-1
=RRI(3, C5, F5)

RRI(n, pv, fv) returns the equivalent compound rate directly.

Explanation

CAGR is the constant annual growth rate that takes the base-year value to the end-year value. It is a geometric mean of the three annual growth factors:

\[ 1 + \text{CAGR}_3 = \big[(1+g_1)(1+g_2)(1+g_3)\big]^{1/3} \]

so it is always at most the arithmetic mean of annual growth rates (equal only if all three rates are equal).

Conditions and pitfalls:

  • Endpoint sensitivity. Only the first and last values matter. A depressed base year (for example FY21 COVID disruption for jewellers) inflates CAGR, and an unusually strong end year does the same. Always show the annual growth path next to it.
  • Same basis at both ends. Both endpoints must be consolidated, continuing operations, calendarized and restated on the same basis (topic t1 s03, s06). A discontinued operation removed from \(t\) but not from \(t-3\) understates growth.
  • Inorganic growth. Acquisitions inside the window inflate CAGR. Report organic CAGR where disclosed.
  • Currency and inflation. For IT exporters, ₹ CAGR includes rupee depreciation. Show USD and constant-currency CAGR. For jewellers, gold-price inflation can contribute a large share of revenue growth, so volume growth is more informative where data permits.
  • Negative or zero base. CAGR is undefined for a non-positive base. This is relevant for EBITDA or PAT CAGR, not usually for revenue.
  • Partial periods. If one endpoint is LTM, the exponent must reflect the actual elapsed time, for example \(1/3.5\) for three and a half years.

Illustration

Jeweller revenue (₹ crore, illustrative): FY22 10,820; FY23 14,070; FY24 18,550; FY25 25,050.

\[ \text{CAGR}_3 = \left(\frac{25{,}050}{10{,}820}\right)^{1/3} - 1 = (2.3152)^{1/3} - 1 \approx 32.3\% \]

Annual growth was 30.0%, 31.8% and 35.0%, with an arithmetic mean of 32.3%. The two figures coincide here only because growth was steady. If gold prices rose about 12% a year over the period and the studded mix was stable, implied volume/value-ex-gold-price growth is roughly \(1.323 / 1.12 - 1 \approx 18\%\). This is a much more modest figure for the narrative.

Practice Questions

Q1. CAGR vs average growth [L1]

An IT company's revenue (₹ crore) was FY22 1,23,940, FY23 1,46,770, FY24 1,53,670 and FY25 1,62,990. Compute annual growth rates, their arithmetic mean and the 3-year CAGR (also write the RRI formula). Explain why the two measures differ.

Q2. Base-year distortion [L2]

A jeweller's revenue was FY21 ₹6,200 crore (lockdown-affected), FY22 ₹10,100 crore, FY23 ₹12,400 crore, FY24 ₹14,900 crore and FY25 ₹17,600 crore. Compute the 3-year CAGR ending FY24 and ending FY25. Explain which you would use in a report and how you would caveat it.

💡 Hint: Check which window includes the abnormal base year.

Q3. Constant-currency and organic CAGR [L2]

An IT peer's USD revenue grew from USD 15,800 million (FY22) to USD 19,300 million (FY25), including an acquisition contributing USD 900 million in FY25. The average ₹/USD rate moved from 74.5 to 84.6. Compute ₹ CAGR, USD CAGR and organic USD CAGR, and state which belongs in the matrix and why.
