Subtopic note

Handling Net-Cash Peers and Outliers in the Matrix

Scope: Handling net-cash peers and outliers (cap/floor, separate net-cash flag)

Why it matters

Two kinds of awkward data break naive matrices. First, net-cash peers. Infosys, TCS and Wipro all hold more cash and liquid investments than debt, including lease liabilities. Their net debt/EBITDA is negative, and the ratio's behaviour near zero and below zero is not monotonic in "risk". Second, outliers: an asset-light peer with 70% ROIC, a jeweller with 300 inventory days, or a year with a one-off margin spike. Under z-score normalisation (s02) one extreme value can dominate a composite (s03), and under ranking it can hide meaningful gaps. Both problems appear in almost every Indian peer set. A reviewer will test how you dealt with them before trusting the ranking.

Core notes

Why net debt/EBITDA misbehaves

\[ \text{ND/EBITDA} = \frac{\text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities} - \text{Cash} - \text{Liquid investments}}{\text{EBITDA}} \]

  • Negative numerator (net cash). A value of −1.5x means net cash equal to 1.5 years of EBITDA. More negative is "safer", but beyond a point extra cash says nothing about operating quality. It may even signal poor capital allocation (lazy balance sheet, low payout).
  • Small or negative denominator. As EBITDA approaches zero the ratio explodes. A peer with net debt ₹500 crore and EBITDA ₹10 crore shows 50x. With EBITDA of −₹10 crore it shows −50x, which a naive "lower is better" ranking would score as the safest company.
  • Sign ambiguity. Negative ND/EBITDA can mean net cash with positive EBITDA (good) or net debt with negative EBITDA (very bad). The ratio alone cannot tell them apart.

Treatment 1: cap/floor the ratio before normalising

Define a bounded "leverage score input":

\[ L_i = \begin{cases} \text{Cap} & \text{if EBITDA}_i \le 0 \text{ and ND}_i > 0 \\ \max(\text{Floor}, \min(\text{Cap}, \text{ND}_i / \text{EBITDA}_i)) & \text{if EBITDA}_i > 0 \end{cases} \]

with, for example, Floor = 0.0x (all net-cash peers treated as equally unlevered) and Cap = 5.0x (stressed). For the rare case of negative EBITDA with net cash, set the flag and assess manually.

=LET(nd, B12, e, B13, cap, 5, flr, 0,
     IF(e<=0, IF(nd>0, cap, NA()),
        MAX(flr, MIN(cap, nd/e))))

With Floor = 0, the three net-cash IT peers all score identically on leverage. That is correct, because leverage then contributes no ranking information, which matches the s03 advice to weight it near zero for IT.

Treatment 2: a separate net-cash flag

Show a net-cash flag (Yes/No) and net cash as % of market cap or of total assets as context columns outside the composite. The reader sees that TCS holds, say, net cash of 5% of market cap without that number distorting the operating ranking. For jewellers, where some peers are levered and others nearly net-cash, use a flag column plus the capped ratio.

Treatment 3: substitute a better-behaved metric

  • Net debt/(Net debt + Equity) (net gearing) is bounded below by a negative number but stays monotonic and is not sensitive to EBITDA near zero.
  • EBITDA/interest (interest coverage) is higher-is-better. Cap it (for example at 30x) because net-cash firms have tiny interest costs (mostly lease interest).
  • Net cash/EBITDA (sign-flipped) for an all-net-cash set, if you genuinely want to reward liquidity. State why.

Outliers in other metrics

Detect. Flag values beyond the peer median ± 2 × MAD (median absolute deviation), or beyond Q1 − 1.5 × IQR and Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. With 3–5 peers, statistical tests are weak, so combine them with judgement: is the value economically explained?

Classify.

Outlier type Example Treatment
Data error Unit mismatch (₹ lakh vs ₹ crore) Fix the data (topic t1 s06)
One-off event Inventory gain on customs-duty cut, divestment gain Normalise (Week 5) and keep
Structural difference 70% ROIC from an asset-light model; 300-day DIO from studded mix Keep, cap the score, caveat
Definition difference One peer includes unbilled revenue in DSO Fix the definition (pitfall 3)

Cap/floor (winsorise) techniques.

  • Winsorise raw values at the peer 5th/95th percentile (large sets) or at fixed economically sensible bounds (ROIC capped at 60%, CCC floored at −30 days).
  • Cap normalised scores, for example z-scores capped at ±2.0 so no single metric contributes more than \(2 \times w_m\) to the composite.
=MAX(-2, MIN(2, z))
  • Use rank or percentile for that metric only if the raw distribution is extremely skewed. Do not mix methods across metrics (s02). If you must switch, switch the whole matrix and show the alternative.

Raw ratios would give J4's 30x an enormous negative z-score and squash J1–J3 together. After capping, J4 is simply "worst" at 5x, and J3 is recognised as unlevered without being over-rewarded for its small cash pile.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cash that is not free. Earmarked balances, margin money, unpaid dividend accounts and customer advances (jewellery gold-saving schemes) are not surplus cash. Exclude them from the cash deducted in net debt.
  • Gold metal loans. Including GML in net debt can swing a jeweller from net-cash to levered. Apply one rule to all peers and disclose it.
  • Lease liabilities. Post-Ind AS 116, a net-cash IT company can become "net debt" if lease liabilities exceed surplus cash. Check both definitions (s06).
  • Choosing the cap. Tie the cap to an economic threshold, such as rating-agency comfort levels (around 3–4x for many corporates). Do not tie it to whatever value makes the chart look tidy.
  • Outliers in the base year. A CAGR base-year outlier (a COVID-hit FY21) distorts growth. Shift the window or show a two-period CAGR as a cross-check.

Key takeaways

  • Net debt/EBITDA is non-monotonic around zero and explodes as EBITDA approaches zero. Never rank it raw.
  • Floor net-cash peers at 0x, cap stressed peers (for example 5x), and treat negative EBITDA with net debt as "worst".
  • Show a separate net-cash flag and net cash as % of market cap or assets outside the composite.
  • Detect outliers with median/MAD or IQR plus judgement, and classify them as error, one-off, structural or definitional before treating them.
  • Cap normalised scores (for example ±2) or winsorise raw values so no single metric dominates, and disclose the rule.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cap/floor leverage inputs [L1]

Four IT peers have net debt (including lease liabilities) of −₹62,000 crore, −₹38,000 crore, −₹14,500 crore and +₹1,200 crore. Their EBITDA is ₹68,000 crore, ₹40,000 crore, ₹18,000 crore and ₹2,100 crore. Compute raw ND/EBITDA and capped/floored inputs with Floor 0x and Cap 4x. Explain what weight you would give the leverage metric and why.

Q2. The negative-EBITDA trap [L2]

A naive matrix ranks five companies on raw ND/EBITDA ascending: A: −1.2x (net cash, EBITDA positive), B: 0.8x, C: 2.1x, D: −6.0x (net debt ₹600 crore, EBITDA −₹100 crore), E: 3.5x. Show the naive ranking and explain the error. Then construct the corrected inputs and ranking, and write the footnote.

💡 Hint: Separate the sign of the numerator from the sign of the denominator before interpreting.

Q3. Outlier classification and treatment [L2]

In a five-peer jewellery set, DIO values are 165, 180, 210, 225 and 340 days. Peer 5's 340 days reflects a 45% studded and diamond mix against 15–25% for others, plus a ₹600 crore inventory pre-build for 40 new stores opening in Q1 of the next year. Classify the outlier, propose a treatment (raw-value adjustment, score cap or caveat), compute median ± 2 × MAD, and draft the matrix footnote.

Q4. Designing net-debt definitions [L3]

Write the definitions-sheet entry for "Net debt" for a jewellery peer set. Cover borrowings, gold metal loans, lease liabilities, cash, bank balances other than cash, earmarked balances, customer advances under gold-savings schemes and current investments. Then explain how each choice could change the ranking of a peer with large gold-savings scheme deposits.

Q5. Excel implementation of score caps [L2]

Write the formulas that (a) compute direction-adjusted z-scores for a seven-metric by five-peer matrix in one dynamic array, (b) cap them at ±2 and (c) flag every capped cell for conditional formatting. Explain how the master check summary should report the number of capped cells.
