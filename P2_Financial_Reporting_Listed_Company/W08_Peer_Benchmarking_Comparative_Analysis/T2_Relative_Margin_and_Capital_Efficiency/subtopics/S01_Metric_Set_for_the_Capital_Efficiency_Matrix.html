Subtopic note

Metric Set for the Capital Efficiency Matrix

Scope: Metric set: 3-year revenue CAGR, EBITDA margin level and volatility, ROIC, CCC, net debt/EBITDA, CFO/EBITDA

Why it matters

A peer matrix compresses three years of financial statements into a handful of numbers that drive a ranking and, ultimately, the governing conclusion of the report. The metric set therefore has to cover the full value-creation chain and nothing redundant. That chain is growth (is the business getting bigger?), profitability (how much of revenue becomes operating profit, and how reliably?), returns (how much profit per rupee of capital?), capital intensity (how much working capital does growth absorb?), balance-sheet risk (how much debt sits on the earnings?) and earnings quality (does profit turn into cash?). Equity research "scorecards", rating agency peer tables and corporate FP&A benchmarking packs use variants of this set. Each metric has a precise definition, a direction ("higher is better" or "lower is better") and known failure modes.

Core notes

The six metrics and what each answers

# Metric Question answered Direction Formula
1 3-year revenue CAGR Growth momentum Higher better \((\text{Rev}_t / \text{Rev}_{t-3})^{1/3} - 1\)
2a EBITDA margin level Operating profitability Higher better Normalised EBITDA ÷ Revenue (3-year average or latest)
2b EBITDA margin volatility Predictability of profitability Lower better Std. dev. of annual margins ÷ mean margin
3 ROIC Return on operating capital Higher better NOPAT ÷ Average invested capital
4 Cash conversion cycle (CCC) Working-capital intensity Lower better DIO + DSO − DPO
5 Net debt/EBITDA Leverage and solvency Lower better (with net-cash caveat) (Borrowings + lease liabilities − cash and liquid investments) ÷ EBITDA
6 CFO/EBITDA Cash conversion / earnings quality Higher better (but values above 100% need explanation) Cash from operations ÷ EBITDA

1. Three-year revenue CAGR

This uses four data points (\(t-3\) to \(t\)) and three growth intervals (concept c01). It smooths single-year noise but is endpoint-sensitive: a weak base year inflates CAGR. Always show annual growth alongside it. For IT peers, also show USD and constant-currency CAGR. For jewellers, consider separating gold-price inflation from volume growth (grams sold, or same-store sales growth where disclosed).

2. EBITDA margin: level and volatility

The level says how profitable the model is. The volatility (coefficient of variation, concept c02) says how reliably it delivers. Two peers with a 21% average margin are not equal if one swung from 17% to 25%. Use normalised EBITDA (Week 5) and decide whether "other income" (large treasury income for net-cash IT firms) is excluded. It must be excluded for all peers.

3. ROIC

\[ \text{ROIC} = \frac{\text{EBIT} \times (1 - t)}{\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{IC}_{\text{open}} + \text{IC}_{\text{close}})} \]

Invested capital (IC) on the operating approach is net working capital plus net PP&E, ROU assets, intangibles and goodwill (state whether goodwill is included). Use a normalised tax rate: 25.17% under Sec 115BAA for most domestic companies, or each peer's sustainable effective rate. Net-cash IT companies have huge cash balances. Excluding surplus cash from IC is essential, or ROIC collapses into a measure of treasury policy.

4. Cash conversion cycle

\[ \text{CCC} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Avg inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365}_{\text{DIO}} + \underbrace{\frac{\text{Avg trade receivables}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 365}_{\text{DSO}} - \underbrace{\frac{\text{Avg trade payables}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365}_{\text{DPO}} \]

Jewellers have very long DIO (often 150–300+ days) and short DSO (retail cash sales). IT has almost no inventory, but DSO should include unbilled revenue (contract assets), or it understates working capital. Gold-metal-loan financing behaves like payables economically, but it is a borrowing. Decide its treatment once and apply it to all peers (topic t1).

5. Net debt/EBITDA

Lower is better, but the ratio breaks when net debt is negative (net cash) or EBITDA is small or negative. See s04 for the cap/floor and net-cash flag. Include lease liabilities when EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 (consistency of numerator and denominator, s06).

6. CFO/EBITDA

This tests whether EBITDA turns into operating cash. Typical drags are tax paid (EBITDA is pre-tax), working-capital build and non-cash income in EBITDA. A ratio persistently below 60–70% in a mature business is a Week 7 forensic flag. A ratio above 100% usually reflects working-capital release, which is not sustainable. Use a 3-year cumulative ratio to smooth timing.

Notice that all three are net-cash. Leverage carries almost no information for ranking quality here, so s03 and s04 handle it with a flag or a reduced weight rather than a naive rank. For jewellers the opposite holds: leverage and CCC are first-order metrics.

Building the metric block in Excel

Keep one row per metric and one column per peer, all driven from the standardized statements by formulas referencing the definitions sheet. A direction row (+1 / −1) next to each metric feeds the normalisation step (s02). A "weight" row feeds the composite (s03).

=LET(r0, INDEX(Std_Rev, , Col_FY22), r3, INDEX(Std_Rev, , Col_FY25),
     (r3/r0)^(1/3) - 1)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Metric overlap. EBITDA margin and ROIC are linked through capital turnover (ROIC ≈ NOPAT margin × IC turnover). Including EBIT margin as well as EBITDA margin double-weights profitability. Choose one per dimension.
  • Sector relevance. Weight leverage low for net-cash IT peers. Weight CCC and leverage high for jewellers. Sector KPIs (Week 6 t6: attrition, utilisation, deal TCV; same-store sales growth, studded ratio, store additions) contextualise but usually do not enter the composite.
  • Acquisitions. An acquisition inflates CAGR and IC (goodwill). Show ROIC with and without goodwill, and organic growth where disclosed.
  • Negative or near-zero denominators. CFO/EBITDA and net debt/EBITDA are meaningless when EBITDA is negative. Flag rather than rank.
  • Gold price effect. A jeweller's revenue CAGR includes gold-price inflation of around 10–15% a year in some periods. Margins in % may fall even as rupee EBITDA rises, because making-charge-based margins do not scale with gold price.

Key takeaways

  • Six metrics cover growth, profitability (level and stability), returns, working capital, leverage and cash quality, with one metric per dimension.
  • Each metric needs a fixed definition and a direction (higher or lower is better) recorded on the definitions sheet.
  • Exclude surplus cash from invested capital and other income from EBITDA consistently, especially for net-cash IT peers.
  • Include unbilled revenue in IT DSO, and decide gold-metal-loan treatment once for jewellers.
  • Sector context decides which metrics carry information. Net debt/EBITDA is near-useless when every peer is net-cash.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the metric block [L2]

Jeweller J reports (₹ crore): revenue FY22 9,800, FY23 11,400, FY24 13,500, FY25 15,900; normalised EBITDA FY23 820, FY24 990, FY25 1,180; EBIT FY25 960; tax rate 25.17%; invested capital opening FY25 6,200 and closing 7,000; average inventory 5,400; COGS 13,100; average receivables 150; average payables 700; borrowings incl. gold metal loans 3,300; lease liabilities 900; cash 400; FY25 CFO 610. Compute all six metrics for FY25 (margin level and CV over FY23–FY25). Comment on which metric most needs a footnote.

💡 Hint: Use STDEV.S-style sample standard deviation for the three margins and state that choice.

Q2. Unbilled revenue in DSO [L1]

An IT company has average trade receivables of ₹28,000 crore, average unbilled revenue (contract assets plus unbilled receivables) of ₹12,000 crore and revenue of ₹1,62,000 crore. Compute DSO with and without unbilled revenue. Explain which belongs in a peer CCC and why.

Q3. Choosing metrics for a sector [L3]

For a jewellery peer set (Titan, Kalyan, Senco) and an IT peer set (Infosys, TCS, Wipro), propose weights for the six metrics summing to 100% for each sector. Justify every weight difference by reference to the economics of the sector, and identify one sector KPI per sector that you would show beside the matrix without entering it into the composite.

Q4. Redundancy test [L2]

A colleague proposes a matrix with 3-year revenue CAGR, 3-year EBITDA CAGR, EBITDA margin, EBIT margin, PAT margin, ROE, ROIC and net debt/equity. Identify the overlaps, explain how they distort a composite score, and propose a non-overlapping replacement set.

Q5. CFO/EBITDA above 100% [L2]

Peer K reports FY25 EBITDA of ₹1,200 crore and CFO of ₹1,350 crore, after a ₹420 crore reduction in inventory following a store-network rationalisation. FY23 and FY24 CFO/EBITDA were 58% and 61%. Compute the three-year cumulative CFO/EBITDA given FY23 EBITDA ₹950 crore and FY24 ₹1,060 crore. Explain how you would present this metric in the matrix and narrative.
