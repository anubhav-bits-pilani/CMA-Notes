Subtopic note

Testing Ranking Robustness to Pre- and Post-Ind AS 116 Definitions

Scope: Testing ranking robustness to pre/post Ind AS 116 definitions

Why it matters

Ind AS 116 Leases took effect for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2019. It moved almost every lease onto the lessee's balance sheet. For lease-heavy businesses such as jewellery and retail chains (store leases) and IT services (office campuses, though many are owned), it changed EBITDA, net debt, invested capital, CFO and every ratio built on them. It did not change the underlying economics. Peers differ in lease intensity: an owned-store jeweller has few leases, a leased-store jeweller has many, and a franchise-heavy jeweller has few on its own books. So the choice between a post-116 definition (as reported) and a pre-116 definition (lease cost treated as an operating expense) can reorder the ranking. A credible matrix shows the conclusion under both. Lenders and rating agencies often look at both views, and many loan covenants are still defined on "frozen GAAP" or pre-116 bases.

Core notes

What Ind AS 116 changed

Item Pre-116 (Ind AS 17 operating lease) Post-116 (as reported)
P&L Rent in other expenses (above EBITDA) Depreciation of ROU asset + interest on lease liability (below EBITDA)
EBITDA Lower Higher, by roughly the lease payments
EBIT Lower Slightly higher (rent minus ROU depreciation ≈ interest component)
PBT — Front-loaded expense (interest is higher in early years)
Balance sheet Off-balance sheet ROU asset and lease liability
Net debt Excludes leases Includes lease liabilities (if defined so)
CFO Rent in operating outflow Principal in financing. Interest in financing or operating (Ind AS 7 policy)
Invested capital Excludes ROU Includes ROU (operating approach)

Short-term (12 months or less) and low-value leases can stay expensed (Ind AS 116.5–8). Variable lease payments not based on an index or rate (for example revenue-share rent common in mall stores) stay in operating expenses even after 116. This is an important source of residual non-comparability between peers.

Constructing the two definitions

Post-116 (reported basis):

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{post}} = \text{reported EBITDA}, \qquad \text{ND}_{\text{post}} = \text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities} - \text{Cash} \]

\[ \text{IC}_{\text{post}} = \text{NWC} + \text{Net PP\&E} + \text{ROU assets} + \text{Intangibles} \]

Pre-116 (proxy):

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{pre}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{post}} - \text{Lease payments (principal + interest)} \]

\[ \text{ND}_{\text{pre}} = \text{Borrowings} - \text{Cash}, \qquad \text{IC}_{\text{pre}} = \text{IC}_{\text{post}} - \text{ROU assets} \]

\[ \text{EBIT}_{\text{pre}} = \text{EBIT}_{\text{post}} - \text{Interest on lease liabilities} \]

Lease payments come from the cash flow statement (financing activities: "payment of lease liabilities" principal and interest) or the maturity-analysis note. Using cash payments approximates the old straight-line rent.

A third, "lease-adjusted" view. Some analysts prefer an EBITDAR-style view: compare EBITDAR (EBITDA before all rent, including variable and short-term) with lease-adjusted net debt. This is useful when peers have very different own-vs-lease mixes.

Running the robustness test

  1. Compute all six metrics (s01) under both definitions for every peer-year.
  2. Normalise and composite each version with the same weights (s02–s03).
  3. Compare ranks in a side-by-side table and compute the rank change.
  4. If any rank changes, explain why in the narrative. Usually it is lease intensity: lease payments as % of revenue, and ROU as % of IC.
  5. Choose a primary definition, typically post-116 because it is reported, auditable and current. Footnote the alternative result.

A Spearman rank correlation between the two rankings summarises stability (1.0 = identical order):

\[ \rho = 1 - \frac{6 \sum d_i^2}{n(n^2 - 1)} \]

where \(d_i\) is the rank difference for peer \(i\) and \(n\) is the number of peers. With 3 peers, ρ can only take the values 1, 0.5, −0.5 and −1, so just report the rank changes directly.

Illustration: three jewellers, FY25 (₹ crore, illustrative)

J1 (owned-heavy) J2 (leased-heavy) J3 (franchise-heavy)
Revenue 20,000 18,000 12,000
EBITDA (post-116) 1,700 1,620 780
Lease payments (cash) 120 540 60
EBITDA (pre-116) 1,580 1,080 720
Margin post / pre 8.5% / 7.9% 9.0% / 6.0% 6.5% / 6.0%
Borrowings − cash 2,200 1,900 500
Lease liabilities 600 2,700 250
ND/EBITDA post 1.65x 2.84x 0.96x
ND/EBITDA pre 1.39x 1.76x 0.69x

On margin, J2 ranks first post-116 (9.0%) but last-equal pre-116 (6.0%). The "best margin" claim is an artefact of lease accounting. On leverage, J2 is worst under both definitions, but the gap to J1 more than triples post-116 (0.37x pre vs 1.19x post). The report should rank on post-116 (reported) for leverage, use pre-116 or EBITDAR for margin comparisons across models, and say so.

Excel implementation

Add a definition switch on the assumptions tab (Def116 = "Post" or "Pre") and route every affected metric through it:

=LET(ebitda, Std_EBITDA, lease, Std_LeasePay,
     IF(Def116="Pre", ebitda - lease, ebitda))

Then run the matrix twice, either with a one-variable Data Table on the switch or with two parallel blocks, and build the rank-comparison table with a "rank changed" flag that feeds the master check summary as information (not as an error).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Variable rent. Revenue-linked rents stay in opex post-116. A mall-heavy peer therefore still looks "pre-116" for part of its leases. Disclose lease cost not capitalised (Ind AS 116.53).
  • Historical series crossing FY20. Any three-year window now sits fully post-116. For longer histories (FY18–FY20), the FY19→FY20 jump in EBITDA is accounting, not performance. Restate with the pre-116 proxy or start the series at FY20.
  • IT peers. Lease intensity is low (often 1–3% of revenue), so rankings rarely change. Say so briefly. The test still matters for retail-like peers.
  • Lease-term judgement. Lease liabilities depend on renewal-option assumptions, so two peers with identical stores can carry different liabilities. Pre-116 EBITDA (cash rent) is less judgement-dependent.
  • Sale-and-leaseback, subleases and the franchise model. Franchise stores often have leases held by the franchisee, so they never appear on the brand-owner's balance sheet. Combine this test with the business-model caveat from topic t1 s01.
  • Covenants. Bank covenants may define EBITDA and debt pre-116 ("frozen GAAP"). For credit-style conclusions, use the covenant definition.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 116 (from 1 April 2019) raises EBITDA, net debt and invested capital for lease-heavy peers without changing economics.
  • Pre-116 proxy: subtract lease cash payments from EBITDA, exclude lease liabilities from net debt and ROU from invested capital.
  • Run the full matrix under both definitions with identical weights, and report rank changes with the lease-intensity explanation.
  • Margin rankings across owned, leased and franchise models are the most definition-sensitive. Leverage rankings usually survive but gaps widen.
  • Choose post-116 as the primary basis (reported, auditable), footnote the pre-116 result, and use covenant definitions for credit conclusions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build pre-116 metrics [L1]

A retailer reports FY25 EBITDA ₹2,450 crore, EBIT ₹1,520 crore, interest on lease liabilities ₹210 crore, principal repayment of lease liabilities ₹380 crore, lease liabilities ₹2,900 crore, ROU assets ₹2,600 crore, borrowings ₹1,100 crore, cash ₹700 crore and invested capital (post-116) ₹9,800 crore. Compute EBITDA, EBIT, net debt, invested capital and ND/EBITDA on both definitions.

Q2. Does the ranking flip? [L2]

Three peers have post-116 EBITDA margins of 18.2%, 17.5% and 16.9%, and lease payments of 1.2%, 4.1% and 0.8% of revenue. Post-116 ROIC (tax 25.17%) is 22%, 19% and 17%, with ROU assets at 6%, 30% and 3% of invested capital. Interest on leases is 40% of lease payments. Assume EBIT margins equal EBITDA margins less 3 percentage points for each peer. Compute pre-116 EBITDA margins and approximate pre-116 ROIC. State whether either ranking changes.

💡 Hint: For pre-116 ROIC, adjust both NOPAT (lease interest) and invested capital (remove ROU). Work in % of revenue using IC/revenue implied by the post-116 ROIC.

Q3. Narrative footnote [L2]

Your post-116 matrix ranks J2 first on EBITDA margin. Under the pre-116 proxy it ranks third. Draft (a) the action-title sentence for the margin slide and (b) a 60-word footnote explaining the definitional sensitivity, including the lease-intensity metric you would quote.

Q4. Definition switch and robustness table [L3]

Design the workbook components for the robustness test: assumptions switch, metric routing formulas, two parallel matrices or a Data Table, a rank-comparison table with change flags, a Spearman ρ cell (for peer sets of 5 or more), and the entry in the master check summary. Specify which checks are "errors" and which are "information".

Q5. Covenant vs reporting definitions [L3]

A jeweller's term loan covenant requires net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5x on a "frozen GAAP as at 31 March 2019" basis. Reported FY25 figures: EBITDA ₹1,620 crore; lease payments ₹540 crore; borrowings ₹2,300 crore including gold metal loan ₹1,100 crore; cash ₹400 crore; lease liabilities ₹2,700 crore. Compute the covenant ratio on reasonable interpretations, with and without the gold metal loan. Discuss which interpretation a lender would apply, and how your peer report should present this company's leverage.
