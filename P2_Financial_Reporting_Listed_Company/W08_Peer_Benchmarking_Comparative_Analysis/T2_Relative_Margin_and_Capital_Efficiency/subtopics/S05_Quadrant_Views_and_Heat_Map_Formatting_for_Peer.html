Quadrant Views and Heat-Map Formatting for Peer Matrices

Scope: Quadrant views: revenue growth vs ROIC; margin vs CCC; heat-map formatting

Why it matters

A composite score answers "who is best overall". It hides why, and it hides trade-offs. Quadrant charts expose the two trade-offs that matter most in capital-efficiency analysis. Growth vs return shows whether a company is growing profitably or buying growth with capital. Margin vs working capital shows whether profitability is bought with inventory and credit. Heat maps make a large metric × peer table readable at a glance. Equity research initiations, consulting benchmarking decks and board strategy packs all use these formats. In Project 2 they are the visual evidence behind the pillars of the executive summary (topic t3) and are built properly as charts in topic t4.

Core notes

Quadrant 1: Revenue growth vs ROIC

  • X-axis: 3-year revenue CAGR (or LTM growth).
  • Y-axis: ROIC. Add a WACC reference line so the spread is visible (concept c05).
  • Quadrant dividers: peer median growth (vertical) and peer median ROIC (horizontal), or WACC for the horizontal line.
  • Bubble size (optional): revenue or invested capital.
Quadrant Position Interpretation Typical narrative
Top-right High growth, high ROIC Value compounder "Growth is creating value: every rupee reinvested earns above WACC"
Top-left Low growth, high ROIC Cash cow / harvest "High returns, limited reinvestment runway; watch payout policy"
Bottom-right High growth, low ROIC Capital-hungry growth "Growth may destroy value if ROIC < WACC; check incremental ROIC"
Bottom-left Low growth, low ROIC Value trap / turnaround "Restructuring required"

The key insight comes from value-driver theory. Growth creates value only when ROIC exceeds WACC. A bottom-right peer with ROIC below WACC destroys more value the faster it grows.

Quadrant 2: EBITDA margin vs CCC

  • X-axis: CCC in days. Consider reversing the axis so that "better" (shorter) sits on the right, or keep it standard and label clearly.
  • Y-axis: EBITDA margin.
  • Dividers: peer medians.

Interpretation: a high margin with a long CCC means profit tied up in working capital. Many jewellers fit this: gold inventory is funded by debt or GML, so margin overstates the cash return. A high margin with a short CCC is the best combination. A low margin with a long CCC is a double drag. For IT peers, the interesting variation is DSO plus unbilled revenue against margin, which shows client-payment-term pressure.

Building a quadrant chart in Excel

  1. Data block: Peer, X, Y, Size, Label, and median X and median Y computed with MEDIAN().
  2. Insert a scatter (or bubble) chart from X and Y.
  3. Divider lines: add two extra series, a vertical line (median X, min Y) to (median X, max Y), and a horizontal line, each plotted as a scatter with straight lines and no markers. Alternatively, set the axis "crosses at" value to the median, which makes the axes the dividers.
  4. Labels: Format Data Labels → "Value From Cells" → Label range (Excel 2013+).
  5. Dynamic: because medians are formulas, the dividers move when data changes (topic t4 s06).

Heat-map formatting

A heat map colours each cell of the metric × peer table by its relative standing.

Rules:

  • Colour the normalised score, not the raw value, so direction is already handled. Green means good on every row, whether the row is CAGR (higher better) or CCC (lower better). If you colour raw values, you must reverse the colour scale on lower-is-better rows. That is a frequent source of errors.
  • Row-wise scales. Apply a separate 3-colour scale per metric row. A single scale across the whole table compares days with percentages.
  • Show the numbers. The cell should display the raw value (for example "82 days") while the colour reflects the normalised score. You can do this with a formula-based conditional format that reads the score from a parallel hidden block:
Rule (Use a formula): =INDEX(ScoreBlock, ROW()-ROW($B$5)+1, COLUMN()-COLUMN($B$5)+1) >= 0.5

Alternatively, colour by percentile buckets with three rules (green ≥ 0.67, amber 0.33–0.67, red < 0.33). - Muted palette. Use light green/amber/red or a single-hue sequential scale (for print and colour-blind readers). Avoid saturated traffic lights over numbers. - Flags. Mark capped or caveated cells with an icon or a superscript note (s04), and grey out metrics with zero weight (leverage for IT).

Illustration (jewellery, FY25, illustrative)

Peer 3-yr CAGR ROIC EBITDA margin CCC (days)
J1 21% 24% 11.0% 190
J2 30% 14% 7.2% 250
J3 26% 16% 6.8% 210
Median 26% 16% 7.2% 210

With WACC of 12.5%: J1 sits top-left (growth below median, high ROIC), J2 bottom-right (fastest growth, lowest ROIC but still above WACC), and J3 on the medians. On margin vs CCC, J1 combines the highest margin with the shortest CCC, J2 the longest CCC. The narrative becomes: "J2's sector-leading growth is capital-hungry: a 250-day CCC drags ROIC 10 points below J1's, though it still clears WACC."

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Three-peer medians. With three peers the median is the middle peer, so that peer always sits on a divider. Consider sector benchmarks or WACC lines instead, or add a wider comps universe as grey background dots.
  • Axis scaling. An outlier (70% ROIC) squashes the others. Use axis caps with a broken-axis note or an inset, and never silently drop the point.
  • Time dimension. A single-year snapshot can mislead. Show arrows from FY23 to FY25 positions (a "connected scatter") to reveal direction of travel.
  • Colour-blind safety. Red/green heat maps fail for about 8% of men. Use blue-orange or sequential single-hue palettes, or add icons.
  • Heat-map overreach. Colouring a 3-peer table on percentiles gives exactly one green, one amber and one red per row, which exaggerates small gaps. Use thresholds relative to economic significance, for example ±100 bps of margin as "neutral".

Key takeaways

  • Growth vs ROIC shows whether growth creates value. Add a WACC line, because growth with ROIC below WACC destroys value.
  • Margin vs CCC shows whether profitability is tied up in working capital, which is critical for jewellers.
  • Use peer medians (or WACC) as dividers, driven by formulas so they update.
  • Heat maps should colour normalised scores row by row while displaying raw values, with a muted, colour-blind-safe palette.
  • With very small peer sets, avoid percentile colouring that turns tiny gaps into red/green contrasts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Place peers in quadrants [L1]

IT peers (illustrative): Peer A CAGR 10.0%, ROIC 55%; Peer B 10.3%, 38%; Peer C 3.9%, 21%; Peer D 14.5%, 17%; Peer E 7.2%, 28%. WACC is 12%. Compute median CAGR and median ROIC, place each peer in a quadrant, and write a one-line interpretation for each.

Q2. Margin vs CCC narrative [L2]

Jewellers: J1 margin 11.0%, CCC 190 days; J2 7.2%, 250 days; J3 6.8%, 210 days; J4 8.9%, 160 days. Assume each jeweller's working capital is funded at 9% pre-tax. Estimate the annual funding cost of each extra 30 days of CCC as % of revenue (COGS is 85% of revenue, and use COGS for the days calculation). Explain how this changes the margin ranking on a "cash-adjusted" basis.

💡 Hint: Thirty days of COGS is (30/365) × 85% of revenue. Multiply by the funding rate.

Q3. Heat-map build specification [L2]

Specify the conditional formatting rules for a 7-metric × 4-peer heat map. It should display raw values, colour by direction-adjusted percentile, grey out zero-weight rows and mark capped cells. Write the exact formula-based rules and explain why a single colour scale over the whole range is wrong.

Q4. Connected scatter interpretation [L3]

Peer X moved from (CAGR 18%, ROIC 22%) in FY23 to (CAGR 24%, ROIC 15%) in FY25, while WACC stayed at 12.5%. Peer Y moved from (12%, 14%) to (15%, 19%). Interpret both trajectories in value-creation terms. Explain what incremental ROIC analysis you would run to confirm your reading, and what data from the annual report you need.

Q5. Critique a quadrant slide [L2]

A draft slide plots EBITDA margin (y) against revenue (x, ₹ crore) for three IT peers. It has red/green quadrant backgrounds split at the axis midpoints, no labels and the title "Peer positioning". List every problem with the slide and redesign it as a growth vs ROIC quadrant with an action title.
