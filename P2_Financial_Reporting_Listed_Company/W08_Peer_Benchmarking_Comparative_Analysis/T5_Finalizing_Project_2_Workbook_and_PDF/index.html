Roadmap topic: Finalizing Project 2 Workbook & PDF Report: Integrating standardized 3-year statements, ratio engine, DuPont tables, and commentary

Overview

This topic turns seven weeks of analysis into a deliverable. By now the components exist — standardized statements (Week 5), a ratio dictionary (Week 6), forensic findings (Week 7), and this week's peer set, capital efficiency matrix, narrative and charts. Finalizing means integrating them into one workbook with a clean, one-directional architecture; proving its integrity with checks; formatting and sourcing every number so a reviewer can verify it; producing a PDF report that leads with the conclusion; and closing with a documented review. The output is a portfolio piece that will be reused as the historical base for the Week 13 three-statement model and shown in interviews.

For FP&A, equity research and transaction roles, the finalization discipline is the job as much as the analysis. A model reviewer at a bank, a Big 4 valuation manager or a CFO receiving a board pack asks the same questions: does it balance, where did this number come from, is this ratio defined the same way for every company, and what changed since the last version? Your audit background maps directly: checks are analytical procedures and reconciliations, source references are audit evidence, the review log mirrors review notes and clearance, and the change log is your audit trail.

The subtopics form a sequence. Start with architecture (subtopic 1): raw extracts → mapping → standardized statements → adjustments log → ratio engine, DuPont, CCC and matrix → charts and commentary. Build integrity checks (subtopic 2) as you go — balance sheet, cash, ratio cross-checks — and roll them into a master check; the four concepts formalize the balance sheet check, the cash check, the DuPont check and the definitions sheet that keeps every peer on one formula. Apply formatting standards (subtopic 3) and source referencing (subtopic 4) so the workbook is readable and verifiable. Then produce the PDF report (subtopic 5) with executive summary first and an appendix of definitions, and finish with review, version control and the change log (subtopic 6).

Related concepts to study

FAST standard and model audit (Week 9)

FAST standard and model audit practices → Week 9

The FAST standard (Flexible, Appropriate, Structured, Transparent) is a widely used set of spreadsheet modelling rules: separate inputs, calculations and outputs; one formula per row copied consistently across time; short, readable formulas; consistent time columns; no hard-codes in calculations; and clear labelling of units. Model audit practice adds systematic testing — formula maps, inconsistency scans, reviews of hard-codes, external links, circularity and error values, and independent recomputation. The Project 2 conventions in this topic are a lightweight version of these; Week 9 formalizes them and introduces tools (Inquire, formula auditing, LET/LAMBDA) that make the workbook easier to audit and extend.

Standardized statements as model historicals (Week 13)

Reuse standardized statements as historicals for the 3-statement model → Week 13

The standardized 3-year statements built here — on a common chart of lines, in ₹ crore, with an adjustments log and a balancing balance sheet and tying cash flow statement — are exactly the historical columns a three-statement model needs. In Week 13 the forecast columns are appended to the right using the same line codes, so historical ratios (growth, margins, working-capital days, capex intensity) become forecast drivers. This is why getting structure, signs and units right now saves time later: a model built on inconsistent historicals inherits every error. Keep the mapping table and line codes stable so the model can pull historicals with the same SUMIFS logic.

Portfolio packaging for capstone and interviews (Weeks 23–24)

Portfolio packaging of projects for capstone and interviews → Weeks 23–24

Project 2 becomes one of the portfolio pieces presented in the capstone and in interviews. Packaging means a sanitized, versioned workbook (no personal paths, no broken links, master check OK), the PDF report, a two- or three-slide summary, and a short write-up of methodology and key judgements. Interviewers often open the workbook and ask you to trace a headline number to its source or explain why a peer ranks where it does — the source references, definitions sheet and change log built in this topic let you answer instantly. Weeks 23–24 cover how to present the project, typical interview probes and how to frame lessons learned.

Common pitfalls

Hard-coded plugs without an adjustments log

Hard-coded plugs or numbers inside formulas without an adjustments log

The mistake: formulas such as ='Std statements'!G14-63 or a typed "balancing" figure to make a check pass. Why it happens: time pressure, a quick normalization fix, or a check that will not clear. Correct treatment: every adjustment is a row in the adjustments log (ID, company, year, line, amount, rationale, source) and enters calculations only through a lookup; checks are fixed by finding the cause, never by plugging. Detection in review: Go To Special for constants on calculation sheets, search formulas for numeric literals (Find "-" and "+" followed by digits, or use Inquire's analysis), confirm adjustments-log totals equal adjusted minus standardized figures, and look for check tolerances that have been widened.

Missing source references

Missing source references, making figures unverifiable

The mistake: raw-extract cells, KPIs or quoted numbers with no document, page, note, filing date or slide. Why it happens: data were copied from a database or an earlier working file, or the analyst intended to add references "at the end". Correct treatment: assign a source ID at the moment of entry, maintain a sources register, cite restated comparatives from the latest document, and flag unaudited KPIs. Detection in review: a COUNTBLANK on the source-ID column of raw extracts (target zero), a random sample of cells traced to source, and tracing every executive-summary figure to a document.

Inconsistent units and signs across outputs

Units or sign conventions differing between tables, charts and narrative

The mistake: a table in ₹ crore, a chart in ₹ million, narrative quoting lakh; net debt positive in one table and negative (net cash) in another; cost bars positive in a bridge while the table shows costs negative. Why it happens: different exhibits built at different times or copied from different templates; unit conversion done in the output layer. Correct treatment: one base unit and sign convention documented on the cover, conversion and sign flips only in the mapping layer, a key-numbers block feeding all text and titles, and units in every label and subtitle. Detection in review: read the PDF and compare each quoted number with its exhibit and appendix table; check every exhibit's subtitle for unit; recompute two or three narrative figures from the engine.

Practice Questions

Q1. End-to-end finalization plan [L3]

You have five working days to finalize Project 2 on Titan, Kalyan Jewellers and Senco Gold. Current state: raw extracts complete without source IDs; standardized statements for Titan and Kalyan balance, Senco's FY24 fails by ₹36.80 crore; ratio engine has company-specific formulas in some rows; charts are typed-value pictures; no commentary sheet. (a) Draft the sheet architecture you will move to. (b) List the integrity checks and their tolerances. (c) Write a day-by-day plan covering formatting, sourcing, PDF production and review. (d) Write the first three change-log entries you expect to make.

Q2. Checks and DuPont for an IT peer [L2]

Infosys FY25 (illustrative, ₹ crore): revenue 1,62,990; EBIT 34,420; profit before tax 37,600 (including other income); profit for the year 26,750, of which NCI 30; average total assets 1,40,200; average total equity 92,500 (of which NCI 380). Opening cash and cash equivalents 14,790; net change from activities +9,400; FX effect +120; BS cash and cash equivalents 24,310 with no overdraft. (a) Compute the cash check. (b) Build the five-step DuPont on an owners' basis and compute the DuPont check against engine ROE. (c) Explain why the interest burden exceeds 1 here and how your definitions sheet should handle EBIT and other income.

💡 Hint: For (b), decide first which profit and equity figures the engine ROE uses, then make every component consistent with them.

Q3. Review findings triage [L2]

A peer reviewer raises these notes on your IT peers workbook: (i) Wipro FY24 taken from the original FY24 report although restated in FY25; (ii) chart titles typed, two disagree with the data; (iii) ROIC for TCS excludes lease liabilities while others include them; (iv) the PDF footer shows v0.9; (v) EBITDA for Infosys differs from the investor presentation by 1.2%. Classify each as error, improvement or query; state the fix, the check or control that should have caught it, and whether each could affect the governing conclusion.

Q4. Definitions sheet and appendix [L3]

Write the definitions-sheet entries for revenue CAGR, EBITDA margin, ROIC, CCC and net debt/EBITDA for a jewellery peer set, including treatment of gold metal loans, franchise-model receivables and Ind AS 116 leases. Then design the appendix pages of the PDF that print these definitions, the standardized statements and the source register, specifying print areas and page setup.
