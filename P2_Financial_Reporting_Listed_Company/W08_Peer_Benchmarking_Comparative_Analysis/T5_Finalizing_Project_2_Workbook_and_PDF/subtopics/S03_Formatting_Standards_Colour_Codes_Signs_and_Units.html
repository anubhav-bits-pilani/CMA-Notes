Subtopic note

Formatting Standards: Colour Codes, Signs and Units

Scope: Formatting standards: blue inputs, black formulas, green cross-sheet links; consistent sign and unit conventions

Why it matters

Formatting in a financial workbook is a control, not cosmetics. A reviewer opening an unfamiliar model needs to know instantly which cells are typed (and must be sourced), which are calculated (and can be trusted if the logic is right) and which pull from elsewhere (and must be traced). Colour coding gives that map. Sign and unit conventions prevent the most common silent errors in peer analysis: a cost entered as positive in one company and negative in another, or a peer reported in ₹ lakh treated as ₹ crore. Investment banks, Big 4 valuation teams and FP&A functions all enforce these conventions, and they are part of the FAST standard covered in Week 9.

Core notes

Font colour convention

Cell type Font colour Example
Hard-coded input (typed number, raw extract, assumption, adjustment amount) Blue Revenue from annual report: 22,600.40
Formula within the same sheet Black =G12/G8
Link to another sheet in the same workbook Green ='Std statements'!G12
Link to another workbook (avoid where possible) Red (common convention) =[Data.xlsx]Raw!G12

Supplementary conventions often used:

  • Light yellow fill for key assumptions or selector cells (e.g., basis selector, company dropdown).
  • Grey fill or italic for check cells; green/red conditional formatting for check status.
  • A legend on the cover sheet stating the conventions used.

The colour must match the content: a formula that mixes a link and a hard-coded number (e.g., ='Std statements'!G12-45.6) breaks the convention and the control — the 45.6 belongs in the adjustments log.

Applying colours efficiently

  • Go To Special (F5 → Special): select Constants → Numbers to colour all hard-codes blue; select Formulas to colour black; then find cross-sheet links (formulas containing !) with Find (Ctrl+F, look in formulas, search for !) and colour green.
  • Cell styles (Home → Cell Styles → New Cell Style) named Input, Calc, Link, Check — applying a style is faster and more consistent than manual formatting.
  • Re-run the colouring as the final step before issue; formulas added late are often left in the wrong colour.

Sign conventions

Choose one convention for each statement and apply it to every peer:

Statement Recommended convention Reason
P&L (standardized) Revenue positive, expenses positive with subtotals as explicit subtractions; or expenses negative and subtotals as SUM Either works; mixing breaks SUM-based totals
Balance sheet Assets, liabilities and equity all positive Matches Schedule III presentation
Cash flow Inflows positive, outflows negative (shown in parentheses) Matches Ind AS 7 presentation and makes SUM work
Bridges Effect on EBITDA: positive = increase, negative = decrease Direct reading of bars
Net debt Positive = net debt, negative = net cash Consistent leverage ratios; flag net cash separately
Variances Positive = favourable to profit Avoids "is higher cost good?" confusion

Raw extracts keep the company's own signs (e.g., "changes in inventories" may be shown as a negative expense in a stock-build year); the mapping/standardization layer converts to the house convention. Add a sign column in the mapping table (+1/−1) rather than editing raw data.

Unit conventions

  • One base unit for all standardized statements: ₹ crore, two decimals. Convert from ₹ lakh, ₹ million or absolute rupees once, in standardization, with a documented factor.
  • Percentages stored as decimals (0.142) and formatted 0.0%; never store 14.2 in one place and 0.142 in another.
  • Days for working capital metrics, x for multiples and coverage, bps for margin changes.
  • Per-share data in ₹ (not crore) with share counts in crore or lakh stated explicitly.
  • Foreign currency: if an IFRS 20-F figure in USD million is used for reconciliation, convert at a stated rate and label the unit.
  • Every row label carries its unit: "Revenue from operations (₹ crore)", "EBITDA margin (%)", "DSO (days)".

Number formats

Measure Format code
₹ crore #,##0.0;(#,##0.0);"–"
% 0.0%;(0.0%);"–"
Multiples 0.0"x"
Days 0
Check cells 0.00;(0.00);"OK"

Showing zeros as a dash helps the eye pick out non-zero values; for check cells, showing zero as "OK" makes the check sheet readable.

Layout conventions

  • Years in columns, oldest left; same column = same year on every calculation sheet.
  • Labels in column B, units in column C, a source or notes column after the years.
  • Headers in bold; subtotals with a top border; totals with a double bottom border.
  • No merged cells in calculation areas (use Center Across Selection instead) — merges break copying and Power Query.

Illustration

Peer C's raw P&L (₹ lakh) shows "Changes in inventories of stock-in-trade: (4,520.00)" in FY25 (a stock build, reducing expenses). Mapping: code COGS, sign +1, factor 100. Standardized: −45.20 ₹ crore within COGS, so COGS = materials + purchases − 45.20. If a colleague had typed +45.20 to "make it positive", gross margin would be understated by ₹90.40 crore — about 40 bps on revenue of ₹22,600 crore.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Formulas that return constants (e.g., =100) are disguised hard-codes; Go To Special treats them as formulas. Search for formulas without cell references during review.
  • Negative revenue lines (e.g., discounts presented separately) need explicit mapping signs.
  • Percentages in source documents sometimes appear as whole numbers in investor presentations; convert on entry and mark with blue font.
  • Printing in black and white removes colour codes; for PDF appendices, add a legend and rely on cell styles that remain distinguishable (e.g., blue inputs also have a light fill).
  • Different house styles: some firms use blue for inputs and black for everything else, or purple for links; consistency within the workbook and a legend matter more than which palette.

Key takeaways

  • Blue = typed inputs, black = same-sheet formulas, green = cross-sheet links; state it on the cover.
  • A formula mixing a link and a typed number is a hidden hard-code; move the number to the adjustments log.
  • Convert signs and units once, in the mapping layer, using sign and factor columns.
  • One base unit (₹ crore), percentages as decimals, units in every label.
  • Use cell styles and Go To Special to apply and re-check formatting before issue.

Practice Questions

Q1. Colour audit [L1]

Classify the correct font colour for each cell: (a) =Raw!F22/100; (b) 22600.4; (c) =G14-G15; (d) =G14*1.05; (e) =[Peers_v3.xlsx]Std!G8; (f) =SUMIFS(Adj[Amount],Adj[Co],"Titan"). Identify any cell that breaks best practice and explain the fix.

Q2. Sign convention design [L2]

Write a one-page sign and unit convention for the Project 2 workbook covering P&L, BS, CFS, bridges, net debt, variances and per-share data, and explain how the mapping table enforces it for a peer that reports in ₹ million with expenses shown as negatives.

Q3. Impact of a sign error [L2]

An analyst entered "changes in inventories" for Peer A as +₹310 crore (an expense) when the reported figure was −₹310 crore (a stock build) for FY25, with revenue ₹14,800 crore. Compute the effect on gross margin and EBITDA margin, and on DIO if inventory itself was correctly mapped. Describe the check that would have caught it.

💡 Hint: Consider which checks compare standardized figures with reported subtotals.

Q4. Formatting for a mixed-unit peer set [L1]

Your peers report in ₹ crore (TCS), ₹ crore (Infosys Ind AS report) and ₹ million (Wipro's 20-F extract used for one reconciliation). List the steps to bring all to a consistent unit and the labels you would use.
