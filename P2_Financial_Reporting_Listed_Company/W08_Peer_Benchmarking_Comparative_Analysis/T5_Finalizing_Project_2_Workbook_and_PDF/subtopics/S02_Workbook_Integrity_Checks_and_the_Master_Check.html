Subtopic note

Workbook Integrity Checks and the Master Check Summary

Scope: Integrity checks: BS balances, CFS closing cash ties to BS, ratio cross-checks, master check summary

Why it matters

A peer report is only as reliable as its weakest number. With three companies, three years, three statements and forty ratios, there are thousands of cells where a mapping slip, a sign error or a unit mismatch can hide. Integrity checks are formulas that prove the workbook is internally consistent — that the balance sheet balances, the cash flow statement ends where the balance sheet says cash is, and derived ratios agree with independent calculations. Model auditors, reviewers in research houses and interviewers all look for a master check on the cover: its absence signals an unchecked model, and its presence lets a reviewer focus on judgement rather than arithmetic.

Core notes

Types of checks

Category Check What it catches
Balance sheet Total assets − (Total equity + Total liabilities) = 0 Mapping omissions, sign errors, unit errors
BS tie to reported Standardized total assets − reported total assets = 0 Lines missing from standardization
Cash Opening cash + net change in cash (CFS) − closing cash (BS) = 0 Wrong cash definition, overdraft and FX effects missed
P&L Standardized PAT − reported PAT = 0; PAT attributable to owners + NCI = total PAT Mapping errors, NCI confusion
Ratio cross-checks DuPont product − ROE = 0; computed EPS vs reported EPS; computed EBITDA vs investor-presentation EBITDA; net debt walk ties Definitional inconsistency
Mapping Count of unmapped raw lines = 0 Incomplete standardization
Adjustments Sum of adjustments in log = adjusted − standardized for each line Adjustments typed outside the log
Bridges Start + Σ drivers − End = 0 Broken EBITDA bridges
Matrix Σ weights = 1; ranks are unique or ties flagged Mis-specified scoring
Presentation Key numbers in commentary = engine values Stale narrative

Balance sheet check

For each company-year:

BS_Check = ROUND(TotalAssets - (TotalEquity + TotalLiabilities), 2)

Total equity includes non-controlling interests in consolidated statements (Schedule III Division II presents NCI within equity). Total liabilities = non-current + current liabilities, including lease liabilities and liabilities directly associated with assets held for sale. Run the check at two levels: standardized statements balance internally and standardized totals equal reported totals.

Cash check

Cash_Check = ROUND(OpeningCash + NetChangeCFS + FXEffect - ClosingCashBS, 2)

Nuances under Ind AS 7:

  • Bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form an integral part of cash management are included in cash and cash equivalents for the cash flow statement (Ind AS 7.8), but appear as borrowings on the balance sheet. The CFS closing figure is then lower than the BS "cash and cash equivalents" line. Ind AS 7.45 requires a reconciliation; use it.
  • Effect of exchange rate changes on cash held in foreign currency (material for IT exporters) is reported separately from operating, investing and financing flows (Ind AS 7.28).
  • "Other bank balances" (deposits with original maturity over three months, earmarked balances) are not cash equivalents; exclude them from the comparison.
  • Held-for-sale disposal groups: cash within a disposal group may be included in CFS cash but presented in the held-for-sale line on the BS.

So the cash check compares CFS closing cash with BS cash and cash equivalents adjusted per the Ind AS 7.45 reconciliation, not blindly with the BS line.

Ratio cross-checks

Cross-checks recompute a figure by a second, independent route:

  • DuPont: net profit margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier − ROE = 0 (same numerators and denominators).
  • EPS: PAT attributable to owners ÷ weighted average shares versus reported basic EPS — differences flag NCI or share count errors (a tolerance is needed because reported EPS is rounded to two decimals).
  • EBITDA: computed EBITDA versus EBITDA disclosed in the investor presentation (differences may be definitional — e.g., inclusion of other income — and should be explained in the definitions sheet rather than "fixed").
  • Net debt: opening net debt + net-debt walk − closing net debt = 0; also borrowings per BS versus the Ind AS 7.44A reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities.
  • Segment totals: sum of segment revenue = consolidated revenue from operations (after inter-segment eliminations).
  • CCC: DIO + DSO − DPO = CCC on the CCC sheet and in the matrix.

Master check summary

Collect every check on one Checks sheet, then summarise:

Total_Errors = SUMPRODUCT(--(ABS(CheckRange) > Tolerance))
Master_Check = IF(Total_Errors = 0, "OK", "ERRORS: " & Total_Errors)

Display Master_Check on the cover and in the header of every output sheet, with conditional formatting (green OK, red otherwise). Rules:

  • Each check row has a description, the sheet it tests, the company-year, the value and a 0/1 flag.
  • Separate errors (must be zero to issue) from warnings (e.g., computed EBITDA differs from investor presentation by more than 2% — must be explained, not necessarily fixed).
  • Never "fix" a check by editing the check formula or by inserting a plug; fix the cause, and log the fix in the change log.

Tolerances

Rounding in source documents means exact zero is not always achievable. Schedule III permits rounding (for companies with turnover of ₹100 crore or more, to the nearest lakh, million or crore, or decimals thereof), so a company reporting in ₹ crore with two decimals can have totals that differ by 0.01–0.02 from the sum of components. Set tolerance per check (e.g., 0.05 for ₹ crore statements, 0.01 for EPS), document it on the Checks sheet, and keep it small enough that genuine errors cannot hide.

Illustration

Peer B, FY25 (₹ crore): total assets 18,420.55; total equity 9,880.10; non-current liabilities 2,140.35; current liabilities 6,402.10. Check = 18,420.55 − 18,422.55 = −2.00 → error. Investigation: "Other financial liabilities (current)" of ₹2.00 crore was mapped twice (once to trade payables). After fixing the mapping, the check is 0 and DPO for Peer B falls slightly — showing how a check failure can quietly distort a ratio.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Restated comparatives can make FY24 closing cash differ from FY25 opening cash in the CFS; check both and use the restated figure.
  • Business combinations: acquired cash appears net within investing activities; the cash check still holds, but the net-debt walk needs an "acquired debt" line.
  • Standalone vs consolidated mix-ups usually show first as reported-total tie failures.
  • IFRS 20-F vs Ind AS data: totals can differ (e.g., presentation of certain items); do not mix sources within one statement.
  • Check fatigue: too many trivial checks bury important ones; group by category and show category subtotals.

Key takeaways

  • Check internal balance and tie to reported totals for every company-year.
  • The cash check must reflect Ind AS 7 overdraft inclusion, FX effects and other bank balances.
  • Cross-check ratios by a second route: DuPont, EPS, EBITDA, net debt, segment totals.
  • One Checks sheet, one master check on the cover; errors must be zero, warnings explained.
  • Tolerances should be explicit, documented and tight.
  • Fix causes, never checks; log every fix.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cash check with overdraft and FX [L2]

An IT services company's FY25 CFS shows opening cash and cash equivalents ₹3,210 crore, net increase ₹640 crore, effect of exchange rate changes +₹42 crore and closing cash and cash equivalents ₹3,892 crore. The BS shows cash and cash equivalents ₹3,967 crore and bank overdraft within short-term borrowings ₹75 crore. Write the cash check, compute the result and explain whether the workbook passes.

Q2. Designing the Checks sheet [L3]

Design the Checks sheet for a three-peer, three-year Project 2 workbook: list every check row by category with its formula logic, tolerance, error/warning classification and the formula for the master check, and explain how the cover sheet will display status.

Q3. Diagnose a balance sheet failure [L2]

A standardized BS for FY24 fails by ₹118.40 crore while the reported BS balances. The company adopted a change in presentation in FY25 and restated FY24, and you mapped FY24 from the FY24 annual report. Outline the steps to locate and fix the problem, and the change-log entry you would write.

💡 Hint: Compare the restated FY24 comparatives in the FY25 annual report line by line with your FY24 raw extract.

Q4. Warning vs error [L1]

Your computed EBITDA for TCS differs from the investor-presentation figure by 1.8%. Explain whether this should be classified as an error or a warning, what you would investigate, and where the explanation would be documented.
