Subtopic note

Project 2 Workbook Architecture: From Raw Extracts to Commentary

Scope: Workbook architecture: cover/index, raw extracts, standardized 3-year statements, adjustments log, ratio engine, DuPont, CCC, matrix, charts, commentary

Why it matters

A comparative analysis workbook is judged on two things: whether the conclusions are right and whether a reviewer can verify them quickly. Architecture determines the second and strongly influences the first. A well-structured workbook separates data (as filed), transformation (standardization and adjustments), calculation (ratios, DuPont, CCC, matrix) and presentation (charts, commentary), so each figure in the PDF can be traced back through a short, one-directional chain to a page in an annual report. The same structure becomes the historical base of the Week 13 three-statement model and is what interviewers expect to see when you share a portfolio project.

Core notes

Design principles

  • One-directional flow: data move left to right through the sheet order — inputs → calculations → outputs. No calculation sheet pulls from a presentation sheet; no backward references.
  • Separation of concerns: raw data are never overwritten; adjustments are never buried inside ratio formulas; presentation sheets contain links and formatting only.
  • Consistent time axis: the same column represents the same year on every calculation sheet (e.g., column F = FY23, G = FY24, H = FY25), so formulas can be copied across and errors stand out.
  • Same layout per company: either one block per company stacked vertically with identical row order, or one sheet per company with identical structure. Identical structure enables INDEX/XLOOKUP-driven peer tables and makes cross-company errors visible.
  • No hard-codes in calculations: every number typed into the workbook lives on raw extract, assumption or adjustments sheets.

Sheet-by-sheet architecture

# Sheet Purpose Contents
1 Cover / Index Orientation and status Title, author, version, date, basis (consolidated, Ind AS, ₹ crore), peer list, hyperlinked index of sheets, master check status, colour legend
2 Raw extracts Data as filed One block per company-year: P&L, BS, CFS, key notes (leases, borrowings, segment, other income), in the company's own line items and units, each with source reference
3 Mapping Translation table Each raw line item → standard line code (e.g., REV, COGS, EMP, OTHEXP, DA, FIN, PPE, ROU, BORR-NC, LEASE-L)
4 Standardized statements Common format, 3 years P&L, BS, CFS on the standard chart of lines, in ₹ crore, built with SUMIFS on mapping codes; ties to reported totals
5 Adjustments log Normalization and policy alignment One row per adjustment: ID, company, year, line code, amount, direction, rationale, source, approver; feeds standardized or adjusted statements
6 Adjusted statements (optional) Standardized + adjustments Normalized EBITDA, net debt, invested capital per Week 5 rules
7 Definitions Metric dictionary One formula definition per metric
8 Ratio engine All ratios Growth, margins, returns, liquidity, leverage, efficiency, cash conversion — calculated from adjusted statements per the definitions sheet
9 DuPont ROE decomposition 3-step and 5-step tables with check against ratio-engine ROE
10 CCC Working capital cycle DIO, DSO, DPO and CCC with average balances and day-count basis
11 Matrix Ranking Metric set, normalization (rank/percentile/z-score), weights, composite score, sensitivity
12 Charts Exhibits Chart-feed blocks (linked), title cells, charts
13 Commentary Narrative Key-numbers block, section text, forensic notes, risks
14 Checks Control Every check in one place, master check summary
15 Sources / Change log Audit trail Source register, version history

Raw extracts

Capture data as filed — the company's own captions, signs and units (₹ lakh, ₹ crore or ₹ million). Include the restated comparative where the latest annual report restates the prior year, and note which version is used. Sources: annual reports (Schedule III financial statements and notes), Reg 33 results, XBRL filings, and for Infosys and Wipro the IFRS 20-F where needed for reconciliation. Power Query (Week 10) can automate this layer later; for Project 2 manual entry with source references is acceptable.

Mapping and standardization

The mapping table is the heart of comparability. Each raw caption maps to one standard code; the standardized sheet sums by code:

=SUMIFS(Raw[Value], Raw[Company], CoName, Raw[Year], Yr, Raw[StdCode], "EMP") / UnitFactor

where UnitFactor converts the company's reporting unit to ₹ crore (e.g., 100 for ₹ lakh to ₹ crore, 10 for ₹ million to ₹ crore). Check that every raw line is mapped (count of unmapped lines = 0) and that standardized totals equal reported totals.

Adjustments log

Every change from reported to analysed figures — one-offs removed from EBITDA, gold metal loan reclassified into debt, lease liabilities included or excluded from net debt, inventory formula alignment estimate — is one row with an ID. Adjusted statements pick up adjustments with SUMIFS by company, year and line code, so the log is the only place an adjustment amount exists.

Calculation and output layers

The ratio engine reads only from adjusted statements and follows the definitions sheet; DuPont and CCC sheets read from the ratio engine or adjusted statements; the matrix reads from the ratio engine; charts and commentary read from the matrix and engine via a key-numbers block. Presentation sheets contain no calculations beyond formatting helpers (label text, chart helper columns).

Navigation

  • Hyperlinked index on the cover (HYPERLINK("#'Ratio engine'!A1","Ratio engine")) and a "back to index" link on each sheet.
  • Sheet tab colours by layer (inputs, calculations, outputs, checks).
  • Freeze panes on row/column headers; group rows for sub-sections.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • One sheet per company vs stacked blocks: per-company sheets are easier to read for three peers; stacked blocks scale better for ten peers and Power Query. Choose one and keep layout identical.
  • Restated comparatives: store both original and restated figures in raw extracts with a flag; standardized statements use the restated version consistently.
  • Different reporting units: never convert in raw extracts; convert once via UnitFactor in standardization.
  • Framework differences: if Infosys data come from the IFRS 20-F for one metric and the Ind AS report for others, the mixing must be logged as a policy adjustment, not left implicit.
  • Scope creep: avoid adding analysis sheets that do not feed the report; move them to a separate working file.

Key takeaways

  • Separate data, transformation, calculation and presentation; flow left to right only.
  • Raw extracts stay as filed, with sources; a mapping table drives SUMIFS-based standardization.
  • Every adjustment lives once, in the adjustments log, with ID, rationale and source.
  • Ratio engine, DuPont, CCC and matrix read from adjusted statements and the definitions sheet.
  • Same column = same year and identical layout for every peer.
  • The cover shows basis, version and master check status at a glance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Sheet design from scratch [L3]

Design the sheet list, sheet order and the cross-sheet data flow for a Project 2 workbook on Titan, Kalyan Jewellers and Senco Gold covering FY23–FY25, including where the gold metal loan reclassification, the franchise-model adjustment and Ind AS 116 lease treatment will live. Draw the flow as a table of "sheet → reads from → feeds".

Q2. Mapping table [L2]

Kalyan's raw P&L shows "Cost of materials consumed", "Purchases of stock-in-trade", "Changes in inventories of stock-in-trade", "Employee benefits expense", "Finance costs", "Depreciation and amortisation expense" and "Other expenses"; Titan's shows similar captions plus "Exceptional items". Write a mapping table to standard codes, explain how exceptional items would be handled, and write the check that confirms no line is unmapped.

💡 Hint: Exceptional items usually map to a separate code so that the adjustments log decides their treatment.

Q3. Unit conversion [L1]

Peer A reports in ₹ crore, Peer B in ₹ lakh and Peer C in ₹ million. Write the UnitFactor for each to convert to ₹ crore and explain why conversion should happen in the standardization layer rather than in raw extracts.

Q4. Review a flawed architecture [L2]

A colleague's workbook has ratio formulas such as =(H12-45.6)/H30 on the ratio sheet, a chart that reads from the commentary sheet, and Wipro's balance sheet on a layout with rows in a different order from TCS and Infosys. Explain the risks in each and how you would restructure.
