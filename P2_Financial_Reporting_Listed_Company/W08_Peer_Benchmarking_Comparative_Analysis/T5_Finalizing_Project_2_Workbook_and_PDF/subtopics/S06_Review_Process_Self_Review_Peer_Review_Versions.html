Subtopic note

Review Process: Self-Review, Peer Review, Versions and Change Log

Scope: Review: self-review checklist, peer review, version control and change log

Why it matters

Spreadsheet error studies consistently find that a large share of operational spreadsheets contain material errors, and most are found only by deliberate review. Your audit background already gives you the mindset — evidence, sampling, independence, documentation; this subtopic applies it to your own work product. For Project 2, a disciplined review process is what separates a "working file" from an issued report: the master check proves arithmetic consistency, but only review catches wrong judgements, stale sources, misleading charts and claims the workbook does not support. Version control and a change log make the process auditable and let you answer the interview question "how do you make sure your models are right?" with specifics.

Core notes

Three lines of review

Stage Who Focus Evidence
Self-review Preparer Completeness, checks, formatting, sources, narrative consistency Completed checklist with date
Peer review Another analyst (or yourself after a 24-hour gap, if working alone) Independent recomputation, logic, judgement, readability Review notes log with responses
Sign-off Reviewer/manager (or mentor) Conclusions, risks, presentation Sign-off line on cover with version

Self-review checklist

Work through it in a fixed order — mechanics first, judgement last:

Integrity

  • Master check = OK; no check tolerances widened since last version.
  • No errors anywhere: search for #REF!, #N/A, #DIV/0!, #VALUE! (Find in values, entire workbook).
  • No hard-codes inside formulas: inspect with Go To Special → Formulas, Show Formulas (Ctrl + grave accent), or the Inquire add-in's workbook analysis where available.
  • No links to external workbooks (Data → Edit Links shows none).

Data and sources

  • Every raw-extract cell has a source ID; a sample of figures traced to source documents.
  • Restated comparatives used consistently.
  • Basis is identical across peers: consolidated, continuing operations, period, framework, units.

Calculations

  • Ratio definitions match the definitions sheet for every peer (spot-check one ratio per peer by hand).
  • DuPont and CCC checks zero; bridges reconcile; matrix weights sum to 1.
  • Adjustments log totals equal adjusted minus standardized figures.

Presentation

  • Every chart has action title, unit, period, basis and source; colours per the key.
  • Numbers in the executive summary, charts, tables and appendix agree (key-numbers block).
  • Sign and unit conventions consistent across tables, charts and narrative.

Judgement

  • Each pillar's claim traces to specific cells; forensic flags are reflected in the ranking or caveated.
  • Risks and "what would change the conclusion" are stated.

Peer review

Effective peer review is independent, not a re-read:

  • Recompute a sample from scratch: e.g., for each peer, recompute ROIC, CCC and net debt/EBITDA for the latest year from the annual report using the definitions sheet, without looking at the workbook formulas.
  • Trace every headline number in the executive summary back to source.
  • Challenge judgements: peer selection, normalization adjustments, weights in the matrix, choice of quadrant thresholds.
  • Stress the conclusion: does it survive a weight change, a pre-Ind AS 116 basis, or excluding a flagged adjustment?
  • Record findings in a review notes log: ID, sheet/cell, finding, severity (error / improvement / query), preparer response, status (open/cleared), cleared by, date.

If you work alone, simulate independence: let 24 hours pass, review the PDF before the workbook, and use the checklist rather than memory.

Version control

Excel files do not merge well, so version control relies on discipline:

  • File naming: Project2_ITPeers_v1.2_2026-11-26.xlsx — semantic version (major.minor) plus ISO date. Major version changes when conclusions or structure change; minor for fixes and formatting.
  • Never overwrite an issued version; save a new file. Store on OneDrive/SharePoint to retain automatic version history as a backup, not as a substitute for named versions.
  • Status flags on the cover: Draft / Under review / Final, with the version number linked into the PDF footer.
  • Freeze at issue: when a version is issued, save the PDF with the same version number and keep both together.
  • Git can store .xlsx files as binaries for history, but cannot meaningfully diff them; tools such as Spreadsheet Compare (Inquire) are better for comparing versions where available.

Change log

A sheet in the workbook, one row per change:

Field Example
Version v1.2
Date 26-Nov-2026
Author AB
Sheet / range Std statements!G40:I44
Change Remapped "Gold metal loan" from trade payables to current borrowings for Peer B
Reason Review note R-07; Note 21(b) of AR FY25
Impact Peer B net debt/EBITDA from 0.8x to 1.6x; Peer B moves from rank 1 to rank 2 on leverage
Conclusion affected? No — governing conclusion unchanged
Reviewed by / date CD, 27-Nov-2026

Recording impact is the valuable part: it shows which changes affect conclusions and protects against silent drift between the workbook and the narrative.

Sequencing to issue

  1. Preparer completes the model and self-review checklist; master check OK.
  2. Peer review; review notes raised; preparer fixes and logs changes; reviewer clears notes.
  3. PDF produced from the reviewed version; PDF reviewed end to end.
  4. Sign-off; status set to Final; version frozen; PDF and workbook archived together.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Late changes: a data correction after the PDF is drafted must go through the change log and a re-check of every exhibit that uses the figure — the key-numbers block makes this tractable.
  • Disagreement on judgement (e.g., whether to normalize a recurring "one-off"): document both views and the decision in the review notes; if unresolved, disclose the alternative in the report.
  • Tolerances drifting: a reviewer should check that no tolerance has been relaxed to make a check pass.
  • Review scope: reviewing everything is impossible; prioritise headline figures, high-judgement areas (adjustments, peer basis) and anything that drives the ranking.
  • Confidential data: if using non-public sources, separate them and confirm you may share the workbook.

Key takeaways

  • Self-review in a fixed order: integrity, data and sources, calculations, presentation, judgement.
  • Peer review recomputes and traces independently and challenges judgements; findings go into a review log.
  • Version files with semantic numbers and ISO dates; never overwrite an issued version.
  • The change log records what changed, why, its impact on numbers and whether conclusions moved.
  • Issue only from a reviewed version with master check OK, and archive the PDF with its workbook.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a self-review checklist [L2]

Write a self-review checklist of at least 20 items, grouped into integrity, data and sources, calculations, presentation and judgement, tailored to a Project 2 workbook on Titan, Kalyan Jewellers and Senco Gold (including gold metal loan and franchise-model points).

Q2. Peer review plan [L3]

You are reviewing a colleague's IT peers workbook (Infosys, TCS, Wipro) in three hours. Write the review plan: what you will recompute independently, what you will trace, which judgements you will challenge and how you will record findings and their severity.

💡 Hint: Allocate time by risk — headline figures and ranking drivers first.

Q3. Change log entry [L1]

During review you discover that Wipro's FY24 figures were taken from the FY24 annual report, but the FY25 report restated FY24 for a discontinued operation, changing FY24 revenue by ₹1,240 crore and EBITDA by ₹185 crore. Write the change log entry, including impact on the 3-year CAGR and margin stability ranking (describe the direction of impact; you need not compute exact values).

Q4. Versioning policy [L1]

Write a versioning and file-naming policy for Project 2 that covers drafts, review versions, the final issue and post-issue corrections, and explain how the cover sheet and PDF footer show the version.
