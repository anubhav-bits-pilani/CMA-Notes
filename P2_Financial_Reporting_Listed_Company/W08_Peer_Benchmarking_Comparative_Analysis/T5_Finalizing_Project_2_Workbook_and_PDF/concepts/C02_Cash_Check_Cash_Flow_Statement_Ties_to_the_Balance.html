Key concept

Cash Check: Cash Flow Statement Ties to the Balance Sheet

Concept: Cash check: Opening cash + Net change in cash (CFS) = Closing cash and cash equivalents (BS)

Statement

For each company \(i\) and year \(t\):

\[ \text{CashCheck}_{i,t} = \text{C}^{\text{open}}_{i,t} + \Delta\text{C}^{\text{CFS}}_{i,t} + \text{FX}_{i,t} - \text{C}^{\text{close,BS adj}}_{i,t} = 0 \]

where \(\text{C}^{\text{open}}\) is opening cash and cash equivalents per the cash flow statement (equal to the prior year's closing CFS figure), \(\Delta\text{C}^{\text{CFS}}\) is the net increase or decrease from operating, investing and financing activities, \(\text{FX}\) is the effect of exchange rate changes on cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.28; zero if not reported separately), and \(\text{C}^{\text{close,BS adj}}\) is balance sheet cash and cash equivalents adjusted per the Ind AS 7.45 reconciliation (e.g., less bank overdrafts included in cash for CFS purposes).

Explanation

The cash flow statement explains the change in cash between two balance sheets; if the standardized CFS does not end at the balance sheet's cash, either a flow has been mis-mapped or the cash definitions differ. A second continuity check is that opening cash this year = closing cash last year, which fails when restated comparatives were not used.

Definition differences that are not errors and must be reconciled, not plugged:

  • Bank overdrafts repayable on demand and integral to cash management are included in CFS cash (Ind AS 7.8) but shown within borrowings on the BS.
  • Other bank balances (deposits over three months' original maturity, unpaid-dividend and margin accounts) are separate BS lines and excluded from cash equivalents.
  • Cash in disposal groups held for sale may be in CFS cash but presented within held-for-sale assets.
  • FX translation effects for cash held abroad (material for IT exporters with USD balances).

Ind AS 7.45 requires entities to disclose the components of cash and cash equivalents and reconcile them to the balance sheet; use that note as the source for the adjustment.

CashCheck = ROUND(OpenCash + NetChange + FXEffect - (BSCash - OverdraftInCFSCash + HFSCash), 2)
Continuity = ROUND(OpenCash - PriorYearCloseCFSCash, 2)

Why the check matters for ratios: CFO feeds CFO/EBITDA, free cash flow and the matrix; if a flow is mis-mapped between operating and investing (e.g., interest received), the cash check may still pass, so pair it with a CFO tie to the reported subtotal.

Illustration

Peer A FY25 (₹ crore): opening cash 1,148.20; net cash from operating 2,315.60, investing −1,604.30, financing −702.10; FX effect +6.40; BS cash and cash equivalents 1,203.80; overdraft included in CFS cash 40.00.

Net change = 2,315.60 − 1,604.30 − 702.10 = 9.20. CFS closing = 1,148.20 + 9.20 + 6.40 = 1,163.80. BS adjusted = 1,203.80 − 40.00 = 1,163.80. Check = 0.00. Without the overdraft adjustment the check would show −40.00 and tempt an analyst to "fix" a flow that was correct.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and diagnose [L2]

An FMCG peer's standardized FY25 CFS shows opening cash ₹612.40 crore, CFO ₹1,980.10 crore, investing −₹1,120.50 crore, financing −₹905.30 crore and no FX line; BS cash and cash equivalents are ₹602.70 crore and "other bank balances" ₹1,450.00 crore. Compute the cash check and explain whether "other bank balances" should enter it.

💡 Hint: Refer to the Ind AS 7 definition of cash equivalents.

Q2. Continuity failure [L1]

FY25 opening cash in your standardized CFS is ₹2,410 crore but your FY24 closing CFS cash is ₹2,385 crore. Give two likely reasons and how you would resolve each.

Q3. Check design for an IT exporter [L2]

Design the cash check rows for Infosys for FY23–FY25 using the Ind AS annual report, including FX effects and any overdraft or held-for-sale reconciling items, and explain how you would document the source of each reconciling item.
