Key concept

Balance Sheet Check for Every Company-Year

Concept: BS check: Total assets − (Total equity + Total liabilities) = 0 for every company-year

Statement

For each company \(i\) and year \(t\) in the workbook:

\[ \text{BSCheck}_{i,t} = \text{TA}_{i,t} - \left(\text{TE}_{i,t} + \text{TL}_{i,t}\right) = 0 \]

where \(\text{TA}\) is total assets (non-current + current, including assets held for sale), \(\text{TE}\) is total equity (equity share capital + other equity + non-controlling interests in consolidated statements), and \(\text{TL}\) is total liabilities (non-current + current, including lease liabilities and liabilities of disposal groups held for sale). The check is computed on the standardized statements, rounded to a stated tolerance, and accompanied by a second check that standardized totals equal reported totals.

Explanation

The accounting equation holds in any correctly prepared balance sheet, so a non-zero check after standardization means the analyst — not the company — introduced an error. Typical causes:

  • A raw line not mapped, or mapped twice (often "other financial liabilities" or "other current assets").
  • A sign error, e.g., a negative "other equity" (accumulated losses) entered as positive.
  • Unit inconsistency within one company (a note in ₹ lakh mixed into a statement in ₹ crore).
  • NCI omitted from total equity in consolidated statements, or included twice.
  • Using standalone figures for one line and consolidated for others.

Why both levels are needed: a standardized balance sheet can balance internally yet be wrong if both sides omit the same item (e.g., a ₹500 crore asset held for sale and its associated liability both dropped). The tie to reported total assets catches this.

Implementation:

BSCheck   = ROUND(TotalAssets - (TotalEquity + TotalLiabilities), 2)
TieCheck  = ROUND(TotalAssets - ReportedTotalAssets, 2)
BSFlag    = IF(OR(ABS(BSCheck) > Tol, ABS(TieCheck) > Tol), 1, 0)

Copy across all years and companies (identical layout makes this one formula), and sum the flags into the master check. A tolerance of a few hundredths of a crore absorbs reporting rounding (Schedule III rounding rules) without hiding real errors.

Illustration

Peer C FY24 (₹ crore), standardized:

Line Value
Total non-current assets 3,215.40
Total current assets 9,860.75
Total assets 13,076.15
Equity share capital + other equity 4,120.30
Non-controlling interests 0.00
Total non-current liabilities 1,402.60
Total current liabilities 7,553.25
Total equity + liabilities 13,076.15

BS check = 0.00. Reported total assets = 13,168.15 → tie check = −92.00. Both sides missed the same ₹92 crore: "assets classified as held for sale" and the related liabilities were not in the mapping table. The internal check alone would have passed.

Practice Questions

Q1. Find the error [L2]

A standardized FY25 balance sheet shows total assets ₹24,610.80 crore, total equity ₹11,204.50 crore (excluding NCI of ₹186.20 crore shown separately in the reported BS), non-current liabilities ₹4,018.90 crore and current liabilities ₹9,201.20 crore. Reported total assets are ₹24,610.80 crore. Compute both checks, identify the likely error and state the fix.

Q2. Two-level check design [L1]

Explain with an example of your own why an internally balanced standardized balance sheet may still be wrong, and write the formula for the check that would detect it.

💡 Hint: Think of an item that appears on both sides of the balance sheet.

Q3. Accumulated losses [L2]

A loss-making peer reports "Other equity" of −₹340 crore. A junior analyst entered +340 in the raw extract because "all BS figures are positive". Explain the effect on the BS check, on ROE and on the equity multiplier in DuPont, and how the mapping table's sign column should handle it.
