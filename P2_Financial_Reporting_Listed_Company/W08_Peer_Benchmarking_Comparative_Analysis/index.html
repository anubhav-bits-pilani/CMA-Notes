Objective: Synthesize multi-company financial data into an institutional-grade comparative financial analysis report and executive presentation.

How this week fits

Week 8 closes Phase 2: Financial Reporting & Listed Company Analysis and delivers Project 2. The previous three weeks built the components. Week 5 set normalisation rules (normalised EBITDA, net debt, invested capital). Week 6 built the ratio dictionary and sector KPIs. Week 7 added the forensic toolkit for earnings quality and red flags. This week assembles them into one product: a fully checked workbook and a PDF report comparing three Indian listed peers, plus a recorded presentation that defends the conclusions.

The five topics follow the build order of the deliverable:

  1. Peer selection and calendarization (t1): choose peers on business economics and align periods, basis, accounting policies and frameworks.
  2. Relative margin and capital efficiency matrix (t2): rank peers on growth, margin level and stability, ROIC, CCC, leverage and cash conversion, with normalisation, weights and robustness tests.
  3. Executive narrative (t3): turn the matrix into a Pyramid Principle argument with a quantified conclusion, three pillars and risks.
  4. Charting (t4): build the EBITDA bridges, trend combos and growth vs ROIC scatters that serve as evidence.
  5. Finalising the workbook and PDF (t5): integrity checks, formatting, source referencing, print layout and review.

Prerequisites: a working standardized 3-year statement template (Week 5), the ratio dictionary and DuPont/CCC calculations (Week 6), and the forensic findings template (Week 7) completed for your chosen peers.

What comes next: Phase 3 starts with Excel modelling discipline (Week 9: FAST standard, LET/LAMBDA, model audit), then Power Query automation (Week 10) and Power BI dashboards (Weeks 11–12). The Project 2 workbook is reused as historicals for the 3-statement model (Week 13), as the comps universe for trading multiples (Week 16) and as a portfolio piece for interviews (Weeks 23–24). Build it so it can be reused.

Refresh before starting

Week 5 normalization, Week 6 ratio dictionary, Week 7 forensic template

Week 5 normalization rules, Week 6 ratio dictionary, Week 7 forensic findings template

  • Normalised EBITDA (Week 5): reported EBITDA excluding other income, exceptional items (Schedule III separate presentation), one-off inventory gains or losses (for example customs-duty-cut losses for jewellers in July 2024), restructuring and litigation settlements. Each adjustment is logged with source and ₹ crore effect.
  • Net debt: borrowings (current and non-current, including current maturities) + lease liabilities (post-Ind AS 116) − cash and cash equivalents − other bank balances (excluding earmarked balances) − liquid current investments. State the treatment of gold metal loans explicitly.
  • Invested capital: operating approach (NWC + net PP&E + CWIP + ROU + intangibles ± goodwill) reconciled to the financing approach (equity + net debt + other long-term liabilities − non-operating assets).
  • Ratio dictionary (Week 6): ROIC = NOPAT ÷ average IC; CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO on averages (DIO and DPO on COGS, DSO on revenue including unbilled for IT); DuPont 3- and 5-step; CFO/EBITDA; interest coverage; sector KPIs (Week 6, topic 6).
  • Forensic template (Week 7): accrual ratios, CFO vs PAT trend, receivables and unbilled growth vs revenue, related-party transactions (Ind AS 24, Sec 188), auditor's report (key audit matters, CARO 2020 remarks, modifications), contingent liabilities, capitalisation policies, Beneish/Altman-type screens. Each finding is rated: adjust / caveat / immaterial.

Excel charting, conditional formatting and print layout

Excel charting, conditional formatting and print layout settings

  • Charts: clustered column, combo (column + line on secondary axis), scatter and bubble, native waterfall (Excel 2016+, "Set as total"). Use Format Data Labels → "Value From Cells" for custom labels, and link a chart title to a cell (select the title, type = and click the cell). Use a consistent colour per company across all charts.
  • Conditional formatting: colour scales (per row, not across a mixed-unit range), formula-based rules (Use a formula to determine which cells to format), icon sets, and rule precedence with "Stop If True". Manage rules through Conditional Formatting → Manage Rules, with the scope set per range.
  • Print layout: Page Layout → Print Area, Print Titles (rows to repeat), Fit to 1 page wide, orientation per sheet, margins, custom headers and footers (file name, sheet name, page X of Y, date). Use Page Break Preview to place manual breaks, and Export → Create PDF/XPS with "Entire workbook" or selected sheets, checking that charts do not split across pages.
  • Number formats: Indian digit grouping ([>=10000000]##\,##\,##\,##0;[>=100000]##\,##\,##0;##,##0 or the system locale), 0.0% for ratios, 0.0"x" for multiples, 0" days" for CCC components.

Peer reports, investor presentations and earnings call transcripts

Peer annual reports, investor presentations and earnings call transcripts for the chosen sector

  • Annual reports (three years): consolidated statements, notes on accounting policies, segment information (Ind AS 108), leases (Ind AS 116), revenue disaggregation (Ind AS 115), related parties, contingent liabilities, auditor's report, and the MD&A (which includes key ratios and explanations for changes of 25% or more, as Schedule III requires).
  • Reg 33 quarterly filings: for calendarization and LTM (NSE/BSE corporate announcements, with filing date recorded), plus half-yearly balance sheets and cash flows.
  • Investor presentations and fact sheets: sector KPIs. For IT: CC growth, deal TCV, headcount, utilisation, attrition, top-client share. For jewellery: SSSG, store count by format (COCO/FOCO), studded share, gold-on-lease share. Record the slide number for every KPI.
  • Earnings call transcripts: management explanations of drivers (margin levers, pricing, hedging, store pipeline), guidance and analyst concerns. These feed the driver explanation in each pillar and the risk section. Quote sparingly, with date and speaker.
  • Optional: credit rating rationales (CRISIL/ICRA/CARE) for each peer, which give independent views on business and financial risk and sensitivities.

Cross-cutting concepts

Comparability is the core

Comparability is the core: same definitions, periods, accounting policies and units for every peer

A peer report is a set of relative statements, so every difference between peers must come from economics, not measurement. Four layers of comparability must hold before any ratio is trusted. Definitions: one formula per metric on a definitions sheet, applied by the same formula to every peer and year (t2 pitfall 3, t5 c04). Periods: the same 12-month window, achieved by calendarizing December- or September-year companies using four Reg 33 quarters, and LTM where the latest data matters (t1 s03, c01, c02). Accounting policies and frameworks: inventory cost formula, depreciation method and lives, borrowing cost capitalisation, gross vs net revenue, Ind AS vs IFRS, and US GAAP lease and LIFO treatment, each adjusted or disclosed (t1 s04, s05). Units and basis: consolidated, continuing operations, ₹ crore, latest restated comparatives, consistent sign conventions (t1 s06, t5 s03). The practical test is a "comparability statement" in the peer-context section of the report, backed by the policy alignment table and adjustments log in the appendix. If a reviewer can find one unexplained basis difference, they will distrust every ranking. Comparability work is therefore not overhead. It is what makes the conclusion credible.

Narrative, matrix and charts converge on one conclusion

Narrative, matrix and charts must converge on one governing conclusion

The report has three expressions of the same analysis: the matrix (t2), the charts (t4) and the narrative (t3). They must tell one story. If the executive summary says "Kalyan is the most improved capital allocator", the matrix must show Kalyan's ROIC and leverage improving most. The growth vs ROIC chart must show Kalyan moving towards the top-right. Every action title must point the same way. Divergence happens when the narrative is written from memory, the charts are built before the conclusion is fixed, or the matrix is changed (weights, definitions) after the narrative is drafted. Prevent it with a Storyline tab. It lists the governing thought, the pillars, each action title, the supporting exhibit and the workbook cells for every number quoted. Link chart titles and narrative numbers to cells where possible (t4 s06). Run a final convergence check: read only the titles and the summary, then look at each exhibit and confirm it proves its title (the "so what" test, t3 c03). Where the evidence is genuinely mixed, the governing thought must state the trade-off. Charts and matrix must not contradict an over-simplified claim.

Forensic flags adjust or caveat the ranking

Forensic flags from Week 7 should adjust or caveat the peer ranking

The matrix ranks reported (normalised) numbers. Week 7's forensic review asks whether those numbers are reliable. The two must be connected explicitly. For each forensic finding, decide one of three treatments. Adjust: the effect is quantifiable, so restate the metric. Examples: strip a one-off land-sale gain from other income, add back capitalised interest to finance cost, exclude unbilled revenue growth judged unsustainable from DSO improvements. Caveat: the effect is real but not reliably quantifiable, such as a key audit matter on revenue recognition, rising related-party purchases or aggressive useful-life extensions. Keep the ranking but disclose that a peer's position is less certain, and say which way the risk cuts. Immaterial: record the review and move on. Re-run the matrix after adjustments and report whether any rank changes. If a forensic caveat could plausibly reverse a ranking, it belongs in the executive summary and possibly in the governing thought ("…subject to the sustainability of Peer X's unbilled revenue build"). The forensic-notes section (t3 s06) and the adjustments log (t5) are where this is documented.

Sector-specific checks for the two exercise options

Exercise: Project 2 on IT (Infosys, TCS, Wipro) needs constant-currency and IFRS/Ind AS checks; jewellery (Titan, Kalyan, Senco) needs gold-loan and franchise-model adjustments

IT option. Revenue is about 95% or more in foreign currency, so ₹ growth mixes demand with rupee movement. Report ₹, USD and constant-currency growth, using each company's disclosed CC rates (t1 s05). Infosys and Wipro file IFRS 20-Fs alongside Ind AS annual reports, while TCS has only Ind AS. Use Ind AS consolidated statements for all three and reconcile any line taken from a 20-F. Also check the treatment of unbilled revenue in DSO, surplus cash in invested capital (all three are net-cash, so ROIC must exclude treasury assets) and other income in EBITDA.

Jewellery option. Gold metal loans (GML) fund a large share of inventory. Decide whether they sit in net debt (they are borrowings, repayable in gold or cash) and apply the rule identically. Note each company's hedging policy, because unhedged inventory creates gold-price gains or losses that must be normalised. Franchise models (FOCO/FOFO) change revenue recognition (wholesale vs retail), margins, inventory and lease intensity. Adjust or caveat both margin and ROIC comparisons, and run the Ind AS 116 robustness test (t2 s06). For Titan, use the jewellery segment where possible to strip out watches, eyecare and other businesses.

Sector KPIs contextualize financial ratios

Sector KPIs (w6_t6) contextualize financial ratios in the report

Financial ratios say what happened. Sector KPIs explain why and make the driver component of each pillar credible (t3 s04). For IT services, the key KPIs are constant-currency growth, deal TCV and large-deal wins (demand), utilisation, offshore effort mix, subcontractor cost and attrition (margin levers), revenue per employee (productivity), and top-client and vertical concentration (risk). For jewellery, they are same-store sales growth (SSSG) versus new-store growth, store count by format (COCO/FOCO/FOFO), studded share (gross margin mix), average ticket size and gold-on-lease share (funding and hedging). Use KPIs in three places: in peer context (why these peers and how their models differ), as driver evidence under each pillar ("margin lead reflects 78% offshore effort vs 68%"), and as monitorables in the risk section. KPIs are company-defined and not audited, and definitions vary (for example SSSG periods and store inclusion rules). Cite the source slide and do not put KPIs into the composite score unless definitions are demonstrably identical.

Hands-on exercise brief

Exercise (roadmap): Complete Project 2: Comparative Financial Analysis Report on 3 Indian listed peers (e.g., Infosys, TCS, Wipro OR Titan, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold).

Scope and inputs

  • Peers: choose one set (IT: Infosys, TCS, Wipro; or jewellery: Titan, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold). You may add a fourth peer (for example HCLTech, or PN Gadgil Jewellers) as a robustness check, with written justification.
  • Period: FY23, FY24 and FY25 (fiscal years ending 31 March), plus FY22 revenue for the 3-year CAGR and the opening balance sheets needed for averages. Add LTM to the latest Reg 33 quarter as a supplementary column.
  • Basis: consolidated, continuing operations, ₹ crore (one decimal), latest restated comparatives.
  • Common assumptions (use unless you justify alternatives): tax rate for NOPAT 25.17% (or each peer's sustainable effective rate, applied consistently); WACC inputs of risk-free rate 6.8%, equity risk premium 6.0%, industry unlevered beta (IT 0.85, jewellery 0.95) relevered at each peer's net debt/equity (zero for net-cash peers), pre-tax cost of debt 8.5–9.5% by peer rating; matrix weights as set in t2 s03 for your sector; leverage floor 0x and cap 5x; z-score cap ±2.
  • Sources: annual reports (three years), Reg 33 quarterly results, investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, and optionally rating rationales.

Deliverables

  • Workbook (.xlsx): all tabs above, formatted with blue inputs, black formulas and green cross-sheet links. Master checks show 0 errors.
  • PDF report (15–25 pages): executive summary (one page, at most 350 words), peer context, three pillar sections, forensic notes, risks, appendix (definitions, standardized statements, adjustments log, sources, full matrix).
  • Presentation (10–12 slides) with action titles, used for the Saturday recording.
  • Change log recording each version, date and changes.
