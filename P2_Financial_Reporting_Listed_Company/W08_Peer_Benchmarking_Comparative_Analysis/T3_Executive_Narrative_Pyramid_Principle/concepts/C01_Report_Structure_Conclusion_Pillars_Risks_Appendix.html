Key concept

Report Structure: Conclusion, Pillars, Risks, Appendix

Concept: Structure = Conclusion → 3 quantitative pillars → key downside risks → supporting appendix

Statement

An executive financial narrative is ordered by importance to the decision, not by the sequence of analysis:

\[ \text{Conclusion} \;\rightarrow\; \text{Pillar}_1, \text{Pillar}_2, \text{Pillar}_3 \;\rightarrow\; \text{Key downside risks} \;\rightarrow\; \text{Appendix} \]

  • Conclusion: the governing thought, one specific, quantified, falsifiable sentence answering the reader's question.
  • Three quantitative pillars: MECE reasons, each stated with metric, trend, peer gap and driver.
  • Key downside risks: the top 3–5 risks to the conclusion, with monitorables and what would change the view.
  • Supporting appendix: definitions, standardized data, adjustments, sources, full tables, so every number can be verified.

Explanation

The structure applies the Minto pyramid (s01) to a financial report and adds two elements that analytical audiences require: risks (intellectual honesty and decision usefulness) and an appendix (verifiability).

Why this order. Decision-makers want the answer first, the reasons second, the caveats third and the evidence only if challenged. Analysts naturally work in the opposite order (data → ratios → patterns → conclusion). The discipline is to reverse the order of discovery when writing.

Why "quantitative" pillars. Qualitative pillars ("strong brand", "good management") cannot be checked and invite disagreement. Each pillar must carry at least one number that traces to the workbook (s04).

Why risks come before the appendix and after the pillars. Risks qualify the conclusion and must be read by anyone who reads the pillars. Putting them in the appendix hides them, which is pitfall 3 in topic.md. Putting them first undermines the answer before it is made.

Why an appendix. It separates proof from argument. The body stays short. A reviewer, interviewer or auditor can still go from any headline number to the definition, the standardized statement line, the adjustment and the source page.

Scaling. The same structure works at every length:

Format Conclusion Pillars Risks Appendix
30-second elevator answer 1 sentence 3 phrases 1 phrase "Happy to walk through the numbers"
One-page executive summary SCQA + governing thought 3 short paragraphs 3 bullets Reference to appendix
15-minute presentation Slide 1 Slides 2–8 Slide 9 Backup slides
25-page PDF report Section 1 Sections 3–5 Section 7 Appendices A–F

Illustration

IT peer report, one-page version (abridged):

  • Conclusion: Peer A is the highest-quality compounder among the three. Peer B is the growth alternative, and Peer C must fix margins before growth creates value.
  • Pillar 1 (Returns): A's ROIC of 55% exceeds WACC by 43 pp, against 26 pp for B and 9 pp for C.
  • Pillar 2 (Stability): A's margin CV of 0.02 is the lowest, reflecting 78% offshore effort and lower subcontracting.
  • Pillar 3 (Cash quality): B converts 81% of EBITDA to CFO vs 76% for A. A's rising unbilled revenue is a watchpoint.
  • Risks: BFSI spending cut (A has 38% BFSI exposure); rupee appreciation (−30 bps margin per 1%); GenAI-led pricing deflation.
  • Appendix: definitions (ROIC ex-surplus cash, CCC incl. unbilled), adjustments log, sources.

Practice Questions

Q1. Re-order a draft [L1]

A draft memo runs: (1) FY23–FY25 revenue table; (2) margin trends; (3) ROIC table; (4) "Risks include competition"; (5) "In conclusion, Titan is strongest." Restructure it into conclusion → pillars → risks → appendix, and identify what is missing for each element.

Q2. Write a one-page structure [L2]

Using the following jewellery data, write the conclusion sentence, three pillar sentences (one number each) and three risk bullets: ROIC Titan 24%, Kalyan 14%, Senco 11%; WACC 12–13%; CAGR 21%, 30%, 26%; CCC 150, 205, 195 days; net debt incl. GML/EBITDA 0.5x, 1.5x, 2.1x; unhedged inventory share 15%, 25%, 40%.

💡 Hint: Decide the reader's question first (investor vs lender), because it changes which pillar leads.

Q3. Scale the structure [L2]

Convert your Q2 answer into (a) a 30-second spoken answer and (b) a slide list for a 15-minute presentation with backup slides. State what moves to backup and why.
