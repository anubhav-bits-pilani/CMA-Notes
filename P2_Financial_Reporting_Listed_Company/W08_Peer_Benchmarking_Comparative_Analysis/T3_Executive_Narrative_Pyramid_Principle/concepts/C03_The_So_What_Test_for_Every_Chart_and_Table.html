Key concept

The "So What" Test for Every Chart and Table

Concept: "So what" test: every chart and table must support a stated claim

Statement

An exhibit (chart, table or number) earns its place in the report only if it passes:

  1. Claim test: there is an explicit claim it supports. Usually this is the action title of its slide or section, or a sentence in the pillar.
  2. Support test: a reader looking at the exhibit can see the evidence for that claim without further explanation.
  3. Necessity test: removing the exhibit would weaken the argument.

If an exhibit fails any test, rewrite its title into a claim it supports, redesign it to show the claim, move it to the appendix, or delete it.

Explanation

Analysts accumulate exhibits as they work: every ratio has a chart, and every year has a table. The "so what" test reverses the logic. Start from the claims (the ghost deck, s03) and keep only the exhibits that prove them. Asking "so what?" of each exhibit forces the analyst to state the implication: "Titan's DIO is 160 days" becomes "So what? Titan ties up 55 fewer days of COGS than Kalyan, which explains a third of its ROIC lead".

Design consequences of passing the test:

  • The action title states the claim (s03), and the caption or source note states basis and units.
  • The exhibit highlights the claim. Colour the subject company, sort bars by value, annotate the gap and show the peer median line.
  • Only relevant series appear. A claim about ROIC does not need PAT margin on the same chart.
  • Raw values stay visible. Ranks or colours alone hide the size of gaps (topic t2 pitfall).

Appendix exception. Reference tables (definitions, standardized statements, full matrix) support the claim "these numbers are verifiable". They pass the test by being referenced from the body, not by carrying an argument.

Common failures:

Failure Example Fix
Orphan exhibit A revenue-by-geography pie chart with no related claim Delete, or link to a growth-driver claim
Contradicting exhibit Title says "margins stable" but the chart shows a 300 bps dip in FY24 Rewrite the title to acknowledge the dip, or explain it
Overloaded exhibit Every metric for all peers and all years on one chart Split. One claim per exhibit
Decorative exhibit 3D pie, stock photos, icons Remove
Unsupported claim Narrative cites "superior cash conversion" with no exhibit or workbook reference Add evidence or drop the claim

Illustration

Draft slide: title "Working capital"; a table of DIO, DSO, DPO and CCC for three jewellers over three years (36 numbers).

So-what review:

  • Claim? None stated. Fails test 1.
  • Rewrite title: "Kalyan's CCC is 55 days longer than Titan's, and all of the gap is inventory."
  • Support? A 36-number table makes the reader hunt. Redesign as a stacked bar of FY25 DIO, DSO and DPO by peer, with the CCC labelled and the DIO gap annotated (215 vs 160 days). Move the 3-year table to the appendix.
  • Necessity? Yes. It supports pillar 1 (returns), because CCC drives invested capital.

Practice Questions

Q1. Run the test on five exhibits [L1]

For each exhibit, state whether it passes, and what you would do: (a) Titan's share-price chart since listing; (b) a bar chart of FY25 ROIC with WACC line for three peers; (c) a table of every Schedule III line item for three years; (d) a scatter of revenue CAGR vs ROIC with median dividers; (e) a pie chart of Infosys revenue by vertical in a report concluding on capital efficiency.

Q2. Rewrite an exhibit [L2]

An exhibit titled "Margins" shows EBITDA, EBIT and PAT margins for three IT peers over FY23–FY25 as a 9-line chart. The report's pillar claims "Peer A's margin is the most stable". Redesign the exhibit: chart type, series, highlighting, annotation, action title and source note.

💡 Hint: The claim is about stability of one margin. Decide which series are necessary.

Q3. Find unsupported claims [L2]

Review this executive summary paragraph and list every claim that would need an exhibit or workbook reference: "Kalyan's franchise model has transformed its returns, with ROIC now above cost of capital, while its improved cash conversion and lower leverage give it the strongest balance sheet in the sector. Gold-price risk is well managed." For each claim, name the exhibit or table you would add.
