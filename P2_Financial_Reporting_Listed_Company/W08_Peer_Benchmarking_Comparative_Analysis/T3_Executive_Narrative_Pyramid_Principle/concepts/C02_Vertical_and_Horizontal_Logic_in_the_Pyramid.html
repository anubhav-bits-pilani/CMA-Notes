Key concept

Vertical and Horizontal Logic in the Pyramid

Concept: Vertical logic: each pillar answers "why?" for the conclusion; horizontal logic: pillars are MECE

Statement

A pyramid is logically sound when it passes two tests:

  • Vertical logic (question–answer dialogue). Every statement raises a question in the reader's mind ("Why?" or "How do you know?"), and the statements directly beneath it answer that question. Each pillar answers "why is the conclusion true?". Each piece of evidence answers "why is the pillar true?".
  • Horizontal logic (MECE grouping). Statements at the same level are mutually exclusive (no overlap) and collectively exhaustive (together sufficient to support the statement above). They are also of the same kind: all reasons, all steps or all segments.

\[ \text{Conclusion} \xleftarrow{\text{why?}} \{\text{Pillar}_1, \text{Pillar}_2, \text{Pillar}_3\}, \qquad \text{Pillar}_i \cap \text{Pillar}_j = \varnothing, \qquad \bigcup_i \text{Pillar}_i \Rightarrow \text{Conclusion} \]

Explanation

Vertical test in practice. Read the conclusion, ask "why?" aloud, and read the three pillars. Each must be a direct answer. A pillar that answers a different question ("What does the company do?") fails. So does one that merely repeats the conclusion in other words. Then repeat one level down. Each exhibit must answer "how do you know?" for its pillar. This is the "so what" test (c03) applied from the bottom up.

Horizontal test in practice.

  • Mutually exclusive: could the same fact be cited in two pillars? If "EBITDA margin expansion" supports both "profitability" and "earnings growth", the pillars overlap, and the reader perceives three arguments when there are two.
  • Collectively exhaustive: if all three pillars are true, does the conclusion follow? A conclusion about "capital efficiency" supported only by growth and margin pillars is not exhaustive, because capital is missing. Add a returns or working-capital pillar, or narrow the conclusion.
  • Same kind: do not mix a reason ("returns are highest") with a recommendation ("investors should buy") or a risk ("gold could fall") at the same level.

Useful MECE frames for financial analysis:

Frame Pillars When to use
Value drivers Growth / Returns / Risk Investment-style peer comparison
ROIC tree Margin / Capital turnover / Reinvestment Capital-efficiency conclusion
Credit Business risk / Financial risk / Liquidity Lender audience
Income statement flow Revenue / Costs / Below-EBITDA items Variance or MIS commentary

A frame guarantees MECE by construction. Choose the one that matches the question.

Illustration

Conclusion: "Kalyan is the most improved capital allocator among listed jewellers."

Draft pillar Vertical test (answers "why most improved capital allocator?") Horizontal test Verdict
(1) EBITDA margin up 100 bps to 7.4% Partly: margin is one input to returns Overlaps with (2) Merge
(2) EBITDA grew 35% a year Weakly: growth is not efficiency Overlaps with (1) Merge
(3) Store count doubled No: this is activity, not allocation quality — Move to evidence
Revised (1) ROIC up 4 pp to 14%, now above WACC Yes Distinct Keep
Revised (2) IC turnover up from 1.9x to 2.5x via FOCO franchise funding Yes Distinct (capital side) Keep
Revised (3) Net debt/EBITDA down from 2.4x to 1.5x, funded by internal cash flow Yes Distinct (balance sheet) Keep

The revised set follows the ROIC tree plus balance sheet. It is MECE and answers "why?" directly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply both tests [L1]

Conclusion: "TCS is the most resilient IT services franchise." Pillars: (a) "Revenue is the largest in the industry", (b) "EBITDA margin has been the most stable (CV 0.02)", (c) "Net cash exceeds ₹50,000 crore". Apply the vertical and horizontal tests to each pillar and propose any changes.

Q2. Choose a MECE frame [L2]

For each question, choose a MECE frame and name its three pillars: (a) "Which jeweller should a bank lend ₹750 crore to?", (b) "Why did Peer B's FY25 EBITDA fall short of budget?", (c) "Which IT company has the best long-term value-creation profile?"

💡 Hint: Match the frame to the decision the reader must make.

Q3. Repair overlapping pillars [L2]

A draft supports "Titan is the best-managed jeweller" with: (1) "highest gross margin", (2) "highest EBITDA margin", (3) "highest PAT margin". Explain the failure on both tests, and rewrite the pillars using data of your choice drawn from ROIC, CCC and cash conversion.
