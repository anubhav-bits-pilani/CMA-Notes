Key concept

One-Page Executive Summary Readable in Under Two Minutes

Concept: One-page executive summary readable in under 2 minutes

Statement

The executive summary must fit on one page and be readable in under two minutes. At a typical business-reading speed of about 200–250 words per minute for careful reading, the budget is:

\[ \text{Word budget} \approx 2 \text{ min} \times 200\text{–}250 \text{ words/min} \approx 300\text{–}450 \text{ words, including one exhibit} \]

In practice, aim for about 300–350 words of text plus one exhibit. That leaves time to glance at the exhibit and allows for numbers, which slow reading.

Explanation

Why one page and two minutes. Senior readers (CFO, investment committee chair, hiring manager) allocate very little time to each document. They decide from page 1 whether to read further, delegate or act. A summary that spills onto page 2 is usually not finished. A summary that takes five minutes forces skimming, which loses the argument.

Recommended layout:

Block Content Words
Title (action title) Governing thought in one line 12–20
SCQA paragraph Situation, complication, (question), answer 60–90
Pillar 1–3 One bold lead sentence + 1–2 supporting sentences each (metric, trend, peer gap, driver) 50–70 each
Key risks 3 bullets with monitorable and threshold 40–60
Exhibit One chart or compact table (matrix heat map or growth vs ROIC quadrant) with action caption and source Caption 15–25
Basis line "Consolidated, FY ending March, ₹ crore, data as of …; definitions in Appendix A" 15–20

Readability techniques:

  • Bold lead sentences so the skimmer reads only the bold text and still gets the argument (the ghost-deck principle on one page).
  • Numbers in the text, not just the exhibit. Each pillar carries its headline number.
  • Plain words. Avoid unexplained jargon ("ROIC ex-surplus cash" can be defined in the appendix, while the summary says "return on operating capital").
  • Short sentences. Aim for 15–20 words on average, with one idea per sentence.
  • White space. Do not shrink the font to 8 pt to fit. Cut words instead.

Test it. Time a colleague reading it. Then ask them to state the conclusion, the three reasons and the main risk from memory. If they cannot, revise.

Illustration

A compliant summary skeleton (jewellery, about 320 words when completed):

Titan remains the most capital-efficient jeweller; Kalyan is closing the gap fastest; Senco's growth is not yet earning its cost of capital (title, 22 words)

SCQA paragraph (about 80 words): organised jewellery is gaining share → record gold prices and franchise-led expansion blur comparability → which chain turns growth into value? → answer above.

Returns: Titan's ROIC of 24% is 11.5 pp above WACC… (60 words) Capital intensity: Kalyan's FOCO model lifted capital turnover from 1.9x to 2.5x… (60 words) Balance sheet: Kalyan halved net debt/EBITDA to 1.5x; Senco's rose to 2.1x… (60 words)

Key risks: gold-price correction (Senco 40% unhedged); franchise funding; customs-duty reversal (45 words)

Exhibit: growth vs ROIC quadrant with WACC line (caption 20 words)

Practice Questions

Q1. Word-budget audit [L1]

A draft executive summary contains 620 words, two charts and a 12-row table. Using the layout above, specify what to cut, what to move to the body or appendix, and the target word count for each block.

Q2. Write the one-pager [L3]

Write a complete executive summary of at most 350 words for an IT peer report, with one exhibit described in words (type, series, highlight, action caption). Use: revenue FY25 Peer A ₹2,55,320 crore, B ₹1,62,990 crore, C ₹89,090 crore; CC CAGR 9.5%, 10.3%, 3.4%; EBITDA margin 27.3%, 24.8%, 20.3%; margin CV 0.020, 0.035, 0.050; ROIC 55%, 38%, 21%; WACC 12%; CFO/EBITDA 76%, 81%, 79%; BFSI share 38%, 32%, 34%; all net-cash.

💡 Hint: Write the title and the three bold lead sentences first, and check that they alone convey the argument.

Q3. Two-minute test protocol [L2]

Design a test to check that your Project 2 summary meets the two-minute standard. Specify who reads it, timing, the three recall questions, the pass criteria and how you will revise if it fails. Explain why the reader should not be someone who helped build the workbook.
