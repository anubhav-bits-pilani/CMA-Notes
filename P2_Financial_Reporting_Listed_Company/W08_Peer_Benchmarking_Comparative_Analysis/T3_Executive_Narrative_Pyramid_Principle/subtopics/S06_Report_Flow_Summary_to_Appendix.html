Subtopic note

Report Flow: Summary to Appendix

Scope: Report flow: summary, peer context, financial pillars, forensic notes, risks, appendix

Why it matters

The order of sections decides what the reader learns first, what they can skip and how easily they can audit the work. A comparative financial analysis report has to serve three readers at once. The executive reads only page 1. The analyst-reviewer reads the pillars and checks the exhibits. The auditor of the work, whether a reviewer, an interviewer or you in six months, goes to the appendix to verify definitions and sources. The six-part flow below applies the pyramid to the whole document. It matches the structure of equity research initiations and credit rating rationales, and it is the required PDF structure for Project 2 (topic t5).

Core notes

The six sections

# Section Purpose Length (Project 2 guide) Key contents
1 Executive summary Governing thought + 3 quantified pillars + top risks, readable in under 2 minutes 1 page SCQA paragraph, three pillar paragraphs, top 3 risks, one summary exhibit (matrix or quadrant)
2 Peer context Why these peers, on what basis, and the industry backdrop 1–2 pages Peer selection rationale (t1), basis and policy alignment summary, sector KPIs, industry situation
3 Financial pillars One section per pillar, each with an action title, evidence and driver analysis 2–3 pages per pillar Matrix extracts, quadrants, bridges, DuPont, CCC decomposition
4 Forensic notes Earnings-quality and accounting observations that adjust or caveat the ranking 1–2 pages Week 7 flags per peer, adjustments made, residual caveats
5 Risks Downside risks, monitorables, what would change the conclusion 1 page Risk table, sensitivities, monitorable dashboard
6 Appendix Everything needed to verify the work As needed Definitions sheet, standardized statements, adjustments log, source references, policy alignment table, full matrix and sensitivity tables

Section-by-section guidance

1. Executive summary. This is written last but placed first. It is the governing thought and key line (s01), opened with SCQA (s02). Each pillar is one quantified paragraph (s04). Add one exhibit that summarises the whole argument, usually the composite matrix heat map or the growth vs ROIC quadrant. It must stand alone: a reader who stops here has the answer, the reasons and the risks.

2. Peer context. Establish credibility before the argument. Explain why these three peers (business-model match, scale, geography, with t1 s01–s02). State the basis (consolidated, continuing operations, FY ending March, calendarized where needed, ₹ crore). Summarise policy and framework alignment, with details in the appendix. Give a short industry situation with the sector KPIs (Week 6 t6) that contextualise the ratios: same-store sales growth, studded share and store count for jewellers, or CC growth, deal TCV, utilisation and attrition for IT.

3. Financial pillars. One section per pillar, in order of importance. Each section starts with an action title (s03), then a lead paragraph (metric, trend, peer gap, driver), then two or three exhibits, each with its own action-title caption and source note. Close each pillar with a one-line "so what" that links it back to the governing thought.

4. Forensic notes. This section adjusts or caveats the ranking, which is one of this week's cross-cutting themes. For each peer, list the material Week 7 findings: accrual quality, CFO/EBITDA gaps, related-party transactions, unbilled revenue trends, capitalisation policies, auditor remarks (CARO, key audit matters, modified opinions). Then state whether each finding was adjusted in the matrix, caveated in the narrative, or assessed as immaterial. If a flag would change the ranking, it must also appear in the executive summary.

5. Risks. Downside risks mapped to pillars, quantified sensitivities, the monitorable dashboard and what would change the conclusion (s05).

6. Appendix. The audit trail:

  • Ratio definitions (one formula per metric, applied to all peers, see topic t5 c04).
  • Standardized 3-year statements for each peer.
  • Adjustments log: each normalisation or policy adjustment with ₹ crore effect and source.
  • Source references: annual report page/note, Reg 33 filing date, investor presentation slide.
  • Policy alignment and basis tables (t1).
  • Full matrix, normalised scores, weight scenarios and Ind AS 116 robustness tables (t2).

Navigation and consistency devices

  • Exhibit numbering ("Exhibit 7") and cross-references from text to exhibits and from exhibits to appendix tables.
  • Consistent units and signs throughout (₹ crore, one decimal for %, days as integers). State them on the inside cover.
  • Source line under every exhibit: "Source: Company annual reports FY23–FY25 (consolidated), Reg 33 filings; analyst calculations. See Appendix D for definitions."
  • Date and basis stamp: "Data as of 30 September 2025 (LTM) unless stated."

Illustrative table of contents (jewellery Project 2)

  1. Executive summary — Titan leads on returns; Kalyan is the most improved; Senco's growth is not yet value-accretive
  2. Peer context — Three national/regional chains with different store models; organised share gains drive 15–20% sector growth
  3. Pillar 1: Returns — Titan's 12 pp ROIC spread is the widest; Kalyan's has improved 4 pp in three years
  4. Pillar 2: Growth quality — Kalyan's 30% CAGR is franchise-funded; over half of sector growth is gold-price inflation
  5. Pillar 3: Balance sheet and cash — Kalyan has halved leverage while Senco's borrowings including GML have risen 57%
  6. Forensic notes — No material red flags; inventory gains from gold-price rises normalised for all three
  7. Risks — Gold-price correction and franchise funding are the two variables that could reverse the ranking
  8. Appendix A–F — Definitions, standardized statements, adjustments log, sources, policy alignment, full matrix and sensitivities

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Forensic findings that dominate. If a forensic issue is material (for example a qualified audit opinion or suspected revenue manipulation), move it up into the executive summary and possibly into the governing thought. Do not leave it on page 12.
  • Short-form outputs. A 5-slide presentation compresses sections 2–5 but keeps the order: answer, context, pillars, caveats, risks.
  • Peer context vs pillars. Do not let the peer context become a data dump. Only the facts needed to accept the comparison belong there.
  • Appendix bloat. The appendix should contain verification material, not orphan analyses. An analysis that supports no claim either belongs in a pillar or should be cut (the "so what" test, concept c03).
  • Audience variations. A credit-focused report might reorder pillars (balance sheet first) and expand liquidity analysis. The six-part logic still holds.

Key takeaways

  • Report flow: executive summary, peer context, financial pillars, forensic notes, risks, appendix.
  • Write the executive summary last, place it first, and make it stand alone.
  • Peer context earns credibility by explaining peer choice, basis and alignment before the argument.
  • Forensic notes state whether each flag was adjusted, caveated or immaterial. Ranking-changing flags go into the summary.
  • The appendix is the audit trail: definitions, standardized statements, adjustments log, sources and full matrix.

Practice Questions

Q1. Order the sections [L1]

A draft report has this order: Appendix of definitions; Revenue analysis FY23–FY25; Margin analysis; Balance sheet; Forensic observations; Conclusion. Reorder it into the six-part flow, rename each section with an action title for a jewellery peer report (use any reasonable data) and state what new content is required.

Q2. Executive summary page plan [L2]

Draft the layout of a one-page executive summary for an IT peer report. Specify the word budget per block, the single exhibit and its action title, and the placement of the top three risks. Use: ROIC A 55%, B 38%, C 21%; WACC 12%; CC CAGR A 9.5%, B 10.3%, C 3.4%; margin CV 0.020, 0.035, 0.050; CFO/EBITDA 76%, 81%, 79%.

💡 Hint: Budget about 300–350 words in total so the page reads in under two minutes.

Q3. Forensic section design [L2]

Your Week 7 review found: (a) Peer X's other income includes ₹180 crore of one-time gain on sale of land; (b) Peer Y's auditor reported a key audit matter on revenue recognition for fixed-price contracts; (c) Peer Z's related-party purchases from a promoter entity rose from 4% to 11% of COGS. Write the forensic-notes entries. Classify each as adjusted, caveated or immaterial, and state whether it changes the ranking or the executive summary.

Q4. Appendix specification [L2]

List the appendix tables required for a Project 2 report on three peers. For each, give its columns and the workbook tab it is linked to, and explain how a reviewer would use it to verify one specific number in the executive summary.

Q5. Adapting the flow for a lender [L3]

A bank's credit committee asks for the same peer analysis, reframed to answer "Which of the three jewellers is the strongest borrower for a ₹750 crore working-capital facility?" Redesign the report flow and pillar order, and specify which additional analyses (liquidity, covenant headroom, GML exposure, rating-agency views) must be added and where they go.
