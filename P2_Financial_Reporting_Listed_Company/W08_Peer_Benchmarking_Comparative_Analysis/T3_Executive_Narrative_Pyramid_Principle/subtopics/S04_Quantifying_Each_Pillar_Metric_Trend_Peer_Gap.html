Quantifying Each Pillar: Metric, Trend, Peer Gap, Driver

Scope: Quantifying each pillar: metric, trend, peer gap, driver explanation

Why it matters

A pillar that says "Titan has superior profitability" is an opinion. A pillar that says "Titan's EBITDA margin of 10.5% is 310 bps above Kalyan's and stable over three years (CV 0.04), because studded jewellery is 30% of its sales against about 20% at Kalyan" is an evidenced argument. It can be checked, challenged and defended. Investment committees, rating committees and interviewers probe each pillar with the same four questions. What is the number? Is it getting better or worse? How does it compare? Why? Answering all four in the pillar itself pre-empts the challenge. It also forces the analyst to confirm that the workbook actually supports the claim.

Core notes

The four components of a quantified pillar

Component Question it answers Source in Project 2 workbook Example
Metric What is the level? Ratio engine / matrix (topic t2) "ROIC of 24% (FY25)"
Trend Improving or deteriorating, and how fast? 3-year standardized statements "up from 19% in FY22"
Peer gap How large is the difference vs peers, in the metric's own units? Matrix raw values, peer median "11 points above the peer median of 13%"
Driver Why? What operational or structural factor explains it? DuPont, CCC decomposition, KPIs (Week 6 t6), segment notes "driven by 1.9x capital turnover from franchise-funded inventory"

Template sentence:

[Company]'s [metric] of [level] ([period]) is [gap] [above/below] [peer/median], [trend direction and magnitude since base year], driven by [driver with its own number].

1. Metric: choose one headline number per pillar

Choose the metric that best proves the pillar's claim and that is defined on the definitions sheet. Do not stack five metrics in one sentence. Supporting metrics go in the exhibit beneath. State the period and basis ("FY25, consolidated, post-Ind AS 116") when it matters for comparability.

2. Trend: direction, magnitude, consistency

  • Express the change in the right unit: percentage points (pp) or basis points (bps) for ratios and margins, % for growth in amounts, days for CCC components.
  • Say whether the trend is consistent (improved every year) or driven by one year.
  • Avoid "%" when you mean "pp". Margin rising from 10% to 12% is +2 pp (200 bps), not +20% (which is the relative change).

3. Peer gap: in units, against the right comparator

  • State the gap to the relevant comparator: the leader, the median or the subject company, depending on the question.
  • Use the metric's own unit ("55 days shorter", "310 bps higher", "0.9x lower leverage").
  • Convert to ₹ where it sharpens the message: a CCC gap of 55 days on daily COGS of ₹110 crore is about ₹6,050 crore of working capital.
  • Indicate significance. A 20 bps margin gap is within noise. Say "in line".

4. Driver: explain, then quantify the explanation

Driver explanation turns description into analysis. Use decomposition tools from earlier weeks:

Pillar metric Decomposition tool Example driver statement
ROIC NOPAT margin × invested-capital turnover "Peer A's ROIC lead is two-thirds turnover (1.9x vs 1.4x), one-third margin"
ROE DuPont (3- or 5-step) "Higher ROE reflects asset turnover, not leverage"
EBITDA margin Gross margin mix, employee cost %, other opex %. EBITDA bridge (topic t4) "Studded mix adds about 200 bps; lower ad spend about 60 bps"
CCC DIO, DSO, DPO "Entire gap is DIO; DSO and DPO are similar"
Revenue growth Price, volume and mix; same-store sales growth vs new stores; CC vs FX "18 of 30 points of growth are gold-price inflation"
CFO/EBITDA Tax paid, working-capital change, non-cash items "Gap explained by ₹1,200 crore inventory build for new stores"

Use sector KPIs (Week 6 t6) to support drivers. For IT: utilisation, offshore effort mix, attrition, subcontractor cost %, large-deal TCV. For jewellery: studded share, same-store sales growth, store additions (COCO vs FOCO), gold-on-lease share, average selling price.

Worked example: a fully quantified pillar

Data (₹ crore, illustrative): Titan jewellery segment ROIC FY22 19%, FY25 24%; Kalyan 10% → 14%; Senco 12% → 11%; peer median FY25 14%. Titan NOPAT margin 9.6%, IC turnover 2.5x. Kalyan NOPAT margin 5.6%, IC turnover 2.5x.

Pillar 1 — Returns: Titan's ROIC of 24% (FY25) is 10 points above the peer median and has risen 5 points since FY22, all of it from margin (NOPAT margin 9.6% vs Kalyan's 5.6% at an identical 2.5x capital turnover). This reflects Titan's higher studded share and brand-led pricing, not a lighter balance sheet.

That one paragraph contains metric (24%), trend (+5 pp since FY22), peer gap (+10 pp to median) and driver (margin, not turnover, with numbers and a business reason).

Checking the pillar against the workbook

For each number in the pillar, record the workbook cell in the Storyline tab (s03). The final review ticks every number: same value, same period, same definition, same rounding. This is the defence against pitfall 3 in topic.md (claims not traceable to the workbook).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Conflicting trend and level. A leader whose metric is deteriorating needs both facts in the pillar ("still leads, but the gap has halved"). Suppressing the trend misleads.
  • Driver uncertainty. If the decomposition does not cleanly explain the gap, say what is known and what is inferred. Equity research uses "we believe" for inferred drivers. Use it sparingly and only with evidence.
  • Definition-sensitive gaps. If the peer gap changes sign under the Ind AS 116 test (topic t2 s06), the pillar must say so or choose a robust metric.
  • Gold-price and currency effects. For jewellers, separate gold inflation from volume. For IT, separate CC growth from FX. Otherwise the driver explanation is wrong.
  • Rounding consistency. "About 24%" in the narrative and 23.6% in the table is fine if the rounding rule is stated. "25%" is not.

Key takeaways

  • Every pillar needs four quantified parts: metric (level), trend (change over the window), peer gap (in the metric's units vs the right comparator) and driver (why, with its own number).
  • Use pp or bps for ratio changes and days for working capital, and convert gaps to ₹ crore where it sharpens the point.
  • Drivers come from decompositions: DuPont, ROIC = margin × turnover, CCC components, price-volume-mix, CC vs FX.
  • Sector KPIs support drivers but do not replace financial evidence.
  • Every number in a pillar must trace to a workbook cell recorded in the Storyline tab.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write a quantified pillar [L2]

Data: Peer A EBITDA margin FY22 25.9%, FY25 27.3%; Peer B 24.0% → 24.8%; Peer C 19.0% → 20.3%. Peer A's offshore effort share is 78% vs 72% (B) and 68% (C), and its subcontractor cost is 6.5% of revenue vs 8.0% and 9.5%. Write a pillar paragraph of at most 70 words containing metric, trend, peer gap and driver.

💡 Hint: Express margin changes in bps and pick one comparator, either the median or the closest rival.

Q2. pp vs % errors [L1]

Correct the errors in this sentence: "Kalyan's EBITDA margin grew 15% from 6.4% to 7.4%, while Titan's ROIC improved by 26% from 19% to 24%, a 5% improvement." Rewrite it correctly in both pp and relative-% terms where appropriate.

Q3. Driver decomposition for ROIC [L2]

Jeweller X: NOPAT ₹1,080 crore, revenue ₹15,900 crore, average invested capital ₹6,600 crore. Jeweller Y: NOPAT ₹3,900 crore, revenue ₹50,000 crore, average invested capital ₹19,500 crore. Decompose each ROIC into NOPAT margin × IC turnover, quantify how much of the ROIC gap comes from each factor (use a sequential substitution approach and state the order), and write the driver sentence.

Q4. Converting a gap to ₹ crore [L2]

Titan's CCC is 150 days and Kalyan's is 205 days. Kalyan's FY25 COGS is ₹21,500 crore and its pre-tax cost of borrowing is 8.8%. Compute the working capital tied up by the gap and its annual pre-tax funding cost. Write the peer-gap clause for a pillar and the action title for the supporting exhibit.

Q5. Audit a pillar [L3]

A draft pillar reads: "Senco is the most efficient operator with the best working capital and strong growth of 26%, driven by its franchise model." The workbook shows Senco's CCC of 195 days (peer median 195), revenue CAGR of 26% (peer range 21–30%), franchise share of 35% (Kalyan 55%) and ROIC of 11% (WACC 13%). Identify every unsupported or misleading element and rewrite the pillar accurately.
