Subtopic note

Action Titles: Headlines That State the Conclusion

Scope: Action titles: each slide/section headline states the conclusion, not the topic

Why it matters

Executives skim. In a 20-slide deck or a 25-page PDF, most readers read only the headlines. If the headlines are topics ("Profitability analysis", "Working capital"), the skimmer learns nothing. If they are action titles ("Titan's 11% EBITDA margin leads peers by 2–4 points, driven by studded mix, not pricing"), the skimmer gets the whole argument from the headlines alone. Consulting firms test decks with the "ghost deck" or "headline read". Equity research reports use bold conclusion sentences at the start of each section. The Saturday assessment asks you to "defend ratio conclusions", and action titles are how each conclusion is stated before it is defended.

Core notes

Topic title vs action title

Topic title (weak) Action title (strong)
Revenue growth Kalyan's 30% revenue CAGR is the fastest among peers, but over half reflects gold-price inflation
ROIC comparison Only Titan and Kalyan earn above their cost of capital; Senco's ROIC (11%) trails its 13% WACC
Working capital Titan's 150-day CCC, 55 days shorter than Kalyan's, frees about ₹6,000 crore of capital
Leverage All three IT majors are net-cash, so balance-sheet risk does not differentiate them
Ind AS 116 impact Lease accounting flatters J2's margin by 3 points; on a pre-116 basis it ranks last

Rules for writing action titles

  1. State the conclusion, not the subject. The title must be a complete sentence with a verb and a claim.
  2. Quantify. Include the key number and, where possible, the peer gap ("…leads by 300 bps").
  3. Add the driver or the "so what" when space allows. "…driven by offshore mix" or "…limiting reinvestment capacity".
  4. One message per slide or section. If the title needs "and" twice, split the slide.
  5. Length: about 1–2 lines (roughly 10–20 words). Longer titles stop being skimmable.
  6. The body must prove the title. Every chart and table on the slide supports the claim (the "so what" test, concept c03). Anything that does not support it moves to another slide or the appendix.
  7. Consistent with the numbers. The figures in the title must match the chart, the table and the workbook cell. Link chart titles to cells where practical (topic t4 s06).

The ghost deck / headline test

Before building charts, write only the titles in sequence, for example in an outline or a sheet called "Storyline". Read them top to bottom. Together they should read like the executive summary. If they do:

  • the storyline is coherent,
  • each slide's purpose is clear, and
  • you know what evidence each slide needs, so you build only charts that prove a title.

If a title cannot be written yet, either the analysis is incomplete or the slide is not needed.

Example storyline (IT peer report)

  1. Peer A is the highest-quality compounder; B offers growth, C needs a turnaround (executive summary)
  2. All three face low single-digit CC demand growth, so margin and capital discipline now drive value (peer context)
  3. Peer A's 55% ROIC is 17 points ahead of B and over twice C's, on a comparable ex-cash basis (pillar 1)
  4. A's margins are the most stable (CV 0.02) because of a flexible pyramid and subcontractor mix (pillar 2)
  5. B converts 81% of EBITDA to cash vs 76% for A; unbilled revenue explains the gap (pillar 3)
  6. No material forensic flags; A's rising unbilled revenue is the one watchpoint (forensic notes)
  7. A large BFSI-spending cut or GenAI pricing deflation above 5% would change the ranking (risks)

Reading only these seven lines gives the full argument.

Action titles in a PDF report

In a written report, the equivalents are section headings plus a bold lead sentence that states the section's conclusion, followed by evidence. Example: "3. Returns — Titan's ROIC spread of 12 points is double Kalyan's and four times its own FY22 level." Table and chart captions follow the same rule: "Exhibit 4: Kalyan's CCC has shortened by 25 days as FOCO stores carry franchisee-funded inventory".

Excel and PowerPoint mechanics

  • Keep a "Storyline" tab in the workbook with section number, action title, the evidence chart or table reference, and the cell links for the numbers in the title. Build titles as formulas so numbers update:
="Titan's ROIC of "&TEXT(ROIC_Titan,"0%")&" exceeds WACC by "&TEXT(ROIC_Titan-WACC_Titan,"0.0%")&", the widest spread in the peer set"
  • Excel chart titles can be linked to a cell (select title, type = then click the cell), so the chart title is the action title.
  • In PowerPoint, use the title placeholder for the action title so the outline view becomes the ghost deck.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Neutral findings. If a slide shows no differentiation, the title says so: "Leverage does not differentiate the IT majors: all hold net cash above 0.5x EBITDA." That is still a conclusion.
  • Contested conclusions. When the result depends on a definition, say so in the title: "On reported Ind AS 116 figures J2 leads on margin; on pre-116 it ranks last."
  • Over-claiming. Action titles tempt causal language ("because of…"). Use causal wording only where the driver analysis (s04) supports it. Otherwise write "coincides with" or "is associated with".
  • Regulatory or client tone. In audit or board documents, avoid promotional language ("dominates", "crushes"). Precise and neutral is still an action title.
  • Appendix slides can carry descriptive titles ("Appendix C: Ratio definitions"). Action titles are for argument slides.

Key takeaways

  • An action title is a full-sentence conclusion with a number, a peer gap and ideally a driver.
  • Reading only the titles in order (the ghost deck) should reproduce the executive summary.
  • Write titles before building charts, and build only the evidence the titles need.
  • The body of each slide or section must prove its title, with numbers that tie to the workbook.
  • Link title numbers to cells so the storyline updates when data changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Convert topic titles [L1]

Rewrite these topic titles as action titles using the data. (a) "Margin trends": Peer A EBITDA margin 26.8% → 27.4% (FY23–25), Peer B 24.3% → 24.8%, Peer C 19.5% → 20.3%. (b) "Cash conversion": CFO/EBITDA A 76%, B 81%, C 79%. (c) "Inventory": DIO Titan 160 days, Kalyan 215 days, Senco 205 days.

Q2. Ghost deck for Project 2 [L2]

Write a seven-title ghost deck for a jewellery peer report using this data: ROIC Titan 24%, Kalyan 14%, Senco 11%; WACC 12–13%; CAGR 21%, 30%, 26%; EBITDA margin 10.5%, 7.4%, 7.0%; CCC 150, 205, 195 days; net debt/EBITDA 0.5x, 1.5x, 0.9x; Kalyan FOCO share 55%; Senco GML-funded inventory 45%. Then identify the chart or table each title needs.

💡 Hint: Write the executive-summary title first, then one title per pillar, forensic notes and risks.

Q3. Dynamic title formula [L2]

Write an Excel formula that produces the action title "Kalyan's CCC is X days longer than Titan's, tying up ₹Y crore of extra working capital". X and Y are computed from cells holding each company's CCC and Kalyan's daily COGS. Format Y with Indian digit grouping (for example 6,000), and explain how you would check that the title matches the chart.

Q4. Critique a slide [L3]

A slide titled "Profitability" shows four charts: EBITDA margin trend for three peers, PAT margin trend, ROE bar chart and a table of other income. Rewrite the title as an action title, decide which charts stay and which move or go (with reasons), and write the replacement title for any chart that moves to its own slide.

Q5. Neutral and contested findings [L2]

Write action titles for (a) a slide showing that all three IT peers have CCC within 75–88 days, and (b) a slide showing that the margin ranking reverses between post-116 and pre-116 definitions (J2 9.0% vs 6.0%; J1 8.5% vs 7.9%; J3 6.5% vs 6.0%). Explain the wording choices.
