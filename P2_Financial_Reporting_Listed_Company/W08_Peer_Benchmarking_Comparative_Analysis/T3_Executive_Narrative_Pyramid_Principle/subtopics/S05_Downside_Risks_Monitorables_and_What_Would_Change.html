Subtopic note

Downside Risks, Monitorables and What Would Change the View

Scope: Downside risks, key monitorables and what would change the conclusion

Why it matters

A conclusion without risks looks like advocacy, not analysis. Every institutional template includes a risk section. Equity research has "key risks to our view". Rating rationales have "rating sensitivities" (factors that could lead to an upgrade or downgrade). Credit appraisal notes have "key risks and mitigants". Board papers have "risks and assumptions". For Project 2 the risk section does three jobs. It shows the reader where the governing thought is fragile. It converts that fragility into monitorables, specific metrics with thresholds to watch each quarter. And it states what would change the conclusion, so the view can be tested against future Reg 33 results. Interview panels routinely ask, "What would make you wrong?" A prepared answer signals judgement.

Core notes

Three linked elements

Element Definition Example (jewellery report concluding "Kalyan is the most improved capital allocator")
Downside risk An event or trend that would weaken the conclusion, with mechanism and rough magnitude Sharp gold-price correction: inventory losses for unhedged stock and lower ticket sizes. A 10% fall could cut EBITDA by about ₹150–250 crore if 30% of inventory is unhedged
Key monitorable An observable metric, its current value and the threshold that signals the risk is materialising FOCO share of new stores (currently 70%); gold-on-lease share of inventory; quarterly SSSG
What would change the conclusion The specific condition under which the governing thought no longer holds "If Kalyan's ROIC falls back below WACC (12.5%) for two consecutive half-years, or net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.0x, the improvement thesis fails"

Identifying risks systematically

Map risks to the pillars. Each pillar's driver is the place where it can fail.

Pillar Driver Risk to the driver
Returns (ROIC) Franchise-funded inventory raises IC turnover Franchise partners' funding dries up; brand must fund inventory
Growth Store additions + gold-price inflation Gold price correction, demand slowdown, competitive store openings
Balance sheet Debt reduction from cash flows and FOCO conversion Working-capital build, capex acceleration, GML cost rise

Then add cross-cutting risks:

  • Regulatory and policy: customs duty on gold (cut from 15% to 6% in July 2024, but could reverse), hallmarking rules, GST rate changes. For IT: US visa policy, tariffs on services, data-localisation rules.
  • Accounting and forensic: Week 7 flags (related-party transactions, unbilled revenue growth, capitalisation). Ind AS 116 definition sensitivity (topic t2 s06).
  • Macro: INR/USD (IT margin sensitivity is often quoted as roughly 20–40 bps of margin per 1% currency move, so use company guidance), interest rates (GML and working-capital cost), consumer demand.
  • Company-specific: promoter pledge, management change, litigation, key-client concentration (top-10 client share for IT).

Quantifying risks

Each material risk should carry at least a rough sensitivity from the workbook:

\[ \Delta \text{EBITDA} \approx \text{Exposure base} \times \text{Shock} \times \text{Pass-through gap} \]

Example: an IT company with USD revenue of USD 19 billion and an EBITDA margin of 24%. A 2% rupee appreciation with a margin sensitivity of 30 bps per 1% gives a margin effect of about −60 bps, which is −₹980 crore on revenue of ₹1,63,000 crore. Put sensitivities in a "Risks" tab so the narrative numbers are traceable.

Rank risks by likelihood × impact and show only the top 3–5 in the executive summary. The full register goes in the appendix.

Writing "what would change our view"

Make it specific, measurable and time-bound:

  • Weak: "Execution risk could affect our view."
  • Strong: "We would revisit the ranking if Peer A's CC revenue growth trails Peer B's by more than 3 points for two consecutive quarters, or if its CFO/EBITDA falls below 70% on an LTM basis."

The rating agencies' sensitivities format is a good template. Upward factors describe what would strengthen the conclusion. Downward factors describe what would weaken or reverse it. Both are quantified.

Monitorable dashboard

Include a small table in the risks section:

Monitorable Current (LTM Sep-25) Threshold Direction to watch Source / frequency
Kalyan ROIC 14.0% < 12.5% (WACC) ↓ Half-yearly BS + Reg 33 quarterly P&L
Kalyan net debt incl. GML / EBITDA 1.5x > 2.0x ↑ Half-yearly
FOCO share of store additions 70% < 50% ↓ Quarterly investor presentation
SSSG (same-store sales growth) 12% < 5% ↓ Quarterly business update
Gold price (₹/10 g) — −15% from current ↓ Daily

Values are illustrative. Use current filings.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Risk inflation. Listing 20 generic risks ("economic slowdown", "competition") dilutes the message. Each listed risk needs a mechanism linked to a pillar.
  • Risks that are already in the numbers. If the FY25 margin already reflects the July 2024 duty-cut inventory loss, the risk is reversal or repetition, not the original event.
  • Symmetric risks. Gold-price moves cut both ways. State the asymmetry (hedged vs unhedged inventory) that makes it a downside for one peer and an upside for another.
  • Forensic flags as risks. An unresolved forensic flag (Week 7) is a risk to the reliability of the numbers themselves. It belongs high in the list, and it may require a caveat in the governing thought.
  • Tone. In board or audit contexts, avoid alarmism, and pair each risk with a mitigant where one exists (hedging policy, contractual pass-through).

Key takeaways

  • Map downside risks to the driver of each pillar, then add regulatory, accounting, macro and company-specific risks.
  • Quantify material risks with workbook-based sensitivities, and rank them by likelihood × impact.
  • Convert risks into monitorables with current values, thresholds, sources and frequencies.
  • State explicitly what would change the conclusion, in measurable, time-bound terms (like rating sensitivities).
  • Keep the top 3–5 risks in the summary and the full register in the appendix.

Practice Questions

Q1. Map risks to pillars [L2]

Governing thought: "Peer A is the highest-quality IT franchise." Pillars: (1) ROIC 55% vs 38% and 21%; (2) margin CV 0.02 vs 0.035 and 0.05; (3) 4 of the top 10 global banks as clients, with BFSI at 38% of revenue. Identify one driver-linked downside risk per pillar, one monitorable for each with a threshold, and one "what would change our view" statement.

💡 Hint: Pillar 3 contains a concentration that is both a strength and a risk.

Q2. Currency sensitivity [L2]

An IT company has revenue of ₹1,62,990 crore (97% in foreign currency), EBITDA margin 24.5%, and guides that a 1% rupee appreciation reduces EBITDA margin by about 30 bps before hedging. 60% of the next 12 months' net exposure is hedged. Estimate the EBITDA impact of a 3% rupee appreciation with and without hedges, and write the risk sentence for the report.

Q3. Rating-style sensitivities [L2]

Write upward and downward "factors that could change our conclusion" (two each, quantified) for a report concluding that "Senco's growth is not yet creating value". Use: ROIC 11%, WACC 13%, CAGR 26%, CCC 195 days, net debt incl. GML/EBITDA 2.1x, GML share of inventory 45%.

Q4. Gold-price shock scenario [L3]

A jeweller holds inventory of ₹18,000 crore, of which 35% is gold funded by gold metal loans (naturally hedged), 15% is hedged by forwards and 50% is unhedged. Gold is 75% of inventory value. Estimate the inventory revaluation loss from a 12% gold price fall, and explain how it flows (or does not) through reported EBITDA and normalised EBITDA. Write the risk paragraph and the monitorable, and state how the peer ranking might change if peers have different hedging levels.

Q5. Interview: "What would make you wrong?" [L3]

For your own Project 2 conclusion, or for "Titan remains the most capital-efficient jeweller" using the data in s04, prepare a 60-second spoken answer naming the two most likely ways the conclusion fails, the monitorables that would reveal it and the threshold at which you would change your view.
