Subtopic note

ROIC vs Revenue Growth Bubble Charts with Median Quadrants

Scope: Scatter/bubble: ROIC vs revenue growth with bubble size = revenue and quadrant lines at peer medians

Why it matters

A capital efficiency matrix ranks peers on several metrics, but readers remember pictures. The ROIC versus revenue growth scatter is the classic "value creation map" used by consulting firms and equity strategists: growth only creates value when returns exceed the cost of capital, and returns only compound when there is growth to reinvest in. Plotting both on one chart, with bubble size showing scale, lets the report say in one exhibit which peer is a compounder, which is a cash cow and which is buying growth at poor returns. It is also the chart most often misread when the quadrant lines are placed carelessly.

Core notes

Axes and encodings

Encoding Metric Typical definition
X-axis Revenue growth 3-year revenue CAGR, \(\left(\frac{\text{Rev}_t}{\text{Rev}_{t-3}}\right)^{1/3} - 1\)
Y-axis ROIC NOPAT ÷ average invested capital (Week 6 definition, applied identically)
Bubble size Scale Latest-year revenue (₹ crore)
Colour Identity Fixed company colour; focal company highlighted, others muted
Quadrant lines Thresholds Peer median growth (vertical) and peer median ROIC (horizontal)

Convention puts growth on the X-axis and returns on the Y-axis, so "up and to the right" is best.

Building the bubble chart in Excel

  1. Prepare a feed block: company name, CAGR, ROIC, revenue. Each row is one bubble.
  2. Insert → Scatter (X, Y) or Bubble Chart → Bubble. Excel creates one series by default; for company-specific colours and legends, add one series per company (Select Data → Add: series name = company cell, X = CAGR cell, Y = ROIC cell, bubble size = revenue cell).
  3. Format Series → Series Options → Size represents: Area of bubbles (the default and the correct choice — perception follows area; "width" exaggerates differences). Use Scale bubble size to (e.g., 50–100%) to stop large bubbles hiding small ones.
  4. Data labels → Label Contains → Value From Cells (Excel 2013+) pointing at the company-name column; untick Y value. Position labels to the right or centre.
  5. Fix axis bounds: X from a round number below the minimum growth to one above the maximum; Y similarly. Unlike columns, scatter axes need not start at zero — position, not length, encodes value — but the bounds must be stated and identical across any comparison charts.
  6. Format percentages as 0% or 0.0% on both axes.

Adding quadrant lines at peer medians

Compute the thresholds in cells:

MedianGrowth = MEDIAN(CAGR_range)
MedianROIC   = MEDIAN(ROIC_range)

Medians are preferred to means because a single outlier (a peer with 60% ROIC from an asset-light model, or a 45% CAGR from a low base) drags the mean and puts most peers on one side.

Two ways to draw the lines:

  • Helper scatter series (most robust): add a series "Vertical" with X values {MedianGrowth, MedianGrowth} and Y values {Ymin, Ymax} (the axis bounds), and a series "Horizontal" with X {Xmin, Xmax} and Y {MedianROIC, MedianROIC}. In a bubble chart, give each helper point a tiny bubble size and show a line; many practitioners instead build the quadrant on a scatter chart overlay. Format as thin grey dashed lines with no markers.
  • Error bars: add one point at (MedianGrowth, MedianROIC), then add X and Y error bars with fixed values large enough to span the plot area, no end caps. Fewer series, but harder to control precisely.

Because the medians sit in cells, the lines move automatically when data change.

Label each quadrant with a small grey text box (or a data label on an invisible point): e.g., top-right "Compounders: above-median growth and returns", top-left "Cash generators", bottom-right "Growth at low returns", bottom-left "Laggards".

Medians versus absolute thresholds

Peer medians make the chart relative: half the peers will always be "below median", even if all are creating value. Consider complementing or replacing the horizontal median line with WACC (the value creation threshold, ROIC − WACC > 0) and the vertical line with nominal GDP growth or sector growth. The choice changes the message, so state it in the subtitle: "Quadrant lines at peer medians (growth 11.2%, ROIC 18.4%)".

Illustration

Illustrative values for five consumer peers:

Peer 3-yr CAGR ROIC FY25 revenue (₹ crore)
A 18% 24% 51,000
B 27% 12% 22,600
C 30% 14% 6,200
D 9% 21% 12,800
E 6% 8% 4,100

Median CAGR = 18%; median ROIC = 14%. Peer A sits exactly on the vertical line (the median observation always lies on its own line), B and C are high growth but at or below median returns, D is a cash generator, E a laggard. With WACC at 12.5%, B's ROIC is below cost of capital: its fast growth is destroying value — a sharper message than "below median".

Small peer sets

With only three peers (the Project 2 minimum), the median is the middle company, which will always sit on both lines and the chart degenerates. Remedies: widen the peer set for this chart only (e.g., add two adjacent peers with a note), use absolute thresholds (WACC, sector growth), or replace the quadrant view with a plain labelled scatter and let the matrix carry the ranking.

Interpreting the chart in the narrative

Each point should support a claim in the report: "Peer A is the only peer combining above-median growth with ROIC 11 percentage points above WACC". If a point does not support a claim, ask whether the chart is needed (the "so what" test from the narrative topic).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative growth or ROIC: bubbles with negative coordinates plot normally; negative bubble sizes (e.g., if size were EBITDA and a peer is loss-making) are hidden unless Show negative bubbles is ticked — better to size by revenue, which is always positive.
  • Inorganic growth: an acquisition-driven CAGR should be flagged (hollow bubble or footnote) or shown on an organic basis.
  • Ind AS 116 effects on ROIC: capitalising leases increases invested capital and NOPAT differently; plot all peers on the same basis.
  • Overlapping labels: move labels manually and lock positions, or use leader lines; check after every data refresh.
  • Time dimension: a single scatter is a snapshot. To show movement, plot two points per company (FY22 and FY25) joined by an arrow — a "connected scatter" — but limit to a few peers.
  • Bubble size scaling: when one peer is ten times larger, scale bubbles down or the small peers vanish; note "bubble area proportional to revenue".

Key takeaways

  • Growth on X, ROIC on Y, bubble area for revenue, fixed company colours.
  • Use one series per company for colour control and Value From Cells for labels.
  • Draw quadrant lines from MEDIAN cells via helper series so they update automatically.
  • Medians make the view relative; WACC as the horizontal line shows value creation in absolute terms.
  • With three peers the median chart degenerates — widen the set or use absolute thresholds.
  • State thresholds, bases and size encoding in the subtitle or footnote.

Practice Questions

Q1. Quadrant thresholds [L1]

Seven peers have 3-year revenue CAGRs of 4%, 9%, 12%, 15%, 16%, 22% and 41% and ROICs of 6%, 31%, 14%, 19%, 11%, 17% and 9% respectively. Compute the median and mean of each metric, explain which you would use for quadrant lines and why, and identify which peers fall in each quadrant under your choice.

Q2. Helper series specification [L2]

Write the exact X and Y values (as cell-driven references in words) for the two helper series needed to draw quadrant lines on a chart whose X-axis runs from 0% to 40% and Y-axis from 0% to 35%, with medians computed in named cells. Explain how you would prevent the helper series from appearing in the legend.

Q3. Median or WACC? [L3]

For an IT services peer set (Infosys, TCS, Wipro, HCLTech, Tech Mahindra), argue whether the horizontal quadrant line should be the peer median ROIC or a WACC of 12%. Explain how the choice changes the governing message, and how you would show both without clutter.

Q4. Three-peer degeneration [L2]

Your Project 2 peer set is Titan, Kalyan Jewellers and Senco Gold. Explain why a median-based quadrant chart is uninformative with three points, and design an alternative exhibit that still conveys growth versus returns, including thresholds and labelling.
