Subtopic note

Chart Hygiene for Peer Financial Reports

Scope: Chart hygiene: consistent company colours, direct labels, units (₹ crore, %), source notes, no 3D/gridline clutter

Why it matters

Research heads, credit committees and CFOs judge an analyst's rigour within seconds of opening a report, and charts are what they see first. A chart with mismatched colours, a legend the reader must decode, no units and no source looks unchecked — and an unchecked-looking chart makes the reader distrust the numbers behind it. Hygiene is not decoration: each rule below removes a specific opportunity for misreading. In Project 2, where the reader compares three peers across a dozen exhibits, consistency across charts matters as much as the quality of any one chart.

Core notes

Consistent company colours

Assign every company one colour once, in a colour key on the cover or definitions sheet, and use it in every chart, table highlight and slide.

  • Focal-company highlighting: if the report is about one company versus peers, give the focal company a strong colour and peers shades of grey. If all peers are equal subjects, use three distinct, colour-blind safe hues (e.g., dark blue, orange, teal) with similar visual weight.
  • Years or scenarios within a company: use tints of the company colour (light for FY23, dark for FY25), following IBCS practice of darker = more recent/actual.
  • Semantic colours are reserved: green/red (or blue/orange for colour-blind safety) only for good/bad variances, never as company colours.
  • Implement through a custom theme (Page Layout → Colors → Customize Colors) so Accent 1–3 are the company colours, and a saved chart template (.crtx). Never rely on Excel's default series colour order, which changes when series are reordered or one company is filtered out.

Direct labels instead of legends

A legend forces the eye to travel between the key and the data. Prefer:

  • Series names placed at the end of each line (a data label on the last point showing Series Name).
  • Company names inside or beside bars, or as category labels.
  • Data labels on bars showing the value, after which the value axis and gridlines can be deleted.

Keep labels sparse: label endpoints and the points the narrative discusses, not every point on a 12-quarter line.

Units and number formats

Every chart states its unit, once, in the subtitle or axis title: "₹ crore", "%", "days", "x". Conventions for Project 2:

Measure Unit Excel format code Example
Revenue, EBITDA, net debt ₹ crore #,##0 or #,##0.0 22,600
Margins, ROIC, growth % 0.0% 14.2%
Change in margin bps #,##0" bps" (on a value already ×10,000) 180 bps
CCC, DSO, DIO days 0" days" or 0 with unit in title 74
Leverage, multiples x 0.0"x" 1.8x
Negatives parentheses #,##0;(#,##0) (27)

Indian lakh-crore grouping (1,23,456) is common in Indian annual reports; Excel needs a custom format or the Windows regional setting to show it. Whichever grouping you pick, use it everywhere in the report. Never mix ₹ crore and ₹ million, or crore and lakh, across exhibits; convert in the standardized statements, not in the chart.

Source notes

Every chart carries a source line in small grey text below the plot area, e.g.:

Source: Company annual reports FY23–FY25 (consolidated, Ind AS); Q4 FY25 Reg 33 results; analyst calculations. EBITDA excludes other income; Ind AS 116 basis. FY ends 31 March.

The source line should name the documents, the basis (consolidated/standalone, framework, normalized or reported) and any definitional choice that a reader might otherwise question. Build it as a text box linked to a cell so it updates with the basis selector.

Removing clutter

Tufte's data-ink principle: erase ink that does not show data. Practical deletions:

  • No 3D — 3D columns and pies distort length and angle perception and hide values behind each other.
  • Gridlines: delete or lighten to very pale grey; if bars are directly labelled, no gridlines are needed.
  • Borders around the chart area and plot area: remove.
  • Axis lines: keep the category axis baseline (zero line) in a darker grey; remove the value axis line.
  • Shadows, gradients, glow, bevel: never.
  • Legends: remove when direct labels are used.
  • Decimal clutter: round to the precision the reader needs (₹ crore with no decimals for large companies).
  • Pie charts: avoid for peer comparisons; a sorted bar chart is easier to compare.

Titles and layout

The chart title is an action title that states the conclusion ("Titan's ROIC lead widened to 9 pp in FY25"); a smaller subtitle gives the metric, unit and period ("ROIC, %, FY23–FY25"). Align charts on a grid, use identical sizes for charts of the same type (Format → Size: exact height and width), and use one font family throughout.

Accessibility

Add alt text (right-click → Edit Alt Text) summarising the message; ensure contrast is sufficient in a greyscale printout; never rely on colour alone to distinguish series — combine colour with labels or marker shapes.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Brand colours: using each company's corporate colour is tempting but often produces clashing or near-identical hues (several Indian IT firms use blue). Readability beats brand fidelity.
  • Many years of quarterly data: direct labels on every point clutter; label only first, last, max and min, and keep a light axis.
  • Unit choice for very different scales: TCS-scale revenue in ₹ crore with no decimals and a small-cap peer in the same chart may need indexed values instead.
  • Rounding mismatch: labels rounded to whole crore may not visibly sum to a rounded total; show one decimal in bridges or footnote "figures may not add due to rounding".
  • Printing: a colour palette that works on screen may be indistinguishable in black-and-white print; test-print or preview in greyscale.

Key takeaways

  • Fix one colour per company in a theme and template; reserve red/green for variances.
  • Direct labels beat legends; once bars are labelled, remove the value axis and gridlines.
  • Every chart states unit, period and basis; every chart has a source line.
  • No 3D, no shadows, minimal gridlines — maximise data ink.
  • Use action titles plus a descriptive subtitle; size and align charts consistently.
  • Consistency across all exhibits matters more than any single chart's styling.

Practice Questions

Q1. Chart audit [L1]

A draft exhibit shows a 3D clustered column chart of FY23–FY25 revenue for three IT peers, default Office colours, a legend at the bottom, major and minor gridlines, value axis labelled "Rs." with values like "245,315.00", and no source. List every hygiene fix in the order you would apply them.

Q2. Colour key design [L2]

Design the colour key for a Project 2 report on Titan, Kalyan Jewellers and Senco Gold that includes peer comparison charts, per-company year trends, budget-versus-actual style variance bars and a colour-blind-safe requirement. Specify how you would implement it in Excel so it cannot drift.

Q3. Writing source notes [L2]

Write source lines for (a) an ROIC chart using consolidated Ind AS figures with leases capitalised and goodwill included in invested capital; (b) a quarterly revenue chart combining Reg 33 results for Q1–Q3 and audited annual results for Q4; (c) a chart showing Infosys revenue derived from the IFRS 20-F converted at an average rate.

Q4. Units from a raw XBRL extract [L2]

Your raw extract holds values in absolute rupees (e.g., 2,26,00,00,00,000). Excel's trailing-comma format scaling divides by 1,000 per comma. Explain whether you can display these values in ₹ crore purely through a number format, what goes wrong if you try, and where in the workbook the unit conversion should happen instead. Then write a format that shows ₹ million from the same raw values with negatives in parentheses.

💡 Hint: Check whether one crore is a power of 1,000.
