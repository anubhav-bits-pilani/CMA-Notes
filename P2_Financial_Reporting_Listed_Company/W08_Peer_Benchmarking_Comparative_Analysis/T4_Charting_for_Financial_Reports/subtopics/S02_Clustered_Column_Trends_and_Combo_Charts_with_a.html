Subtopic note

Clustered Column Trends and Combo Charts with a Margin Axis

Scope: Clustered column trend charts; combo charts with margin % on secondary axis

Why it matters

Most of Project 2's quantitative story is a comparison across peers and across years: revenue, EBITDA, CFO, ROIC. The clustered column chart is the workhorse for showing absolute values side by side, and the combo chart (columns for ₹ crore, a line for margin %) is the standard equity-research exhibit for "revenue grew, but did profitability keep up?". Both are easy to build and very easy to build badly — especially the secondary axis, which can make a 50 bps margin wobble look like a collapse. Reviewers in research houses and FP&A teams check axis scaling before they read anything else.

Core notes

Clustered column: what to cluster

A clustered column chart places several series next to each other within each category. The key design decision is which dimension sits on the category axis and which becomes the series, because the eye compares adjacent bars most easily.

Layout Category axis Series Comparison the eye makes
Peer-first Company FY23, FY24, FY25 Each company's own trend
Year-first FY23, FY24, FY25 Company Ranking of peers in each year

For a "who grew fastest" message, use peer-first with years in a sequential shade of each company's colour (light to dark). For "who is largest each year", use year-first with fixed company colours. Excel's Switch Row/Column (Chart Design tab) flips between them without rebuilding.

Rules for columns:

  • Columns encode value by length, so the value axis must start at zero. A truncated column axis is a misrepresentation, not a style choice.
  • Gap width around 50–80% and series overlap 0% to −10% keep clusters readable.
  • Three peers × three years = nine bars is the practical maximum; more than that, use small multiples (one mini-chart per company with identical axes) or switch to a line chart.
  • Label the top of each bar (data labels, number format #,##0) and delete the value axis and gridlines once labelled — the labels carry the data.
  • Time runs left to right, oldest first (IBCS convention); fiscal years labelled FY23, FY24, FY25 with a footnote that FY ends 31 March, or calendarized years labelled CY with the method stated.

Combo chart: columns plus margin line

A combo chart combines two chart types; the common form is a clustered column for revenue or EBITDA (₹ crore) with a line with markers for EBITDA margin % plotted on a secondary axis.

Building it (Excel 2013 and later):

  1. Select the data block (years in the first row, revenue and EBITDA margin rows below).
  2. Insert → Recommended Charts → All Charts → Combo, or Insert → Combo → Clustered Column – Line on Secondary Axis.
  3. In the combo dialog, set revenue/EBITDA to Clustered Column (primary) and margin to Line with Markers with Secondary Axis ticked.
  4. Format both axes with fixed bounds (Format Axis → Axis Options → Bounds: Minimum, Maximum, and Major unit). Auto bounds silently change when data refresh, which breaks comparability across peer charts.

Scaling the secondary axis honestly

The secondary axis is where most distortion happens. Principles:

  • Start the margin axis at 0% unless there is a strong reason not to, and then say so. A margin moving from 20.0% to 21.5% plotted on an axis of 19.5%–22.0% looks like a 60% increase.
  • Align gridlines: choose bounds so both axes have the same number of major units. E.g., primary ₹0–₹8,000 crore with major unit ₹2,000 (4 intervals) and secondary 0%–40% with major unit 10% (4 intervals). Misaligned gridlines invite the reader to read margin values off the revenue grid.
  • Use the same bounds across all peer combo charts in the report so the reader can compare charts across pages. Set bounds from the peer maximum, not each company's own maximum.
  • Label the axes with units: "₹ crore" on the left, "EBITDA margin (%)" on the right, and colour each axis label to match its series.
  • Consider not using a secondary axis at all: put margin % as data labels above columns, or stack two panels (columns above, margin line below) sharing the time axis. Many style guides (including IBCS) discourage dual axes because they imply a relationship whose visual strength depends on arbitrary scaling.

Illustration

Illustrative figures (₹ crore) for one jewellery peer:

FY23 FY24 FY25
Revenue 14,000 18,500 22,600
EBITDA 1,050 1,300 1,480
EBITDA margin 7.5% 7.0% 6.5%

A combo chart with revenue columns on ₹0–₹25,000 crore and margin on 0%–10% shows the essential message: scale is growing 27% a year while margin is drifting down 50 bps a year (consistent with a shift toward franchise-owned stores, where the franchisee earns part of the margin, or toward plain gold). If the secondary axis were auto-scaled to 6.4%–7.6%, the same 100 bps decline would appear as a plunge from the top to the bottom of the chart.

Combo charts for three peers

With three companies, a combo chart becomes six series. Options, in order of preference:

  1. Small multiples: one combo chart per company, identical axes, arranged in a row.
  2. Two separate charts: clustered revenue columns for all peers; a separate line chart of margins for all peers on a 0-based axis.
  3. A single chart with columns per company (company colours) and margin lines in darker tints of the same colours — only if the message is simple.

Excel implementation notes

  • Keep chart source data in a dedicated chart-feed block on the charts sheet, linked to the ratio engine with green cross-sheet links; never type values into a chart's series.
  • Use Excel Tables for the feed block so new years extend the chart automatically.
  • Number formats for labels: #,##0 for ₹ crore, 0.0% for margins.
  • Save one finished combo chart as a template (right-click → Save as Template, .crtx) so every peer chart has identical fonts, colours and axis formatting.
  • To fix axis bounds dynamically, Excel has no native cell link for axis bounds; either set them manually from a documented peer maximum or use a small VBA routine. Document whichever you choose.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative values (losses, negative CFO): columns below zero are fine on a zero-based axis; a margin axis must then extend below 0% too, and gridline alignment becomes harder — prefer separate panels.
  • Very different scales (TCS revenue several times that of a mid-cap peer) can make the small peer's bars invisible. Show growth indices (FY23 = 100) or use small multiples with independent but clearly labelled axes — and say they are independent.
  • Calendarized vs fiscal years must not mix in one chart.
  • Restated comparatives: if a peer restated FY24 (e.g., discontinued operations), the chart must use the restated figure and footnote it.
  • A margin line on a secondary axis invites readers to infer correlation between bar height and line level; if the point is only the margin trend, a simple line chart is cleaner.

Key takeaways

  • Choose the category axis based on the comparison you want the eye to make; Switch Row/Column flips it.
  • Column axes must start at zero; truncating them misrepresents magnitude.
  • Combo charts need fixed, aligned, labelled axis bounds, identical across all peer charts.
  • Start margin axes at 0% or disclose otherwise; auto-scaling exaggerates small changes.
  • For more than three series, use small multiples rather than one crowded combo chart.
  • Feed every chart from a linked block, formatted with consistent number formats and a saved template.

Practice Questions

Q1. Fixing a misleading combo chart [L1]

A draft chart shows revenue columns on an auto-scaled axis from ₹9,800 to ₹12,400 crore and EBITDA margin on a secondary axis from 18.2% to 19.4%. List every change you would make before the chart goes into the report and explain why each matters.

Q2. Aligned gridlines [L2]

Peer revenues range up to ₹2,41,000 crore and EBITDA margins range from 16.8% to 27.5%. Choose primary and secondary axis bounds and major units that start at zero, align gridlines and apply to all three peer combo charts. Justify your choice.

💡 Hint: Pick a number of intervals first, then round each axis maximum up to a multiple of that interval count.

Q3. Build specification for small multiples [L3]

Write a build specification (data layout, named ranges or Table names, chart type, axis settings, labels, colour rules) for a row of three combo charts showing FY23–FY25 revenue and EBITDA margin for Infosys, TCS and Wipro, such that adding FY26 later requires no manual chart edits.

Q4. Choosing the layout [L2]

For each message below, state whether you would use a peer-first clustered column, a year-first clustered column, a combo chart, or a different chart, and why: (a) "Kalyan grew revenue fastest every year"; (b) "Titan remains the largest by a wide margin"; (c) "Senco's growth came with falling margins".
