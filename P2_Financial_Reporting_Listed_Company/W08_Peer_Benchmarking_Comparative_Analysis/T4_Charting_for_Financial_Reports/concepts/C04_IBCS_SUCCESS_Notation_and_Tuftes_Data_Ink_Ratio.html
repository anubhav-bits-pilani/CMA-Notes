Key concept

IBCS SUCCESS Notation and Tufte's Data-Ink Ratio

Concept: Design principles: IBCS (SUCCESS) notation and Tufte data-ink ratio

Statement

IBCS (International Business Communication Standards) organises report and chart design under the acronym SUCCESS:

Letter Rule set Meaning in practice
S Say Convey a message: each chart has a stated conclusion (action title)
U Unify Apply semantic notation: the same thing always looks the same
C Condense Increase information density: small multiples, more data per page where useful
C Check Ensure visual integrity: no truncated or manipulated axes, consistent scales
E Express Choose proper visualisation: right chart type for the comparison
S Simplify Avoid clutter: remove decoration, redundancy, 3D, heavy gridlines
S Structure Organise content: logical, MECE grouping and ordering

Tufte's data-ink ratio:

\[ \text{Data-ink ratio} = \frac{\text{Data-ink}}{\text{Total ink used to print the graphic}} \]

where data-ink is the non-erasable core of the graphic — the ink that, if removed, would lose data information — and total ink includes frames, gridlines, backgrounds, labels and decoration. The principle is to maximise the ratio within reason: erase non-data ink and redundant data-ink.

Explanation

IBCS semantic notation (Unify) is the part most relevant to financial reports:

  • Scenarios: actuals (AC) solid dark fill; previous year (PY) grey; plan/budget (PL) outlined (white fill, dark border); forecast (FC) hatched.
  • Time runs horizontally (columns or lines, left to right); structure (companies, segments, line items) runs vertically (horizontal bars). A peer ranking of ROIC is therefore a horizontal bar chart.
  • Variances: good variances green, bad variances red (with colour-blind safe alternatives); absolute variances as bars, relative variances (%) as "pins".
  • Highlighting: one emphasis colour for the point of the message, everything else neutral.
  • Units and scaling indicators: identical scales across comparable charts, or explicit scaling markers when they differ.

Check formalises chart integrity: column axes start at zero, scales are identical when charts are compared, and visual size matches data size. Tufte expresses the same idea as the lie factor:

\[ \text{Lie factor} = \frac{\text{Size of effect shown in graphic}}{\text{Size of effect in data}} \]

which should be close to 1. A margin rising from 20% to 22% (10% relative change) drawn on an axis from 19% to 23% appears as a bar three times taller (the visible height goes from 1 to 3 units, a 200% change): lie factor ≈ 20.

Data-ink in practice: remove chart borders, background fills, heavy gridlines, redundant axis labels when bars are directly labelled, legends when series are labelled, 3D effects and drop shadows ("chartjunk"). Tufte also advocates small multiples — the same chart repeated for each company with identical scales — which matches IBCS Condense.

Limits: maximising data-ink should not strip context. A source note, unit and baseline are non-data ink but essential. IBCS is a voluntary standard; many Indian brokers and corporates use house styles, but the principles (message, integrity, consistency) are universal.

Illustration

A draft Project 2 exhibit: a 3D pie of FY25 revenue share for three jewellers, rainbow colours, legend, title "Revenue". Applying SUCCESS and data-ink:

  • Say: title becomes "Titan earns over half of peer-set revenue".
  • Express: replace the pie with a sorted horizontal bar chart (structure → vertical axis).
  • Unify: company colours from the report key; FY24 shown as grey PY markers.
  • Check/Simplify: no 3D, no legend, direct value labels in ₹ crore, axis removed.
  • Add source line and unit.

The rebuilt chart uses perhaps a third of the ink and answers the question at a glance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply SUCCESS to an exhibit [L2]

A CCC chart shows FY23–FY25 days for three peers as a stacked 3D column chart with a gradient background, budget values shown as solid bars in the same colour as actuals, and the title "Working capital". Rewrite the design specification rule by rule under the seven SUCCESS headings.

Q2. Lie factor [L1]

EBITDA margin moved from 15.0% to 16.5%. A column chart's value axis runs from 14.0% to 17.0%. Compute the lie factor of the visual change and state what axis setting would bring it to 1.

💡 Hint: Compare relative change in visible bar height with relative change in the value.

Q3. Interview: data-ink trade-off [L3]

An interviewer argues that removing gridlines and axes makes charts harder for senior readers who want to read exact values. Argue both sides and state the design you would actually ship in a Project 2 PDF report.
