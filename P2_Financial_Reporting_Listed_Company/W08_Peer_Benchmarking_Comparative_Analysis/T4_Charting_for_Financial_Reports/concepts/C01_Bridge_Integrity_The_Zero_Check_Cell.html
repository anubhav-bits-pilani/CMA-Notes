Key concept

Bridge Integrity: The Zero-Check Cell

Concept: Bridge integrity: Start EBITDA + Σ driver effects = End EBITDA (zero-check cell)

Statement

For any bridge (waterfall) from a starting value to an ending value:

\[ \text{Check} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{start}} + \sum_{k=1}^{n} D_k - \text{EBITDA}_{\text{end}} = 0 \]

where \(\text{EBITDA}_{\text{start}}\) is the prior-period EBITDA, \(D_k\) is the signed effect on EBITDA of driver \(k\) (positive if it increases EBITDA, negative if it reduces it), \(n\) is the number of driver bars, and \(\text{EBITDA}_{\text{end}}\) is the current-period EBITDA. Both endpoints must be linked to the same EBITDA definition used elsewhere in the report (reported or normalized), not typed. The check is held in a dedicated cell, rounded to a tolerance, and rolled into the workbook's master check summary.

Explanation

The identity is trivially true if one driver is computed as a balancing figure — which is exactly why balancing figures are dangerous. A bridge whose last bar is "Others = End − Start − Σ other drivers" will always pass, even if the endpoints are wrong or a driver is double-counted. Robust practice is therefore:

  1. Compute every driver independently from source data (price, volume, cost line changes).
  2. Link the endpoints to the ratio engine's EBITDA for each year.
  3. Calculate the check; any non-zero value signals an error in mapping, sign or definition.
  4. If an "Others" bar is used, show it explicitly, cap its acceptable size (e.g., under 10% of the total change) and explain what it contains.

Typical causes of a non-zero check:

  • Endpoints on different bases (normalized start, reported end; pre- vs post-Ind AS 116).
  • A cost line omitted (e.g., "changes in inventories of finished goods" left out of raw material).
  • Other operating revenue or other income treated inconsistently between endpoints and drivers.
  • Sign errors (a cost increase entered as a positive driver).
  • Rounding: drivers computed from rounded ₹ crore figures may differ from the end total by a few tenths.

Tolerance should reflect rounding of the source: Schedule III permits companies with turnover of ₹100 crore or more to round to the nearest lakh, million or crore (or decimals thereof), so if inputs are in ₹ crore with two decimals, a tolerance of ±0.05 crore per bridge is reasonable; anything larger is an error, not rounding.

In Excel:

BridgeCheck = ROUND(StartEBITDA + SUM(Drivers) - EndEBITDA, 2)
CheckFlag   = IF(ABS(BridgeCheck) <= Tolerance, 0, 1)

Conditional-format the check cell (green 0, red otherwise) and add CheckFlag to the master check.

Illustration

A bridge for a jewellery peer (₹ crore): FY24 normalized EBITDA 1,300; drivers: revenue growth at prior gross margin +410, gross margin % change −190, employee cost −45, other opex −60, advertising −20. Sum of drivers = +95, implying end EBITDA 1,395. The ratio engine shows FY25 normalized EBITDA of 1,480. Check = 1,300 + 95 − 1,480 = −85. Investigation shows the FY25 endpoint was pulled from the reported row, which includes ₹85 crore of gold-metal-loan hedge gains that normalization removes — the endpoint, not the drivers, was wrong.

Practice Questions

Q1. Locate the break [L2]

An EBITDA bridge for an FMCG peer shows: FY24 EBITDA ₹2,140 crore; volume +260; price +185; raw material −310; employee cost −72; other opex −48; FY25 EBITDA ₹2,190 crore. Compute the check value, list three plausible causes of the difference in order of likelihood, and describe how you would test each.

💡 Hint: Compare the size of the gap with individual cost lines that are often omitted or reclassified.

Q2. Balancing-figure policy [L3]

Draft a short workbook policy on when an "Others" bar is allowed in a bridge, the maximum acceptable size, how it must be documented, and how the zero-check should be designed so a balancing figure cannot mask an endpoint error.

Q3. Tolerance setting [L1]

Your standardized statements are in ₹ crore to two decimals, converted from annual reports presented in ₹ lakh. Propose a tolerance for bridge checks and justify it.
