Key concept

Price and Volume Effects in a Revenue or EBITDA Bridge

Concept: Price effect = (Price_t − Price_t−1) × Volume_t; Volume effect = (Volume_t − Volume_t−1) × Price_t−1

Statement

\[ \text{Price effect} = (P_t - P_{t-1}) \times V_t \]

\[ \text{Volume effect} = (V_t - V_{t-1}) \times P_{t-1} \]

where \(P_t\) and \(P_{t-1}\) are the average realised prices per unit in the current and prior period, and \(V_t\) and \(V_{t-1}\) are units sold in the current and prior period. Their sum equals the revenue change exactly:

\[ (P_t - P_{t-1})V_t + (V_t - V_{t-1})P_{t-1} = P_tV_t - P_{t-1}V_{t-1} = \Delta\text{Revenue} \]

Explanation

Why this pairing. Any two-factor decomposition of a product \(P \times V\) has an interaction term \(\Delta P \times \Delta V\). Valuing price at current volume and volume at prior price allocates the interaction entirely to price, so no residual remains. This is the most common convention in management accounts and equity research and mirrors the standard-costing convention where price variances are measured on actual quantities.

Alternatives and their effects:

Convention Price effect Volume effect Residual
Price at \(V_t\), volume at \(P_{t-1}\) (this concept) \(\Delta P \times V_t\) \(\Delta V \times P_{t-1}\) None
Price at \(V_{t-1}\), volume at \(P_t\) \(\Delta P \times V_{t-1}\) \(\Delta V \times P_t\) None (interaction in volume)
Both at prior values \(\Delta P \times V_{t-1}\) \(\Delta V \times P_{t-1}\) \(\Delta P \times \Delta V\) shown separately

When price and volume move a lot (e.g., 15% price hikes with 20% volume growth), the choice moves several crore between bars. State the convention in the footnote and use the same one for every peer and year.

Extending to EBITDA. In an EBITDA bridge the price effect flows through 100% (assuming no price-linked costs such as royalty or commission), but volume should be valued at prior unit contribution rather than prior price: \((V_t - V_{t-1}) \times (P_{t-1} - c_{t-1})\), where \(c\) is variable cost per unit. Unit cost inflation is then a separate bar, \(-(c_t - c_{t-1}) \times V_t\).

Multi-product businesses. Apply price effects product by product, then split the volume effect into pure volume at prior average price and mix (see the EBITDA bridge subtopic).

Currency as price. For Indian IT exporters, revenue in rupees = revenue in USD × exchange rate. The same structure separates constant-currency growth (a volume/mix-like effect, at the prior rate) from the currency effect (rate change × current-period USD revenue).

Illustration

A two-wheeler maker: FY24 4.0 lakh units at ₹85,000; FY25 4.4 lakh units at ₹88,000.

  • Price effect = ₹3,000 × 4.4 lakh = ₹132 crore.
  • Volume effect = 0.4 lakh × ₹85,000 = ₹340 crore.
  • Revenue change = ₹3,872 crore − ₹3,400 crore = ₹472 crore = 132 + 340. ✓

Under the "both at prior values" convention, price = ₹120 crore, volume = ₹340 crore and the interaction ₹12 crore would be shown separately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and compare conventions [L2]

A tractor maker sold 1,20,000 units at ₹6.8 lakh in FY24 and 1,05,000 units at ₹7.3 lakh in FY25. Compute price and volume effects under all three conventions in the table and explain why the price effect differs.

Q2. Constant-currency split [L2]

An IT services company earned USD 2,050 million in FY24 at an average ₹82.8 per USD and USD 2,140 million in FY25 at ₹84.6 per USD. Decompose the rupee revenue change into constant-currency growth and currency effect using the convention in this concept, in ₹ crore.

💡 Hint: Treat the exchange rate as the "price" and USD revenue as the "volume".

Q3. EBITDA volume valuation [L1]

Explain, with a short numerical example of your own, why valuing the volume effect at prior price rather than prior unit contribution overstates the volume bar in an EBITDA bridge, and which bar absorbs the offset.
