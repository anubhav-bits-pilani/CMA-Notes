Key concept

Gross Margin Effect: Isolating Raw Material Inflation

Concept: Gross margin effect = ΔGross margin % × Revenue_t; isolates raw material inflation in the bridge

Statement

When unit prices and volumes are not disclosed, the gross profit change is split into a revenue-growth effect and a gross margin effect:

\[ \Delta \text{GP} = \underbrace{(\text{Rev}_t - \text{Rev}_{t-1}) \times m_{t-1}}_{\text{revenue growth at prior margin}} + \underbrace{(m_t - m_{t-1}) \times \text{Rev}_t}_{\text{gross margin effect}} \]

where \(\text{Rev}_t\) is revenue from operations in period \(t\), \(m_t = \frac{\text{GP}_t}{\text{Rev}_t}\) is the gross margin percentage, and \(\text{GP}_t = \text{Rev}_t - \text{Material cost}_t\) with material cost = cost of materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories of finished goods, WIP and stock-in-trade (the Schedule III Division II face lines). The two terms sum exactly to \(\Delta\text{GP}\).

Explanation

Why it isolates raw material. Revenue growth valued at the prior gross margin answers "what would gross profit have been if the business grew but kept last year's material-cost ratio?". Whatever remains is attributable to the change in the material cost ratio — driven by raw material inflation or deflation net of price pass-through, and by product mix. In commodity-linked sectors (jewellery, paints, FMCG, cement, auto), this is the bar investors most want to see.

What it does not separate. The gross margin effect blends three things: input cost inflation, pricing actions (price increases raise margin %), and mix shifts. A pure raw-material bar needs unit data; without it, label the bar honestly ("gross margin change: RM inflation net of pricing and mix") and use management commentary or investor-presentation KPIs (gold price, crude-linked input index, studded share) to explain it.

Operating-cost lines. After gross profit, the EBITDA bridge continues with employee cost and other expenses either in absolute change (\(-\Delta\text{Cost}\)) or as an operating-leverage split: cost growth at the revenue growth rate versus cost growth above or below it. Choose one approach and apply it to all peers.

Exceptions and caveats

  • Jewellery: a gold price rise lifts revenue and material cost proportionally, so margin % may fall even with stable rupee gross profit per gram; gains on gold metal loans or hedges may sit in other income or in cost of goods — align treatment across Titan, Kalyan and Senco before computing \(m\).
  • IT services: there is no meaningful material cost; the equivalent is the employee-cost ratio (\(\Delta\) employee cost % × Revenue\(_t\)).
  • Inventory valuation: FIFO versus weighted average (Ind AS 2) shifts the timing of input-cost changes into gross margin; peers on different formulas will show different lag patterns.
  • Changes in inventories must be included in material cost, or gross margin is overstated in a stock-build year.

Illustration

Paints peer (₹ crore): FY24 revenue 10,000, material cost 5,900 (GM 41.0%); FY25 revenue 10,800, material cost 6,480 (GM 40.0%).

  • Revenue growth at prior margin = 800 × 41.0% = +328.
  • Gross margin effect = (40.0% − 41.0%) × 10,800 = −108.
  • Total = +220 = GP change (4,320 − 4,100). ✓

The bridge shows growth adding ₹328 crore, of which ₹108 crore was given back to input-cost inflation not fully passed through.

Practice Questions

Q1. Gross margin bridge [L2]

An FMCG peer reported FY24 revenue ₹15,600 crore, cost of materials consumed ₹7,200 crore, purchases of stock-in-trade ₹1,150 crore and change in inventories −₹90 crore (a stock build); FY25 revenue ₹16,900 crore, materials ₹8,050 crore, purchases ₹1,240 crore and change in inventories +₹60 crore. Compute gross margins, the revenue-growth-at-prior-margin effect and the gross margin effect, and verify they sum to the gross profit change.

💡 Hint: Confirm the sign convention of "changes in inventories" on the Schedule III face before adding it to material cost.

Q2. Interpreting a jewellery margin bar [L3]

Gold prices rose 22% in FY25 and a jewellery peer's gross margin fell 60 bps while gross profit per gram sold was unchanged. Explain what the gross margin effect bar would show, why it may mislead, and what alternative bar design you would use in the report.

Q3. Sector adaptation [L1]

Explain how you would adapt the gross margin effect for an IT services peer that has no material cost, and write the formula for the equivalent bar.
