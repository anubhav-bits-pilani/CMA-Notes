Roadmap topic: Charting for Financial Reports: Building clean waterfall charts (EBITDA bridge), clustered column trend charts, and scatter plots (ROIC vs Revenue Growth)

Overview

Charts are where a comparative financial analysis report wins or loses its reader. The analysis in Project 2 — standardized statements, a ratio engine, a capital efficiency matrix, forensic flags — is only persuasive if three or four exhibits make the governing conclusion obvious. This topic covers the three chart families that carry almost every equity research note, credit memo and FP&A pack: the waterfall (EBITDA bridge and other walks), the clustered column and combo chart (trends in ₹ crore with margins), and the scatter/bubble chart (ROIC versus growth, the value-creation map). Around them sit the disciplines that make charts trustworthy: bridge integrity, honest axes, consistent colours and units, source notes and cell-linked text that updates with the model.

For the target roles the skill is practical and tested. An FP&A analyst rebuilds the EBITDA bridge every month; an equity research associate builds price-volume-mix splits for every results note; a transaction services or valuation analyst shows a net-debt walk in every report. Interviewers frequently ask candidates to build a waterfall from raw data, to explain why a dual-axis chart misleads, or to critique a cluttered exhibit. Because the CMA background already covers variance analysis, the new element here is translating variance algebra into correctly reconciled, cleanly designed visuals.

The learning sequence runs from analysis to visual to production. Start with the EBITDA bridge (subtopic 1) and its algebra — price and volume effects, the gross margin effect when unit data are missing, and the zero-check that proves the bridge reconciles. Then learn the two ways to draw it in Excel: the native waterfall and the stacked-column workaround (subtopic 4). Next, the trend and combo charts (subtopic 2) and the bubble chart with median quadrants (subtopic 3) cover peer comparison across time and across two metrics at once. Finally, chart hygiene (subtopic 5), anchored in IBCS SUCCESS and Tufte's data-ink ratio, and cell-linked titles and labels (subtopic 6) turn individual charts into a consistent, self-updating set ready for the Project 2 PDF.

Related concepts to study

Price-volume-mix variance analysis (Week 19)

Price-volume-mix variance analysis → Week 19

The EBITDA bridge in this topic is the year-on-year version of the budget variance analysis covered in Week 19. There, actuals are compared with budget or forecast, and the same algebra splits sales variances into price, volume (quantity) and mix, and cost variances into rate and efficiency components — familiar from CMA standard costing. The difference is presentation and data: FP&A works with internal unit-level data by product, channel and region, so mix effects can be computed precisely, whereas peer analysis relies on disclosed totals and proxies. Learn the conventions here (price at current volume, volume at prior price) so that the Week 19 budget bridges use consistent definitions and the same zero-check discipline.

Power BI waterfall, scatter and small multiples (Weeks 11–12)

Power BI waterfall, scatter and small multiples → Weeks 11–12

Power BI has a native waterfall visual (with a "breakdown" field that can generate driver bars automatically), a scatter chart that supports bubble size, play axis and constant/average lines (via the Analytics pane), and a small-multiples option on many visuals. The design principles from this topic carry over unchanged — fixed company colours, honest axes, direct labels and action titles — but implementation moves from cell formulas to DAX measures: a bridge needs measures for each driver and a disconnected table of bar labels, and quadrant lines come from MEDIAN-based measures. Building the Excel versions first makes the DAX logic much easier to verify in Weeks 11–12.

Normalized EBITDA as bridge endpoints (Week 5)

Normalized EBITDA as bridge endpoints → Week 5 (w5_t2)

Week 5 defined normalized EBITDA: reported EBITDA adjusted for one-offs (impairments, restructuring, litigation settlements, inventory write-downs, exceptional items), with consistent treatment of other income, Ind AS 116 lease costs and ESOP expense. The bridge endpoints in Project 2 should normally be normalized EBITDA, so that driver bars explain the recurring business rather than one-off noise. If the report shows reported figures, one-offs must appear as an explicit bar. Either way, the start and end values must be linked to the same row of the ratio engine used for margins and leverage, and the normalization log must explain every adjustment that separates reported from normalized EBITDA.

Common pitfalls

Bridges that do not reconcile

Bridges that do not reconcile to reported or normalized EBITDA

The mistake: the bridge's end bar differs from the EBITDA in the ratio tables, or an "Others" plug forces it to close. Why it happens: endpoints typed rather than linked; drivers computed from a different mapping (e.g., changes in inventories omitted); start normalized but end reported; Ind AS 116 basis inconsistent between years. Correct treatment: compute every driver from source, link both endpoints to the ratio engine, use a zero-check cell with a rounding tolerance and route it to the master check; show any residual explicitly and cap its size. Detection in review: trace both endpoints to the ratio engine; recompute the check; look for an unlabeled or large "Others" bar.

Dual-axis charts with misleading scales

Dual-axis charts with truncated or mismatched scales

The mistake: a combo chart with an auto-scaled secondary axis (e.g., 18.2%–19.4%) that turns a 50 bps margin change into a dramatic swing, or a primary column axis that does not start at zero; different peers' charts using different bounds. Why it happens: Excel's automatic bounds and a desire to make movements "visible". Correct treatment: start column axes at zero; set fixed bounds for the margin axis (ideally from 0%), align gridlines, use identical bounds across peer charts, label both axes with units, or avoid dual axes altogether with stacked panels or data labels. Detection in review: open Format Axis on every combo chart and check Bounds are fixed and documented; compare the visual change with the numerical change (lie factor).

Overloaded charts

Overloading one chart with all peers and all years

The mistake: one chart with three peers × five years × two metrics, a legend of ten entries and overlapping labels. Why it happens: the wish to show "everything" in one place, or a lack of a clear message for the chart. Correct treatment: decide the single message (action title) first; then show only the series needed, using small multiples with identical axes for per-company trends, or a focal company highlighted against grey peers; move detail to an appendix table. Detection in review: apply the "so what" test — can the action title be read off the chart in five seconds? Count series (more than six is a warning) and check whether a legend is needed at all.

Practice Questions

Q1. Integrated EBITDA bridge and exhibit build [L3]

A consumer durables peer reports (₹ crore unless stated):

Item FY24 FY25
Units sold (lakh) 52.0 56.5
Average realisation (₹ per unit) 8,400 8,650
Material cost (₹ per unit) 5,880 6,140
Employee cost 410 452
Other expenses 930 985
One-time warranty provision release included in other expenses (credit) 0 38

(a) Compute reported and normalized EBITDA for both years. (b) Build a normalized EBITDA bridge with bars for volume (at prior unit contribution), price, material cost inflation, employee cost and other opex, and show the zero-check. (c) Write the action title and source note. (d) State which waterfall construction you would use if the workbook must open in Excel 2013, and list the helper columns.

Q2. Peer exhibit set for an IT services report [L3]

For Infosys, TCS and Wipro (FY23–FY25), design three exhibits: a revenue and EBITDA margin trend, a constant-currency versus currency revenue bridge for one company, and an ROIC versus 3-year revenue CAGR bubble chart. For each, specify chart type, layout (category axis and series), axis bounds policy, colour rules, labels, title-cell formula logic and source note, and explain how framework differences (IFRS 20-F versus Ind AS annual report) will be reflected in the basis note.

Q3. Critique and rebuild [L2]

A draft Project 2 page contains: a 3D clustered column chart of revenue for three jewellers over five years with a legend; a combo chart whose secondary margin axis runs from 6.0% to 8.0% while the next page's peer chart runs from 0% to 12%; and a bubble chart with bubble width (not area) proportional to revenue and quadrant lines at the peer means. Identify every problem using IBCS SUCCESS and Tufte principles, and write the rebuild instructions.

Q4. Gross margin bridge with dynamic labels [L2]

A paints peer had FY24 revenue ₹11,200 crore and gross margin 42.5%, and FY25 revenue ₹12,100 crore and gross margin 41.2%; employee cost rose from ₹820 crore to ₹880 crore and other expenses from ₹2,150 crore to ₹2,290 crore. (a) Compute the revenue-growth-at-prior-margin and gross margin effects and complete the EBITDA bridge. (b) Write the cell formula for a dynamic action title that states whether EBITDA margin expanded or contracted and by how many bps. (c) Write the signed-label formula for the waterfall helper column.

💡 Hint: Compute both years' EBITDA independently from the data before building the bridge so the zero-check is meaningful.
