Key concept

Calendarizing a March Fiscal Year to a Calendar Year

Concept: CY_t = (3/12) × FY ending Mar_t + (9/12) × FY ending Mar_t+1, or preferably sum of four reported quarters

Statement

To restate a company with a fiscal year ending 31 March onto calendar year \(t\) (1 January to 31 December of year \(t\)):

\[ \text{CY}_t = \frac{3}{12} \times \text{FY}_{\text{Mar } t} + \frac{9}{12} \times \text{FY}_{\text{Mar } t+1} \]

where:

  • \(\text{FY}_{\text{Mar } t}\) is the fiscal year 1 April \(t-1\) to 31 March \(t\). Its last three months (January–March \(t\)) fall inside \(\text{CY}_t\).
  • \(\text{FY}_{\text{Mar } t+1}\) is the fiscal year 1 April \(t\) to 31 March \(t+1\). Its first nine months (April–December \(t\)) fall inside \(\text{CY}_t\).

The preferred alternative is exact:

\[ \text{CY}_t = Q_{\text{Jan–Mar } t} + Q_{\text{Apr–Jun } t} + Q_{\text{Jul–Sep } t} + Q_{\text{Oct–Dec } t} \]

using the four quarterly results published under SEBI LODR Reg 33.

Explanation

The weighting assumes the flow (revenue, EBITDA) accrues evenly through each fiscal year. That is rarely true:

  • Seasonality. Indian jewellery revenue is concentrated in October–December (Dhanteras/Diwali, weddings) and around Akshaya Tritiya (April/May). Lubricant and consumer demand follows the monsoon and festivals. Equal monthly weights misallocate those peaks.
  • Trend. In a fast-growing business, the 9/12 weight on the later year also pulls a forecast into a historical number. If \(\text{FY}_{\text{Mar } t+1}\) is not yet reported, \(\text{CY}_t\) becomes partly an estimate.
  • Step changes. Acquisitions, price increases or store openings mid-year break the even-accrual assumption.

Summing reported quarters avoids all three problems, because every listed Indian company reports standard calendar quarter-ends (March, June, September, December) regardless of fiscal year. Use the weighting formula only when quarterly data does not exist (unlisted peers, foreign peers with odd quarter-ends, or very old years), and footnote it.

The direction of conversion is a choice. In Indian peer sets the majority usually have March years. It is then simpler to convert the minority December-year peers (Castrol India, ABB India) onto the March basis by the mirror formula \(\text{FY}_{\text{Mar } t} = \tfrac{9}{12}\text{CY}_{t-1} + \tfrac{3}{12}\text{CY}_t\), or by summing quarters. Convert to calendar years when comparing with global peers that mostly report on calendar years.

Illustration

An Indian IT company reports revenue of ₹1,53,700 crore for FY ending March 2024 and ₹1,62,990 crore for FY ending March 2025 (illustrative).

\[ \text{CY}_{2024} = 0.25 \times 1{,}53{,}700 + 0.75 \times 1{,}62{,}990 = 38{,}425 + 1{,}22{,}242.5 = 1{,}60{,}667.5 \]

Reported quarters were (₹ crore) Jan–Mar 24: 37,920; Apr–Jun 24: 39,315; Jul–Sep 24: 40,986; Oct–Dec 24: 41,764. Their sum is ₹1,59,985 crore. The weighting overstates CY24 by about ₹680 crore (0.4%) for two reasons. It gives Jan–Mar 24 an "average" FY24 quarter of ₹38,425 crore against ₹37,920 crore actual. It also assumes Apr–Dec 24 was exactly 75% of FY25, when the above-average Jan–Mar 25 quarter (implied ₹40,925 crore) means Apr–Dec 24 was only about 74.9%. For margin metrics, the error can be larger, because seasonal cost items such as wage hikes in Q1/Q2 and furloughs in Q3 do not spread evenly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the weighting formula [L1]

A March-year company reports EBITDA of ₹2,400 crore (FY ending March 2024) and ₹2,880 crore (FY ending March 2025). Compute calendarized CY2024 EBITDA. State the assumption the formula makes and one reason it may fail for a jeweller.

Q2. Quarters vs weighting with seasonality [L2]

A jeweller's quarterly revenue (₹ crore) was: Jan–Mar 24: 3,900; Apr–Jun 24: 4,300; Jul–Sep 24: 3,700; Oct–Dec 24: 6,100; Jan–Mar 25: 4,800. FY ending March 2024 revenue was ₹15,100 crore. Compute FY ending March 2025 revenue, CY2024 by the weighting formula and CY2024 by summing quarters. Express the weighting error as a % of the quarterly sum and explain it.

💡 Hint: FY ending March 2025 is the sum of the April 2024 to March 2025 quarters.

Q3. Choosing the direction of conversion [L3]

Your peer set contains three March-year Indian companies, one December-year Indian MNC subsidiary and two US-listed companies (one December year-end, one August year-end). Recommend the common window for (a) an Indian equity research peer report and (b) a global benchmarking deck for a multinational parent. For each, explain the conversions required and what you would footnote.
