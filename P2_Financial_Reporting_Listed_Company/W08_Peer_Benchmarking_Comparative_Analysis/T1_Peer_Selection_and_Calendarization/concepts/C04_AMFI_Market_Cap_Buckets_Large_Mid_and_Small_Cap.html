Key concept

AMFI Market-Cap Buckets: Large, Mid and Small Cap

Concept: AMFI: large cap = top 100, mid cap = 101–250, small cap = 251 onwards by full market capitalisation

Statement

Under SEBI's circular on categorisation and rationalisation of mutual fund schemes (October 2017), companies are classified using a ranked list published by AMFI:

Bucket Rank by full market capitalisation
Large cap 1st to 100th
Mid cap 101st to 250th
Small cap 251st onwards

The rank is based on average full market capitalisation over the preceding six months:

\[ \overline{\text{MCap}}_i = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{d=1}^{n} \text{Price}_{i,d} \times \text{Shares outstanding}_{i,d} \]

where \(n\) is the number of trading days in the six-month window. For companies listed on more than one exchange, the average across exchanges is used. AMFI publishes the list half-yearly (based on data to 30 June and 31 December). Mutual funds then rebalance portfolios within a prescribed window.

Explanation

Full vs free-float. AMFI uses full market capitalisation, so all shares, including promoter holdings, count. NSE indices (Nifty 50, Nifty Midcap 150) weight by free-float market cap. A company with 75% promoter holding can be "large cap" for AMFI yet carry a small index weight. Do not mix the two measures in one table.

What the bucket proxies. Scale, liquidity, analyst coverage, institutional ownership and, in valuation, the size/liquidity premium. It is a starting filter in peer screening (s02), combined with NSE's industry classification.

What it does not capture. Market cap = earnings × multiple. A richly valued company with modest revenue (a high-growth small jeweller) can sit above a larger, cheaply valued one. For operating comparisons, use revenue, EBITDA, capital employed or store/headcount bands alongside the AMFI bucket.

Thresholds move. The market-cap cut-off for the 100th and 250th ranks changes with every list and has risen sharply with market levels. Cite the list date ("AMFI list for the six months ended 30 June 2025") rather than a rupee threshold.

Regulatory link. Large-cap funds must invest at least 80% in large caps, mid-cap funds 65% in mid caps, small-cap funds 65% in small caps, and large & mid-cap funds at least 35% in each. Bucket migration therefore drives fund flows, which is relevant when discussing valuation re-rating of a peer.

Illustration

A peer set for a jewellery report, with the AMFI bucket noted from one half-yearly list (illustrative ranks):

Company Illustrative AMFI rank Bucket Revenue FY25 (₹ crore, illustrative)
Titan ~20–30 Large cap ~57,000
Kalyan Jewellers ~100–150 Large/Mid (near boundary) ~25,000
Senco Gold ~400–600 Small cap ~6,300

The table shows a near 10x revenue spread and two bucket boundaries. A valuation multiple comparison must discuss the size/liquidity gap. An operating benchmarking report can still use all three, with caveats on scale (s01). Always replace these illustrative values with the current AMFI list and annual report figures.

Practice Questions

Q1. Bucket classification [L1]

On the latest AMFI list, Company R ranks 98th, Company S 250th, Company T 251st and Company U 101st. Classify each and state which fund categories must hold them within their minimum allocation limits. Explain why S and T may switch buckets on the next list.

Q2. Full vs free-float market cap [L2]

Company V has 100 crore shares at ₹1,800 per share, with promoters holding 72%. Company W has 150 crore shares at ₹900 per share, with promoters holding 40%. Compute full and free-float market capitalisation for each. Explain which ranks higher on the AMFI list and which carries the higher index weight, and why a peer table should label the measure used.

Q3. Screening judgement [L3]

A small-cap jeweller has a higher six-month average market cap than a mid-cap jeweller with twice its revenue, because it trades at 60x earnings. Your client wants a "mid- and large-cap only" peer set. Argue whether the small-cap jeweller should be included in (a) an operating benchmarking report and (b) a trading-multiples valuation, and state what you would disclose.

💡 Hint: Separate the purpose of the comparison from the screening rule.
