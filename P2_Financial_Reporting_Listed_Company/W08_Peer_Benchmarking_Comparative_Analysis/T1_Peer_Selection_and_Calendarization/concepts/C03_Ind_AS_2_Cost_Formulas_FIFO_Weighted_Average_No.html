Key concept

Ind AS 2 Cost Formulas: FIFO, Weighted Average, No LIFO

Concept: Ind AS 2: FIFO or weighted average (specific identification for non-interchangeable items); LIFO prohibited, permitted under US GAAP

Statement

Under Ind AS 2 Inventories:

  • Specific identification (Ind AS 2.23) is required for inventories that are not ordinarily interchangeable, and for goods or services produced and segregated for specific projects. Specific individual costs are attributed to the identified items.
  • FIFO or weighted average cost (Ind AS 2.25) must be used for all other inventories. The same cost formula applies to all inventories of a similar nature and use. Different formulas may be justified for inventories of a different nature or use (Ind AS 2.26). Location or tax rules alone are not a justification.
  • LIFO is not permitted. It is absent from Ind AS 2, as it was removed from IAS 2 in 2003. US GAAP (ASC 330) permits LIFO.

Weighted average cost per unit:

\[ \text{WAC} = \frac{\text{Cost of opening inventory} + \text{Cost of purchases}}{\text{Units in opening inventory} + \text{Units purchased}} \]

computed periodically or on each receipt (moving average). Inventory is then carried at the lower of cost and net realisable value (Ind AS 2.9).

Explanation

Why the choice matters for peers. With rising input prices, FIFO assigns the oldest (cheapest) costs to COGS, so gross margin is higher and closing inventory is nearer current cost. Weighted average lags current cost and LIFO lags most. With falling prices the order reverses. The effect is proportional to the price change and to inventory days. Jewellers (gold, long inventory holding) and commodity processors feel it most. IT services firms have negligible inventory, so for them this concept is mostly irrelevant.

Why Ind AS bans LIFO. LIFO leaves very old costs in the balance sheet, so inventory can be carried at decades-old prices, and it allows profit management through LIFO liquidations. US companies keep LIFO partly because of the US tax "LIFO conformity rule" (LIFO for tax requires LIFO in financial statements).

Specific identification in jewellery. High-value studded pieces, certified diamonds and solitaires are often unique, so specific identification fits them. Plain gold of the same purity is interchangeable and is valued on FIFO or weighted average. One jeweller can therefore legitimately apply two formulas to different categories.

Measurement techniques vs cost formulas. The standard cost method and retail method (Ind AS 2.21–22) are permitted techniques if results approximate cost. They are not cost formulas. A retailer using the retail method still has an underlying FIFO or weighted-average assumption.

Disclosure. Ind AS 2.36(a) requires the accounting policies, including the cost formula used. Change in cost formula is a change in accounting policy (Ind AS 8), applied retrospectively.

Illustration

A jeweller starts the year with 100 kg gold at ₹60 lakh/kg (₹6,000 lakh = ₹60 crore). It buys 100 kg at ₹70 lakh/kg (₹70 crore) and sells 120 kg.

₹ crore FIFO Weighted average LIFO (US GAAP only)
COGS 100 × 0.60 + 20 × 0.70 = 74.0 120 × 0.65 = 78.0 100 × 0.70 + 20 × 0.60 = 82.0
Closing inventory (80 kg) 80 × 0.70 = 56.0 80 × 0.65 = 52.0 80 × 0.60 = 48.0

WAC = (60 + 70) / 200 kg = ₹0.65 crore/kg. On revenue of ₹100 crore, gross margin is 26% under FIFO, 22% under weighted average and 18% under LIFO. The economics are identical, but there is a 400 bps reported gap between the two Ind AS-permitted formulas alone.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute COGS under each formula [L1]

Opening inventory: 2,000 units at ₹500. Purchases: 3,000 units at ₹560, then 1,000 units at ₹620. Sales: 4,500 units at ₹900 (periodic system). Compute COGS, closing inventory and gross margin % under FIFO and periodic weighted average. Explain why LIFO cannot be used for the Ind AS financial statements.

Q2. Two formulas in one jeweller [L2]

A jeweller values plain gold jewellery on weighted average and certified solitaires by specific identification. An analyst says this is "inconsistent policy" and adjusts the solitaires to weighted average for peer comparison. Evaluate the analyst's view by reference to Ind AS 2.23–2.26. State what adjustment, if any, is appropriate in a peer report.

💡 Hint: Consider whether solitaires are "ordinarily interchangeable" and whether they differ in nature or use.

Q3. Interview question: rising gold prices [L2]

"Gold rose about 30% in FY25. How would FIFO versus weighted average affect two jewellers' gross margins and inventory days, and how would gold-price hedging change your answer?" Prepare a structured verbal answer of about 150 words.
