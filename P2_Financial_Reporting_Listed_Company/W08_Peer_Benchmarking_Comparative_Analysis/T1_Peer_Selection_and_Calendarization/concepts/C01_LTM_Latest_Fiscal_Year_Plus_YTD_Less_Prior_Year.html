Key concept

LTM: Latest Fiscal Year Plus YTD Less Prior-Year YTD

Concept: LTM = Latest FY + Current YTD − Prior-year YTD

Statement

The last-twelve-months (LTM, also called trailing-twelve-months, TTM) value of any flow item is:

\[ \text{LTM} = \text{FY}_{\text{latest}} + \text{YTD}_{\text{current}} - \text{YTD}_{\text{prior}} \]

where:

  • \(\text{FY}_{\text{latest}}\) is the latest completed audited fiscal year (for example FY ending 31 March 2025),
  • \(\text{YTD}_{\text{current}}\) is the cumulative figure from the start of the current fiscal year to the latest reported quarter-end (for example April–September 2025, i.e. H1 FY26),
  • \(\text{YTD}_{\text{prior}}\) is the same cumulative period one year earlier (April–September 2024, H1 FY25).

Equivalently, LTM is the sum of the latest four reported quarters.

Explanation

Annual numbers go stale fast. By November 2025, the FY25 figures are eight months old, and Q1 and Q2 FY26 results have been published under Reg 33. LTM rolls the window forward to the latest data, keeping a full 12 months so seasonality is neutralised. This matters a lot for jewellers (Diwali and Akshaya Tritiya fall in specific quarters) and IT (Q3 furloughs).

Key rules:

  • Flows only. Apply LTM to revenue, EBITDA, PAT, CFO and capex. Balance-sheet items (debt, cash, inventory) are taken at the latest balance sheet date, which is the 30 September half-year statement of assets and liabilities under Reg 33.
  • Same basis. All three components must be consolidated (or all standalone), on continuing operations, with the same normalisation adjustments. If the YTD period restated the prior-year YTD, use the restated prior-year YTD from the current filing.
  • Non-recurring items. An exceptional item in the prior-year YTD drops out of LTM. One in the current YTD enters it. Normalise both.
  • LTM vs calendarization. LTM aligns peers with different reporting lags to a common latest date. Calendarization (concept c02) aligns different fiscal year-ends. Using a common LTM end date (for example 12 months to 30 September 2025) achieves both, because Reg 33 quarters share standard quarter-ends.
  • Valuation use. LTM EBITDA paired with current enterprise value gives the trailing multiple (Week 16).

Illustration

Titan-style data (₹ crore, illustrative): FY25 revenue 57,800; H1 FY26 revenue 31,400; H1 FY25 revenue 26,700.

\[ \text{LTM revenue (to 30 Sep 2025)} = 57{,}800 + 31{,}400 - 26{,}700 = 62{,}500 \]

If H1 FY25 included a one-off ₹300 crore custom-duty-related inventory loss within EBITDA, while H1 FY26 had none, then the normalised LTM EBITDA adds back nothing for H1 FY26. FY25 normalised EBITDA must already exclude the ₹300 crore. The prior-year YTD subtracted must also be the normalised figure. Otherwise the add-back is double counted or lost.

Excel, with a quarterly table:

=SUMIFS(tblQ[EBITDA], tblQ[Company], $A5,
        tblQ[QtrEnd], ">"&EDATE($B$1,-12), tblQ[QtrEnd], "<="&$B$1)

Connections

  • s03 (calendarization): LTM to a common quarter-end is the cleanest way to align Dec-year peers like Castrol India.
  • s06 (consistency of basis): all three LTM components must be on the same consolidated, continuing-operations basis.
  • Week 5 normalisation: exceptional items in either YTD period must be treated identically.
  • Week 16 trading comparables: LTM EV/EBITDA and P/E; Week 13 three-statement model historicals.
  • FP&A practice: rolling LTM KPIs in monthly MIS and covenant tests (many loan covenants use LTM EBITDA).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute LTM EBITDA and leverage [L1]

A company reports FY25 EBITDA ₹6,200 crore, Q1+Q2 FY26 EBITDA ₹3,450 crore, and Q1+Q2 FY25 EBITDA ₹2,980 crore. Net debt at 30 September 2025 is ₹4,100 crore. Compute LTM EBITDA and LTM net debt/EBITDA. State which balance sheet date you used and why.

Q2. LTM with exceptional items [L2]

FY25 reported EBITDA is ₹1,900 crore, including a one-off ₹120 crore gain on a store-lease termination recorded in Q2 FY25. H1 FY26 reported EBITDA is ₹1,050 crore, including a ₹40 crore restructuring cost. H1 FY25 reported EBITDA is ₹880 crore. Compute reported and normalised LTM EBITDA to 30 September 2025.

💡 Hint: Normalise each of the three components first, then combine.

Q3. LTM across peers with different reporting lags [L2]

At 10 November 2025, Peer A has published Q2 FY26 (quarter to 30 September 2025) results. Peer B has published only Q1 FY26. Peer C is a December-year company that has published its quarter to 30 September 2025. Explain how you would build comparable LTM figures for all three, what you would do about Peer B, and how you would label the table.
