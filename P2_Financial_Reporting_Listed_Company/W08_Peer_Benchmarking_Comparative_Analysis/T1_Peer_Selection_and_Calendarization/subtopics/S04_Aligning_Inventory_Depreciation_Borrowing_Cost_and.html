Subtopic note

Aligning Inventory, Depreciation, Borrowing Cost and Revenue Policies

Scope: Policy alignment: inventory cost formula (FIFO vs weighted average), SLM vs WDV depreciation, borrowing cost capitalisation, revenue gross vs net

Why it matters

Ind AS allows real choices and judgements in measurement. Two companies with identical economics can report different gross margins, EBIT, asset bases and revenue because they apply different accounting policies. In a peer matrix those differences show up as "performance" gaps unless you neutralise them. This is the heart of the roadmap's "apples-to-apples" requirement. It is also where audit experience pays off: you know where policy notes hide (Note 1/2 "Material accounting policy information", required by Ind AS 1 after the 2023 amendment replaced "significant" with "material") and how estimates move numbers.

The workflow for each policy area is the same:

  1. Read each peer's policy note and tabulate it.
  2. Quantify the difference from disclosures.
  3. Adjust to a common basis in the adjustments log, or caveat if you cannot quantify.

Core notes

1. Inventory cost formula: FIFO vs weighted average

Ind AS 2.23 requires specific identification for items that are not ordinarily interchangeable. Ind AS 2.25 requires FIFO or weighted average cost for all other items, and the same formula for inventories of similar nature and use. LIFO is not permitted (concept c03).

Effect. When input prices are rising, FIFO charges older, cheaper costs to COGS. Closing inventory sits near current cost and gross margin is higher. Weighted average sits between FIFO and current cost. The gap is largest when:

  • the price trend is steep (gold rose sharply in 2024–25), and
  • inventory days are long (jewellers hold 150–300+ days of inventory).

Quantifying. Companies rarely disclose an alternative-formula figure. Estimate the effect with a price-index approach:

\[ \Delta \text{COGS} \approx \text{Average inventory quantity} \times (\text{Price}_{\text{FIFO layer}} - \text{Price}_{\text{WA}}) \]

In practice, analysts often avoid the problem for jewellers. Gold-price pass-through and gold-on-loan hedging (see below) mean the bigger comparability driver is hedging policy, not the cost formula.

Jewellery-specific: gold metal loans and hedging. Jewellers borrow physical gold from banks under gold metal loan (GML) schemes and repay in gold or its value at a later date. Some designate GML or forwards as fair-value hedges of inventory price risk (Ind AS 109 hedge accounting). Others leave inventory unhedged. A hedged jeweller's gross margin is protected from gold-price swings. An unhedged one shows inventory gains in a rising market. Those gains are non-recurring and should be identified in normalisation (Week 5). Also check where GML is presented: borrowings or other financial liabilities. Net debt must treat it consistently across peers.

2. Depreciation: SLM vs WDV

Ind AS 16.60–62 says the method must reflect the expected pattern of consumption of economic benefits. Schedule II of the Companies Act 2013 gives indicative useful lives, and a company using a different life must disclose the justification. A change in method is a change in accounting estimate applied prospectively (Ind AS 16.61, Ind AS 8).

Effect. WDV front-loads depreciation, so early-year EBIT is lower and net block is lower. SLM is level. EBITDA is unaffected. EBIT, PAT, EPS, ROE and ROIC (through both NOPAT and invested capital) are affected.

Adjusting.

  • Prefer EBITDA-based and pre-depreciation metrics for cross-peer ranking where depreciation policy differs.
  • For EBIT/ROIC, compute depreciation as % of opening gross block for each peer. If one peer's rate is far above the others with no asset-mix reason, restate D&A to the peer-median rate and adjust net block cumulatively. Log it.
  • Note useful-life differences too. A peer depreciating store fit-outs over 5 years versus 10 years has the same effect as a method difference.

Illustration. Asset of ₹100 crore, 10-year life. SLM charges ₹10 crore a year. WDV at 25.9% (rate that leaves 5% residual after 10 years) charges ₹25.9 crore in year 1. A peer three years into a capex cycle on WDV reports EBIT lower by roughly ₹10–16 crore a year per ₹100 crore of new assets.

3. Borrowing cost capitalisation

Ind AS 23.8 makes capitalisation mandatory for borrowing costs directly attributable to the acquisition, construction or production of a qualifying asset (one that necessarily takes a substantial period to get ready). There is no free choice, but judgement drives the answer:

  • what counts as "substantial period";
  • whether general borrowings are pooled (capitalisation rate on general borrowings, Ind AS 23.14);
  • exemptions for inventories manufactured in large quantities on a repetitive basis and for assets measured at fair value (Ind AS 23.4).

Effect. Capitalised interest leaves finance cost and enters asset cost. It later returns through depreciation (PP&E) or COGS (inventory, which matters for real estate developers and long-maturing inventories such as aged spirits). Interest coverage and PBT look better during construction. EBITDA can be inflated later if capitalised interest is released through COGS, because it then sits above the EBITDA line.

Adjusting. Use the "borrowing costs capitalised" disclosure (Ind AS 23.26) and the gross interest paid in the cash flow statement. For coverage ratios, compute total interest incurred = expensed + capitalised for every peer. For a developer, add back interest included in COGS to get a clean operating EBITDA.

4. Revenue gross vs net (principal vs agent)

Ind AS 115.B34–B38: an entity is a principal if it controls the specified good or service before transfer to the customer. Indicators are primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk and pricing discretion. A principal reports revenue gross. An agent reports only its commission or fee (net).

Where it bites in our sectors.

Situation Gross reporter Net reporter Effect on ratios
IT: resale of third-party hardware/software licences Revenue includes pass-through Only margin recognised Gross reporter shows lower margin %, same EBITDA
Jewellery: franchise / consignment arrangements Retail value booked Wholesale value or commission Revenue and margin % differ (s01)
Jewellery: gold-exchange / old-gold schemes Varies with contract terms — Check revenue note
Marketplace or aggregator channels Gross merchandise value (GMV) Take rate Revenue growth not comparable

GST collected is never revenue (it is collected on behalf of government). Make sure every peer's revenue excludes GST and that older-year data does not include excise duty (pre-July 2017) for any multi-year series.

Adjusting. Where the segment or revenue-disaggregation note (Ind AS 115.114) shows pass-through revenue, restate to a net basis for margin comparison. Keep gross revenue for scale. If not quantifiable, compare EBITDA in ₹ crore and growth rather than margin %, and caveat.

Fill each cell from the company's own note and cite the page. Do not assume any company's policy.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disclosure gaps. Many companies disclose only "weighted average" or "FIFO" without any quantification. Do not invent an adjustment. Disclose the residual difference qualitatively (pitfall 3 in topic.md).
  • Mixed policies within one company. Ind AS 2.25 allows different formulas for inventories of different nature or use (for example FIFO for raw gold, specific identification for diamonds). Compare like with like at category level.
  • Transition effects. On Ind AS transition, companies could use deemed cost (fair value or previous GAAP carrying amount) for PP&E (Ind AS 101.D5–D7). Old asset bases carried at fair value change depreciation and ROIC permanently. This is relevant for comparing companies that transitioned in different phases (2016–2019).
  • Estimate vs policy. A useful-life change is an estimate change, not a policy change. It does not restate comparatives, so the three-year trend has a kink. Spot it in the PP&E note.
  • Materiality. Adjust only where the effect could change a ranking or a conclusion. Record immaterial items as "reviewed, not adjusted".

Key takeaways

  • Policy choices create fake performance gaps. Tabulate every peer's policy from the notes before computing ratios.
  • FIFO vs weighted average matters most with steep price trends and long inventory days. For jewellers, hedging policy and GML presentation often matter more.
  • Depreciation method and useful lives affect EBIT and ROIC but not EBITDA. Normalise using D&A % of gross block.
  • Borrowing cost capitalisation is mandatory under Ind AS 23 but judgement-driven. Use total interest incurred for coverage.
  • Principal-vs-agent judgements under Ind AS 115 change revenue and margin %, not EBITDA in ₹. Restate or switch to ₹-based comparisons.

Practice Questions

Q1. FIFO vs weighted average in a rising gold market [L2]

A jeweller holds 10,000 kg of gold-equivalent inventory throughout FY25. Gold cost ₹6,000 per gram at the start of FY25, rose evenly through the year and ended at ₹8,400 per gram. Inventory turns about 1.5 times a year. Ignoring making charges and hedging, estimate the direction and approximate size of the difference in FY25 COGS between FIFO and weighted average. State the assumptions you need and how this affects a gross-margin peer ranking.

💡 Hint: With a 1.5x turn, FIFO COGS is drawn from layers bought roughly eight months earlier. Compare that with an average-cost layer.

Q2. Depreciation restatement [L2]

Peer A (SLM) and Peer B (WDV) each report gross block ₹2,000 crore at the start of FY25. Peer A's D&A is ₹160 crore and Peer B's is ₹290 crore. Both have similar asset mixes. EBIT is ₹900 crore (A) and ₹780 crore (B). Average invested capital is ₹4,500 crore (A) and ₹4,100 crore (B). The tax rate is 25.17%. Restate Peer B's D&A to Peer A's rate, recompute EBIT and post-tax ROIC for both, and explain whether to adjust B's invested capital for cumulative depreciation differences.

Q3. Journal entries for borrowing cost capitalisation [L1]

A company borrows ₹500 crore at 9% on 1 April 2024 specifically to build a manufacturing plant. Construction runs until 31 March 2025, and ₹20 crore of temporary investment income was earned on unutilised funds. Pass the journal entries for interest and its capitalisation for FY25 under Ind AS 23. State the effect on FY25 EBITDA, EBIT and interest coverage compared with a peer that expenses equivalent interest on an operating asset.

Q4. Principal or agent in IT services [L3]

An IT services company resells third-party cloud subscriptions to a client as part of a managed-services contract. It negotiates price with the vendor, has no right to return unused subscriptions, bundles them with its own integration services and is responsible to the client for provisioning. Its peer reports similar resale on a net basis. Analyse the principal-vs-agent indicators under Ind AS 115. Then explain how you would put both peers on a common basis in a margin ranking, and the disclosure you would add.

Q5. Policy alignment table from notes [L3]

For your Project 2 peers, list the specific annual report notes you would read to complete the policy alignment table (inventory, depreciation and useful lives, borrowing costs, revenue recognition, hedging). For each, define a quantitative test that decides whether the difference is "adjust", "disclose only" or "immaterial". Include the threshold you would use, for example the effect on a ranking or more than 50 bps of margin.
