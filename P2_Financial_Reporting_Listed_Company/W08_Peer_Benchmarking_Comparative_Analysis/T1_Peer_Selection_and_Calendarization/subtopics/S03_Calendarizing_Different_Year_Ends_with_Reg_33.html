Subtopic note

Calendarizing Different Year-Ends with Reg 33 Quarterly Data

Scope: Calendarization of differing year-ends (e.g., Dec year-end MNC subsidiaries such as Castrol India) using quarterly Reg 33 data

Why it matters

Sec 2(41) of the Companies Act 2013 makes 1 April–31 March the default financial year. A company that is a holding company, subsidiary or associate of a company incorporated outside India can apply to the Tribunal (NCLT) for a different financial year if it needs one to align with its parent's consolidation. That is why several MNC subsidiaries do not end their year in March. Castrol India, ABB India, Linde India and Schaeffler India use a January–December year. Siemens Ltd uses October–September. Gillette India and P&G Hygiene use July–June. Nestlé India historically used a calendar year and has since moved to an April–March year after a transition period, so always check the latest filing for any peer with a changed year-end.

If you compare Castrol's "FY2024" (Jan–Dec 2024) with a March-year lubricant peer's FY2024 (Apr 2023–Mar 2024), the periods overlap by only nine months. Base-oil price swings, monsoon demand and festive seasons fall into different windows, so growth and margin comparisons become distorted. Calendarization restates every peer to a common 12-month window. It is standard in equity research, trading comparables and FP&A benchmarking.

Core notes

Which window to align to

  • Align to the subject company's fiscal year, or to the majority basis of the peer set. In India this is usually the year ending March.
  • For trading comparables (Week 16), align to a forward window as well, for example the FY ending March 2026 estimate (FY26E).
  • State the common window in every table header, for example "12 months to 31 March 2025 (Castrol calendarized)".

Method 1 (preferred): sum four reported quarters

SEBI LODR Reg 33 requires listed companies to publish quarterly financial results within 45 days of quarter-end. The last quarter goes with the audited annual results within 60 days of year-end. Quarterly results are subject to limited review by the statutory auditor. Both standalone and consolidated quarterly results have been mandatory since FY2019-20. So Castrol publishes results for quarters ending 31 March, 30 June, 30 September and 31 December. These are exactly the quarter-ends a March-year peer uses.

To build Castrol's 12 months ending 31 March 2025:

\[ \text{Flow}_{\text{Apr24–Mar25}} = Q_{\text{Apr–Jun 24}} + Q_{\text{Jul–Sep 24}} + Q_{\text{Oct–Dec 24}} + Q_{\text{Jan–Mar 25}} \]

Castrol's own labels for these are Q2 CY24, Q3 CY24, Q4 CY24 and Q1 CY25. Map them carefully in the workbook. A "quarter label" column plus a "calendar quarter-end date" column prevents errors.

Q4 figures are balancing figures. For the fourth quarter of its own year, a company reports figures equal to audited full-year figures minus published year-to-date figures for nine months (a note under Reg 33 formats states this). If annual audit adjustments were large, the Q4 figure absorbs them. Check whether Q4 looks anomalous before summing.

Method 2 (fallback): time-weighting annual figures

When quarterly data is unavailable (unlisted foreign peers, or older years), weight the two overlapping fiscal years. To restate a December-year company onto a March year:

\[ \text{FY}_{\text{Mar }t} = \tfrac{9}{12} \times \text{CY}_{t-1} + \tfrac{3}{12} \times \text{CY}_{t} \]

Here \(\text{CY}_{t-1}\) is the January–December year ending nine months before 31 March of year \(t\), and \(\text{CY}_t\) is the following calendar year. (Concept c02 states the reverse direction, March-year figures onto a calendar year.) The weighting assumes revenue and profit accrue evenly through the year. That fails for seasonal businesses. Lubricants peak around festive and post-monsoon periods, jewellery around Diwali, Akshaya Tritiya and the wedding season. It also fails for businesses with a step-change (an acquisition or price hike) mid-year. Use Method 2 only with a caveat.

Illustration

Castrol India quarterly revenue (₹ crore, illustrative): Jan–Mar 24: 1,425; Apr–Jun 24: 1,330; Jul–Sep 24: 1,240; Oct–Dec 24: 1,385; Jan–Mar 25: 1,470. Reported CY24 revenue = 1,425 + 1,330 + 1,240 + 1,385 = 5,380.

  • Method 1, 12 months to March 2025: 1,330 + 1,240 + 1,385 + 1,470 = 5,425.
  • Method 2 needs CY25. Using an analyst estimate of 5,600 for CY25: \(0.75 \times 5{,}380 + 0.25 \times 5{,}600 = 5{,}435\). The 10-crore gap shows the estimate risk Method 2 introduces.

Balance-sheet (stock) items

Flows can be summed. Stocks cannot. Reg 33 requires a half-yearly statement of assets and liabilities and, since 2019, a half-yearly cash flow statement. A December-year company therefore publishes balance sheets at 30 June and 31 December. A March-year peer publishes at 30 September and 31 March. Options:

  • Use the balance sheet nearest to the common period end (31 December for a 31 March window, three months off) and disclose the gap.
  • For ratios using averages (ROIC, CCC), average the two half-year balance sheets inside the window: 30 June 2024 and 31 December 2024 for the Apr 2024–Mar 2025 window. This is a reasonable approximation.
  • Never mix a calendarized income statement with a balance sheet from a different year without flagging it.

Cash flow items

Half-yearly cash flow statements give two data points a year: January–June (H1) and January–December (full year, from which July–December is derived). The April 2024–March 2025 window cannot be built exactly from these, because quarterly cash flows are not published. The nearest 12-month windows are calendar 2024 (three months early) or July 2024–June 2025 (H2 CY24 plus H1 CY25, three months late). Pick one, apply it to both CFO and the matching EBITDA so CFO/EBITDA stays internally consistent, and caveat the offset.

Workbook implementation

Build a "Quarterly" tab with one row per company per quarter: company, own fiscal label, calendar quarter-end date, revenue, EBITDA, PAT and the source (Reg 33 filing date). Then:

=SUMIFS(tblQ[Revenue], tblQ[Company], $A5,
        tblQ[QtrEnd], ">"&EDATE(WindowEnd,-12),
        tblQ[QtrEnd], "<="&WindowEnd)

Add a check that exactly four quarters were summed:

=COUNTIFS(tblQ[Company],$A5, tblQ[QtrEnd],">"&EDATE(WindowEnd,-12), tblQ[QtrEnd],"<="&WindowEnd)=4

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Change of financial year. A company moving to a March year presents a transition period longer or shorter than 12 months (for example 15 months). Never treat a 15-month period as a year. Rebuild 12-month windows from quarters.
  • Restated quarters. Later quarters may restate earlier ones (discontinued operations, common-control mergers, Ind AS 8 errors). Use the latest restated comparative published for each quarter and log the restatement.
  • Standalone vs consolidated quarters. Some companies emphasise standalone quarterly results. Make sure all four quarters are on the same basis (s06).
  • Exceptional items. Quarterly results show exceptional items separately. Treat them consistently with the Week 5 normalisation rules across all four quarters.
  • Tax computation. The Income-tax Act's previous year is always April–March, so a December-year company's tax computation already uses a different period from its books. This is why effective tax rates by quarter can look odd.
  • Foreign peers. US registrants file 10-Qs. Accenture's fiscal year ends 31 August, so its quarters end in November, February, May and August and never line up with Indian quarter-ends. Method 1 cannot be applied exactly. Use the nearest four quarters and disclose the one-month offset.

Key takeaways

  • Sec 2(41) lets foreign-linked companies keep non-March years with NCLT approval. Expect Dec, Sep and Jun year-ends among MNC subsidiaries.
  • Prefer summing four reported Reg 33 quarters. Time-weighted annual figures assume no seasonality.
  • Reg 33 Q4 figures are balancing figures and can absorb annual audit adjustments.
  • Balance-sheet items cannot be calendarized by summing. Use the nearest half-year balance sheet or the average of two, and disclose the offset.
  • Automate with dated quarterly tables, SUMIFS on the window, and a four-quarter count check.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a March-window revenue [L1]

A December-year company reported quarterly revenue (₹ crore): Q1 CY24 (Jan–Mar) 820; Q2 CY24 900; Q3 CY24 870; Q4 CY24 950; Q1 CY25 880; Q2 CY25 940. Calculate revenue for the 12 months ending 31 March 2025 and for the 12 months ending 30 June 2025. State which quarters are balancing figures under Reg 33.

Q2. Method 1 vs Method 2 under seasonality [L2]

A jewellery company with a December year-end earns 40% of annual revenue in October–December. CY24 revenue is ₹4,000 crore and CY25 revenue is ₹5,000 crore. Within each year, revenue is spread 18%, 20%, 22% and 40% across Q1–Q4. Compute FY ending 31 March 2025 revenue (a) using time-weighting and (b) by summing quarters. Explain the source of the difference and which number belongs in a peer table.

💡 Hint: Derive each quarter from the seasonal percentages first, then pick the Apr 2024–Mar 2025 quarters.

Q3. Balance-sheet alignment for ROIC [L2]

A December-year peer publishes balance sheets at 30 June and 31 December. Its invested capital was ₹2,100 crore at 31 December 2023, ₹2,300 crore at 30 June 2024 and ₹2,250 crore at 31 December 2024. Calendarized NOPAT for April 2024–March 2025 is ₹520 crore. March-year peers use the average of 31 March 2024 and 31 March 2025 invested capital. Propose the invested-capital denominator for this peer, compute ROIC, and write the footnote.

Q4. Excel calendarization engine [L3]

Specify a workbook tab that calendarizes any number of peers with any fiscal year-end to a user-selected window-end date. Include the quarterly data table layout, the SUMIFS formula, a four-quarter completeness check, a flag when a Q4 balancing figure deviates by more than 25% from the average of the other three quarters, and a source column. Explain how the master check summary picks up a failed completeness check.

Q5. Change of financial year [L3]

A peer changed its financial year from January–December to April–March, presenting a 15-month period from 1 January 2024 to 31 March 2025 in its annual report. Explain the problems this causes for a three-year CAGR and margin-volatility analysis. Describe how you would rebuild comparable 12-month periods for FY23, FY24 and FY25 using Reg 33 quarterly filings.
