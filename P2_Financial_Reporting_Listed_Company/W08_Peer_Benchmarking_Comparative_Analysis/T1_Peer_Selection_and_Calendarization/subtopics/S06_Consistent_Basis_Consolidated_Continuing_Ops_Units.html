Subtopic note

Consistent Basis: Consolidated, Continuing Ops, Units, Restatements

Scope: Consistency of basis: consolidated vs standalone, continuing operations only, ₹ crore units, restated comparatives

Why it matters

Before accounting policies are aligned (s04) or frameworks reconciled (s05), the data basis must be the same for all peers. The most common reasons a peer table fails review are mechanical. One peer is standalone and another consolidated. A divested business is still inside one peer's revenue. One column is in ₹ lakh and another in ₹ crore. Last year's figure is taken from the old annual report rather than from the restated comparative. These errors are easy to make because Indian companies publish both standalone and consolidated statements (Sec 129(3)), quarterly results show both, and data aggregators mix them. Reviewers and interviewers check this first.

Core notes

1. Consolidated vs standalone

Sec 129(3) of the Companies Act 2013 requires a company with one or more subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures to prepare consolidated financial statements in addition to standalone ones. Reg 33 requires both in quarterly results.

Default rule: use consolidated for every peer. Consolidated statements capture the economic group that shareholders own: overseas subsidiaries (critical for IT, where Infosys, TCS and Wipro run large foreign subsidiaries), CaratLane inside Titan, Kalyan's Middle East operations and so on.

When standalone is appropriate.

  • The question is about the Indian legal entity specifically (credit analysis of a borrower whose lenders have no recourse to subsidiaries, dividend capacity under Sec 123, tax analysis).
  • A peer has no subsidiaries, so standalone and consolidated coincide.
  • Segment carve-outs need the standalone entity because the subsidiary is a different business.

Whichever basis you choose, apply it to all peers and all years, and label it in every table header. If one peer only publishes standalone data for an early year (for example pre-IPO), flag the cell.

Also check: non-controlling interests. Use PAT attributable to owners for ROE and EPS, but total PAT and total equity (including NCI) for ROIC/enterprise-level metrics.

2. Continuing operations only

Ind AS 105 requires a discontinued operation (a separate major line of business or geographical area disposed of or held for sale) to be presented as a single amount, post-tax profit or loss from discontinued operations, with comparatives re-presented (Ind AS 105.33–34).

Rule: build revenue, EBITDA, margins and growth on continuing operations for every year. This is usually automatic for the current and the immediately preceding year in the latest annual report, because the comparatives are re-presented. For the third year back (needed for a three-year CAGR), the older annual report will not be re-presented. You must remove the discontinued unit yourself, using the Ind AS 105 note or segment data.

Divestments that do not meet the "discontinued" test (a smaller unit, or a sale of part of a segment) stay in continuing operations. Strip them out on a pro-forma basis if they distort growth, and log the adjustment. Acquisitions create the mirror problem: inorganic growth inflates CAGR. Show organic growth where the company discloses it, or caveat.

3. ₹ crore units and sign conventions

Indian annual reports use ₹ crore, ₹ lakh or ₹ million. Schedule III rounding rules allow presentation in hundreds, thousands, lakhs, millions or crores depending on turnover, and the unit must be used uniformly within the statements. IT companies often report in ₹ crore (TCS, Infosys) or ₹ million (Wipro, in its 20-F). Older jewellery DRHPs may use ₹ lakh or ₹ million.

Convert Multiply by
₹ lakh → ₹ crore 0.01
₹ million → ₹ crore 0.1
₹ thousand → ₹ crore 0.0001
USD million → ₹ crore rate (₹ per USD) × 0.1

Keep a unit column in the raw-extracts tab and convert by formula, never by retyping. Adopt one sign convention, for example costs shown as negatives in standardized statements and positives in ratio inputs, and state it on the cover tab.

4. Restated comparatives

Prior-year figures change after publication because of:

  • Ind AS 8 retrospective policy changes and prior-period error corrections (with a third balance sheet when material, Ind AS 1.40A);
  • Ind AS 105 re-presentation for discontinued operations;
  • common-control business combinations (Ind AS 103 Appendix C), where comparatives are restated as if the combination occurred from the beginning of the earliest period presented (for example group mergers approved by NCLT);
  • reclassifications (Schedule III line changes, regrouping notes).

Rule: for each year, use the most recently published version of that year's figures, usually the comparative column in the next year's annual report. Record in the source log which document each number came from. A three-year history therefore uses the FY25 annual report for FY25 and FY24, and the FY24 annual report for FY23, adjusted for anything restated in FY24's comparatives. Reconcile FY24 as reported in the FY24 report with FY24 as restated in the FY25 report, and explain any difference.

Illustration: basis error that flips a ranking

₹ crore Peer A consolidated Peer A standalone Peer B consolidated
Revenue 51,000 45,000 22,500
EBITDA 5,600 5,300 1,700
EBITDA margin 11.0% 11.8% 7.6%
Net debt 3,100 1,200 2,400
Net debt/EBITDA 0.55x 0.23x 1.41x

Mixing Peer A standalone with Peer B consolidated understates A's leverage by more than half and overstates its margin by 80 bps. Ranking on leverage could change the conclusion of the report entirely if a third peer sat between 0.23x and 0.55x.

Basis checklist (put it on the cover tab)

  1. Basis: consolidated, all peers, all years.
  2. Period: 12 months to 31 March (calendarized where needed, s03).
  3. Continuing operations only; discontinued and pro-forma adjustments logged.
  4. Units: ₹ crore, one decimal for amounts, one decimal for %.
  5. Comparatives: latest restated version, with source.
  6. Attributable vs total PAT used consistently.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Business combinations under common control. A group merger restates comparatives as if the companies were always combined. CAGR from restated figures is "like-for-like", while CAGR from originally reported figures shows reported growth. Choose and disclose.
  • Demergers. The demerged company's history may be carved out in its listing document. For the parent, treat the demerged business as discontinued if Ind AS 105 criteria are met.
  • Associates and JVs. Equity-accounted profits sit below EBITDA. A peer with large JV operations will look smaller on revenue and EBITDA but not on PAT. Consider proportionate adjustments or disclose.
  • Quarterly data basis drift. Some companies headline standalone figures in quarterly press releases. Always pull the consolidated section of the Reg 33 filing.
  • Rounding. Converting ₹ million to ₹ crore and then summing can create small mismatches with reported totals. Tolerate ±0.1 crore in checks, not more.
  • Hyperinflationary or disposed subsidiaries. Rare for our sectors, but check the consolidation note for entities added or removed during the window.

Key takeaways

  • Default to consolidated for every peer and year. Use standalone only for entity-specific questions, applied uniformly and labelled.
  • Use continuing operations only. Older years need manual removal of discontinued units because only the latest two years are re-presented.
  • Convert all amounts to ₹ crore by formula from a unit column, and fix one sign convention.
  • Always use the latest restated comparatives and reconcile them to originally reported figures.
  • Put a basis checklist on the cover tab and a basis label on every table.

Practice Questions

Q1. Unit normalisation [L1]

Peer data arrives as: Peer A revenue ₹2,45,315 crore; Peer B revenue ₹8,95,280 million; Peer C revenue ₹1,62,99,000 lakh; Peer D revenue USD 4,860 million at an average rate of ₹84.2/USD. Convert all to ₹ crore. Describe the raw-extracts tab layout that makes this conversion formula-driven and auditable.

Q2. Re-presenting a three-year history for a discontinued operation [L2]

A company sold its eyewear-manufacturing business in FY25 and classified it as discontinued. The FY25 annual report shows continuing-operations revenue of ₹9,800 crore (FY25) and ₹8,900 crore (FY24, re-presented). The FY24 annual report shows total revenue of ₹9,400 crore for FY24 and ₹8,600 crore for FY23. The discontinued unit's revenue was ₹500 crore (FY24) and ₹460 crore (FY23) per the Ind AS 105 note. Compute the three-year continuing-operations revenue series and the two-year CAGR. Explain the source of each number in your source log.

💡 Hint: Check whether FY24 total less the discontinued unit equals the re-presented FY24 figure before trusting your FY23 adjustment.

Q3. Common-control merger and growth [L3]

Peer X merged a fellow subsidiary under common control in FY25. Comparatives were restated as if the merger occurred at the beginning of FY24. Originally reported revenue was ₹12,000 crore (FY24), and restated FY24 revenue is ₹14,100 crore. FY25 revenue is ₹15,300 crore. Compute reported and like-for-like growth. Decide which you would show in the peer matrix and what footnote you would write.

Q4. Standalone vs consolidated choice [L2]

You are preparing (a) an equity peer comparison of Titan, Kalyan and Senco and (b) a credit note for a bank lending only to Kalyan's Indian entity, with no guarantee from its overseas subsidiaries. For each, state the basis, the PAT measure (attributable or total), and the treatment of non-controlling interests and overseas cash in net debt.

Q5. Restatement reconciliation [L2]

FY24 EBITDA was ₹4,250 crore in the FY24 annual report and ₹4,180 crore as the comparative in the FY25 annual report. The FY25 notes show two changes. First, ₹50 crore of export incentives was reclassified from "other operating revenue" to "other income" (your EBITDA definition excludes other income). Second, a prior-period error correction increased FY24 employee cost by ₹20 crore. Prepare the reconciliation, decide which figure enters your FY24 column, and explain the source-log entry.
