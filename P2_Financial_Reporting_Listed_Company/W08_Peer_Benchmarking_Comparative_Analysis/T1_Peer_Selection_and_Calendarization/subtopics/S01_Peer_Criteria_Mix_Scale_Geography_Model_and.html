Subtopic note

Peer Criteria: Mix, Scale, Geography, Model and Customer Segment

Scope: Peer criteria: revenue mix, scale, geography, business model (owned vs franchise stores, onsite/offshore), customer segment

Why it matters

A peer comparison is only as good as the peer set. Every ratio in Project 2 (margins, ROIC, cash conversion cycle, leverage) is read relative to peers, so a badly chosen peer turns a ranking into noise. Sell-side analysts, rating agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE all name "peer comparison" in their rationales), FP&A teams benchmarking cost structures and valuation teams picking trading comparables all start with the same question: which companies face the same economics? Interviewers often ask you to defend a peer set. The answer they want is not "they are all in the same NSE industry". They want the economic drivers you matched on, and how you handled the ones you could not match.

Core notes

The principle: match on economic drivers, not labels

Two companies are good peers when the same external forces move their revenue, margins and capital needs in the same direction. These are the demand drivers, input costs, pricing power, capital intensity and risk. Five criteria cover most of this ground. Rank them by how strongly each one drives the metric you are comparing.

1. Revenue mix

Revenue mix is the split of revenue by product, service line, vertical or channel. It sets the margin structure and the growth drivers.

  • Jewellery: studded (diamond) jewellery earns a much higher gross margin than plain gold jewellery. Gold coins and bullion earn close to zero. Titan's jewellery division is only part of the group, which also has watches, eyecare and emerging businesses. Kalyan and Senco are close to pure-play jewellers. If studded share is 30% at one peer and 10% at another, a gross margin gap is expected. It does not show better management.
  • IT services: BFSI exposure, the share of digital/cloud work and the share of fixed-price versus time-and-material contracts all matter. A peer with 40% BFSI revenue is hit harder by a US banking slowdown.
  • Test: pull segment information (Ind AS 108) and the KPI disclosures in investor presentations. Tabulate mix for each peer before you accept it.

2. Scale

Scale drives operating leverage, bargaining power with suppliers (gold suppliers, hyperscalers), brand spend efficiency and access to cheap funding.

  • Measure scale on revenue, EBITDA, total assets, store count or headcount, plus market capitalisation.
  • A 10x scale gap is tolerable in an investment-thesis comparison if you discuss it. It is usually fatal for multiple-based valuation. TCS (revenue above ₹2.4 lakh crore) and a ₹5,000 crore mid-tier IT firm have different deal-win profiles.
  • In practice, set a band (for example 0.3x to 3x the target's revenue) and justify any peer that falls outside it.

3. Geography

Geography sets currency exposure, regulatory regime, demand cycle and competitive set.

  • IT services earn 50–60% of revenue from North America for most large Indian vendors. Europe exposure brings a different cycle and euro/pound translation effects. That is why constant-currency growth matters.
  • Jewellery demand differs by region. South India (Kalyan's origin) prefers heavier gold jewellery, and East India is Senco's base. Titan is national. Regional concentration affects wedding-season seasonality, hallmarking rollout timing and competitive intensity from local family jewellers.
  • Domestic versus export share also changes the effect of GST, export incentives and working capital.

4. Business model

Same industry, different models, different ratios. This criterion is the one most often missed.

Owned versus franchise stores (retail/jewellery).

Feature Company-owned (COCO) Franchise-owned (FOFO / FOCO)
Revenue booked Full retail price to the end customer Usually a wholesale sale to the franchisee, or a fee/commission, depending on contract terms and the principal-vs-agent analysis
Gross margin % Higher (retail margin retained) Lower (franchisee margin given away)
Inventory and store capex On the company's balance sheet Largely funded by the franchisee
ROU assets (Ind AS 116) Large lease liabilities Small
ROIC Lower capital turns Higher capital turns, lower margin

Kalyan has been expanding through FOCO stores. Titan's Tanishq network is a mix of owned and franchised stores. Senco runs both company and franchise showrooms. A peer that franchises heavily can show a lower EBITDA margin and a higher ROIC at the same time. If you rank on margin alone, that peer looks worse when it may actually be better.

Onsite versus offshore (IT services). Onsite effort is billed at higher rates but costs much more (US/EU salaries, visas, subcontractors). Offshore effort carries lower rates and higher margins. A peer with 25% onsite effort will have a structurally different revenue per employee, utilisation profile and EBITDA margin than one with 15%. Subcontracting cost is a related lever and is disclosed by most large vendors.

5. Customer segment

  • Jewellery: wedding versus daily-wear, mass versus premium, urban versus tier-2/3 customers. Purchase frequency, ticket size and price sensitivity all differ by segment.
  • IT: Fortune-500 versus mid-market clients, client concentration (top-10 client share), and the number of USD 100 million+ relationships.
  • B2B versus B2C changes the working-capital cycle. Jewellery retail collects in cash. IT services carry 60–80 days of receivables plus unbilled revenue.

Building the peer selection matrix

Document the selection in a scoring table and keep it in the workbook (cover or assumptions tab):

Criterion (weight) Titan (Jewellery) Kalyan Senco Candidate X
Revenue mix — jewellery share of revenue (30%) ~85–90% of group ~100% ~100% ?
Scale — revenue band vs median (20%) Largest Mid Smallest ?
Geography (20%) Pan-India South-led, pan-India + GCC East-led ?
Model — share of franchise stores (20%) Mixed High FOCO growth Mixed ?
Segment (10%) Premium/mass Mass/wedding Mass/value ?

The approximate figures above are for illustration only. Always use the latest annual report and investor presentation.

Score each criterion 1–5 for closeness to the subject company. Apply a threshold, such as a weighted score of 3.5 or more for inclusion. Then write one sentence of judgement per included or excluded peer. The score structures the debate. It does not replace judgement.

Illustration: why mix changes the conclusion

Assume two jewellers, each with ₹10,000 crore revenue. Plain gold earns a 12% gross margin and studded jewellery earns 30%.

  • Peer A: 30% studded mix gives \(0.3 \times 30\% + 0.7 \times 12\% = 17.4\%\) blended gross margin.
  • Peer B: 12% studded mix gives \(0.12 \times 30\% + 0.88 \times 12\% = 14.16\%\).

The 324 bps gap comes entirely from mix. If the report says "Peer A has superior sourcing efficiency", that is unsupported until you show a mix-adjusted margin, for example by applying a common mix to each peer's segment margins.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Conglomerate peers. Titan's group numbers include watches and eyecare. Use the jewellery segment for margin/ROIC where segment data permits (segment EBIT and segment capital employed are disclosed under Ind AS 108). Group numbers remain relevant for leverage and cash flow.
  • Too few true peers. Indian listed universes are thin. You can widen geographically (Chow Tai Fook, Signet), but then framework and currency adjustments become necessary (see s05). You can also accept an imperfect domestic peer and caveat it.
  • Recently listed peers. Senco listed in 2023, so pre-IPO years may come from the DRHP/RHP restated financials, which are prepared on a different basis. Flag this.
  • Business-model drift. A peer converting COCO to FOCO over the three-year window will show a mechanical margin decline and ROIC rise. Comparing the trend against a static-model peer can mislead.
  • Survivorship and selection bias. Choosing only successful peers flatters the median. If a delisted or distressed peer was economically relevant, mention why you excluded it.
  • Onsite/offshore shifts. Visa costs and travel restrictions (for example during 2020–21) shifted mix temporarily, so read margin trends with that in mind.

Key takeaways

  • Pick peers on the economic drivers of the metrics you will compare (mix, scale, geography, model, segment), not on sector labels.
  • Business model (owned versus franchise, onsite versus offshore) can invert margin and ROIC rankings. Identify it before ranking.
  • Use segment data (Ind AS 108) to strip out non-comparable businesses in conglomerate peers.
  • Record the selection in a scored matrix with written judgement for every inclusion and exclusion.
  • Differences you cannot remove by choosing peers must be adjusted or caveated in the report.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mix-adjusted gross margin [L2]

Jeweller P reports ₹12,000 crore revenue with a 22% studded share and a 16.5% blended gross margin. Jeweller Q reports ₹6,000 crore revenue with a 9% studded share and a 13.8% blended gross margin. Assume both earn the same 11.5% gross margin on plain gold. (a) Derive the implied studded gross margin for each. (b) Restate both peers' blended margin at a common 15% studded mix. (c) Explain in three sentences what the restatement tells you about "sourcing efficiency".

💡 Hint: Blended margin is the mix-weighted average of plain and studded margins; solve for the one unknown.

Q2. Franchise model and the ROIC vs margin paradox [L2]

Company F operates 400 stores, 60% of them franchise (FOCO). It books wholesale revenue to franchisees at a 9% discount to retail price. Company O owns all 300 of its stores. Retail revenue per store is ₹40 crore for both. Retail EBITDA margin is 12% on owned stores. The franchise EBITDA margin is 6% on the wholesale value. Capital employed is ₹18 crore per owned store and ₹3 crore per franchise store at the company level. Compute each company's revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA margin and EBITDA/capital employed. Then write a two-line caveat for a peer table that ranks them on EBITDA margin.

Q3. Defending a peer set in an interview [L3]

An interviewer says: "You used Infosys, TCS and Wipro as peers. Why not HCLTech and Tech Mahindra, and why not Accenture?" Prepare a structured 2-minute answer. Cover revenue mix (including HCLTech's products and platforms segment), scale, geography, delivery model (onsite/offshore), customer segment and reporting-framework differences. State what you would add to the peer set and what you would caveat.

Q4. Scoring matrix design [L3]

Design a peer-selection scoring matrix in Excel for an IT services subject company. Use five criteria, weights summing to 100%, a 1–5 closeness scale and an inclusion threshold. Specify the exact data sources (annual report note, investor presentation slide, segment disclosure) for each criterion. Also write the formula that computes each candidate's weighted score and flags inclusion.

💡 Hint: SUMPRODUCT of the weights row and each candidate's score row, compared with the threshold cell, keeps the logic auditable.

Q5. Conglomerate segment carve-out [L2]

A group reports consolidated revenue ₹51,000 crore and EBIT ₹5,100 crore. Its jewellery segment reports revenue ₹44,000 crore, segment EBIT ₹4,800 crore and segment capital employed ₹16,000 crore. Unallocable expenses are ₹400 crore. Explain which numbers you would use in a jeweller peer table for EBIT margin and pre-tax ROCE, how you would treat unallocable corporate costs, and what you would disclose.
