Subtopic note

NSE Industry Classification and AMFI Buckets as Peer Filters

Scope: Using NSE four-level industry classification and AMFI market-cap buckets as starting filters, then economic judgement

Why it matters

The listed Indian universe has over 2,000 actively traded companies on NSE alone. You need a reproducible, defensible first cut before applying judgement. Two public taxonomies give you that cut. NSE's four-level industry classification is also used by AMFI for mutual-fund sector disclosures and by NSE indices. AMFI's half-yearly market-capitalisation list fixes what "large", "mid" and "small" cap mean under SEBI's mutual-fund categorisation rules. Using both makes the screening step auditable: a reviewer can rerun your filter and get the same long list. The economic-judgement step (s01) then turns the long list into a short list.

Core notes

NSE four-level industry classification

NSE moved to a four-tier hierarchy in 2023, replacing its older single-level industry tags. Each listed company maps to exactly one node at every level:

Level Name Granularity (approx.) Example: Titan Example: Infosys
1 Macro-economic sector ~12 Consumer Discretionary Information Technology
2 Sector ~22 Consumer Durables Information Technology
3 Industry ~59 Consumer Durables IT – Software
4 Basic industry ~197 Gems, Jewellery and Watches Computers – Software & Consulting

The node counts are approximate and labels are revised periodically. Download the current classification file from NSE rather than relying on memory. Classification is usually based on the company's dominant revenue source, and NSE reviews it periodically.

How to use it. Screen at Level 4 (basic industry) first. That is the closest to economic peers. Widen to Level 3 only if the Level 4 list is too thin. Never stop at Level 1 or 2. "Consumer Discretionary" puts Titan alongside auto makers and hotels.

Why it is only a starting filter.

  • Classification follows the dominant revenue stream. A diversified company gets one tag even if 30% of its revenue sits elsewhere.
  • Basic-industry buckets still combine different business models. "Gems, Jewellery and Watches" includes branded retailers (Titan, Kalyan, Senco), B2B diamond manufacturers/exporters and gold-refining or bullion-trading companies. These have opposite working-capital and margin profiles.
  • Companies change economically faster than tags are updated.

AMFI market-capitalisation buckets

SEBI's October 2017 circular on categorisation and rationalisation of mutual-fund schemes defined:

  • Large cap: 1st to 100th company by full market capitalisation.
  • Mid cap: 101st to 250th.
  • Small cap: 251st onwards.

AMFI publishes the ranked list every six months. The ranking uses the average full market capitalisation over the preceding six months. Where a company is listed on more than one exchange, AMFI averages across exchanges. Mutual funds must rebalance portfolios within a specified window after each list is published. Full market cap (not free-float) is used. A company with low promoter float can therefore rank higher than its index weight suggests.

How to use it. Market-cap bucket is a proxy for scale, liquidity, analyst coverage and investor base. It is useful when the analysis feeds valuation (trading multiples carry a size/liquidity premium or discount) or when the client is a fund manager restricted to a bucket. It is a weak proxy for operating scale. Market cap reflects valuation multiple, so a richly valued small company can be ranked above a larger, cheaply valued one.

The three-step screening workflow

  1. Taxonomy filter. Take the Level 4 basic industry (and Level 3 if needed) and exclude suspended or illiquid scrips.
  2. Size filter. Keep companies in the same or adjacent AMFI bucket, or within a revenue band of the subject. Note the bucket and the list date used.
  3. Economic judgement. Apply the s01 criteria (mix, scale, geography, model, segment) with written reasons. Drop B2B exporters from a retail-jeweller set. Drop ER&D-only firms from a broad IT-services set, or keep them with a caveat.

Record each step in a "Peer universe" tab: company, NSE symbol, Level 1–4 tags, AMFI rank and bucket (with list date), revenue, inclusion flag and reason. This is the audit trail for the peer set.

Excel implementation

With the NSE classification file and AMFI list loaded as tables, a dynamic filter returns the long list:

=FILTER(tblUniverse[Company],
        (tblUniverse[BasicIndustry]=$B$2) *
        (tblUniverse[AMFI_Rank]<=$B$3) *
        (tblUniverse[Revenue_Cr]>=$B$4),
        "No match")

Join the two sources on ISIN, not company name. AMFI's list is keyed on ISIN and names differ across sources ("Titan Company Ltd" vs "TITAN"). In Week 10 the same join is automated in Power Query (merge on ISIN, then filter).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unlisted or recently listed competitors. Big competitors such as Malabar Gold, Joyalukkas, Tata-owned CaratLane (now inside Titan) or Accenture (foreign) do not appear in NSE filters. Mention them in the competitive context even if they are not in the quantitative set.
  • Bucket migration. A peer moving from small to mid cap between two AMFI lists can change fund ownership and the liquidity premium. For valuation work, state the list date (for example "AMFI list for H1 2025").
  • Holding-company and group tags. Some groups (for example the Tata group's listed entities) have very different classifications. Titan is not "Diversified".
  • Mis-tagging. If a company's tag looks wrong against its segment disclosure, override it with a documented reason. Do not let the taxonomy override the annual report.
  • Free float vs full market cap. For index-weight or liquidity arguments use free-float market cap. For AMFI buckets use full market cap. Do not mix them in one table without labelling.

Key takeaways

  • NSE's four levels are macro-economic sector, sector, industry and basic industry. Screen at basic industry, widen one level only if needed.
  • AMFI buckets: large cap is ranks 1–100, mid cap 101–250, small cap 251 onwards, by six-month average full market cap, published half-yearly.
  • Both are reproducible starting filters. Neither captures business model, revenue mix or geography.
  • Join datasets on ISIN and record list dates so the screen can be rerun.
  • The final peer set needs written economic judgement for every inclusion and exclusion.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and screen [L1]

List the four levels of the NSE industry classification in order from broadest to narrowest. For each of Kalyan Jewellers, TCS and Castrol India, state the level at which you would start a peer screen and why stopping at Level 1 would be inadequate.

Q2. AMFI bucket mechanics [L2]

Company Z's full market capitalisation (₹ crore, NSE) at the month-ends of a six-month window was 48,000; 51,000; 55,000; 47,000; 52,000; 54,000. BSE figures were identical. On the AMFI list the 250th company's six-month average full market cap is ₹50,500 crore and the 251st's is ₹50,200 crore. (a) Compute Z's average. (b) State its bucket and the consequence for a small-cap fund holding it. (c) Explain why a free-float-based ranking might place Z differently.

💡 Hint: AMFI ranks on average full market cap over the period. Compare Z's average with the threshold companies.

Q3. Long list to short list memo [L3]

You screened NSE basic industry "Computers – Software & Consulting" and got 60 names. Write a screening memo (steps, filters, thresholds, reasons) that reduces this to five peers for a comparative report on Infosys. Show at least three excluded categories with the business-model reason for each exclusion. Then list the fields your "Peer universe" tab will hold.

Q4. Formula build with ISIN join [L2]

You have two Excel tables. tblNSE has ISIN, Symbol, BasicIndustry. tblAMFI has ISIN, CompanyName, AvgFullMcap, Rank. Write formulas (XLOOKUP and FILTER, or a LET-based single formula) that return every company in a chosen basic industry ranked 1–250 by AMFI, sorted by rank. Explain why joining on company name would be error-prone.

Q5. When the taxonomy is wrong [L3]

A listed company is tagged "Gems, Jewellery and Watches" at Level 4. Its segment note shows 70% of revenue from gold-bullion trading at a 1.2% gross margin and 30% from branded retail at 18%. Discuss whether to include it in a branded-jewellery peer set, how you would document the override, and what alternative adjustment could keep it in the set.
