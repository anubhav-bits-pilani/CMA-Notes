Subtopic note

Framework Alignment: IFRS 20-F, Ind AS, ASC 842 and LIFO Peers

Scope: Framework alignment: Infosys/Wipro IFRS 20-F vs Ind AS annual reports; US peers under ASC 842 and LIFO

Why it matters

The same company can publish two sets of financial statements under different frameworks. Infosys and Wipro have ADRs listed on the NYSE. They file annual reports on Form 20-F with the US SEC under IFRS as issued by the IASB, alongside their Ind AS annual reports filed under the Companies Act and SEBI LODR. TCS is not SEC-registered and reports only under Ind AS. Once the peer set widens to US companies (Accenture, Cognizant, Signet Jewelers), US GAAP rules such as ASC 842 operating leases and LIFO inventory change EBITDA, net debt and inventory directly. If an analyst pulls Infosys from a US data terminal (IFRS, often in USD), TCS from the annual report (Ind AS, ₹) and Accenture from a 10-K (US GAAP, USD, August year-end), the comparison is broken before any ratio is computed.

Core notes

Ind AS vs IFRS: what differs for Infosys and Wipro

Ind AS is converged with IFRS but has carve-outs and carve-ins. Indian standard-setters deliberately departed from IFRS in a small number of areas. Notable ones:

Area IFRS Ind AS Relevance to IT peers
Bargain purchase gain (business combinations) Profit or loss Capital reserve via OCI/equity (Ind AS 103) Small, acquisition-dependent
Discount rate for employee benefits High-quality corporate bonds Government bonds (market yields on government bonds at the reporting date) per Ind AS 19 Gratuity/leave liabilities differ slightly
Investment property Cost or fair value model Cost model only (Ind AS 40) Rare in IT
Common-control combinations No specific standard Pooling of interests (Ind AS 103 Appendix C) Group restructurings
Presentation IAS 1 flexibility Schedule III Division II formats Line-item classification differs

For Infosys and Wipro, the IFRS (20-F) and Ind AS consolidated numbers in ₹ are usually very close, often identical at revenue and operating profit level. The differences tend to sit in presentation, some reserves and specific items. Rule: for an Indian peer set, use the Ind AS consolidated figures for every Indian peer, because TCS has nothing else. Use the 20-F for extra disclosures (segment detail, risk factors, critical audit matters) and reconcile any line you borrow.

Currency. The presentation currency of 20-F statements need not be ₹. Infosys presents its IFRS statements in US dollars. Wipro presents in ₹ with US dollar convenience translations. Confirm this in the current filing. US data providers also often show USD-translated figures. IT companies also guide and report revenue growth in constant currency (CC), which strips out exchange-rate translation. In the peer report, show:

  • ₹ reported growth (what shareholders earn),
  • USD reported growth (industry convention), and
  • CC growth (operational demand).

Use each company's own disclosed CC growth rather than computing it yourself. Methods vary slightly in which prior-period rates are applied.

US peers: ASC 842 vs Ind AS 116

Ind AS 116 (effective 1 April 2019) uses a single lessee model. Every lease (except short-term and low-value, if elected) produces an ROU asset depreciated through D&A and a lease liability whose interest runs through finance cost. Lease payments therefore sit below EBITDA.

US GAAP ASC 842 has a dual model:

Item Finance lease (ASC 842) Operating lease (ASC 842) Ind AS 116 (all leases)
Balance sheet ROU asset + lease liability ROU asset + lease liability ROU asset + lease liability
P&L Amortisation + interest Single straight-line lease cost in opex Depreciation + interest
EBITDA Excludes lease cost Includes lease cost (lower EBITDA) Excludes lease cost
Cash flow Principal in financing Payments in operating Principal (and interest, by policy) in financing

Most retail and office leases are operating leases under ASC 842. A US jeweller's or IT vendor's EBITDA is therefore understated relative to an Indian peer, and its CFO is lower.

Adjusting a US peer to the Ind AS 116 basis:

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{Ind AS 116 basis}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{US GAAP}} + \text{Operating lease cost} \]

\[ \text{Net debt}_{\text{incl. leases}} = \text{Net debt} + \text{Operating lease liabilities} \]

Operating lease cost and liabilities are disclosed in the lease footnote. Strictly, the Ind AS 116 basis splits the lease cost into depreciation and interest. For EBITDA the whole cost is added back. For EBIT, add back only the implied interest component. Alternatively, convert all peers to a pre-116 basis (EBITDAR-style, or EBITDA less lease cash payments). Topic t2 s06 tests the ranking under both definitions.

US peers: LIFO

US GAAP (ASC 330) permits LIFO. Ind AS and IFRS do not. US companies using LIFO disclose the LIFO reserve, the excess of FIFO (or current) cost over LIFO carrying amount. To convert to FIFO:

\[ \text{Inventory}_{\text{FIFO}} = \text{Inventory}_{\text{LIFO}} + \text{LIFO reserve} \]

\[ \text{COGS}_{\text{FIFO}} = \text{COGS}_{\text{LIFO}} - (\text{LIFO reserve}_{\text{end}} - \text{LIFO reserve}_{\text{begin}}) \]

Equity increases by the cumulative LIFO reserve net of deferred tax. Watch for LIFO liquidation: when inventory quantities fall, old cheap layers flow into COGS and inflate margins temporarily. The 10-K usually discloses the effect.

Illustration. A US peer reports LIFO inventory of USD 900 million, a LIFO reserve rising from USD 210 million to USD 260 million, and COGS of USD 5,000 million. FIFO COGS = 5,000 − (260 − 210) = USD 4,950 million. FIFO inventory = USD 1,160 million. Gross profit rises by USD 50 million and inventory days rise, which lengthens the CCC. Then translate to ₹ at the period-average rate (P&L) and the closing rate (balance sheet), and calendarize (Accenture's year ends 31 August).

A framework checklist per peer

  1. Framework (Ind AS / IFRS / US GAAP) and source document (annual report, 20-F, 10-K).
  2. Reporting currency and translation rates used.
  3. Fiscal year-end and calendarization method (s03).
  4. Lease model and the adjustment applied.
  5. Inventory method (LIFO reserve adjustment if US).
  6. Non-GAAP measures used by the company (adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS). Recompute from GAAP lines, never copy.
  7. Residual differences disclosed (for example US GAAP impairment model, R&D capitalisation rules, stock-based compensation presentation).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mixed sources for one company. Never take revenue from the 20-F and balance sheet from the Ind AS report without reconciling them. Treat one source as primary.
  • Stock-based compensation. US companies and their analysts often exclude SBC from "adjusted" metrics. Indian peers include it in employee cost. Recompute on a GAAP basis for all.
  • Restructuring/business-optimisation costs. Accenture and Cognizant often report material "business optimisation" charges. Normalise them consistently with Indian peers' exceptional items (Week 5 rules).
  • Hyper-specific IFRS/Ind AS differences. If the difference is immaterial, state "Ind AS and IFRS figures reconciled; differences below 0.5% of revenue". That is a legitimate caveat.
  • Exchange-rate choice. Using closing rates for P&L items inflates growth when the rupee depreciates. Follow Ind AS 21-style logic: average rates for flows, closing rates for stocks.
  • Lease-term judgements. ASC 842 and Ind AS 116 both rely on lease-term estimates (renewal options). Lease liabilities are not perfectly comparable even after adjustment. Say so.

Key takeaways

  • Infosys and Wipro file IFRS 20-Fs and Ind AS annual reports. TCS has only Ind AS. Use Ind AS consolidated for all Indian peers.
  • Report ₹, USD and constant-currency growth for IT peers, using company-disclosed CC rates.
  • ASC 842 operating leases keep lease cost inside EBITDA and CFO. Add it back, and add lease liabilities to net debt, to compare with Ind AS 116 peers.
  • Convert LIFO to FIFO using the LIFO reserve for both inventory and COGS, and watch for LIFO liquidations.
  • Document framework, currency, year-end, lease and inventory treatment for every peer, and disclose residual differences.

Practice Questions

Q1. ASC 842 operating-lease adjustment [L2]

A US-listed jeweller reports (USD million): revenue 7,000; EBITDA 840; operating lease cost 420 (all in SG&A); operating lease liabilities 1,500; net debt excluding leases 300. An Indian peer under Ind AS 116 reports EBITDA margin 11.5% and net debt including lease liabilities/EBITDA of 1.2x. Restate the US peer's EBITDA margin and net debt/EBITDA to the Ind AS 116 basis and compare.

💡 Hint: Adjust both numerator and denominator of leverage, not just one.

Q2. LIFO to FIFO conversion [L2]

A US peer reports LIFO inventory USD 1,240 million (opening 1,180 million), LIFO reserve USD 310 million (opening 270 million), COGS USD 6,800 million, revenue USD 9,500 million and a 24% tax rate. Restate gross margin, closing inventory, inventory days on a FIFO basis (365-day year, average inventory) and the cumulative equity effect.

Q3. Choosing the source document [L1]

For each of Infosys, Wipro and TCS, state which document you would use as the primary source for a ₹ crore consolidated peer table, and which document you would consult for additional segment and risk disclosures. Explain in two sentences why mixing data-terminal USD IFRS figures with Ind AS ₹ figures is a problem.

Q4. Constant-currency reporting [L2]

An IT company's USD revenue grew from USD 18,200 million to USD 19,300 million. The company discloses 6.9% CC growth. The ₹/USD average rate moved from 82.8 to 84.6. Compute USD reported growth and ₹ reported growth (convert at average rates). Explain what the gap between USD reported and CC growth implies about cross-currency movements (EUR, GBP, AUD), and how you would present all three in the peer table.

Q5. Framework-alignment memo [L3]

You are adding Accenture (US GAAP, fiscal year ending 31 August, USD) and Cognizant (US GAAP, December year-end, USD) to an Indian IT peer set. Write a one-page alignment memo covering calendarization, currency, lease, SBC, restructuring costs and non-GAAP measures. For each item, state "adjust" or "disclose" with the data source.
