How this week fits

This week sits in Phase 1: Advanced Financial Accounting & Close Mastery. Week 1 covered presentation (Ind AS 1, Schedule III), revenue and cash flow classification; Week 2 moves to the long-dated, estimate-heavy side of the balance sheet. Each standard this week (Ind AS 16, 116, 36, 37 and 19, plus Ind AS 24 disclosures) produces a schedule, not a single journal entry. You will see the same pattern repeatedly: measure a present value at inception, accrete it over time, reassess it at every reporting date, and roll it forward in a reconciliation that ties to the balance sheet, P&L, OCI and cash flow statement.

Prerequisites are the CMA Final Corporate Financial Reporting and SFM syllabus (DCF, WACC, CAPM, Ind AS 16/36/37 basics), comfortable use of Excel financial functions, and a working memory of Income-tax depreciation and Sec 43B. Week 3 builds directly on this week: Ind AS 12 deferred tax uses the temporary differences created by every schedule you build here (book SLM vs tax WDV, ROU vs lease liability, gratuity provisions), and Week 3's business combinations topic creates the goodwill you test for impairment. Week 4's close process consumes the roll-forwards as standard close deliverables.

Learning sequence: Ind AS 16 components and decommissioning (t1) → Ind AS 116 lessee accounting (t2) → Ind AS 36 impairment of PPE, ROU and goodwill (t3) → Ind AS 37 provisions (t4) → Ind AS 19 gratuity and leave (t5) → Ind AS 24 related parties (t6).

Refresh before starting

Time value of money: PV, annuities, rate conversion

Time value of money: PV, ordinary annuity vs annuity due, nominal vs effective rate conversion

  • PV of a single sum: \( PV = \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} \).
  • Ordinary annuity (payments in arrears): \( PV = PMT \times \frac{1-(1+r)^{-n}}{r} \). Annuity due (payments in advance, typical for Indian rent) = ordinary annuity PV \( \times (1+r) \).
  • Nominal rate compounded \( m \) times a year: effective annual rate \( EAR = \left(1+\frac{i}{m}\right)^m - 1 \).
  • Effective annual to effective periodic: \( r_{\text{month}} = (1+EAR)^{1/12} - 1 \). Dividing an effective annual rate by 12 overstates the PV discount slightly and is the classic lease-schedule error.
  • Growing annuity (escalating rent): \( PV = \frac{PMT}{r-g}\left[1-\left(\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right)^n\right] \) when escalation is annual; for step-ups every 3 years, model each payment explicitly instead.

CMA Final SFM/CFR recap

CMA Final SFM/CFR: DCF, WACC, CAPM and Ind AS 16/36/37 fundamentals

  • CAPM: \( k_e = R_f + \beta \times (R_m - R_f) \); in India \( R_f \) is typically the 10-year G-sec yield (around 6.3–7.0% in 2025–26, check the reporting date).
  • WACC \( = k_e \times \frac{E}{D+E} + k_d(1-t) \times \frac{D}{D+E} \), using target market-value weights.
  • Free cash flow to firm = EBIT(1−t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC; terminal value by Gordon growth or exit multiple.
  • Ind AS 16 basics: recognition criteria (probable future economic benefits, reliable measurement), cost vs revaluation model, depreciation of each significant part.
  • Ind AS 36 basics: recoverable amount = higher of FVLCD and VIU; CGU concept; goodwill tested annually.
  • Ind AS 37 basics: provision recognition triad (present obligation, probable outflow, reliable estimate); contingent liabilities disclosed, not recognised.

Income-tax Sec 32 and Sec 43B

Income-tax Sec 32 block depreciation and Sec 43B payment-basis deductions

  • Sec 32 allows depreciation on the WDV of a block of assets at prescribed rates (plant & machinery generally 15%, buildings 10%, computers 40%, intangibles 25%). Assets put to use for less than 180 days in the year get half the rate.
  • Additions increase the block; sale proceeds reduce it; a block ceasing to exist or turning negative gives short-term capital gain/loss under Sec 50.
  • Companies opting for Sec 115BAA (22% base rate, 25.168% effective with surcharge and cess) cannot claim additional depreciation under Sec 32(1)(iia).
  • Sec 43B allows certain expenses only on actual payment: contributions to gratuity/PF funds, leave encashment (Sec 43B(f), constitutionally upheld in Exide Industries, 2025), bonus, interest to banks/NBFCs, and from AY 2024-25 MSME dues beyond Sec 43B(h) time limits. Provisions for gratuity and leave therefore create deductible temporary differences.
  • Rent under a lease is deductible under Sec 37/30 on accrual of the contractual rent, ignoring ROU depreciation and lease interest — the root of Ind AS 116 deferred tax.

Excel functions for schedules

Excel: PMT, PV, NPV, XNPV, IRR, EDATE, data tables, goal seek

=PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type])     ' type 1 = payments in advance
=PV(rate, nper, pmt, [fv], [type])
=NPV(rate, values)                      ' first value discounted one period; add t0 separately
=XNPV(annual_rate, values, dates)      ' actual/365 basis; rate is an effective annual rate
=IRR(values) / =XIRR(values, dates)
=EDATE(start_date, months)             ' next payment dates; EOMONTH for month-end

Data tables (What-If Analysis → Data Table) recalculate a model output for a grid of two inputs (e.g. discount rate × terminal growth). Goal Seek sets one cell to a target by changing one input — used to solve the pre-tax discount rate that equates pre-tax and post-tax VIU, or the IBR implied by a quoted EMI.

Cross-cutting concepts

Discount-rate discipline across standards

Discount-rate discipline: IBR (Ind AS 116), pre-tax risk-specific (Ind AS 36/37), government bond yields (Ind AS 19)

Each standard asks a different question, so each uses a different rate. Ind AS 116 discounts lease payments at the rate implicit in the lease or, if not readily determinable (almost always for lessees), the incremental borrowing rate — what the lessee would pay to borrow, over a similar term and with similar security, funds to obtain an asset of similar value (Ind AS 116 Appendix A). It reflects the lessee's credit and is locked at commencement unless a reassessment or modification triggers a revised rate. Ind AS 36 uses a pre-tax rate reflecting current market assessments of the time value of money and risks specific to the asset for which cash flows have not been adjusted (Ind AS 36.55); it is a market-participant rate, independent of how the entity is financed. Ind AS 37 likewise uses a pre-tax rate reflecting time value and liability-specific risks (Ind AS 37.47); since the liability's risk is often reflected in the cash flows, many Indian companies use a risk-free G-sec rate matched to the timing. Ind AS 19.83 uses market yields on government bonds at the reporting date matched to the currency and duration of the obligation — different from IAS 19, which uses high-quality corporate bonds. Mixing these up (for example, discounting a decommissioning provision at post-tax WACC, or a lease at a G-sec rate) is a recurring review finding.

The shared unwinding pattern

Shared unwinding pattern: lease liabilities, decommissioning, long-term provisions and DBO net interest all accrete through finance/employee cost

Every discounted liability this week follows the same mechanics: initial measurement at PV, then accretion each period at the locked (or reset) rate: \( \text{Closing} = \text{Opening} + \text{Opening} \times r - \text{Payments/Utilisation} \pm \text{Remeasurement} \). For lease liabilities the accretion is interest expense within finance costs (Ind AS 116.36). For decommissioning and other Ind AS 37 provisions the unwinding is a borrowing cost (Ind AS 37.60) presented in finance costs, though it is not eligible for capitalisation under Ind AS 23. For defined benefit plans, interest cost on DBO less interest income on plan assets is net interest (Ind AS 19.123), which Indian companies typically present within employee benefit expense (Ind AS 19 does not specify the line; disclose the policy). The modelling consequence: build a single reusable "accretion block" (opening, interest, cash, remeasurement, closing) and use it for all four schedules, so that finance cost in the P&L forecast is the sum of debt interest, lease interest and provision unwinding.

Reporting-date reassessment is prospective

Reporting-date reassessment of lives, lease terms, provisions and actuarial assumptions → prospective under Ind AS 8

Useful lives, residual values and depreciation methods (Ind AS 16.51, 16.61), lease terms when significant events within the lessee's control occur (Ind AS 116.20), provision best estimates (Ind AS 37.59) and actuarial assumptions are all accounting estimates. Ind AS 8 (as amended to define accounting estimates) requires changes in estimates to be applied prospectively — in the current and future periods affected (Ind AS 8.36–38). Nothing is restated. The mechanics differ by schedule: PPE depreciation is recomputed as remaining depreciable amount over remaining life; a lease term change remeasures the liability at a revised discount rate with the adjustment to ROU; a change in decommissioning estimate adjusts the asset cost (Appendix A to Ind AS 16, the Ind AS equivalent of IFRIC 1); actuarial changes flow to OCI for post-employment plans and to P&L for other long-term benefits. Distinguish these from errors, which are corrected retrospectively (Ind AS 8.42): using a life that was unreasonable on the information available at the time is an error, not an estimate change.

Lease exercise inputs

Lease exercise inputs per lease: commencement, term, rent, frequency, advance/arrears, escalation %, IBR, termination date

Each of the eight leases needs one input row: lease ID and store; commencement date (drives the first payment date and period count via EDATE); lease term in months, after the judgement on lock-ins and reasonably certain renewals; base rent per period, excluding recoverable GST and variable revenue-share; frequency (monthly or quarterly) which determines the periodic rate and step; advance/arrears toggle (1 = payment at start of period, reducing the first-period interest base to zero for the first instalment); escalation % and escalation interval (e.g. 15% every 36 months), applied with a flag =INT((period-1)/interval); IBR as an effective annual rate converted to a periodic rate; and optional termination date (and modification date for the Year 3 change). Keep inputs on one tab with data validation and a unique key so schedules pick them up by INDEX/MATCH or XLOOKUP rather than hard-coding.

Lease exercise outputs

Lease exercise outputs: liability current/non-current split (principal due in 12 months), ROU roll-forward, P&L vs old straight-line rent, termination gain/loss

The schedule must produce: (1) lease liability split — the current portion is the principal reduction scheduled in the next 12 months (sum of payments due in 12 months less the interest component within them), not the total payments; Schedule III requires lease liabilities shown separately under financial liabilities, current and non-current; (2) ROU roll-forward — opening, additions, remeasurement adjustments, depreciation, derecognition on termination, closing — per lease and in total, matching the Schedule III PPE-style note; (3) P&L comparison — Ind AS 116 cost (depreciation + interest) versus the old straight-line rent expense, showing the front-loading in early years and the EBITDA uplift; and (4) termination gain/loss — liability derecognised less ROU derecognised, recognised in P&L at the termination date, with any termination penalty included in the remeasured payments. Cash flow outputs: principal and interest within financing (or interest as per policy) and short-term/variable payments in operating.

Ind AS 7 and Ind AS 12 knock-ons

Knock-ons: Ind AS 7 classification and Ind AS 12 temporary differences for every schedule built

Every schedule this week has a cash flow and a deferred tax consequence. Ind AS 7: PPE and CWIP capex is investing; lease principal repayments are financing and lease interest follows the entity's interest policy (financing or operating; Ind AS 116.50); short-term, low-value and variable lease payments are operating; decommissioning spend is usually operating (or investing if part of a capital project — be consistent); gratuity payments and contributions are operating. Non-cash additions to ROU and the decommissioning asset are excluded from cash flows and disclosed (Ind AS 7.43). Ind AS 12: book SLM vs tax WDV creates a DTL; ROU asset (taxable temporary difference) and lease liability (deductible) are recognised gross after the 2023 amendment narrowing the initial recognition exemption (Ind AS 12.15 and 12.24 as amended, effective 1 April 2023); decommissioning asset and provision behave similarly; impairment losses reduce carrying amount but not tax base, creating a DTA; gratuity and leave provisions deductible on payment under Sec 43B create DTAs, with deferred tax on OCI remeasurements taken to OCI (Ind AS 12.61A).

Hands-on exercise brief

Roadmap exercise: Build a dynamic 5-year Ind AS 116 Lease Liability & ROU Depreciation schedule for 8 commercial store leases including a Year 3 rent escalation and early termination modification.

Scenario

Saffron Retail Ltd (NSE-listed apparel chain, FY ends 31 March) has eight store leases. Model FY 2025-26 to FY 2029-30. All rents exclude GST at 18% (input credit available). Initial direct costs (brokerage) and restoration estimates are given where relevant. IBR is the effective annual rate at commencement.

L1 Mumbai 01-Apr-2025 108 (3+3+3, lock-in 36, renewal reasonably certain) 12.00 Advance 15% every 36 months 9.0% IDC ₹6 lakh
L2 Pune 01-Apr-2025 60 4.50 Advance 5% p.a. 9.0% Restoration ₹10 lakh at end
L3 Bengaluru 01-Jul-2025 72 8.00 Advance 15% every 36 months 9.25% Rent-free first 3 months (incentive)

Required tabs and schedules

  1. Inputs — one row per lease with all fields above, data validation for timing (Advance/Arrears), frequency (M/Q) and flags for exemption, modification and termination.
  2. Rates — conversion of each annual IBR to a periodic rate using \( (1+r)^{1/12}-1 \) (or \( ^{1/4} \) for quarterly).
  3. Payment calendar — EDATE-driven payment dates for each lease up to the end of term; escalation flag and rent per period; zero rent in rent-free periods; MG component only for L4.
  4. Liability schedule — per lease: opening, interest, payment, remeasurement, derecognition, closing; advance/arrears toggle handled in interest base.
  5. ROU schedule — initial ROU (liability + IDC + restoration PV + deposit fair value difference − incentives), depreciation SLM over the shorter of lease term and useful life, remeasurement, derecognition.
  6. Modification and termination — L8 remeasured at the revised rate from 01-Apr-2027 with adjustment to ROU; L5 derecognised at 31-Mar-2028 with gain/loss computed.
  7. Security deposit — L6 deposit at fair value (PV at IBR), unwinding as interest income, difference added to ROU.
  8. Outputs — annual summary FY26–FY30: interest, depreciation, short-term rent, variable rent, total P&L vs straight-line rent; EBITDA impact; liability current/non-current split at each 31 March; ROU roll-forward; termination gain/loss; Ind AS 7 split; Ind AS 12 temporary differences and deferred tax.

Required checks

  • Liability for every lease reaches zero (±₹0.01 lakh) at the end of term or termination.
  • Sum of payments = sum of interest + initial liability (+ remeasurements) per lease.
  • ROU closing balance zero at end of the depreciation period.
  • Current + non-current liability = total closing liability at every 31 March.
  • Total P&L cost over full life (Ind AS 116) = total straight-line rent + IDC + restoration accretion (timing only differs).
  • Error flags display "CHECK" in red via conditional formatting.

Deliverable

One Excel workbook with the tabs above, no hard-codes in calculation tabs, named ranges for key inputs, a one-page dashboard and a short memo (≤1 page) explaining the lease-term judgement on L1, the MG treatment on L4 and the journal entries for the L8 modification and L5 termination.

  • Self-test: rebuild the exercise workbook from a blank sheet in under 3 hours; change any lease's IBR, timing toggle or termination date and confirm all checks still show zero.
  • I can compute pre-tax VIU and allocate an impairment loss across a CGU carrying goodwill
  • Self-test: solve Q1 and Q2 of the mock paper without notes, including the goal-seek pre-tax rate and the floor test, then explain the result to a peer in 3 minutes.
  • I can read an actuarial report and post P&L, OCI and balance sheet entries for gratuity and leave encashment
  • Self-test: take a published NSE company's gratuity note, rebuild the DBO and plan asset roll-forwards in Excel and tie them to P&L, OCI and the balance sheet.
  • I can decide provision vs contingent liability vs Ind AS 12 uncertain tax position for any dispute
  • Self-test: classify ten disputes (income-tax, GST, customs, labour, civil, guarantees) from an annual report's contingent liability note and justify each in one line.
  • I can prepare an Ind AS 24 note and a SEBI Reg 23 / Sec 188 approval matrix
  • Self-test: draft a related party list, KMP compensation table and approval matrix (audit committee, Board, shareholders) for a listed group with a holding company, two subsidiaries, a JV and promoter-family entities.
