Subtopic note

Identifying Cash-Generating Units

Scope: CGU identification: smallest group with largely independent inflows; active-market output test; internal transfer pricing effects

Why it matters

Most assets do not generate cash flows on their own — a machine, a store fit-out, a network tower produce cash only with other assets. Ind AS 36 therefore tests impairment at the level of the cash-generating unit. The CGU definition decides whether losses in one location are offset by profits in another: define CGUs too broadly and impairment is hidden; too narrowly and every weak asset is impaired. CGU identification is one of the most frequently challenged judgements in Indian impairment reviews, especially for retail chains, telecom circles, integrated steel and power plants, and multi-plant manufacturers.

Core notes

Definition and the general rule

A cash-generating unit is the smallest identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows that are largely independent of the cash inflows from other assets or groups of assets (Ind AS 36.6).

If there is any indication that an asset may be impaired, recoverable amount is estimated for the individual asset. If it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of the individual asset, the entity determines the recoverable amount of the CGU to which the asset belongs (Ind AS 36.66). The individual asset's recoverable amount cannot be determined if (Ind AS 36.67):

  • its VIU cannot be estimated to be close to its FVLCD; and
  • it does not generate cash inflows that are largely independent of those from other assets.

Factors for identifying CGUs (Ind AS 36.68–69)

Identification involves judgement. Consider:

  • Cash inflows, not net cash flows — the test is about independence of inflows from external parties;
  • how management monitors the entity's operations — by product lines, businesses, individual locations, districts or regional areas;
  • how management makes decisions about continuing or disposing of the entity's assets and operations;
  • the existence of separately identifiable revenue streams (separate customers, contracts, pricing).

Example (Ind AS 36 Illustrative Example 1 style): a mining company owning a private railway to support its mining — the railway has no independent inflows (scrap value only), so the CGU is the mine including the railway. A bus company with contracts for five routes where each route must be operated: the CGU is the five routes together, because the entity cannot curtail any one route.

Active-market output test (Ind AS 36.70–71)

If an active market exists for the output produced by an asset or group of assets, that asset or group is identified as a CGU even if some or all of the output is used internally. An active market is one in which transactions for the asset or liability take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing basis (Ind AS 113 definition).

Examples:

  • An integrated steel plant's pellet unit supplies the blast furnace, but pellets trade actively in the domestic market → the pellet unit can be a separate CGU.
  • A captive power plant supplying a smelter, where power can be sold on the Indian Energy Exchange (IEX) → potentially a separate CGU if output could be sold.
  • An in-house packaging unit with no external market for its specific output → not a separate CGU.

Internal transfer pricing effects (Ind AS 36.70)

If cash inflows of an asset or CGU are affected by internal transfer pricing, the entity uses management's best estimate of future prices that could be achieved in arm's length transactions in estimating:

  • the future cash inflows used to determine the VIU of the asset or CGU; and
  • the future cash outflows used to determine the VIU of any other assets or CGUs affected by the internal transfer pricing.

So, for the pellet unit, its VIU uses market prices for pellets, and the steel CGU's VIU uses market prices as its input cost — not the internal transfer price, which may be set for tax or management reasons.

Consistency (Ind AS 36.72)

CGUs are identified consistently from period to period for the same asset or types of assets, unless a change is justified (e.g. reorganisation, new reporting structure). If a change occurs and an impairment loss is recognised or reversed, disclose the change (Ind AS 36.130(d)(iii)).

Typical Indian CGU determinations

Industry Typical CGU Rationale
Retail / QSR Each store (sometimes cluster in a city if pricing and supply are interdependent) Separate customer inflows per store
Hotels Each hotel Separate inflows; managed individually
Telecom Entire national network or each licensed service area (circle) Network interdependence; pricing often national; licence by circle — judgement
Power generation Each plant (or each PPA-backed unit) Separate PPAs/merchant sales
Multi-plant manufacturing (same product, central sales) Group of plants if production is allocated centrally and customers served from any plant Inflows not independent per plant
Cement Each plant or regional cluster Freight limits markets; central dispatch decisions
Toll roads / annuity projects Each concession SPV Separate cash flows
Shipping Each vessel or fleet pool Pooling arrangements affect independence

Illustration

Kaveri Foods Ltd has three plants making biscuits (Plant A, B, C). Sales are made by a central sales team to distributors; production is allocated among plants by central planning based on capacity and freight. Each plant sells nothing directly. There is no active market for semi-finished dough.

Conclusion: individual plants do not have largely independent inflows; the three plants together form one CGU. If Plant C were idle and planned for closure, it would be tested individually (FVLCD) because its VIU would be negligible and FVLCD can be estimated (Ind AS 36.22, 36.67).

Contrast: Kaveri's dairy unit sells milk powder to external customers and also supplies the biscuit plants; an active market exists for milk powder → the dairy unit is a separate CGU, with VIU using market prices for internal supplies.

Documentation

A CGU memo should record: the asset map, inflow analysis (customers, contracts, pricing), management reporting and decision-making structure, active markets for intermediate products, transfer pricing adjustments, and changes from prior year.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Stores in a mall cluster sharing a single lease and management — may justify a cluster CGU, but avoid aggregating purely to absorb losses.
  • Telecom circles — licences are per circle but networks and pricing are national; Indian telecom companies have used both approaches; consistency and disclosure are key.
  • Vertical integration — cement plant with captive limestone mine: mine output usually has no active market (limestone is location-specific) → combined CGU.
  • Assets held for disposal — once sale is planned and Ind AS 105 criteria are not yet met, the asset may be tested individually at FVLCD.
  • Shared corporate assets (head office, IT platforms) are not CGUs — allocated to CGUs (s03).
  • Internal transfer prices set at cost-plus for tax purposes should not drive VIU — use arm's length estimates; this may also align with Sec 92BA domestic transfer pricing analyses.

Key takeaways

  • A CGU is the smallest group of assets with largely independent cash inflows.
  • Test individually where possible; otherwise test the CGU to which the asset belongs.
  • Consider management monitoring and decision-making, and separately identifiable revenue streams.
  • An active market for output makes a group a CGU even if output is used internally; use arm's length prices, not internal transfer prices, in VIU.
  • Identify CGUs consistently and disclose changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Retail chain CGU [L1]

A chain of 60 pharmacies has central procurement, uniform pricing and a shared loyalty programme, but each store has its own customers and P&L reviewed monthly. Determine the CGU and explain why central procurement does not change the conclusion.

Q2. Integrated steel plant [L2]

An integrated steel company operates a coke oven, sinter plant, blast furnace and rolling mill on one site. Coke and pig iron have active external markets; sinter does not. Identify the CGUs and explain how internal transfers of coke and pig iron are priced in each CGU's VIU.

💡 Hint: Apply the active-market test separately to each intermediate product.

Q3. Telecom CGU change [L3]

A telecom company has always treated its national network as a single CGU. After a reorganisation, circle heads now have P&L responsibility and pricing differs by circle. Analyse whether the CGU definition should change, the effect on impairment testing, and the disclosures if an impairment loss is recognised after the change.

Q4. Plants with central allocation [L2]

A tyre manufacturer has four plants; three serve the domestic replacement market through a central dispatch system, one is dedicated to an export customer under a 10-year contract. Identify the CGUs and justify your answer with Ind AS 36.68–69 factors.

Q5. Idle plant [L2]

A pharmaceutical company's API plant, part of a combined API–formulations CGU, has been idle for 18 months after a regulatory warning letter, and the Board has decided to sell it. Explain how the plant should be tested for impairment before it meets Ind AS 105 held-for-sale criteria.
