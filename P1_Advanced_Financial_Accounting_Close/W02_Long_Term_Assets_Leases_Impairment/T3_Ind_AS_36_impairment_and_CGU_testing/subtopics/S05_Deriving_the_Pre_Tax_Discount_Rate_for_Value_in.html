Subtopic note

Deriving the Pre-Tax Discount Rate for Value in Use

Scope: Pre-tax discount rate (36.55): post-tax WACC grossed up iteratively so pre-tax VIU equals post-tax VIU

Why it matters

Ind AS 36 requires VIU to be computed from pre-tax cash flows discounted at a pre-tax rate, and the pre-tax rate must be disclosed for goodwill CGUs (Ind AS 36.134(d)(v)). But markets only give us post-tax returns — CAPM, betas and WACC are all post-tax. Converting post-tax WACC to a pre-tax rate is therefore a mandatory step in every Indian impairment model, and simple gross-ups (dividing by one minus the tax rate) are generally wrong because they ignore the timing of tax cash flows and tax depreciation. Reviewers check that the disclosed pre-tax rate is internally consistent with the model.

Core notes

Requirement (Ind AS 36.55–57)

The discount rate (rates) shall be a pre-tax rate (rates) that reflect(s) current market assessments of:

  • (a) the time value of money; and
  • (b) the risks specific to the asset for which the future cash flow estimates have not been adjusted.

The rate is the return investors would require if they were to choose an investment that would generate cash flows of amounts, timing and risk profile equivalent to those the entity expects to derive from the asset (Ind AS 36.56). It is estimated from the rate implicit in current market transactions for similar assets or from the WACC of a listed entity with a single asset (or portfolio) similar in service potential and risks (Ind AS 36.56).

Ind AS 36 Appendix A (A15–A21) gives guidance: as a starting point, the entity may take into account its WACC (determined using techniques such as CAPM), its incremental borrowing rate and other market borrowing rates; adjust these to reflect the way the market would assess the specific risks and to exclude risks not relevant or already adjusted in cash flows. The discount rate is independent of the entity's capital structure and the way the entity financed the purchase of the asset (A19). Different rates may be used for different periods if VIU is sensitive to risk differences or the term structure (A21).

Why a simple gross-up is wrong

Because VIU excludes tax cash flows (Ind AS 36.50), the rate must be pre-tax. A common shortcut is:

\[ r_{\text{pre}} \approx \frac{WACC_{\text{post}}}{1 - t} \]

This is accurate only when there are no temporary differences, tax cash flows are proportional to pre-tax cash flows and the cash flows are a level perpetuity. In practice, tax depreciation (Sec 32 WDV), tax holidays, MAT/AMT credits, Sec 115BAA rates and carry-forward losses change the timing of tax payments, so the gross-up mis-states the rate. The IAS 36 Basis for Conclusions (BCZ85) explains that the pre-tax rate is the rate that, applied to pre-tax cash flows, gives the same result as discounting post-tax cash flows at the post-tax rate.

Iterative method

  1. Build post-tax cash flows: pre-tax VIU cash flows less notional tax computed on the CGU's own taxable profits using the tax base of the assets (tax depreciation on the CGU's assets, not the entity's actual tax position) and the statutory rate (e.g. 25.168% under Sec 115BAA). Include a terminal-year tax with normalised tax depreciation.
  2. Discount post-tax cash flows at the post-tax WACC → post-tax VIU \( V^{*} \).
  3. Solve for \( r_{\text{pre}} \) such that discounting the pre-tax cash flows (including pre-tax terminal value) at \( r_{\text{pre}} \) gives \( V^{*} \) — using Excel Goal Seek or iteration.
  4. Use \( r_{\text{pre}} \) in the VIU model; disclose it.

The recoverable amount is \( V^{*} \) either way — the iterative pre-tax rate is the disclosed and consistent rate. Notional tax uses the tax base of assets because the VIU should reflect the asset's own tax profile, not group tax planning.

Worked illustration

Konark Chemicals Ltd CGU (₹ crore). Pre-tax VIU cash flows FY27–FY31: 100, 105, 110, 115, 120; terminal growth 4%; tax depreciation on the CGU's assets: 40, 36, 32, 29, 26 (terminal year 27.04, growing at 4%); tax rate 25.168%; post-tax WACC 11%.

Year Pre-tax CF Tax depreciation Notional tax Post-tax CF
FY27 100.00 40.00 15.10 84.90
FY28 105.00 36.00 17.37 87.63
FY29 110.00 32.00 19.63 90.37
FY30 115.00 29.00 21.64 93.36
FY31 120.00 26.00 23.66 96.34
Terminal (FY32) 124.80 27.04 24.60 100.20

Post-tax VIU at 11%: PV of explicit = 332.36; TV = 100.20 ÷ (0.11 − 0.04) = 1,431.37, PV = 849.45; \( V^{*} \) = ₹1,181.81 crore.

Goal Seek: find \( r \) such that \( \sum_{t=1}^{5} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} + \frac{124.80/(r-0.04)}{(1+r)^5} = 1,181.81 \). Solution: \( r_{\text{pre}} \approx 12.65\% \).

Simple gross-up: 11% ÷ (1 − 0.25168) = 14.70%. At 14.70%, pre-tax VIU ≈ ₹954 crore — understating recoverable amount by about ₹228 crore and potentially triggering a spurious impairment. The gross-up ignores that tax depreciation keeps notional tax well below 25.168% of pre-tax cash flow. In other fact patterns (e.g. assets with little remaining tax base), the gross-up can understate the rate instead.

Excel implementation

Post-tax VIU:  =NPV(WACC, PostTaxCF) + (PostTaxCF_T/(WACC-g))/(1+WACC)^5
Pre-tax VIU:   =NPV(PreTaxRate, PreTaxCF) + (PreTaxCF_T/(PreTaxRate-g))/(1+PreTaxRate)^5
Difference:    =PreTaxVIU - PostTaxVIU
Goal Seek:     Set cell = Difference, To value = 0, By changing = PreTaxRate

Avoid circularity by storing the goal-seek result as a value and re-running whenever inputs change (or use a small VBA routine). Record the pre-tax rate per CGU in the disclosure table.

Building the post-tax WACC (Indian inputs)

\[ WACC = k_e \times \frac{E}{D+E} + k_d \times (1-t) \times \frac{D}{D+E}, \qquad k_e = R_f + \beta_L \times ERP + \text{premia} \]

with \( R_f \) = 10-year G-sec yield, \( \beta_L \) relevered from NSE peer betas at a market-participant target gearing, ERP for India, size/company-specific premia only where justified and not double-counted in cash flows, and \( k_d \) a market pre-tax cost of debt for the target rating. The WACC must reflect market-participant (not entity-specific) capital structure (Appendix A19).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Tax losses and holidays — if the CGU benefits from a tax holiday (e.g. Sec 80-IA legacy, SEZ Sec 10AA), notional tax reflects it, which changes the pre-tax rate; disclose.
  • Sec 115BAA vs old regime — use the rate the CGU's profits would bear.
  • Different rates per period — if the explicit period carries higher risk (e.g. ramp-up), a rate term structure is allowed (A21).
  • Country risk for foreign CGUs — use local-currency rates and local tax.
  • Lease approach — if lease payments are excluded from cash flows (ROU in carrying amount, liability not deducted), WACC must reflect lease-inclusive leverage; if lease payments are included, use a pre-IFRS 16-style WACC — consistency matters.
  • Disclosure — disclose the pre-tax rate even if the computation was done post-tax.

Key takeaways

  • VIU uses a pre-tax rate reflecting time value and asset-specific risks not adjusted in cash flows, independent of the entity's capital structure.
  • Start from a market-participant post-tax WACC built from Indian inputs.
  • The pre-tax rate is the rate that equates pre-tax VIU to post-tax VIU — solve iteratively with notional tax based on the assets' tax base.
  • Dividing WACC by (1 − t) is only an approximation and can mis-state recoverable amount materially.
  • Disclose the pre-tax rate for each goodwill CGU.

Practice Questions

Q1. Gross-up vs iteration [L2]

For a CGU with level pre-tax cash flows of ₹50 crore in perpetuity (no growth), tax rate 25.168% and no temporary differences (tax depreciation equals the cash cost of maintenance capex), post-tax WACC 10.5%, show that the simple gross-up gives the correct pre-tax rate. Then explain why the result changes if tax depreciation in the first five years is ₹15 crore above the steady state.

💡 Hint: In a level perpetuity with tax proportional to pre-tax cash flow, post-tax VIU = pre-tax CF × (1 − t) ÷ WACC.

Q2. Goal seek model build [L3]

Build the specification for an Excel tab that computes post-tax VIU with notional tax (using tax WDV of the CGU's assets at 15% Sec 32 rates on a block of ₹600 crore), derives the pre-tax rate with goal seek, and produces the Ind AS 36.134 disclosure line (pre-tax rate, growth rate, period). List inputs, calculations and checks.

Q3. WACC build-up [L2]

Construct a post-tax WACC for an Indian cement CGU using: 10-year G-sec 6.5%; ERP 7%; unlevered peer beta 0.85; target D/E 30:70; pre-tax cost of debt 8.8%; tax rate 25.168%. Show relevered beta, cost of equity and WACC, and discuss whether a size premium should be added.

Q4. Capital structure independence [L2]

The CFO argues that because the company is debt-free, the discount rate for VIU should be its cost of equity only. Respond with reference to Ind AS 36.55–56 and Appendix A19.

Q5. Disclosure review [L1]

A draft note discloses "discount rate 11.2% (post-tax)" for a goodwill CGU. Explain what is missing under Ind AS 36.134 and what additional information should be presented.
