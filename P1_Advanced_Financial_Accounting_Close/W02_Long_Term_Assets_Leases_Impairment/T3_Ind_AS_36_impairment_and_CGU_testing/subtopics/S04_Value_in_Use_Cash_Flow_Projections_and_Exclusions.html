Subtopic note

Value-in-Use Cash Flow Projections and Exclusions

Scope: VIU projections: budgets ≤5 years (36.33), extrapolation growth, terminal value; exclude uncommitted restructuring, enhancements (36.44), financing and tax (36.50)

Why it matters

Value in use is an entity-specific DCF, but it is not a free-form valuation. Ind AS 36 restricts the cash flows: they must be based on reasonable and supportable assumptions, on the most recent Board-approved budgets covering at most five years, for the asset in its current condition, and before financing and tax. FP&A teams typically supply the budget; the technical accounting team must then strip out what Ind AS 36 does not allow. Auditors focus on these exclusions, on the growth rate used beyond the budget and on the terminal value, which usually forms 60–80% of VIU.

Core notes

Basis for estimates (Ind AS 36.33)

In measuring VIU an entity shall:

  • (a) base cash flow projections on reasonable and supportable assumptions representing management's best estimate of the range of economic conditions over the remaining useful life, giving greater weight to external evidence;
  • (b) base projections on the most recent financial budgets/forecasts approved by management, excluding cash flows from future restructurings or from improving or enhancing performance; projections based on budgets/forecasts shall cover a maximum period of five years, unless a longer period can be justified;
  • (c) estimate cash flows beyond the budget period by extrapolating using a steady or declining growth rate for subsequent years, unless an increasing rate can be justified; this growth rate shall not exceed the long-term average growth rate for the products, industries or country in which the entity operates, or for the market in which the asset is used, unless a higher rate can be justified.

Management assesses the reasonableness of assumptions by examining the causes of differences between past projections and actual cash flows (Ind AS 36.34) — a budget-accuracy back-test is expected evidence.

Longer than five years is justifiable where forecasts are reliable and management has a demonstrated ability to forecast over longer periods — e.g. a toll road or power plant with contracted cash flows over a concession or PPA term (Ind AS 36.35).

Composition of cash flows (Ind AS 36.39–43)

Include:

  • projections of cash inflows from continuing use of the asset/CGU;
  • cash outflows necessarily incurred to generate those inflows, including outflows to prepare the asset for use (for assets under construction) and day-to-day servicing / maintenance capex, and overheads that can be directly attributed or allocated on a reasonable and consistent basis;
  • net cash flows to be received/paid on disposal at the end of useful life (Ind AS 36.52–53).

Exclude:

  • outflows relating to obligations already recognised as liabilities (e.g. payables, pensions, provisions), unless the liabilities are deducted consistently (Ind AS 36.43(b), 36.78);
  • inflows from assets that generate inflows largely independent of the asset under review (e.g. financial assets such as receivables — typically excluded consistently with carrying amount).

Cash flows reflect general inflation if the discount rate includes inflation (nominal); otherwise real (Ind AS 36.40).

Current-condition rule (Ind AS 36.44–49)

Future cash flows are estimated for the asset in its current condition. VIU shall not include estimated future cash inflows or outflows expected to arise from:

  • (a) a future restructuring to which the entity is not yet committed — commitment means an Ind AS 37 restructuring provision has been recognised (detailed formal plan and valid expectation). Once committed, VIU includes the cost savings and the restructuring outflows (the latter already in a provision, so excluded consistently) (Ind AS 36.46–47);
  • (b) improving or enhancing the asset's performance — e.g. capacity expansion, debottlenecking, new product lines requiring capex. Until the enhancement capex is incurred, its benefits are excluded (Ind AS 36.48).

Maintenance capex necessary to maintain the current level of performance (replacing components, overhauls) is included (Ind AS 36.49). The distinction between maintenance and enhancement capex is a key review point — budgets usually blend them.

Financing and tax (Ind AS 36.50–51)

VIU cash flows shall not include:

  • (a) cash inflows or outflows from financing activities — interest, debt repayments, dividends; and (under the non-deduction approach) lease payments;
  • (b) income tax receipts or payments.

The discount rate is determined on a pre-tax basis accordingly (s05). Excluding tax avoids double counting and makes VIU independent of tax structure; excluding financing reflects that the time value of money is captured in the discount rate.

Terminal value

Most CGUs have indefinite lives (or lives far beyond five years), so cash flows after the budget period are captured by a terminal value, usually the Gordon growth model:

\[ TV_n = \frac{CF_{n+1}}{r - g} \]

where \( CF_{n+1} = CF_n \times (1+g) \) is a normalised year (steady-state capex equal to depreciation-like maintenance levels, normalised working capital, normalised margins), \( r \) is the pre-tax discount rate and \( g \) the long-term growth rate. Indian practice: \( g \) typically 3–5% nominal (aligned with long-term inflation of about 4% and real growth), not the 8–10% nominal GDP growth for a mature CGU unless justified. Exit multiples are an FVLCD technique and generally not appropriate for VIU.

For finite-life CGUs (concessions, leases without renewal, mines), project to the end of life with disposal proceeds — no perpetuity.

Excel implementation

=CF_t/(1+r)^t                              ' end-year discounting
=CF_t/(1+r)^(t-0.5)                        ' mid-year convention (if cash flows arise evenly)
=CF_n*(1+g)/(r-g)/(1+r)^n                  ' PV of Gordon TV
=NPV(r, CF_range) + TV/(1+r)^n             ' compact form

Keep separate rows for budget cash flows, "Ind AS 36 adjustments" (remove enhancements, uncommitted restructuring, tax, financing, lease payments if applicable) and VIU cash flows, so reviewers can trace from budget to VIU.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Assets under construction — include outflows to complete the asset (Ind AS 36.42), but not to enhance beyond the original plan.
  • Committed restructuring — once provided under Ind AS 37, include savings; the provision outflows are excluded because the provision is a recognised liability.
  • Working capital — include changes in working capital in cash flows only if working capital is in the carrying amount (consistency).
  • Foreign currency cash flows — estimate in the foreign currency, discount at a rate for that currency, translate at the spot rate at the VIU date (Ind AS 36.54).
  • Budget not yet Board-approved at year-end — use the most recent approved budget, updated for known changes; document.
  • Terminal year normalisation — capex in the terminal year should support the assumed growth; avoid a terminal year with abnormally low capex or high margins.
  • Corporate overheads allocated reasonably and consistently are included as outflows.

Key takeaways

  • VIU uses Board-approved budgets for up to five years, then a steady or declining growth rate not exceeding long-term averages.
  • Cash flows are for the asset in its current condition: exclude uncommitted restructuring and enhancement benefits; include maintenance capex.
  • Exclude financing and income tax cash flows; use a pre-tax rate.
  • Terminal value usually dominates VIU — normalise the terminal year and justify growth.
  • Keep carrying amount and cash flows consistent for working capital, provisions and leases.

Practice Questions

Q1. Strip the budget [L2]

A hospital CGU's budget for FY27–FY31 includes: EBITDA 80, 95, 110, 120, 128 (₹ crore); capex 15 per year (maintenance) plus 60 in FY28 for a new oncology wing adding EBITDA of 12 from FY29; working capital increase 3 per year; tax paid 15–25; interest 10. Working capital is included in the CGU carrying amount. Prepare VIU cash flows for FY27–FY31 under Ind AS 36, listing every adjustment.

Q2. Compute VIU with terminal value [L2]

Pre-tax VIU cash flows: FY27 50, FY28 54, FY29 57, FY30 60, FY31 62 (₹ crore); terminal growth 4%; pre-tax rate 13.5%. Compute VIU using end-year and mid-year conventions and the TV share of VIU in each.

💡 Hint: Under the mid-year convention, discount each year's cash flow by half a period less, including the terminal value consistently.

Q3. Growth rate challenge [L3]

Management proposes 9% perpetual growth for a branded snacks CGU, citing India's nominal GDP growth of 10–11%. Prepare a reviewer's challenge using Ind AS 36.33(c), considering inflation, category penetration, competitive intensity and the effect on VIU at a 13% pre-tax rate (show the TV multiple at 9% vs 5%).

Q4. Restructuring timing [L2]

A board approved a plan in February 2026 to close two plants and consolidate production, expected to save ₹30 crore annually from FY28; the plan was announced to employees and unions on 20 April 2026, after the 31 March 2026 reporting date but before approval of the financial statements. Should the savings be included in VIU at 31 March 2026? Explain with reference to Ind AS 36.44–47 and Ind AS 37.

Q5. Contracted cash flows beyond five years [L2]

A solar power SPV has a 25-year PPA with a state DISCOM at a fixed tariff, 19 years remaining. Explain whether projections beyond five years can be used, how terminal value should be treated, and which risks the discount rate must reflect.
