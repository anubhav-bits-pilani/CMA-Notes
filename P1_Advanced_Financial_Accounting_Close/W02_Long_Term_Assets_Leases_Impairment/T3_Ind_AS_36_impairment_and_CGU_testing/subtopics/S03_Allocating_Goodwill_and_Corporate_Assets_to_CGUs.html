Subtopic note

Allocating Goodwill and Corporate Assets to CGUs

Scope: Goodwill allocation to CGUs/groups no larger than an operating segment (36.80); corporate asset allocation (36.102)

Why it matters

Goodwill and corporate assets (head office, shared IT platforms, R&D centres, brand assets serving multiple units) do not generate independent cash flows, so they can only be tested by allocating them to CGUs. The level of allocation determines whether goodwill from a struggling acquisition is shielded by healthy legacy businesses. Indian acquirers — FMCG, pharma, IT services, cement consolidators — disclose goodwill by CGU group; auditors and NFRA check that allocation matches management monitoring and the Ind AS 108 segment structure. Corporate asset allocation is also where shared costs and assets most often go missing from CGU carrying amounts.

Core notes

Goodwill allocation (Ind AS 36.80–87)

For impairment testing, goodwill acquired in a business combination shall, from the acquisition date, be allocated to each of the acquirer's CGUs, or groups of CGUs, that is expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination, irrespective of whether other assets or liabilities of the acquiree are assigned to those units (Ind AS 36.80). Each unit or group to which goodwill is so allocated shall:

  • (a) represent the lowest level within the entity at which the goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes; and
  • (b) not be larger than an operating segment as defined by paragraph 5 of Ind AS 108, before aggregation.

Points to note:

  • Goodwill may be allocated to the acquirer's existing CGUs that benefit from synergies, not only to the acquiree's CGUs.
  • "Monitored" relates to goodwill monitoring by management — often at segment or sub-segment level. Entities that do not monitor goodwill still cannot go above the operating segment level.
  • Initial allocation must be completed before the end of the first annual period beginning after the acquisition date (Ind AS 36.84). If PPA is provisional, allocation may also be provisional.
  • Disposal of an operation within a CGU with goodwill: goodwill associated with the disposed operation is included in its carrying amount for gain/loss, measured on relative values of the operation disposed and the portion retained, unless another method better reflects it (Ind AS 36.86).
  • Reorganisation changing the composition of CGUs: reallocate goodwill using a relative value approach similar to disposals (Ind AS 36.87).
  • Non-controlling interests: if NCI is measured at proportionate share of net assets (partial goodwill), gross up goodwill for impairment testing to include the notional goodwill attributable to NCI, then allocate any loss between parent and NCI (Ind AS 36 Appendix C, C3–C9).

Testing sequence with goodwill (Ind AS 36.88–99)

  • A CGU without goodwill but with an indicator is tested on its own (Ind AS 36.88).
  • A CGU (or group) with goodwill is tested annually and whenever there is an indicator (Ind AS 36.90).
  • If CGUs within a group to which goodwill is allocated have indicators, test those individual CGUs first (without goodwill), recognise any losses, then test the group including goodwill (Ind AS 36.97–98) — "bottom-up".
  • Detailed recoverable amount calculations from a previous period may be carried forward if specific conditions are met: composition unchanged, large headroom, remote likelihood of impairment (Ind AS 36.99).

Corporate assets (Ind AS 36.100–103)

Corporate assets are assets other than goodwill that contribute to the future cash flows of both the CGU under review and other CGUs (Ind AS 36.6) — e.g. head office building, central IT/ERP, R&D centre, shared distribution centre.

When testing a CGU, identify all corporate assets that relate to it (Ind AS 36.102):

  • (a) if a portion of the corporate asset can be allocated on a reasonable and consistent basis to that unit, compare the CGU's carrying amount including the allocated portion with its recoverable amount;
  • (b) if a portion cannot be allocated on a reasonable and consistent basis:
  • (i) compare the carrying amount of the unit, excluding the corporate asset, with its recoverable amount and recognise any loss;
  • (ii) identify the smallest group of CGUs that includes the unit under review and to which a portion of the corporate asset can be allocated reasonably and consistently; and
  • (iii) compare the group's carrying amount including the allocated corporate asset with its recoverable amount.

Allocation bases: carrying amounts weighted by remaining useful lives (Ind AS 36 Illustrative Example 8), revenue, headcount, floor space, or usage data. Corporate costs (head office expenses) must be consistently treated: if the corporate asset is allocated, include an allocation of related corporate overheads in VIU cash outflows (Ind AS 36.41).

Worked illustration — corporate asset allocation

Narmada Retail Ltd has three CGUs and a head office (HQ) with carrying amount ₹150 crore that supports all three. Allocation basis: carrying amount weighted by remaining useful life.

Worked illustration — goodwill monitoring level

Vista Consumer Ltd acquired a personal-care company in FY2023; goodwill ₹600 crore. Management reviews performance through two operating segments (Personal Care, Home Care). Within Personal Care, the CODM reviews three brand clusters monthly, but goodwill is tracked at the Personal Care level in the acquisition business case. Synergies (distribution cost savings) benefit both the acquired brands and the existing hair-care brands.

Conclusion: allocate goodwill to the Personal Care segment group (the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored), including the existing hair-care CGU that benefits from synergies. It cannot be tested at the entity level combining Home Care, which is a separate operating segment.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Segment changes: if Ind AS 108 segments are redefined, goodwill groups may need to be split and reallocated on relative values.
  • Acquired brands used across CGUs can be corporate assets or indefinite-life intangibles tested individually if FVLCD is measurable (relief-from-royalty).
  • Central IT platforms that cannot be allocated reasonably → test at the smallest group where allocation is possible, often the entity.
  • Corporate costs excluded from CGU cash flows while corporate assets are allocated → inconsistent; VIU overstated.
  • Goodwill in the parent's separate financial statements does not exist (investment at cost); test the investment instead.
  • Goodwill relating to deferred tax on fair value uplifts — consider including DTLs in carrying amounts consistently with recoverable amount (FVLCD perspective).
  • Partial goodwill gross-up — only the parent's share of the loss on goodwill is recognised.

Key takeaways

  • Allocate goodwill to CGUs or groups expected to benefit from synergies, at the lowest level monitored, never above an operating segment before aggregation.
  • Complete allocation before the end of the first annual period after acquisition.
  • Test lower-level CGUs with indicators first, then the goodwill group (bottom-up).
  • Allocate corporate assets on a reasonable and consistent basis; otherwise test at the smallest group where allocation is possible.
  • Corporate overheads in VIU must be consistent with corporate asset allocation.
  • Gross up partial goodwill for NCI before testing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Goodwill allocation level [L2]

An IT services company acquired a cloud consultancy in FY2025, creating goodwill of ₹1,100 crore. It has three operating segments (BFSI, Manufacturing, Retail); the acquired firm's revenue is spread across all three, and the CODM reviews segment performance only. Recommend the goodwill allocation and testing level, and explain what would change if the company monitored goodwill only at the entity level.

Q2. Corporate asset not allocable [L2]

A company's ERP platform (carrying amount ₹80 crore) serves four CGUs but no reasonable allocation basis exists for CGU-level testing; it can be allocated reasonably to the entity as a whole. CGU 3 has an indicator: carrying amount ₹260 crore, recoverable ₹230 crore. Entity-level carrying amount excluding ERP is ₹1,400 crore; entity recoverable amount ₹1,450 crore. Perform the two-step test under Ind AS 36.102(b).

💡 Hint: First test CGU 3 without the ERP, then test the smallest group that can absorb the ERP allocation, after recognising CGU 3's loss.

Q3. Disposal of an operation with goodwill [L2]

A CGU with goodwill of ₹90 crore and recoverable amount of ₹900 crore sells an operation with fair value ₹180 crore; the retained portion has a recoverable amount of ₹720 crore. Compute the goodwill to include in the carrying amount of the operation disposed and the goodwill remaining for testing.

Q4. Partial goodwill gross-up [L3]

A parent acquired 70% of a subsidiary, measuring NCI at proportionate share of net assets; recognised goodwill is ₹140 crore. The subsidiary is a CGU with identifiable net assets of ₹500 crore and recoverable amount of ₹580 crore. Compute the impairment loss, the portion recognised in the consolidated statements, and its attribution between owners of the parent and NCI.

Q5. Corporate allocation with life weighting [L2]

Using the weighted carrying amount approach, allocate a ₹60 crore R&D centre to CGUs X (₹200 crore, 8 years), Y (₹350 crore, 12 years) and Z (₹150 crore, 4 years), and compute impairment if recoverable amounts are X ₹230 crore, Y ₹360 crore, Z ₹150 crore.
