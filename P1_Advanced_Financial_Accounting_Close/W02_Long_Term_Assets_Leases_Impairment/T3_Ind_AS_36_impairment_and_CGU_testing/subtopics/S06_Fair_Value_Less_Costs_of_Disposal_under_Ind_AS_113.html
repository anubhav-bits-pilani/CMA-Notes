Subtopic note

Fair Value Less Costs of Disposal under Ind AS 113

Scope: FVLCD via Ind AS 113: market multiples, market-participant DCF, costs of disposal

Why it matters

Recoverable amount is the higher of VIU and FVLCD, so FVLCD is the second route to avoiding — or measuring — an impairment. It matters most where VIU is constrained: when management plans enhancements or restructuring a buyer would also pursue, when the asset is idle or held for disposal, or when the entity's own cash flows understate what a market participant would earn. Indian listed groups frequently use FVLCD for goodwill CGUs with growth plans, for real estate and hotels, and for investments in subsidiaries in separate financial statements. Because FVLCD is fair value, Ind AS 113's hierarchy and disclosure regime apply.

Core notes

Definitions

  • Fair value: the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (Ind AS 113.9; Ind AS 36.6).
  • Costs of disposal: incremental costs directly attributable to the disposal of an asset or CGU, excluding finance costs and income tax expense (Ind AS 36.6).
  • FVLCD = fair value − costs of disposal.

It is not always necessary to determine both FVLCD and VIU: if either exceeds carrying amount, the asset is not impaired (Ind AS 36.19). FVLCD can be measured even if there is no quoted price; if it cannot be measured reliably, recoverable amount is VIU (Ind AS 36.20).

Ind AS 113 framework applied

  • Unit of account: the CGU (or asset) as defined in Ind AS 36.
  • Market participants: buyers that are independent, knowledgeable, able and willing — e.g. strategic peers or financial investors in the Indian market.
  • Highest and best use (for non-financial assets): physically possible, legally permissible and financially feasible use — e.g. a mill on urban land may have a higher value as real estate redevelopment if rezoning is legally permissible.
  • Valuation techniques (Ind AS 113.62): market approach, income approach, cost approach — use those appropriate and maximise observable inputs.
  • Fair value hierarchy (Ind AS 113.72–90): Level 1 quoted prices for identical assets; Level 2 observable inputs; Level 3 unobservable inputs. Most CGU FVLCDs are Level 3.

Technique 1 — Market multiples

FVLCD estimated by applying multiples from comparable listed companies or transactions:

\[ FV_{\text{EV}} = \text{Multiple} \times \text{Metric} \]

  • Trading multiples: EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/E of NSE/BSE-listed peers, adjusted for size, growth, margins and liquidity.
  • Transaction multiples: recent M&A deals in the sector (Indian cement, pharma, hospital deals) — embed control premiums.
  • Enterprise vs equity: an EV multiple gives the value of operating assets; to compare with CGU carrying amount (operating assets), use EV; if carrying amount includes or deducts debt-like items, adjust consistently.
  • Control premium: a CGU is usually sold as a controlling unit; trading multiples reflect minority interests, so a control premium may be justified, supported by evidence of synergies a market participant would obtain.
  • Post-Ind AS 116 consistency: EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 (excludes rent), so EV must include lease liabilities when comparing.

Technique 2 — Market-participant DCF

An income-approach DCF differs from VIU:

Feature VIU (Ind AS 36) FVLCD DCF (Ind AS 113)
Perspective Entity-specific Market participant
Restructuring and enhancements Excluded unless committed / incurred Included if a market participant would undertake them
Tax Pre-tax cash flows, pre-tax rate Usually post-tax cash flows, post-tax rate (market convention)
Discount rate Pre-tax asset-specific Market-participant post-tax WACC
Budget limit Max 5 years unless justified No such limit; market-participant assumptions
Synergies Entity's own Only those available to market participants generally
Costs of disposal Not deducted Deducted

Market-participant synergies and tax amortisation benefits (for intangible assets) may be considered. Assumptions must be those a buyer would use — not management's own optimistic plans.

Technique 3 — Cost approach and other

For specialised assets with no market and no separate cash flows (e.g. specialised plant within an otherwise healthy CGU), depreciated replacement cost may be used, though for impairment the cost approach is more relevant to individual assets than CGUs. For land and buildings, market comparables (circle rates are not fair value; use transacted prices adjusted for location) and income capitalisation.

Costs of disposal

Include: legal costs, stamp duty and similar transaction taxes, costs of removing the asset, direct incremental costs to bring the asset into condition for sale (Ind AS 36.28). Exclude: finance costs, income tax, termination benefits (Ind AS 19) and costs associated with reorganising a business following disposal (Ind AS 36.28). In India: stamp duty on asset transfers, advisory and banker fees, due diligence, registration charges.

Worked illustration

Sahyadri Hospitals Ltd's Nashik hospital CGU (carrying amount ₹410 crore, including ROU, lease liability not deducted). VIU (current condition) = ₹380 crore. Management evaluates FVLCD.

Market approach: listed hospital peers trade at EV/EBITDA 18–22×; recent transactions for single-city hospitals 14–17×. Nashik hospital EBITDA (post-Ind AS 116) ₹28 crore. Applying 15× (single-asset, tier-2 city discount) gives EV ₹420 crore. Lease liability (₹45 crore) is debt-like and must be consistent: because the CGU carrying amount includes ROU and excludes the lease liability, compare EV (which includes lease liability value) directly with carrying amount.

Market-participant DCF: includes a planned 100-bed expansion any buyer would undertake (capex ₹60 crore; incremental EBITDA ₹12 crore from year 3), post-tax WACC 12%, terminal growth 5% → EV ₹445 crore.

Weighting/triangulation → fair value ₹430 crore; costs of disposal (advisers 1%, stamp duty on business transfer 0.5%) ≈ ₹6.5 crore → FVLCD ₹423.5 crore.

Recoverable amount = max(380, 423.5) = ₹423.5 crore > ₹410 crore → no impairment. Disclosure: FVLCD, Level 3, techniques, key assumptions (multiples, WACC, growth) and sensitivity (Ind AS 36.134(e)).

Disclosures (Ind AS 36.130(f), 36.134(e))

When recoverable amount is FVLCD: the level of the fair value hierarchy; for Level 2 and 3, the valuation technique(s) and any changes; key assumptions and the discount rate(s) if a present value technique is used; management's approach to determining the values assigned to each key assumption.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Quoted price for a listed subsidiary — Level 1 price × shares (unit of account is the investment); a control premium may not be added to Level 1 prices for the investment unit — debated; for the CGU unit, a premium may be justified with evidence.
  • Binding sale agreement — the best evidence of FVLCD is the price in a binding agreement in an arm's length transaction, adjusted for incremental disposal costs.
  • Distressed sales are not orderly transactions — adjust or exclude.
  • Using VIU-like inputs in FVLCD — if the DCF is entity-specific, it is not FVLCD; label correctly.
  • Deferred tax in FVLCD — buyers may price tax attributes; consider including DTL in carrying amount consistently.
  • Circle rates/ready reckoner rates for land are statutory valuations for stamp duty — not fair value, though they may be inputs.

Key takeaways

  • FVLCD = Ind AS 113 fair value (market participant, orderly transaction, highest and best use) minus incremental disposal costs.
  • Use market multiples (trading and transaction), market-participant DCF and, for some assets, cost approaches — maximising observable inputs.
  • FVLCD may include market-participant enhancements and restructurings that VIU excludes, usually on a post-tax basis.
  • Costs of disposal exclude finance costs, income tax, termination benefits and post-disposal reorganisation costs.
  • Disclose hierarchy level, techniques, key assumptions and discount rates.

Practice Questions

Q1. Multiples-based FVLCD [L2]

A cement CGU with 5 MTPA capacity has EBITDA of ₹420 crore (post-Ind AS 116) and carrying amount (including ROU, excluding lease liability of ₹60 crore) of ₹5,200 crore. Recent Indian cement transactions were at an EV of ₹1,100–1,300 crore per MTPA of capacity (EV/tonne approach) and listed peers trade at EV/EBITDA 13–16×. Estimate FVLCD (costs of disposal 1.2%) using both approaches, explain the adjustments for the lease liability and conclude on impairment.

💡 Hint: Make sure the EV you compare includes or excludes the lease liability consistently with the carrying amount.

Q2. VIU vs FVLCD DCF [L2]

List five differences in assumptions between a VIU and an FVLCD DCF for a pharma formulations CGU that plans to launch new products requiring ₹200 crore capex over three years, and explain which recoverable amount is likely to be higher and why.

Q3. Costs of disposal [L1]

Classify each as an includible cost of disposal or not: investment banker's fee; stamp duty on transfer; VRS payments to employees of the disposed unit; legal fees for the share purchase agreement; capital gains tax; costs of relocating head office staff after disposal; interest on a bridge loan.

Q4. Highest and best use [L3]

A textile mill on 25 acres in a metro suburb is a loss-making CGU; the land has been rezoned for residential use, and developers have paid ₹40 crore per acre for nearby land. Explain how highest and best use affects FVLCD, the unit of account considerations, and whether the CGU is impaired if its carrying amount is ₹650 crore and VIU ₹180 crore.

Q5. Level 3 disclosure drafting [L2]

Draft the Ind AS 36.134(e) disclosure for a goodwill CGU whose recoverable amount was determined using a market-participant DCF with post-tax WACC 11.8%, terminal growth 5%, EBITDA margin 18–21%, and a sensitivity showing that a 70 bp increase in WACC would eliminate headroom.
