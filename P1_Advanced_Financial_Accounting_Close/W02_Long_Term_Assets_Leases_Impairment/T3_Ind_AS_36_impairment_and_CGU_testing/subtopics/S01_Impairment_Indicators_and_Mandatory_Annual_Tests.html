Subtopic note

Impairment Indicators and Mandatory Annual Tests

Scope: Indicators (Ind AS 36.12): market cap below net assets, rate increases, adverse regulation, obsolescence, underperformance; annual tests for goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles (36.10)

Why it matters

Impairment testing starts with a trigger assessment at each reporting date. Missing a trigger means an overstated balance sheet and a delayed, larger loss later; auditors and NFRA routinely challenge companies whose market capitalisation sits below book value with no impairment test. Conversely, goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles must be tested every year regardless of triggers. For FP&A and controllership roles, the trigger memo is a quarterly close deliverable, and indicators such as G-sec yield increases, adverse tariff orders or plant underutilisation must be monitored continuously.

Core notes

The two requirements

Indicator-based testing (Ind AS 36.9): at the end of each reporting period, assess whether there is any indication that an asset may be impaired. If any indication exists, estimate the recoverable amount. If none exists, no estimate is required (except for the annual tests below).

Mandatory annual testing (Ind AS 36.10): irrespective of indicators, an entity shall also:

  • test an intangible asset with an indefinite useful life, or an intangible asset not yet available for use, annually by comparing carrying amount with recoverable amount — at any time during the year, provided at the same time every year (different intangibles may be tested at different times); if initially recognised during the current year, test before the end of the year;
  • test goodwill acquired in a business combination annually (Ind AS 36.90), at the same time every year; if acquired in the current year, test before the end of that year (Ind AS 36.96).

Indian examples of indefinite-life intangibles: acquired brands with no foreseeable limit (e.g. consumer brands), certain licences renewable at negligible cost. Intangibles under development include software, product development and pharma pipeline (IPR&D) assets.

Indicators in Ind AS 36.12 (minimum list)

External sources:

  • (a) observable indications that the asset's value has declined during the period significantly more than expected from time or normal use;
  • (b) significant changes with an adverse effect in the technological, market, economic or legal environment in which the entity operates or in the market to which the asset is dedicated — e.g. adverse regulation (tariff orders, spectrum pricing, ban on a product, change in import duty, environmental restrictions), technological disruption;
  • (c) market interest rates or other market rates of return have increased during the period, and those increases are likely to affect the discount rate used in calculating VIU and decrease recoverable amount materially;
  • (d) the carrying amount of net assets of the entity is more than its market capitalisation.

Internal sources:

  • (e) evidence of obsolescence or physical damage;
  • (f) significant adverse changes in the extent or manner of use — asset becoming idle, plans to discontinue or restructure the operation, plans to dispose before the previously expected date, reassessing useful life of an intangible from indefinite to finite;
  • (g) evidence from internal reporting that economic performance is or will be worse than expected — actual cash flows or operating profit significantly worse than budget, net cash outflows or losses when current and budgeted figures are aggregated (Ind AS 36.14).

Dividends from subsidiaries, JVs, associates (separate financial statements, Ind AS 36.12(h)): dividend exceeds total comprehensive income of the investee in the period, or the carrying amount of the investment in separate statements exceeds the carrying amount of the investee's net assets (including goodwill) in consolidated statements.

The list is not exhaustive (Ind AS 36.13). Materiality applies (Ind AS 36.15–16): if previous calculations showed a large headroom and no events have eliminated it, a new estimate may not be needed; an interest rate rise need not trigger a test if it is unlikely to affect the discount rate materially (e.g. short-term rates rising while long-term rates are stable) or if previous headroom was large.

Interpreting key indicators in India

Indicator Indian examples Practitioner notes
Market cap below net assets Listed PSU, telecom, infrastructure, mid-cap chemicals trading below book Not conclusive (market cap reflects minority, liquidity, other factors) but requires a test; reconcile market cap plus control premium to total recoverable amount
Interest rate increases RBI rate hiking cycle 2022–23; G-sec yield rise Affects WACC; check effect on VIU discount rate; also affects Ind AS 19/37 rates
Adverse regulation CERC/SERC tariff orders, AGR judgement in telecom, ban on single-use plastic, price control under DPCO for pharma, export bans Often CGU-specific; may also affect useful life
Obsolescence 4G to 5G network equipment, ICE-to-EV tooling, legacy software Also review useful life and residual value (Ind AS 36.17)
Underperformance Store EBITDA below budget, plant utilisation at 40%, losses in a new geography Compare actuals vs budget; aggregated current + budgeted losses

Link to useful life and depreciation (Ind AS 36.17)

An indication that an asset may be impaired may also indicate that its remaining useful life, depreciation method or residual value needs review — even if no impairment loss is recognised. Record both conclusions in the trigger memo.

Process for the trigger assessment

  1. Maintain a trigger checklist per CGU/asset with each Ind AS 36.12 indicator, data source and threshold (e.g. EBITDA shortfall > 15% vs budget; market cap/book < 1.0; 10-year G-sec up > 50 bp since last test).
  2. Compare market capitalisation with book net assets at each quarter-end for listed entities.
  3. Review Board minutes for restructuring, closure or disposal plans.
  4. Review regulatory developments affecting each CGU.
  5. Document conclusions, and where triggers exist, perform the full test.
  6. Schedule annual goodwill/indefinite-life tests (commonly at 31 December or 31 March) using the latest Board-approved budget.

Illustration

Ashoka Cables Ltd (NSE-listed) at 31 March 2026: net assets ₹2,400 crore; market cap ₹1,950 crore; 10-year G-sec up 40 bp in the year; the specialty cables CGU's EBITDA is 28% below budget due to the loss of a key export customer; goodwill of ₹180 crore from a FY2022 acquisition sits in the power cables CGU group.

Conclusion: indicators (d) and (g) exist; full tests are required for the specialty cables CGU and, considering (d), at entity level reconciliation. Goodwill in the power cables group is tested annually regardless. The rate increase (c) is considered in all tests. Useful lives of specialty cables machinery are reviewed under Ind AS 36.17.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Market cap below book for a short period (market dip at year-end) is still an indicator; judgement on materiality and temporariness must be documented.
  • Subsidiary-level separate financial statements: an investment carried at cost is tested if the investee's net assets are below the investment or after large dividends.
  • New acquisitions: goodwill acquired during the year must be tested before year-end even if the PPA is provisional — allocate provisionally.
  • Assets not yet available for use (CWIP-like intangibles, IPR&D) — annual test because uncertainty is higher; tangible CWIP is not annually tested but triggers apply (e.g. suspension).
  • Interest rate falls do not trigger a test but may support reversals (s08).
  • Interim reports (SEBI quarterly results, Ind AS 34) — indicators are assessed at each quarter-end; impairment losses on goodwill recognised in an interim period are not reversed in later interims (Appendix A to Ind AS 34, the Ind AS equivalent of IFRIC 10).

Key takeaways

  • Assess indicators at every reporting date; test only when an indicator exists, except for annual tests.
  • Goodwill, indefinite-life intangibles and intangibles not yet available for use are tested annually at a consistent time.
  • Ind AS 36.12 lists external (value decline, adverse environment, rate increases, market cap below net assets) and internal (obsolescence, adverse use changes, underperformance) indicators.
  • Materiality and previous headroom can justify not re-estimating, but must be documented.
  • Indicators also prompt review of useful life, method and residual value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Trigger memo [L2]

Prepare a trigger assessment for a hotel company at 31 March 2026: market cap ₹3,200 crore vs net assets ₹3,050 crore; occupancy 72% vs budget 70%; 10-year G-sec down 20 bp; a new state tax on liquor reducing F&B margins by 4 percentage points at its Goa resorts; a Board-approved plan to sell two loss-making hotels in FY27. Identify which indicators exist, for which CGUs, and what work follows.

Q2. Market cap shortfall [L2]

A listed EPC company has net assets of ₹5,600 crore and market cap of ₹4,100 crore; its three CGUs have recent VIUs of ₹2,400, ₹2,200 and ₹1,800 crore against carrying amounts of ₹2,000, ₹1,900 and ₹1,700 crore. Explain how you would reconcile the market cap with total recoverable amount and what this implies about the assumptions in the VIUs.

💡 Hint: Consider control premium, net debt and corporate costs not allocated to CGUs.

Q3. Annual test timing [L1]

A company acquires a business on 15 January 2026 creating goodwill of ₹90 crore; its policy is to test goodwill annually at 31 December. State when the first test must be performed and what happens if the PPA is still provisional at 31 March 2026.

Q4. Interest rate trigger materiality [L2]

At the last test, a CGU's VIU exceeded carrying amount by 35% using a pre-tax rate of 13%. During the year, the 10-year G-sec rose by 60 bp, but the company's beta and the ERP were unchanged. Discuss whether a new estimate is required under Ind AS 36.15–16 and what evidence you would document.

Q5. Separate financial statements indicator [L2]

A parent's investment in a subsidiary is carried at ₹500 crore at cost. The subsidiary paid a dividend of ₹120 crore when its total comprehensive income for the year was ₹70 crore, and its net assets in the consolidated statements (including goodwill) are ₹430 crore. Identify the indicators and describe the test in the parent's separate financial statements.
