Subtopic note

Impairment Reversals and Ind AS 36 Disclosures

Scope: Reversals (36.110–125), goodwill never reversed (36.124), disclosures (36.134) incl. key-assumption sensitivity

Why it matters

Ind AS permits reversal of impairment losses on assets other than goodwill when recoverable amount recovers — a material difference from US GAAP and an area where Indian companies in cyclical sectors (steel, telecom, power, aviation, hotels) see large swings. Reversals must be capped and allocated carefully, and they are scrutinised because they boost profit. Disclosures under Ind AS 36.134 — key assumptions, discount rates, growth rates, and sensitivity when headroom is thin — are among the most commented-upon notes by NFRA and are read closely by analysts to judge the risk of future impairments.

Core notes

Indicators of reversal (Ind AS 36.110–112)

At the end of each reporting period, assess whether there is any indication that an impairment loss recognised in prior periods for an asset other than goodwill may no longer exist or may have decreased. Indicators mirror Ind AS 36.12:

External: observable increase in the asset's value; significant favourable changes in the technological, market, economic or legal environment; decrease in market interest rates affecting the discount rate.

Internal: significant favourable changes in the extent or manner of use (e.g. capex that improves or enhances performance, or restructuring); evidence from internal reporting that economic performance is or will be better than expected.

If such an indication exists, estimate the recoverable amount. An indication may also mean that useful life, depreciation method or residual value should be reviewed (Ind AS 36.113).

Conditions for reversal (Ind AS 36.114–116)

An impairment loss recognised in prior periods for an asset other than goodwill is reversed if, and only if, there has been a change in the estimates used to determine the asset's recoverable amount since the last impairment loss was recognised. The unwinding of the discount (passage of time) alone is not a basis for reversal (Ind AS 36.116) — VIU increases simply because cash flows are closer, which does not reflect an increase in service potential.

Reversal for an individual asset (Ind AS 36.117–121)

  • The increased carrying amount shall not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of amortisation or depreciation) had no impairment loss been recognised in prior years (Ind AS 36.117) — the reversal cap (c04).
  • Reversal recognised immediately in P&L, unless the asset is carried at a revalued amount — then treated as a revaluation increase (OCI), except to the extent the original loss was recognised in P&L (Ind AS 36.119–120).
  • After reversal, depreciation is adjusted prospectively to allocate the revised carrying amount, less residual value, over the remaining useful life (Ind AS 36.121).

Reversal for a CGU (Ind AS 36.122–123)

  • Allocate the reversal to the assets of the unit, except goodwill, pro rata with the carrying amounts of those assets.
  • In allocating, the carrying amount of each asset shall not be increased above the lower of: (a) its recoverable amount (if determinable); and (b) the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation) had no impairment been recognised in prior periods.
  • The amount that would otherwise be allocated above the cap is allocated pro rata to the other assets (except goodwill).

Goodwill (Ind AS 36.124–125)

An impairment loss recognised for goodwill shall not be reversed in a subsequent period. Ind AS 38 prohibits recognition of internally generated goodwill, and any increase in recoverable amount of goodwill after an impairment is likely to be an increase in internally generated goodwill rather than a reversal of the acquired goodwill impairment.

Interim periods: an impairment of goodwill recognised in an interim period (quarterly results under SEBI LODR Reg 33 / Ind AS 34) is not reversed in a subsequent interim or annual period.

Worked illustration — CGU reversal

A power CGU was impaired on 31 March 2024 by ₹230 crore: goodwill 80, plant 120, ROU 30. Post-impairment carrying amounts: plant ₹600 crore (remaining life 15 years), ROU ₹90 crore (remaining 15 years). By 31 March 2026, after a favourable tariff order, recoverable amount is ₹780 crore.

Increase in recoverable amount over carrying amount = 780 − 598 = ₹182 crore, but the total cap is 104 + 26 = ₹130 crore. Total reversal = ₹130 crore (plant 104, ROU 26). The remaining ₹52 crore of headroom relates to goodwill (not reversed) and internally generated value.

Disclosures

For each class of assets (Ind AS 36.126): impairment losses and reversals recognised in P&L and OCI, and the line items in which they are included.

For each material impairment loss or reversal (Ind AS 36.130): events and circumstances; amount; nature of asset or description of CGU; reportable segment; whether recoverable amount is FVLCD or VIU; for FVLCD, fair value hierarchy level and valuation technique; for VIU, the discount rate(s) used in current and previous estimates.

For each CGU (group) with significant goodwill or indefinite-life intangibles (Ind AS 36.134):

  • (a) carrying amount of goodwill allocated;
  • (b) carrying amount of indefinite-life intangibles allocated;
  • (c) the basis for recoverable amount (VIU or FVLCD);
  • (d) if VIU: each key assumption to which recoverable amount is most sensitive; management's approach to determining values (whether they reflect past experience or are consistent with external sources); the period of projections and justification if more than five years; the growth rate to extrapolate and justification if it exceeds the long-term average; the discount rate(s);
  • (e) if FVLCD: valuation technique, key assumptions, approach, fair value hierarchy level, and for DCF the projection period, growth rate and discount rate;
  • (f) sensitivity: if a reasonably possible change in a key assumption would cause carrying amount to exceed recoverable amount: (i) the amount of headroom; (ii) the value assigned to the key assumption; (iii) the amount by which the value must change, after incorporating consequential effects on other variables, for recoverable amount to equal carrying amount (breakeven).

Goodwill allocated to multiple CGUs where amounts are individually insignificant is disclosed in aggregate (Ind AS 36.135).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Reversal driven only by passage of time — not permitted (Ind AS 36.116).
  • Rate decreases are a reversal indicator; but if the original impairment was due to rates, document that market rates genuinely fell, not merely the entity's view.
  • Reversals on revalued assets — credit OCI except to the extent the loss went to P&L.
  • Investments in subsidiaries (separate financial statements) — impairment reversals are allowed (they are not goodwill).
  • Equity-accounted investments — impairment of the investment as a whole is reversed under Ind AS 28.42, as goodwill is not separately recognised.
  • Disclosure of "no reasonably possible change" — a statement that no reasonably possible change would cause impairment must be supportable by the sensitivity analysis.
  • Deferred tax on reversals reverses the earlier DTA/DTL movements.

Key takeaways

  • Reverse impairments of non-goodwill assets only when estimates used to determine recoverable amount change — not from unwinding.
  • Reversal is capped at the carrying amount that would have existed without impairment (net of depreciation).
  • CGU reversals go pro rata to non-goodwill assets, capped at the lower of recoverable amount and the no-impairment carrying amount.
  • Goodwill impairment is never reversed, including interim-period losses.
  • Ind AS 36.134 requires key assumptions, rates, growth, projection period and sensitivity (headroom, assumption value, breakeven change) for significant goodwill CGUs.

Practice Questions

Q1. Individual asset reversal [L1]

A machine (cost ₹100 crore on 1 April 2021, life 10 years, nil RV) was impaired by ₹20 crore at 31 March 2023. At 31 March 2026, recoverable amount is ₹60 crore due to a new export contract. Compute the reversal, pass the entry and compute FY27 depreciation.

Q2. Unwinding vs estimate change [L2]

A CGU's VIU increased from ₹410 crore to ₹445 crore over a year; the only changes were the passage of time (₹40 crore effect) and a 50 bp increase in the discount rate (−₹12 crore), with a favourable change in margins (+₹7 crore). The carrying amount after the earlier impairment is ₹420 crore. Can a reversal be recognised? Explain with Ind AS 36.114–116.

💡 Hint: Separate the movement in VIU attributable to unwinding from that attributable to changed estimates.

Q3. CGU reversal with caps [L2]

A CGU impaired two years ago now has carrying amounts: PPE ₹300 crore (would have been ₹350 crore without impairment), intangibles ₹80 crore (would have been ₹90 crore), goodwill nil (originally ₹40 crore, fully impaired). Recoverable amount is ₹450 crore; PPE's own FVLCD is ₹330 crore. Compute and allocate the reversal.

Q4. Draft Ind AS 36.134 disclosure [L3]

Draft the disclosure for a goodwill CGU group with goodwill ₹520 crore, recoverable amount by VIU, five-year projections, terminal growth 4.5%, pre-tax discount rate 14.1%, headroom ₹95 crore, where a 0.9 percentage point increase in the discount rate or a 1.2 percentage point decrease in terminal growth would eliminate headroom.

Q5. US GAAP contrast [L1]

An Indian company with a US-listed parent reverses a ₹75 crore impairment on a plant under Ind AS. Explain how the parent's US GAAP reporting would treat the same facts and what reconciling adjustment would be needed.
