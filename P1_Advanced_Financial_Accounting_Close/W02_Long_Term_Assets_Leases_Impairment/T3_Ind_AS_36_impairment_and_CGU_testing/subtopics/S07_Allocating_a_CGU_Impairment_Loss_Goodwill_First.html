Subtopic note

Allocating a CGU Impairment Loss: Goodwill First, Then Pro Rata

Scope: Loss allocation (36.104–105): goodwill first, then pro rata, floor at highest of FVLCD, VIU and zero

Why it matters

Once a CGU impairment loss is measured, it must be allocated to individual assets because assets — not CGUs — are recorded in the ledger, depreciated, and presented in the PPE and intangibles notes. The allocation sequence decides whether goodwill absorbs the loss (permanent, never reversed) or whether PPE, ROU and intangibles are written down (reversible, affecting future depreciation). The floor rule prevents individual assets from being written below their own recoverable amounts and frequently causes iterative reallocation. This is a standard CMA and interview computation and the core of the week's Saturday case test.

Core notes

The rule (Ind AS 36.104)

An impairment loss shall be recognised for a CGU (the smallest group of CGUs to which goodwill or a corporate asset has been allocated) if, and only if, the recoverable amount of the unit is less than its carrying amount. The impairment loss shall be allocated to reduce the carrying amount of the assets of the unit in the following order:

  • (a) first, to reduce the carrying amount of any goodwill allocated to the CGU (group of units); and
  • (b) then, to the other assets of the unit pro rata on the basis of the carrying amount of each asset in the unit.

These reductions are treated as impairment losses on individual assets (recognised per Ind AS 36.60).

The floor (Ind AS 36.105)

In allocating, an entity shall not reduce the carrying amount of an asset below the highest of:

  • (a) its fair value less costs of disposal (if measurable);
  • (b) its value in use (if determinable); and
  • (c) zero.

The amount of the impairment loss that would otherwise have been allocated to the asset shall be allocated pro rata to the other assets of the unit (group of units).

If the recoverable amount of an individual asset cannot be estimated (Ind AS 36.106), the loss is allocated to it per the pro rata rule only if the asset is impaired or the CGU is impaired; no loss is recognised for the individual asset if the CGU is not impaired, even if the asset's FVLCD is below its carrying amount (Ind AS 36.107).

Which assets are in the pool?

  • Only assets within the scope of Ind AS 36 absorb the loss: PPE, ROU, intangibles, goodwill, allocated corporate assets.
  • Assets outside Ind AS 36 scope (inventories — Ind AS 2; receivables and other financial assets — Ind AS 109; deferred tax assets — Ind AS 12; held-for-sale assets — Ind AS 105) may be included in the CGU carrying amount for comparison (for consistency with cash flows), but no impairment loss is allocated to them; they are measured under their own standards first.
  • Liabilities deducted for consistency (e.g. lease liability under the deduction approach, decommissioning provision) reduce the carrying amount compared but are not "allocated" a loss.

Step-by-step procedure

  1. Measure carrying amount (CA) and recoverable amount (RA) of the CGU; loss = CA − RA (if positive).
  2. Write down goodwill — up to its full carrying amount.
  3. Allocate the remainder pro rata to other Ind AS 36 assets based on carrying amounts.
  4. Check each asset against its floor (highest of its own FVLCD, VIU, zero).
  5. Reallocate any excess pro rata among assets not yet at their floor; iterate until fully allocated or all assets at floors.
  6. Any loss that cannot be allocated (all assets at floors) — a liability is recognised only if required by another standard (Ind AS 36.108), e.g. an onerous contract provision; otherwise the unallocated loss is not recognised.

Worked illustration

Malwa Foods Ltd's snacks CGU at 31 March 2026 (₹ crore): goodwill 100; PPE 400 (FVLCD of the plant 360); acquired brand 100 (FVLCD by relief-from-royalty 85); ROU 100 (no separate recoverable amount determinable); inventory 60 (measured under Ind AS 2, at cost, not impaired). Carrying amount for testing (Ind AS 36 assets + inventory, consistent with cash flows that include inventory realisation) = 760. Recoverable amount (VIU) = 580. Loss = 180.

Consequences: future depreciation/amortisation of PPE, brand and ROU is on reduced carrying amounts over remaining lives (Ind AS 36.63); deferred tax — carrying amounts fall while tax bases are unchanged, so DTLs decrease or DTAs arise (goodwill typically has no deferred tax under the initial recognition exception).

Excel implementation

Use an iterative allocation table:

Max_i        = CA_i - MAX(FVLCD_i, VIU_i, 0)
Round1_i     = MIN(Max_i, Remaining * CA_i / SUM(CA_eligible))
Remaining2   = Remaining - SUM(Round1)
Eligible2_i  = IF(Round1_i < Max_i, CA_i, 0)

Repeat rounds (3–4 rounds are usually enough) and check that total allocated = loss, and no asset falls below its floor. A small VBA loop or LAMBDA function can automate it.

Presentation

  • Impairment loss in P&L — Schedule III has no specific line; present within "depreciation, amortisation and impairment" or as an exceptional item if material and non-recurring (Ind AS 1.97–98).
  • PPE/intangibles roll-forwards show impairment in the accumulated depreciation/impairment columns.
  • Goodwill note shows accumulated impairment.
  • Segment note: impairment losses by segment (Ind AS 36.129).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Revalued assets — the allocated loss is a revaluation decrease (OCI first to the extent of surplus) (c03).
  • Corporate assets allocated to the CGU absorb losses pro rata like other assets.
  • NCI with partial goodwill — gross up goodwill, allocate loss to the grossed-up goodwill, recognise only the parent's share on recognised goodwill (Appendix C).
  • All assets at floor — unrecognised excess loss; consider onerous contracts (Ind AS 37.69 requires impairment of dedicated assets first).
  • ROU assets often have no separate recoverable amount; sublease market evidence may provide an FVLCD floor.
  • CWIP in the CGU shares the loss pro rata; assess its own FVLCD if measurable.
  • Working capital included in carrying amount never absorbs loss — but its inclusion increases the loss measured, so it must be consistent with cash flows.

Key takeaways

  • Allocate the CGU loss to goodwill first, then pro rata to other Ind AS 36 assets based on carrying amounts.
  • No asset goes below the highest of its FVLCD, VIU and zero; reallocate excesses pro rata and iterate.
  • Assets outside Ind AS 36 scope and liabilities deducted for consistency are not allocated losses.
  • Unallocable losses are not recognised unless another standard requires a liability.
  • Post-impairment depreciation uses the reduced carrying amounts; deferred tax changes accordingly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic allocation [L1]

A CGU has goodwill ₹50 crore, PPE ₹300 crore and intangibles ₹150 crore; recoverable amount ₹380 crore; no individual floors are known. Allocate the impairment loss and pass the entry.

Q2. Allocation with floors [L2]

A CGU (₹ crore): goodwill 60; land 200 (FVLCD 230); building 150 (FVLCD 120); plant 250 (no separate recoverable amount); ROU 90 (FVLCD via sublease 70). Recoverable amount 520. Allocate the loss with floors, showing each iteration, and compute post-impairment carrying amounts.

💡 Hint: Land with FVLCD above carrying amount cannot absorb any loss; remove it from the pro rata base at the first round.

Q3. Working capital in carrying amount [L2]

A CGU's carrying amount includes PPE ₹500 crore, trade receivables ₹80 crore and inventory ₹70 crore, less trade payables ₹50 crore; VIU cash flows include working capital realisation. Recoverable amount ₹520 crore; no goodwill. Compute the impairment loss and its allocation, and explain why receivables and inventory are not written down under Ind AS 36.

Q4. Saturday case drill [L3]

A CGU group with goodwill ₹120 crore contains PPE ₹640 crore (FVLCD ₹590 crore), ROU ₹90 crore, CWIP ₹30 crore and net working capital ₹40 crore; lease liability of ₹90 crore is deducted from carrying amount; recoverable amount (after deducting lease liability consistently) is ₹610 crore. Compute the loss, allocate it with the floor test, pass the entry, and state the deferred tax effects at 25.168% (tax base of PPE ₹480 crore; goodwill not deductible).

Q5. Unallocable loss [L2]

A CGU's loss of ₹90 crore exceeds the amount that can be allocated after floors (₹70 crore). The CGU is party to a supply contract that is loss-making. Explain the treatment of the ₹20 crore remainder under Ind AS 36.108 and Ind AS 37.
