Key concept

Journal Entries for Impairment, Including Revalued Assets

Concept: Entry: Dr Impairment loss (P&L) Cr Accumulated impairment/Goodwill; revalued assets debit revaluation surplus first

Statement

Cost-model assets and goodwill (Ind AS 36.60):

Account Dr Cr
Impairment loss (P&L) \( IL \)
Accumulated impairment — PPE / ROU / Intangibles \( IL_{\text{asset}} \)
Goodwill (or accumulated impairment on goodwill) \( IL_{\text{GW}} \)

Revalued assets (Ind AS 36.60–61, Ind AS 16.40):

\[ IL_{\text{OCI}} = \min(IL, \; RS_{\text{asset}}), \qquad IL_{\text{P\&L}} = IL - IL_{\text{OCI}} \]

Account Dr Cr
Revaluation surplus (OCI) \( IL_{\text{OCI}} \)
Impairment loss (P&L) \( IL_{\text{P\&L}} \)
Asset / Accumulated impairment \( IL \)

where \( RS_{\text{asset}} \) is the revaluation surplus relating to that specific asset.

Explanation

An impairment loss on a cost-model asset is recognised immediately in P&L. For an asset carried at a revalued amount, the loss is treated as a revaluation decrease: it is recognised in OCI to the extent it does not exceed the revaluation surplus for the same asset, and any excess in P&L (Ind AS 36.61). The surplus of one asset cannot absorb another asset's impairment.

Presentation choices:

  • Accumulated impairment may be shown with accumulated depreciation ("accumulated depreciation and impairment") — the Schedule III PPE note format includes impairment within the accumulated depreciation columns. Some companies credit the asset cost directly; a separate accumulated impairment account preserves the reversal cap information (c04).
  • Goodwill impairment is credited to goodwill (or its accumulated impairment), and disclosed in the goodwill roll-forward.
  • In P&L, impairment is presented within depreciation, amortisation and impairment, or as an exceptional item if material (Ind AS 1.97).
  • Deferred tax: impairment reduces carrying amount without changing tax base → reduces DTL or creates DTA; for revalued assets, the tax effect of the OCI portion goes to OCI (Ind AS 12.61A).
  • Cash flow statement: impairment is non-cash — added back in the indirect method.

Illustration

A company's office building (revaluation model): carrying amount ₹120 crore; revaluation surplus for the building ₹15 crore (net of deferred tax, gross ₹20.04 crore at 25.168%). A CGU test allocates an impairment of ₹28 crore to the building. Its plant (cost model) in the same CGU receives ₹12 crore; goodwill ₹10 crore.

Gross surplus available = ₹20.04 crore → OCI portion 20.04; P&L portion 7.96.

Account Dr (₹ crore) Cr (₹ crore)
Revaluation surplus (OCI) 20.04
Impairment loss (P&L) — building 7.96
Impairment loss (P&L) — plant 12.00
Impairment loss (P&L) — goodwill 10.00
Building (accumulated depreciation and impairment) 28.00
Accumulated impairment — plant 12.00
Goodwill 10.00
Deferred tax liability 5.04
Deferred tax (OCI) 5.04

(Deferred tax on the P&L portion and on the plant follows the normal Ind AS 12 computation; goodwill typically carries no deferred tax.)

Practice Questions

Q1. Cost model entry [L1]

An impairment loss of ₹45 crore on a CGU is allocated: goodwill ₹25 crore, PPE ₹15 crore, software ₹5 crore. Pass the entry and show the presentation in the Schedule III PPE note.

Q2. Revalued land [L2]

Freehold land carried under the revaluation model at ₹80 crore (surplus ₹30 crore gross) is impaired to a recoverable amount of ₹42 crore. Pass the entries including deferred tax at 25.168% on the OCI portion (assume land's DTL was computed at the same rate), and state the effect on P&L and OCI.

💡 Hint: The OCI portion is limited to the surplus relating to that land.

Q3. Presentation in quarterly results [L2]

A listed company records goodwill impairment of ₹600 crore in Q4. Explain how it should be presented in the Schedule III P&L, in the SEBI Reg 33 results format, in the cash flow statement and in management's adjusted EBITDA, and why it is not reversed in later quarters.
