Key concept

Headroom and Breakeven Sensitivity Analysis

Concept: Headroom = Recoverable amount − Carrying amount; breakeven sensitivity on discount rate, terminal growth, EBITDA margin

Statement

\[ H = RA - CA \]

\[ \text{Breakeven } x^{*}: \; RA(x^{*}) = CA, \qquad \Delta x = x^{*} - x_0 \]

where \( H \) is headroom (positive means no impairment), \( RA \) and \( CA \) are recoverable and carrying amounts, \( x \) is a key assumption (pre-tax discount rate, terminal growth rate, EBITDA margin, revenue growth, capacity utilisation), \( x_0 \) its base value and \( x^{*} \) the value at which headroom is zero. Headroom is often also expressed as a percentage: \( H\% = H \div CA \).

Explanation

Ind AS 36.134(f) requires, for each CGU with significant goodwill or indefinite-life intangibles, disclosure when a reasonably possible change in a key assumption would cause carrying amount to exceed recoverable amount: (i) the amount of headroom; (ii) the value assigned to the key assumption; and (iii) the amount by which the value must change, after incorporating any consequential effects on other variables, for recoverable amount to equal carrying amount.

Practical points:

  • Key assumptions are those to which recoverable amount is most sensitive — usually discount rate, terminal growth and terminal EBITDA margin; for some CGUs, volume, tariff or commodity price.
  • Breakeven is solved with Goal Seek (set headroom = 0 by changing the assumption) — one variable at a time.
  • Consequential effects: e.g. lower revenue growth may lower working capital investment; a margin change may change tax (for FVLCD). Model these linkages rather than flexing one cell mechanically.
  • Two-way data tables (discount rate × terminal growth) show the combined effect and help management judge "reasonably possible" ranges.
  • Thin headroom (e.g. < 10–15% of CA) is a signal for audit focus and enhanced disclosure; auditors often treat it as a key audit matter.
  • Headroom is also tracked over time as an early-warning metric in FP&A dashboards.

Illustration

A goodwill CGU group: CA ₹1,000 crore; VIU ₹1,150 crore at pre-tax rate 13.5%, terminal growth 5%, terminal EBITDA margin 20%. Headroom = ₹150 crore (15%).

Breakeven results (solved by Goal Seek):

Key assumption Base Breakeven Change
Pre-tax discount rate 13.5% 14.6% +1.1 pp
Terminal growth 5.0% 3.5% −1.5 pp
Terminal EBITDA margin 20.0% 17.6% −2.4 pp

Two-way data table (headroom, ₹ crore):

Growth ↓ / Rate → 12.5% 13.5% 14.5%
4.0% 190 40 (70)
5.0% 340 150 10
6.0% 560 300 120

(Illustrative values.) If management considers a 1 pp rate increase reasonably possible, the CGU is close to breakeven, so the Ind AS 36.134(f) disclosure is required.

Excel set-up:

Headroom cell:  =VIU - CarryingAmount
Data table:     row input = DiscountRate, column input = TerminalGrowth, corner = Headroom
Goal Seek:      Set Headroom to 0 by changing DiscountRate (repeat for each assumption)

Practice Questions

Q1. Breakeven discount rate [L2]

A CGU's pre-tax cash flows are ₹100 crore in year 1 growing at 4% in perpetuity; carrying amount ₹950 crore; pre-tax rate 13%. Compute VIU, headroom and the breakeven discount rate analytically.

💡 Hint: For a growing perpetuity, VIU equals the first-year cash flow divided by (r − g), so the breakeven rate can be solved directly.

Q2. Reasonably possible change [L2]

Management states that no reasonably possible change in key assumptions would cause impairment for a CGU with headroom of 6% of carrying amount, where a 0.5 pp increase in the discount rate eliminates headroom. Evaluate the statement and draft the disclosure that should be made instead.

Q3. Data table design [L3]

Specify an Excel model that produces (a) a two-way data table of headroom for discount rates 11–15% and terminal growth 3–6%, (b) a breakeven table for three key assumptions with consequential effects on working capital, and (c) a chart suitable for an audit committee pack. List the inputs, links and checks.
