Key concept

Recoverable Amount and Measurement of Impairment Loss

Concept: Recoverable amount = Higher of FVLCD and VIU (36.18); Impairment loss = Carrying amount − Recoverable amount

Statement

\[ RA = \max(FVLCD, VIU) \]

\[ IL = \max(0, \; CA - RA) \]

where \( RA \) is the recoverable amount of an asset or CGU, \( FVLCD \) is fair value less costs of disposal (Ind AS 113 fair value minus incremental disposal costs), \( VIU \) is value in use (present value of future cash flows expected from the asset or CGU), \( CA \) is the carrying amount and \( IL \) is the impairment loss (Ind AS 36.18, 36.59).

Explanation

The "higher of" logic reflects rational management: an entity will either continue to use an asset or sell it, whichever yields more. So an asset is impaired only if both routes yield less than carrying amount.

Practical implications:

  • Only one measure may be needed (Ind AS 36.19): if either FVLCD or VIU exceeds carrying amount, there is no impairment and the other need not be estimated.
  • FVLCD not measurable (no basis for reliable estimate): use VIU (Ind AS 36.20).
  • Assets held for disposal: VIU consists mainly of disposal proceeds, so FVLCD often approximates recoverable amount (Ind AS 36.21).
  • Individual asset vs CGU: recoverable amount is determined for an individual asset unless it does not generate largely independent inflows, in which case it is determined for its CGU (Ind AS 36.22, 36.66).
  • Consistency: carrying amount must be determined on a basis consistent with recoverable amount (Ind AS 36.75) — same assets, same liabilities, same treatment of working capital and leases.
  • Recognition: the loss is recognised immediately in P&L, unless the asset is revalued (treated as a revaluation decrease) (Ind AS 36.60). If the loss exceeds the asset's carrying amount, a liability is recognised only if required by another standard (Ind AS 36.62).
  • After recognition: depreciation is based on the revised carrying amount (Ind AS 36.63); deferred tax is adjusted (Ind AS 36.64).

Illustration

A hotel CGU at 31 March 2026 (₹ crore): carrying amount 540 (building, FF&E, ROU; working capital excluded).

Measure Amount
VIU (pre-tax cash flows at 13.8%) 495
Fair value (transaction multiples) 520
Costs of disposal (1.5%) (7.8)
FVLCD 512.2
Recoverable amount 512.2
Impairment loss = 540 − 512.2 27.8

FVLCD is higher here because a market participant would undertake a renovation that VIU, restricted to current condition, excludes. The loss is allocated across the hotel's assets (s07).

Practice Questions

Q1. Determine the loss [L1]

For each case determine recoverable amount and impairment loss (₹ crore): (a) CA 200, VIU 180, FVLCD 210; (b) CA 200, VIU 170, FVLCD not measurable; (c) CA 200, VIU 150, FVLCD 160; (d) CA 200, VIU 230, FVLCD not estimated.

Q2. Consistency adjustment [L2]

A CGU's VIU cash flows include payments to settle a recognised decommissioning provision of ₹40 crore. The carrying amount of assets is ₹600 crore and the VIU including decommissioning outflows is ₹550 crore. Explain how Ind AS 36.78 allows the comparison to be made consistently and compute the impairment loss, if any.

💡 Hint: Either deduct the provision from both carrying amount and recoverable amount, or exclude the outflows from VIU.

Q3. Interview: why "higher of"? [L1]

Explain in 4–5 sentences to a non-accountant why the recoverable amount is the higher of value in use and fair value less costs of disposal, giving an Indian example where FVLCD would exceed VIU.
