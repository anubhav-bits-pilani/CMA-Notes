Key concept

Impairment Reversal Cap

Concept: Reversal cap = Carrying amount had no impairment been recognised (net of depreciation)

Statement

For an asset other than goodwill, at the reversal date:

\[ CA_{\text{after reversal}} \le CA_{\text{no-imp}} = C - \sum D_{\text{original}} \]

\[ \text{Reversal} = \min\left(RA, \; CA_{\text{no-imp}}\right) - CA_{\text{current}} \]

where \( CA_{\text{no-imp}} \) is the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation or amortisation) had no impairment loss been recognised in prior years — cost \( C \) less depreciation on the original schedule \( \sum D_{\text{original}} \) — \( RA \) is the asset's recoverable amount and \( CA_{\text{current}} \) is its current carrying amount after post-impairment depreciation (Ind AS 36.117). For CGUs, the cap is applied asset by asset, and the reversal is allocated pro rata to non-goodwill assets (Ind AS 36.122–123).

Explanation

The cap prevents a reversal from creating a revaluation through P&L. Any increase in recoverable amount beyond the no-impairment carrying amount is an increase in value that the cost model does not recognise (it would be a revaluation, permitted only under the revaluation model via OCI, Ind AS 36.118).

Mechanics:

  • Track a "shadow" depreciation schedule for impaired assets from the impairment date — the original cost and life, as if no impairment occurred. A separate accumulated impairment account in the fixed-asset register makes this automatic.
  • If useful life or residual value was revised after the impairment, the no-impairment carrying amount reflects those revisions as they would have applied.
  • The reversal is recognised in P&L (cost model) or as a revaluation increase (revalued assets, to the extent the original loss went to OCI).
  • Goodwill is never reversed (Ind AS 36.124), so any CGU reversal goes only to other assets.
  • Depreciation after reversal is based on the new carrying amount over the remaining life (Ind AS 36.121).

Illustration

A cement grinding unit: cost ₹200 crore on 1 April 2020, life 20 years, nil RV → original depreciation ₹10 crore per year. Impaired at 31 March 2023 to ₹136 crore (loss ₹34 crore; CA before impairment 170). Post-impairment depreciation over 17 years = ₹8 crore per year.

At 31 March 2026:

  • \( CA_{\text{current}} \) = 136 − 3 × 8 = ₹112 crore.
  • \( CA_{\text{no-imp}} \) = 200 − 6 × 10 = ₹140 crore.
  • Recoverable amount = ₹155 crore.

\[ \text{Reversal} = \min(155, 140) - 112 = ₹28 \text{ crore} \]

Account Dr (₹ crore) Cr (₹ crore)
Accumulated impairment 28
Reversal of impairment loss (P&L) 28

Depreciation from FY27 = 140 ÷ 14 = ₹10 crore — back on the original schedule. The remaining ₹15 crore of recoverable amount above the cap is not recognised.

Connections

Applies the reversal rules of s08; complements c01 (loss) and c03 (entries). Requires the fixed-asset register design from t1 (separate impairment columns) and the depreciation formula after revision (t1 c01). Deferred tax: the reversal reverses the DTA/DTL movement from the impairment (Week 3). Under US GAAP, no reversal would be recognised at all.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the cap [L1]

A software platform (cost ₹60 crore on 1 April 2022, life 6 years) was impaired by ₹12 crore at 31 March 2024. At 31 March 2026, its recoverable amount is ₹30 crore. Compute the current carrying amount, the cap, the reversal and FY27 amortisation.

Q2. Life revised after impairment [L2]

A plant (cost ₹500 crore, life 25 years from 1 April 2016) was impaired by ₹90 crore at 31 March 2021. On 1 April 2023 its total useful life was revised to 30 years. At 31 March 2026 recoverable amount is ₹420 crore. Compute the no-impairment carrying amount reflecting the life revision, the reversal and the subsequent depreciation.

💡 Hint: Build both the actual and the shadow depreciation schedules, applying the life revision prospectively in each.

Q3. CGU reversal with goodwill [L2]

A CGU impaired in FY24 (goodwill ₹50 crore written off, PPE written down by ₹70 crore) now has recoverable amount exceeding its carrying amount by ₹110 crore. The PPE's no-impairment carrying amount exceeds its current carrying amount by ₹58 crore. Determine the reversal and explain what happens to the rest of the increase.
