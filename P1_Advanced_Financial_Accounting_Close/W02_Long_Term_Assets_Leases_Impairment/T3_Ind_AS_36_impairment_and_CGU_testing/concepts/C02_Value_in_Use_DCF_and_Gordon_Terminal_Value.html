Key concept

Value-in-Use DCF and Gordon Terminal Value

Concept: VIU = Σ CFt ÷ (1 + r_pre-tax)^t + TV ÷ (1 + r_pre-tax)^n; Gordon TV = CF(n+1) ÷ (r − g)

Statement

\[ VIU = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + r_{\text{pre}})^t} + \frac{TV_n}{(1 + r_{\text{pre}})^n} \]

\[ TV_n = \frac{CF_{n+1}}{r_{\text{pre}} - g}, \qquad CF_{n+1} = CF_n^{\text{norm}} \times (1+g) \]

where \( CF_t \) is the pre-tax, pre-financing cash flow in year \( t \) for the asset/CGU in its current condition, \( r_{\text{pre}} \) is the pre-tax discount rate (Ind AS 36.55), \( n \) is the explicit forecast period (normally ≤ 5 years, Ind AS 36.33(b)), \( TV_n \) is the terminal value at the end of year \( n \), \( CF_n^{\text{norm}} \) is a normalised steady-state cash flow, and \( g \) is the long-term growth rate (steady or declining, not exceeding long-term average growth, Ind AS 36.33(c)), with \( g < r_{\text{pre}} \).

Explanation

The explicit period captures the budget; the Gordon model capitalises a growing perpetuity from year \( n+1 \). Conditions:

  • Pre-tax consistency: cash flows exclude income tax; the rate is pre-tax (derived iteratively, s05).
  • Nominal consistency: if cash flows include inflation, \( r \) and \( g \) are nominal.
  • Normalisation: the terminal-year cash flow should reflect steady-state margins, maintenance capex roughly in line with depreciation adjusted for growth, and working capital investment consistent with \( g \): \( \Delta WC_{n+1} = g \times WC_n \).
  • \( g \) close to \( r \) makes TV explode — sensitivity is acute; check the implied TV multiple \( \frac{1+g}{r-g} \) of terminal cash flow.
  • Finite life: for concessions, mines or leases without renewal, replace TV with remaining-period cash flows and disposal proceeds.
  • Timing: end-year discounting is standard; mid-year (exponent \( t - 0.5 \)) is acceptable if cash flows arise evenly — apply to TV consistently (TV discounted at \( n - 0.5 \) when the TV is measured as at mid-year, or at \( n \) with an adjusted formula).

Connections

Operationalises s04 (projections and exclusions) and s05 (pre-tax rate); feeds c01 (recoverable amount) and c05 (headroom and breakeven sensitivity on \( r \), \( g \) and margin). The same Gordon formula is used in Week 3 PPA valuations and in DCF valuation modules; differences in VIU are the exclusions (tax, financing, enhancements) and the pre-tax rate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute VIU [L1]

Pre-tax cash flows of ₹30, 32, 34, 35, 36 crore for five years, terminal growth 4%, pre-tax rate 12.5%. Compute VIU and the proportion represented by terminal value.

Q2. Normalising the terminal year [L2]

A CGU's year-5 cash flow of ₹80 crore includes maintenance capex of ₹10 crore against depreciation of ₹25 crore, and no working capital investment, although working capital is ₹120 crore and growth is 5%. Normalise the terminal cash flow and explain the effect on VIU at a 13% pre-tax rate.

💡 Hint: Steady-state capex and working capital must be consistent with the assumed perpetual growth.

Q3. Mid-year convention [L2]

Recompute Q1 using the mid-year convention for the explicit period and terminal value, and explain when the convention is appropriate for an Indian retail CGU with even monthly cash flows.
