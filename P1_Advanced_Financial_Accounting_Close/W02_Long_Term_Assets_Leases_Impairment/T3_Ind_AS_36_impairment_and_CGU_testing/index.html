Roadmap topic: Ind AS 36 Impairment of Assets: Trigger assessment, Cash Generating Units (CGUs), Value-in-Use (DCF pre-tax cash flows) vs Fair Value Less Costs of Disposal

Overview

Ind AS 36 ensures that assets are not carried above the amount recoverable from use or sale. It applies to PPE, ROU assets, intangible assets, goodwill, investments in subsidiaries/associates/JVs in separate financial statements, and assets carried at revalued amounts — but not to inventories, contract assets, deferred tax assets, financial assets within Ind AS 109, investment property at fair value or non-current assets held for sale (Ind AS 36.2). Impairment is one of the highest-judgement areas in Indian financial reporting: goodwill from acquisitions by NSE-listed groups, telecom and power assets hit by regulation, retail stores after footfall shifts, and stressed EPC and infrastructure companies all generate impairment tests where a few basis points of discount rate or terminal growth decide whether a loss is recognised.

For the target roles the topic is the meeting point of accounting and valuation. A technical accountant must identify triggers, define CGUs, allocate goodwill and corporate assets, and draft disclosures that NFRA and auditors will challenge. An FP&A or valuation analyst builds the DCF, reconciles cash flows to the Board-approved budget, derives a pre-tax discount rate from a post-tax WACC, and runs sensitivities and breakeven analysis. The same DCF skills reappear in Week 3 (purchase price allocation, fair value) and in the valuation phase of the curriculum.

The learning sequence follows the test. Start with when to test: indicators and the annual-test requirement for goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles (s01). Then what to test: identify CGUs (s02) and allocate goodwill and corporate assets to them (s03). Next, how much is recoverable: VIU projections with their strict exclusions (s04), the pre-tax discount rate (s05), and FVLCD under Ind AS 113 (s06). Then allocate any loss with the goodwill-first and floor rules (s07), and finally handle reversals and disclosures (s08). The concepts (c01–c05) are the formulas: recoverable amount and loss, VIU and Gordon terminal value, the entries, the reversal cap, and headroom/breakeven sensitivity.

Related concepts to study

US GAAP impairment model

US GAAP: ASC 360 undiscounted recoverability test then fair value; ASC 350 single-step goodwill test with qualitative option; reversals prohibited

US GAAP uses different models. For long-lived assets held and used (ASC 360-10), a two-step approach applies: first a recoverability test comparing carrying amount with undiscounted cash flows; only if carrying amount exceeds them is an impairment loss measured as carrying amount minus fair value. Because the first step uses undiscounted cash flows, US GAAP impairments occur later than under Ind AS. For goodwill (ASC 350, after ASU 2017-04), the test is a single step at the reporting-unit level: impairment equals the excess of the reporting unit's carrying amount over its fair value, limited to the goodwill balance, with an optional qualitative "step zero" assessment. Reversals of impairment are prohibited for assets held and used and for goodwill, whereas Ind AS permits reversals except for goodwill.

Goodwill from PPA and fair value hierarchy

Goodwill from PPA → Week 3 (w3_t4); FVLCD hierarchy → Week 3 (w3_t6)

Goodwill tested in this topic is created in a business combination under Ind AS 103 as the excess of consideration (plus NCI and previously held interest) over the fair value of identifiable net assets — Week 3's purchase price allocation (PPA) topic covers its measurement, including deferred tax on fair value uplifts that inflates goodwill. Separately, FVLCD in s06 is measured using Ind AS 113, whose fair value hierarchy (Level 1 quoted prices, Level 2 observable inputs, Level 3 unobservable inputs) determines disclosure. Most FVLCD measurements for CGUs are Level 3 DCFs or market multiples, requiring disclosure of valuation techniques and key assumptions under Ind AS 36.134(e). Week 3's fair value topic expands the hierarchy and valuation techniques.

Ind AS 116 interplay in CGU testing

Ind AS 116 interplay: include ROU in CGU and either exclude lease payments or deduct lease liability — consistently

Since Ind AS 116, ROU assets are part of CGU carrying amounts (a store's ROU is often its largest asset). Two consistent approaches exist. Approach 1: include the ROU in carrying amount, exclude the lease liability, and exclude lease payments from VIU cash flows (lease payments are financing cash flows) — VIU is then pre-lease, so the discount rate must reflect a capital structure including leases. Approach 2: include ROU, deduct the lease liability from carrying amount, and include lease payments in cash flows — permitted because Ind AS 36.78 allows deducting a recognised liability that a buyer would assume. Mixing the two (excluding lease payments but deducting the liability) double-counts and understates impairment. Lease renewals beyond the recognised lease term also require care.

Indian WACC inputs

Indian WACC inputs: 10-year G-sec, equity risk premium, betas from NSE comparables

Indian VIU and FVLCD models typically build the discount rate from: a risk-free rate equal to the 10-year G-sec yield at the measurement date (around 6.3–6.6% in late 2025; check the FBIL benchmark); an equity risk premium for India commonly in the 6.5–8% range (from surveys, implied ERP or long-run Nifty returns vs G-sec); betas from NSE-listed comparables, regressed against the Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 over 2–5 years, unlevered and relevered to a target capital structure; a size or company-specific premium where justified; and a pre-tax cost of debt from the entity's or peers' borrowing spreads. The post-tax WACC is then converted into a pre-tax rate for VIU (s05). Document sources for each input.

Excel DCF with data tables and goal seek

Excel: DCF with two-way data table (discount rate × terminal growth) and goal seek for pre-tax rate

The impairment workbook should have an inputs tab (budget cash flows, growth, tax, WACC components), a VIU tab (explicit forecast, terminal value, discount factors with mid-year option), and an outputs tab (recoverable amount, carrying amount, headroom). A two-way data table (Data → What-If Analysis → Data Table) places discount rates across columns and terminal growth down rows, with the cell computing headroom at the corner — giving the sensitivity matrix required for disclosure. Goal Seek solves for the pre-tax rate that makes pre-tax VIU equal post-tax VIU, and for breakeven assumptions (rate, growth, margin) that reduce headroom to zero. Use named ranges and avoid circular references.

Common pitfalls

Mixing pre-tax/post-tax and nominal/real

Mixing pre-tax and post-tax, or nominal and real, cash flows and rates

The mistake: discounting pre-tax cash flows at a post-tax WACC (overstating VIU) or post-tax cash flows at a grossed-up rate, or nominal cash flows at a real rate. It happens because valuation teams think in post-tax FCFF while Ind AS 36.55 requires a pre-tax rate, and because inflation assumptions are buried in budgets. The correct treatment: VIU uses pre-tax cash flows with a pre-tax rate; if a post-tax computation is run, it must produce the same VIU, and the pre-tax rate must be derived iteratively and disclosed. Nominal cash flows (including inflation) need a nominal rate. Detect it by checking the tax line in cash flows, comparing the disclosed pre-tax rate with WACC (it should be higher), and checking that growth rates are nominal.

Including enhancement capex or uncommitted restructuring

Including future enhancement capex benefits or uncommitted restructuring savings in VIU

The mistake: VIU cash flows include revenue from a planned capacity expansion or cost savings from a restructuring that has not been committed. Teams use the Board budget, which naturally includes growth initiatives. Ind AS 36.44 requires cash flows for the asset in its current condition, excluding inflows/outflows from future restructurings to which the entity is not yet committed (no Ind AS 37 provision) and from improving or enhancing performance. Maintenance capex is included. If expansion capex is excluded, so must be its benefits. Detect it by reconciling projected revenue to current capacity and by tracing savings to a recognised restructuring provision. FVLCD, by contrast, may include enhancements a market participant would make.

Testing goodwill at entity level

Testing goodwill at entity level instead of the CGU group monitored by management

The mistake: goodwill from several acquisitions is tested against the entity's total market capitalisation or total cash flows. Profitable businesses then shield impaired acquisitions. Ind AS 36.80 requires allocation to each CGU or group of CGUs expected to benefit from the synergies, representing the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes, and not larger than an operating segment (Ind AS 108) before aggregation. Detect it by comparing the impairment note's CGU groups with the Ind AS 108 segment note and with management reporting packs reviewed by the CODM.

Double-counting working capital or lease liabilities

Double-counting working capital or lease liabilities in CGU carrying amount

The mistake: carrying amount excludes receivables and payables but cash flows include working capital movements, or vice versa; or the lease liability is deducted from carrying amount while lease payments are also excluded from cash flows. It happens when the accounting carrying amount and the finance team's DCF are prepared separately. Ind AS 36.75–79 require carrying amount and recoverable amount to be determined consistently: include working capital in both or neither; for leases, follow one of the two approaches consistently. Detect it by building a reconciliation from the balance sheet to CGU carrying amount line by line against the DCF's cash flow items.

Practice Questions

Q1. Retail CGU impairment with ROU [L3]

Elite Footwear Ltd operates 40 stores; each store is a CGU. Store 17 (Pune) at 31 March 2026 (₹ lakh): leasehold improvements 180; ROU 620; lease liability 680; inventory 90; payables 60. Pre-tax cash flows excluding lease payments for the remaining lease term of 5 years: 150, 160, 165, 170, 170 (year-end). Annual lease payments 165 (fixed). Pre-tax discount rate excluding leases 13%; IBR 9.5%. The store has no terminal value (the lease will not be renewed).

(a) Compute VIU under both consistent approaches to leases and show that they lead to comparable conclusions. (b) Determine the impairment loss and its allocation between leasehold improvements and ROU (FVLCD of leasehold improvements is nil; ROU sub-lease FVLCD ₹480 lakh). (c) Pass entries. (d) Explain the effect on FY27 depreciation and interest.

💡 Hint: Decide first whether inventory and payables are inside or outside the carrying amount, and keep cash flows consistent with that choice.

Q2. Goodwill test for a segment [L3]

Vista Pharma Ltd acquired a formulations business in FY2023, generating goodwill of ₹450 crore, monitored at the "Domestic Formulations" operating segment, which comprises three CGUs (Acute, Chronic, OTC). Carrying amounts (₹ crore): Acute 600, Chronic 900, OTC 300 (excluding goodwill); a corporate R&D centre of ₹150 crore can be allocated on revenue (Acute 40%, Chronic 45%, OTC 15%). Recoverable amounts: Acute 720, Chronic 1,180, OTC 280; segment-level VIU 2,300.

(a) Identify the testing levels and sequence (bottom-up for CGUs, then the group with goodwill). (b) Compute impairment at each level. (c) Allocate any loss. (d) Draft key disclosures under Ind AS 36.134.

Q3. Pre-tax rate and sensitivity build [L3]

A thermal power CGU has post-tax cash flows for FY27–FY31 of ₹210, 225, 240, 250 and 255 crore, tax payments included at 25.168% with tax depreciation shield, terminal growth 3%, post-tax WACC 10.5%; carrying amount ₹2,600 crore.

(a) Describe how to derive the pre-tax rate using goal seek. (b) Specify the two-way data table to show headroom for WACC 9.5–11.5% and growth 2–4%. (c) Explain the breakeven disclosures required if a reasonably possible change would cause impairment.

Q4. Reversal and US GAAP comparison [L2]

A telecom company impaired a circle CGU by ₹800 crore in FY24 (goodwill ₹300 crore, spectrum ₹350 crore, network PPE ₹150 crore). In FY26 tariff increases raise recoverable amount above carrying amount by ₹900 crore. Carrying amounts that would have existed without impairment are higher than current carrying amounts by ₹290 crore for spectrum and ₹110 crore for PPE. Determine the reversal, pass entries, and explain how the result would differ under US GAAP.
