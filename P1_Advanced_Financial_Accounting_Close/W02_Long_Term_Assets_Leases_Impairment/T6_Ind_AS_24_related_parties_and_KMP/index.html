Roadmap topic: Ind AS 24 Related Party Disclosures: Identifying KMP, holding/subsidiary/associate relationships, and arm's-length transaction schedules

Overview

Related party transactions (RPTs) are where Indian corporate governance failures usually begin: promoter-group loans routed through subsidiaries, royalty payments to a parent, assets bought from a director's relative at inflated prices, guarantees for promoter entities. In response, India layers four regimes on top of each other. Ind AS 24 requires disclosure of relationships, transactions, balances and KMP compensation in the financial statements. The Companies Act 2013 (Sec 2(76), 2(77), 177 and 188, Form AOC-2) imposes approval requirements and Board-report disclosure. SEBI LODR (Reg 2(1)(zb), 2(1)(zc) and Reg 23) adds a wider definition for listed entities, audit-committee approval by independent directors, shareholder approval for material RPTs with a ban on related parties voting, and half-yearly disclosure. The Income-tax Act's transfer pricing provisions (Sec 92–92F, Sec 92BA) require arm's-length pricing and documentation. CARO 2020 clause 3(xiii) asks the auditor to report on compliance with Sec 177 and 188.

A finance professional working on the close, a listed company's secretarial-finance interface or an audit needs to: identify related parties under each definition (they differ); build and maintain a related-party master and group map; capture transactions and balances through the ERP; classify each RPT by approval route; evidence arm's-length pricing; and draft the Ind AS 24 note and regulatory disclosures. NFRA and SEBI enforcement in recent years (including adjudication orders on undisclosed promoter-group RPTs) shows that "we disclosed it somewhere" is not enough — the definitions and approvals must each be satisfied.

The learning sequence: start with the Ind AS 24 definition (s01, c01) and KMP (s02, c02), map the group (s03), then overlay the Companies Act (s04, c04) and SEBI LODR (s05, c03) approval regimes, evidence arm's-length pricing (s06, c05) and finally prepare disclosures (s07).

Related concepts to study

Control and significant influence assessment

Control/significant influence assessment → Week 3 (w3_t5)

Ind AS 24's entity tests depend on whether one party controls, jointly controls or significantly influences another. Those terms are defined in Ind AS 110 (control: power, exposure to variable returns, ability to use power to affect returns), Ind AS 111 (joint control: contractually agreed sharing requiring unanimous consent for relevant activities) and Ind AS 28 (significant influence: power to participate in policy decisions, presumed at 20% or more of voting power). Week 3 Topic 5 covers these in depth, including potential voting rights, de facto control and substantive versus protective rights. For RPT work, any change in those conclusions — for example, a 22% investee where a shareholders' agreement gives the investor no board seat — changes the related-party population and must be reflected in the RPT master.

US GAAP ASC 850

US GAAP ASC 850: lighter disclosures, no KMP compensation note (covered by SEC proxy rules)

ASC 850 requires disclosure of material related party transactions: the nature of the relationship, a description of the transactions, dollar amounts, amounts due to or from related parties and, if not apparent, the terms and manner of settlement. It excludes compensation arrangements, expense allowances and similar items in the ordinary course of business, so there is no KMP compensation note equivalent to Ind AS 24.17. For SEC registrants, executive compensation is disclosed in the proxy statement under Regulation S-K Item 402, and related person transactions above USD 120,000 under Item 404. ASC 850 also permits an arm's-length assertion only if it can be substantiated — the same rule as Ind AS 24.23. Indian subsidiaries of US groups therefore prepare more detailed Ind AS 24 notes than their parents' US GAAP notes.

CARO 2020 and enforcement focus

CARO 2020 clause 3(xiii): RPT compliance with Sec 177 and 188; NFRA/SEBI enforcement focus on RPTs

CARO 2020 clause 3(xiii) requires the statutory auditor to report whether all transactions with related parties are in compliance with Sec 177 and 188 of the Companies Act, where applicable, and whether the details have been disclosed in the financial statements as required by applicable accounting standards. The auditor's procedures (SA 550 Related Parties) include obtaining management's related-party list, testing completeness against registers (Sec 189 register of contracts, MBP-1 disclosures of interest by directors), and checking approvals. NFRA audit-quality inspection reports have cited inadequate RPT identification and testing; SEBI has passed orders against listed companies and promoters for undisclosed or unapproved RPTs and for diversion through group entities. Treat RPTs as a significant risk in every audit plan.

Inter-company reconciliations feeding RPT schedules

→ Week 4 (w4_t4): inter-company reconciliations feed RPT schedules

The Ind AS 24 note in standalone financial statements, the Reg 23(9) half-yearly disclosure and the Form AOC-2 all draw on the same data: transactions and balances with group companies. That data originates in inter-company accounts, which must be reconciled (both sides agreeing, cut-off aligned, foreign exchange differences explained) before consolidation eliminations in Week 4 Topic 4. An unreconciled inter-company balance usually means one of the RPT disclosures is wrong. Build the RPT schedule as a by-product of the inter-company reconciliation: one row per counterparty and transaction type, with the matching counterparty balance and the difference explained.

Common pitfalls

Using only Companies Act definitions

Using only Companies Act definitions and missing Ind AS 24 parties (KMP close-family entities, post-employment plans)

Secretarial teams maintain the Sec 2(76) list, and finance often reuses it for Ind AS 24. But Ind AS 24 captures parties the Act omits: entities controlled or jointly controlled by close family members of KMP; entities where KMP has significant influence; post-employment benefit plans (the gratuity trust, PF trust, superannuation trust); management entities providing KMP services; and associates and JVs of group members. The Act also captures parties Ind AS 24 may not (e.g., a private company where a director is merely a member). Detect it by maintaining one master with a column per definition (Ind AS 24, Sec 2(76), Reg 2(1)(zb)) and checking that the gratuity and PF trusts appear in the Ind AS 24 note.

Disclosing transactions but not balances and commitments

Disclosing transactions but not outstanding balances, guarantees and commitments

The RPT note often lists the year's sales, purchases and loans but omits closing receivables, payables, loans outstanding, guarantees given for subsidiaries' borrowings and commitments such as capital contribution undertakings. Ind AS 24.18 requires the amount of outstanding balances, including commitments, with their terms and conditions (secured or not, nature of consideration), details of guarantees given or received, provisions for doubtful debts and bad-debt expense. People miss them because the ERP report used is a transaction report. Detect by tying closing balances to the inter-company reconciliation and the guarantee register.

Assuming Board or audit committee approval satisfies SEBI

Assuming Board/audit committee approval satisfies SEBI material-RPT shareholder approval

Under Sec 188, a transaction in the ordinary course of business at arm's length needs no Board or shareholder approval. Under SEBI LODR Reg 23(4), a material RPT requires prior shareholders' approval regardless of ordinary course or arm's length, and no related party may vote in favour. Teams often rely on audit-committee omnibus approval and the Sec 188 exemption, then discover mid-year that cumulative transactions with a group company crossed the materiality threshold. Detect by tracking cumulative RPT value per counterparty against the Reg 23 threshold monthly, and by obtaining shareholder approval in advance (typically at the AGM for the next year).

Omitting independent directors' fees from KMP compensation

Omitting independent directors' sitting fees and commission from KMP compensation

Ind AS 24 defines KMP to include "any director (whether executive or otherwise)", so independent and non-executive directors are KMP. Their sitting fees (Sec 197(5), up to ₹1 lakh per meeting) and profit-linked commission (Sec 197(1), within 1% of net profits for non-executive directors where there is an MD/WTD) are KMP compensation under 24.17 (short-term benefits). Preparers omit them because the Companies Act KMP definition (Sec 2(51)) excludes non-executive directors. Detect by tying the KMP note to the corporate-governance report's remuneration table.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the related-party list for a listed group [L3]

Vindhya Industries Ltd (NSE-listed) has: Promoter Mr A (holds 38% directly; Chairman, non-executive); Mr A's son B (MD and CEO); B's wife C, who owns 70% of C Interiors Pvt Ltd; Mr A's brother D, who owns 100% of D Logistics LLP; a 65% subsidiary V Steel Ltd; V Steel's 30% associate V Coal Ltd; a 50:50 JV V-Kansai Ltd with an unrelated Japanese company; an independent director E who is a partner in E & Co, the company's legal advisers; an investor F Capital holding 11% and nominating one director; the V Industries Employees' Gratuity Trust; and CFO G whose mother owns 30% of G Foods Pvt Ltd (no board seat). Required: (a) for each party, state whether it is a related party under Ind AS 24, Sec 2(76) and SEBI Reg 2(1)(zb), with reasons; (b) identify which of their transactions require audit committee approval under Reg 23(2).

💡 Hint: Work through each definition separately; they diverge on family members, shareholders and plans.

Q2. RPT approval matrix [L2]

For FY27, Vindhya expects: sales of steel to V Steel ₹1,350 crore (consolidated turnover ₹11,200 crore; standalone turnover ₹8,400 crore; net worth ₹3,600 crore); interior fit-out contract with C Interiors ₹4.5 crore at market rates; logistics services from D Logistics ₹28 crore; legal fees to E & Co ₹1.2 crore; a loan of ₹40 crore to V-Kansai at 9%; a corporate guarantee of ₹300 crore for V Steel's bank loan. Required: prepare an approval matrix showing, for each, the audit committee, Board and shareholder approvals required under Sec 177, Sec 188 and Reg 23, and the disclosures in AOC-2 and the half-yearly Reg 23(9) filing.

Q3. Draft the Ind AS 24 note [L2]

Using the following FY26 data for Vindhya (₹ crore), draft the related-party note: sales to V Steel 1,120 (receivable 186); dividend received from V Steel 45; purchases from V Coal 210 (payable 34); loan to V-Kansai 40 (interest 3.6); guarantee for V Steel loan 300 (outstanding 240); contribution to gratuity trust 6; B's remuneration: salary 6.2, perquisites 0.4, PF 0.5, gratuity (actuarial allocation not available), ESOP charge 2.1; commission to Mr A 1.5; sitting fees to independent directors 0.36; legal fees to E & Co 1.0. Required: present by category per Ind AS 24.19, with KMP compensation per 24.17.

Q4. Arm's-length defence [L3]

The audit committee asks whether the purchase of coal from V Coal at ₹6,800 per tonne is at arm's length. Market data: Coal India e-auction average ₹6,200 per tonne for comparable grade; two unrelated suppliers quoted ₹6,600 and ₹7,100 delivered; V Coal's price includes delivery and a 60-day credit period. Required: (a) select and justify a method; (b) design the benchmarking analysis; (c) state what the audit committee should minute; (d) explain whether Sec 92BA applies.

Q5. Interview: three definitions of related party [L1]

Explain to an interviewer, in a structured answer, why an Indian listed company must maintain three related-party lists, and give one example of a party that appears on each list but not the others.
