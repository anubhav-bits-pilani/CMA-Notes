Key concept

Arm's-Length RPT Schedule: Column Design

Concept: Arm's-length schedule columns: party, relationship, nature, approved limit, actual value, pricing basis/benchmark, closing balance, guarantees

Statement

An RPT schedule (register) maintained for audit committee review, Companies Act and SEBI compliance and Ind AS 24 disclosure contains, for each related party and transaction type, at least:

# Column Content
1 Party Legal name, CIN/PAN, ERP codes
2 Relationship Nature and path (e.g., "fellow subsidiary", "entity controlled by spouse of MD"); flags for Ind AS 24, Sec 2(76), Reg 2(1)(zb)
3 Nature of transaction Sec 188(1) clause or other (loan, guarantee, reimbursement), and Ind AS 24.21 type
4 Approved limit Value approved, approving body (AC omnibus / Board / shareholders), resolution date, validity
5 Actual value Year-to-date and period values (quarter, half-year)
6 Pricing basis / benchmark Method (CUP, cost-plus, TNMM, valuer), benchmark data, conclusion, evidence reference
7 Closing balance Receivable/payable/loan outstanding, terms (secured, interest, tenure), provisions, bad-debt expense
8 Guarantees Given/received, limit, amount outstanding under guaranteed facility, Ind AS 109 liability

Key derived metrics: \(\text{Utilisation} = \frac{\text{Actual value}}{\text{Approved limit}}\) and \(\text{Materiality headroom} = T - \text{Cumulative value}\), where \(T\) is the SEBI material RPT threshold.

Explanation

The schedule turns three separate compliance requirements into one controlled record:

  • Approvals. Columns 4 and 5 show whether actual transactions stay within the approved limit (omnibus approvals specify maximum values; SEBI quarterly review of omnibus transactions). A utilisation above, say, 80% should trigger a fresh approval before the limit is breached.
  • Arm's length. Column 6 records why the transaction qualifies as arm's length — needed for the Sec 188 exemption, for any Ind AS 24.23 statement and for transfer pricing. A blank or "market rate" without evidence fails review.
  • Disclosure. Columns 2, 3, 5, 7 and 8 map directly to the Ind AS 24.18–19 note (category, transactions, balances, terms, guarantees), Form AOC-2 and the Reg 23(9) half-yearly filing.

Additional useful columns: ordinary-course assessment (yes/no with reason); cumulative value versus SEBI threshold \(T\) and Sec 188 Rule 15(3) threshold; materiality flag for AOC-2 Part 2; counterparty confirmation status (inter-company reconciliation); responsible business owner.

Build tips:

  • Drive columns 5 and 7 from the ERP using the related-party flag on vendor/customer masters; avoid manual re-keying.
  • Use Power Query to append monthly GL extracts and map counterparties to the master; a Power BI page can show utilisation and headroom per party for the audit committee.
  • Lock the approved-limit column to board/committee minutes; change only with a new resolution reference.

Practice Questions

Q1. Design the schedule [L3]

Build an Excel/Power BI RPT schedule for a listed group with 30 related parties. Specify: the master-data table, the monthly GL extract mapping, the approval register, the calculated columns (utilisation, SEBI headroom, Rule 15(3) test), the exception reports for the audit committee and the outputs for the Ind AS 24 note and the Reg 23(9) filing.

💡 Hint: Separate static master data, approval data and transactional data into different tables joined by party ID.

Q2. Populate from data [L2]

From the following, prepare the schedule rows and identify exceptions: (a) purchases from a promoter company ₹420 crore against an omnibus limit of ₹400 crore, priced at the supplier's list price with no benchmark; (b) rent paid to the chairman ₹0.6 crore, approved ₹0.6 crore, benchmarked by a valuer; (c) a ₹75 crore guarantee for a JV's loan (outstanding ₹60 crore), approved by the Board, no audit committee approval recorded.
