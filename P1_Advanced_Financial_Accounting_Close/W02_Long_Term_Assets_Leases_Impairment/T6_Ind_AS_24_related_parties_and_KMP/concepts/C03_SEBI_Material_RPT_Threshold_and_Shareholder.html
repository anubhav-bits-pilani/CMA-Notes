Key concept

SEBI Material RPT Threshold and Shareholder Approval

Concept: SEBI material RPT: above ₹1,000 Cr or 10% of annual consolidated turnover, whichever lower — prior shareholder approval; related parties cannot vote in favour

Statement

Under SEBI LODR Reg 23(1), a transaction with a related party is material if the transaction(s) to be entered into, individually or taken together with previous transactions during a financial year, exceed:

\[ T = \min\left(\text{₹}1{,}000 \text{ crore},\ 10\% \times \text{Annual consolidated turnover}_{\text{last audited}}\right) \]

(For payments for brand usage or royalty, the threshold is 5% of annual consolidated turnover.) Under Reg 23(4), every material RPT and every subsequent material modification requires prior approval of shareholders through a resolution, and no related party shall vote to approve the resolution, whether or not it is a party to the particular transaction.

Explanation

Measuring the threshold.

  • Turnover is the consolidated turnover per the last audited financial statements of the listed entity (for FY27 transactions, FY26 audited accounts once available; until then, FY25).
  • The test is per related party, cumulative over the financial year, aggregating all transactions with that related party (including those by subsidiaries with that party, since Reg 2(1)(zc) covers subsidiaries' RPTs).
  • For contracts spanning years, the value for each financial year is assessed; many companies seek approval for the expected annual value with a cushion.
  • Loans and guarantees are measured by the principal amount (and interest) involved; treat guarantees at their limit.

Approvals. Material RPTs need, in sequence: audit committee approval (independent directors only), Board approval (usually, to propose the resolution), and prior shareholder approval — by ordinary resolution with related parties abstaining from voting in favour. Postal ballot or AGM; omnibus approvals at an AGM are valid until the next AGM (maximum 15 months). Ratification after the event is not permitted under LODR.

Interaction with Sec 188. Sec 188 exempts ordinary-course arm's-length transactions; Reg 23 does not. A material RPT at arm's length in the ordinary course still needs SEBI shareholder approval. Conversely, a non-material transaction outside the ordinary course may need Sec 188 shareholder approval under Rule 15(3) even though Reg 23(4) does not apply.

Exemptions (Reg 23(5)): transactions between a listed holding company and its wholly owned subsidiary (or between two WOSs) whose accounts are consolidated and placed before shareholders; transactions between government companies; and other notified cases.

Date sensitivity. SEBI's Board approved in 2025 a move from the flat "₹1,000 crore or 10%" test to turnover-scaled thresholds (higher absolute thresholds for larger companies). Confirm the threshold in the LODR text applicable on the date of the transaction.

Illustration

Sabarmati Pharma Ltd: FY25 audited consolidated turnover ₹8,400 crore. Threshold \(T = \min(1{,}000,\ 840) = \text{₹}840\) crore. Royalty threshold \(= 5\% \times 8{,}400 = \text{₹}420\) crore.

Purchases from the API maker require audit-committee approval by independent directors and prior shareholder approval at the AGM (or postal ballot) before the cumulative value crosses ₹840 crore; the promoter and all promoter-group shareholders cannot vote in favour.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute thresholds [L1]

For listed companies with FY25 consolidated turnover of (a) ₹4,200 crore, (b) ₹10,000 crore and (c) ₹62,000 crore, compute the material RPT threshold and the royalty threshold under the flat test.

Q2. Aggregation across group entities [L2]

A listed company (consolidated turnover ₹15,000 crore) buys ₹520 crore from a promoter-group company; its 80% subsidiary buys ₹380 crore from the same company; its wholly owned subsidiary sells ₹200 crore to that company. Required: determine whether the related party's transactions are material, which entity's shareholders must approve and who cannot vote.

💡 Hint: Reg 2(1)(zc) brings subsidiaries' transactions with the listed entity's related parties into scope.

Q3. Crossing the threshold mid-year [L2]

A company obtained audit committee omnibus approval for ₹700 crore of purchases from a promoter entity for FY26 (threshold ₹900 crore). By January 2026 purchases reach ₹860 crore and are expected to reach ₹980 crore by March. Required: explain the steps required before further purchases, the consequences of continuing without approval, and how the half-yearly disclosure is affected.
