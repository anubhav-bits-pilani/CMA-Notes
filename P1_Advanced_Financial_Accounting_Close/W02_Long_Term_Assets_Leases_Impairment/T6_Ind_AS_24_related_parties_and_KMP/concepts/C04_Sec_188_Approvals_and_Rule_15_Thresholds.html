Key concept

Sec 188 Approvals and Rule 15 Thresholds

Concept: Sec 188: Board approval; ordinary resolution above Rule 15 thresholds; exempt if in ordinary course and at arm's length

Statement

For a contract or arrangement with a related party falling within Sec 188(1)(a)–(g):

  1. Exemption. If the transaction is in the ordinary course of business and on an arm's length basis, Sec 188(1) does not apply (proviso to Sec 188(1)).
  2. Otherwise, Board consent by a resolution at a meeting of the Board is required (Rule 15(1)–(2)).
  3. Prior shareholders' approval by ordinary resolution is required where the transaction exceeds the thresholds in Rule 15(3) of the Companies (Meetings of Board and its Powers) Rules 2014, and related-party members do not vote on the resolution (except for holding–WOS transactions covered by the exemption).

Rule 15(3) thresholds (turnover and net worth per the audited financial statements of the preceding financial year):

Sec 188(1) clause Transaction Threshold for shareholder approval
(a) Sale, purchase or supply of goods or materials, directly or through an agent ≥ 10% of turnover
(b) Selling or otherwise disposing of, or buying, property of any kind ≥ 10% of net worth
(c) Leasing of property of any kind ≥ 10% of turnover
(d) Availing or rendering of any services, directly or through an agent ≥ 10% of turnover
(f) Appointment to any office or place of profit in the company, its subsidiary or associate Monthly remuneration > ₹2.5 lakh
(g) Underwriting the subscription of securities or derivatives Remuneration > 1% of net worth

Thresholds apply to transactions individually or taken together with previous transactions during a financial year. (Clause (e), appointment of agents, is covered within (a) and (d) "through an agent".)

Explanation

Sequence of tests. First establish that the counterparty is a Sec 2(76) related party and the transaction is a Sec 188(1) category (loans, guarantees and investments are not — they fall under Sec 185/186). Then apply the ordinary course and arm's length exemption — both limbs must be met, with documented evidence (c05, s06). If not exempt, Board approval is required at a meeting, with the agenda disclosing the prescribed particulars (related party name, relationship, nature, duration, material terms, monetary value, advance, pricing method). Interested directors may not participate (Sec 184(2)). If the Rule 15(3) threshold is crossed, the Board proposes an ordinary resolution with an explanatory statement (Sec 102).

Audit committee overlay. For companies with an audit committee (Sec 177), every RPT — including exempt ordinary-course transactions — needs audit committee approval (possibly omnibus). So the exemption removes Board/shareholder approval under Sec 188, not audit-committee approval.

Listed entities. SEBI Reg 23 applies on top, with no ordinary-course exemption for material RPTs (c03).

Consequences of non-compliance. Contract voidable at the option of the Board or shareholders unless ratified within three months; the director concerned indemnifies the company against loss (Sec 188(3)–(4)); penalty under Sec 188(5) (₹25 lakh for listed, ₹5 lakh for other companies, on the director or employee who entered into or authorised the contract). CARO 2020 3(xiii) reporting.

Disclosure. Form AOC-2 in the Board's report (non-arm's-length contracts; material arm's-length contracts); Sec 189 register of contracts (Form MBP-4).

Illustration

Krishna Textiles Ltd (unlisted public company): FY25 turnover ₹1,800 crore; net worth ₹640 crore. Proposed FY26 transactions:

Transaction Counterparty Value Ordinary course & arm's length? Approvals
Purchase of yarn Fellow subsidiary ₹210 crore Yes (benchmarked to market) Audit committee only (exempt from Sec 188); AOC-2 Part 2 if material
Sale of a closed mill Promoter's company ₹70 crore (valuer ₹82 crore) No (not ordinary course; below value) Board at meeting; 70 ≥ 10% × 640 = 64 → ordinary resolution, related parties not voting; AOC-2 Part 1
Leasing office from director's wife Relative of director ₹1.2 crore p.a. (market ₹1.0 crore) No (above market) Board; 1.2 < 180 → no shareholder approval; AOC-2 Part 1
Appointment of MD's son as GM Relative ₹3.1 lakh per month Office or place of profit (not exempt in practice) Board; > ₹2.5 lakh per month → ordinary resolution

Connections

Implements the Companies Act framework in s04; contrasts with SEBI materiality in c03. The arm's-length limb uses evidence from s06 and the schedule format in c05. CARO 2020 3(xiii) reporting (topic related concept) depends on this analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply Rule 15(3) [L1]

A company has FY25 turnover ₹950 crore and net worth ₹310 crore. For each transaction (none in the ordinary course at arm's length) state whether shareholder approval is required: (a) purchase of goods ₹88 crore; (b) purchase of land ₹33 crore; (c) services availed ₹96 crore; (d) appointment of a director's daughter at ₹2.4 lakh per month; (e) lease rentals ₹12 crore.

Q2. Exempt but still approved [L2]

A transaction is in the ordinary course and at arm's length. Explain which approvals are still required for (a) a listed company and (b) an unlisted public company with turnover ₹80 crore, paid-up capital ₹4 crore and borrowings ₹20 crore, considering Sec 177 applicability.

💡 Hint: Check Rule 6 of the Meetings of Board Rules for when an audit committee is mandatory.

Q3. Loans to a related party [L2]

A company proposes a ₹50 crore loan to a private company in which its director is a director and member. Required: explain why Sec 188 does not apply, which provisions do apply (Sec 185, 186, 177), and the Ind AS 24 and Ind AS 109 implications.
