KMP Under Ind AS 24 Versus Companies Act Sec 2(51)

Scope: Ind AS 24 KMP (incl. non-executive directors) vs Companies Act Sec 2(51) KMP (MD/CEO, WTD, CFO, CS)

Why it matters

"KMP" means different people depending on which law you are applying. Ind AS 24 uses a functional definition that includes every director, executive or not, plus senior managers who plan, direct and control the business. The Companies Act uses a designated-office definition covering the MD/CEO/manager, whole-time directors, CFO and CS, plus officers the Board designates. Using the wrong list leads to incomplete KMP compensation disclosure, missed related parties (entities controlled by KMP's families) and wrong approvals. It is also an interview favourite because the two definitions are easy to confuse.

Core notes

Ind AS 24 definition (24.9)

Key management personnel are those persons having authority and responsibility for planning, directing and controlling the activities of the entity, directly or indirectly, including any director (whether executive or otherwise) of that entity.

Features:

  • All directors are KMP — executive directors, non-executive directors, independent directors and nominee directors.
  • Functional, not titular. Senior executives below Board level are KMP if they have authority and responsibility for planning, directing and controlling the entity's activities — e.g., a group COO or business head with entity-wide authority. Plant heads or functional heads with authority only over their function are usually not KMP.
  • "Directly or indirectly" captures people who exercise that authority through others (for example, a promoter-chairman emeritus who is not a director but directs the business — a shadow director).
  • KMP of a parent are related parties of the reporting entity (24.9(a)(3)).
  • Management entities providing KMP services are related parties; the services are disclosed under 24.17A.

Companies Act Sec 2(51)

"Key managerial personnel", in relation to a company, means:

  1. the Chief Executive Officer or the Managing Director or the Manager;
  2. the Company Secretary;
  3. the Whole-time Director;
  4. the Chief Financial Officer;
  5. such other officer, not more than one level below the directors who is in whole-time employment, designated as key managerial personnel by the Board; and
  6. such other officer as may be prescribed.

Sec 203 with Rule 8 of the Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules 2014 requires every listed company and every other public company with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more to appoint whole-time KMP: MD/CEO/manager (or WTD), CS and CFO. Private companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more must appoint a whole-time CS (Rule 8A).

Comparison

Feature Ind AS 24 KMP Sec 2(51) KMP

Non-executive and independent directors Included Excluded
CFO, CS Included if they meet the functional test (CFO usually does; CS often does by practice, but assess) Always included
Senior management below Board (e.g., business CEO) Included if functional test met Only if designated by the Board (one level below directors)

SEBI LODR adds a third concept — "senior management" (Reg 16(1)(d)), the core management team one level below the executive directors, including functional heads, CS and CFO — for disclosure of changes and Board-level information; it is not a related-party test.

Why the CS is debated under Ind AS 24

Many companies include the Company Secretary as KMP in the Ind AS 24 note simply because the CS is Sec 2(51) KMP. Strictly, Ind AS 24 asks whether the CS has authority for planning, directing and controlling the entity's activities; a compliance-focused CS may not. Practice in India is mixed; inclusion is common and conservative. Document the assessment, and in any case include all directors.

Practical identification

  1. Start with the Board: every director is Ind AS 24 KMP.
  2. Add Sec 2(51) KMP (MD/CEO, WTD, CFO, CS) and test each against the functional definition.
  3. Review the organisation chart for executives with entity-wide authority (CXOs reporting to the MD).
  4. Add KMP of the parent (for the reporting entity's related-party list).
  5. Obtain declarations from each KMP of close family members and entities they or their family control, jointly control or significantly influence (MBP-1 covers directors' interests; extend the form for Ind AS 24 purposes).
  6. Refresh on every appointment or cessation.

Example

Kaveri Software Ltd's Board: Executive Chairman (promoter), MD & CEO, one WTD (CTO), four independent directors, one nominee of a PE investor. Sec 2(51) KMP: MD & CEO, WTD, CFO, CS. Executive leadership: President — Americas (entity-wide revenue authority), CHRO, Head of Delivery.

Person Ind AS 24 KMP? Sec 2(51) KMP?
Executive Chairman Yes (director) No, unless also MD/WTD or designated
MD & CEO Yes Yes
WTD (CTO) Yes Yes
Independent directors (4) Yes No

KMP compensation disclosure under 24.17 covers all Ind AS 24 KMP — including independent directors' sitting fees and commission (topic pitfall).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Executive Chairman designated "Manager"? Not usually; Sec 2(51) applies only if the Chairman is also MD, WTD, CEO or manager.
  • Shadow directors. A promoter who is not on the Board but on whose instructions the Board acts is KMP under Ind AS 24 (indirect control) and an "officer who is in default" under Sec 2(60).
  • Group executives paid by the parent. If the parent pays the KMP and recharges nothing, the subsidiary still discloses that KMP services are provided by the parent; amounts are disclosed where determinable.
  • KMP for part of the year. Disclose compensation for the period as KMP.
  • Nominee directors of lenders. They are KMP under Ind AS 24, but the lender is not thereby a related party (24.11(c)).

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 24 KMP = authority and responsibility for planning, directing and controlling, and includes every director, executive or not.
  • Sec 2(51) KMP = MD/CEO/manager, WTD, CFO, CS and Board-designated officers; excludes non-executive directors.
  • Maintain both lists; use Ind AS 24 for the financial statement note and Sec 2(51) for appointment and Sec 188 purposes.
  • Independent directors' fees and commission are KMP compensation under Ind AS 24.
  • Obtain annual KMP declarations of close family and their entities to complete the related-party population.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify KMP [L1]

A pharma company's officers: Chairman (non-executive, promoter); Vice-Chairman & MD; two WTDs; five independent directors; CFO; CS; Chief Scientific Officer (reports to MD, heads R&D globally); Head of Internal Audit; Plant Head — Baddi. Required: prepare a two-column table showing who is KMP under Ind AS 24 and under Sec 2(51), with brief reasons for any judgement.

Q2. KMP of the parent [L2]

A listed subsidiary's parent has a CFO who is not on the subsidiary's Board. The parent CFO's wife owns 100% of a consultancy that billed the subsidiary ₹45 lakh. Required: determine whether the consultancy is a related party of the subsidiary under Ind AS 24, Sec 2(76) and Reg 2(1)(zb), and state the disclosure required.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 24.9(a)(3) refers to KMP of a parent; check what the Companies Act rules say about holding-company KMP.

Q3. CS as KMP under Ind AS 24 [L2]

An audit manager challenges the inclusion of the CS in the Ind AS 24 KMP note on the grounds that the CS has no operational authority. Required: argue both sides and recommend a documented position.

Q4. Interview: one list or two? [L1]

Answer in under 150 words: "Why can't we just use the Companies Act KMP list for our Ind AS 24 note?"
