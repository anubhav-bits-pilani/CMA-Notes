Subtopic note

Companies Act RPT Framework: Sec 2(76), 2(77), 177, 188 and AOC-2

Scope: Companies Act: Sec 2(76) related party, Sec 2(77) relatives, Sec 188 approvals, Sec 177 audit committee omnibus approval, Form AOC-2

Why it matters

The Companies Act governs who must approve an RPT and what the Board's report must say, and non-compliance makes contracts voidable and directors personally liable. For every company — listed or not — the finance and secretarial teams must classify each proposed RPT into an approval route before it is executed. CARO 2020 clause 3(xiii) requires auditors to report on compliance with Sec 177 and 188, so gaps surface in the audit report. This note covers the definitions and approval mechanics; SEBI's additional layer for listed entities is in s05.

Core notes

Sec 2(76) — related party

With reference to a company, "related party" means:

  1. a director or his relative;
  2. a key managerial personnel or his relative;
  3. a firm, in which a director, manager or his relative is a partner;
  4. a private company in which a director or manager or his relative is a member or director;
  5. a public company in which a director or manager is a director and holds, along with his relatives, more than 2% of its paid-up share capital;
  6. any body corporate whose Board of Directors, managing director or manager is accustomed to act in accordance with the advice, directions or instructions of a director or manager;
  7. any person on whose advice, directions or instructions a director or manager is accustomed to act (except advice given in a professional capacity);
  8. any body corporate which is (A) a holding, subsidiary or an associate company of such company; (B) a subsidiary of a holding company to which it is also a subsidiary; or (C) an investing company or the venturer of the company (the investing company or venturer being a body corporate whose investment results in the company becoming its associate);
  9. such other person as may be prescribed — Rule 3 of the Companies (Specification of Definitions Details) Rules 2014: a director (other than an independent director) or KMP of the holding company, or his relative.

Sec 2(77) — relative

A person is a relative of another if (i) they are members of a Hindu Undivided Family; (ii) they are husband and wife; or (iii) one is related to the other in the manner prescribed — Rule 4: father (including step-father), mother (including step-mother), son (including step-son), son's wife, daughter, daughter's husband, brother (including step-brother), sister (including step-sister).

Note the differences from Ind AS 24 "close members of the family": the Act includes son's wife and daughter's husband and HUF members, but not spouse's children or dependants; Ind AS 24 is a judgement-based inclusive list.

Sec 188 — approvals for specified contracts

Sec 188(1) covers contracts or arrangements with a related party with respect to:

  • (a) sale, purchase or supply of any goods or materials;
  • (b) selling or otherwise disposing of, or buying, property of any kind;
  • (c) leasing of property of any kind;
  • (d) availing or rendering of any services;
  • (e) appointment of any agent for purchase or sale of goods, materials, services or property;
  • (f) such related party's appointment to any office or place of profit in the company, its subsidiary or associate company;
  • (g) underwriting the subscription of any securities or derivatives of the company.

Approval mechanics:

Requirement Detail
Board consent Resolution at a meeting of the Board (not by circulation) — Sec 188(1), Rule 15(1)–(2) of the Companies (Meetings of Board and its Powers) Rules 2014; agenda must disclose name of related party, nature of relationship, nature, duration and particulars of contract, material terms, advance paid, pricing method, whether all factors relevant were considered
Interested director Must not be present during discussion if interested (Sec 184(2))
Prior shareholders' approval by ordinary resolution Where the transaction exceeds Rule 15(3) thresholds (c04)
Related party voting No member shall vote on the resolution if such member is a related party (in the context of the contract) — proviso to Sec 188(1)
Wholly owned subsidiary exemption Shareholder approval not required for transactions between a holding company and its WOS whose accounts are consolidated and placed before the shareholders (proviso to Sec 188(1), inserted in 2017)
Ordinary course and arm's length exemption Nothing in Sec 188(1) applies to transactions entered into in the ordinary course of business that are on an arm's length basis (proviso to Sec 188(1))
Board's report Every contract referred to in Sec 188(1) is referred to in the Board's report with justification (Sec 188(2)) — Form AOC-2
Ratification If entered without consent, ratification by Board or shareholders within three months; otherwise voidable at the option of the Board/shareholders, and the director concerned indemnifies the company for any loss (Sec 188(3))
Penalties Sec 188(5): director or employee who authorised the contract — ₹25 lakh for listed companies, ₹5 lakh for others (post-2020 decriminalisation)

"Arm's length transaction" means a transaction between two related parties that is conducted as if they were unrelated, so that there is no conflict of interest (Explanation (b) to Sec 188). "Office or place of profit" means an office for which the holder receives remuneration beyond sitting fees or reimbursements (Explanation (a)).

Sec 177 — audit committee

Sec 177 applies to every listed company and to public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore, turnover ≥ ₹100 crore, or aggregate loans, debentures and deposits ≥ ₹50 crore (Rule 6 of the Meetings of Board Rules). Its terms of reference include approval or any subsequent modification of transactions of the company with related parties (Sec 177(4)(iv)).

Key mechanics:

  • Approval applies to all RPTs — not only those covered by Sec 188, and not only those outside the ordinary course.
  • Omnibus approval (proviso to Sec 177(4)(iv), Rule 6A): the audit committee may grant omnibus approval for RPTs proposed to be entered into, subject to criteria including: maximum value per transaction that can be allowed; extent and manner of disclosure to the audit committee; review at such intervals as it deems fit; transactions not subject to omnibus approval (e.g., sale of an undertaking); the approval being valid for not more than one financial year; and for unforeseen transactions where details are not available, omnibus approval for value up to ₹1 crore per transaction. The committee must satisfy itself of the need and the repetitive nature of transactions.
  • Where the audit committee does not approve a transaction not covered by Sec 188 (i.e., ordinary course and arm's length), it makes recommendations to the Board.
  • Ratification (Sec 177(4) provisos, 2017 amendment): for transactions up to ₹1 crore entered into by a director or officer without prior approval, ratification within three months; otherwise voidable.
  • For listed entities, SEBI LODR Reg 23(2) additionally requires that only the independent directors on the audit committee approve RPTs (s05); the Companies Act itself relies on the composition rules of Sec 177(2) (majority independent).

Form AOC-2

Prescribed under Sec 134(3)(h) and Rule 8(2) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014, attached to the Board's report. Two parts:

  1. Details of contracts or arrangements or transactions not at arm's length basis: name of related party and relationship, nature of contracts, duration, salient terms including value, justification for entering, date of Board approval, amount paid as advances, date of special/ordinary resolution in general meeting.
  2. Details of material contracts or arrangements or transactions at arm's length basis: name and relationship, nature, duration, salient terms including value, date of Board approval, advances.

In practice, most companies state "Nil" in Part 1 (all contracts at arm's length) and list material contracts in Part 2, where "material" follows the company's RPT policy.

Approval decision tree

Question Yes No
Is the counterparty a related party under Sec 2(76)? Continue Sec 188/177 do not apply (Ind AS 24 and SEBI may still)
Does the company have an audit committee? Audit committee approval (or omnibus) required for every RPT Board only, where Sec 188 applies
Is it a Sec 188(1) category? Continue Audit committee approval only
Ordinary course and arm's length? Exempt from Sec 188 Board/shareholder approval; disclose material ones in AOC-2 Part 2 Board approval at a meeting; continue
Exceeds Rule 15(3) threshold? Prior ordinary resolution; related parties do not vote (WOS exemption) Board approval suffices

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • "Ordinary course" without definition. Assess frequency, MoA objects, historical pattern, whether the transaction is normal for the industry, and whether it is revenue/operational in nature (s06).
  • Loans and guarantees. Not Sec 188(1) categories; governed by Sec 185 (loans to directors and interested entities) and Sec 186 (inter-corporate loans, guarantees, investments). Still RPTs for Sec 177, Ind AS 24 and SEBI.
  • Remuneration of directors. Governed by Sec 197; but appointment of a relative to an office or place of profit is Sec 188(1)(f).
  • Private companies. MCA exemption notifications (June 2015) exempt private companies from the related-party voting restriction and exclude some relationships; check the current exemptions.
  • Government companies. Specific exemptions apply under the 2015 notifications.

Key takeaways

  • Sec 2(76) lists nine categories of related parties; Sec 2(77) and Rule 4 define relatives narrowly by blood/marriage list.
  • Sec 188 approvals apply to seven transaction types, unless in the ordinary course and at arm's length.
  • Above Rule 15(3) thresholds, prior ordinary resolution is required, and related-party members cannot vote.
  • Sec 177 requires audit-committee approval of all RPTs, with omnibus approval limited to one year and ₹1 crore per unforeseen transaction.
  • Form AOC-2 discloses non-arm's-length contracts and material arm's-length contracts in the Board's report.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify Sec 2(76) related parties [L1]

For an unlisted public company: (a) MD's son-in-law's partnership firm; (b) a private company where a director's brother is a member holding 1 share; (c) a public company where a director is a director and holds 1.5% with his wife holding 0.8%; (d) the holding company's CFO; (e) an independent director of the holding company; (f) the company's associate. Required: state whether each is a related party, citing the sub-clause.

Q2. Approval route for five transactions [L2]

A listed company (FY25 turnover ₹2,400 crore; net worth ₹900 crore) plans: (i) purchase of raw material from a fellow subsidiary ₹310 crore at market price in the ordinary course; (ii) sale of a factory building to the promoter's company for ₹95 crore; (iii) lease of office space from the MD's wife at ₹30 lakh per annum (market rate ₹22 lakh); (iv) appointment of the CFO's son as manager at ₹2.2 lakh per month; (v) unforeseen purchase of spares from an associate ₹60 lakh. Required: for each, state the audit committee, Board and shareholder approvals under the Companies Act and the AOC-2 disclosure.

💡 Hint: Test ordinary course and arm's length first, then the Rule 15(3) thresholds.

Q3. Ratification failure [L2]

A company entered into a ₹14 crore service contract with a director's firm without audit committee or Board approval; it was not in the ordinary course. Four months have passed. Required: explain the legal consequences under Sec 188(3), the director's liability, and the CARO 2020 clause 3(xiii) reporting.

Q4. Draft AOC-2 [L1]

Draft Form AOC-2 Part 2 entries for two material arm's-length contracts: supply of components to a subsidiary for ₹520 crore (Board approval 12 May 2025) and a 5-year brand licence from the parent at 1% of net sales (Board approval 12 May 2025, shareholders' approval 25 August 2025).
