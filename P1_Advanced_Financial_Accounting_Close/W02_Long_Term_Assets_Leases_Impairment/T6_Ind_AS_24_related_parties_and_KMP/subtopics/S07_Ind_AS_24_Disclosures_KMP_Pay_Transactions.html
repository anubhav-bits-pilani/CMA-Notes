Subtopic note

Ind AS 24 Disclosures: KMP Pay, Transactions, Balances, Exemptions

Scope: Disclosures: KMP compensation (24.17), transactions and balances by category (24.18–19), guarantees, commitments, government-related entity exemption (24.25)

Why it matters

The related-party note is where all the identification and approval work becomes visible. Users read it to judge whether the entity's results depend on related-party support, whether resources are leaking to promoters and how much management is paid. Auditors under SA 550 evaluate whether the note is complete and not misleading; CARO 3(xiii) asks whether details are disclosed as required by Ind AS. For PSUs and their counterparties, the government-related entity exemption changes the scale of the note dramatically. A well-built note is a structured table driven by the related-party master and the inter-company reconciliation.

Core notes

Relationships (24.13–16)

  • Relationships between a parent and its subsidiaries are disclosed irrespective of whether there have been transactions between them (24.13; concept c01).
  • Disclose the name of the parent and, if different, the ultimate controlling party. If neither produces consolidated financial statements available for public use, disclose the name of the next most senior parent that does (24.13, 24.16).
  • These relationship disclosures are in addition to the Ind AS 27, 28, 112 requirements.

KMP compensation (24.17–17A)

Disclose KMP compensation in total and for each of the following categories (24.17; concept c02):

  • (a) short-term employee benefits;
  • (b) post-employment benefits;
  • (c) other long-term benefits;
  • (d) termination benefits;
  • (e) share-based payment.

If the entity obtains KMP services from a management entity, it need not apply 24.17 to the compensation paid by the management entity to its employees or directors, but must disclose amounts incurred for the provision of KMP services by the management entity (24.17A, 24.18A).

Transactions and balances (24.18)

If there have been related-party transactions during the periods covered, disclose the nature of the related-party relationship and information about the transactions and outstanding balances, including commitments, necessary for users to understand the potential effect of the relationship. At a minimum:

  • (a) the amount of the transactions;
  • (b) the amount of outstanding balances, including commitments, and (i) their terms and conditions, including whether they are secured, and the nature of the consideration to be provided in settlement; and (ii) details of any guarantees given or received;
  • (c) provisions for doubtful debts related to the amount of outstanding balances; and
  • (d) the expense recognised during the period in respect of bad or doubtful debts due from related parties.

Commitments include executory contracts such as committed purchases, loan commitments and capital contribution undertakings (24.21(i)), whether recognised or not.

Categories (24.19)

The disclosures in 24.18 are made separately for each of:

  1. the parent;
  2. entities with joint control of, or significant influence over, the entity;
  3. subsidiaries;
  4. associates;
  5. joint ventures in which the entity is a joint venturer;
  6. KMP of the entity or its parent; and
  7. other related parties.

Examples of transactions (24.21)

Purchases or sales of goods; purchases or sales of property and other assets; rendering or receiving of services; leases; transfers of research and development; transfers under licence agreements; transfers under finance arrangements (loans, equity contributions in cash or kind); provision of guarantees or collateral; commitments to do something if a particular event occurs; settlement of liabilities on behalf of the entity or by the entity on behalf of the related party. Participation by a parent or subsidiary in a DB plan that shares risks between group entities is also a related-party transaction (24.22).

Other rules

  • Arm's-length statements (24.23) only if substantiated (s06).
  • Aggregation (24.24): items of a similar nature may be disclosed in aggregate except where separate disclosure is necessary to understand the effects. Material individual transactions (e.g., a large asset sale to a promoter entity) should be disclosed separately.
  • Intra-group transactions are eliminated in consolidated financial statements; the consolidated note covers transactions with parties outside the consolidated group (associates, JVs, KMP, promoter entities) (24.4).

Government-related entities (24.25–27)

A reporting entity is exempt from the disclosure requirements of 24.18 (transactions, balances, commitments) in relation to related-party transactions and balances with:

  • (a) a government that has control or joint control of, or significant influence over, the reporting entity; and
  • (b) another entity that is a related party because the same government has control, joint control or significant influence over both (24.25).

If the exemption is applied, the entity discloses (24.26):

  • the name of the government and the nature of its relationship with the reporting entity;
  • for each individually significant transaction, its nature and amount; and for other transactions that are collectively, but not individually, significant, a qualitative or quantitative indication of their extent.

Judgement on significance (24.27): size, whether carried out on non-market terms, outside normal day-to-day operations (e.g., purchase and sale of businesses), disclosed to regulatory authorities, reported to senior management, subject to shareholder approval.

"Government" refers to government, government agencies and similar bodies whether local, national or international (24.9). For Indian CPSEs, the Government of India controls the entity and many counterparties (other CPSEs, PSU banks); the exemption makes the note workable. KMP compensation and relationships with non-government related parties are still fully disclosed.

Ind AS 24 also carries an Indian deviation permitting non-disclosure where it would conflict with the reporting entity's duties of confidentiality required by statute or a regulator (check the notified text and apply narrowly).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Gross vs net. Show sales and purchases gross, not netted.
  • Reimbursements and cost recharges. Disclose as transactions (often aggregated as "reimbursement of expenses").
  • Dividends paid to parent/promoters. Transfers of resources — disclose.
  • Guarantee amounts. Disclose both the guarantee limit and the amount outstanding under the guaranteed facility; note the financial guarantee liability under Ind AS 109 if recognised.
  • Changes during the year. Disclose transactions while the relationship existed; balances only if related at year-end.
  • Schedule III. Requires separate disclosure of loans or advances to promoters, directors, KMP and related parties repayable on demand or without terms (2021 amendment), and promoter shareholding — complementary to Ind AS 24.

Key takeaways

  • Disclose parent–subsidiary relationships always, parent and ultimate controlling party names, and the next senior parent producing public consolidated statements.
  • KMP compensation in total and by five categories; management-entity fees separately.
  • Transactions, outstanding balances, commitments, terms, guarantees, provisions and bad-debt expense — by the seven categories of 24.19.
  • Arm's-length assertions only if substantiated; aggregation only for similar items.
  • Government-related entities may use the 24.25 exemption but must disclose the government relationship and significant transactions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Complete the note [L2]

Draft the Ind AS 24 note for a company with: parent (royalty ₹22 crore; payable ₹4 crore); subsidiary (sales ₹140 crore; receivable ₹36 crore, of which ₹6 crore overdue and provided for; bad-debt expense ₹6 crore); JV (loan ₹30 crore at 8.5%, secured by pledge of JV shares; undrawn loan commitment ₹10 crore); KMP (MD salary ₹4.1 crore, PF ₹0.3 crore, ESOP charge ₹1.2 crore; independent directors' sitting fees ₹0.28 crore and commission ₹0.6 crore). Required: present the note with categories, terms and conditions and KMP compensation.

Q2. Government-related entity exemption [L2]

A CPSE oil marketing company buys crude from another CPSE (₹48,000 crore), borrows from PSU banks (₹22,000 crore outstanding) and receives subsidy compensation from the Government of India (₹9,500 crore). Required: explain how the 24.25 exemption applies, what must still be disclosed under 24.26, and whether KMP compensation is affected.

💡 Hint: Consider which transactions are individually significant under the 24.27 indicators.

Q3. Consolidated versus standalone [L1]

Explain which related-party transactions appear in the consolidated Ind AS 24 note but not in the standalone note, and vice versa, for a group with a parent, two subsidiaries, an associate and KMP.

Q4. Review the draft note [L2]

A draft note lists only transactions (sales, purchases, loans given) and omits: closing balances; a ₹500 crore guarantee for a subsidiary; a commitment to subscribe ₹80 crore to a JV's rights issue; and independent directors' commission. Required: list the deficiencies with Ind AS 24 paragraph references and explain the audit reporting consequences under CARO 2020.
