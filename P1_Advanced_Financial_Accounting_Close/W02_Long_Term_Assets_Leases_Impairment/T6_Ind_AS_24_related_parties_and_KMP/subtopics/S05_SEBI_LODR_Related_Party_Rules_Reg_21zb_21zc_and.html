Subtopic note

SEBI LODR Related Party Rules: Reg 2(1)(zb), 2(1)(zc) and Reg 23

Scope: SEBI LODR Reg 2(1)(zb)/(zc) and Reg 23: promoter-group and ≥10% holders as related parties, material RPT approval, half-yearly RPT disclosure

Why it matters

For listed entities, SEBI's RPT framework is stricter than the Companies Act in three ways: it widens who is a related party (every promoter-group entity and every 10% shareholder), it widens what is an RPT (including transactions by subsidiaries and transactions with unrelated parties that benefit a related party), and it removes the ordinary-course-and-arm's-length exemption for shareholder approval of material RPTs. Violations attract SEBI orders against the company, directors and promoters, and stock exchange fines. A listed company's finance function must monitor cumulative RPT values in real time, prepare approval papers to SEBI-prescribed standards and file half-yearly disclosures.

The framework was overhauled by SEBI's November 2021 amendments (phased in from 1 April 2022 and 1 April 2023) and has been refined repeatedly since; it is date-sensitive — always check the latest consolidated LODR text and SEBI master circular.

Core notes

Reg 2(1)(zb) — related party

A related party means a related party as defined under Sec 2(76) of the Companies Act or under the applicable accounting standards (Ind AS 24), and additionally includes:

  • (a) any person or entity forming part of the promoter or promoter group of the listed entity, irrespective of shareholding; and
  • (b) any person or any entity holding equity shares of 10% or more (20% or more from 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2023) in the listed entity, either directly or on a beneficial-interest basis under Sec 89 of the Companies Act, at any time during the immediately preceding financial year.

Certain exclusions exist (for example, for specified institutional investors in some cases; check current text). The combined effect: a listed entity's related-party list is the union of the Sec 2(76) list, the Ind AS 24 list, the promoter group (SEBI ICDR definition) and 10% shareholders.

Reg 2(1)(zc) — related party transaction

A related party transaction means a transaction involving a transfer of resources, services or obligations between:

  • (i) a listed entity or any of its subsidiaries on one hand and a related party of the listed entity or of any of its subsidiaries on the other hand; or
  • (ii) a listed entity or any of its subsidiaries on one hand, and any other person or entity on the other hand, the purpose and effect of which is to benefit a related party of the listed entity or any of its subsidiaries (from 1 April 2023),

regardless of whether a price is charged; a "transaction" with a related party includes a single transaction or a group of transactions in a contract.

Exclusions include, among others: issue of specified securities on a preferential basis in compliance with ICDR; corporate actions uniformly applicable to all shareholders (dividends, sub-division, consolidation, rights, bonus, buy-back); acceptance of fixed deposits by banks/NBFCs at uniformly applicable terms; retail purchases by directors or employees on the same terms as other employees; and certain other items added by later amendments.

Reg 23 — approvals

Policy (Reg 23(1)). The listed entity formulates a policy on materiality of RPTs and on dealing with RPTs, including clear threshold limits approved by the Board, reviewed at least once every three years.

Material RPT definition (Reg 23(1) proviso). A transaction with a related party is material if the transaction(s) to be entered into individually or taken together with previous transactions during a financial year exceed ₹1,000 crore or 10% of the annual consolidated turnover of the listed entity as per the last audited financial statements, whichever is lower (c03). For brand usage or royalty payments, material if exceeding 5% of annual consolidated turnover. SEBI's Board approved in 2025 a move to turnover-scaled thresholds for material RPTs; confirm whether and from when the amended thresholds apply before relying on the ₹1,000 crore limb.

Audit committee (Reg 23(2)–(3)).

  • All RPTs and subsequent material modifications require prior approval of the audit committee; only independent directors on the audit committee shall approve RPTs.
  • RPTs to which a subsidiary is a party (but the listed entity is not) require prior audit committee approval of the listed entity if the value exceeds a threshold (originally 10% of the subsidiary's annual standalone turnover, later refined — check current text).
  • Omnibus approval (Reg 23(3)) for repetitive transactions, subject to criteria: valid for one year; maximum value per transaction; unforeseen transactions up to ₹1 crore per transaction; quarterly review of transactions entered under omnibus approval.
  • Audit committee approval papers must contain the minimum information prescribed by SEBI (Industry Standards on minimum information for approval of RPTs, applicable from 2025).

Shareholders (Reg 23(4)). All material RPTs and subsequent material modifications require prior approval of shareholders through a resolution, and no related party shall vote to approve such resolutions, whether the entity is a related party to the particular transaction or not. Omnibus shareholder approvals obtained at an AGM are valid until the next AGM (within 15 months); those at other general meetings for up to one year (SEBI circular).

Unlike Sec 188, there is no ordinary-course-and-arm's-length exemption from shareholder approval for material RPTs.

Exemptions (Reg 23(5)). Approvals under 23(2) and 23(4) do not apply to: transactions between two government companies; transactions between a holding company and its wholly owned subsidiary whose accounts are consolidated and placed before shareholders at the general meeting; transactions between two wholly owned subsidiaries of the listed holding company whose accounts are consolidated and placed before shareholders; and certain other notified cases.

Disclosures

Disclosure Requirement
Half-yearly RPT disclosure (Reg 23(9)) Disclosure of RPTs on a consolidated basis in the SEBI-specified format, submitted to stock exchanges and published on the website — every six months, on the date of publication of standalone and consolidated financial results (timing tightened from "within 15/30 days" by 2023 amendments)
Annual report (Schedule V) Disclosures of RPTs, policy weblink, transactions with promoter/promoter group holding 10% or more
Corporate governance report Materially significant RPTs that may have potential conflict with interests of the listed entity at large
Website (Reg 46) RPT policy
Explanatory statement for shareholder approval Prescribed information including value, tenure, justification, valuation reports

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cumulative tracking. Materiality is assessed on aggregated transactions with a related party during the year; a contract approved below the threshold can become material on renewal or volume growth — obtain approval before crossing, not after.
  • Subsequent material modification. Defined in the RPT policy (often a variance of 20% or more in value); without a clear definition, changes to contract value can trigger fresh approval.
  • Indirect benefit transactions. Purchases from an unrelated supplier who pays a commission to a promoter entity are RPTs under Reg 2(1)(zc)(ii).
  • 10% shareholders. Institutional investors crossing 10% become related parties; all transactions with their group (e.g., an insurer's investments, banking relationships) need review.
  • Listed subsidiaries. Each listed entity in a group applies Reg 23 separately; a parent's shareholder approval does not cover the subsidiary's own Reg 23 requirement.

Key takeaways

  • Reg 2(1)(zb) adds all promoter-group members and ≥10% shareholders (in the preceding year) to the related-party list.
  • Reg 2(1)(zc) covers transactions of subsidiaries and transactions with third parties that benefit a related party, regardless of price.
  • All RPTs need prior audit committee approval by independent directors; omnibus approval is limited to one year and ₹1 crore per unforeseen transaction.
  • Material RPTs (lower of ₹1,000 crore and 10% of consolidated turnover, subject to SEBI's scale-based revision) need prior shareholders' approval; no related party may vote in favour.
  • Half-yearly consolidated RPT disclosure is due with the financial results; the framework is date-sensitive.

Practice Questions

Q1. Is it a related party under SEBI? [L1]

For a listed company: (a) an FII that held 10.4% for two months in FY25 and now holds 8%; (b) the promoter's sister's 100%-owned company, where the sister is part of the disclosed promoter group; (c) a mutual fund holding 6%; (d) the listed company's subsidiary's 30% associate; (e) a vendor that pays a 2% referral fee to the promoter's son. Required: classify each under Reg 2(1)(zb) and say whether transactions with them are RPTs under Reg 2(1)(zc).

Q2. Material RPT computation and approval [L2]

A listed auto company has consolidated turnover of ₹14,600 crore (FY25 audited) and standalone turnover of ₹9,800 crore. For FY26 it expects purchases from its promoter-group forging company of ₹1,120 crore and a royalty to its foreign parent of 3.5% of net sales (expected net sales ₹10,200 crore). Required: (a) determine which transactions are material; (b) state the approvals required and their timing; (c) explain who may not vote; (d) explain whether Sec 188 approval is additionally required.

💡 Hint: Apply the "whichever is lower" test and the separate royalty threshold.

Q3. Subsidiary transactions [L2]

A listed company's unlisted subsidiary (standalone turnover ₹1,800 crore) plans to buy land from the listed company's promoter for ₹240 crore. The listed company is not a party. Required: identify the approvals required under Reg 23 and the Companies Act at both entities, and the disclosure in the half-yearly filing.

Q4. Audit committee paper [L3]

Draft the information pack that the CFO should place before the audit committee for approval of a 3-year logistics contract worth ₹180 crore with a promoter-group company, aligned with SEBI's minimum information standards: include justification, pricing benchmarks, comparison with unrelated quotes, the value as a percentage of turnover of both parties, and the proposed omnibus terms.
