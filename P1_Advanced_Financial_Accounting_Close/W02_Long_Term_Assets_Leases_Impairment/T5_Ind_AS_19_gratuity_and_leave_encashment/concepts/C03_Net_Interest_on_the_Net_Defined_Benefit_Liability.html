Key concept

Net Interest on the Net Defined Benefit Liability

Concept: Net interest = Discount rate × Net defined benefit liability (DBO − plan assets), adjusted for contributions and benefits in the year

Statement

\[ \text{NI} \approx r_0 \times \left(\text{DBO}_0 - \text{FVPA}_0\right) \quad \text{(before in-year cash-flow adjustments)} \]

With the in-year adjustments made explicit:

\[ \text{NI} = \underbrace{r_0\left(\text{DBO}_0 - \sum_j \text{BP}_j w_j\right)}_{\text{interest cost}} - \underbrace{r_0\left(\text{FVPA}_0 + \sum_j C_j w_j - \sum_j \text{BP}^{\text{fund}}_j w_j\right)}_{\text{interest income}} \]

where \(r_0\) is the discount rate determined at the start of the annual reporting period (Ind AS 19.123); \(\text{DBO}_0\) and \(\text{FVPA}_0\) are the opening DBO and fair value of plan assets; \(C_j\) are contributions, \(\text{BP}_j\) benefits paid (from the fund, \(\text{BP}^{\text{fund}}_j\)); and \(w_j\) is the fraction of the year remaining after each cash flow. If there is an asset ceiling, interest on the effect of the ceiling is included (19.124).

Explanation

Net interest is the change in the net defined benefit liability (asset) during the period that arises from the passage of time (19.8). Treating the net position as a single financing item means a fully funded plan has near-zero net interest, while an unfunded plan carries a finance-like charge equal to the discount rate on the liability.

Key rules:

  • Single rate on both sides (19.125). Interest income on plan assets uses the discount rate, not the expected or actual return. The difference between actual return and interest income is a remeasurement in OCI (19.130).
  • Start-of-year rate and balances (19.123). Determined at the start of the annual period, then adjusted for contributions and benefit payments during the period. For quarterly reporting, Indian entities typically pro-rate the annual figure.
  • Plan amendments (19.123A). If a plan amendment, curtailment or settlement occurs mid-year, the net interest for the remainder of the period is determined using the net liability and the discount rate used to remeasure at that date.
  • Asset ceiling (19.126). Interest on the effect of the asset ceiling is part of net interest; changes in the ceiling effect beyond this go to OCI.
  • Presentation. Ind AS 19 does not specify the P&L line (19.134); Indian practice usually includes it within employee benefits expense; some present it in finance costs. Disclose the policy.

Illustration

Opening DBO ₹5,000 lakh; opening plan assets ₹3,800 lakh; discount rate at 1 April 2025 6.9%. Employer contribution ₹600 lakh on 30 June 2025 (9 months remaining, \(w = 0.75\)); benefits of ₹400 lakh paid from the fund on 31 December 2025 (\(w = 0.25\)).

Interest cost = 6.9% × (5,000 − 400 × 0.25) = 6.9% × 4,900 = ₹338.1 lakh. Interest income = 6.9% × (3,800 + 600 × 0.75 − 400 × 0.25) = 6.9% × 4,150 = ₹286.4 lakh (rounded). Net interest = ₹51.7 lakh, charged to P&L.

If the fund actually earned ₹250 lakh, the return on plan assets excluding interest is 250 − 286.4 = −₹36.4 lakh, an OCI loss.

Simple check: \(6.9\% \times (5{,}000 - 3{,}800) = 82.8\); the contribution early in the year reduces net interest to ₹51.7 lakh — a reason to fund early.

Connections

Part of the P&L cost in s05 and the roll-forwards in c02. Mirrors the unwinding of discount on provisions (Topic 4, c03) and the interest on lease liabilities (Topic 2) — the week's shared accretion pattern, with the distinctive feature that the asset side earns interest at the same rate. Also used in FP&A: funding the plan earlier reduces P&L net interest by the discount rate, while the fund's actual return above the rate reaches OCI, not profit — a common Treasury–Finance discussion.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute net interest with cash flows [L1]

Opening DBO ₹1,850 lakh; plan assets ₹1,400 lakh; discount rate 7.2%. Contribution ₹250 lakh on 1 October; benefits ₹180 lakh from the fund on 31 March. Required: compute interest cost, interest income and net interest.

Q2. Expected return misuse [L2]

A company's actuary computes interest income using the LIC-declared rate of 7.9% while the discount rate is 7.0%, on average plan assets of ₹30 crore. Required: compute the effect on P&L and OCI and explain why the approach is not permitted.

💡 Hint: Total comprehensive income is unchanged by the error.

Q3. Asset ceiling interest [L2]

At the start of the year, plan assets are ₹62 crore, DBO ₹55 crore and the asset ceiling ₹4 crore (effect of ceiling ₹3 crore). Discount rate 7%. Required: compute net interest including interest on the ceiling effect, and explain the result conceptually.
