Key concept

Statutory Gratuity Formula, ₹20 Lakh Cap and Five-Year Vesting

Concept: Gratuity = Last drawn wages × 15/26 × Completed years of service, capped at ₹20 lakh; 5-year vesting except death/disablement

Statement

\[ G = \min\left(W \times \frac{15}{26} \times Y,\ \text{Ceiling}\right) \]

where \(G\) is the gratuity payable on exit, \(W\) is the last drawn monthly wages (as defined — basic + DA, and under the Code on Social Security 2020 including the 50% add-back of excluded components), \(15/26\) represents 15 days' wages per year based on 26 working days per month, \(Y\) is completed years of service (a part year of more than six months counts as a full year), and the ceiling is ₹20 lakh (or the amount notified under the Code). Eligibility requires at least five years of continuous service, except on death or disablement; fixed-term employees qualify pro rata after one year under the Code.

Explanation

The formula defines the benefit the actuary projects; Ind AS 19 then measures its present value using PUCM. Several statutory details matter for the numbers:

  • Wages. Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, wages = basic + DA. Under the Code on Social Security 2020 (effective 21 November 2025), the Sec 2(88) definition adds back excluded allowances exceeding 50% of total remuneration. Many companies' gratuity bases rose as a result.
  • 15/26 versus 15/30. The divisor 26 reflects that a month has 26 working days (Supreme Court in Digvijay Woollen Mills, 1980). For seasonal establishments it is 7 days' wages per season; for piece-rated employees average daily wages of the last three months are used.
  • Rounding of service. 10 years 7 months = 11 years; 10 years 6 months = 10 years.
  • Vesting. No gratuity is payable on resignation before five years (except death/disablement; and one year for fixed-term employees). The actuary models this through attrition: leavers before vesting reduce the DBO.
  • Ceiling. ₹20 lakh since 29 March 2018; the actuary must cap projected benefits, which also stops attribution once the cap is reached (Ind AS 19.73).
  • Enhanced schemes. Company schemes may be more generous (e.g., uncapped, 30 days per year, or CTC-based wages); the DBO must reflect the higher of the statutory and the company scheme.
  • Tax exemption. Sec 10(10)(ii) exempts the lower of actual gratuity, the formula amount and ₹20 lakh for employees covered by the Act.

Illustration

An employee resigns on 31 March 2026 after 12 years and 8 months of service. Monthly basic ₹48,000, DA ₹6,000, HRA ₹24,000, special allowance ₹32,000; total remuneration ₹1,10,000.

Old definition: \(W = 54{,}000\); \(Y = 13\). \(G = 54{,}000 \times 15/26 \times 13 = \text{₹}4{,}05{,}000\).

Code definition: excluded components = 24,000 + 32,000 = ₹56,000; 50% of remuneration = ₹55,000; excess = ₹1,000 added back. \(W = 55{,}000\). \(G = 55{,}000 \times 15/26 \times 13 = \text{₹}4{,}12{,}500\).

A senior manager with \(W = \text{₹}3{,}00{,}000\) and 25 years: \(3{,}00{,}000 \times 15/26 \times 25 = \text{₹}43{,}26{,}923\), capped at ₹20 lakh (unless the company scheme is uncapped).

An employee who resigns after 4 years 4 months receives nothing; if she dies in service after 2 years, the nominee receives \(W \times 15/26 \times 2\).

Connections

Defines the benefit measured in s02 (statutory changes), s03 (actuarial data) and s04 (PUCM projection and cap-based attribution). The change in \(W\) from the Code drives the past service cost discussed in c06. In FP&A, the formula supports a quick "statutory gratuity if everyone left today" (vested benefit obligation) as a sanity check on the DBO — the DBO is usually below the vested amount for young workforces with high attrition and above it for older workforces, because of salary escalation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute gratuity in four cases [L1]

Compute gratuity payable (statutory, ₹20 lakh cap) for: (a) 7 years 5 months service, wages ₹36,000; (b) 3 years 11 months service, resignation, wages ₹52,000; (c) 2 years service, death in accident, wages ₹40,000; (d) 31 years 9 months service, wages ₹1,90,000.

Q2. Vested benefit obligation versus DBO [L2]

A company has 1,000 employees: 600 with under 5 years' service (average wages ₹30,000, average service 2.5 years) and 400 vested (average wages ₹65,000, average service 11 years). The actuarial DBO is ₹14.8 crore. Required: compute the vested benefit obligation if all vested employees left at the reporting date (ignore cap), compare it with the DBO and explain the difference.

💡 Hint: Think about which effects in PUCM increase the obligation above today's vested amount and which reduce it.

Q3. Code wage add-back [L1]

An employee's monthly remuneration is: basic ₹30,000, HRA ₹15,000, special allowance ₹35,000, LTA ₹5,000, statutory bonus ₹2,500 (assume the bonus is excluded and not part of the 50% test computation base for this question; other components are). Required: compute wages for gratuity under the 50% rule and the gratuity for 9 years' service.
