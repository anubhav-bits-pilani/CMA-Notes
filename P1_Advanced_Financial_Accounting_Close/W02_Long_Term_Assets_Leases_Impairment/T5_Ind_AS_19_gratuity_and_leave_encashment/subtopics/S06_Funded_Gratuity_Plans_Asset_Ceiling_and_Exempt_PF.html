Subtopic note

Funded Gratuity Plans, Asset Ceiling and Exempt PF Trusts

Scope: Funded plans via LIC/insurer group gratuity schemes, asset ceiling, expected contributions; exempt PF trusts as DB (interest-guarantee shortfall)

Why it matters

Most large Indian companies fund gratuity through a trust that buys a group gratuity policy from LIC or a private insurer, and many legacy groups (Tata, Birla, L&T-style conglomerates, PSUs) run exempt provident fund trusts. Both create accounting questions beyond the basic DBO: what qualifies as plan assets, how to value them, what happens when the fund is in surplus, and whether an exempt PF trust is a DC or DB plan. These questions also drive cash: expected contributions are disclosed and feed treasury forecasts, and Sec 43B deductions depend on payment to an approved fund.

Core notes

Funding structure in India

The typical structure: the company sets up an approved gratuity fund (irrevocable trust, approval under Part C of the Fourth Schedule to the Income-tax Act); the trust enters into a group gratuity scheme with LIC (e.g., Group Gratuity Cash Accumulation Scheme) or a private insurer. The company pays contributions to the trust, which pays premiums to the insurer; the insurer maintains a fund account credited with declared interest and pays benefits on claims.

Plan assets (Ind AS 19.8, 19.113–119)

Plan assets comprise:

  • assets held by a long-term employee benefit fund — held by an entity legally separate from the reporting entity, existing solely to pay or fund employee benefits, available only for that purpose and not available to the entity's creditors even in bankruptcy, and not returnable to the entity except in limited cases; and
  • qualifying insurance policies — issued by an insurer that is not a related party, where proceeds can be used only to pay or fund employee benefits under a DB plan and are not available to the entity's creditors.

A group gratuity policy held by an approved trust with LIC generally meets these conditions.

Measurement. Plan assets are measured at fair value (19.113). For a cash-accumulation policy, fair value is normally the fund balance per the insurer's statement (including declared interest to the reporting date); for unit-linked policies, the NAV-based value. Where qualifying insurance policies exactly match the amount and timing of benefits, fair value is deemed to be the PV of the related obligations (19.115). Unpaid contributions due from the employer are not plan assets (19.114).

Reimbursement rights (19.116–119): where it is virtually certain that another party will reimburse expenditure, but the asset does not qualify as a plan asset (e.g., a policy held directly by the company, not the trust), recognise a separate asset at fair value, with the same P&L/OCI treatment as plan assets, presented separately.

Insurance that transfers risk. If the policy is an "insured benefit" where the insurer takes on the full obligation and the employer has no further legal or constructive obligation, the plan is in substance DC (19.46). Indian group gratuity cash-accumulation schemes do not transfer the obligation; the employer bears any shortfall. Hence DB.

Asset ceiling (19.64–65, 19.8)

When plan assets exceed the DBO (a surplus), the net defined benefit asset is measured at the lower of:

  • the surplus in the plan; and
  • the asset ceiling — the present value of any economic benefits available in the form of refunds from the plan or reductions in future contributions to the plan (19.64, 19.8).

\[ \text{Net DB asset} = \min\left(\text{FVPA} - \text{DBO},\ \text{Asset ceiling}\right) \]

An entity recognises a surplus because it controls a resource (ability to use the surplus) that results from past events (contributions and service) and from which future benefits are available (19.65). Indian guidance and practice draw on IFRIC 14 principles (Ind AS 19 Appendix B, "The Limit on a Defined Benefit Asset, Minimum Funding Requirements and their Interaction"): a refund is available only if the entity has an unconditional right to it; a contribution reduction is measured as the PV of future service cost less any minimum funding contributions.

Indian trust deeds for approved gratuity funds generally restrict refunds to the employer while the fund continues (refunds are usually only on winding-up, after satisfying liabilities). So the ceiling is often the PV of future contribution reductions, i.e., the employer can stop or reduce contributions while future service cost is met from the surplus.

Change in the effect of the ceiling (other than interest) goes to OCI (19.127(c)); interest on the effect of the ceiling forms part of net interest (19.126).

Illustration: FVPA ₹52 crore, DBO ₹46 crore → surplus ₹6 crore. Refunds not permitted. PV of future service cost (reductions in contributions available) ₹4.5 crore. Net asset = ₹4.5 crore; ceiling effect = ₹1.5 crore, recognised as a remeasurement loss in OCI (first year).

Expected contributions (19.147(a)–(b))

The entity discloses a description of funding arrangements and policy, and the expected contributions to the plan for the next annual reporting period. Practical sources: the actuary's recommendation, the LIC renewal premium demand (often based on the LIC's own valuation of the funding requirement, which differs from the Ind AS 19 DBO), and the company's tax-planning (contributions are deductible only when paid). FP&A uses this disclosure for the cash budget.

LIC's funding valuation vs Ind AS 19 DBO: LIC's demand is a funding calculation using its own assumptions; it does not replace the Ind AS valuation. Paying LIC's demand does not determine the expense (pitfall: "expensing only LIC contributions").

Exempt PF trusts as DB plans

Under the EPF and MP Act 1952, an establishment may be granted exemption (Sec 17) to manage its own PF through a trust, on condition that the benefits are no less favourable than the EPFO scheme — in particular, members must be credited at least the interest rate declared by EPFO each year (8.25% for FY24 and FY25). Any shortfall between the trust's investment earnings and the guaranteed rate must be made good by the employer.

Because the employer bears investment risk on the guarantee, the plan is a DB plan for that element (Ind AS 19.30). Institute of Actuaries of India guidance provides methods (typically a deterministic or stochastic projection of the trust's yield versus expected declared rates over the members' remaining service) for valuing the interest-rate guarantee obligation. Many companies obtain an actuarial valuation of the shortfall; where the trust's yield comfortably exceeds the guaranteed rate and the reserve is adequate, the shortfall may be nil, but the plan is still disclosed as DB.

Element Accounting
Employer's monthly PF contribution (12%) Expensed as service rendered (like DC contribution)
Interest guarantee shortfall obligation Measured actuarially; changes split P&L/OCI as for DB
Trust's investments Plan assets only for the guarantee measurement; disclose PF trust details
EPS (8.33% to EPFO pension) DC (paid to EPFO even for exempt establishments)

Additional Indian points: credit losses on trust investments (e.g., IL&FS, DHFL defaults in 2018–19) directly increase the shortfall; trusts must follow the investment pattern notified by the Ministry of Labour; Sec 17 exemption conditions and the 2021–22 tax on interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh do not change the employer's accounting.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Contributions paid after year-end. Not plan assets at the reporting date; they reduce the liability only when paid.
  • Policy held in company name. Not a qualifying insurance policy; recognise a reimbursement right, not net.
  • Insurer default risk. Fair value of an insurer's fund includes credit risk; LIC is government-backed, private insurers may need assessment.
  • Group plans. In a group trust covering several subsidiaries, allocate DBO and assets per a contractual or stated policy (19.41); otherwise the sponsoring entity recognises the net cost.
  • Surplus with minimum funding requirements. Rare in India, but if a trust deed requires minimum contributions, apply Appendix B.

Key takeaways

  • LIC/insurer group gratuity schemes held via an approved trust are plan assets measured at fair value; the employer still bears DB risk.
  • Net DB asset is limited to the asset ceiling — PV of refunds or contribution reductions; ceiling effects (other than interest) go to OCI.
  • Indian trust deeds usually prohibit refunds, so the ceiling often equals the PV of future contribution reductions.
  • Disclose expected contributions for next year; LIC's funding demand is not the Ind AS expense.
  • Exempt PF trusts with an EPFO-rate guarantee are DB plans for the interest shortfall, valued actuarially.

Practice Questions

Q1. Asset ceiling computation [L2]

At 31 March 2026, a company's gratuity DBO is ₹38 crore and the LIC fund balance is ₹44 crore. The trust deed prohibits refunds except on winding-up. The actuary estimates the PV of future service cost (over the expected life of the plan) at ₹3.8 crore. At 31 March 2025, the asset ceiling effect was nil. Required: compute the net asset, the ceiling effect, and the FY26 OCI entry; explain what would change if the trust deed allowed refunds of surplus to the employer.

Q2. Exempt PF trust shortfall [L2]

A company's exempt PF trust has a corpus of ₹1,200 crore. EPFO has declared 8.25%; the trust earned 7.70% after a write-down on a defaulted bond of ₹14 crore. Required: (a) compute the shortfall for the year that the employer must make good; (b) explain how this is accounted for and where the actuarial valuation of future shortfall is recognised; (c) list the disclosures.

💡 Hint: Distinguish the current-year shortfall (a contribution payable) from the actuarial obligation for future guarantees.

Q3. LIC demand versus actuarial cost [L1]

LIC has raised a renewal premium demand of ₹2.4 crore based on its funding valuation. The Ind AS 19 report shows CSC ₹1.6 crore, net interest ₹0.3 crore and an OCI loss of ₹0.5 crore. The draft accounts show gratuity expense of ₹2.4 crore. Required: correct the entries and explain the difference to the plant controller.

Q4. Policy held by the company [L2]

A small listed company holds a group gratuity policy directly in the company's name (no trust). The policy fund value is ₹6.2 crore and the DBO is ₹7.5 crore. Required: explain the classification of the policy under Ind AS 19.116–119, the balance sheet presentation and the tax implications under Sec 40A(7) and Sec 36(1)(v).
