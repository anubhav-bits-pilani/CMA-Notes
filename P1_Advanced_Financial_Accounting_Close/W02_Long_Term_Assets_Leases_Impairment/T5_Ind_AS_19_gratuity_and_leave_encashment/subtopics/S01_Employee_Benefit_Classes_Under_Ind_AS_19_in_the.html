Subtopic note

Employee Benefit Classes Under Ind AS 19 in the Indian Context

Scope: Benefit classes: short-term, post-employment (gratuity DB; PF/ESI DC), other long-term (compensated absences), termination benefits (VRS)

Why it matters

Classification determines the entire accounting model: whether a benefit is expensed as earned at an undiscounted amount, measured actuarially with OCI remeasurements, measured actuarially with everything in P&L, or recognised only when an offer becomes irrevocable. A misclassified benefit produces wrong P&L/OCI splits, wrong presentation and wrong tax entries. In India the same payroll typically contains all four classes — salary and bonus, PF/ESI/gratuity, leave encashment, and VRS or severance in restructurings — so the payroll-to-GL mapping must be classification-aware.

Core notes

The four classes (Ind AS 19.5, 19.8)

Class Definition Indian examples Measurement model
Short-term employee benefits Expected to be settled wholly before twelve months after the end of the annual reporting period in which employees render the service (19.8) Salaries, wages, statutory bonus (Payment of Bonus Act), performance bonus payable within 12 months, short-term compensated absences, medical reimbursement, non-monetary benefits (housing, cars) Undiscounted amount expected to be paid; expense as service is rendered (19.11)
Post-employment benefits Payable after completion of employment (other than termination and short-term benefits) Gratuity (DB); PF and EPS contributions to EPFO (DC); ESI contributions (DC, though ESI is not strictly post-employment — it is a social security contribution treated like DC); superannuation (usually DC via LIC); post-retirement medical (DB); pension schemes DC: contribution expensed. DB: actuarial measurement, remeasurements in OCI
Other long-term employee benefits All employee benefits other than short-term, post-employment and termination Compensated absences (leave encashment) not expected to be settled wholly within 12 months; long-service awards; long-term incentive cash plans; deferred bonuses beyond 12 months Actuarial (PUCM), all components including remeasurements in P&L (19.156)
Termination benefits Provided in exchange for the termination of employment as a result of the entity's decision to terminate before normal retirement or the employee's decision to accept an offer VRS ex-gratia, severance on closure, retrenchment compensation under the ID Act/IR Code Recognised at the earlier of when the offer cannot be withdrawn and when restructuring costs are recognised (19.165); measured per nature (19.169)

Short-term benefits (19.9–25)

Recognised as an expense (or capitalised where another standard permits, e.g. Ind AS 16 for employees constructing an asset) and a liability (accrued expense) as service is rendered, at the undiscounted amount. Compensated absences that are short-term:

  • Accumulating absences (carried forward) — recognise the expected cost of unused entitlement at the reporting date (19.16), measured as the additional amount expected to be paid (19.16).
  • Non-accumulating absences (lapse if unused, e.g. sick leave that lapses) — recognise when the absence occurs (19.18).

Profit-sharing and bonus plans are recognised when there is a present legal or constructive obligation and a reliable estimate (19.19). Statutory bonus under the Payment of Bonus Act 1965 (minimum 8.33%, maximum 20%) is a legal obligation accrued during the year.

Post-employment benefits: DC vs DB (19.26–30)

  • Defined contribution plan: the entity pays fixed contributions into a separate entity (fund) and has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions if the fund lacks assets. Actuarial and investment risk fall on the employee (19.28). Accounting: expense the contribution payable for the period (19.51).
  • Defined benefit plan: any post-employment plan other than DC. The entity's obligation is to provide agreed benefits; actuarial and investment risk fall on the entity (19.30).

Indian classification:

Benefit Classification Reason
Employer PF contribution to EPFO (12% of basic + DA, of which 8.33% to EPS subject to wage ceiling ₹15,000) DC EPFO declares rate; employer has no obligation for shortfall
Employer PF to an exempt trust with interest-rate guarantee DB (for the guarantee) Employer must make good shortfall vs EPFO-declared rate (see s06)
ESI (employer 3.25%, employee 0.75% of gross wages, for employees up to ₹21,000/month) DC Fixed contribution; no further obligation
Gratuity (Payment of Gratuity Act / Code on Social Security) DB Benefit formula based on final wages and service
Superannuation via LIC (fixed % of salary, e.g. 15% of basic up to ₹1.5 lakh tax-free limit) DC Fixed contribution
Post-retirement medical for retired staff (common in PSUs, banks) DB Benefit promise
NPS employer contribution DC Fixed contribution

A multi-employer or state plan is classified by its terms (19.32–49); EPFO is a state plan accounted as DC.

Other long-term benefits (19.153–158)

Compensated absences in India are usually accumulating, often encashable on separation, and employees may carry forward up to a cap (e.g. 300 days under many state Shops and Establishments Acts or company policy). Because they are not expected to be settled wholly within 12 months, they are other long-term benefits measured actuarially, with all remeasurements in P&L (s07). A simplified approach to recognition is the point of this class: no OCI.

Termination benefits (19.159–171)

Termination benefits arise from the termination, not from service. Benefits conditional on future service (retention bonuses) are not termination benefits. Recognition (19.165) at the earlier of:

  • (a) when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer — for a VRS, when the employee accepts or when a restriction on withdrawal takes effect (19.166); for an entity-initiated termination, when the plan is communicated with the required detail (19.167); and
  • (b) when the entity recognises related restructuring costs under Ind AS 37 (19.165(b)).

Measurement: if expected to be settled wholly within 12 months, apply short-term requirements; otherwise apply other long-term requirements (discounting) (19.169). For VRS offers, measure based on the number of employees expected to accept (19.170).

Illustration: A PSU bank announces a VRS on 1 February 2026, open until 15 March 2026, with ex-gratia of 60 days' salary per completed year. 1,250 employees apply; the scheme says applications cannot be withdrawn after submission and the bank may not withdraw after 15 March. Expected payout ₹410 crore, paid by June 2026. The obligation is recognised at 15 March 2026 (earliest date the offer cannot be withdrawn and employees have accepted), undiscounted as it is settled within 12 months. Gratuity and leave for these employees continue under their own plans; the VRS may also trigger a curtailment/settlement effect on gratuity (past service cost or settlement gain/loss).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bonus payable after 12 months. A deferred bonus payable in 18 months is other long-term, discounted.
  • Reclassification. Short-term classification is reassessed only if expectations of settlement timing change permanently; temporary changes do not trigger reclassification (19.10).
  • Gratuity payable to employees on resignation within 12 months. Still a post-employment DB plan; the expected payout is the current portion, not a short-term benefit.
  • ESI. Not a post-employment benefit in substance (it funds medical and sickness benefits during employment), but its accounting is the same as DC: expense contributions.
  • Ex-gratia on retirement by practice. A consistent practice may create a constructive DB obligation (19.61).

Key takeaways

  • Four classes, four models: short-term (undiscounted, as earned), post-employment DC (contribution), post-employment DB (actuarial, OCI remeasurements), other long-term (actuarial, all in P&L), plus termination benefits on irrevocability.
  • In India: EPFO PF, ESI, NPS and superannuation are DC; gratuity, exempt PF guarantees and retiree medical are DB.
  • Leave encashment carried forward beyond 12 months is other long-term, not post-employment.
  • VRS is a termination benefit recognised when the offer can no longer be withdrawn or with the restructuring provision.
  • Classification feeds the Schedule III expense breakdown and the tax analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the payroll [L1]

Classify each benefit and state the measurement model: (a) Diwali bonus payable in October; (b) statutory bonus for FY26 payable by November 2026; (c) employer PF to EPFO; (d) employer PF to an exempt trust guaranteeing the EPFO rate; (e) gratuity; (f) 30 days of accumulating privileged leave encashable on separation; (g) sick leave that lapses at year-end; (h) cash LTIP payable after 3 years subject to service; (i) VRS ex-gratia; (j) retention bonus payable if the employee stays until closure.

Q2. VRS recognition date [L2]

A manufacturing company announces a VRS on 10 March 2026, open until 20 April 2026. The scheme permits the company to reject applications and withdraw the scheme until 20 April. By 31 March, 140 employees have applied; the expected total is 220 at an average ex-gratia of ₹9 lakh. A restructuring provision for the related plant closure meets Ind AS 37.72 at 31 March 2026. Required: determine the termination benefit to recognise at 31 March 2026 and the basis for measurement.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 19.165 uses the earlier of two dates — check both.

Q3. Accumulating short-term absences [L1]

Employees of a BPO are entitled to 12 days of casual leave a year, which accumulates for one year and then lapses; it is not encashable. At 31 March 2026, 1,800 employees have an average of 3 unused days; historically 70% of carried-forward days are used, and the average daily cost is ₹2,100. Required: compute the liability and explain why it is a short-term benefit.

Q4. Interview: gratuity versus PF [L2]

Explain to a hiring manager why gratuity is a defined benefit plan while EPFO provident fund is a defined contribution plan, and why an exempt PF trust can change that conclusion.
