Subtopic note

Gratuity Formula, Ceiling and the Code on Social Security 2020

Scope: Gratuity formula and ceiling; Code on Social Security 2020 (effective 21 Nov 2025) — wider wage definition, fixed-term employees' gratuity

Why it matters

The gratuity DBO is only as good as the benefit formula the actuary applies. The Code on Social Security 2020 (CoSS), brought into force with effect from 21 November 2025, replaced the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972 and changed two things that move the number: the wage base and eligibility of fixed-term employees. For FY26 year-end, finance teams must confirm that the actuary has applied the new formula, quantify the past service cost, decide its presentation (many NSE companies reported an exceptional item in Q3 FY26), and update payroll structures for future cost. This is also an FP&A topic: the wage-definition change affects gratuity, and through related definitions PF and bonus, for every future year's staff-cost budget.

Core notes

The statutory benefit (Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, carried into CoSS Chapter V)

Gratuity is payable on termination of employment after at least five years of continuous service, on:

  • superannuation (retirement);
  • retirement or resignation;
  • death or disablement due to accident or disease — the five-year condition does not apply.

Amount for monthly-rated employees:

\[ G = W \times \frac{15}{26} \times Y \]

where \(W\) is the last drawn monthly wages, \(15/26\) converts monthly wages to 15 days' wages (26 working days in a month), and \(Y\) is completed years of service, with a part year exceeding six months counted as a full year. For seasonal establishments, 7 days' wages per season; piece-rated employees use average daily wages of the last three months.

Ceiling. The statutory maximum is ₹20 lakh (raised from ₹10 lakh with effect from 29 March 2018). Under CoSS the ceiling is as notified by the Central Government; check the current notification at each reporting date. Employers may pay more under contract or policy (for example, uncapped gratuity or 30 days per year for senior staff); the DBO must reflect the plan's actual formula, including the constructive part.

Vesting. Five years of continuous service; courts and the Act treat 4 years and 240 days (190 days for six-day establishments, in some interpretations) as satisfying continuous service for the fifth year. Actuaries model attrition before vesting: employees leaving before five years receive nothing, which reduces the DBO.

Forfeiture. Wholly or partly forfeitable for termination due to riotous conduct, violence or moral turpitude offences; ignored in normal actuarial valuation.

Tax. Gratuity received by non-government employees is exempt under Sec 10(10) up to the lower of the statutory formula amount and ₹20 lakh (half-month salary per year formula for employees not covered by the Act). Employer contributions to an approved gratuity fund are deductible under Sec 36(1)(v) on payment (Sec 43B); unfunded provisions are disallowed (Sec 40A(7)).

What CoSS 2020 changed

1. Wider definition of wages (CoSS Sec 2(88)). "Wages" includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, and excludes listed components such as HRA, conveyance, overtime, statutory bonus, employer's PF/pension contributions, commission and gratuity itself. The proviso: if the excluded components (other than specified items) exceed 50% of total remuneration (or such percentage as notified), the excess is added back to wages. Many Indian salary structures kept basic at 30–40% of CTC with large allowances; for them, wages for gratuity rise to at least 50% of remuneration.

Illustration: Monthly CTC components of an employee — basic + DA ₹40,000; HRA ₹20,000; special allowance ₹30,000; conveyance ₹10,000. Total remuneration (for this test) ₹1,00,000. Excluded components = ₹60,000, which exceeds 50% by ₹10,000. Wages for gratuity = 40,000 + 10,000 = ₹50,000. With 12 years' service, gratuity rises from 40,000 × 15/26 × 12 = ₹2,76,923 to 50,000 × 15/26 × 12 = ₹3,46,154 — a 25% increase for past service.

2. Fixed-term employees (CoSS Sec 53(1) proviso). Fixed-term employees are entitled to gratuity on a pro rata basis on completion of one year of service, rather than five. This adds a new population to the valuation (or shortens vesting for an existing population) and increases the DBO.

3. Other features. Coverage extended across establishments as per the Code; gratuity for working journalists continues at three years; procedural rules (nomination, payment within 30 days, interest on delay) carry over. State and central rules under the Code were still being harmonised at the time of the FY26 closes — confirm the rules applied.

Accounting consequence of the change

The increase in DBO attributable to past service, caused by a change in the benefit formula, is a plan amendment and therefore past service cost (Ind AS 19.102, 19.104), recognised immediately in P&L (19.103) at the earlier of the amendment date and recognition of related restructuring/termination costs. The amendment date is the effective date of the Code provisions (21 November 2025), so the effect is measured at that date and recognised in Q3 FY26, with service cost for the rest of the year based on the amended plan. The remainder of the year-end movement (changes in discount rate, salary escalation and attrition) remains an actuarial gain/loss in OCI.

Entry (illustrative) Dr (₹ crore) Cr (₹ crore)
Employee benefits expense — past service cost (presented as exceptional item) 14.6
Net defined benefit liability — gratuity 14.6

Presentation: Ind AS 1.97–98 requires separate disclosure of material items of income and expense; the SEBI result format has an "exceptional items" line. Disclose the nature (labour code), amount and the fact that it relates to past service. Leave encashment may also be affected if the leave encashment policy is based on "wages" as defined, and that effect (other long-term benefit) goes to P&L.

Checklist for the year-end

  1. Confirm the employee data file contains the new wage base (computed per Sec 2(88)) for each employee.
  2. Confirm fixed-term employees are included with the one-year eligibility.
  3. Obtain from the actuary a separate measurement of the DBO immediately before and after the amendment at 21 November 2025, using assumptions at that date.
  4. Recognise PSC in P&L; do not mix it into OCI.
  5. Update the FY27 budget for higher service cost.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Uncapped or enhanced company schemes. If the company scheme already used CTC-based wages, the Code may have little effect; if the company scheme references "wages as per the Act", it automatically changes.
  • Interpretive uncertainty. Where the treatment of specific allowances is unclear, document the interpretation, consider sensitivity disclosure and treat later clarifications as changes in estimate (or further plan amendments, if the benefit formula changes).
  • Interim measurement. For quarterly results, an approximate roll-forward may be used for the PSC if a full valuation is not available, with true-up at year-end.
  • Effect on PF and bonus. The wage definition also feeds PF and bonus computations; these are DC or short-term, so changes flow to P&L as incurred.
  • Tax. PSC increases the DTA (deductible on payment); the deferred tax follows P&L, not OCI.

Key takeaways

  • Gratuity = last drawn wages × 15/26 × completed years, capped at the notified ceiling (₹20 lakh), vesting after five years except death or disablement.
  • CoSS 2020 (effective 21 November 2025) widens wages through the 50% rule and gives fixed-term employees gratuity after one year.
  • The past-service increase is a plan amendment → past service cost in P&L immediately, often presented as an exceptional item.
  • Ensure the actuary's data reflects the new wage base and the fixed-term population.
  • Year-end assumption changes remain OCI; do not blend them with the PSC.

Practice Questions

Q1. Gratuity under old and new wage definitions [L1]

An employee with 17 years and 7 months of service has monthly basic ₹55,000, DA nil, HRA ₹27,500, special allowance ₹42,500, conveyance ₹5,000 (total ₹1,30,000). Required: compute gratuity under the old definition (basic + DA) and under the CoSS 50% rule, applying the ₹20 lakh ceiling.

Q2. Past service cost measurement and presentation [L2]

An actuary measures a company's gratuity DBO at 21 November 2025 at ₹86 crore under the old formula and ₹101 crore under the new formula, using assumptions at that date. At 31 March 2026 the DBO is ₹108 crore after a 0.3% fall in the discount rate (effect ₹2.4 crore). Required: (a) determine the past service cost and its recognition; (b) identify the OCI component; (c) draft the exceptional-item disclosure for Q3 FY26 results.

💡 Hint: Measure the amendment effect at the amendment date with assumptions at that date before analysing the year-end movement.

Q3. Fixed-term employees [L2]

A logistics company has 900 fixed-term employees on 2-year contracts at an average monthly wage of ₹22,000, with an average of 14 months of completed service at 31 March 2026. Previously none qualified for gratuity. Required: estimate the order of magnitude of the past service cost (ignore discounting and attrition), and explain what additional data the actuary will need.

Q4. Budget impact [L2]

The HR head proposes restructuring CTC from FY27 so that basic + DA equals exactly 50% of total remuneration, to "avoid uncertainty". Required: explain the impact on gratuity service cost, PF contributions and take-home pay, and how you would present the change in the FY27 staff-cost budget.
