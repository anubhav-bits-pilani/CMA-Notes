Subtopic note

Ind AS 19 Disclosures: Sensitivity, Maturity Profile and Duration

Scope: Disclosures: sensitivity (discount rate, salary growth), maturity profile, weighted average duration

Why it matters

The defined benefit note is one of the longest in an Indian annual report, and the sensitivity and maturity disclosures are where analysts, auditors and lenders get the information they need to judge risk: how much the DBO swings with rates, when cash will be paid and how long the obligation is. For FP&A, the maturity profile is a cash-flow forecast input and duration links directly to the choice of discount rate. For review, these disclosures let you test the actuary's numbers for internal consistency — a 1% rate change should move the DBO by roughly the duration.

Core notes

Disclosure objectives (Ind AS 19.135)

An entity discloses information that:

  • (a) explains the characteristics of its DB plans and risks associated with them;
  • (b) identifies and explains the amounts in its financial statements arising from DB plans; and
  • (c) describes how its DB plans may affect the amount, timing and uncertainty of its future cash flows.

The detailed requirements (19.136–152) serve these objectives; an entity considers the level of detail and aggregation (19.136–138), disaggregating plans with materially different risks (e.g., gratuity vs exempt PF vs retiree medical).

Characteristics and amounts (19.139–144)

  • Nature of benefits, regulatory framework (Payment of Gratuity Act/Code on Social Security; EPF Act for PF trusts), governance (trustees), and risks: interest rate risk, salary risk, investment risk, longevity risk, asset–liability mismatch, concentration risk (single insurer) (19.139).
  • Reconciliations of opening to closing DBO, plan assets and asset-ceiling effect, showing separately each component (19.140–141): CSC, interest, remeasurements split (demographic, financial, experience; return on assets excluding interest), PSC and settlements, contributions (employer and employee), benefits paid, business combinations and disposals, exchange differences.
  • Disaggregation of fair value of plan assets into classes, distinguishing those with quoted market prices; for insurer-managed funds, "insurer-managed funds 100%" (19.142).
  • Significant actuarial assumptions used (19.144): discount rate, salary escalation, attrition, mortality table.

Sensitivity analysis (19.145)

The entity discloses:

  • (a) a sensitivity analysis for each significant actuarial assumption as of the end of the reporting period, showing how the DBO would have been affected by changes in the relevant assumption that were reasonably possible at that date;
  • (b) the methods and assumptions used, and their limitations;
  • (c) changes from the previous period in methods and assumptions, and reasons.

Typical Indian format (₹ lakh):

Assumption Change DBO increase / (decrease)
Discount rate +1% (412)
Discount rate −1% 470
Salary escalation +1% 455
Salary escalation −1% (405)
Attrition +50% of rates (96)
Attrition −50% of rates 130

The analysis typically changes one assumption at a time holding others constant — a stated limitation, since assumptions are correlated.

Duration and the sensitivity cross-check

Weighted average duration of the DBO (19.147(c)) is the PV-weighted average time to payment:

\[ D = \frac{\sum_{t} t \times \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^{t}}}{\sum_{t} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^{t}}} \]

where \(CF_t\) is the expected benefit payment in year \(t\) attributable to past service and \(r\) the discount rate (this is Macaulay duration; modified duration is \(D \div (1+r)\)).

Cross-check: for small rate changes,

\[ \frac{\Delta \text{DBO}}{\text{DBO}} \approx -\frac{D}{1+r} \times \Delta r \]

With DBO ₹5,870 lakh, duration 7.6 years and \(r\) = 6.7%, a +1% change implies a decrease of about \(7.6 \div 1.067 \times 1\% \approx 7.1\%\), or ₹418 lakh — consistent with the (412) above. If the actuary's duration and sensitivity disagree badly, ask why (convexity explains why the −1% effect exceeds the +1% effect).

The salary sensitivity is roughly symmetric to the discount rate sensitivity for a pure final-salary gratuity plan (both act through the same compounding), unless the ₹20 lakh cap binds for many employees, in which case salary sensitivity is lower.

Maturity profile and cash flows (19.146–147)

  • Description of asset–liability matching strategies (19.146).
  • Funding arrangements and funding policy; expected contributions for the next annual period (19.147(a)–(b)).
  • Information about the maturity profile of the DBO, including the weighted average duration and may include a distribution of the timing of benefit payments (19.147(c)).

Typical maturity table (undiscounted expected payments, ₹ lakh):

Period Expected payout
Year 1 (FY27) 640
Years 2–5 2,280
Years 6–10 2,910
Beyond 10 years 4,750

Year 1 is consistent with (but undiscounted relative to) the actuary's current-liability figure used for Schedule III presentation. FP&A should compare year-1 payouts with the expected contribution and fund balance to plan cash.

Other disclosures

  • Multi-employer and group plans (19.148–150); DC plan expense — employer PF/ESI/superannuation contributions recognised in P&L (19.53); other long-term benefits and termination benefits are disclosed per Ind AS 1 and Ind AS 24 where relevant (KMP compensation).
  • Schedule III: employee benefits expense break-up (salaries and wages, contribution to provident and other funds, share-based payments, staff welfare).
  • Labour code past service cost: separate line in the DBO reconciliation and, if presented as exceptional, in the exceptional items note.

Building the note

  1. Map each report table to the note template; keep a tick-mark index.
  2. Tie reconciliation totals to the balance sheet, P&L and OCI.
  3. Recompute sensitivity directionally using duration; recompute duration from the maturity table if not provided.
  4. Confirm assumptions in the note match the report (rounding, mortality table name "IALM 2012–14 Ult").
  5. Update narrative on risks (e.g., concentration in LIC; regulatory change from the Code).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Reasonably possible change. A ±1% change may be unrealistic in a low-volatility year, or too small for attrition; companies typically use ±0.5% or ±1% for rates and ±10–50% of rates for attrition — choose and justify.
  • Immaterial plans. Aggregate small plans; materiality applies to disclosure (Ind AS 1.31).
  • Funded and unfunded plans in one note. Disaggregate where risks differ materially.
  • Leave encashment disclosures. Not required under 19.135–152 (other long-term), but many companies voluntarily give assumptions and the amount charged; follow the actuary's report but label it correctly.
  • Interim periods. Ind AS 34 requires only significant changes, not full disclosures.

Key takeaways

  • Disclosures explain plan characteristics and risks, amounts recognised, and future cash-flow effects (19.135).
  • Sensitivity analysis for each significant assumption — reasonably possible changes, methods, limitations (19.145).
  • Weighted average duration and maturity profile show timing (19.147(c)); duration × rate change ≈ % change in DBO.
  • Disclose expected contributions for the next year and funding policy (19.147).
  • Use the sensitivity–duration check and maturity table to test the actuary's internal consistency.

Practice Questions

Q1. Duration cross-check [L2]

A gratuity DBO of ₹72 crore is disclosed with a weighted average duration of 9.2 years at a discount rate of 6.6%. The sensitivity table reports a DBO change of −₹3.1 crore for +1% and +₹3.4 crore for −1%. Required: estimate the expected sensitivity from duration, identify the inconsistency and list possible explanations.

💡 Hint: Compare the reported percentage changes with modified duration.

Q2. Compute duration from a maturity profile [L2]

Expected undiscounted payouts (₹ lakh) attributable to past service: year 1: 200; year 2: 220; year 3: 240; year 4: 250; year 5: 260; year 6–10: 300 per year; nothing thereafter. Discount rate 7%. Required: compute the DBO and Macaulay and modified duration (Excel formula approach acceptable), and draft the maturity profile disclosure.

Q3. Draft the sensitivity note [L1]

From an actuarial report: DBO ₹2,480 lakh; discount rate 6.75% (+0.5%: −₹104 lakh; −0.5%: +₹112 lakh); salary escalation 8% (+0.5%: +₹108 lakh; −0.5%: −₹101 lakh); attrition 10% (+10% of rates: −₹22 lakh; −10%: +₹24 lakh). Required: draft the Ind AS 19.145 disclosure including methods and limitations.

Q4. FP&A use of disclosures [L2]

Using the maturity table and the expected contribution disclosure of a peer company (year-1 payout ₹48 crore, expected contribution ₹30 crore, plan assets ₹210 crore), explain how you would forecast its gratuity cash outflow and its effect on operating cash flow for FY27, and what other disclosures you need.
