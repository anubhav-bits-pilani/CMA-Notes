Subtopic note

Reading an Ind AS 19 Actuarial Valuation Report

Scope: Actuarial report anatomy: discount rate, salary escalation, attrition, IALM 2012–14 mortality; DBO and plan asset reconciliations

Why it matters

The actuarial report is the single source document for gratuity and leave entries, notes and tax. Auditors under SA 500 and SA 620 must evaluate the actuary as management's expert, but the finance team owns the numbers: it supplies the data, approves the assumptions and translates the report into entries and disclosures. Reading the report well means knowing where each number goes, which assumptions drive the result, and which reconciliation lines to sanity-check before posting.

Core notes

Typical structure of an Indian Ind AS 19 report

Section Content Where it goes
1. Scope and data Plan rules, valuation date, membership data summary (count, average age, average past service, average salary, total monthly salary) Data reconciliation with HR master
2. Assumptions Financial (discount rate, salary escalation) and demographic (attrition, mortality, retirement age) Note on significant actuarial assumptions (19.144)
3. Change in DBO Opening DBO → closing DBO DBO reconciliation note (19.140–141)
4. Change in plan assets Opening FV → closing FV Plan asset reconciliation note
5. Net liability (asset) DBO − FV of assets; asset ceiling Balance sheet; current/non-current split
6. Amounts in P&L CSC, PSC, net interest Employee benefits expense
7. Amounts in OCI Actuarial (gains)/losses by source; return on assets excluding interest OCI — items that will not be reclassified
8. Sensitivity analysis DBO effect of ±0.5% or ±1% in discount rate, salary escalation, attrition Sensitivity note (19.145)
9. Maturity profile Expected payouts by year (year 1, 2–5, 6–10, beyond); weighted average duration Maturity note (19.147(c))
10. Expected contributions Employer contribution for next year Note (19.147(b))
11. Experience history DBO, assets, experience adjustments for past years Optional supplementary

Financial assumptions

Discount rate. Determined by reference to market yields at the reporting date on government bonds of currency and term consistent with the obligation (Ind AS 19.83; see s04). An Indian gratuity plan with a duration of 7–10 years typically uses the 7–10-year G-sec zero-coupon yield from the FBIL/CCIL curve at 31 March. Typical FY26 range: roughly 6.3%–7.0% depending on duration (verify at the reporting date). A 50-basis-point change commonly moves the DBO by 3–5%.

Salary escalation. Long-term average expected increase including inflation, seniority and promotion (19.87–90). Indian assumptions: 5%–10% depending on industry; IT services often 7%–9%; PSUs follow pay commission/wage revisions. It should be consistent with the inflation embedded in the discount rate. Short-term known increments (agreed wage settlements) should be reflected.

Benefit ceiling. The ₹20 lakh cap (or notified ceiling) is modelled; with high salaries, the cap binds and salary escalation matters less.

Demographic assumptions

Attrition (withdrawal) rates. Often the single most important demographic assumption for Indian gratuity because the five-year vesting means early leavers get nothing. Typically age-banded or service-banded, for example: up to 30 years 15%; 31–44 years 8%; 45 and above 3%. IT and BPO companies may use 20%–30% for young staff. Higher attrition lowers the DBO for unvested employees but can increase it slightly for vested employees (earlier payment).

Mortality. Indian Assured Lives Mortality (IALM) 2012–14 (Ultimate) is the standard table used by Indian actuaries for in-service mortality. For post-retirement benefits (pension, medical), annuitant tables apply. Mortality rarely moves gratuity materially because the in-service period is short.

Retirement age. 58 or 60 per company policy; some sectors 62–65. Gratuity is paid at the earlier of exit and retirement.

Disability. Often ignored or included with mortality.

Reconciliation of DBO (illustrative, ₹ lakh)

Line Amount Sanity check
Opening DBO 5,200 Equals prior-year closing
Current service cost 520 ≈ prior-year CSC × salary growth × headcount change
Interest cost 365 ≈ opening DBO × prior-year-end discount rate (7.0%) = 364
Past service cost 0 Only for plan amendments (FY26: labour code)
Benefits paid (410) Agrees to payroll/LIC claim settlement records
Actuarial loss — financial assumptions 140 Discount rate fell 7.0% → 6.7%
Actuarial (gain) — demographic assumptions (35) Attrition table updated
Actuarial loss — experience 90 Actual salary increases above assumption, new joiners data
Closing DBO 5,870

Reconciliation of plan assets (₹ lakh)

Line Amount Sanity check
Opening fair value 3,900 Agrees to prior-year LIC fund statement
Interest income 273 ≈ opening FV × 7.0% = 273
Employer contributions 600 Agrees to bank payments to LIC trust
Benefits paid from fund (410) Same as DBO line if all benefits paid through fund
Return on plan assets excluding interest (18) Actual return (LIC credited ~6.5%) less interest income
Closing fair value 4,345 Agrees to LIC fund statement at 31 March

Net liability = 5,870 − 4,345 = ₹1,525 lakh.

P&L = CSC 520 + net interest (365 − 273 = 92) = ₹612 lakh. OCI loss = 140 − 35 + 90 + 18 = ₹213 lakh. Check the net liability movement: opening (5,200 − 3,900 = 1,300) + P&L 612 + OCI 213 − contributions 600 = 1,525. Benefits paid out of the fund cancel out.

Reviewing the report: questions to ask

  1. Data: does the employee count and total salary agree to payroll at the valuation date (or the census date, rolled forward)? Are joiners, leavers and fixed-term staff included?
  2. Assumptions: is the discount rate at the reporting date and for the right duration? Is salary escalation consistent with budgets and recent increments? Is attrition supported by the last 3–5 years' experience?
  3. Reconciliations: does opening equal last year's closing? Do interest cost and interest income approximate opening balance × rate? Are benefits paid agreed to records?
  4. Experience adjustments: large recurring experience losses suggest systematic assumption bias (salary escalation too low, attrition too high).
  5. Current portion: is the next-12-month expected payout provided (for Schedule III)?
  6. Asset ceiling: if assets exceed DBO, has the ceiling been tested?
  7. Interim: if the report uses a census date before 31 March, is the roll-forward reasonable?

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unfunded vs funded plans. For unfunded plans, benefits are paid by the company and the asset reconciliation is absent; benefits paid reduce the DBO and cash directly.
  • Benefits paid directly by the company for a funded plan. These reduce the DBO but not plan assets; the company may later claim reimbursement from LIC — a reimbursement right, not a plan asset, until received (19.116).
  • Acquired businesses. Business combinations add a DBO line ("acquisition"), measured at acquisition-date assumptions.
  • Transfer of employees within group. Transfer-in/out lines appear; ensure symmetry between group entities.
  • Actuary's own assumption choice. Responsibility for assumptions rests with management (19.75–80 require unbiased and mutually compatible assumptions); "the actuary chose it" is not an audit defence.

Key takeaways

  • The report has a fixed anatomy: data, assumptions, DBO and asset reconciliations, P&L, OCI, sensitivity, maturity, expected contributions.
  • Key financial assumptions: reporting-date government bond yield for the plan duration; salary escalation consistent with inflation and HR policy.
  • Key demographic assumptions: attrition (dominant for Indian gratuity), IALM 2012–14 Ultimate mortality, retirement age.
  • Sanity-check interest lines against opening balance × rate and benefits paid against records.
  • Net liability movement = P&L cost + OCI remeasurement − contributions; use this to tie the report to the ledger.

Practice Questions

Q1. Reconciliation from a report extract [L1]

An actuarial report shows (₹ lakh): opening DBO 3,100; CSC 290; interest cost 214; benefits paid 260; actuarial loss on financial assumptions 75; experience gain 40. Plan assets: opening 2,450; interest income 169; contributions 400; benefits paid 260; return excluding interest 12 (gain). Required: compute closing DBO, closing assets, net liability, P&L cost and OCI, and verify the net-liability roll-forward.

Q2. Challenge the assumptions [L2]

A mid-sized IT company's report uses: discount rate 7.30% (G-sec yield for 6-year duration at the reporting date 6.55%), salary escalation 5% (actual increments over three years averaged 9%), attrition 5% across all ages (actual attrition for employees under 30 was 24%). Required: discuss the direction of the DBO effect of correcting each assumption and draft three review points for the actuary.

💡 Hint: Consider separately the effect on unvested employees of a higher attrition rate.

Q3. Data reconciliation [L2]

The actuary's data summary shows 4,820 employees with total monthly salary ₹21.6 crore at 31 March 2026. Payroll shows 5,140 employees and ₹23.4 crore, including 410 fixed-term employees and 30 employees on notice. Required: explain how you would investigate the difference, which employees should be included, and the possible effect on the DBO.

Q4. Experience history interpretation [L2]

Over five years, a plan shows experience losses on the DBO of 3.1%, 4.0%, 3.6%, 2.9% and 4.4% of opening DBO. Required: explain what this pattern suggests, which assumptions you would revisit, and why Ind AS 19.75–76 matters for this conclusion.
