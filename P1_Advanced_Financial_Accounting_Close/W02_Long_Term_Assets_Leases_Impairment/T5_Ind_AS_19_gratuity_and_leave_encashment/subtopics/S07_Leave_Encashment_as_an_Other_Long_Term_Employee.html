Subtopic note

Leave Encashment as an Other Long-Term Employee Benefit

Scope: Leave encashment as other long-term benefit — all remeasurements in P&L; current/non-current split from actuary

Why it matters

Compensated absences are often the second-largest employee liability in Indian balance sheets after gratuity, and in banks, PSUs and manufacturing companies with generous accumulation caps they can exceed it. The accounting is deceptively similar to gratuity — the same actuary, the same assumptions, the same report format — but the P&L/OCI split is different, and the classification between current and non-current is a recurring discussion with auditors. Getting it wrong misstates profit, OCI, EPS and working capital.

Core notes

Classifying compensated absences

Feature Short-term (19.13–18) Other long-term (19.153–158)
Expected settlement Wholly before 12 months after the end of the annual reporting period in which service is rendered Not expected to be settled wholly within 12 months
Typical Indian example Casual leave that lapses in the next year Privilege/earned leave accumulating up to 240–300 days, availed or encashed on separation or periodically
Measurement Undiscounted additional amount expected to be paid for unused entitlement PUCM, discounted, with actuarial assumptions
Remeasurements n/a All in P&L

The test is applied to the benefit as a whole, based on expected settlement, not per employee (19.8 definition "wholly before twelve months"). Where a large part of accumulated leave is expected to be carried forward, the entire benefit is other long-term.

Accumulating vs vesting: accumulating leave can be carried forward; vesting leave entitles employees to cash on exit. Both create obligations; non-vesting accumulating leave is measured allowing for the probability that employees leave before using it (19.15).

Measurement (19.155)

The same methodology as for DB post-employment plans: PUCM, actuarial assumptions (discount rate, salary escalation, attrition, mortality), plus leave-specific assumptions:

  • leave availment rate — proportion of accumulated leave availed in service each year (valued at the cost of salary while on leave, often with no incremental cost if no replacement is hired — policy and actuarial judgement);
  • leave encashment rate — proportion encashed in service (if permitted) and on exit;
  • leave accrual rate — days credited per year (e.g., 30 days/year), and the cap.

The DBO for leave is the PV of expected cash (or cost of absence) attributable to leave already accrued at the reporting date.

Recognition in P&L (19.156)

For other long-term employee benefits, the entity recognises the net total of the following in P&L, except to the extent another Ind AS requires or permits inclusion in an asset:

  • (a) service cost;
  • (b) net interest on the net defined benefit liability (asset);
  • (c) remeasurements of the net defined benefit liability (asset).

The rationale (19.154): remeasurements of other long-term benefits are not usually subject to the same degree of uncertainty as post-employment benefits, so a simplified method is required — no OCI.

Employee benefits expense — compensated absences (service cost + interest + actuarial loss) X
Provision for compensated absences X
Provision for compensated absences (encashment paid) Y
Bank Y

Leave plans are almost always unfunded in India; occasionally a company buys an LIC leave-encashment policy (which may qualify as plan assets if held via a trust).

Current vs non-current split

Schedule III requires provisions to be split into current and non-current. Two approaches meet in practice:

  1. Actuary's split based on expected settlement. The actuary estimates the PV of leave expected to be availed or encashed in the next 12 months (current) and the remainder (non-current). This is the Indian market practice and the approach the brief's scope reflects.
  2. Ind AS 1.69(d) right-to-defer test. A liability is current if the entity does not have the right at the reporting date to defer settlement for at least 12 months. Where employees can demand encashment or availment of all accumulated leave at any time, the entity has no such right, which would point to classifying the entire obligation as current (this is the common IFRS view under IAS 1 as amended).

Reconciling the two: where policy only allows encashment on separation and availment subject to approval and caps, the entity does have a right to defer much of the leave; the actuary's expected-settlement split is a reasonable proxy. Where employees have an unconditional right to encash at will, classify the encashable portion as current regardless of expected behaviour. Document the policy terms, the classification rationale and apply consistently. The IASB's 2020 and 2022 amendments to IAS 1 on classification reinforced the right-based test; check the currently notified text of Ind AS 1.69–76 (and any Indian carve-outs) at the reporting date.

Worked example (₹ lakh)

Actuarial report for FY26: opening obligation 1,240; service cost 162; interest cost 84; benefits paid (encashment) 205; actuarial loss — financial 32; actuarial gain — demographic (11); actuarial loss — experience 48; closing obligation 1,350. Actuary's split: current 395, non-current 955.

P&L charge = 162 + 84 + (32 − 11 + 48) = 162 + 84 + 69 = ₹315 lakh, all in employee benefits expense. OCI = nil.

Entry Dr (₹ lakh) Cr (₹ lakh)
Employee benefits expense 315
Provision for compensated absences 315
Provision for compensated absences 205
Bank (encashment paid, via payroll) 205

Closing = 1,240 + 315 − 205 = 1,350 ✓. Present 395 as current provision and 955 as non-current (subject to the Ind AS 1.69(d) analysis above). Deferred tax: leave encashment is deductible on payment (Sec 43B(f)); DTA on 1,350 at 25.168% = ₹339.8 lakh, movement entirely through P&L.

Long-term incentives and long-service awards

The same "all in P&L" treatment applies to other long-term benefits such as cash LTIPs payable after three years, long-service awards and deferred bonuses beyond 12 months. These are measured with PUCM-style attribution (often straight-line over the vesting period) and discounted where material.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Leave availed vs encashed. If availed leave has no incremental cost (no replacement), some actuaries value availment at nil cost; others at full salary. The policy choice changes the DBO materially — agree it with the auditor.
  • Sick leave accumulating but not encashable. Measure only the expected additional sick leave taken beyond annual entitlement.
  • Policy changes. A reduction in the encashment cap is a change in benefits; for other long-term benefits, the effect goes to P&L in any case.
  • Labour code effects. If encashment is computed on "wages" under the Code, the wage-base increase raises the obligation, recognised in P&L.
  • Quarterly results. Remeasurement volatility in leave hits P&L every quarter; companies often recompute the obligation quarterly using a roll-forward.

Key takeaways

  • Accumulating leave not expected to be settled wholly within 12 months is an other long-term benefit (19.153).
  • Measured actuarially like DB, but service cost, net interest and all remeasurements go to P&L (19.156) — never OCI.
  • Leave-specific assumptions (availment, encashment, accrual, cap) matter as much as discount rate.
  • Current/non-current: Indian practice uses the actuary's expected-settlement split; test it against Ind AS 1.69(d) and the encashment terms.
  • Leave is deductible on payment (Sec 43B(f)), creating a DTA whose movement goes through P&L.

Practice Questions

Q1. Leave encashment entries [L1]

A company's leave report (₹ lakh): opening 860; service cost 118; interest cost 59; benefits paid 140; actuarial loss 37; current portion at year-end 260. Required: compute closing obligation, P&L charge, and pass the entries; present the balance sheet split.

Q2. Reclassification from OCI [L2]

A listed company recorded leave encashment actuarial losses of ₹4.2 crore in OCI in FY26 (and ₹3.1 crore in FY25). Tax rate 25.168%. Required: (a) state the correction and its classification under Ind AS 8; (b) quantify the effect on FY26 profit after tax, OCI and retained earnings; (c) state the comparative restatement.

💡 Hint: Total equity is unaffected; consider the line items in profit and OCI, and the opening balance of retained earnings versus other equity.

Q3. Current vs non-current [L2]

Under a bank's policy, officers may encash up to 15 days of privilege leave every year at their option and the balance (up to 240 days) only on retirement. Accumulated leave liability is ₹180 crore; the actuary estimates ₹38 crore will be availed or encashed in the next 12 months; the maximum that officers could demand in the next 12 months under policy is ₹52 crore. Required: determine the current and non-current classification and justify it under Ind AS 1.69(d).

Q4. Interview: why no OCI for leave? [L1]

Explain in under 150 words why Ind AS 19 requires leave encashment remeasurements in P&L while gratuity remeasurements go to OCI.
