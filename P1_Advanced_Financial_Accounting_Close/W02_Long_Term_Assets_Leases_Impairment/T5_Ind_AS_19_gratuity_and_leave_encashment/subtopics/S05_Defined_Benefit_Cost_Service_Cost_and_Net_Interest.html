Subtopic note

Defined Benefit Cost: Service Cost and Net Interest vs Remeasurements

Scope: P&L vs OCI: service cost and net interest in P&L; actuarial gains/losses and return on plan assets excluding interest in OCI

Why it matters

The P&L/OCI split decides how much of a defined benefit plan's volatility reaches reported profit and EPS. Ind AS 19 (like IAS 19 since 2013) puts only the "cost of the promise" and a financing element in P&L, and sends all valuation volatility — discount rate swings, salary experience, investment performance — to OCI, never to be recycled. Getting the split right affects EBITDA, finance cost (if net interest is presented there), OCI, deferred tax in OCI and analysts' adjusted earnings. Errors such as using expected return on assets, or taking leave encashment remeasurements to OCI, are recurring review points.

Core notes

The three components (Ind AS 19.120)

An entity recognises the components of defined benefit cost as follows, except where another Ind AS requires or permits inclusion in the cost of an asset (e.g., Ind AS 2 inventories, Ind AS 16 PPE under construction):

Component Recognised in Contents
(a) Service cost P&L Current service cost; past service cost (plan amendments and curtailments); gain or loss on settlement (19.8)
(b) Net interest on the net defined benefit liability (asset) P&L Interest cost on DBO − interest income on plan assets + interest on the effect of the asset ceiling (19.124)
(c) Remeasurements of the net defined benefit liability (asset) OCI Actuarial gains and losses; return on plan assets excluding amounts in net interest; any change in the effect of the asset ceiling excluding amounts in net interest (19.127)

Remeasurements recognised in OCI are not reclassified to P&L in a subsequent period, though the entity may transfer them within equity (19.122; concept c06). Schedule III presents them under "Items that will not be reclassified to profit or loss — Remeasurements of defined benefit plans", with the related income-tax line.

Service cost (19.66–112)

  • Current service cost: increase in the PV of the DBO resulting from employee service in the current period (19.8), measured using assumptions at the start of the annual reporting period (19.122A, introduced with the 2018/2019 plan amendment requirements) — unless a plan amendment, curtailment or settlement during the year triggers remeasurement, in which case the remainder of the period uses updated assumptions.
  • Past service cost: change in PV of the DBO for service in prior periods resulting from a plan amendment (introduction, withdrawal or change of a plan) or a curtailment (significant reduction in number of employees covered) (19.102). Recognised immediately at the earlier of the amendment/curtailment and the recognition of related restructuring or termination costs (19.103).
  • Settlement gain/loss: difference between the PV of DBO being settled and the settlement price (19.109), recognised when the settlement occurs (19.110).

Net interest (19.123–126)

\[ \text{Net interest} = r_{\text{open}} \times (\text{DBO}_{\text{open}} - \text{FVPA}_{\text{open}}) \text{, adjusted for contributions and benefits during the period} \]

where \(r_{\text{open}}\) is the discount rate determined at the start of the annual reporting period (19.123), DBO is the defined benefit obligation and FVPA the fair value of plan assets. Effectively:

  • Interest cost = \(r \times\) DBO (adjusted for benefit payments during the year);
  • Interest income on plan assets = \(r \times\) FVPA (adjusted for contributions and benefits) — using the discount rate, not the expected or actual return (19.125).

Because the same rate is applied to assets, any excess of the actual return over \(r \times\) FVPA is a remeasurement in OCI. A fund earning 9% when the discount rate is 7% shows a 2% gain in OCI, not in P&L.

Presentation in P&L: Ind AS 19 does not specify where net interest is presented (19.134). Indian companies commonly include the entire gratuity cost (CSC + net interest) in employee benefits expense; some present net interest in finance costs. Disclose the policy and apply it consistently; analysts adjusting EBITDA need to know.

Remeasurements (19.127–130)

  1. Actuarial gains and losses (19.128): changes in the PV of DBO from - experience adjustments — differences between previous assumptions and what actually occurred (salary increases, attrition, deaths); and - changes in actuarial assumptions — split in disclosure into demographic (attrition, mortality, retirement age) and financial (discount rate, salary escalation). Actuarial gains and losses exclude changes in DBO due to introduction, amendment, curtailment or settlement of the plan (these are service cost) and changes in benefits payable under the plan (19.129).
  2. Return on plan assets excluding interest (19.130): actual return (interest, dividends, realised and unrealised gains, net of plan administration costs and tax payable by the plan) minus interest income computed at the discount rate.
  3. Change in the effect of the asset ceiling, excluding interest on the ceiling effect.

Worked example (₹ lakh)

Data: opening DBO 2,000; opening plan assets 1,500; discount rate at start of year 7.0%, at end of year 6.6%. CSC (start-of-year assumptions) 180. Contribution of 300 paid on 1 October; benefits of 120 paid from the fund on 1 January. Closing DBO per actuary 2,215; closing plan assets per LIC statement 1,740.

Interest cost = 2,000 × 7% − 120 × 7% × 3/12 = 140 − 2.1 = 137.9. Interest income = 1,500 × 7% + 300 × 7% × 6/12 − 120 × 7% × 3/12 = 105 + 10.5 − 2.1 = 113.4. Net interest = 137.9 − 113.4 = 24.5.

DBO: 2,000 + 180 + 137.9 − 120 + actuarial loss = 2,215 → actuarial loss = 17.1. Assets: 1,500 + 113.4 + 300 − 120 + return excluding interest = 1,740 → return excluding interest = −53.4 (loss).

Statement Amount (₹ lakh)
P&L — current service cost 180.0
P&L — net interest 24.5
Total P&L 204.5
OCI — actuarial loss 17.1
OCI — return on plan assets below interest income 53.4
Total OCI loss 70.5

Check: net liability opening 500 + 204.5 + 70.5 − 300 = 475 = 2,215 − 1,740. ✓

Deferred tax

Gratuity is deductible on payment to the fund (Sec 43B, 36(1)(v)) and unfunded provisions are disallowed (Sec 40A(7)). The net liability's tax base is nil, giving a DTA. Movement in DTA attributable to OCI remeasurements is recognised in OCI (Ind AS 12.61A): at 25.168%, OCI tax = 70.5 × 25.168% = ₹17.7 lakh credit in OCI.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capitalised employee cost. Service cost of employees building a plant is capitalised under Ind AS 16; remeasurements are never capitalised.
  • Mid-year remeasurement. A plan amendment (e.g., labour code at 21 November 2025) requires remeasurement of the net liability at that date, and CSC and net interest for the rest of the year use updated assumptions (19.122A, 19.123A).
  • Plan administration costs. Costs of managing plan assets reduce return on assets (OCI); other administration costs are expensed in P&L when incurred (19.130).
  • Insurer-guaranteed returns. LIC cash-accumulation schemes credit a declared rate; the difference from the discount rate is still OCI.
  • Interim reporting. Under Ind AS 34, many Indian companies record one-quarter of the annual projected cost and remeasure only at year-end; material discount-rate moves may warrant interim remeasurement.

Key takeaways

  • P&L: service cost (current, past, settlement) and net interest; OCI: remeasurements (19.120).
  • Net interest applies the start-of-year discount rate to the net liability, adjusted for in-year cash flows (19.123–126).
  • Interest income on assets uses the discount rate; excess or shortfall of actual return is OCI (19.125, 19.130).
  • Actuarial gains/losses come from experience adjustments and changes in demographic or financial assumptions.
  • OCI remeasurements are never recycled; deferred tax on them goes to OCI.

Practice Questions

Q1. Split P&L and OCI [L1]

Opening DBO ₹4,500 lakh; opening plan assets ₹3,600 lakh; discount rate 7.2% (start), 6.9% (end). CSC ₹410 lakh. Contribution ₹500 lakh and benefits ₹280 lakh (from fund) both paid on 31 March. Closing DBO ₹4,990 lakh; closing plan assets ₹4,050 lakh. Required: compute interest cost, interest income, actuarial gain/loss, return excluding interest, total P&L and total OCI.

Q2. Expected return error [L2]

A company computed FY26 gratuity cost using an "expected return on plan assets" of 8.5% (LIC's indicative rate) instead of the discount rate of 7.0%, on opening plan assets of ₹22 crore. Required: quantify the misstatement in P&L and OCI and explain why Ind AS 19 prohibits the approach.

💡 Hint: The total comprehensive income is unchanged; only the split moves.

Q3. Where does net interest go? [L2]

An analyst compares two FMCG companies: one includes gratuity net interest in employee benefits expense, the other in finance costs. Required: explain the effect on EBITDA, the disclosures that let the analyst normalise, and the Ind AS 19.134 position.

Q4. Mid-year plan amendment [L3]

A plan is amended on 21 November 2025, increasing DBO by ₹6 crore (past service cost). The discount rate at 1 April 2025 was 7.0% and at 21 November 2025 6.8%. Opening net liability was ₹30 crore. Required: explain how the net interest and CSC for FY26 are computed before and after the amendment under 19.122A and 19.123A, and outline the actuarial information you need.
