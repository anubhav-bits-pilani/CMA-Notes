Roadmap topic: Ind AS 19 Employee Benefits: Reading actuarial valuation reports for Gratuity & Leave Encashment—splitting Current Service Cost, Net Interest (P&L) vs Remeasurements (OCI)

Overview

Almost every Indian company carries two actuarial liabilities: gratuity, a statutory defined benefit plan under the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972 (now subsumed in the Code on Social Security 2020), and compensated absences (leave encashment), an other long-term benefit. A few also carry exempt provident fund trusts, post-retirement medical schemes or pension plans. Each year-end an actuary — usually an Institute of Actuaries of India member — issues an Ind AS 19 valuation report. The finance team's job is not to redo the actuarial maths but to read the report critically: check the data sent, challenge assumptions (especially the discount rate), split the cost correctly between P&L and OCI, post the entries, present current and non-current portions and prepare the disclosures. Errors here are common and visible: leave remeasurements taken to OCI, LIC premiums expensed instead of actuarial cost, stale discount rates and entire liabilities shown as non-current.

FY26 adds a one-off complication. The four labour codes, including the Code on Social Security 2020, were brought into force with effect from 21 November 2025. The Code widens the "wages" base used for gratuity (the 50% rule on excluded allowances) and grants gratuity to fixed-term employees after one year. For many companies the DBO jumped, producing a past service cost recognised immediately in P&L and, in many NSE results for Q3 FY26, presented as an exceptional item.

The learning sequence is: classify benefits (s01); understand the gratuity entitlement and its statutory changes (s02, c01); read an actuarial report (s03); understand how the DBO is measured under the projected unit credit method and why Ind AS uses government bond yields (s04); split the cost between P&L and OCI (s05, c02–c06); handle funded plans, the asset ceiling and PF trusts (s06); treat leave encashment correctly (s07); and prepare disclosures (s08).

Related concepts to study

Deferred tax on employee benefits

Ind AS 12: gratuity/leave deductible on payment (Sec 43B) → DTA; tax on OCI remeasurements in OCI (12.61A) → Week 3 (w3_t3)

Under the Income-tax Act, Sec 43B allows employer contributions to approved gratuity funds and leave encashment only on actual payment (leave encashment under Sec 43B(f), upheld by the Supreme Court in Exide Industries, 2020), and Sec 40A(7) disallows unfunded gratuity provisions. The book liability therefore exceeds its tax base (nil), creating a deductible temporary difference and a DTA, subject to probable future taxable profits. Because part of the movement in the liability is recognised in OCI, the related deferred tax follows the item into OCI (Ind AS 12.61A). Under the 115BAA regime the rate is 25.168%. Week 3 Topic 3 builds the full DTA schedule.

Labour code transition and exceptional items

Labour code transition: past service cost in P&L, widely shown as exceptional item in FY26 NSE results

When the Code on Social Security 2020 took effect from 21 November 2025, the widened definition of wages and fixed-term employee gratuity increased the benefit payable for past service. Because the benefit formula changed, the increase is a plan amendment giving rise to past service cost, recognised immediately in P&L (Ind AS 19.103) rather than an actuarial loss in OCI. Many listed companies presented it as an exceptional item under Ind AS 1.97–98 in Q3 FY26 results because of its one-off nature and size. Questions remain about state rules and wage-component interpretation; treat amounts as estimates and follow ICAI/ASB communications current at the reporting date.

US GAAP ASC 715 corridor approach

US GAAP ASC 715: actuarial gains/losses deferred in AOCI and amortised (corridor) — recycling, unlike Ind AS

Under ASC 715, net periodic pension cost includes service cost, interest cost, expected return on plan assets (using an expected long-term rate, not the discount rate), amortisation of prior service cost and amortisation of net gains/losses. Gains and losses are first recorded in AOCI and recycled to P&L when the cumulative net amount exceeds a corridor of 10% of the greater of the projected benefit obligation and the market-related value of plan assets, amortised over remaining service. Prior service cost is also deferred in AOCI and amortised. ASU 2017-07 presents only service cost in operating income. Ind AS 19 recognises past service cost immediately and never recycles OCI, so US GAAP reporting packs of Indian subsidiaries need reconciling adjustments.

Ind AS 102 share-based payments

Ind AS 102 share-based payments as adjacent employee-cost area

ESOPs, RSUs and SARs are employee compensation but fall outside Ind AS 19 (19.1, scope exclusion) and are measured under Ind AS 102. Equity-settled awards are measured at grant-date fair value (Black-Scholes or binomial), expensed over the vesting period with true-up for service and non-market performance conditions; cash-settled SARs are remeasured to fair value each reporting date like a liability. In India, the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations 2021 govern listed-company schemes, and perquisite tax arises under Sec 17(2) on exercise. In a cost analysis, employee benefits expense in Schedule III includes share-based payment expense, so FP&A bridges must separate the non-cash ESOP charge from Ind AS 19 costs.

Rebuilding the actuary's roll-forward in Excel

Excel: rebuild actuary's DBO roll-forward and tie P&L, OCI and balance sheet

Build three linked blocks: (1) DBO roll-forward (opening, current service cost, interest cost, past service cost, benefits paid, actuarial gains/losses split three ways, closing); (2) plan-asset roll-forward (opening fair value, interest income, contributions, benefits paid, return excluding interest, closing); (3) net liability and statement mapping (P&L = CSC + PSC + interest cost − interest income; OCI = actuarial losses − return excluding interest; balance sheet = DBO − assets, split current/non-current). Recompute interest cost as opening DBO × discount rate (adjusted for mid-year flows) and flag differences above a tolerance. Tie closing net liability to the TB and benefits paid to the payroll register and LIC statements.

Common pitfalls

Taking leave encashment remeasurements to OCI

Taking leave encashment remeasurements to OCI

Because gratuity remeasurements go to OCI, preparers apply the same split to leave encashment, sometimes because actuaries present the leave report in the same format. Compensated absences that are not expected to be settled wholly within 12 months are other long-term employee benefits; Ind AS 19.154–156 requires service cost, net interest and remeasurements all in P&L. Detect it by checking the OCI note: the only Ind AS 19 OCI line should relate to post-employment DB plans. Correct by reclassifying from OCI to employee benefits expense (with the related deferred tax).

Expensing only LIC contributions

Expensing only LIC contributions instead of actuarially determined cost

Companies with LIC group gratuity schemes often debit the premium paid to "Gratuity expense" and stop there, treating the LIC policy as if it settled the obligation. A group gratuity cash-accumulation policy is a plan asset, not a transfer of the obligation; the employer still bears the DB risk. The expense is the actuarially determined service cost plus net interest, and the contribution simply reduces the net liability. Detect by comparing the P&L charge with the actuary's report and checking whether the balance sheet shows the net DBO. The fix is to recognise the DBO and plan assets at fair value per the actuary and the LIC fund statement.

Presenting the entire gratuity liability as non-current

Presenting entire gratuity liability as non-current, ignoring the actuary's current portion

Some preparers classify the whole gratuity provision as non-current because gratuity is a "retirement" benefit. Schedule III requires provisions to be split into current and non-current, and benefits expected to be paid within 12 months (resignations, retirements, deaths) are current. Ind AS 19.133 does not mandate a split for post-employment benefits, but Schedule III presentation does, and Indian practice uses the actuary's estimate of next-year outflow. Detect by checking whether the actuary's current/non-current table has been used and by comparing last year's current portion with this year's actual payments.

Rolling forward prior-year assumptions

Rolling forward prior-year assumptions instead of reporting-date discount rates

Actuaries sometimes reuse last year's assumptions when the company does not respond to the assumption-setting email, or the company asks for the "same rate as last year" for comparability. Ind AS 19.83 requires the discount rate to reflect market yields at the end of the reporting period on government bonds of matching currency and term; salary escalation and attrition must reflect current best estimates (19.75–80). Stale rates mis-state both the DBO and the OCI split. Detect by comparing the discount rate with the FBIL G-sec yield curve at 31 March for the plan's duration; a variance of more than a few basis points needs explanation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Post entries from a gratuity actuarial report [L3]

Narmada Textiles Ltd's actuary reports for FY26 (₹ lakh): opening DBO 1,840; current service cost 182; interest cost 128; past service cost (labour code) 216; benefits paid 154; actuarial loss from financial assumptions 46; actuarial gain from demographic assumptions (12); experience loss 38. Plan assets with LIC: opening fair value 1,210; interest income 84; employer contributions 300; benefits paid by LIC 154; return on plan assets excluding interest (9). Discount rate 6.75% (prior year 7.10%), salary escalation 8%. Current portion of the net liability per actuary: 140. Tax rate 25.168%. Required: (a) prepare the DBO and plan-asset roll-forwards and the closing net liability; (b) compute the P&L charge and OCI; (c) pass all journal entries including deferred tax; (d) present the balance sheet split; (e) state how the past service cost should be presented in the quarterly results.

💡 Hint: Check that interest cost and interest income are each close to the opening balance times the opening discount rate before posting.

Q2. Leave encashment and the OCI error [L2]

The same company's leave encashment report shows: opening obligation 620; service cost 74; interest cost 41; benefits paid 88; actuarial loss 29; closing 676; current portion 210. The draft financial statements show ₹29 lakh in OCI. Required: (a) correct the accounting; (b) state the effect on profit before tax, OCI and EPS (52 lakh shares); (c) explain whether a ₹676 lakh liability with ₹210 lakh current is appropriate under Ind AS 1.69(d).

Q3. Discount rate challenge and sensitivity [L2]

The actuary has used a discount rate of 7.25% (last year's rate) for a gratuity plan with a weighted average duration of 8 years. The 8-year G-sec yield at 31 March 2026 is 6.60%. The sensitivity table shows a 1% decrease in discount rate increases the DBO of ₹2,400 lakh by 7.8%. Required: (a) estimate the effect of correcting the rate; (b) state where the effect is recognised; (c) draft the review note to the actuary; (d) explain how the sensitivity and maturity-profile disclosures support your estimate.

Q4. Exempt PF trust and asset ceiling [L3]

A company runs an exempt PF trust with a guaranteed return equal to the EPFO-declared rate (8.25% for FY25). The trust's portfolio yields 7.6%. The actuary values the interest-guarantee shortfall at ₹3.4 crore at 31 March 2026. Separately, the company's gratuity fund is in surplus by ₹1.8 crore; refunds are not permitted under the trust deed, and the present value of future contribution reductions is ₹0.9 crore. Required: (a) explain why the PF trust is accounted for as a DB plan; (b) compute the net asset to recognise for gratuity under the asset ceiling; (c) explain where the change in asset-ceiling effect is recognised.

Q5. FP&A forecast of employee benefit cost [L2]

Build the FY27 forecast for gratuity cost using: opening DBO ₹48 crore, plan assets ₹30 crore, current service cost growth 9% on FY26 CSC of ₹5.2 crore, discount rate 6.7%, expected benefits paid ₹3.6 crore mid-year, expected contribution ₹6 crore at the start of the year. Required: (a) forecast P&L cost; (b) explain why OCI is not forecast in the budget; (c) list the cash-flow items and their classification.
