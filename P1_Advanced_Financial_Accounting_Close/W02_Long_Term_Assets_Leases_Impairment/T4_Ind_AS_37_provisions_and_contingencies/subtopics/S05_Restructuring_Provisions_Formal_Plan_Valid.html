Subtopic note

Restructuring Provisions: Formal Plan, Valid Expectation, Direct Costs

Scope: Restructuring (37.70–83): detailed formal plan + valid expectation; only direct costs — exclude retraining, relocation, marketing

Why it matters

Plant closures, business-line exits, shared-service migrations and labour-code-driven workforce redesign are common in Indian manufacturing, IT services and banking. Restructuring charges are usually presented as exceptional items in quarterly results under SEBI LODR Reg 33 and Schedule III, and analysts add them back to "adjusted EBITDA". That combination — a charge that analysts ignore — makes restructuring provisions a favourite place to bury normal operating costs or future losses. Ind AS 37 counters this with strict timing and content rules, which auditors test line by line.

Core notes

What counts as a restructuring (37.70)

A restructuring is a programme that is planned and controlled by management and materially changes either the scope of a business undertaken by an entity or the manner in which that business is conducted. Examples (37.70):

  • sale or termination of a line of business;
  • closure of business locations in a country or region, or relocation of activities from one country or region to another;
  • changes in management structure, for example eliminating a layer of management;
  • fundamental reorganisations that have a material effect on the nature and focus of the entity's operations.

A restructuring provision is recognised only when the general recognition criteria of 37.14 are met (37.71), applied through specific rules on when a constructive obligation exists.

When a constructive obligation arises (37.72)

Both conditions must exist at the reporting date:

(a) A detailed formal plan identifying at least:

  1. the business or part of a business concerned;
  2. the principal locations affected;
  3. the location, function and approximate number of employees who will be compensated for terminating their services;
  4. the expenditures that will be undertaken;
  5. when the plan will be implemented; and

(b) A valid expectation in those affected that the entity will carry out the restructuring, raised by starting to implement the plan or announcing its main features to those affected by it.

Evidence of starting implementation includes dismantling plant, selling assets or the public announcement of the plan's main features (37.73). The announcement must be specific enough to raise valid expectations in customers, suppliers and employees (or their representatives — unions). For a plan to give rise to a constructive obligation, implementation must begin as soon as possible and be completed in a timeframe that makes significant changes unlikely; a long delay suggests the plan is not yet an obligation (37.74).

Board decisions (37.75–76). A decision by management or the Board before the reporting date does not create a constructive obligation unless, before that date, implementation has started or the main features have been announced. Where the Board includes employee or other representatives whose approval is required, or the entity must consult a works council, the decision may create an obligation if communicated. Decisions taken after the reporting date are non-adjusting events disclosed under Ind AS 10 if material.

Sale of an operation (37.78–79). No obligation arises for the sale of an operation until the entity is committed to the sale, that is, there is a binding sale agreement. Until then, assets are reviewed for impairment (Ind AS 36) and may be classified as held for sale (Ind AS 105).

Which costs qualify (37.80–83)

A restructuring provision includes only the direct expenditures arising from the restructuring, which are both:

  • necessarily entailed by the restructuring; and
  • not associated with the ongoing activities of the entity (37.80).

Explicitly excluded (37.81), because they relate to the future conduct of the business:

  • retraining or relocating continuing staff;
  • marketing;
  • investment in new systems and distribution networks.

Also excluded:

  • Identifiable future operating losses up to the date of restructuring, unless they relate to an onerous contract (37.82).
  • Gains on the expected disposal of assets, even if the sale is part of the restructuring (37.83).

Worked illustration

A consumer-goods company closes its Kolkata factory. On 15 March 2026 the Board approved a plan covering 320 workers, with closure by 30 September 2026; the plan and severance terms were communicated to the workers and the union on 25 March 2026. Estimates: severance ₹11.2 crore; penalty for terminating a raw-material contract ₹1.4 crore; dismantling and clearance ₹0.9 crore; retraining 40 workers moving to Pune ₹0.35 crore; relocation of moulding machines ₹0.8 crore; operating losses April–September ₹2.6 crore; expected gain on sale of land ₹18 crore.

Detailed formal plan and communication both exist before 31 March 2026, so a constructive obligation exists. Provision = 11.2 + 1.4 + 0.9 = ₹13.5 crore. Retraining, relocation and operating losses are excluded; the land gain is ignored until realised.

Presentation and disclosure

  • Exceptional item presentation: Schedule III does not use the term but requires disclosure of items whose size, nature or incidence makes separate presentation relevant (Ind AS 1.97–98 lists restructurings). SEBI's quarterly result format has an "exceptional items" line.
  • Ind AS 37.84–85: roll-forward, nature, expected timing and uncertainties.
  • If the restructuring is a discontinued operation, Ind AS 105 presentation applies.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Announcement to unions vs individual employees. Communication to the recognised union in a factory with collective bargaining is typically sufficient to raise valid expectations.
  • Restructuring spanning several years. A three-year transformation plan with vague later phases supports a provision only for the phases that meet 37.72 at the reporting date.
  • Industrial Disputes Act requirements. Closure of an establishment with 100 or more workers needs prior government permission (Sec 25-O, ID Act; now Industrial Relations Code 2020 thresholds — 300 workers — check current applicability). Where permission is required and not obtained, consider whether the plan can be implemented as communicated.
  • Staff who will be redeployed. Costs of those redeployed are not severance and are excluded.
  • Provision vs accrual. Once severance amounts are agreed and paid, residual balances become payables.

Key takeaways

  • Restructuring = a management-controlled programme that materially changes scope or manner of business (37.70).
  • Provide only when a detailed formal plan exists and a valid expectation has been raised by implementation or announcement before the reporting date (37.72).
  • A Board decision alone is insufficient; sales of operations need a binding agreement (37.75, 37.78).
  • Include only direct costs necessarily entailed and not associated with ongoing activities (37.80).
  • Exclude retraining, relocation, marketing, new systems, future operating losses and expected disposal gains (37.81–83).
  • Severance follows Ind AS 19 termination-benefit recognition, linked to the restructuring date.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying restructuring costs [L1]

An IT services company is closing its Kochi delivery centre. Estimated costs: severance ₹6.8 crore; garden-leave salaries for employees not required to work during notice ₹0.9 crore; salaries of employees working through notice ₹1.6 crore; relocating 60 engineers to Bengaluru ₹1.2 crore; early lease termination penalty (lease is a short-term lease accounted under the exemption) ₹0.4 crore; new collaboration software for the remaining centres ₹0.7 crore; outplacement services for leavers ₹0.2 crore. Required: compute the restructuring provision and justify each inclusion or exclusion.

Q2. Timing across the reporting date [L2]

A steel company's Board approved a detailed plan to shut a rolling mill on 10 March 2026. A press release dated 2 April 2026 announced the closure, and individual notices were issued on 5 April 2026. The financial statements for FY26 will be approved on 22 May 2026. Required: (a) is a provision recognised at 31 March 2026? (b) what is disclosed? (c) what if the union had been formally informed of the plan's main features on 27 March 2026?

Q3. Sale of a division [L2]

A pharma company decided in January 2026 to sell its animal-health division and had signed a non-binding term sheet by 31 March 2026. Expected costs: legal and advisory ₹3 crore, carve-out IT costs ₹5 crore, severance for employees not transferring ₹2 crore. Expected gain on sale ₹140 crore. Required: explain the Ind AS 37, Ind AS 105 and Ind AS 36 analysis at 31 March 2026 and identify which costs, if any, are provided.

💡 Hint: Check Ind AS 37.78 on binding agreements before looking at the cost list.

Q4. Audit review of an exceptional charge [L3]

A listed FMCG company reports a ₹48 crore exceptional "transformation charge" in Q4 FY26. The breakdown provided: severance (communicated) ₹19 crore; consultants designing the new route-to-market ₹8 crore; brand relaunch campaign ₹7 crore; provision for losses of the old distribution entity for FY27 ₹9 crore; write-down of obsolete packaging ₹5 crore. Required: evaluate each component against Ind AS 37, Ind AS 19, Ind AS 2 and Ind AS 1, state the adjustments you would propose and explain the effect on reported EBITDA and exceptional items.
