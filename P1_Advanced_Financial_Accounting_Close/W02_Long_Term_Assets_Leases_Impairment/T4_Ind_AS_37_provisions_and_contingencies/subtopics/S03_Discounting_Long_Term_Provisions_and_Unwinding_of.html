Subtopic note

Discounting Long-Term Provisions and Unwinding of Discount

Scope: Discounting (37.45–47) at pre-tax rate reflecting time value and specific risks; unwinding as borrowing cost (37.60)

Why it matters

Long-dated provisions — mine closure, landfill aftercare, decommissioning of telecom towers and offshore platforms, long-tail litigation, multi-year CSR commitments — can be a multiple of their present value. Discounting therefore changes the balance sheet materially, and the unwinding creates a recurring finance cost that sits below EBITDA. Analysts strip it out of operating cost; lenders often include provisions in net debt definitions; FP&A models must forecast it. The discount rate is also a frequent audit finding: entities use post-tax WACC, borrowing rates or stale rates instead of a current pre-tax, liability-specific rate.

Core notes

When to discount (37.45–46)

Where the effect of the time value of money is material, the provision is the present value of the expenditures expected to be required to settle the obligation (37.45). Because of the time value of money, provisions relating to cash outflows that arise soon after the reporting date are more onerous than those of the same amount arising later (37.46). Materiality is judged by the gap between nominal and discounted amounts, not by the length of time alone: a ₹5 lakh obligation payable in 3 years is usually left undiscounted; a ₹200 crore obligation payable in 15 years cannot be.

The discount rate (37.47)

The discount rate (or rates) shall be:

  • a pre-tax rate (or rates), because provisions are measured before tax;
  • reflecting current market assessments of the time value of money — that is, at the reporting date, not historical;
  • reflecting the risks specific to the liability;
  • not reflecting risks for which future cash flow estimates have already been adjusted.

Building the rate in practice (India):

Component Typical source
Risk-free time value FBIL/CCIL zero-coupon G-sec yield for the matching tenor (e.g., 10-year at about 6.3–7.0% in recent years; check the reporting-date yield)
Currency/inflation consistency Nominal rate for nominal cash flows (inflated for cost escalation); real rate if cash flows are in today's prices
Liability-specific risk Either adjust cash flows or adjust the rate; for a liability, adding risk lowers the rate
Own credit risk Generally excluded in practice for Ind AS 37 (entity's credit spread is not a risk "specific to the liability"); policy should be stated

Post-tax WACC is wrong on two counts — it is post-tax and it reflects the risks of the entity's assets and capital structure, not of the obligation. The entity's incremental borrowing rate is also inappropriate because it embeds own credit risk.

A single rate or a yield curve (rates for each period) may be used. For multi-year outflows, discount each year's cash flow at the spot rate for that tenor.

Measuring the present value

\[ PV = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^{t}} \]

where \(CF_t\) is the expected (nominal) outflow in year \(t\), \(r\) is the pre-tax rate, \(n\) is the final settlement year. For irregular dates use XNPV:

=XNPV(rate, cash_flows, dates)          ' note: XNPV discounts from the first date in the range
=PV(rate, nper, 0, -future_amount)      ' single future outflow

Unwinding of discount (37.60)

Where discounting is used, the carrying amount of the provision increases in each period to reflect the passage of time. This increase is recognised as borrowing cost — in practice within finance costs in the statement of profit and loss (37.60):

\[ \text{Unwinding}_t = \text{Opening provision}_t \times r \]

The unwinding is not a borrowing cost eligible for capitalisation under Ind AS 23 (Ind AS 16 Appendix A confirms this for decommissioning liabilities); it is expensed as it occurs.

Changes in the discount rate

At each reporting date the provision is remeasured using the current rate. The effect of a rate change is reported separately in the roll-forward (37.84(e) requires disclosure of "the increase during the period in the discounted amount arising from the passage of time and the effect of any change in the discount rate"). For general provisions the remeasurement goes to P&L; many entities present it in finance costs alongside unwinding, others in the original expense line — choose a policy and apply it consistently. For decommissioning provisions under the cost model, the remeasurement adjusts the related asset (Ind AS 16 Appendix A), not P&L (see Week 2 Topic 1).

Worked illustration

A landfill operator must pay ₹30 crore for capping and aftercare on 31 March 2031. At 1 April 2025, the pre-tax rate is 7.5%.

\[ PV_0 = \frac{30}{(1.075)^{6}} = \frac{30}{1.5433} = \text{₹}19.44 \text{ crore} \]

Year Opening Unwinding at 7.5% Closing
FY26 19.44 1.46 20.90
FY27 20.90 1.57 22.47
FY28 22.47 1.69 24.16
FY29 24.16 1.81 25.97
FY30 25.97 1.95 27.92
FY31 27.92 2.09 30.00 (rounded)

If at 31 March 2026 the rate falls to 7.0% with the cash flow unchanged, the provision becomes \(30 \div 1.07^{5} = \text{₹}21.39\) crore; the ₹0.49 crore increase over ₹20.90 crore is the rate-change effect, disclosed separately from the ₹1.46 crore unwinding.

Presentation and modelling

  • Split the provision between current (expected to be settled within 12 months or no right to defer) and non-current under Schedule III.
  • In the cash flow statement unwinding is non-cash; add it back in operating cash flow reconciliation (indirect method). Settlement cash flows are usually operating; decommissioning outflows may be classified as investing by some entities — policy matters.
  • In a 3-statement model: provision closing = opening + new charges + unwinding − utilisation − reversals; unwinding feeds the finance-cost line, not EBITDA.
  • Deferred tax: provisions are typically deductible on payment under the Income-tax Act, giving a deductible temporary difference and DTA on the discounted carrying amount.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative real rates. If cash flows are in today's prices and inflation exceeds the nominal rate, a real rate may be negative, producing a PV above the nominal amount. This is mathematically correct; check consistency rather than floor at zero.
  • Long tenors beyond the G-sec curve. For 40-year mine closure, extrapolate the curve and document the method.
  • Short-term provisions. Do not discount current warranty provisions where the effect is immaterial; documenting the materiality test is enough.
  • Unwinding classification in EBITDA definitions. Loan covenants may define EBITDA differently; reconcile the accounting presentation to the covenant definition.
  • Rate change and estimate change at the same date. Compute the cash-flow change at the old rate first, then the rate change on the revised cash flows, so the attribution is reproducible.

Key takeaways

  • Discount when the time value effect is material (37.45).
  • Use a current, pre-tax rate reflecting liability-specific risks not already in the cash flows (37.47); never post-tax WACC or IBR.
  • Unwinding = opening provision × rate, charged to finance costs as borrowing cost (37.60), never capitalised.
  • Disclose unwinding and the effect of rate changes separately in the roll-forward (37.84(e)).
  • Keep nominal/real and pre-tax consistency between cash flows and rate.
  • Under the cost model, decommissioning rate changes go to the asset, other provisions to P&L.

Practice Questions

Q1. PV and unwinding schedule for a mine closure [L1]

A coal-washery operator expects to spend ₹48 crore on closure on 31 March 2034 (nominal, inflated). On 1 April 2025 the appropriate pre-tax rate is 7.3%. Required: compute the provision at 1 April 2025, the unwinding for FY26 and FY27, and the entries for FY26.

Q2. Choice of discount rate [L2]

The CFO proposes discounting a 12-year restoration provision at the company's post-tax WACC of 11.8% "because that is our hurdle rate". The 12-year G-sec yield is 6.9%; the tax rate is 25.17%; cash flows are best-estimate nominal amounts with no risk adjustment. Required: (a) explain why the proposal fails Ind AS 37.47; (b) recommend a rate and justify each element; (c) quantify the difference in the provision for a ₹100 crore outflow in year 12.

💡 Hint: Separate the time-value element from the risk element and ask whose risk each rate reflects.

Q3. Rate change versus cash-flow change [L2]

A provision for long-tail litigation stands at ₹14.2 crore at 31 March 2025, being ₹18 crore payable on 31 March 2029 discounted at 6.1%. At 31 March 2026 the expected payment is revised to ₹19.5 crore on the same date and the rate rises to 6.8%. Required: prepare the FY26 roll-forward showing unwinding, change in estimate and change in discount rate separately, with the P&L lines affected.

Q4. Model build: multi-year provision with yield curve [L3]

Build an Excel schedule for a provision with outflows of ₹4 crore in each of FY28–FY32. Use spot rates of 6.6%, 6.7%, 6.8%, 6.9% and 7.0% for years 2 to 6 from 31 March 2026. Required: (a) specify the input block, PV formula per row and the check against a single equivalent rate; (b) design the next-year unwinding calculation when spot rates are used; (c) list the outputs that feed the P&L, balance sheet and cash flow statement.
