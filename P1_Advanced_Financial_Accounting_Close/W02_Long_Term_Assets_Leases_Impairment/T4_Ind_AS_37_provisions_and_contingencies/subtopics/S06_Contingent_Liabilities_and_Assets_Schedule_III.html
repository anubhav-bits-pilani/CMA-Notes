Subtopic note

Contingent Liabilities and Assets: Schedule III Note in Practice

Scope: Contingent liabilities/assets: Schedule III note for tax demands, GST notices, guarantees, legal claims; seriously prejudicial exemption (37.92)

Why it matters

The contingent-liability note is among the most read notes in Indian annual reports. Lenders and rating agencies add a share of it to adjusted debt; equity analysts track the growth of disputed tax and GST demands; SEBI and NFRA have flagged notes that list amounts without the nature, status or likelihood of outflow. For a reporting role you will compile it from the legal and indirect-tax trackers every quarter; for audit, you will test completeness (every demand order captured), classification (provision vs contingent liability vs Ind AS 12) and whether "remote" items are properly excluded.

Core notes

Definitions (37.10)

A contingent liability is:

  • (a) a possible obligation that arises from past events and whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the control of the entity; or
  • (b) a present obligation that arises from past events but is not recognised because (i) it is not probable that an outflow will be required, or (ii) the amount cannot be measured with sufficient reliability.

A contingent asset is a possible asset that arises from past events and whose existence will be confirmed only by uncertain future events not wholly within the control of the entity.

Recognition and assessment

  • Contingent liabilities are not recognised (37.27). They are disclosed unless the possibility of an outflow is remote (37.28, 37.86).
  • Where an entity is jointly and severally liable, the part expected to be met by other parties is a contingent liability; the entity's own probable share is a provision (37.29).
  • Contingent liabilities are assessed continually; if an outflow becomes probable, a provision is recognised in the period of the change (37.30).
  • Contingent assets are not recognised (37.31); when the inflow becomes virtually certain, the asset is not contingent and is recognised (37.33). They are disclosed when an inflow is probable (37.34, 37.89). See concept c04.

Disclosure content (37.86, 37.89, 37.91)

For each class of contingent liability, unless remote (37.86):

  1. a brief description of the nature;
  2. where practicable, an estimate of its financial effect (measured per 37.36–52);
  3. an indication of the uncertainties relating to amount or timing of any outflow;
  4. the possibility of any reimbursement.

Where a provision and a contingent liability arise from the same circumstances, the link is disclosed (37.88). For probable contingent assets: brief description and, where practicable, estimate of financial effect, avoiding misleading indications of likelihood (37.89–90). Where any information is not disclosed because it is impracticable, that fact is stated (37.91).

Schedule III requirements

Schedule III (Division II, General Instructions for the Balance Sheet) requires disclosure, to the extent not provided for, of:

Contingent liabilities, classified as:

  • (a) claims against the company not acknowledged as debt;
  • (b) guarantees;
  • (c) other money for which the company is contingently liable.

Commitments, classified as:

  • (a) estimated amount of contracts remaining to be executed on capital account and not provided for;
  • (b) uncalled liability on shares and other investments partly paid;
  • (c) other commitments (specify nature).

Ind AS 37 governs recognition and the content of disclosure; Schedule III dictates the classification headings. Both apply.

Typical Indian items and their home

Matter Standard Usual position
GST show-cause notice/demand under appeal Ind AS 37 Contingent liability if possible; provision if probable; include interest and penalty where demanded
Customs, excise, service-tax legacy disputes Ind AS 37 As above; check pre-deposit paid under appeal (asset, not reduction of contingent amount — disclose both)
Income-tax demands and disallowances Ind AS 12 Appendix C Not in Ind AS 37; uncertain tax treatments reflected in current/deferred tax; many entities still disclose demands not provided as "contingent liabilities" in the tax note — label the standard correctly
Corporate guarantee for a subsidiary loan Ind AS 109 in issuer's books (financial guarantee contract at fair value, then higher of ECL and amortised amount) Financial guarantee liability recognised; the gross exposure is still commonly disclosed under the Schedule III "guarantees" heading
Bank guarantees given on own behalf (performance, bid bonds) Ind AS 37 Contingent liability only for the underlying obligation if possible; the guarantee itself is not a separate exposure
Legal claims by customers, employees, regulators Ind AS 37 Assess per claim or class
Bills discounted with recourse Ind AS 109 (derecognition) Often not derecognised; if derecognised, disclose recourse exposure
Labour and PF damages claims Ind AS 37 Assess; PF-related Supreme Court (2019) "basic wages" ruling exposures disclosed widely

The seriously prejudicial exemption (37.92)

In extremely rare cases, disclosure of some or all of the information required by 37.84–89 can be expected to prejudice seriously the position of the entity in a dispute with other parties on the subject matter of the provision, contingent liability or contingent asset. In such cases the entity need not disclose the information, but shall disclose the general nature of the dispute, together with the fact that, and reason why, the information has not been disclosed (37.92).

The exemption is narrow:

  • it covers some or all of the detailed information (amount, assumptions), not the existence of the dispute;
  • it relates to disputes with other parties (litigation, arbitration, competition cases), not to general commercial sensitivity;
  • it is used in "extremely rare" cases — routine tax demands are not covered.

Example wording: "The Company is a defendant in an arbitration initiated by a former joint venture partner alleging breach of the shareholders' agreement. The Company has recognised a provision in respect of this matter. Disclosure of the amount and assumptions has not been made because, in the opinion of the Board, it could seriously prejudice the Company's position in the arbitration."

Practical compilation process

  1. Pull the legal tracker, indirect-tax tracker, income-tax tracker and guarantee register; reconcile counts to the prior year.
  2. For each matter capture: forum, amount demanded (tax, interest, penalty), date, pre-deposit, counsel's probability assessment, expected timing.
  3. Classify: provision (probable), contingent liability (possible), remote (exclude), Ind AS 12 (move).
  4. Aggregate by Schedule III class and by nature; disclose material individual matters separately.
  5. Tie amounts in the note to supporting orders; tie provisions to the roll-forward.

Illustration

A cement company has: GST demand ₹14.6 crore (tax ₹9.0 crore, interest ₹4.1 crore, penalty ₹1.5 crore) before the first appellate authority, 10% pre-deposit of tax ₹0.9 crore paid, counsel rates outcome "possible"; a customer claim ₹2.4 crore assessed "probable", best estimate ₹1.6 crore; a ₹40 crore corporate guarantee for a subsidiary's loan (outstanding ₹32 crore), fair value at issue ₹0.6 crore. Note: claims not acknowledged as debt include GST ₹14.6 crore; customer claim is provided at ₹1.6 crore, with the excess disclosed only if a further outflow is possible; the guarantee is recognised under Ind AS 109 and the ₹32 crore exposure (or ₹40 crore limit, per policy) disclosed under guarantees; the pre-deposit sits in other non-current assets.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Amount to disclose for guarantees. Use the outstanding borrowing covered, and state the guarantee limit; disclosing only the limit overstates, disclosing only outstanding may omit undrawn exposure.
  • Interest and penalty. Include where demanded or legally accruing; state if excluded.
  • Matters won at one forum and appealed by the department. Still contingent until finally resolved; the likelihood may now be remote.
  • Remote is a high bar. "Strong legal grounds" alone does not make a matter remote; consider the forum history.
  • Group notes. Consolidated contingent liabilities exclude intra-group guarantees but include the group share of associates' and JVs' contingent liabilities (Ind AS 112 requires separate disclosure).

Key takeaways

  • Contingent liabilities are possible obligations, or present obligations failing probability or reliable measurement; never recognised, disclosed unless remote.
  • Schedule III classifies them into claims not acknowledged as debt, guarantees and other; commitments are separate.
  • GST and customs disputes are Ind AS 37; income-tax disputes are Ind AS 12 Appendix C.
  • Financial guarantees issued are recognised under Ind AS 109; the exposure is still disclosed.
  • The 37.92 exemption permits withholding details, never the existence and general nature of a dispute, and requires stating why.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draft a Schedule III contingent liability note [L2]

From the following, draft the note for an NSE-listed auto-parts company at 31 March 2026: (i) GST demand on classification of goods ₹6.2 crore plus penalty ₹6.2 crore, assessed possible; (ii) customs demand ₹1.1 crore, assessed remote after a favourable High Court ruling on identical facts; (iii) employee compensation claims ₹0.8 crore across 14 cases, probable, best estimate ₹0.5 crore; (iv) corporate guarantee to a bank for a JV's working-capital limit of ₹25 crore, drawn ₹18 crore; (v) capital contracts not provided ₹42 crore; (vi) income-tax demand ₹9.5 crore under appeal. Required: present the note, the commitments note and a list of items excluded with reasons.

Q2. Seriously prejudicial exemption [L2]

A company is defending a ₹600 crore damages claim from a competitor in a patent case and has recognised a provision of ₹85 crore based on settlement discussions. The legal team wants to omit the matter entirely. Required: draft the minimum disclosure permitted under Ind AS 37.92 and explain why complete omission is not permitted.

Q3. Jointly and severally liable [L2]

A consortium of three contractors is jointly and severally liable for ₹30 crore of liquidated damages on a metro project. The company's share is one-third; one partner is in insolvency proceedings and expected to pay nothing; the other partner is solvent. The outflow is probable. Required: determine the provision and the contingent liability under Ind AS 37.29.

💡 Hint: Separate the share the entity expects to bear from the share it expects others to meet.

Q4. Audit test of completeness [L3]

As statutory auditor of a large FMCG company, design procedures to test the completeness and classification of the contingent liability note, covering GST portal notices, legal confirmations, Board minutes, and income-tax orders. Include what evidence would cause you to move an item from contingent liability to provision.
