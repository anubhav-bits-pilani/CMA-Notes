Subtopic note

Reimbursements, Future Operating Losses and Unspent CSR

Scope: Reimbursements (37.53), no provision for future operating losses (37.63), unspent CSR on ongoing projects (Sec 135(6))

Why it matters

These three rules close common loopholes. Insurance and indemnity recoveries tempt preparers to net provisions down; expected future losses tempt them to front-load charges; CSR shortfalls tempt them to treat unspent statutory amounts as voluntary. Each shows up regularly in audit qualifications, CARO reporting (clause 3(xx) on unspent CSR) and NFRA observations. In FP&A, CSR spend is a budgeted P&L line tied to the average net profit computation, and the unspent ongoing-project transfer is a cash event that treasury must plan for within 30 days of year-end.

Core notes

Reimbursements (37.53–58)

Where some or all of the expenditure required to settle a provision is expected to be reimbursed by another party (insurer, indemnifier under an indemnity clause, supplier under a warranty back-to-back), the reimbursement:

  • is recognised when, and only when, it is virtually certain that reimbursement will be received if the entity settles the obligation (37.53);
  • is treated as a separate asset, not netted against the provision in the balance sheet;
  • is recognised at an amount not exceeding the provision.

In the statement of profit and loss, the expense relating to the provision may be presented net of the amount recognised for a reimbursement (37.54).

Rationale: the entity remains liable for the full amount if the third party fails to pay (37.56). Where the entity has no liability for costs if the third party fails to pay, those costs are not included in the provision at all (37.57) — for example, where a lessor bears certain costs directly. Where the entity is jointly and severally liable, the portion expected to be met by others is a contingent liability (37.58, 37.29).

Disclosure: the amount of any expected reimbursement, stating the amount of any asset recognised (37.85(c)).

Situation Balance sheet P&L
Product liability ₹8 crore; insurer has accepted claim for ₹6 crore Provision ₹8 crore (liability); Reimbursement receivable ₹6 crore (asset) Expense may be shown net at ₹2 crore
Insurer has only acknowledged receipt of claim Provision ₹8 crore; no asset Expense ₹8 crore

Virtual certainty is a high bar: written acceptance of liability by the insurer, no dispute on coverage, policy limits and deductibles confirmed, and an insurer of sound credit standing. A surveyor's report alone is generally insufficient unless the insurer has accepted it.

No provision for future operating losses (37.63–65)

Provisions shall not be recognised for future operating losses (37.63). Future operating losses do not meet the definition of a liability — there is no present obligation from a past event — nor the recognition criteria of 37.14 (37.64).

An expectation of future operating losses is an indication that certain assets of the operation may be impaired; the entity tests those assets under Ind AS 36 (37.65). The P&L effect of a weak business therefore appears through impairment of PPE, ROU assets, goodwill and intangibles — not through a provision.

Distinguish:

Item Allowed?
Expected losses of a division next year No provision; test for impairment
Unavoidable losses on a specific onerous contract Provision under 37.66
Operating losses up to the date of a restructuring Excluded from restructuring provision (37.82)
Expected losses on a construction contract with a customer Onerous test under Ind AS 37 applied to the Ind AS 115 contract

Unspent CSR on ongoing projects — Sec 135(6)

The statutory obligation. Sec 135 applies to companies with net worth ≥ ₹500 crore, or turnover ≥ ₹1,000 crore, or net profit ≥ ₹5 crore in the immediately preceding financial year. They must spend at least 2% of the average net profit of the three immediately preceding financial years (Sec 135(5)) on Schedule VII activities through the CSR policy. The Companies (Amendment) Act 2019 and 2020, with the Companies (CSR Policy) Amendment Rules 2021 effective 22 January 2021, made the unspent amount mandatory to transfer:

  • Unspent amount relating to an ongoing project (a multi-year project undertaken with a timeline not exceeding three years excluding the year of commencement, including projects initially not approved as multi-year but extended by the Board): transfer within 30 days from the end of the financial year to a special account called the Unspent Corporate Social Responsibility Account opened in a scheduled bank; spend within three financial years from the date of transfer; any amount remaining unspent thereafter is transferred to a Schedule VII fund (for example, PM CARES or PM National Relief Fund) within 30 days from the end of the third financial year (Sec 135(6)).
  • Unspent amount not relating to an ongoing project: transfer to a Schedule VII fund within six months of the end of the financial year (second proviso to Sec 135(5)).
  • Non-compliance attracts penalties under Sec 135(7) (twice the amount required to be transferred or ₹1 crore, whichever is less, for the company; one-tenth or ₹2 lakh for officers in default).
  • Excess spend may be set off against the requirement of up to three succeeding financial years, subject to the Rules.

Accounting. Because the transfer is a statutory obligation arising from the past event (the shortfall in the financial year), a liability (provision) is recognised at the reporting date for the amount required to be transferred to the Unspent CSR Account or to the Schedule VII fund. The ICAI Guidance Note on Accounting for Expenditure on Corporate Social Responsibility Activities supports recognition of this liability; the debit is CSR expense in P&L (other expenses). Amounts spent by the company itself are expensed as incurred; contributions to its own CSR trust are expensed when paid/obligated; capital assets created by the company for CSR are generally expensed unless the company retains control and benefits (Guidance Note).

The transfer only moves cash into a restricted account; the provision is extinguished as the money is spent on the project over the following three years (or on transfer to a Schedule VII fund for any residue).

Schedule III disclosure (2021 amendment, Division II, Statement of Profit and Loss additional information). Amount required to be spent; amount of expenditure incurred; shortfall at the end of the year; total of previous years' shortfall; reason for shortfall; nature of CSR activities; details of related party transactions (for example, contribution to a trust controlled by the company); and where a provision is made for a liability incurred by entering into a contractual obligation, the movements in the provision during the year shown separately.

Worked illustration

Konark Steels Ltd's average net profit for FY23–FY25 is ₹420 crore; FY26 CSR obligation = 2% × 420 = ₹8.4 crore. Spent during FY26: ₹5.1 crore, including ₹2.0 crore on a 3-year rural water project (ongoing). Remaining project commitment: ₹2.5 crore budgeted for FY26 not spent. No other ongoing projects. Shortfall = 8.4 − 5.1 = ₹3.3 crore, of which ₹2.5 crore relates to the ongoing project and ₹0.8 crore does not.

  • Transfer ₹2.5 crore to the Unspent CSR Account by 30 April 2026.
  • Transfer ₹0.8 crore to a Schedule VII fund by 30 September 2026.
  • At 31 March 2026: Dr CSR expense ₹3.3 crore, Cr Provision for CSR ₹3.3 crore (current).
  • Cash in the Unspent CSR Account is restricted; present it as other bank balances with disclosure.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Reimbursement from a supplier under a back-to-back warranty. Recognise only if virtually certain; supplier insolvency risk matters.
  • Insurance for property damage (not a provision). An insurance claim for damaged own inventory is not a reimbursement of a provision; it is a contingent asset until virtually certain (s06, c04).
  • CSR projects implemented through an implementing agency. Amounts released to the agency but unspent by the agency are not "spent" for Sec 135 unless the Rules treat them so; check the utilisation certificate.
  • CSR excess spend carried forward. Recognise a prepaid asset only to the extent the Rules permit set-off and the company intends to use it; ICAI guidance supports presenting it as a prepayment.
  • Future losses in a start-up phase. Losses from ramp-up of a new plant are not provided; they flow through P&L as incurred.

Key takeaways

  • Reimbursements: separate asset, only if virtually certain, capped at the provision; net presentation permitted only in P&L (37.53–54).
  • Never provide for future operating losses (37.63); they signal possible impairment under Ind AS 36 (37.65).
  • Sec 135(6): unspent ongoing-project CSR goes to the Unspent CSR Account within 30 days, spend within 3 years; other unspent amounts go to a Schedule VII fund within 6 months.
  • The statutory transfer obligation is a liability at year-end; debit CSR expense.
  • Schedule III requires detailed CSR disclosures including shortfall, reasons and movements in provisions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Insurance recovery on a product recall [L1]

A food company recalled a batch in February 2026 and estimates recall costs of ₹4.6 crore, of which ₹3.5 crore is covered by its product-recall policy subject to a ₹0.25 crore deductible. At 31 March 2026 the insurer has issued a written admission of liability for the covered amount. Required: show the balance sheet and P&L presentation, and explain what would change if the insurer had only appointed a surveyor.

Q2. CSR obligation and transfers [L2]

A company's net profits under Sec 198 for FY23, FY24 and FY25 were ₹180 crore, ₹210 crore and ₹(30) crore (loss). In FY26 it spent ₹1.9 crore on CSR, including ₹1.2 crore on an ongoing 3-year skilling project whose FY26 budget was ₹2.0 crore. Required: (a) compute the CSR obligation and shortfall; (b) split the shortfall between Sec 135(5) and Sec 135(6) transfers with deadlines; (c) pass the year-end and transfer entries; (d) list the Schedule III disclosures.

💡 Hint: Use the average of all three years' Sec 198 profits, including the loss year.

Q3. Division losses and impairment [L2]

The Board of a retail chain wants a ₹12 crore provision for the expected FY27 losses of its 22 loss-making stores, which are planned to be closed only in FY28. Store ROU assets and fit-outs total ₹38 crore. Required: (a) explain why the provision is not permitted; (b) describe the Ind AS 36 testing required, including the CGU level; (c) identify any contracts that could legitimately give rise to an onerous provision.

Q4. Indemnity from a seller after acquisition [L3]

A company acquired a subsidiary in FY25; the seller indemnified it for pre-acquisition tax and labour claims. In FY26 a labour court awarded ₹2.2 crore against the subsidiary for a pre-acquisition dispute; the seller has disputed the indemnity claim in writing. Required: explain the treatment in the subsidiary's standalone financial statements and in the consolidated financial statements, considering Ind AS 37.53 and the indemnification-asset rules in Ind AS 103.
