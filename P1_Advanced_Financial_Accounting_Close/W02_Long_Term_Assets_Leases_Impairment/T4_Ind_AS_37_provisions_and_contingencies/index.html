Roadmap topic: Ind AS 37 Provisions, Contingent Liabilities & Assets: Onerous contracts, restructuring provisions, discounting long-term provisions, and unwinding of discount

Overview

Ind AS 37 governs every liability whose timing or amount is uncertain and that is not covered by a more specific standard: litigation, warranties, environmental clean-up, onerous supply contracts, restructuring, CSR obligations and the long tail of GST, customs and labour disputes that fill the contingent-liability note of almost every Indian listed company. It is a judgement standard. Two finance teams with the same facts can reach different numbers, which is exactly why auditors, NFRA inspection reports and SEBI's financial-reporting reviews keep returning to it. For an FP&A or reporting role the standard matters in three places: the close (recognising, measuring and rolling forward provisions), the forecast (provisions and their unwinding drive finance cost, cash timing and EBITDA adjustments) and the narrative (exceptional items, contingent liability disclosure and management commentary).

The logic runs in a fixed sequence. First decide whether a present obligation exists as a result of a past event, whether an outflow is probable and whether the amount can be estimated reliably (s01, c01). If all three hold, recognise a provision. Next measure it at the best estimate, using expected value for large populations and the most likely outcome for single obligations, adjusted for risk (s02, c02, c06). Where the time value of money is material, discount at a pre-tax rate and unwind the discount through finance cost every period (s03, c03). Two special applications follow: onerous contracts, where you test dedicated assets for impairment before providing for the unavoidable cost (s04, c05), and restructurings, where a provision is allowed only after a detailed formal plan and a valid expectation exist and only for direct costs (s05). Anything that fails recognition is assessed for disclosure as a contingent liability, or as a contingent asset where an inflow is probable (s06, c04). Reimbursements, the ban on providing for future operating losses and the statutory CSR obligation under Sec 135(6) complete the picture (s07).

Ind AS 37 also sits between other standards. Uncertain income-tax treatments are carved out to Ind AS 12 Appendix C, employee termination benefits go to Ind AS 19, onerous revenue contracts were historically split between Ind AS 115 and Ind AS 37, and decommissioning obligations feed into Ind AS 16 asset cost. Getting the boundary right is as much of the job as the measurement.

Related concepts to study

Uncertain income-tax positions under Ind AS 12 Appendix C

Uncertain income-tax positions fall under Ind AS 12 Appendix C, not Ind AS 37; GST/customs disputes remain in Ind AS 37

Ind AS 37.5(b) excludes income taxes from scope, so disputes about taxable profit, disallowances under the Income-tax Act, transfer-pricing adjustments and MAT credits are measured under Ind AS 12 Appendix C (Uncertainty over Income Tax Treatments, effective in India from 1 April 2019). Appendix C asks whether it is probable that the tax authority will accept the treatment, assuming full knowledge; if not, the effect is reflected using the most likely amount or the expected value, whichever better predicts resolution, and the result sits in current or deferred tax, not in provisions. GST, customs, excise, service-tax and stamp-duty disputes are not taxes on income and stay inside Ind AS 37. Interest and penalties on income-tax disputes are a policy choice (Ind AS 12 or Ind AS 37) that must be applied consistently. Week 3 covers the deferred-tax mechanics.

US GAAP ASC 450 loss contingencies

US GAAP ASC 450: "probable" means likely to occur (higher bar), low end of range when no best estimate, discounting uncommon

ASC 450-20 accrues a loss contingency only when it is "probable", meaning the future event is likely to occur, a threshold generally read as well above 50%; Ind AS 37 uses "more likely than not". So a claim assessed at 60% is provided under Ind AS but may only be disclosed under US GAAP. When a range exists and no amount is a better estimate, ASC 450 accrues the low end and discloses the possible additional loss; Ind AS 37.39 takes the midpoint. ASC 450 accruals are usually undiscounted unless amount and timing are fixed or reliably determinable, whereas Ind AS 37.45 requires discounting where material. Gain contingencies are not recognised until realised. These differences matter when reconciling an Indian subsidiary's reporting pack to a US parent.

Links to EPC contracts, decommissioning and termination benefits

Onerous EPC contracts → w1_t4; decommissioning → w2_t1; VRS/termination benefits under Ind AS 19 → w2_t5

Three neighbouring areas reuse Ind AS 37 mechanics. Loss-making EPC and construction contracts measured over time under Ind AS 115 (Week 1 Topic 4) use the Ind AS 37 onerous-contract test for the expected loss on the remaining performance obligation. Decommissioning and restoration obligations (Week 2 Topic 1) are Ind AS 37 provisions, but under the cost model the initial amount and subsequent estimate changes are added to or deducted from the asset under Ind AS 16 Appendix A, with only unwinding through finance cost. Voluntary retirement schemes and other termination benefits (Week 2 Topic 5) are measured under Ind AS 19.159–171, though their recognition date is linked to the Ind AS 37 restructuring provision (Ind AS 19.165).

Excel provision roll-forward

Excel: provision roll-forward (opening + additions + unwinding − utilisation − reversals = closing)

Ind AS 37.84 requires, for each class of provision, a reconciliation of opening to closing balance: additional provisions (including increases to existing ones), amounts used, unused amounts reversed and the increase from the passage of time and any change in discount rate. Build it as a columnar schedule with one row per provision class and a check column that must equal zero against the trial balance. Keep utilisation (cash paid or cost charged) separate from reversal (estimate no longer needed); they hit different lines — utilisation hits the balance sheet only, reversal credits P&L. Drive unwinding from opening balance × periodic rate so that the finance-cost line in the model reconciles to the schedule.

Common pitfalls

Providing for future operating losses or restructuring too early

Providing for future operating losses or restructuring before announcement/commencement

The mistake is booking a "restructuring provision" when the Board approves a plan in March but the plan is neither announced to affected employees nor started until May, or creating a provision for next year's expected losses of a weak division. It happens because management wants to take the hit in a year already labelled bad ("big bath") and because Board minutes feel like an obligating event. Ind AS 37.63 prohibits provisions for future operating losses, and 37.72–75 require a detailed formal plan plus a valid expectation created by announcement or start of implementation before the reporting date. A Board decision alone is not enough unless implementation has started or the main features have been communicated (37.75). In review, check the date of employee and union communication, any public announcement, and whether costs provided include operating losses up to the restructuring date (37.82).

Recording income-tax disputes as Ind AS 37 provisions

Recording income-tax disputes as Ind AS 37 provisions

Many Indian trial balances still carry "Provision for income-tax disputes" inside other provisions, a habit from pre-Ind AS practice. Because income taxes are outside Ind AS 37, these amounts belong in current tax liabilities (or deferred tax) measured under Ind AS 12 Appendix C, and the related disclosure is in the tax note, not the contingent liability note. Misclassification distorts the effective tax-rate reconciliation, EBITDA (if charged to other expenses) and the Schedule III split between provisions and current tax liabilities. Detect it by listing every dispute by statute: anything under the Income-tax Act moves to the tax workpaper; GST, customs and labour claims stay in Ind AS 37.

Netting expected insurance reimbursements against the provision

Netting expected insurance reimbursements against the provision

A product-liability claim of ₹8 crore with ₹6 crore expected from insurers is often booked as a ₹2 crore provision. Ind AS 37.53 requires the provision to be shown gross and the reimbursement recognised as a separate asset only when it is virtually certain that it will be received, capped at the provision. Only in the statement of profit and loss may the expense be presented net (37.54). People net because the economic exposure is the net amount and because insurers are often slow to confirm. In review, look for claims with insurer involvement and check the balance sheet presentation and the evidence for virtual certainty (policy cover confirmed, no dispute on admissibility).

Not separating utilisation from release of excess provisions

Not separating utilisation from release of excess provisions in the roll-forward

A single "movement" or "utilised/reversed" column hides whether a provision fell because cash was paid or because management decided it was over-provided. The distinction matters: reversals credit P&L and may need to be shown as exceptional or explained, while utilisation is a balance-sheet movement. Ind AS 37.61 also restricts use of a provision to the expenditure for which it was originally recognised, so charging unrelated costs against an old provision is itself an error. Detect it by tying utilisation to cash or invoice evidence and reversals to a documented change in estimate, and by checking that the roll-forward has separate columns as required by 37.84.

Practice Questions

Q1. Manufacturing company year-end provisions pack [L3]

Sahyadri Auto Components Ltd (NSE-listed, FY ending 31 March 2026) presents the following matters for its provisions memo:

  1. A customer filed suit in October 2025 claiming ₹12 crore for defective brake assemblies. External counsel estimates a 65% chance of losing, with damages equally likely anywhere between ₹6 crore and ₹10 crore. The insurer has acknowledged cover in writing for ₹4 crore of any award, net of a deductible.
  2. Warranty: 4,20,000 units sold in FY26 with a 2-year warranty. Engineering expects 1.5% of units to need minor repair at ₹2,400 and 0.3% to need replacement at ₹9,500. Opening warranty provision ₹1.10 crore; claims paid in FY26 ₹0.82 crore relating to prior-year sales; the unused balance of ₹0.18 crore on FY24 sales expired.
  3. The Board approved closure of the Nashik plant on 20 March 2026. Workers and the union were informed on 15 April 2026. Estimated costs: severance ₹5.5 crore, retraining of transferred staff ₹0.6 crore, plant operating losses April–June ₹1.8 crore, lease exit penalty ₹0.9 crore.
  4. A GST show-cause notice of ₹3.2 crore (plus interest and penalty) is under appeal; the tax consultant rates the chance of an adverse outcome as possible but below 50%.
  5. An income-tax assessment disallowed ₹7 crore of expenditure; the company considers it probable that the disallowance will be upheld on 40% of the amount.

Required: (a) state for each item whether it is a provision, contingent liability, contingent asset or out of scope, with paragraph references; (b) compute the amounts to recognise or disclose; (c) prepare the warranty provision roll-forward; (d) draft the journal entries at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: Deal with the scope boundary for item 5 first, and check the communication date in item 3 against the reporting date.

Q2. Long-dated environmental provision with rate change [L2]

Ganga Chemicals Ltd must remediate a contaminated site; legislation enacted in FY25 obliges it to do so. On 1 April 2025 the best estimate of the outflow is ₹25 crore payable on 31 March 2030. The pre-tax risk-free rate is 7.2% and the cash flows are not risk-adjusted; the liability-specific premium is assessed at 0.8%. On 31 March 2026 the estimate is revised to ₹27 crore and the appropriate discount rate falls to 7.5%. Required: (a) the provision at 1 April 2025; (b) unwinding for FY26; (c) the remeasurement at 31 March 2026, separating the effect of the change in cash flows from the effect of the change in rate; (d) presentation of each component in the statement of profit and loss and in the Ind AS 37.84 roll-forward.

Q3. Onerous offtake contract and dedicated plant [L3]

Deccan Power Ltd has a fixed-price contract to supply 600 million units of power over the remaining 3 years at ₹4.10 per unit. Fuel costs have risen and the incremental cost to supply is now ₹4.40 per unit; allocated depreciation of the dedicated 120 MW plant is ₹0.35 per unit, and allocated corporate overheads ₹0.12 per unit. Exit compensation under the contract is ₹28 crore. The plant has a carrying amount of ₹190 crore and a recoverable amount of ₹165 crore, and has no use other than this contract. Required: (a) compute the unavoidable cost under both routes; (b) explain the order of accounting under Ind AS 37.69; (c) quantify the impairment and the onerous-contract provision; (d) explain how the answer would change if the company were contractually able to sell the power on the exchange at ₹4.60 per unit.

Q4. Contingent liability note review for a listed group [L2]

You are reviewing the draft contingent liability note of a mid-cap pharma company. It lists: (i) income-tax demands ₹46 crore under appeal; (ii) GST demands ₹18 crore; (iii) corporate guarantee of ₹120 crore given for a subsidiary's term loan (subsidiary outstanding ₹95 crore); (iv) a patent suit by a multinational claiming ₹300 crore, where disclosure of the company's own estimate would weaken its negotiating position; (v) bonus claims by a contractor's workers, assessed as remote. Required: (a) redraft the note in Schedule III format; (b) identify which items belong elsewhere and why; (c) explain the disclosure permitted for item (iv) under Ind AS 37.92; (d) explain how the guarantee is treated in standalone financial statements under Ind AS 109 versus Ind AS 37.

Q5. CSR, reimbursements and operating losses [L2]

Konkan Retail Ltd's CSR obligation for FY26 is ₹6.4 crore. It spent ₹3.1 crore, of which ₹2.2 crore relates to an ongoing 3-year school project that will need a further ₹2.8 crore; it has no other ongoing projects. The Board expects the loss-making Goa division to lose ₹4 crore in FY27 and wants a provision. A fire damaged inventory; a ₹1.5 crore insurance claim has been surveyed and the insurer's approval letter was received on 28 March 2026. Required: (a) the Sec 135(5)/135(6) transfer obligations and the accounting entries; (b) the Schedule III CSR disclosures; (c) your response to the Board on the Goa provision, including any Ind AS 36 implications; (d) the treatment of the insurance claim.
