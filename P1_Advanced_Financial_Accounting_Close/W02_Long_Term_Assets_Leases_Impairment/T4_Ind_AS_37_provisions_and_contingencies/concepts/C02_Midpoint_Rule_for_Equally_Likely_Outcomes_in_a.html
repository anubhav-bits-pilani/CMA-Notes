Key concept

Midpoint Rule for Equally Likely Outcomes in a Range

Concept: Equally likely outcomes in a continuous range → midpoint (37.39)

Statement

Where there is a continuous range of possible outcomes and each point in that range is as likely as any other, the provision is measured at the midpoint of the range (Ind AS 37.39):

\[ \text{Provision} = \frac{L + H}{2} \]

where \(L\) is the lowest and \(H\) the highest amount in the range of equally likely outcomes.

Explanation

The midpoint rule is a special case of the expected-value approach in 37.39. If outcomes are uniformly distributed between \(L\) and \(H\), the probability-weighted mean of the distribution is exactly the midpoint, so the rule is the expected value of a uniform distribution. It is therefore consistent with the best-estimate objective in 37.36.

Conditions:

  • The obligation has already passed recognition: a present obligation exists and an outflow is probable. The midpoint rule is a measurement tool, not a recognition test.
  • The range must be continuous and outcomes equally likely. If some amounts within the range are more likely than others — for example, counsel thinks ₹6–7 crore is the most likely band within a ₹4–10 crore range — the midpoint is not the best estimate; use the most likely outcome (single obligation, 37.40) or the probability-weighted mean (populations).
  • Discrete outcomes (win/lose, three settlement scenarios) are not a continuous range; weight them or apply 37.40.

Contrast with US GAAP: ASC 450-20 accrues the low end of a range when no amount within it is a better estimate, and discloses the reasonably possible additional exposure. An Indian subsidiary of a US group with a ₹4–10 crore range would provide ₹7 crore under Ind AS but report ₹4 crore in the US GAAP pack — a common GAAP-difference item.

Disclosure: Ind AS 37.85(b) requires an indication of the uncertainties about the amount or timing; when using the midpoint, disclosing the range is usually the clearest way to meet this. Where part of the range above the provision represents a possible further outflow, that excess may also need contingent-liability disclosure (37.88 linkage).

Discounting still applies: if the obligation will be settled in, say, three years, the midpoint of nominal outcomes is discounted under 37.45–47.

Illustration

A cement company is liable for compensation to farmers for crop damage from dust. An independent assessor concludes damages will fall anywhere between ₹2.4 crore and ₹4.0 crore, with no amount more likely than another; settlement is expected within 6 months (no discounting).

\[ \text{Provision} = \frac{2.4 + 4.0}{2} = \text{₹}3.2 \text{ crore} \]

Disclosure: "The provision represents the midpoint of the independent assessor's range of ₹2.4–4.0 crore."

If the assessor instead said ₹3.0–3.5 crore was most likely within the same overall range, the provision would move toward that band rather than ₹3.2 crore by formula.

Connections

Applied within s02 (measurement) alongside expected value and most-likely-outcome approaches; contrasts with the ASC 450 low-end rule noted in the topic's related concepts. The same "where outcomes are uniform, use the midpoint" logic appears in budgeting when ranges are given without distributions; in FP&A sensitivity work, show L, midpoint and H as downside, base and upside.

Practice Questions

Q1. Midpoint versus most likely [L1]

A logistics company faces a probable claim. Case A: damages equally likely anywhere between ₹80 lakh and ₹1.4 crore. Case B: the same range, but counsel believes ₹1.0–1.1 crore is twice as likely as any other band. Required: compute the provision in Case A and explain the measurement approach in Case B.

Q2. GAAP difference for a US parent [L2]

An Indian subsidiary of a US-listed group records a provision for a probable environmental fine, equally likely between ₹6 crore and ₹12 crore, payable in 2 years. The pre-tax discount rate is 7%. Required: compute the Ind AS provision, the likely US GAAP accrual (assume ASC 450 does not require discounting here), and the reconciling adjustment in the group reporting pack.

💡 Hint: Address both the range rule and discounting separately.

Q3. Range that straddles the probable threshold [L2]

Counsel states the company is 55% likely to be found liable and, if liable, damages would be equally likely between ₹5 crore and ₹9 crore. Required: determine the provision and draft the related disclosure, including any contingent-liability element.
