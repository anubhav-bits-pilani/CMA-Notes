Key concept

Contingent Assets: Virtually Certain to Recognise, Probable to Disclose

Concept: Contingent asset recognised only when inflow is virtually certain (37.33); disclosed when probable (37.89)

Statement

For a possible asset arising from past events whose existence will be confirmed only by uncertain future events not wholly within the entity's control:

Likelihood of inflow Accounting
Virtually certain Not a contingent asset — recognise the asset (37.33)
Probable (more likely than not) but not virtually certain Do not recognise; disclose as a contingent asset (37.34, 37.89)
Not probable Neither recognise nor disclose

Explanation

Ind AS 37.31 prohibits recognition of contingent assets because doing so could result in recognising income that may never be realised. Only when the realisation of income is virtually certain is the related asset no longer contingent, and its recognition is appropriate (37.33). "Virtually certain" is not defined numerically; in practice it means there is no realistic doubt — for example, a final, non-appealable court order with a solvent counterparty, or an insurer's unconditional written acceptance of a claim.

Disclosure for probable inflows (37.89) is a brief description of the nature of the contingent assets at the end of the reporting period and, where practicable, an estimate of their financial effect. Disclosures must avoid misleading indications of the likelihood of income arising (37.90). Contingent assets are assessed continually (37.35): when an inflow becomes virtually certain, the asset and income are recognised in the period of change; when it becomes probable, disclosure begins.

The asymmetry with liabilities is deliberate prudence: liabilities are provided when more likely than not; assets wait for virtual certainty. The same virtual-certainty threshold applies to reimbursements of provisions (37.53).

Common Indian examples:

  • refund claims under GST or customs where the appellate order is in favour but the department may appeal;
  • claims against insurers for fire or flood damage to own assets;
  • counter-claims in arbitrations (EPC, construction);
  • favourable Supreme Court rulings on industry-wide issues before the company's own case is disposed;
  • incentive claims under state industrial policies where sanction is pending.

Recognition timing for government incentives is governed by Ind AS 20 (reasonable assurance of compliance and receipt), not Ind AS 37; income-tax refunds are governed by Ind AS 12.

An event after the reporting period that makes the inflow virtually certain is generally a non-adjusting event if it creates the certainty (for example, a later settlement), but may be adjusting where it provides evidence of conditions that existed at the reporting date (for example, a final judgment on a claim pending at year-end) — analyse under Ind AS 10.

Illustration

A textile company's warehouse was damaged by flood in January 2026; inventory with cost ₹6.0 crore was written off. Position at three dates:

Date Status of insurance claim Treatment
31 March 2026 Claim lodged; surveyor appointed; no admission by insurer; counsel says recovery probable Disclose contingent asset (~₹5.5 crore, net of deductible)
30 June 2026 (Q1) Insurer issues written acceptance of ₹5.2 crore, payment due in 30 days Virtually certain: Dr Insurance claim receivable ₹5.2 crore, Cr Other income ₹5.2 crore
31 July 2026 Cash received Dr Bank, Cr Receivable

The inventory write-off is recognised in FY26 regardless; the gain is recognised only when virtually certain.

Connections

Paired with c01's probability ladder (liabilities) and with s07 on reimbursements (same virtual-certainty bar). Feeds the contingent-asset note in s06. In valuation and FP&A, probable contingent assets (refund claims, arbitration awards) are often shown as upside scenarios rather than in base forecasts; lenders rarely give them credit.

Practice Questions

Q1. Arbitration award under challenge [L2]

An EPC company obtained an arbitral award of ₹42 crore against a state utility in February 2026. The utility has filed a Sec 34 challenge under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 in the High Court; the company has received 75% of the award against a bank guarantee under the NITI Aayog/government scheme for such awards. Required: explain the treatment of the amount received and the balance at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: Separate the cash received (and the related obligation to refund) from the uncollected balance.

Q2. Customs refund after favourable order [L1]

A company has a customs refund claim of ₹3.8 crore. The Commissioner (Appeals) ruled in its favour in March 2026; the department's time to appeal expires in June 2026, and departmental appeals are common on this issue. Required: classify at 31 March 2026 and state the disclosure.

Q3. Asymmetry in a case study [L2]

A pharma company is suing a former distributor for ₹20 crore of unpaid dues and has been counter-sued for ₹12 crore. Counsel rates both the company's claim succeeding and the counter-claim succeeding at 60%. Required: explain the recognition and disclosure for each side and why netting is not permitted.
