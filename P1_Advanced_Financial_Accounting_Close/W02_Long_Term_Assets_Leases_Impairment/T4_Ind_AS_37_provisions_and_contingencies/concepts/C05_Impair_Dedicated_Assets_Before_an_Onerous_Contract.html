Key concept

Impair Dedicated Assets Before an Onerous Contract Provision

Concept: Recognise impairment on dedicated assets before an onerous contract provision (37.69)

Statement

Before a separate provision for an onerous contract is established, an entity recognises any impairment loss that has occurred on assets dedicated to that contract, in accordance with Ind AS 36 (Ind AS 37.69). The onerous provision is then:

\[ \text{Provision} = \max\left(0,\ \text{UC}^{*} - B\right) \]

where \(\text{UC}^{*}\) is the unavoidable cost of meeting the contract after the impairment (so that depreciation or amortisation of the written-down dedicated assets reflects the new carrying amounts), and \(B\) is the economic benefits expected under the contract.

Explanation

The cost to fulfil a contract includes an allocation of directly related costs such as depreciation of plant used to fulfil it (37.68A). If the dedicated plant is overstated, its depreciation inflates the cost to fulfil and the onerous provision; if the plant is then also impaired, the same loss is recognised twice. Testing the asset first removes that double count and places the loss where it belongs: as a reduction of the asset's carrying amount, which is subsequently reversed only under Ind AS 36 rules.

Practical sequence:

  1. Identify dedicated assets. PPE, ROU assets, intangibles, capitalised contract costs (Ind AS 115.91–104) and contract assets whose cash flows depend on the contract. Capitalised contract costs are tested under Ind AS 115.101 before Ind AS 36 assets.
  2. Determine the CGU. If an asset generates cash flows only from the contract, the contract may be its own CGU; if shared with other contracts, the CGU is larger and the test is less likely to produce an impairment at contract level.
  3. Apply Ind AS 36. Recoverable amount = higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use. VIU uses the contract cash flows (revenue less costs excluding depreciation).
  4. Recompute the unavoidable cost with depreciation on the post-impairment carrying amounts.
  5. Compare with exit cost — the lower of net cost to fulfil and penalty remains the unavoidable cost.
  6. Recognise the residual provision, if any.

If an exit route via penalty is cheaper and will be taken, the dedicated asset's VIU reflects that (no contract cash flows), and the impairment and provision are measured on the exit basis.

Illustration

A contract manufacturer has a 2-year supply contract with remaining revenue of ₹60 crore. A dedicated moulding line has a carrying amount of ₹14 crore, remaining life 2 years, nil residual value and no other use (FVLCD ₹1 crore). Other costs to fulfil (materials, labour, power, supervision) are ₹58 crore. Exit penalty ₹9 crore. Ignore discounting.

Step 1 — impairment of the line: VIU = 60 − 58 = ₹2 crore; FVLCD = ₹1 crore; recoverable amount ₹2 crore; impairment = 14 − 2 = ₹12 crore.

Step 2 — unavoidable cost after impairment: cost to fulfil = 58 + depreciation 2 = ₹60 crore; net cost to fulfil = 60 − 60 = 0. Exit route net cost = 9 (less any benefit of releasing the line at FVLCD 1, say ₹8 crore). Unavoidable cost = fulfil route, net nil.

Provision = ₹0. Total loss = ₹12 crore, all as impairment.

Had the provision been computed first using the old depreciation of ₹14 crore: cost to fulfil = 72, net = 12, provision ₹12 crore — and an impairment test afterwards would add another ₹12 crore, doubling the loss.

Practice Questions

Q1. Sequence and amounts [L2]

A solar EPC company has a fixed-price O&M contract for 3 years with remaining revenue ₹18 crore. Dedicated cleaning robots have a carrying amount of ₹5 crore (3-year remaining life, nil residual, FVLCD ₹0.5 crore). Other costs to fulfil ₹19 crore. Exit penalty ₹4 crore. Ignore discounting. Required: compute the impairment and the onerous provision in the correct order and show the entries.

💡 Hint: Compute VIU from contract cash flows before depreciation, and remember VIU cannot drive recoverable amount below FVLCD.

Q2. Shared assets [L2]

Explain how the answer to Q1 changes if the robots are also used on five other profitable O&M contracts and are part of a regional CGU with ample headroom.

Q3. Reviewer's challenge [L1]

A draft provisions memo recognises an onerous provision of ₹9 crore computed with full depreciation of a dedicated plant, and separately notes that the plant "may be impaired; to be tested next quarter". As reviewer, explain the error and the corrected approach.
