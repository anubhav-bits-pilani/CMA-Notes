Key concept

Warranty Provision: Volume × Defect Rate × Repair Cost

Concept: Warranty provision = Σ (units sold × expected defect rate × average repair cost), trued up to claims experience

Statement

For assurance-type warranties (those that assure the product meets agreed specifications and are not a distinct service under Ind AS 115.B29), the provision for products sold and still under warranty is:

\[ W = \sum_{c} \sum_{v} N_{c,v} \times d_{c,v} \times C_{c,v} \times \rho_{c,v} \]

where for each product category \(c\) and sales vintage (period of sale) \(v\): \(N\) is units sold; \(d\) is the expected defect (claim) rate over the warranty period; \(C\) is the expected average cost per claim (parts, labour, logistics, net of supplier recoveries only if virtually certain — otherwise separate asset); and \(\rho\) is the proportion of expected claims not yet incurred at the reporting date (unexpired portion). Closing provision is trued up so that:

\[ W_{\text{close}} = W_{\text{open}} + \text{Charge} - \text{Claims utilised} - \text{Releases} + \text{Unwinding} \]

Explanation

Warranties are the textbook large-population obligation: the obligating event is the sale; the class of obligations makes an outflow probable even if each unit is unlikely to fail (37.24); and the expected-value approach of 37.39 applies. Warranty costs are expensed in the period of sale (matching), within cost of sales or other expenses per policy.

Building the inputs:

  • Defect rate from claims history by model, adjusted for known quality issues, new launches with no history (use engineering estimates or comparable models), and changes in warranty terms.
  • Cost per claim from the service ledger, including dealer labour rates, parts at cost, freight; escalate for inflation if claims extend beyond a year.
  • Claim emergence pattern (\(\rho\)): failures cluster early and near expiry. Use a run-off triangle of claims by vintage to estimate the unexpired portion rather than straight-line.
  • Discounting: usually immaterial for 1–2 year warranties; required for long-tail (5-year powertrain warranties) if material (37.45).
  • True-up: compare each vintage's actual claims to expectations; revise rates prospectively (change in estimate, Ind AS 8). Persistent over-provision released in bulk is a reviewer red flag.

Service-type warranties (extended warranties sold separately or providing a service beyond assurance) are separate performance obligations under Ind AS 115 — revenue is deferred, not a provision. Supplier recoveries under back-to-back warranties are separate assets only if virtually certain (37.53).

An air-conditioner maker sells with a 2-year warranty. FY26 data:

The full expected cost of ₹5.41 crore is charged in FY26. Claims history shows 35% of two-year claims arise in the year of sale (sales spread evenly), so FY26-vintage claims paid were about ₹1.89 crore and the unexpired FY26-vintage provision is 5.41 × 65% = ₹3.52 crore. The opening provision of ₹2.80 crore related entirely to the FY25 vintage; FY26 claims on that vintage were ₹1.85 crore, leaving ₹0.95 crore carried against residual expected claims of only ₹0.62 crore, so ₹0.33 crore is released.

Connections

Applies the expected-value method in s02 and the recognition logic in s01; the roll-forward structure is the Excel provision roll-forward listed under related concepts. In FP&A, warranty cost is forecast as a percentage of revenue or per unit by model and is a key metric in auto and consumer-durables earnings calls. Deferred tax: following the Supreme Court in Rotork Controls India (2009), a warranty provision made on a scientific, history-based method is generally deductible when made (ICDS X also applies its own "reasonable certainty" recognition test), so the book–tax temporary difference may be small. Unscientific or general provisions are disallowed until claims are paid and create a DTA — covered in Week 3.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute a warranty provision [L1]

A smartphone brand sold 9,00,000 units in Q4 FY26 with a 1-year warranty. Expected claim rate 7%, average cost ₹950; historically 20% of claims for a quarter's sales are incurred within that quarter. Required: compute the provision at 31 March 2026 for Q4 sales and the entry.

Q2. True-up and release [L2]

At 31 March 2025 a provision of ₹6.0 crore was carried for FY25 sales (2-year warranty). FY26 claims on FY25 sales were ₹3.4 crore; remaining expected claims for FY25 sales are ₹1.1 crore. FY26 sales require a provision of ₹7.2 crore (unexpired), and FY26 claims on FY26 sales were ₹2.0 crore. Required: prepare the roll-forward by vintage, identify utilisation and release separately, and comment on what a large release implies about the estimation process.

💡 Hint: Keep each vintage in its own row so that utilisation and release are not netted.

Q3. Model build: warranty schedule [L3]

Design an Excel warranty model for a two-wheeler company with 4 models, 3-year warranty, quarterly sales and a claims emergence curve by quarter since sale. Specify inputs, the run-off triangle, formulas for the unexpired provision, the roll-forward, and checks against the GL.
