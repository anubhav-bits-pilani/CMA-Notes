Subtopic note

Lease Term: Non-Cancellable Period, Options and Indian Lock-Ins

Scope: Lease term: non-cancellable period + reasonably certain extension/termination options; lock-ins in Indian retail/office leases

Why it matters

Lease term is the single biggest driver of the lease liability and ROU asset: a 9-year store lease measured over 3 years produces roughly a third of the liability. Indian leases are structured as leave-and-licence agreements or registered lease deeds with lock-in periods, mutual or one-sided termination rights and renewal clauses "on mutually agreed terms" — each requiring judgement. Lease term also drives depreciation of leasehold improvements, impairment testing and deferred tax. Regulators and auditors focus on whether term judgements are consistent with business plans and with the lives used for fit-outs.

Core notes

Definition

The lease term is the non-cancellable period for which a lessee has the right to use an underlying asset, together with (Ind AS 116.18):

  • (a) periods covered by an option to extend the lease if the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise that option; and
  • (b) periods covered by an option to terminate the lease if the lessee is reasonably certain not to exercise that option.

The lease term begins at the commencement date (when the lessor makes the asset available for use) and includes any rent-free periods (B36).

Enforceability (B34–B35)

A lease is no longer enforceable when both the lessee and the lessor each have the right to terminate without permission from the other with no more than an insignificant penalty. If only the lessee has a termination right, it is an option available to the lessee considered in the lease term; if only the lessor has the right, the non-cancellable period includes the period covered by that option.

"Penalty" is interpreted broadly — it includes economic penalties such as the loss of significant non-removable leasehold improvements or relocation costs, not only contractual termination payments (consistent with the IFRS Interpretations Committee agenda decision of November 2019, which Indian practice follows). So a lease where both parties can cancel with 3 months' notice may still have an enforceable period beyond the notice period if the lessee would suffer a more than insignificant economic penalty.

"Reasonably certain" assessment (B37–B40)

Consider all relevant facts and circumstances that create an economic incentive for the lessee to exercise or not exercise the option:

  • contractual terms for optional periods compared with market rates (bargain renewal rent);
  • significant leasehold improvements expected to have significant economic benefit when the option becomes exercisable;
  • costs relating to termination — negotiation, relocation, identifying another asset, integrating it, termination penalties, restoration costs;
  • importance of the underlying asset to operations (specialised asset, flagship store, location critical to a hub);
  • conditionality of the option;
  • past practice of the entity regarding the period it typically uses particular types of assets (B40).

The shorter the non-cancellable period, the more likely a lessee will exercise an extension option (B39).

"Reasonably certain" is a high threshold — higher than "more likely than not", lower than "virtually certain".

Reassessment (Ind AS 116.20–21)

The lessee reassesses whether it is reasonably certain to exercise (or not exercise) an option upon the occurrence of a significant event or significant change in circumstances that (a) is within the control of the lessee and (b) affects reasonable certainty. Examples (B41): significant leasehold improvements not anticipated at commencement, significant modification or customisation of the asset, a business decision directly relevant (e.g. decision to extend use of a complementary asset or to dispose of an alternative asset), subleasing beyond the end of the previously determined term.

The lessee revises the lease term if there is a change in the non-cancellable period (e.g. the lessee actually exercises an option not previously included, or a contractual obligation prohibits termination) (Ind AS 116.21).

A change in lease term triggers remeasurement of the liability at a revised discount rate with the adjustment against ROU (Ind AS 116.40, 116.39) — see s07 and c05. Market changes (e.g. a fall in market rent) alone are not lessee-controlled events and do not trigger reassessment.

Worked illustration

Urban Threads Ltd signs a 9-year leave-and-licence for a flagship store on 1 April 2025: lock-in 36 months for both parties; after lock-in, the licensee may terminate with 3 months' notice and no penalty; the licensor may terminate only for breach. Rent ₹8 lakh/month in advance, escalating 15% every 36 months. It spends ₹2.5 crore on non-removable fit-outs (economic life 10 years). Store EBITDA margin projections are strong, and the company's historical practice is to keep flagship stores for 9+ years. IBR 9.5%.

Analysis: non-cancellable period = 36 months. Months 37–108 are covered by a lessee termination option; given significant fit-outs that would be lost, relocation costs and past practice, Urban Threads is reasonably certain not to terminate. Lease term = 108 months. Fit-outs are depreciated over 9 years (shorter of life and lease term).

If the term were set at 36 months, the liability would be roughly ₹2.53 crore instead of ₹6.69 crore (PVs at the monthly equivalent of 9.5%) — a material difference in assets, liabilities, EBITDA and depreciation pattern.

Documentation

Prepare a lease-term memo for each lease or portfolio: contractual terms, options, economic incentives (fit-out value, store performance, strategic importance), past practice, conclusion. Revisit at each reporting date for lessee-controlled triggering events.

Excel implementation

Input fields per lease: contractual term, lock-in months, lessee termination option (Y/N, earliest date), extension options (periods, rent), judgement flag "include optional periods (Y/N)". Computed term:

=IF(IncludeOptions="Y", ContractTerm+ReasonablyCertainExtensions, LockInMonths)

The payment engine uses =IF(PeriodNo<=LeaseTermMonths, Payment, 0) so changing the judgement flag updates the whole schedule.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Renewal "at mutually agreed terms" is not an option of the lessee; include only if enforceability analysis shows both parties have more than insignificant penalties to not renew.
  • Lessor-only termination rights do not shorten the term.
  • Cancellable leases with significant fit-outs — the economic penalty can extend the enforceable period beyond the notice period.
  • Holding over after expiry: treat as a new lease or extension from the date of agreement; month-to-month tenancy may be short-term.
  • Store closure plans approved by the Board are a lessee-controlled event triggering reassessment of the term (and impairment of ROU).
  • Consistency with leasehold improvement lives, impairment plans and business plans is a key review point.
  • Purchase options are assessed with the same reasonably certain threshold; if included, ROU is depreciated over the asset's useful life (Ind AS 116.32).

Key takeaways

  • Lease term = non-cancellable period + reasonably certain extension periods + periods of termination options reasonably certain not to be exercised.
  • Enforceability considers economic penalties, not only contractual penalties.
  • Lock-ins define the minimum non-cancellable period; periods beyond are judgements on economic incentive.
  • Reassess only on significant lessee-controlled events; changes are remeasured at a revised rate.
  • Document term judgements and align them with fit-out lives and business plans.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mall lease with mutual lock-in [L2]

A cinema operator signs a 15-year lease from 1 April 2025 with a mutual lock-in of 5 years; thereafter the operator alone may terminate on 6 months' notice. It installs screens and acoustics costing ₹18 crore with a 12-year life. The operator's history shows 80% of screens run for the full lease term. Determine the lease term, the depreciation period for fit-outs, and the evidence you would document.

Q2. Short leave-and-licence renewals [L2]

A bank branch occupies premises under successive 11-month leave-and-licence agreements since 2019; either party may terminate with one month's notice. The bank has invested ₹60 lakh in strong-room construction. Analyse whether the short-term exemption applies and what the lease term should be.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the bank faces a more than insignificant economic penalty from not continuing, even though both parties have termination rights.

Q3. Reassessment trigger [L2]

Two years into a 9-year office lease (term originally assessed as 3 years, the lock-in period), the lessee spends ₹4 crore on unanticipated fit-outs and announces the office as its regional headquarters. Explain whether reassessment is required, what the new term would likely be, and the accounting mechanics, with references.

Q4. Effect of term judgement on metrics [L3]

For a retailer with 200 stores each paying ₹5 lakh per month in advance (no escalation), IBR 9.5% effective, compare the total lease liability and the first-year P&L split (depreciation, interest) between a 3-year and a 9-year lease term. Explain the effect on EBITDA, PBT and net debt and how analysts might react.

Q5. Land lease with premium [L1]

A company obtains 95-year leasehold land from a state industrial development corporation for an upfront premium of ₹12 crore plus annual rent of ₹1 lakh. State the lease term, the measurement of the ROU and lease liability, and the depreciation period.
