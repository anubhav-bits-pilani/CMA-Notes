Subtopic note

Building the Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR)

Scope: IBR build-up: reference rate (G-sec/benchmark) + entity credit spread + lease-specific adjustments (term, security, currency); portfolio approach

Why it matters

Lessees almost never know the rate implicit in the lease, because it depends on the lessor's unguaranteed residual value and initial direct costs. So nearly every Indian lessee discounts at its IBR. A 100-basis-point change in IBR on a 10-year lease shifts the liability by roughly 4–5% and moves the interest/depreciation split. IBR methodology is a recurring audit area and a key judgement disclosed by retailers, airlines and telecom companies. FP&A teams need a defensible, repeatable IBR curve for new leases, budgeting and modification remeasurements.

Core notes

Definition

The incremental borrowing rate is the rate of interest that a lessee would have to pay to borrow over a similar term, and with a similar security, the funds necessary to obtain an asset of a similar value to the right-of-use asset in a similar economic environment (Ind AS 116 Appendix A).

The lessee uses the interest rate implicit in the lease if it can be readily determined; otherwise the IBR (Ind AS 116.26). The implicit rate is the rate that equates PV of lease payments plus unguaranteed residual value to the fair value of the asset plus lessor's initial direct costs — typically determinable only for vehicle, equipment and aircraft leases where lessors disclose pricing.

The IBR is lessee-specific and lease-specific: it reflects what this lessee would pay, for this term, for this kind of collateral (the ROU), in this currency.

Build-up approach

\[ IBR = R_{\text{ref}} + S_{\text{credit}} + A_{\text{lease}} \]

  1. Reference rate (\( R_{\text{ref}} \)) — a risk-free or benchmark rate in the lease currency for a tenor matching the lease's weighted average life of payments (or term): Indian G-sec yields from FBIL/CCIL par curves for INR; SOFR/US Treasury for USD; or a benchmark such as T-bill/MIBOR-OIS for shorter tenors.
  2. Entity credit spread (\( S_{\text{credit}} \)) — the spread the lessee would pay over the reference rate on unsecured borrowing, derived from: - the entity's own recent borrowings (term loans, NCDs) — e.g. a 5-year NCD issued at G-sec + 180 bp; - credit rating-based spreads (CRISIL/ICRA/CARE rating) from corporate bond yields of similar rating and tenor (FIMMDA/FBIL corporate bond matrices); - bank sanction letters (MCLR/EBLR-linked pricing converted to fixed equivalents); - for subsidiaries, consider whether the parent would provide funding or guarantee — use the subsidiary's own standalone credit with any implicit support (rating agencies' notching).
  3. Lease-specific adjustments (\( A_{\text{lease}} \)): - term — adjust for tenor if the credit spread curve differs by maturity; - security — a borrowing secured on the ROU (or the underlying asset) is cheaper than unsecured; property ROU is weaker collateral than an aircraft; adjust downward for strong security; - currency — USD-denominated aircraft leases use a USD IBR; INR leases use INR rates; - asset value / amount — larger amounts may attract different pricing; - economic environment — jurisdiction of the lessee entity.

Effective vs nominal rate and periodicity

Express the IBR as an effective annual rate and convert to periodic rates consistently: \( r_m = (1+IBR)^{1/12}-1 \). G-sec yields are quoted as semi-annual yields; convert to effective annual: \( (1+y/2)^2 - 1 \). Bank loan rates quoted p.a. with monthly compounding are nominal; convert to effective. Inconsistency here is a common pitfall.

Portfolio approach

Ind AS 116.B1 allows a lessee to apply the standard to a portfolio of leases with similar characteristics if it reasonably expects the effect not to differ materially from applying it to individual leases. For IBR, entities build a rate matrix by tenor bucket (e.g. 1–3, 3–5, 5–7, 7–10, 10–15 years), currency and asset type, and apply the bucket rate to every lease commencing in a period (e.g. a quarter). Update the matrix each quarter for new leases and for remeasurements needing revised rates.

Worked illustration

Southern Spice Ltd (rated CRISIL AA−), INR leases, quarter ended 30 June 2025.

A 9-year store lease commencing in the quarter (weighted average life of payments ~5 years given escalations) may use the 7-year bucket or interpolate; the policy should specify term vs weighted-average-life matching. The monthly rate for the 10-year bucket = \( 1.08^{1/12} - 1 = 0.6434\% \).

When to use revised rates

The IBR is determined at commencement and not updated for market movements. A revised discount rate (IBR at the reassessment/modification date) is used when (Ind AS 116.40, 116.45):

  • the lease term changes;
  • the assessment of a purchase option changes;
  • a modification is not accounted for as a separate lease;
  • floating interest rate changes affect payments (revised rate reflecting the change, Ind AS 116.43).

See s07 and c05. The rate matrix therefore must be retained historically and updated quarterly.

Disclosure and documentation

Disclose the weighted average IBR applied to lease liabilities at the date of initial application (on transition) and the policy; many Indian companies disclose ranges (e.g. 8%–10.5%). Document data sources (FBIL yields, rating reports, recent NCD pricing), the matrix, and approval by the CFO/audit committee.

Excel implementation

=INDEX(IBR_Matrix, MATCH(TenorYears, TenorBuckets, 1), MATCH(Currency, Currencies, 0))
=(1+IBR)^(1/12)-1
=FORECAST.LINEAR(TenorYears, {7.24%,7.75%,8.00%}, {3,7,10})   ' interpolation option

Lock the IBR per lease as a value at commencement (paste the rate into the input table via a macro or store it in a "rate at commencement" column) so later matrix updates do not alter historic leases.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Subsidiary with no borrowings: use parent's credit adjusted for subsidiary standalone credit quality and any guarantee; document notching.
  • Very long leases (30–99 years) — G-sec curve extends to 40 years; beyond that, extrapolate flat and consider liquidity.
  • Foreign currency leases in an INR-functional entity — use a currency-specific IBR (e.g. USD SOFR-based + entity's USD spread); liability is a monetary item retranslated under Ind AS 21.
  • Lessee rating changes after commencement do not change the IBR unless a revised-rate trigger occurs.
  • Implicit rate readily determinable (vehicle leases with disclosed pricing) — must be used instead of IBR.
  • Security deposit benefit is accounted separately (s06), not by lowering the IBR.
  • Group treasury policy — centrally issued quarterly IBR curves by tenor/currency ensure consistency across subsidiaries.

Key takeaways

  • Use the implicit rate if readily determinable, otherwise IBR — the norm for lessees.
  • IBR = reference rate for tenor and currency + entity credit spread + lease-specific adjustments (term, security, currency).
  • Express rates as effective annual and convert to periodic rates consistently.
  • A portfolio rate matrix by tenor bucket and currency is acceptable if differences are immaterial.
  • The rate is locked at commencement and revised only for term/purchase option changes, non-separate modifications and floating-rate changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Construct an IBR [L2]

A BBB+ rated logistics company in India signs a 7-year warehouse lease on 1 October 2025. Data: 7-year G-sec par yield 6.30% (semi-annual); BBB+ corporate spread over G-sec for 7 years 2.60%; the company's 5-year secured term loan last year at 9.6% (effective); security adjustment for ROU −0.20%. Construct the IBR (effective annual), compute the monthly rate, and explain how you would reconcile it with the term-loan evidence.

Q2. Portfolio matrix design [L2]

Design an IBR matrix for a retail group with 600 leases across three entities (parent rated AA, two unrated subsidiaries), INR only, with terms from 1 to 15 years. Specify the buckets, data sources, update frequency, governance and how each lease is assigned a rate.

💡 Hint: Decide whether to match on lease term or weighted average life of payments and apply the choice consistently.

Q3. Sensitivity [L2]

A 10-year lease has annual payments of ₹1 crore in arrears. Compute the lease liability at IBRs of 8%, 9% and 10%, and the first-year interest and depreciation at each rate. Explain the effect of a higher IBR on EBITDA, EBIT and PBT in year 1.

Q4. USD aircraft lease [L3]

An Indian airline (INR functional currency) leases an A320neo for 12 years with USD rentals. Its INR IBR is 9.5%; its recent USD-denominated ECB was priced at SOFR + 250 bp (SOFR 4.3%). Determine the appropriate discount rate, explain why using the INR rate would be wrong, and describe the subsequent foreign exchange accounting for the liability and ROU.

Q5. Interview question: implicit rate vs IBR [L1]

Explain in 5 sentences why lessees rarely use the rate implicit in the lease, and give one situation in India where the implicit rate may be readily determinable.
