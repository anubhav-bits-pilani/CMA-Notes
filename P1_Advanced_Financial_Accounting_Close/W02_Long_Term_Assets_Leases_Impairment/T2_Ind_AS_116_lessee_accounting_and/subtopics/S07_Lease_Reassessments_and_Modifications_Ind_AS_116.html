Subtopic note

Lease Reassessments and Modifications (Ind AS 116.39–46)

Scope: Reassessment (116.39–43) and modifications (116.44–46): scope increase, term extension, partial/full early termination, rent reduction

Why it matters

Leases change constantly: retailers close underperforming stores, renegotiate rents, surrender part of a floor, extend flagship leases; offices add floors; index-linked rents reset. Each change must be classified correctly — reassessment under the original contract, a modification accounted for as a separate lease, or a modification that remeasures the existing lease — because the classification decides the discount rate, whether a gain or loss hits P&L and how ROU is adjusted. The week's exercise includes a Year 3 rent change with term extension and an early termination; this note is the rulebook.

Core notes

Two categories of change

Category What it is Source
Reassessment / remeasurement Changes in cash flows or judgements under the original terms — lease term, purchase option, RVG amounts, index/rate changes Ind AS 116.39–43
Lease modification A change in the scope of a lease, or the consideration, that was not part of the original terms and conditions Ind AS 116 Appendix A, 116.44–46

Reassessment (Ind AS 116.39–43)

After commencement, the lessee remeasures the lease liability to reflect changes in lease payments and recognises the amount of remeasurement as an adjustment to the ROU asset; if the ROU is reduced to zero, any further reduction goes to P&L (Ind AS 116.39).

Revised discount rate (Ind AS 116.40–41) — remeasure using the revised rate (implicit rate for the remainder of the term if determinable, otherwise IBR at the reassessment date) when:

  • (a) there is a change in the lease term (s03 triggers, Ind AS 116.20–21);
  • (b) there is a change in the assessment of an option to purchase the underlying asset.

Unchanged discount rate (Ind AS 116.42–43) — remeasure using the original rate when:

  • (a) there is a change in the amounts expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee;
  • (b) there is a change in future payments resulting from a change in an index or rate (e.g. CPI reset, market rent review) — only when the change in cash flows takes effect. Exception: if the change results from a change in floating interest rates, use a revised rate reflecting that change (Ind AS 116.43).

Modifications (Ind AS 116.44–46)

Step 1 — Separate lease? A modification is accounted for as a separate lease if both (Ind AS 116.44):

  • it increases the scope by adding the right to use one or more underlying assets; and
  • the consideration increases by an amount commensurate with the stand-alone price for the increase in scope, adjusted for the particular contract's circumstances (c07).

A separate lease is accounted for from its own commencement date with its own IBR; the original lease continues unchanged.

Step 2 — Not a separate lease. At the effective date of the modification, the lessee (Ind AS 116.45):

  • allocates the consideration in the modified contract to components;
  • determines the lease term of the modified lease;
  • remeasures the lease liability by discounting the revised lease payments using a revised discount rate (IBR at the effective date, unless implicit rate determinable).

Then (Ind AS 116.46):

  • (a) for modifications that decrease the scope (partial or full termination, reduction in space or term), decrease the carrying amount of the ROU to reflect the partial or full termination and recognise in P&L any gain or loss relating to it;
  • (b) for all other modifications (scope increase not qualifying as separate, term extension, rent change), make a corresponding adjustment to the ROU asset.

Modification types and treatment

Type Example Treatment
Scope increase at stand-alone price Additional floor at market rent Separate lease (116.44)
Scope increase at discount Additional floor at below-market rent Remeasure existing lease at revised rate; adjust ROU (116.46(b))
Term extension (negotiated, not original option) Extend 5-year lease by 3 years Remeasure at revised rate; adjust ROU (116.46(b))
Partial termination Surrender 40% of space Reduce ROU proportionately; gain/loss in P&L; remeasure remainder at revised rate against ROU (116.46(a))
Full early termination (by agreement) Exit after 3 of 9 years Derecognise ROU and liability; gain/loss in P&L incl. termination penalty
Rent reduction only Rent cut from ₹100 to ₹80 Remeasure at revised rate; adjust ROU (116.46(b)) — not a P&L gain
Term reduction 10-year term cut to 7 years Scope decrease — 116.46(a)

Exercising a termination option that was already within the original terms (and anticipated in the term judgement) is not a modification; exercising one previously assumed not to be exercised is a reassessment of lease term (revised rate). Termination by agreement outside original terms is a modification.

Worked illustration

Office lease from 1 April 2023: 10 years, ₹100 lakh per year in arrears, IBR 9%. Initial liability = ROU = 100 × annuity factor (9%, 10) = ₹641.77 lakh. Depreciation ₹64.18 lakh per year. At 1 April 2026 (7 years remaining): liability ₹503.30 lakh; ROU ₹449.24 lakh. Revised IBR at that date 10%; annuity factor (10%, 7) = 4.8684.

A — Rent reduction to ₹80 lakh for the remaining 7 years: revised liability = 80 × 4.8684 = ₹389.47 lakh. Reduction ₹113.82 lakh adjusted against ROU (new ROU ₹335.41 lakh). No P&L gain.

Other points

  • Ind AS 116.46(a) does not prescribe how to measure the proportionate decrease; common approaches are based on remaining space (floor area) or remaining term. Choose consistently.
  • Modifications that both decrease scope and change consideration: apply 116.46(a) for the decrease, then 116.46(b) for the rest.
  • COVID-19 rent concessions practical expedient (Ind AS 116.46A–46B, payments originally due on or before 30 June 2022) allowed concessions to be treated as variable payments rather than modifications — now largely historical, but explains FY21–FY23 disclosures.
  • Disclosures: gains/losses from modifications, additions to ROU, and the maturity analysis must reflect changes.

Excel implementation

Add to the inputs: modification date, type (rent change / extension / partial termination / full termination), revised rent, revised term, % scope retained, revised IBR, penalty. In the schedule, at the modification period:

=IF(Period=ModPeriod, RevisedPV - OpeningLiability*ScopeRetained, 0)   ' remeasurement
=IF(Period=ModPeriod, (1-ScopeRetained)*(OpeningLiability-OpeningROU), 0)   ' gain/loss

After the modification, switch the periodic rate and payment vector with =IF(Period>=ModPeriod, RevisedRate, OriginalRate).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Modification effective date vs agreement date — measure at the date both parties agree (effective date), not the date the change takes effect if earlier agreement exists.
  • Lump-sum incentive for extension — reduces revised lease payments.
  • Rent-free period granted in exchange for extension — single modification; remeasure total.
  • Surrender of a store with a new lease elsewhere from the same landlord — assess whether contracts are combined.
  • ROU reduced to zero on a large rent reduction (reassessment) — excess to P&L (Ind AS 116.39).
  • Impairment — a decision to close stores is also an impairment indicator for the ROU before the modification is agreed.
  • Deferred tax on gains and ROU adjustments follows the gross temporary difference approach.

Key takeaways

  • Reassessments arise under original terms; modifications change scope or consideration outside original terms.
  • Lease term and purchase option changes use a revised rate; RVG and index changes use the unchanged rate (floating-rate exception).
  • A modification adding an asset at a commensurate stand-alone price is a separate lease.
  • Scope decreases reduce ROU proportionately with gain/loss in P&L, then remeasure the remainder at a revised rate.
  • Other modifications (extensions, rent changes) are remeasured at a revised rate with the adjustment to ROU, no P&L impact.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the changes [L1]

Classify each as reassessment (revised or unchanged rate), separate lease or modification (and which paragraph): (a) CPI-linked rent increases 5.2%; (b) lessee exercises a renewal option previously judged not reasonably certain; (c) lessee leases an additional floor at market rent; (d) landlord agrees to cut rent by 20% for the remaining term; (e) lessee and landlord agree to end the lease 2 years early with a penalty.

Q2. Partial termination with rent change [L2]

A 12-year warehouse lease (₹60 lakh per year in arrears, IBR 9.5%) is modified at the end of year 4: the lessee gives up 25% of the space and rent for the remaining space falls to ₹42 lakh per year; remaining term unchanged; revised IBR 10.25%. Compute the gain or loss, the remeasurement and the closing ROU, and pass entries.

💡 Hint: First apply the proportionate decrease to both ROU and liability, then remeasure the remaining liability with the revised payments and rate.

Q3. Extension plus additional space [L3]

A company leases 2 floors for 8 years (₹40 lakh per floor per year, arrears, IBR 9%). After 3 years, it agrees to (i) add a third floor for the remaining 5 years at ₹38 lakh per year (market rent ₹45 lakh) and (ii) extend the whole lease by 2 years at unchanged rents; revised IBR 9.75%. Analyse whether any part is a separate lease and compute the accounting.

Q4. Early termination in the exercise [L2]

A store lease (liability ₹2.3 crore, ROU ₹2.0 crore, deposit carrying amount ₹28 lakh with face value ₹36 lakh) is terminated by agreement on 31 March 2028 with a penalty of ₹15 lakh and immediate refund of the deposit. Pass all entries and compute the net P&L effect.

Q5. Model design for modifications [L3]

Specify the inputs, switches and formulas needed in a monthly multi-lease Excel model to handle, for any lease, a rent change, a term extension and a partial termination on a user-selected date, and list four integrity checks that confirm the modification has been processed correctly.
