Subtopic note

Separating Lease and Non-Lease Components (Ind AS 116.12–16)

Scope: Separating lease and non-lease components (CAM, maintenance) and the practical expedient (116.15)

Why it matters

Indian commercial leases rarely charge only for space. Mall and office rent agreements bundle common area maintenance (CAM), housekeeping, security, utilities, parking and signage; equipment and vehicle leases bundle maintenance; aircraft wet leases bundle crew. Whether these charges are part of the lease liability changes the liability's size, EBITDA (non-lease services stay in operating expenses) and cash flow classification. The practical expedient simplifies the model but inflates ROU and liability. Reviewers check that components are separated consistently by class of underlying asset and that allocation uses stand-alone prices.

Core notes

Identifying components

For a contract that is, or contains, a lease, the entity accounts for each lease component separately from non-lease components (Ind AS 116.12), unless the practical expedient applies. The right to use an underlying asset is a separate lease component if (Ind AS 116.B32):

  • the lessee can benefit from use of the asset either on its own or together with other readily available resources; and
  • the asset is neither highly dependent on, nor highly interrelated with, other underlying assets in the contract.

So a lease of a store and an adjacent parking area may be two lease components; a lease of land and building is generally two components (with land classification considered by lessors).

What is a non-lease component?

A non-lease component transfers a good or service to the lessee separately from the right to use the asset. Examples:

  • CAM covering maintenance of common areas, housekeeping, security, lifts, HVAC operation;
  • maintenance services for leased equipment, vehicles or aircraft;
  • utilities supplied by the lessor;
  • crew/operator services in wet leases and time charters.

Activities and costs that do not transfer a good or service are not components (Ind AS 116.B33): e.g. administrative fees for setting up the lease, reimbursement of the lessor's property taxes and building insurance — these are part of total consideration allocated to the components. Practitioners often misclassify property tax reimbursement as a non-lease component; it is consideration for the lease.

Allocation by the lessee (Ind AS 116.13–14)

The lessee allocates the consideration in the contract to each lease component on the basis of the relative stand-alone price of the lease component and the aggregate stand-alone price of the non-lease components. Stand-alone prices are determined on the basis of the price the lessor, or a similar supplier, would charge an entity separately for that component or a similar one; if observable prices are not readily available, the lessee estimates them, maximising observable information.

Note: contractual labels ("rent ₹X, CAM ₹Y") are not determinative — if the contract's split does not reflect stand-alone prices (e.g. rent is set low and CAM high), reallocate.

The practical expedient (Ind AS 116.15)

As a practical expedient, a lessee may elect, by class of underlying asset, not to separate non-lease components from lease components, and instead account for each lease component and any associated non-lease components as a single lease component. The expedient:

  • is not available for embedded derivatives meeting Ind AS 109.4.3.3 separation criteria;
  • applies by class (e.g. all retail store leases), not lease-by-lease;
  • increases lease liability and ROU (more fixed payments capitalised), raises EBITDA further (CAM moves from opex to depreciation/interest), and is attractive when non-lease components are small or hard to price.

Variable non-lease payments (e.g. CAM actuals billed on cost) are variable payments not depending on an index or rate, so even under the expedient they are expensed as incurred, not capitalised.

Lessor perspective (brief)

Lessors must separate and allocate consideration under Ind AS 115.73–90 (Ind AS 116.17); no practical expedient. Non-lease services are revenue under Ind AS 115.

Worked illustration

Metro Mart Ltd leases a store for 5 years: rent ₹10 lakh per month, CAM ₹2 lakh per month (fixed, covering housekeeping and security), property tax reimbursement ₹0.5 lakh per month — all payable monthly in advance, fixed for the term. Stand-alone prices: comparable bare-shell rent ₹11 lakh per month; CAM services ₹1.5 lakh per month (from a facility-management vendor quote). IBR 9.5% effective; monthly rate \( (1.095)^{1/12}-1 = 0.7591\% \); 60 payments.

Total fixed consideration per month = 10 + 2 + 0.5 = ₹12.5 lakh (property tax is consideration, not a component).

Component Stand-alone price Allocation Monthly amount
Lease (store) 11.0 11.0 ÷ 12.5 × 12.5 ₹11.0 lakh
CAM service 1.5 1.5 ÷ 12.5 × 12.5 ₹1.5 lakh
Total 12.5 12.5

(Here the sum of stand-alone prices happens to equal the consideration; usually a pro rata scaling is needed.)

PV annuity-due factor at 0.7591% for 60 months ≈ 48.41. Lease liability (separated) ≈ 11.0 × 48.41 = ₹532.6 lakh. Under the practical expedient (single component of ₹12.5 lakh) ≈ ₹605.2 lakh — about 14% higher.

(Interest accrual on the lease liability is booked separately.)

Excel implementation

Add two columns to the lease inputs table: "Non-lease component (₹/period)" and "Expedient elected (Y/N)" by asset class. Payment in the lease engine:

=IF(Expedient="Y", Rent+FixedCAM+PropTax, AllocatedLeasePortion)

Allocation:

=TotalFixedConsideration * SSP_Lease / (SSP_Lease + SSP_NonLease)

Disclosure

Disclose the election of the practical expedient (Ind AS 116.60) and expenses relating to variable payments not included in the liability (Ind AS 116.53(e)).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • CAM billed on actuals (pass-through of cost) is variable and excluded from the liability even if the expedient is elected.
  • CAM fixed but escalating with rent is fixed consideration — part of the liability under the expedient, or allocated to the service if separated.
  • GST on CAM and rent with ITC is excluded in both cases.
  • Lock-in on CAM — if CAM is cancellable separately, it may not be part of the same contract term.
  • Fuel and maintenance in vehicle leases — maintenance is a non-lease component; fuel reimbursement is variable.
  • Lessor-provided furniture in an office lease may be a separate lease component (furniture is usable on its own) — usually immaterial, often combined by policy.
  • Consistency: electing the expedient for retail but not for offices must be by class and applied consistently.

Key takeaways

  • Separate each lease component from non-lease services (CAM, maintenance) unless the practical expedient is elected by asset class.
  • Allocate consideration on relative stand-alone prices; contractual labels are not decisive.
  • Property tax and insurance reimbursements are not components — they are consideration allocated to components.
  • The expedient increases ROU and liability and lifts EBITDA; variable non-lease charges are still expensed.
  • Disclose the election and variable payments.

Practice Questions

Q1. Allocation with scaling [L2]

An office lease charges ₹20 lakh per month for "rent" and ₹5 lakh per month for "maintenance" (both fixed for 6 years, payable monthly in arrears). Stand-alone prices: comparable space ₹23 lakh per month; maintenance ₹4 lakh per month. IBR 9% effective annual. Allocate consideration, compute the lease liability if separated and if the practical expedient is elected, and state the FY effect on EBITDA in year 1.

💡 Hint: Scale stand-alone prices so the allocation adds up to the total fixed consideration of ₹25 lakh per month.

Q2. Classifying charges [L1]

Classify each charge in a mall lease as lease component, non-lease component or neither: base rent; fixed CAM; signage rights in the atrium; reimbursement of municipal property tax; electricity billed at actual consumption; marketing fund contribution of 1% of sales; 18% GST on rent with ITC.

Q3. Election by class [L2]

A conglomerate has (i) 400 retail store leases with fixed CAM averaging 12% of rent, (ii) 30 vehicle leases with full maintenance, and (iii) 5 office leases where CAM is variable. Recommend whether to elect the practical expedient for each class, considering liability size, EBITDA, covenant definitions and operational simplicity.

Q4. Aircraft wet lease [L3]

An airline wet-leases two aircraft for 9 months for a peak season, with the lessor providing crew, maintenance and insurance; the airline decides routes and schedules. Analyse lease identification, the term exemption, and the separation of components if the arrangement were for 18 months.
