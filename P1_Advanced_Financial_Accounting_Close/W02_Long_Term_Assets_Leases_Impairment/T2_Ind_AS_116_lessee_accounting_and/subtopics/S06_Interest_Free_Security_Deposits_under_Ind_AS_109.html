Subtopic note

Interest-Free Security Deposits under Ind AS 109 and Ind AS 116

Scope: Interest-free security deposits at fair value under Ind AS 109; difference added to ROU as prepaid rent

Why it matters

Indian commercial leases routinely require large interest-free refundable security deposits — 6 to 12 months' rent for offices, sometimes more for retail. A retailer with 300 stores may carry deposits worth hundreds of crore. Because the deposit earns no interest, its fair value is below face value; the difference is an economic benefit given to the lessor, effectively additional rent. Ind AS requires the deposit to be recognised at fair value as a financial asset, with the discount treated as prepaid lease payment in the ROU asset. The resulting interest income and extra depreciation are small per lease but material in aggregate, and the classification affects working capital and net debt presentations.

Core notes

Nature of the deposit

A refundable security deposit is not a lease payment — it is refundable and so is a financial asset of the lessee (a receivable from the lessor) within the scope of Ind AS 109. It is not part of the lease liability.

Ind AS 109.5.1.1 requires a financial asset to be initially recognised at fair value (plus transaction costs for assets not at FVTPL). Ind AS 109.B5.1.1 states that the fair value of a long-term loan or receivable that carries no interest can be measured as the present value of all future cash receipts discounted using the prevailing market rate(s) of interest for a similar instrument (similar as to currency, term, type of interest rate and other factors) with a similar credit rating. If the entity pays more than fair value, any additional amount is accounted for under the relevant standard — here, as a payment for the right to use the asset under Ind AS 116 (Ind AS 109.B5.1.1 and Ind AS 116.24(b)).

Measurement steps

  1. Fair value of deposit at commencement:

\[ FV_0 = \frac{D}{(1+r)^n} \]

where \( D \) is the deposit refundable at the end of the term, \( n \) is the expected period to refund (usually the lease term as determined in s03), and \( r \) is a market rate for a similar receivable from the lessor — reflecting the lessor's credit risk in principle. In practice, many Indian entities use their own IBR or a similar market rate for simplicity; the policy should be justified (the deposit is often secured by the lessee's right to withhold rent).

  1. Difference \( D - FV_0 \) is treated as prepaid lease payment and added to the ROU asset (Ind AS 116.24(b) — lease payments made at or before commencement).

  2. Subsequent measurement — the deposit is measured at amortised cost (business model of hold to collect, cash flows solely principal) using the effective interest rate: interest income \( = FV_{t-1} \times r \), so the deposit accretes to face value \( D \) by the refund date.

  3. The prepaid rent component in ROU is depreciated with the rest of the ROU over the lease term.

Net P&L effect over the term: interest income (deposit unwinding) and extra depreciation (prepaid rent) total the same amount, \( D - FV_0 \), but the timing differs — depreciation is straight-line while interest income increases over time.

Journal entries

At commencement (₹ lakh, illustrative):

Account Dr Cr
Security deposit (financial asset at FV) FV₀
ROU asset (prepaid rent) D − FV₀
Bank D

Each period:

Account Dr Cr
Security deposit Interest
Finance income Interest
Depreciation — ROU (D − FV₀) ÷ term
ROU asset (accumulated depreciation) (D − FV₀) ÷ term

At refund: Dr Bank D; Cr Security deposit D.

Worked illustration

Coastal Cafés Ltd pays an interest-free deposit of ₹36 lakh on 1 April 2025 for a 6-year store lease (term 72 months), refundable at the end. Market rate for a similar 6-year receivable: 9% effective annual.

\[ FV_0 = \frac{36}{1.09^6} = \frac{36}{1.6771} = ₹21.47 \text{ lakh} \]

Prepaid rent added to ROU = 36 − 21.47 = ₹14.53 lakh, depreciated at ₹2.42 lakh per year.

Year Opening deposit Interest income (9%) Closing deposit
FY26 21.47 1.93 23.40
FY27 23.40 2.11 25.51
FY28 25.51 2.30 27.80
FY29 27.80 2.50 30.30
FY30 30.30 2.73 33.03
FY31 33.03 2.97 36.00

Total interest income = ₹14.53 lakh = total additional depreciation.

Presentation

  • The deposit is a financial asset — "Other financial assets" in Schedule III, non-current until within 12 months of refund, then current. It is not part of trade receivables or cash.
  • Interest income on deposits is "Other income" (finance income).
  • The ROU prepaid component is part of the ROU asset line.
  • Cash flow: payment of the deposit is generally an investing outflow (a refundable advance/loan), though some entities classify it as operating as a working-capital item; the policy should be consistent and disclosed. The ROU portion is not separately a cash flow at commencement since the entire deposit is paid as one amount; Ind AS 7 classification follows the nature of the payment.
  • Ind AS 107 disclosures: carrying amount and fair value of deposits as financial assets at amortised cost.

Modification and early termination

If the lease term is reassessed or the lease terminated early, the expected refund date changes. The deposit carrying amount is adjusted under Ind AS 109.B5.4.6 (revised estimated cash flows discounted at the original EIR, with the adjustment in P&L), while the prepaid rent in ROU is adjusted as part of the ROU remeasurement or derecognised on termination. On early termination with immediate refund, the unamortised discount is recognised as interest income and the remaining prepaid ROU component is written off with the ROU.

Deposits from the lessor's side

The lessor receives the deposit as a financial liability at fair value; the difference is deferred rent income recognised over the lease term (operating lease) — a mirror treatment.

Excel implementation

Inputs: deposit amount, refund date (default = lease end), deposit discount rate. Calculations:

=Deposit/(1+DepRate)^(YEARFRAC(Commencement,RefundDate,1))     ' FV at commencement
=OpeningDeposit*((1+DepRate)^(1/12)-1)                          ' monthly interest

Add the prepaid amount to the ROU schedule's initial measurement. Check: closing deposit balance at refund date = face value.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rate choice: lessor-credit-based market rate vs lessee IBR — choose and document; differences can be material for long leases.
  • Deposit adjustable against last months' rent — the deposit is effectively prepaid future rent; the refund date is the adjustment date, and on adjustment it settles lease liability payments.
  • Deposits refundable on demand after the lock-in, or short leases — discounting may be immaterial.
  • Interest-bearing deposits at below-market rates — fair value at market rate; difference to ROU.
  • Deposits with related-party lessors (promoter-owned premises) — the off-market element may instead be a transaction with owners if not lease-related; consider substance.
  • Deposit for non-lease component (e.g. utility deposit) — the difference is not prepaid rent; it relates to the service contract.
  • Short-term or low-value exempt leases — deposit still at fair value under Ind AS 109; the difference is prepaid rent expensed over the lease term.

Key takeaways

  • Refundable interest-free deposits are Ind AS 109 financial assets, recognised at fair value (PV at a market rate).
  • The excess of face value over fair value is prepaid rent added to the ROU asset under Ind AS 116.24(b).
  • The deposit accretes to face value through interest income; the prepaid component is depreciated with ROU.
  • Total interest income equals total extra depreciation over the term; timing differs.
  • Present deposits as other financial assets, split current/non-current by refund date.

Practice Questions

Q1. Initial and subsequent measurement [L1]

An IT company pays an interest-free security deposit of ₹1.2 crore on 1 April 2025 for a 5-year office lease refundable at the end. Market rate for a similar receivable is 8.5% effective. Compute the fair value at commencement, the amount added to ROU, and FY26 interest income and additional depreciation. Pass the entries.

Q2. Early termination [L2]

Continuing Q1, the lessee terminates the lease on 31 March 2028 and receives the deposit in full. Compute the deposit carrying amount and the unamortised prepaid ROU component at that date, and describe the entries on termination.

💡 Hint: The deposit is settled at face value, so the remaining discount is recognised as income; the prepaid component is derecognised with the ROU.

Q3. Rate choice [L2]

For a 10-year deposit of ₹50 lakh, compute the fair value at a rate of 7.5% (lessor-credit market rate) and at 9.5% (lessee IBR). Discuss which rate is conceptually correct, and the effect of the choice on ROU, depreciation and interest income.

Q4. Presentation review [L2]

A retailer presents ₹180 crore of rent deposits at face value within "Loans — current" and does not discount them. Identify the issues under Ind AS 109, Ind AS 116 and Schedule III, and estimate the order of magnitude of the adjustment if the average remaining term is 6 years and the market rate 9%.

Q5. Lessor mirror [L2]

A mall owner receives an interest-free deposit of ₹24 lakh for a 3-year operating lease. Explain the lessor's initial and subsequent accounting at a market rate of 9%, and the effect on rental income each year.
