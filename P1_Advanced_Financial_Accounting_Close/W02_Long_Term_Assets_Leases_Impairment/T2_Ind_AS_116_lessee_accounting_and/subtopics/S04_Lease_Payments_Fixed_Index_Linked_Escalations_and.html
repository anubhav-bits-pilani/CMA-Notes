Subtopic note

Lease Payments: Fixed, Index-Linked, Escalations and Variable Rent

Scope: Lease payments: fixed and in-substance fixed, index-linked, contractual escalations (e.g., 15% every 3 years), excluded revenue-share/variable rent, MG vs revenue share

Why it matters

Once lease term is set, the payment vector determines the liability. Indian leases mix fixed rent, periodic escalations (5% annually or 15% every three years are standard), CPI-linked clauses in some office and warehousing leases, revenue-share rent in malls and airports, and "higher of minimum guarantee (MG) or revenue share" structures in food courts, multiplexes and airport retail. Each element has a different treatment: fixed and in-substance fixed payments are capitalised; revenue-linked amounts are expensed. Misclassification distorts both the liability and EBITDA, and it is a frequent review comment.

Core notes

What lease payments include (Ind AS 116.27)

At commencement, the lease liability includes the following payments for the right to use the asset during the lease term that are not paid at that date:

  • (a) fixed payments, including in-substance fixed payments (B42), less any lease incentives receivable;
  • (b) variable lease payments that depend on an index or a rate, initially measured using the index or rate at the commencement date;
  • (c) amounts expected to be payable under residual value guarantees;
  • (d) the exercise price of a purchase option if reasonably certain to be exercised;
  • (e) termination penalties if the lease term reflects the lessee exercising a termination option.

Excluded: variable payments that depend on the lessee's performance or use (e.g. percentage of sales, usage-based charges) — recognised in P&L in the period in which the triggering event occurs (Ind AS 116.38(b)); payments for non-lease components (unless the expedient is elected); recoverable taxes.

Fixed payments and contractual escalations

Escalations specified in the contract — "15% increase on completion of every 36 months", "5% p.a. compounded" — are fixed payments known at commencement. They are included in the initial measurement with the exact amount and timing. They are not remeasurements or modifications when they occur.

Example payment vector for ₹10 lakh/month, 9 years, 15% every 36 months:

Months Monthly rent (₹ lakh)
1–36 10.000
37–72 11.500
73–108 13.225

Excel: =BaseRent*(1+Esc)^INT((n-1)/StepMonths).

Straight-lining is irrelevant to the lessee's liability; the escalation pattern simply affects the discounted amount and the interest profile.

In-substance fixed payments (B42)

Payments that may in form contain variability but that in substance are unavoidable:

  • payments structured as variable but with no genuine variability (e.g. payable only if the asset is operational, when it will clearly be operational);
  • a lessee choice between sets of payments where only one set is realistic — the lowest realistic set is in-substance fixed;
  • minimum guarantees in "higher of MG or revenue share" structures — the MG portion is fixed.

Index-linked payments

Payments linked to CPI, WPI or a benchmark interest rate (e.g. rent that resets with MCLR or repo) are included at the index/rate value at commencement — no forecast of future inflation. When the index changes and cash flows change, the liability is remeasured using the unchanged discount rate (unless floating rate changes, where a revised rate reflecting the change is used — Ind AS 116.43) with the adjustment to ROU (Ind AS 116.42(b)). See s07 and c05.

Variable payments linked to performance

Revenue-share rent (e.g. 12% of store sales in a mall, concession fee as % of revenue at an airport) depends on the lessee's future performance and is excluded from the liability, even if highly predictable. It is expensed when the sales occur, typically in "rent" or "other expenses", and it remains in EBITDA. Usage-based payments (per-km for vehicles, per-hour for equipment) are similarly excluded.

MG vs revenue share

The common Indian structure: rent = higher of (a) MG of ₹X per month and (b) Y% of revenue.

  • MG is in-substance fixed → included in lease payments and capitalised.
  • The excess of revenue share over MG in any period → variable payment, expensed as incurred.
  • If the MG escalates (e.g. 10% p.a.), include the escalated MG schedule.

Illustration: an airport café concession for 5 years: MG ₹3 lakh/month (escalating 7% annually), revenue share 18% of sales. Expected sales ₹25 lakh/month in year 1 → revenue share ₹4.5 lakh. Liability is measured using MG only (₹3 lakh/month with 7% escalation); each month's excess of ₹1.5 lakh is a variable lease expense.

Journal (monthly, year 1, ₹ lakh):

Account Dr Cr
Lease liability (MG payment, principal + accrued interest) 3.0
Variable lease expense (P&L) 1.5
Bank 4.5

Note that airport concessions may also fall within service concession or licence analysis; apply lease identification first.

Other inclusions

  • Lease incentives received or receivable (e.g. lessor fit-out contributions, rent-free periods) reduce fixed payments if receivable, or reduce ROU if received at or before commencement (Ind AS 116.24(c)). Rent-free months appear as zero payments in the vector.
  • Residual value guarantees: only the amount expected to be payable, not the maximum guarantee (e.g. vehicle leases with RVGs).
  • Purchase options reasonably certain to be exercised.
  • Termination penalties if the term assumes termination.

GST and other taxes

GST charged on rent is a tax collected by the lessor on behalf of the government. If input tax credit is available, it is excluded from lease payments. If ITC is blocked (e.g. lessee makes exempt supplies — banks/insurers with partial reversal, hospitals), the non-recoverable GST forms part of the lease payment (cost of the right of use). Property taxes reimbursed are part of consideration (s02).

Worked illustration — full payment vector

Lease from 1 April 2025, 6 years, monthly in advance. Base rent ₹5 lakh; 15% step after 36 months; first 2 months rent-free (incentive in the form of reduced payments); CAM ₹0.8 lakh fixed (separated); revenue share 5% of sales above ₹1.2 crore per month (expected sales ₹1.4 crore); GST 18% with ITC.

Lease payments in the liability: months 1–2: ₹0; months 3–36: ₹5.00 lakh; months 37–72: ₹5.75 lakh. CAM excluded (separated), revenue share (expected ₹1 lakh per month) excluded, GST excluded.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Escalation linked to CPI with a floor of 5%: the 5% floor is in-substance fixed; CPI excess is variable dependent on an index — remeasure when it changes.
  • Rent reductions for low footfall (COVID-type concessions): a change in consideration not in original terms is a modification unless the specific relief was in the contract or the concession expedient applies.
  • Turnover rent with a cap: only the minimum is fixed; caps do not make variable rent fixed.
  • Variable payments that become fixed (e.g. MG reset annually to 80% of prior-year revenue share) — when the reset happens, the newly fixed amount is a change in payments; treat as reassessment/modification depending on whether the mechanism was in the original contract (remeasure at unchanged rate if it was).
  • Security deposits are not lease payments (s06).
  • Payments made before commencement are included in ROU (prepaid), not in the liability.

Key takeaways

  • Lease payments = fixed and in-substance fixed (less incentives), index/rate-linked at commencement value, expected RVG payments, reasonably certain purchase options and relevant termination penalties.
  • Contractual escalations such as 15% every 3 years are fixed payments included from commencement.
  • Revenue-share and usage-based payments are excluded and expensed when incurred.
  • In MG vs revenue-share structures, MG is capitalised; the excess is variable expense.
  • Exclude recoverable GST; include it only where ITC is not available.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a payment vector [L2]

A warehouse lease from 1 April 2025 for 10 years has rent of ₹12 lakh per month payable in arrears, increasing 5% compounded annually, with the first 3 months rent-free. Specify the payment for months 1, 4, 13, 60 and 120, and write the Excel formula that generates the whole vector.

Q2. MG with escalation [L2]

A multiplex operator's lease: higher of MG ₹20 lakh/month (escalating 10% every 3 years) or 15% of net box-office and F&B revenue, for 12 years; expected revenue share ₹26 lakh/month in year 1. IBR 10% effective. Identify the payments to be capitalised, the variable lease expense for year 1, and how you would present them in the P&L and cash flow statement.

💡 Hint: Treat the minimum guarantee as in-substance fixed and the excess as performance-based variable rent.

Q3. CPI-linked office lease [L2]

An office lease commencing 1 April 2025 has rent ₹30 lakh/year in advance, increasing annually by CPI (CPI at commencement 4.5%). Explain how the initial liability is measured and what happens on 1 April 2026 when CPI for the year turns out to be 5.2%.

Q4. Blocked GST [L1]

A private hospital leases a building for ₹40 lakh per month plus GST at 18%. ITC is not available because healthcare services are exempt. State whether GST forms part of the lease payments and quantify the effect on the monthly payment used in the schedule.

Q5. Reviewer's checklist [L3]

Draft a checklist of 8–10 tests a reviewer should perform on the payment vectors in a 300-lease Ind AS 116 model, covering escalations, rent-free periods, variable rent, GST, MG structures and index-linked clauses.
