Key concept

Scope-Decrease Modifications: Gain or Loss Mechanics

Concept: Scope decrease: reduce ROU proportionately, P&L gain/loss = Liability derecognised − ROU derecognised; remeasure remainder at revised rate against ROU (116.46(a))

Statement

For a modification that decreases the scope of a lease (partial or full termination), at the effective date:

\[ \text{Gain/(loss)} = s \times LL_{\text{before}} - s \times ROU_{\text{before}} \]

where \( s \) is the proportion of scope given up (e.g. 40% of floor area or a proportion of remaining term), \( LL_{\text{before}} \) and \( ROU_{\text{before}} \) are carrying amounts immediately before the modification. Then:

\[ LL' = PV_{r^{*}}(\text{revised payments}), \qquad \Delta ROU = LL' - (1-s) \times LL_{\text{before}} \]

with \( r^{*} \) the revised discount rate at the effective date; \( \Delta ROU \) adjusts the remaining ROU (no P&L). For a full termination (\( s = 1 \)), the gain/loss is \( LL_{\text{before}} - ROU_{\text{before}} - \text{termination penalty} \).

Explanation

Ind AS 116.46(a) requires the lessee to decrease the ROU carrying amount "to reflect the partial or full termination" and recognise a gain or loss "relating to the partial or full termination". The standard does not prescribe the method of measuring the decrease; common practice (and the IFRS 16 illustrative examples) reduces both ROU and liability by the same proportion of scope, so the P&L effect is the difference between the derecognised liability and derecognised ROU.

Why a gain usually arises: the ROU is depreciated straight-line while the liability declines slowly in early years (interest is front-loaded), so mid-lease the liability exceeds the ROU; derecognising equal proportions produces a gain. A penalty paid reduces it.

The second step — remeasuring the remaining liability using revised payments and the revised rate — reflects the new terms of the continuing lease. Because the continuing lease is not a termination, this adjustment goes to ROU (as in Ind AS 116.46(b)). Term reductions are also scope decreases: \( s \) may be measured as the proportion of the remaining term given up, or by comparing ROU-in-use (practice varies; disclose policy).

Associated items: unamortised prepaid rent from deposits (s06) and leasehold improvements in the surrendered space (impairment or accelerated depreciation) are dealt with alongside.

Illustration

Before modification at 1 April 2026 (₹ lakh): liability 503.30, ROU 449.24. The lessee surrenders 40% of space; remaining rent ₹60 lakh per year in arrears for 7 years; revised IBR 10% (annuity factor 4.8684).

  1. Derecognise 40%: liability 201.32; ROU 179.69; gain 21.62.
  2. Remaining liability 301.98 → remeasured 60 × 4.8684 = 292.11; reduce ROU by 9.87.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full early termination [L1]

A lease with liability ₹86 lakh and ROU ₹71 lakh is terminated by agreement with a penalty of ₹10 lakh paid immediately. Pass the entry and compute the P&L effect.

Q2. Term reduction as a scope decrease [L2]

A 10-year lease (₹40 lakh per year in arrears, IBR 9%) is modified after 4 years to end after year 7 instead of year 10; rent unchanged; revised IBR 9.8%. Measure the scope decrease using the proportion of remaining term given up, compute the gain or loss and the remeasured liability, and pass entries.

💡 Hint: Remaining term falls from 6 to 3 years, so half the remaining scope is surrendered.

Q3. Partial surrender with penalty and fit-outs [L3]

A company surrenders 30% of an office (liability ₹520 lakh, ROU ₹470 lakh before modification) and pays a ₹15 lakh surrender fee; leasehold improvements in the surrendered area have a carrying amount of ₹22 lakh and will be abandoned; the remaining rent is ₹63 lakh per year in arrears for 8 years at a revised IBR of 9.4%. Compute all P&L effects and the closing balances.
