Key concept

Modification as a Separate Lease (Ind AS 116.44)

Concept: Separate contract if modification adds right-of-use of additional assets at commensurate standalone price (116.44)

Statement

A lessee accounts for a lease modification as a separate lease if both:

\[ \text{(1) the modification adds the right to use one or more underlying assets, and} \]

\[ \text{(2) } \Delta C \approx SSP_{\text{added}} \pm \text{contract-specific adjustments} \]

where \( \Delta C \) is the increase in lease consideration and \( SSP_{\text{added}} \) is the stand-alone price for the increase in scope, adjusted for the particular contract's circumstances (Ind AS 116.44). If either condition fails, the modification is accounted for by remeasuring the existing lease (Ind AS 116.45–46).

Explanation

The separate-lease route treats the added asset as if a new, independent lease had been signed: it gets its own commencement date, lease term, IBR at that date, and its own ROU and liability. The original lease continues unchanged — no remeasurement, no change to its rate.

Condition (1) requires an additional underlying asset (e.g. an additional floor, an extra warehouse unit, additional aircraft). Extending the term of an existing asset or increasing a non-distinct capacity portion does not add a right to use an additional asset; it is a modification of the existing lease even if priced at market.

Condition (2) requires pricing at the stand-alone price, with adjustments reflecting the contract — e.g. a discount because the lessor avoids marketing costs it would incur with a new tenant may still be "commensurate". A significant discount beyond such adjustments fails the condition; the modification is then remeasured as a whole at the revised rate with the adjustment to ROU (Ind AS 116.46(b)).

This mirrors the contract-modification logic in Ind AS 115.20 for revenue.

Illustration

A company leases Floor 1 of an IT park for 10 years from 1 April 2023 at ₹60 lakh per year. On 1 April 2026 it agrees to add Floor 2 for the remaining 7 years.

  • Scenario A: Floor 2 rent ₹62 lakh per year; market rent for a similar 7-year lease ₹64 lakh; the lessor saves ₹2 lakh per year of brokerage and vacancy costs. The price is commensurate after contract-specific adjustment → separate lease: new ROU and liability for Floor 2 at the IBR on 1 April 2026 (say 9.8%): 62 × annuity factor (9.8%, 7) ≈ 62 × 4.9 ≈ ₹304 lakh (arrears). Floor 1 continues at its original 9% schedule.
  • Scenario B: Floor 2 rent ₹45 lakh per year (a 30% discount to market as an inducement to stay). Not commensurate → not a separate lease: remeasure the combined lease (Floor 1 at ₹60 lakh + Floor 2 at ₹45 lakh for 7 years) at the revised rate of 9.8%, and adjust ROU by the difference between the remeasured liability and the existing Floor 1 liability.
  • Scenario C: Instead of Floor 2, the company extends Floor 1 by 3 years at market rent. No additional asset → not a separate lease; remeasure at the revised rate (Ind AS 116.46(b)).

Connections

In models, a separate lease is simply a new row in the lease input table with its own commencement date, which is why the exercise's input design should allow adding leases at any date.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the two conditions [L1]

For each modification, decide whether it is a separate lease: (a) adding 10 more delivery vans at the lessor's standard rate card; (b) increasing contracted capacity in a shared pipeline from 40% to 60% at market rate; (c) adding a warehouse unit at a 25% discount to market; (d) extending a lease of 5 aircraft by 2 years at market rent.

Q2. Separate lease accounting [L2]

A retailer adds an adjacent shop unit to an existing store lease on 1 October 2025 for the remaining 5 years at ₹3 lakh per month in advance (market rent ₹3.1 lakh; the landlord saves fit-out allowance worth ₹0.1 lakh per month). IBR on that date 9.6% effective annual. Determine the accounting and compute the initial liability and ROU for the added unit.

💡 Hint: Compare the price with the stand-alone price after contract-specific adjustments before deciding on the model.

Q3. Combined modification [L3]

A lessee adds a new floor at a 20% discount to market and simultaneously extends the existing floor's lease by 3 years. Explain how the whole modification is accounted for, the rate used, how the consideration is allocated between components, and the effect on ROU and P&L.
