Key concept

Lease Liability Interest Recursion and Periodic Rate Conversion

Concept: Interest = Opening liability × periodic rate; Closing liability = Opening + Interest − Payment; monthly rate = (1 + annual)^(1/12) − 1

Statement

For each period \( t \):

\[ I_t = LL_{t-1} \times r_p, \qquad LL_t = LL_{t-1} + I_t - P_t \]

where \( LL_{t-1} \) is the opening lease liability, \( r_p \) is the periodic rate, \( I_t \) is interest expense for the period and \( P_t \) is the lease payment in the period.

Conversion from an effective annual rate \( r_a \):

\[ r_m = (1+r_a)^{1/12} - 1, \qquad r_q = (1+r_a)^{1/4} - 1 \]

For payments in advance, the payment at the start of a period reduces the balance on which that period's interest accrues: \( I_t = (LL_{t-1} - P_t) \times r_p \) and \( LL_t = LL_{t-1} - P_t + I_t \).

Explanation

Ind AS 116.36 requires the liability to be measured subsequently by increasing the carrying amount to reflect interest, reducing it to reflect payments, and remeasuring for reassessments/modifications. Interest is computed so as to produce a constant periodic rate on the remaining balance (Ind AS 116.37) — the effective interest method.

The conversion matters because IBRs are expressed as effective annual rates. Using \( r_a/12 \) treats the rate as nominal compounded monthly: the true effective rate becomes \( (1+r_a/12)^{12}-1 \), higher than \( r_a \). Using \( r_a \) on monthly payments (no conversion) massively understates the liability. If the IBR is itself derived as a nominal monthly-compounded rate (e.g. from an EMI-based bank quote), \( r_a/12 \) is correct — the key is consistency.

Timing convention matters: with advance payments, interest is lower in each period than with arrears. The liability at the end of the term must be zero; a non-zero balance indicates a timing or rate error.

Year-end split: the current portion of the liability is the reduction in principal scheduled in the next 12 months — total payments due in 12 months less the interest component within them (equivalently, \( LL_{\text{now}} - LL_{\text{12 months later}} \) absent new remeasurements).

Illustration

Liability ₹100 lakh, 4 quarterly payments in arrears, IBR 10% effective annual → \( r_q = 1.10^{0.25} - 1 = 2.411\% \). Level payment = PMT(2.411%, 4, −100) = ₹26.53 lakh.

Quarter Opening Interest (2.411%) Payment Closing
1 100.00 2.41 26.53 75.89
2 75.89 1.83 26.53 51.19
3 51.19 1.23 26.53 25.90
4 25.90 0.62 26.53 0.00 (rounding)

Total interest ₹6.10 lakh. Had \( r_a/4 = 2.5\% \) been used, payment would be ₹26.58 lakh and interest higher; had 10% per quarter been used, the error would be gross.

Excel:

=(1+IBR)^(1/12)-1                      ' monthly rate
=IF(Timing="Advance",(Open-Pmt)*rm,Open*rm)   ' interest
=Open+Interest-Pmt                     ' closing
=SUMIFS(Pmt,Date,">"&RepDate,Date,"<="&EDATE(RepDate,12))-SUMIFS(Int,...)  ' current portion

Practice Questions

Q1. Monthly schedule [L1]

A lease liability of ₹50 lakh is repaid by 24 monthly payments in arrears; IBR 9% effective annual. Compute the monthly rate, the level payment and the first three rows of the schedule.

Q2. Conversion error quantified [L2]

For a 10-year lease of ₹5 lakh per month in advance, compute the liability using (a) \( (1.095)^{1/12}-1 \), (b) 9.5%/12, and (c) 9.5% per month in error. Quantify the differences and explain when (b) would be correct.

💡 Hint: The question is whether the 9.5% is an effective annual rate or a nominal rate compounded monthly.

Q3. Current/non-current split [L2]

At 31 March 2026, a lease liability is ₹420 lakh; payments due in FY27 total ₹72 lakh, of which the schedule shows interest of ₹35 lakh. Compute the current and non-current portions and explain why presenting ₹72 lakh as current is wrong.
