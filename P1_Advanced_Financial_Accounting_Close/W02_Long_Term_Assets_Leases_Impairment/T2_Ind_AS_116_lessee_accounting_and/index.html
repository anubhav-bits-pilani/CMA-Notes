Roadmap topic: Ind AS 116 Leases: Lease identification, Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR), Right-of-Use (ROU) asset & lease liability amortization schedules, and lease modifications

Overview

Ind AS 116 (effective in India from 1 April 2019) put almost every lease on the lessee's balance sheet. For Indian retailers, QSR chains, airlines, telecom companies, hospitals and IT services firms, ROU assets and lease liabilities are among the largest balance sheet items; for a typical NSE-listed retailer, lease liabilities can exceed financial debt. The standard replaced straight-line rent expense with depreciation plus interest, lifted EBITDA, front-loaded total P&L cost, and moved lease principal payments into financing cash flows. These effects run through covenants, credit ratings, management incentive metrics and valuation multiples, so FP&A and reporting roles must be able to build, explain and adjust lease schedules.

Technically, the work is a sequence of judgements followed by a schedule. First, is the contract a lease at all (s01) — an identified asset, without substantive substitution rights, where the customer obtains substantially all economic benefits and directs the use? Then separate lease and non-lease components such as CAM and maintenance, or elect the practical expedient (s02). Determine the lease term, including reasonably certain extensions and the effect of lock-ins common in Indian leave-and-licence and retail agreements (s03), and the lease payments — fixed, in-substance fixed, index-linked and contractually escalating amounts, but not revenue-share variable rent (s04). Choose the discount rate: the implicit rate if determinable, otherwise the IBR built from a reference rate, credit spread and lease-specific adjustments (s05). Account for interest-free security deposits at fair value (s06). After commencement, apply the reassessment and modification rules (s07), and know the exemptions, lessor classification and sale-and-leaseback rules (s08).

The concepts (c01–c07) are the formulas you will put into Excel: initial liability and ROU, the interest/closing liability recursion with correct periodic rates, the four core journal entries, the remeasurement rate rules, the scope-decrease gain/loss mechanics, and the separate-contract test for modifications. The week's hands-on exercise — an 8-lease dynamic schedule with escalation, remeasurement and early termination — draws on every one of them.

Ind AS 116 as notified by MCA is largely aligned with IFRS 16, with India-specific carve-outs being limited (the COVID-19 rent concession expedient was adopted and later extended). The 2024 amendment (Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2024, effective 1 April 2024) added requirements on measuring the lease liability in a sale-and-leaseback, aligned with IFRS 16's 2022 amendment.

Related concepts to study

US GAAP ASC 842 dual model

US GAAP ASC 842: lessee finance vs operating classification (straight-line single cost for operating) vs single on-balance-sheet model in Ind AS 116

ASC 842 also puts leases on the balance sheet, but lessees classify each lease as finance or operating using criteria similar to the old IAS 17 tests. Finance leases are accounted for like Ind AS 116 (amortisation plus interest). Operating leases recognise a single straight-line lease cost in operating expenses; the ROU asset is the plug that makes the total expense straight-line, and the liability is still a PV. The result: US retailers' EBITDA does not get the Ind AS 116 uplift for operating leases, and their cash flows show operating lease payments within operating activities. When benchmarking an Indian retailer against US peers, adjust EBITDA, net debt and operating cash flow for these differences or compare on EBITDAR.

Tax: rent deduction and 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment

Tax: rent deductible on payment; ROU and lease liability temporary differences under 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment → Week 3 (w3_t3)

For income-tax, the lessee deducts lease rentals as paid or payable under the contract; ROU depreciation and lease interest are disallowed in the computation. The ROU asset therefore has a tax base of nil (taxable temporary difference) and the lease liability also has a tax base of nil (deductible temporary difference). Before 2023, many entities applied the initial recognition exemption to avoid deferred tax at commencement. The Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2023 narrowed the exemption so it does not apply to transactions giving rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences, so deferred tax is recognised gross on both sides from FY 2023-24 (with transitional adjustment to opening retained earnings). Week 3 covers the computation and presentation.

EBITDA, covenants, ratings, net debt and cash flows

EBITDA uplift, covenant and rating impacts; lease liabilities in net debt; CF classification → w1_t6

Replacing rent with depreciation and interest raises EBITDA by the full fixed rent; EBIT usually rises too because straight-line ROU depreciation is lower than the rent it replaces, while profit before tax falls in early years because interest is front-loaded and rises after the crossover point. Net debt increases by lease liabilities, so leverage ratios (net debt/EBITDA) move in both numerator and denominator. Bank covenants drafted before 2019 may be "frozen GAAP" or may need amendment; rating agencies such as CRISIL and ICRA typically adjust for leases in their own way. In the cash flow statement, principal repayments are financing; interest follows the entity's Ind AS 7 policy; short-term, low-value and variable payments are operating (Ind AS 116.50). Week 1's cash flow topic covers classification consistency and the net debt reconciliation.

ROU assets in CGU carrying amounts

ROU inclusion in CGU carrying amounts → w2_t3

ROU assets are tested for impairment under Ind AS 36, usually as part of a CGU (a store, a cluster of stores, a route). Including the ROU in the carrying amount requires a consistent treatment of lease payments: either exclude lease payments from VIU cash flows and do not deduct the lease liability (pure asset view), or include lease payments in cash flows and deduct the lease liability from the carrying amount — but not a mixture. Loss-making stores often show impairment of ROU assets, which then reduces future depreciation while the liability continues unchanged. Topic 3 covers the mechanics and the related pitfalls.

Excel multi-lease schedule design

Excel: multi-lease schedule with XNPV/PV, advance vs arrears payment toggle, escalation flags, modification-date switches

A robust multi-lease model has an inputs table (one row per lease), a period engine (monthly columns or a long-format table), and output summaries. Payments are derived from base rent × escalation factor, with an escalation flag =INT((n-1)/36) for a 15% step every 36 months, zero for rent-free months, and an advance/arrears toggle that shifts payments to the start or end of each period. Initial liability uses PV with type = 1 for advance or a SUMPRODUCT of payments × discount factors, or XNPV for irregular dates. Modification-date switches change the rate and payment vector from the modification date, with the liability remeasured at that date. Checks: closing liability nil at end, current portion equal to next-12-month principal.

Common pitfalls

Annual rate applied to monthly payments

Applying an annual rate to monthly payments without conversion

The mistake: monthly rents are discounted using the annual IBR as if it were monthly, or simply divided by 12 without considering whether the IBR is an effective or nominal rate. People make it because Excel's PV function takes "rate" without context. The correct approach: if the IBR is an effective annual rate, the monthly rate is \( (1+r)^{1/12} - 1 \); if it is a nominal rate compounded monthly, divide by 12. Using r/12 on an effective rate overstates the effective rate slightly; using the annual rate monthly produces a grossly understated liability. Detect it by recomputing the liability with XNPV on the actual payment dates and the annual effective rate — results should match.

Fixed escalations treated as modifications

Treating contractually fixed escalations as modifications instead of including them from commencement

The mistake: a lease with 15% rent increases every 3 years is measured at commencement using only the initial rent, and each step-up is booked as a "modification" or remeasurement. This happens when schedules are built from the current rent roll. Contractual escalations are fixed lease payments known at commencement and must be included in the initial measurement (Ind AS 116.27). A modification is a change in scope or consideration that was not part of the original terms (Appendix A). Detect it by comparing the payment vector in the model with the lease deed's escalation clause and by checking for remeasurement entries on escalation dates.

Ignoring reasonably certain renewals or lock-ins

Ignoring reasonably certain renewals or lock-ins when setting lease term

The mistake: lease term is set to the lock-in period (e.g. 3 years in a 9-year leave-and-licence) or to the initial term, ignoring renewals the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise given significant leasehold improvements, relocation costs and the store's profitability. It happens because the legal minimum term is easy to evidence. Ind AS 116.18–19 and B37–B40 require the lease term to include reasonably certain extension periods and periods covered by termination options the lessee is reasonably certain not to exercise; the enforceable period analysis considers both parties' rights. Detect it by comparing lease term with the depreciation life of leasehold improvements — leasehold improvements depreciated over 9 years with a 3-year lease term is a red flag.

Recoverable GST or revenue share in lease payments

Including recoverable GST or revenue-share rent in lease payments

The mistake: the liability is measured on rent inclusive of 18% GST, or includes the expected revenue-share rent in a mall lease. GST for which input tax credit is available is a recoverable tax, not a payment for the right to use the asset; include it only where ITC is blocked or not available. Variable payments linked to sales are excluded from lease payments (Ind AS 116.27, 116.38(b)) and expensed when incurred, unless they are in-substance fixed (e.g. a minimum guarantee). Detect it by tying the monthly rent in the model to the invoice net of GST, and by testing that P&L shows variable rent separately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Retail lease from identification to termination [L3]

Pinnacle Fashion Ltd signs a 9-year leave-and-licence agreement for a 6,000 sq ft mall store from 1 April 2025: lock-in 3 years for both parties, then lessee-only termination right with 3 months' notice. Rent ₹6 lakh per month payable in advance plus 18% GST (ITC available), escalating 15% every 3 years; CAM ₹1 lakh per month (fixed); revenue share 10% of store sales above ₹90 lakh per month; interest-free security deposit ₹36 lakh refundable at the end; brokerage ₹4 lakh; fit-outs costing ₹1.2 crore with a 10-year life; IBR 9.5% effective annual. Lessor contributes ₹10 lakh towards fit-outs (incentive).

(a) Justify the lease term. (b) Identify lease payments and the treatment of CAM if the practical expedient is not elected. (c) Compute the initial liability and ROU (show the deposit fair value adjustment). (d) Prepare FY26 entries. (e) On 31 March 2029 the lessee terminates with effect from 30 June 2029; describe the accounting.

💡 Hint: Use the leasehold improvement life and the economic penalty of abandoning fit-outs as evidence on lease term.

Q2. Data centre and captive power contracts [L3]

A cloud services company enters into (i) a 10-year co-location contract for 200 racks in a specified data hall where the provider can move the customer between halls at any time at its own cost, and (ii) a 20-year PPA with a captive solar plant built solely for it, under which it takes the entire output and decides when the plant is maintained within agreed parameters. For each, analyse whether a lease exists under Ind AS 116.9 and B9–B31, and state the accounting consequences if it does.

Q3. Modification package [L3]

A company leases two floors of an office for 10 years from 1 April 2023 at ₹50 lakh per floor per year payable in arrears; IBR 9%. On 1 April 2026 it (a) surrenders one floor and (b) extends the remaining floor's lease by 4 years at the same rent; revised IBR 10%. Compute the gain/loss on the scope decrease, the remeasurement of the remaining liability and the adjustment to ROU, and pass the entries.

Q4. Model build and reporting impacts [L3]

For a chain of 120 QSR outlets with total annual fixed rent of ₹180 crore (average remaining term 7 years, IBR 9.25%, payments monthly in advance), design an Excel model to compute the lease liability, ROU, FY27 P&L and cash flow effects, and the current/non-current split. Explain the expected effect on EBITDA, net debt/EBITDA and operating cash flow compared with straight-line rent, and how you would present lease liabilities in a lender covenant certificate.
