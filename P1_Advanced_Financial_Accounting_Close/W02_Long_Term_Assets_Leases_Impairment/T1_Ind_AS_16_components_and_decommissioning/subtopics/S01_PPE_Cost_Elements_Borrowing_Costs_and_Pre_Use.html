Subtopic note

PPE Cost Elements, Borrowing Costs and Pre-Use Proceeds

Scope: Cost elements: directly attributable costs, site preparation, testing, borrowing costs (Ind AS 23); proceeds before intended use to P&L (Ind AS 16.20A)

Why it matters

The initial cost of PPE determines every future depreciation charge, the tax-book difference and the denominator of return-on-capital metrics. In Indian greenfield and brownfield projects — cement lines, refineries, solar parks, data centres — capitalisation decisions on pre-operative costs, borrowing costs and trial-run output can swing EBITDA by crores in the commissioning year. Auditors test capitalisation as a significant risk where management has an incentive to inflate assets; NFRA inspection reports have repeatedly flagged capitalisation of general overheads. In FP&A, the capex budget must distinguish capitalisable cost from opex so the model's PPE additions, depreciation and EBITDA are consistent with the accounting policy.

Core notes

Measurement at recognition

An item of PPE that qualifies for recognition is measured at cost (Ind AS 16.15). Cost comprises (Ind AS 16.16):

  1. Purchase price, including import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, after deducting trade discounts and rebates. Recoverable GST (input tax credit available) is excluded; GST that is blocked under Sec 17(5) of the CGST Act (e.g. for construction of immovable property on own account) is included.
  2. Directly attributable costs of bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of operating in the manner intended by management.
  3. The initial estimate of dismantling, removal and site restoration costs, for obligations incurred on acquisition or through use other than to produce inventories (covered in s05).

Directly attributable costs (Ind AS 16.17)

Examples given by the standard:

  • employee benefits (Ind AS 19) arising directly from construction or acquisition — e.g. salaries of the project engineering team, not corporate finance;
  • costs of site preparation — levelling, clearing, soil testing, demolition of an old structure on land acquired for the new plant;
  • initial delivery and handling costs;
  • installation and assembly costs;
  • costs of testing whether the asset is functioning properly;
  • professional fees — architects, engineers, project management consultants.

The test is causation: would the cost have been incurred had the asset not been constructed? Corporate overheads that would exist anyway fail the test.

Costs excluded (Ind AS 16.19–20)

  • costs of opening a new facility, introducing a new product (including advertising and promotion);
  • costs of conducting business in a new location or with a new class of customer (including staff training);
  • administration and other general overheads;
  • costs incurred while an asset capable of operating as intended is not yet brought into use or is operated below full capacity;
  • initial operating losses while demand builds up;
  • relocation and reorganisation costs.

Recognition of costs ceases when the asset is in the location and condition necessary for it to operate as intended (Ind AS 16.20). The commissioning date (often documented by a commissioning certificate or management declaration) is therefore a key audit document.

Incidental operations that are not necessary to bring the asset to the intended condition — e.g. using a construction site as a car park — are recognised in P&L (Ind AS 16.21). Abnormal wastage of material, labour or other resources is excluded (Ind AS 16.22), as are internal profits on self-constructed assets.

Testing and proceeds before intended use (Ind AS 16.20A)

The 2022 amendment (Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules, 2022, effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2022) changed a long-standing Indian practice. Earlier, net sale proceeds of trial-run output were deducted from the cost of testing (and often from capitalised cost). Now:

  • Testing means assessing whether the technical and physical performance of the asset is such that it is capable of being used in production, supply, rental or administration — not assessing financial performance.
  • Items produced while bringing the asset to the intended location and condition (e.g. samples produced when testing equipment) are inventory. Sale proceeds and the cost of those items are recognised in P&L under Ind AS 2 and Ind AS 115 (Ind AS 16.20A).
  • Entities must disclose the amounts of proceeds and cost included in P&L that relate to items produced that are not an output of ordinary activities, and the line items in which they are included (Ind AS 16.74A(d)).

The cost of producing those items is measured under Ind AS 2, so input costs (raw material, power used for trial production) are moved out of CWIP into inventory/cost of sales. The remaining testing costs (engineers, inspection agencies) stay capitalised.

Borrowing costs (Ind AS 23)

Borrowing costs directly attributable to the acquisition, construction or production of a qualifying asset (one that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for intended use) form part of its cost (Ind AS 23.8). Key mechanics:

Element Rule
Specific borrowings Actual borrowing costs incurred less investment income on temporary investment of those funds (Ind AS 23.12)
General borrowings Capitalisation rate (weighted average of general borrowing costs, excluding specific borrowings until the asset is substantially complete) × expenditure (Ind AS 23.14)
Commencement When expenditure is incurred, borrowing costs are incurred and activities to prepare the asset have begun (Ind AS 23.17)
Suspension During extended periods when active development is suspended (Ind AS 23.20)
Cessation When substantially all activities to prepare the asset are complete (Ind AS 23.22)
Exchange differences Included to the extent regarded as an adjustment to interest costs (Ind AS 23.6(e))

Borrowing costs include interest computed using the effective interest method, finance charges on lease liabilities, and exchange differences as above. The unwinding of discount on Ind AS 37 provisions is described as a borrowing cost but is generally not capitalised into a qualifying asset.

Model implementation

In a project model, tag every capex line as "capitalisable", "Ind AS 2 trial cost" or "opex". Capitalised interest is computed in the debt schedule as a separate line and routed to CWIP until the commissioning date flag switches; after that, interest goes to P&L. The tax model treats capitalised interest under the proviso to Sec 36(1)(iii) (interest till the asset is first put to use is capitalised for tax as well) and Sec 43(1) Explanation 8.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Stabilisation period: a plant commissioned but running at 40% capacity for six months — costs after it is capable of operating as intended are expensed, even if management calls it stabilisation.
  • Testing vs ramp-up: assessing whether output meets quality specifications is testing; adjusting a process to improve yield is generally post-commissioning operation.
  • Blocked GST on construction of a factory building is capitalised; GST on plant and machinery with ITC is not.
  • Advance payment interest: if a vendor offers deferred credit beyond normal terms, the difference between the cash price equivalent and total payment is interest (Ind AS 16.23), recognised over the credit period unless capitalised under Ind AS 23.
  • Suspension vs normal delay: monsoon delays that are a normal part of construction do not suspend capitalisation (Ind AS 23.21); a funding dispute or litigation stoppage does.
  • Government grants related to assets: Ind AS 20 permits deducting the grant from cost or presenting deferred income — this is a policy choice affecting reported cost.

Key takeaways

  • Cost = purchase price (net of recoverable taxes and discounts) + directly attributable costs + initial dismantling estimate.
  • Site preparation, installation, testing and professional fees are directly attributable; general overheads, start-up and initial operating losses are not.
  • Capitalisation stops when the asset is capable of operating as intended, not when it reaches full capacity.
  • Since 2022, sale proceeds and cost of items produced before intended use go to P&L, with disclosure under Ind AS 16.74A(d).
  • Borrowing costs on qualifying assets are capitalised from commencement to substantial completion, net of temporary investment income, with suspension for extended halts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify cost items for a solar park [L1]

Surya Renewables Ltd builds a 100 MW solar park. Classify each item as capitalised or expensed with the Ind AS reference: land levelling ₹4 crore; module cost ₹220 crore plus IGST ₹11 crore (ITC available); grid connectivity charges paid to the transmission utility ₹6 crore; inauguration event ₹0.8 crore; project head's salary ₹1.2 crore; group HR allocation ₹0.6 crore; insurance during construction ₹1.5 crore; performance testing by an independent engineer ₹0.9 crore; electricity sold during commissioning tests ₹2.4 crore.

Q2. Borrowing cost capitalisation with suspension [L2]

Krishna Infra Ltd borrows ₹200 crore at 9.5% p.a. specifically for a toll road on 1 April 2025. ₹80 crore is spent immediately; the balance is invested in a liquid fund earning 6.5% until spent on 1 October 2025. Construction is halted from 1 December 2025 to 31 January 2026 due to a court stay. The road is substantially complete on 31 March 2026. General borrowings of ₹500 crore carry a weighted rate of 10%; additional expenditure of ₹40 crore funded from general borrowings is incurred on 1 January 2026.

Compute borrowing costs to be capitalised for FY26 and pass the journal entry.

💡 Hint: Treat the suspension period separately for both specific and general borrowings, and net investment income only on specific funds.

Q3. Trial production before and after the 2022 amendment [L2]

A cement plant incurs ₹12 crore on trial-run inputs and sells trial clinker for ₹10 crore before the plant is capable of operating as intended. Testing fees are ₹1.5 crore. Show the accounting under the pre-2022 practice of netting proceeds against cost and under current Ind AS 16.20A, including the effect on capitalised cost, FY revenue/other income, and the disclosure required.

Q4. Commissioning date judgement [L3]

A pharmaceutical formulations plant passed mechanical completion on 1 November 2025, received regulatory (USFDA) approval on 15 February 2026 and reached 80% capacity on 31 March 2026. Costs between November and February included validation batches (destroyed), staff salaries and power. Argue when capitalisation should stop, what costs between these dates may be capitalised, and what audit evidence supports your conclusion.

Q5. Deferred payment terms [L2]

Equipment with a cash price of ₹50 crore is bought on 1 April 2025 under terms requiring ₹28 crore on 31 March 2026 and ₹28 crore on 31 March 2027. The asset is not a qualifying asset. Compute the initial cost, the interest expense for FY26 and FY27, and pass the entries.
