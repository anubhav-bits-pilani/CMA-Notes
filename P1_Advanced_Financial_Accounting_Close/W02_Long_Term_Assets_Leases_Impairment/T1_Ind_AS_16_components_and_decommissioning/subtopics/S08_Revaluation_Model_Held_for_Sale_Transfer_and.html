Revaluation Model, Held for Sale Transfer and Derecognition

Scope: Revaluation model, transfer to held for sale (Ind AS 105), derecognition gains/losses

Why it matters

Most Indian listed companies use the cost model, but the revaluation model appears in hotels, real estate-heavy groups and some PSUs for land and buildings, and its mechanics (OCI surplus, deferred tax, transfers to retained earnings) are tested in CMA and interviews. Asset sales — closing a plant, monetising land, selling towers to an InvIT — trigger Ind AS 105 held-for-sale classification, which stops depreciation and changes presentation. Derecognition gains/losses affect EBITDA definitions and are often shown as exceptional items. Analysts and FP&A teams need to strip these one-offs from run-rate earnings.

Core notes

Revaluation model (Ind AS 16.31–42)

After recognition, an item of PPE whose fair value can be measured reliably may be carried at a revalued amount: fair value at the date of revaluation less subsequent accumulated depreciation and impairment (Ind AS 16.31). Key rules:

  • Class-wide: if an item is revalued, the entire class to which it belongs is revalued (Ind AS 16.36) — e.g. all freehold land, or all buildings — to avoid selective revaluation.
  • Frequency: sufficiently regular that carrying amount does not differ materially from fair value at the reporting date (Ind AS 16.31, 16.34) — annually for volatile items, every 3–5 years for stable ones.
  • Fair value is measured under Ind AS 113 (market approach for land; income or cost approach for specialised buildings).
  • Accumulated depreciation at revaluation is either restated proportionately with the gross carrying amount, or eliminated against the gross carrying amount (Ind AS 16.35).
  • Increase: credited to OCI and accumulated in equity as revaluation surplus, except to the extent it reverses a decrease previously recognised in P&L for the same asset (Ind AS 16.39).
  • Decrease: recognised in P&L, except debited to OCI to the extent of any credit balance in revaluation surplus for that asset (Ind AS 16.40).
  • Transfer of surplus to retained earnings: may be made when the asset is derecognised, or as the asset is used (the difference between depreciation on revalued amount and on original cost) — directly, not through P&L (Ind AS 16.41).
  • Deferred tax on revaluation is recognised in OCI (Ind AS 12.61A, 12.62).

Companies Act restrictions: revaluation surplus is not a free reserve — dividends are paid only out of profits (Sec 123, which excludes unrealised gains and remeasurements), and bonus shares cannot be issued by capitalising reserves created by revaluation of assets (proviso to Sec 63(1)). Where a company revalues, Schedule III requires disclosure of whether the revaluation was based on a registered valuer's valuation (as defined in Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017).

Worked revaluation example

Ooty Resorts Ltd's hotel building: cost ₹80 crore on 1 April 2020, life 40 years, nil residual. On 1 April 2025 (carrying amount ₹70 crore after 5 years) it is revalued to ₹105 crore by a registered valuer; remaining life 35 years. Tax rate 25.168%. Elimination method.

Transfer to held for sale (Ind AS 105)

A non-current asset (or disposal group) is classified as held for sale if its carrying amount will be recovered principally through a sale transaction rather than continuing use (Ind AS 105.6). Criteria (Ind AS 105.7–8):

  • available for immediate sale in its present condition subject to usual terms;
  • sale highly probable: management committed to a plan, active programme to locate a buyer initiated, actively marketed at a reasonable price, expected to qualify for recognition as a completed sale within one year (extensions allowed in limited circumstances, Ind AS 105.9), and unlikely that significant changes to the plan will be made or the plan withdrawn;
  • for distribution to owners, a similar test (Ind AS 105.12A).

Measurement:

  1. Immediately before classification, measure the asset under applicable Ind AS — for revalued PPE, revalue it to fair value with movements to OCI/P&L as usual (Ind AS 105.18).
  2. On classification, measure at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell (Ind AS 105.15). Write-downs are impairment losses in P&L (Ind AS 105.20); subsequent increases in FVLCTS are gains, but not beyond cumulative impairment losses recognised (Ind AS 105.21).
  3. Depreciation ceases from the date of classification (Ind AS 105.25).
  4. Presentation: separately on the face of the balance sheet as "Assets classified as held for sale" (current), and associated liabilities separately (Ind AS 105.38). Schedule III requires this line within current assets. If the disposal group is a component that is a separate major line of business, results are presented as discontinued operations (Ind AS 105.32–33).

If the criteria cease to be met, the asset is measured at the lower of (a) carrying amount before classification adjusted for depreciation/revaluation that would have been recognised and (b) recoverable amount at the date of the decision not to sell (Ind AS 105.27).

Derecognition (Ind AS 16.67–72)

The carrying amount of PPE is derecognised on disposal or when no future economic benefits are expected from its use or disposal (Ind AS 16.67). The gain or loss:

\[ \text{Gain/(loss)} = \text{Net disposal proceeds} - \text{Carrying amount} \]

  • Recognised in P&L when the item is derecognised; gains shall not be classified as revenue (Ind AS 16.68). Exception: entities that routinely sell PPE previously held for rental (e.g. car rental fleets) transfer them to inventory at carrying amount when they cease to be rented and recognise sale proceeds as revenue under Ind AS 115 (Ind AS 16.68A).
  • Date of disposal is when the recipient obtains control under Ind AS 115 (Ind AS 16.69).
  • Consideration is measured under the transaction price requirements of Ind AS 115 (Ind AS 16.72); deferred consideration is discounted.
  • On derecognition, any revaluation surplus for the asset is transferred directly to retained earnings — not recycled to P&L.

Worked derecognition example

Continuing Ooty Resorts: the building is classified as held for sale on 1 October 2026. Carrying amount after FY26 and half of FY27 depreciation = 105 − 3 − 1.5 = ₹100.5 crore. Fair value less costs to sell = ₹98 crore. Impairment loss of ₹2.5 crore to P&L on classification (after first revaluing to fair value if it differed materially). Depreciation stops. Sold on 31 January 2027 for ₹99.2 crore net of costs: gain of ₹1.2 crore in P&L (other income). The remaining revaluation surplus is transferred to retained earnings.

Presentation in India

  • Gains/losses on sale of PPE are shown in other income/other expenses; material ones as exceptional items (Schedule III permits an exceptional items line; Ind AS 1.97–98 requires separate disclosure of material items).
  • Cash flow: proceeds from sale of PPE are investing inflows; the gain is deducted in reconciling profit to operating cash flow (indirect method).
  • Tax: on sale of an asset in a block, proceeds reduce the WDV of the block; capital gains arise only if the block ceases or proceeds exceed block WDV (Sec 50). Land is a capital asset outside the depreciable block.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Revaluation of one building only is not permitted — the class rule applies, although a class can be defined narrowly (e.g. "hotel buildings" vs "office buildings") if genuinely distinct.
  • Impairment of revalued assets is a revaluation decrease (debit surplus first), consistent with Ind AS 36.61.
  • Held-for-sale beyond 12 months — permissible only if delays are caused by events outside the entity's control and the entity remains committed (Ind AS 105 Appendix B).
  • Abandonment is not held for sale; the asset continues to be depreciated until abandoned, and may be impaired (Ind AS 105.13).
  • Sale and leaseback of property is accounted for under Ind AS 116.98–103 — gain restricted to rights transferred (covered in t2).
  • Scrapped assets with no proceeds are derecognised at full carrying amount loss; scrap sales are disposal proceeds.
  • Slump sales under Sec 50B: tax computation of the undertaking differs from the book gain; reconcile in the tax note.

Key takeaways

  • The revaluation model applies class-wide, with increases to OCI (revaluation surplus) and decreases to P&L after exhausting the surplus.
  • Revaluation surplus transfers to retained earnings directly, never through P&L; it cannot fund dividends or bonus issues.
  • Held-for-sale assets are measured at lower of carrying amount and FVLCTS, stop depreciating and are presented separately.
  • Derecognition gain/loss = net disposal proceeds − carrying amount; it is not revenue, except for routinely sold rental assets under Ind AS 16.68A.
  • Material disposal gains/losses are presented as exceptional items and removed from run-rate EBITDA in analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Revaluation increase after a prior decrease [L2]

Land carried under the revaluation model at ₹40 crore was revalued down to ₹34 crore in FY25 (no surplus existed). In FY26 it is revalued to ₹46 crore. Pass the entries for FY25 and FY26, including deferred tax at 25.168% on the OCI portion.

Q2. Held for sale with costs to sell [L2]

A plant with carrying amount ₹55 crore (cost model) meets the held-for-sale criteria on 1 January 2026; fair value is ₹52 crore and costs to sell ₹1.5 crore. At 31 March 2026, fair value less costs to sell is ₹54 crore. Pass the entries on classification and at year-end and state the balance sheet presentation.

💡 Hint: Subsequent gains on held-for-sale assets are capped by cumulative impairment previously recognised.

Q3. Derecognition with deferred consideration [L2]

A company sells a warehouse (carrying amount ₹18 crore) on 1 April 2025 for ₹10 crore immediately and ₹12 crore on 31 March 2027. The appropriate discount rate is 9%. Compute the gain on disposal, the interest income in FY26 and FY27, and show the cash flow classification.

Q4. Rental fleet sales [L1]

A car rental company sells 400 cars each year after 3 years of rental use. Explain how Ind AS 16.68A changes the presentation of proceeds and the cash flow classification compared with an ordinary disposal.

Q5. Plan to sell a hotel subsidiary's property [L3]

A listed group announces at its Board meeting on 15 March 2026 a plan to sell a hotel (carrying amount ₹320 crore, revaluation surplus ₹90 crore) to a REIT; due diligence starts in April 2026 and SEBI/REIT approvals are expected in 10 months. Evaluate whether held-for-sale classification applies at 31 March 2026, the measurement and presentation consequences either way, and whether discontinued operations presentation could apply if the hotel is one of 25 hotels.
