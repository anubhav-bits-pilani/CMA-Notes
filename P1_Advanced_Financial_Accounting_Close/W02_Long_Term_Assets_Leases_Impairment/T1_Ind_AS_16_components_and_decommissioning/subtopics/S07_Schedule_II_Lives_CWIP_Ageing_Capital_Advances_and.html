Subtopic note

Schedule II Lives, CWIP Ageing, Capital Advances and Spares

Scope: Schedule II Part C useful lives vs technically assessed lives, CWIP ageing schedule, capital advances, spares classification

Why it matters

These are the "Indian overlay" items in every PPE note. Schedule II of the Companies Act 2013 sets benchmark useful lives that most companies adopt; departures require technical justification and disclosure. The 2021 amendment to Schedule III added a mandatory CWIP ageing schedule and completion schedule for overdue/over-budget projects, exposing stuck projects to scrutiny. Capital advances are a classic misclassification (current vs non-current), and spare parts sit on the boundary between inventory and PPE. Each of these items is checked in statutory audit, in CARO 2020 reporting (clause 3(i) on PPE records and physical verification) and by analysts examining capital efficiency.

Core notes

Schedule II Part C vs technically assessed lives

Schedule II, Part A:

  • Depreciation is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount over useful life; the depreciable amount is cost less residual value.
  • The useful life of an asset shall not ordinarily be different from Part C and the residual value shall not be more than 5% of original cost. If a company uses a different life or residual value, it shall disclose the justification, supported by a technical advice (Part A, para 3(i) for companies generally; for specified regulated classes, lives notified by the regulator apply — e.g. CERC tariff regulations for power, under para 3(ii)).
  • Useful life of significant parts is determined separately (Part A Note 4) — see s02.

Selected Part C lives (single shift, years):

Asset class Part C life
Buildings (RCC frame, other than factory) 60
Factory buildings 30
Plant and machinery (general, continuous process plant other than specified) 15 (continuous process plant 25)
Computers — end-user devices (desktops, laptops) 3
Servers and networks 6
Furniture and fittings (general) 10
Motor cars (other than used in rental business) 8
Office equipment 5
Roads (carpeted, RCC) 10
Motor buses, lorries, cars and taxis used in a business of running them on hire 6

Lives are for single-shift working; double shift raises depreciation by 50% and triple by 100% for the relevant period, except for assets marked NESD (no extra shift depreciation).

Technically assessed lives: a company may adopt a longer or shorter life based on a technical assessment considering usage, maintenance, technology and legal limits (Ind AS 16.56). Disclosure is required: the assessed life, the fact that it differs from Schedule II and the basis. Common departures: vehicles (5 years vs 8), mobile phones (2 years), plant in harsh environments (shorter), well-maintained buildings (longer), telecom towers (25–35 years vs 15 for plant). For intangible assets, Schedule II does not prescribe lives — Ind AS 38 governs.

CWIP ageing schedule (Schedule III, 2021 amendment)

Capital work-in-progress is presented separately on the face of the balance sheet. Schedule III (Division II, as amended on 24 March 2021, effective FY 2021-22) requires:

(a) CWIP ageing schedule:

CWIP < 1 year 1–2 years 2–3 years > 3 years Total
Projects in progress
Projects temporarily suspended

(b) Completion schedule for CWIP whose completion is overdue or has exceeded its cost compared to its original plan, project-wise, showing amounts expected to be completed in < 1, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years. Details of projects temporarily suspended are given separately.

Ageing is by the date the expenditure was incurred (ICAI Guidance Note on Schedule III). Similar ageing applies to intangible assets under development.

Rules: transfer from CWIP to PPE on the date the asset is capable of operating as intended (s01); stop capitalising borrowing costs at substantial completion; review long-suspended projects for impairment (Ind AS 36 indicators) and for suspension of borrowing-cost capitalisation (Ind AS 23.20).

Capital advances

Advances paid for acquisition of PPE are not CWIP and not current assets. Schedule III requires capital advances to be shown under Other non-current assets separately (Schedule III Part I, Note on Other non-current assets), irrespective of expected delivery date, because they will be converted into non-current assets. In cash flow statements they are investing outflows. Audit checks: ageing of capital advances, recoverability where suppliers have defaulted, and related party nature.

Contrast with creditors for capital goods, which are presented within other financial liabilities (not trade payables).

Spares classification (Ind AS 16.8)

Spare parts, stand-by equipment and servicing equipment are recognised as PPE when they meet the definition of PPE — i.e. held for use in production or supply and expected to be used during more than one period. Otherwise, they are inventory (Ind AS 2) and expensed when consumed.

Type Classification Depreciation
Routine consumable spares (bearings, filters) used within a year Inventory (stores and spares) Expensed on issue
Critical/insurance spares for a specific machine, expected life > 1 year PPE From the date available for use (often with the parent asset) over the shorter of spare's life and parent's remaining life
Stand-by equipment (spare transformer, standby DG) PPE From available for use
Servicing equipment (special tools) PPE if > 1 period SLM over life

The Ind AS treatment differs from old AS 2/AS 10 practice, where only machinery spares used "only in connection with" a specific item and irregular in use were capitalised. Under Ind AS 16.8 the test is simpler: the PPE definition and multi-period use. When a capitalised spare is fitted, transfer it to the parent asset and derecognise the part replaced (s04).

Model implementation

In Excel, build CWIP ageing via SUMIFS on a transaction-level register with an ageing bucket column: =IFS(DATEDIF(TxnDate,RepDate,"m")<12,"<1",DATEDIF(...)<24,"1-2",DATEDIF(...)<36,"2-3",TRUE,">3"). The FP&A capex forecast should show CWIP opening, spend, capitalisation and closing, with capitalisation dates driving the start of depreciation in the PPE schedule. In Power BI, a CWIP ageing visual with drill-through to projects is a common management dashboard.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ageing a project with multiple phases: age expenditure by incurrence date, not by project start date.
  • Revised budgets: "exceeded cost compared to original plan" uses the original plan, so a revised board-approved budget does not avoid the completion schedule disclosure.
  • Capital advances for land to related parties often hide fund diversion — CARO and NFRA focus.
  • Spares acquired with the machine and expected to be used over its life are capitalised with the asset; spares held for sale or repair contracts are inventory.
  • Schedule II for regulated entities — use the regulator's lives (e.g. CERC) where the Companies Act allows.
  • Ind AS vs Schedule II conflict: Ind AS 16 requires lives reflecting consumption; Schedule II is a ceiling/benchmark — technical assessment resolves the tension and must be disclosed.

Key takeaways

  • Schedule II Part C lives and the 5% residual cap are benchmarks; departures need technical justification and disclosure.
  • Schedule III requires CWIP ageing (in progress vs suspended, four buckets) and a completion schedule for overdue/over-budget projects measured against the original plan.
  • Capital advances are always "other non-current assets"; creditors for capital goods are other financial liabilities.
  • Spares, stand-by and servicing equipment are PPE when expected to be used over more than one period; otherwise inventory.
  • A project-level CWIP register is the basis for ageing, capitalisation and impairment reviews.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify year-end balances [L1]

Classify each balance for a Schedule III balance sheet at 31 March 2026: (a) advance of ₹5 crore to a contractor for a factory building; (b) payable of ₹3 crore to a machinery supplier; (c) spare gearbox ₹1.2 crore for a specific kiln, expected life 6 years; (d) lubricants ₹0.4 crore; (e) CWIP of ₹22 crore for a warehouse substantially complete but not yet capitalised because the occupancy certificate is pending.

Q2. Prepare a CWIP ageing and completion schedule [L2]

A pharmaceutical company has three projects at 31 March 2026 (₹ crore). Project A: spend FY24 10, FY25 18, FY26 12; original completion March 2026, now September 2026; original budget 45. Project B: spend FY26 30; on schedule. Project C: spend FY22 8, FY23 4; suspended since FY23. Prepare both Schedule III tables and the narrative disclosure.

💡 Hint: Projects overdue or over their original budget go into the completion schedule; suspended projects are shown separately in both tables.

Q3. Departing from Schedule II [L2]

A logistics company proposes depreciating trucks over 5 years (Part C: 6 years for motor lorries used in a business of running them on hire) and laptops over 2 years (Part C: 3 years), with a 10% residual on trucks. List the evidence required, draft the accounting policy disclosure, and compute FY26 depreciation on a truck fleet costing ₹60 crore acquired on 1 April 2025 under both the Schedule II benchmark (5% residual) and the proposed estimate.

Q4. Suspended project review [L3]

A ₹180 crore CWIP balance relates to a steel plant expansion suspended for 30 months due to a land dispute; management continues to capitalise interest of ₹14 crore per year. Evaluate the accounting under Ind AS 23 and Ind AS 36, the Schedule III disclosures, and the CARO 2020 and audit reporting considerations.

Q5. Excel build: ageing buckets [L2]

Write the Excel formulas to age a CWIP transaction register (columns: Project, Txn date, Amount, Status) into Schedule III buckets as at a reporting date in cell B1, and to produce a pivot-ready summary by status and bucket using SUMIFS.
