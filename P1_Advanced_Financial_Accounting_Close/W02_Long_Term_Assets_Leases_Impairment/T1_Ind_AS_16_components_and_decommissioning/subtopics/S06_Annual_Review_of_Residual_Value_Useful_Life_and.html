Subtopic note

Annual Review of Residual Value, Useful Life and Method

Scope: Annual review of residual value, useful life and method (Ind AS 16.51, 16.61) as prospective estimate changes

Why it matters

Depreciation is an estimate, and Ind AS forces the estimate to be refreshed every year. Small changes have large effects: extending the life of a ₹5,000 crore power plant from 25 to 30 years can lift annual EBIT by over ₹30 crore. Analysts watch for life extensions that coincide with weak results; auditors ask for technical evidence; NFRA has questioned unexplained revisions. In FP&A, the review affects the depreciation forecast, and in modelling it determines whether depreciation is recomputed on the remaining carrying amount or on the original cost. Getting the prospective mechanics right is essential.

Core notes

The requirements

  • Residual value and useful life shall be reviewed at least at each financial year-end, and if expectations differ from previous estimates, the change(s) shall be accounted for as a change in an accounting estimate in accordance with Ind AS 8 (Ind AS 16.51).
  • Depreciation method shall be reviewed at least at each financial year-end; if there has been a significant change in the expected pattern of consumption of future economic benefits, the method shall be changed to reflect the changed pattern, and such a change is also accounted for as a change in accounting estimate (Ind AS 16.61).
  • Depreciation is recognised even if fair value exceeds carrying amount, as long as residual value does not exceed carrying amount (Ind AS 16.52); if residual value increases to an amount equal to or greater than carrying amount, depreciation is zero until it falls below (Ind AS 16.54).
  • A depreciation method based on revenue generated by an activity that includes the use of an asset is not appropriate (Ind AS 16.62A).

Definitions

  • Residual value: the estimated amount an entity would currently obtain from disposal, after deducting estimated costs of disposal, if the asset were already of the age and in the condition expected at the end of its useful life (Ind AS 16.6). It is measured in current prices, not inflated future prices.
  • Useful life: the period over which an asset is expected to be available for use by the entity, or the number of production or similar units expected (Ind AS 16.6). Factors (Ind AS 16.56): expected usage, physical wear and tear (shifts, repair programme), technical or commercial obsolescence (including expected reductions in selling price of output), legal or similar limits (lease expiry, licence terms).
  • Useful life is entity-specific — it can be shorter than economic life if the entity disposes of assets after a set period (Ind AS 16.57), e.g. a car rental company replacing fleet every 3 years.

Prospective application under Ind AS 8

Ind AS 8.36 requires the effect of a change in estimate to be recognised prospectively by including it in P&L of the period of change (if it affects that period only) or the period of change and future periods. Practically for PPE:

\[ \text{Revised annual depreciation} = \frac{\text{Carrying amount at date of change} - \text{Revised residual value}}{\text{Remaining useful life}} \]

Where the review occurs at year-end, entities typically apply the revision from the beginning of the current year (if the information existed throughout) or from the date of change; the policy should be consistent. Many Indian companies apply revised estimates from the start of the year in which the review concludes, treating the year-end review as evidence of conditions existing during the year — acceptable if justified, but be explicit.

No restatement: prior-year depreciation and opening retained earnings are not adjusted. Disclose the nature and amount of the change affecting the current period and expected future effect if practicable (Ind AS 8.39–40, Ind AS 16.76).

Change in estimate vs error

An estimate change results from new information or developments. If the original estimate was unreasonable based on information available when the financial statements were approved (e.g. ignoring a known licence expiry), the correction is an error — retrospective restatement under Ind AS 8.42. Distinguishing the two requires evidence of what was known when.

Worked illustration

Yamuna Power Ltd's turbine: cost ₹240 crore on 1 April 2020, original life 20 years, residual value ₹12 crore (5%), SLM. On 31 March 2026 a technical study (applied from 1 April 2025) concludes total life is 25 years and residual value ₹6 crore.

Original annual depreciation = (240 − 12) ÷ 20 = ₹11.4 crore.

Carrying amount at 1 April 2025 (5 years used) = 240 − 57 = ₹183 crore. Remaining life = 25 − 5 = 20 years.

Revised depreciation from FY26 = (183 − 6) ÷ 20 = ₹8.85 crore.

Change in method

A switch from SLM to WDV (or to units of production) because the consumption pattern has changed is treated similarly: the carrying amount at the date of change is depreciated using the new method over the remaining life. Example: a mine's crushing plant shifts from SLM to units-of-production when ore throughput becomes highly variable. A change in method is not a change in accounting policy under Ind AS (unlike some old Indian GAAP readings that treated it as a policy change under AS 6 before revision).

Evidence for the annual review

  • Engineering/technical assessment reports (internal technical teams or chartered engineers).
  • Utilisation data (shifts, hours, cycles), maintenance history and planned replacement programmes.
  • Market evidence of second-hand prices for residual value (fleet, aircraft, vehicles).
  • Regulatory or contractual limits (PPA tenure for power plants, concession period for toll roads — note Appendix D to Ind AS 115 service concession assets, licence terms for telecom spectrum).
  • Technology roadmaps (e.g. 4G equipment obsolescence with 5G rollout, ICE vehicle tooling with EV transition).

Document conclusions annually, even where no change results, so auditors can see the review occurred.

Model implementation

In an asset-level model, depreciation in each year should be computed as:

=IF(Year<ChangeYear, (Cost-OrigRV)/OrigLife,
    (CA_at_change-RevRV)/RemainingLife)

with a cap so carrying amount never falls below revised residual value: =MIN(computed_dep, CA_open - RevRV). For portfolio models, apply revised lives to the remaining net block by vintage. Sensitivity tables on life (±2 years) and residual (±2% of cost) help explain depreciation forecasts to management.

Schedule II interplay

Schedule II, Part A permits useful lives different from Part C if technically justified and disclosed (c04). An annual review that moves a life away from Part C must be supported by a technical assessment, and the financial statements must disclose the justification. Residual value above 5% of original cost also requires justification. The notes to Schedule II Part C also prescribe depreciation on extra-shift working (increase by 50% for double shift and 100% for triple shift for assets not marked "NESD"), now largely superseded where entities use technically assessed lives reflecting shift usage.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Residual value at or above carrying amount (e.g. land-heavy hotels, aircraft in strong secondary markets) stops depreciation — challenge whether residual value is in current prices.
  • Idle assets continue to be depreciated (Ind AS 16.55) unless fully depreciated or held for sale; units-of-production may produce zero depreciation in idle periods.
  • Mid-year revisions: if a significant event (e.g. regulatory ban on a product line) occurs mid-year, apply the change from that date and consider impairment (Ind AS 36.17 — an impairment indicator also signals review of life and residual value).
  • Concession assets: lives cannot exceed the concession period unless renewal is reasonably certain.
  • ROU assets follow the same review logic but are bounded by lease term (Ind AS 116.31–32).
  • Tax: Sec 32 rates are unaffected; changes widen or narrow the book-tax temporary difference.

Key takeaways

  • Review residual value, useful life and depreciation method at least every year-end.
  • All three revisions are changes in accounting estimates — prospective, never restated.
  • Revised depreciation = (carrying amount − revised RV) ÷ remaining life.
  • Residual value is in current prices; useful life is entity-specific.
  • Revenue-based depreciation is not permitted; method changes need a changed consumption pattern.
  • Distinguish estimate changes from errors using information available at the time.

Practice Questions

Q1. Life reduction and residual change [L1]

A machine costing ₹30 crore on 1 April 2022 has an original life of 10 years and residual value ₹1.5 crore. On 1 April 2025 its total life is revised to 8 years and residual value to nil. Compute depreciation for FY26 and the effect on FY26 P&L compared with the old estimate.

Q2. Method change mid-life [L2]

A coal-handling plant (cost ₹120 crore on 1 April 2021, SLM over 15 years, nil residual) is switched to units-of-production from 1 April 2025 because throughput will vary sharply. Remaining expected throughput is 48 million tonnes: FY26 6 MT, FY27 9 MT. Compute depreciation for FY26 and FY27 and draft the Ind AS 8 disclosure.

Q3. Estimate change or error [L3]

In FY26, a company discovers that telecom equipment depreciated over 10 years should have been depreciated over 6 years because the vendor's end-of-support notice was received in FY23 (before FY23 financial statements were approved), but the accounts team did not act on it. Determine whether this is an estimate change or a prior-period error, describe the accounting, and list the disclosures.

💡 Hint: Ask what information was available when the earlier financial statements were approved for issue.

Q4. Residual value exceeds carrying amount [L2]

An aircraft's carrying amount at 1 April 2025 is ₹210 crore; the latest appraisal shows a residual value (current prices, age and condition at end of life) of ₹215 crore. What is FY26 depreciation? Explain the rule and the evidence an auditor would scrutinise.

Q5. FP&A sensitivity [L3]

A cement company's net block of ₹6,000 crore (average remaining life 18 years, residual 5% of ₹9,000 crore gross block) is under review. Management proposes extending average remaining life to 22 years. Build the specification of an Excel sensitivity table showing FY27 depreciation and EBIT for remaining lives of 16–24 years and residual values of 0–8% of gross block, and list the questions you would ask before accepting the change.
