Subtopic note

Decommissioning and Restoration Costs in PPE (Ind AS 16.16(c))

Scope: Decommissioning/restoration in cost (Ind AS 16.16(c)): telecom towers, mines, leasehold restoration; subsequent estimate changes

Why it matters

Telecom tower companies, oil and gas producers, coal and iron-ore miners, renewable developers on leased land and retailers in leased premises all carry obligations to dismantle assets and restore sites. These obligations can be large (an offshore platform's abandonment cost runs to thousands of crore) and very long-dated, so their measurement is dominated by discount-rate and inflation assumptions. The accounting adds an asset, a provision and two P&L lines (depreciation and unwinding), and every change in estimate flows through the asset rather than P&L under the cost model. Errors in rate, inflation or remeasurement mechanics are frequent audit findings.

Core notes

The rule

Cost of PPE includes the initial estimate of the costs of dismantling and removing the item and restoring the site on which it is located, the obligation for which an entity incurs either when the item is acquired or as a consequence of having used the item during a particular period for purposes other than to produce inventories during that period (Ind AS 16.16(c)). The obligation is measured under Ind AS 37 (Ind AS 16.18). Obligations incurred through production of inventory (e.g. incremental damage from extracting ore in a period) are accounted for under Ind AS 2 as a production cost.

Recognition conditions (Ind AS 37.14)

  • Present obligation from a past event: legal (licence condition, lease clause, mining lease under MMDR Act and the approved mine closure plan, environmental clearance) or constructive (published policy, consistent practice of restoring sites).
  • Probable outflow — almost always when legally required.
  • Reliable estimate — engineering cost studies.

The past event is the construction/installation (for towers, platforms) or the disturbance caused (for mines — the obligation grows as more land is disturbed, so additional obligations are added over time).

Measurement

  1. Estimate future cash outflows at the expected date of decommissioning: current cost estimate × \( (1+\text{inflation})^{n} \), or directly estimate in nominal terms.
  2. Discount at a pre-tax rate reflecting current market assessments of time value and risks specific to the liability (Ind AS 37.45–47). Common practice in India: G-sec yield of a matching tenor (nominal) with risk captured in cash flows. Use a real rate only with real (uninflated) cash flows.
  3. Initial entry: Dr PPE (decommissioning asset), Cr Provision for decommissioning, at PV.
  4. Depreciate the decommissioning asset over the related asset's useful life (or the period of the obligation).
  5. Unwind the discount annually: Dr Finance cost, Cr Provision (Ind AS 37.60).

Worked illustration — telecom tower portfolio

Bharat Towers Ltd erects 1,000 rooftop towers on 1 April 2025 at ₹0.5 crore each (₹500 crore). Leases require removal and restoration at the end of 15 years at an estimated ₹3 lakh per tower in today's prices; cost inflation 5%; pre-tax risk-free rate (15-year G-sec) 7.0%.

Future cost per tower = 3 × 1.05^15 = ₹6.24 lakh; portfolio = ₹62.37 crore.

PV = 62.37 ÷ 1.07^15 = 62.37 ÷ 2.7590 = ₹22.61 crore.

Account Dr (₹ crore) Cr (₹ crore)
PPE — towers 500.00
PPE — decommissioning asset 22.61
Bank / Creditors 500.00
Provision for decommissioning 22.61

FY26: depreciation of decommissioning asset = 22.61 ÷ 15 = ₹1.51 crore; unwinding = 22.61 × 7% = ₹1.58 crore; closing provision ₹24.19 crore.

Subsequent estimate changes (Appendix A to Ind AS 16 / Ind AS 37 interaction)

Changes in the estimated timing or amount of outflows, or in the discount rate, are accounted for as follows (the Ind AS equivalent of IFRIC 1, located in Appendix A to Ind AS 16, "Changes in Existing Decommissioning, Restoration and Similar Liabilities"):

Cost model:

  • Changes are added to or deducted from the cost of the related asset in the current period.
  • A decrease deducted from cost shall not exceed the asset's carrying amount; any excess is recognised immediately in P&L.
  • If an increase results in an addition to cost, consider whether it indicates the asset's new carrying amount may not be fully recoverable — if so, test for impairment under Ind AS 36.
  • The adjusted depreciable amount is depreciated prospectively over the remaining useful life.
  • Unwinding (periodic accretion) is not an estimate change; it is always finance cost in P&L and is not capitalised under Ind AS 23.
  • Once the related asset reaches the end of its useful life, all subsequent changes go to P&L.

Revaluation model: changes alter the revaluation surplus/deficit previously recognised: a decrease in the liability is credited to OCI (revaluation surplus) except to the extent it reverses a previous revaluation deficit in P&L; an increase is debited to OCI to the extent of any surplus, then to P&L. A decrease beyond the carrying amount had the asset been carried under the cost model is taken to P&L. A change may also indicate that the asset needs revaluation.

Continuing the illustration: on 31 March 2027 (13 years remaining) the per-tower estimate rises to ₹4 lakh at today's prices and the rate falls to 6.8%. Provision before remeasurement = 24.19 × 1.07 = ₹25.88 crore (after FY27 unwinding). Revised PV = 1,000 × 4 × 1.05^13 ÷ 1.068^13 lakh = 4,000 × 1.8856 ÷ 2.3519 lakh ≈ ₹32.07 crore. Increase ≈ ₹6.19 crore: Dr PPE decommissioning asset, Cr Provision; the decommissioning asset's carrying amount (22.61 − 3.02 + 6.19) is depreciated over the remaining 13 years.

Leasehold restoration and Ind AS 116 interplay

Restoration of the leased premises to their original condition (e.g. removing partitions installed by the landlord at the lessee's request, or reinstating the premises as required by the lease) is included in the ROU asset under Ind AS 116.24(d). Removal of the lessee's own fit-outs (leasehold improvements recognised as PPE) is included in the cost of those improvements under Ind AS 16.16(c). Both are depreciated over the lease term if shorter than useful life.

Presentation and disclosure

  • Provision split current/non-current per expected timing (Schedule III).
  • Ind AS 37.84 roll-forward: opening, additions, unwinding (increase from passage of time and effect of discount-rate changes), utilisation, reversals, closing.
  • Disclose discount rate, inflation, expected timing and uncertainty (Ind AS 37.85).
  • Ind AS 7: the decommissioning asset addition is non-cash; actual decommissioning spend is usually operating cash outflow.
  • Ind AS 12: post the 2023 amendment, deferred tax is recognised on both the decommissioning asset (DTL) and provision (DTA) — the initial recognition exemption no longer applies to transactions giving rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mine closure obligations grow progressively; each new disturbance adds a new layer to the asset and provision, while obligations linked to ore extracted in the period are inventory costs.
  • Timing uncertainty for towers — lease renewals may extend the decommissioning date; align with Ind AS 116 lease-term judgements.
  • Salvage value of dismantled steel: net against the outflow only if part of the same obligation estimate and reliably measurable; otherwise treat as residual value of the asset.
  • Foreign currency obligations (offshore platforms in USD) use USD cash flows and a USD rate; translation differences on the provision under Ind AS 21.
  • Constructive obligations — a published sustainability commitment to restore all sites may create an obligation even without a legal requirement.
  • Fully depreciated assets: subsequent increases go directly to P&L because the asset has no remaining life.

Key takeaways

  • Dismantling and restoration obligations arising from acquisition or use are part of PPE cost, measured at PV under Ind AS 37.
  • Use a pre-tax rate reflecting liability-specific risk, consistent with nominal or real cash flows — never WACC.
  • Unwinding is always finance cost; estimate and rate changes adjust asset cost under the cost model.
  • Decreases beyond carrying amount go to P&L; increases may trigger an impairment test.
  • Leasehold restoration of the premises is part of the ROU asset; removal of lessee fit-outs is part of the leasehold improvement cost.
  • Deferred tax is recognised on both sides after the 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mine closure provision [L2]

Satpura Minerals Ltd begins mining on 1 April 2025. The approved mine closure plan estimates final closure costs of ₹45 crore in 20 years at current prices; inflation 5%; 20-year G-sec yield 7.1%. Compute the initial provision and asset, FY26 unwinding and depreciation (asset life 20 years), and pass the entries.

Q2. Estimate decrease exceeding carrying amount [L2]

A decommissioning asset has a carrying amount of ₹3 crore and the provision ₹12 crore at 31 March 2026. A revised engineering study reduces the provision to ₹7.5 crore. Pass the entry under the cost model and explain where the excess decrease goes. Then show the treatment if the related asset were carried under the revaluation model with a revaluation surplus of ₹4 crore.

💡 Hint: Under the cost model, the deduction from cost is capped at the asset's carrying amount.

Q3. Retail store restoration [L2]

A retailer leases a store for 9 years from 1 April 2025. It installs fit-outs costing ₹2.5 crore (life 10 years). The lease requires removal of fit-outs and restoration to bare shell at the end of the term, estimated at ₹40 lakh today; inflation 6%; pre-tax rate 7.3%. Separately, the lessor's own partitions must be reinstated at ₹10 lakh (today's prices). Identify which amounts go into PPE and which into the ROU asset, compute the initial provision(s), and state the depreciation period.

Q4. Rate change effect and impairment trigger [L3]

At 31 March 2026, a refinery's site restoration provision (₹800 crore, 18 years remaining) is remeasured as the matching G-sec yield falls from 7.3% to 6.6%. Estimate the remeasurement using a single-cash-flow approximation, pass the entry, and explain whether and how the increase should trigger an Ind AS 36 assessment.

Q5. Rate selection in an audit committee note [L3]

The CFO proposes discounting all decommissioning provisions at 11.5% (group post-tax WACC). Write a short note to the audit committee explaining the correct rate under Ind AS 37.47, the effect on the provision and asset, and the disclosure implications, using a ₹100 crore obligation due in 20 years to illustrate the sensitivity.
