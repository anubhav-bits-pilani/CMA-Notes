Key concept

PPE Derecognition Gain or Loss and Its Presentation

Concept: Derecognition gain/loss = Net disposal proceeds − Carrying amount; not classified as revenue (Ind AS 16.68, 16.71)

Statement

On derecognition of an item of PPE (or a part of it):

\[ G = NDP - CA \]

where \( G \) is the gain (if positive) or loss (if negative), \( NDP \) is net disposal proceeds (consideration measured under Ind AS 115 transaction price rules, less directly attributable costs of disposal), and \( CA \) is the carrying amount at the date of derecognition (cost or revalued amount less accumulated depreciation and impairment). The gain or loss is recognised in P&L when the item is derecognised and gains shall not be classified as revenue (Ind AS 16.68, 16.71).

Explanation

Derecognition occurs on disposal (sale, finance lease out, donation) or when no future economic benefits are expected from use or disposal (Ind AS 16.67). The disposal date is when the recipient obtains control under Ind AS 115 (Ind AS 16.69). Consideration is measured under Ind AS 115's transaction price requirements, so variable consideration is constrained and significant financing components are separated (Ind AS 16.72); later changes in estimated variable consideration adjust the gain or loss.

Why not revenue? Revenue arises from the ordinary activities of the entity; disposal of a productive asset is incidental. The exception (Ind AS 16.68A) is entities that routinely sell items previously held for rental — they transfer the assets to inventory at carrying amount when rental ceases and present proceeds as revenue, with cash flows in operating activities (Ind AS 7.14).

Other points:

  • Revaluation surplus attached to the asset is transferred directly to retained earnings, not to P&L.
  • Part derecognition (replacements, inspections) uses the same formula on the part's carrying amount.
  • Presentation in India: other income or other expenses; material items as exceptional items (Ind AS 1.97–98) — common for land sales and plant closures.
  • Cash flow: proceeds are investing inflows; the gain/loss is reversed in the indirect method reconciliation.
  • Tax: proceeds reduce the Sec 32 block; capital gain/loss only under Sec 50 if the block is exhausted, so book gains create temporary differences.

Illustration

Tapi Textiles Ltd sells a spinning machine on 30 September 2025. Cost ₹8 crore (1 April 2019), life 12 years, nil RV, SLM. Sale price ₹3.4 crore; dismantling and brokerage ₹0.15 crore.

Accumulated depreciation to 30 September 2025 = 8 × 6.5 ÷ 12 = ₹4.333 crore; CA = ₹3.667 crore.

\[ G = (3.40 - 0.15) - 3.667 = -₹0.417 \text{ crore (loss)} \]

Practice Questions

Q1. Gain on sale with revaluation surplus [L2]

Land carried at a revalued amount of ₹25 crore (revaluation surplus ₹15 crore, net of deferred tax ₹11.22 crore) is sold for ₹27 crore with brokerage of ₹0.5 crore. Pass the entries for the sale and the treatment of the revaluation surplus.

Q2. Variable consideration on disposal [L2]

A company sells a hotel building (CA ₹60 crore) for ₹55 crore plus an earn-out of up to ₹10 crore payable in two years based on occupancy; the most likely earn-out is ₹6 crore but there is significant uncertainty. Explain how Ind AS 16.72 and Ind AS 115 constraints affect the gain recognised at disposal and later.

💡 Hint: Variable consideration is included only to the extent a significant reversal is highly unlikely.

Q3. Presentation case [L1]

A listed FMCG company reports a ₹140 crore gain on sale of surplus factory land in Q3 FY26. Explain where it should appear in the Schedule III P&L, in SEBI quarterly results, in the cash flow statement and in an analyst's adjusted EBITDA.
