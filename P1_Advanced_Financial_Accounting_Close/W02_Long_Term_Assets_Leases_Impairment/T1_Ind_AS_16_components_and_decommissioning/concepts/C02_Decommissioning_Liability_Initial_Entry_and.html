Key concept

Decommissioning Liability: Initial Entry and Unwinding

Concept: Decommissioning: Dr PPE Cr Provision at PV (pre-tax risk-specific rate); unwinding Dr Finance cost Cr Provision

Statement

At the date the obligation arises:

\[ P_0 = \frac{E \times (1+i)^n}{(1+r)^n} \]

where \( P_0 \) is the initial provision and the amount added to PPE cost, \( E \) is the current-price estimate of decommissioning cost, \( i \) is expected cost inflation, \( n \) is years to settlement, and \( r \) is the pre-tax discount rate reflecting the time value of money and liability-specific risks (Ind AS 37.47).

Each period:

\[ U_t = P_{t-1} \times r, \qquad P_t = P_{t-1} + U_t - \text{Utilisation}_t \pm \Delta\text{Estimate}_t \]

Entries:

Event Dr Cr
Initial recognition PPE — decommissioning asset Provision for decommissioning
Annual unwinding Finance cost Provision for decommissioning
Annual depreciation Depreciation expense Accumulated depreciation
Settlement Provision Bank

Explanation

The obligation is part of PPE cost under Ind AS 16.16(c) and measured under Ind AS 37. The provision is discounted because the time value of money is material for long-dated obligations (Ind AS 37.45). The rate must be pre-tax and must not reflect risks already adjusted in the cash flows (Ind AS 37.47). If cash flows are risk-adjusted expected values, a risk-free rate (G-sec of matching tenor) is appropriate; if cash flows are a single best estimate without a risk margin, a risk premium may be added to the rate. Nominal cash flows require a nominal rate; real cash flows a real rate. Post-tax WACC is wrong on every dimension — it is post-tax, entity-specific and reflects business risk.

Unwinding is the increase in the provision due to the passage of time and is recognised as a borrowing cost in finance costs (Ind AS 37.60). It is not capitalised into the asset and is separate from estimate changes (c03). The decommissioning asset is depreciated over the asset's useful life. Deferred tax is recognised on both the decommissioning asset and the provision following the 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment.

Illustration

An offshore wind developer erects a turbine on 1 April 2025 with a dismantling obligation in 25 years: current estimate ₹20 crore, inflation 4%, pre-tax risk-free rate 7%.

Future cost = 20 × 1.04^25 = 20 × 2.6658 = ₹53.32 crore. PV = 53.32 ÷ 1.07^25 = 53.32 ÷ 5.4274 = ₹9.82 crore.

Year Opening provision Unwinding (7%) Closing provision
FY26 9.82 0.69 10.51
FY27 10.51 0.74 11.25
FY28 11.25 0.79 12.04

Annual depreciation of the decommissioning asset = 9.82 ÷ 25 = ₹0.39 crore. Over the asset's life, total P&L cost = depreciation (9.82) + unwinding (43.50) = ₹53.32 crore — the undiscounted future cost.

Had the developer used a post-tax WACC of 11%, PV would be 53.32 ÷ 13.585 = ₹3.92 crore — understating the provision by 60%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Initial measurement and two years' entries [L1]

A mining company's restoration estimate at 1 April 2025 is ₹15 crore (current prices), payable in 12 years; inflation 5%; pre-tax rate 7.2%. Compute the initial provision and pass the entries for FY26 and FY27 (asset life 12 years).

Q2. Real vs nominal consistency [L2]

An analyst discounts a ₹30 crore current-price dismantling estimate (due in 20 years, no inflation applied) at a nominal G-sec rate of 7%. Expected inflation is 5%. Compute the provision under the analyst's approach and under a consistent approach, and explain the error.

💡 Hint: Either inflate the cash flow and use the nominal rate, or keep current prices and use the real rate derived from the Fisher relation.

Q3. Presentation and cash flows [L2]

For a telecom tower company with a ₹180 crore decommissioning provision (unwinding ₹12 crore, utilisation ₹4 crore in FY26), describe the presentation in the balance sheet, P&L, cash flow statement (indirect method) and the Ind AS 37.84 roll-forward.
