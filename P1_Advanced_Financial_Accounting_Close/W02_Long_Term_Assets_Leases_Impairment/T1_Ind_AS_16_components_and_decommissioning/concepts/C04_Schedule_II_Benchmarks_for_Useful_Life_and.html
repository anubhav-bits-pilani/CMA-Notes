Key concept

Schedule II Benchmarks for Useful Life and Residual Value

Concept: Schedule II benchmarks: Part C useful lives and residual value ≤5% of cost; different estimates allowed with disclosed technical justification

Statement

Under Schedule II to the Companies Act 2013 (Part A), for companies other than those covered by regulatory lives:

\[ N_{\text{used}} = N_{\text{Part C}} \quad \text{and} \quad RV \le 5\% \times C \]

unless the company adopts a different useful life or residual value based on technical advice, in which case the financial statements must disclose the justification for the difference. Here \( N_{\text{Part C}} \) is the life prescribed in Part C for the asset class (single-shift basis), \( RV \) is residual value and \( C \) is original cost.

Explanation

Schedule II replaced the rate-based Schedule XIV (which prescribed minimum depreciation rates) with a life-based approach from 1 April 2014. Its benchmarks interact with Ind AS 16 as follows:

  • Ind AS 16 is primary: useful life must reflect the expected consumption pattern (Ind AS 16.50–56). Schedule II Part C provides indicative lives; if the technical assessment differs, the company uses the technical life and discloses why.
  • Both directions allowed: lives may be longer or shorter than Part C, and residual values may exceed 5% if justified (e.g. aircraft or ships with strong secondary markets).
  • Regulated entities: where a regulator (e.g. CERC for generation and transmission companies) notifies lives/rates for financial statement purposes, those apply (Schedule II Part A, para 3(ii)).
  • Components: significant parts with different lives are determined separately (Schedule II Note 4).
  • Extra shifts: Part C lives assume single shift; double/triple shift increases depreciation by 50%/100% unless the asset is NESD.
  • Intangibles: Part C does not apply; Ind AS 38 governs.
  • Transition: on adoption of Schedule II in FY 2014-15, carrying amounts of assets whose remaining life was nil were adjusted to retained earnings; later changes are Ind AS 8 estimate changes.

Disclosure practice: the accounting policy note states lives by class and identifies those that differ from Schedule II, e.g. "Plant and machinery: 15–25 years (Schedule II: 15 years) based on technical evaluation of usage and maintenance." Auditors obtain the technical assessment (internal engineers or external chartered engineers) and test its assumptions.

Illustration

A cement company's kiln (cost ₹500 crore) is classified as continuous process plant (Part C: 25 years). Technical assessment supports 30 years and residual value of 3%.

Basis Life RV Annual SLM (₹ crore)
Schedule II benchmark (max RV) 25 25.0 (5%) 19.00
Technical assessment 30 15.0 (3%) 16.17

Annual EBIT difference ₹2.83 crore. The company may adopt 30 years with disclosure: "Based on technical evaluation, management believes the useful life of the kiln is 30 years, which is different from that prescribed in Part C of Schedule II."

Practice Questions

Q1. Residual value above 5% [L1]

An airline proposes a residual value of 15% of cost for aircraft (life 20 years). State the Schedule II requirement, the evidence needed, and compute the annual depreciation on a ₹700 crore aircraft under the proposal and under the 5% benchmark.

Q2. Disclosure drafting [L2]

Draft the accounting policy disclosure for a telecom company depreciating towers over 30 years, batteries over 4 years and network equipment over 8 years, explaining which differ from Schedule II and why.

💡 Hint: Identify the Part C class each asset falls into before stating the difference.

Q3. Extra shift depreciation [L2]

A textile mill's looms (cost ₹40 crore, Part C life 15 years, RV 5%) work double shift for 200 days and triple shift for 60 days in FY26 out of 300 working days. Compute FY26 depreciation if the company follows Schedule II shift adjustments, and explain how a technically assessed life could replace this approach.
