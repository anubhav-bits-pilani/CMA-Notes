Key concept

Decommissioning Estimate Changes under the Cost Model

Concept: Cost model: changes in decommissioning estimate adjust asset cost; decrease beyond carrying amount → P&L

Statement

For PPE measured under the cost model, a change in the measurement of an existing decommissioning, restoration or similar liability resulting from changes in (a) the estimated timing or amount of outflows or (b) the current discount rate is accounted for as follows (Appendix A to Ind AS 16):

\[ \Delta P = P_{\text{revised}} - P_{\text{before revision}} \]

  • If \( \Delta P > 0 \): Dr PPE (decommissioning asset), Cr Provision; assess whether the asset is impaired.
  • If \( \Delta P < 0 \): Dr Provision, Cr PPE, up to the asset's carrying amount \( CA \); any excess \( \lvert \Delta P \rvert - CA \) is credited to P&L.

The adjusted depreciable amount is depreciated prospectively over the remaining useful life. Where \( P_{\text{revised}} \) is remeasured at a new rate, unwinding thereafter uses the new rate.

Explanation

The logic is that the decommissioning cost is part of the asset's cost; a revised estimate of that cost should revise the asset, not current profit. Remeasurement under Ind AS 37 is at the current rate (Ind AS 37.47 requires current market assessments), so a fall in G-sec yields increases provisions and assets across the sector.

Key conditions and exceptions:

  • Unwinding (passage of time) is never a change in estimate — always finance cost.
  • Excess decrease beyond carrying amount goes to P&L because an asset cannot be negative.
  • Increase → impairment check: an addition to cost may make the asset's carrying amount exceed recoverable amount (Ind AS 36 test).
  • End of useful life: once the related asset has reached the end of its life, all changes are recognised in P&L.
  • Revaluation model: changes go to revaluation surplus/deficit instead (decrease credited to OCI except to the extent reversing a P&L deficit; increase debited to OCI to the extent of surplus), with excess decrease beyond the cost-model carrying amount to P&L.
  • Disclosure: changes in estimates of the provision are shown in the Ind AS 37.84 roll-forward and, if material, as Ind AS 8 estimate changes.

Illustration

A tower company's decommissioning asset (cost ₹10 crore, depreciated over 10 years, now 4 years old) has carrying amount ₹6 crore; provision ₹13 crore at 31 March 2026 after unwinding.

Case A — estimate rises to ₹15.5 crore: Dr PPE 2.5, Cr Provision 2.5. New asset carrying amount ₹8.5 crore, depreciated over the remaining 6 years = ₹1.417 crore per year.

Case B — many sites are expected to be sold with the towers, so the provision falls to ₹5 crore (decrease ₹8 crore):

Account Dr (₹ crore) Cr (₹ crore)
Provision for decommissioning 8.00
PPE — decommissioning asset 6.00
Other income (P&L) 2.00

The asset is reduced to nil, the ₹2 crore excess goes to P&L, and future depreciation on the decommissioning asset stops.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rate fall remeasurement [L2]

A provision of ₹40 crore (single outflow of ₹100 crore in 13 years, discounted at 7.3%) is remeasured at 31 March 2026 when the rate falls to 6.6%. The related asset's decommissioning component has a carrying amount of ₹22 crore with 13 years remaining. Compute the remeasurement, pass the entry and compute next year's depreciation and unwinding.

Q2. Decrease after end of life [L1]

A fully depreciated mining asset has a restoration provision of ₹9 crore. A new closure plan reduces it to ₹6 crore. Pass the entry and explain why it differs from a decrease during the asset's life.

Q3. Revaluation model variant [L3]

A port's jetty carried under the revaluation model has a revaluation surplus of ₹5 crore relating to it. Its dismantling provision increases by ₹7 crore. Explain the accounting, pass the entries and state the further analysis needed under Ind AS 16 and Ind AS 36.

💡 Hint: Under the revaluation model, an increase in the liability is debited to the revaluation surplus to the extent of any credit balance, then to P&L.
