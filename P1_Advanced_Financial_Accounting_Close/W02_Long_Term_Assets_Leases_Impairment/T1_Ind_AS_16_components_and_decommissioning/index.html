Roadmap topic: Ind AS 16 Property, Plant & Equipment: Component accounting, major inspection capitalization, decommissioning liabilities, and residual value/useful life reassessment

Overview

Ind AS 16 looks simple — capitalise cost, depreciate it — but most of the judgement in a capital-intensive Indian company's PPE note sits in four places: what goes into cost (and what must be expensed), how the asset is split into components, how major inspections, replacements and dismantling obligations enter and leave the carrying amount, and how estimates of residual value and useful life are reassessed every year. Each of these is a recurring audit focus area, a frequent NFRA observation, and a direct driver of EBITDA, depreciation and return-on-capital metrics in an FP&A model.

For the target roles, the topic matters in three ways. In technical accounting, you must document capitalisation policies, component maps and decommissioning estimates that withstand audit. In reporting analysis, you read PPE notes to understand whether a company's depreciation charge is aggressive (long lives, high residuals) or conservative, and whether CWIP is stuck. In modelling, the fixed-asset schedule (opening + additions − disposals − depreciation = closing) is the backbone of the 3-statement model, and decommissioning provisions feed finance cost through unwinding.

The learning sequence follows the asset's life. Start with initial cost (s01): directly attributable costs, testing, borrowing costs, and the 2022 amendment pushing sale proceeds of pre-commissioning output to P&L. Then split cost into components (s02) under Ind AS 16.43 and Schedule II. Next, handle subsequent costs: major inspections (s03) and replacement parts (s04), both of which require derecognising the old part. Add the decommissioning element of cost and its remeasurement (s05). Then cover the annual review of residual value, life and method (s06), the Schedule II benchmarks and presentation items such as CWIP ageing, capital advances and spares (s07), and finally the revaluation model, held-for-sale transfer and derecognition (s08). The five concepts (c01–c05) are the formulas and entries you will use in every schedule.

The Ind AS 16 positions here are those notified by MCA through the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, as amended (including the 2022 amendment on proceeds before intended use). Schedule II to the Companies Act 2013 governs useful lives and residual values for companies; Schedule III (amended 2021) governs presentation, including the CWIP ageing schedule.

Related concepts to study

Sec 32 WDV vs book SLM and deferred tax

Income-tax block WDV depreciation (Sec 32) vs book SLM → DTL → Week 3 (w3_t3)

Book depreciation under Ind AS 16 follows the consumption pattern, usually SLM over Schedule II or technically assessed lives, per asset and per component. Tax depreciation under Sec 32 is computed on the WDV of a block at prescribed rates, with half-rate for assets used under 180 days. Because tax depreciation is front-loaded, the tax base of PPE falls faster than the book carrying amount in early years, creating a taxable temporary difference and a deferred tax liability. Componentisation, decommissioning assets and impairment all alter the book side only, so each creates its own temporary difference. Week 3 builds the full Ind AS 12 computation; in this topic, keep a parallel tax block register in the fixed-asset model so the DTL can be calculated automatically.

US GAAP differences

US GAAP: componentisation permitted not required, revaluation prohibited, ARO (ASC 410) at credit-adjusted risk-free rate

Under US GAAP (ASC 360), component depreciation is permitted but not required, so many US companies depreciate a building as one unit and expense major overhauls (or use the deferral or built-in overhaul methods for aircraft). Revaluation of PPE upward is prohibited; assets stay at historical cost less depreciation and impairment. Asset retirement obligations under ASC 410-20 are measured at fair value using a credit-adjusted risk-free rate, and each upward revision is a new "layer" discounted at the current rate while downward revisions use the historical rates of the original layers — unlike Ind AS 37/Appendix A, where the whole provision is remeasured at the current rate. Analysts comparing Indian and US peers should adjust for these differences in EBITDA and depreciation.

Links to discounting and impairment

→ w2_t4 discounting/unwinding; → w2_t3 impairment of PPE and CGUs

Decommissioning provisions are Ind AS 37 provisions, so the discounting rules of Ind AS 37.45–47 (pre-tax rate reflecting time value and specific risks) and unwinding as a borrowing cost (Ind AS 37.60) covered in t4 apply directly. When carrying amounts include decommissioning assets, impairment testing in t3 must be consistent: the CGU's carrying amount includes the decommissioning asset, and Ind AS 36.78 allows deducting the provision from both carrying amount and recoverable amount when a buyer would assume it. Revalued and componentised PPE also flows into CGU carrying amounts, and an impairment loss on revalued PPE is debited to revaluation surplus first.

Excel fixed asset roll-forward and CWIP tracker

Excel: fixed asset roll-forward (opening + additions − disposals − depreciation = closing) and CWIP capitalisation tracker

A fixed-asset model has two linked schedules. The gross block roll-forward per class (opening, additions, disposals, reclassifications, closing) and the accumulated depreciation roll-forward (opening, charge, disposals, impairment, closing) produce net block; the integrity check is =Opening+Additions-Disposals-Depreciation-Closing=0 per class. The CWIP tracker lists each project with budget, spend to date, expected commissioning date and ageing bucket (<1, 1–2, 2–3, >3 years per Schedule III), and transfers to the gross block on the capitalisation date with a flag. Use SUMIFS on a project-level register to feed the class-level roll-forward, so that the Schedule III note and the tax block register reconcile automatically.

Common pitfalls

Expensing overhauls or not derecognising the old inspection

Expensing major overhauls as repairs or not derecognising the old inspection component

The mistake: a ₹40 crore refinery turnaround or aircraft C-check is debited to repairs and maintenance, or it is capitalised but the carrying amount of the previous inspection stays on the books. Teams make it because maintenance budgets sit in opex and plant accountants follow tax practice (current repairs under Sec 31). The correct treatment under Ind AS 16.14 is to capitalise the cost of each major inspection as a component when the recognition criteria are met and derecognise any remaining carrying amount of the previous inspection, whether or not it was separately identified (using the estimated cost of a similar future inspection as a proxy). Detect it in review by scanning repairs for large, periodic spends linked to shutdowns and by checking that each overhaul capitalisation has a matching derecognition entry.

Retrospective useful-life revisions

Applying useful-life revisions retrospectively

The mistake: when a technical review extends plant life from 15 to 20 years, the accountant recomputes accumulated depreciation as if 20 years had always applied and credits the difference to P&L or retained earnings. This happens because a "catch-up" seems more accurate. Ind AS 16.51 and Ind AS 8.36 require a prospective change: the remaining depreciable amount (carrying amount less revised residual value) is spread over the revised remaining life from the start of the period of change. Detect it by looking for credits in the accumulated depreciation account without disposals, and for opening reserves adjustments labelled "change in useful life".

Capitalising pre-operative overheads and abnormal trial-run losses

Capitalising general pre-operative overheads or abnormal trial-run losses

The mistake: a greenfield plant capitalises corporate salaries, advertising, general administration and the losses of an extended trial run as "pre-operative expenses". It is common because the old Indian GAAP guidance (AS 10 and the ICAI Guidance Note on Treatment of Expenditure during Construction Period) was read liberally. Under Ind AS 16.19–20, administration and general overheads, start-up costs, initial operating losses and costs incurred after the asset is capable of operating as intended are excluded; since the 2022 amendment (Ind AS 16.20A), sale proceeds from testing output and the related cost of producing those items go to P&L. Only costs of testing whether the asset functions properly are capitalised. Detect it by reviewing the nature of each pre-operative ledger and the commissioning date certificate.

Discounting decommissioning at post-tax WACC

Discounting decommissioning provisions at post-tax WACC

The mistake: the provision for dismantling a telecom tower or restoring a mine is discounted at the group's post-tax WACC of 11–12% because that rate is "the company's cost of capital". This understates the provision and the asset. Ind AS 37.47 requires a pre-tax rate reflecting current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the liability, and not risks already reflected in the cash flow estimates. WACC reflects the risk of the entity's equity and debt-financed business, not the liability. Practice is to use a G-sec yield matched to the timing of outflows (risk-adjusting the cash flows) or a risk-free rate with a liability-specific adjustment. Detect it by comparing the disclosed discount rate with G-sec yields; a rate well above G-sec signals a problem.

Practice Questions

Q1. Greenfield plant: cost, components and decommissioning [L3]

Narmada Chemicals Ltd builds a new plant commissioned on 1 April 2025. Costs (₹ crore): equipment 180; civil works 60; import duty (non-refundable) 12; IGST paid (input credit available) 32; site preparation 5; installation 8; testing 4; sale of test output 3 (cost of producing it 2.5); general administration during construction 6; borrowing costs on a specific loan during construction 9 (including ₹1 crore for a two-month suspension period when work was halted due to a funding dispute); an obligation to remediate the site in 25 years at an estimated ₹30 crore, pre-tax risk-free rate 7.2%. Components: reactor lining (life 5 years, ₹25 crore of equipment cost), rest of equipment (20 years), civil works (30 years). Residual values nil.

(a) Compute the capitalised cost and allocate it to components. (b) Compute FY26 depreciation per component and the unwinding of the provision. (c) Pass the journal entries for FY26. (d) State the P&L treatment of items not capitalised.

💡 Hint: Allocate the decommissioning asset and directly attributable costs to components on a rational basis and state that basis.

Q2. Overhaul, life revision and disposal [L2]

Malabar Airways Ltd's aircraft (cost ₹600 crore on 1 April 2022, of which ₹30 crore represents the embedded heavy check component with a 6-year cycle; airframe life 20 years, residual value 5%) undergoes an early heavy check on 1 April 2026 costing ₹36 crore. On the same date, management revises the airframe's remaining life to 14 years and residual value to 8% of original cost. On 31 March 2028 the aircraft is sold for ₹450 crore with selling costs of ₹5 crore.

(a) Compute carrying amounts at 1 April 2026 before the check. (b) Pass the entries for the check and the revision. (c) Compute depreciation for FY27 and FY28. (d) Compute the gain or loss on sale and state its presentation.

Q3. Review case: PPE note of a listed infrastructure company [L3]

You are reviewing the draft FY26 PPE note of a port operator. You find: (i) CWIP of ₹210 crore of which ₹80 crore relates to a berth suspended for 3 years; (ii) capital advances of ₹45 crore included in other current assets; (iii) critical spares of ₹12 crore expected to be used over 4 years shown in inventory; (iv) depreciation on cranes using 25 years against a Schedule II life of 15 years with no technical assessment on file; (v) the dismantling provision for a leasehold jetty discounted at 11.8% post-tax WACC; (vi) a ₹6 crore credit to accumulated depreciation labelled "life extension catch-up".

For each item, identify the issue, the correct treatment with references, the presentation/disclosure required and the review evidence you would request.

Q4. Revaluation, held for sale and model build [L3]

Kosi Hotels Ltd uses the revaluation model for land and buildings. A building (cost ₹50 crore, accumulated depreciation ₹10 crore, revalued on 1 April 2025 to ₹64 crore with remaining life 32 years) is classified as held for sale on 1 October 2026 when its fair value less costs to sell is ₹58 crore; it is sold on 31 January 2027 for ₹61 crore net.

(a) Pass the entries for the revaluation, FY26 depreciation and the transfer from revaluation surplus (if the entity chooses to transfer as the asset is used). (b) Account for the held-for-sale classification and the sale. (c) Design the Excel fixed-asset roll-forward columns needed to show these movements, including the revaluation surplus roll-forward and the integrity checks.
