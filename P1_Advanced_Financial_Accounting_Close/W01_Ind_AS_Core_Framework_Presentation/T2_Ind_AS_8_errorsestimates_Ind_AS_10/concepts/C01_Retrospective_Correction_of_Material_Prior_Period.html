Key concept

Retrospective Correction of Material Prior-Period Errors (Ind AS 8.42)

Concept: Material prior-period errors corrected retrospectively in first FS approved after discovery — restate comparatives or opening balances of earliest period (Ind AS 8.42)

Statement

Subject to the impracticability relief in Ind AS 8.43–45, an entity shall correct material prior period errors retrospectively in the first set of financial statements approved for issue after their discovery by:

  • (a) restating the comparative amounts for the prior period(s) presented in which the error occurred; or
  • (b) if the error occurred before the earliest prior period presented, restating the opening balances of assets, liabilities and equity for the earliest prior period presented.

The correction is excluded from profit or loss for the period in which the error is discovered (Ind AS 8.46).

Explanation

Why retrospective. An error is a failure to use reliable information that was available; correcting it in current profit would distort two periods (the one that was wrong and the one that absorbs the fix). Restating makes the comparatives what they should always have been and keeps current-period profit clean. This is the most significant break from AS 5, which routed prior-period items through the current P&L.

"First set approved after discovery." If an error is found in October 2025, the correction goes into the FY 2025-26 annual statements (and, for listed entities, the next quarterly results with restated comparatives). It does not wait for a convenient year.

"Material." Immaterial errors may be corrected in the current period — but Ind AS 8.41 states that financial statements do not comply with Ind AS if they contain material errors or immaterial errors made intentionally to achieve a particular presentation. Materiality is judged for each affected period and in aggregate.

Which periods. With one comparative year (standard in India): an error that originated in the comparative year restates that year's figures; an older error restates the opening balance sheet of the comparative year (1 April 2024 for FY 2025-26 statements), usually retained earnings, and the comparative year to the extent it continued to have effects.

Scope of "restating". All affected primary statements (balance sheet, P&L and OCI, SOCIE, cash flows), notes, EPS (Ind AS 33.64), segment information and historical summaries. A third balance sheet follows if 1.40A is triggered.

Illustration

FY 2025-26 statements of Mahanadi Cables Ltd. In September 2025 it discovers that bonuses of ₹10 crore payable for FY 2023-24 (paid in FY 2024-25) were expensed when paid in FY 2024-25 rather than accrued in FY 2023-24. Tax rate 25.168%; bonus deductible on payment under Sec 43B.

Practice Questions

Q1. Which period is restated? [L1]

For FY 2025-26 financial statements (one comparative year), state which balances and periods are restated for: (a) an error originating in FY 2022-23 that fully reversed in FY 2023-24; (b) an error originating in FY 2023-24 that continued to affect depreciation in FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26; (c) an error in the Q3 FY 2025-26 results discovered in Q4.

💡 Hint: Trace whether each error still affects any balance at the start of the earliest period presented.

Q2. Intentional immaterial error [L2]

A company deliberately deferred ₹3 crore of March 2025 advertising expense to April 2025 to meet a PAT covenant in FY 2024-25 (PAT ₹180 crore). The amount is below performance materiality. Discuss whether Ind AS 8 requires restatement and what the auditor and audit committee should do.

Q3. Restate and compute [L2]

In FY 2025-26, it is discovered that an FVOCI equity investment's fair value at 31 March 2025 was overstated by ₹6 crore (deferred tax at 14.30%). Show the restatement of the FY 2024-25 OCI, the OCI reserve at 31 March 2025, and any effect on FY 2025-26 OCI.
