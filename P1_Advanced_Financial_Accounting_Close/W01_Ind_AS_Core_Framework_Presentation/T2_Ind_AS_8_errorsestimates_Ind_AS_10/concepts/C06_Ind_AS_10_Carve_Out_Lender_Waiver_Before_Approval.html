Key concept

Ind AS 10 Carve-Out: Lender Waiver Before Approval Is Adjusting

Concept: Ind AS 10 carve-out: lender agreeing before approval not to demand repayment after covenant breach is adjusting — loan can remain non-current

Statement

Where there is a breach of a material provision of a long-term loan arrangement on or before the end of the reporting period with the effect that the liability becomes payable on demand at the reporting date, and the lender agrees, after the reporting period and before the approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach, the agreement is treated under Ind AS as an adjusting event, and the liability is not classified as current (Ind AS 1.74 read with the corresponding Ind AS 10 modification).

Under IFRS (IAS 1.74 and IAS 10), the same agreement is a non-adjusting event and the liability is current at the reporting date.

Explanation

The IFRS logic is strict: classification depends on rights at the reporting date. If on 31 March the lender could demand repayment, the entity had no right to defer settlement; a later waiver is a new condition.

The Indian carve-out reflects practice: covenant compliance is often computed only after audited figures are available, consortium lenders take weeks to issue waivers, and banks almost never recall performing loans for technical breaches. Reclassifying large term loans to current on technical grounds would make current ratios meaningless, trigger cross-defaults in other agreements and distort regulatory ratios. ICAI and MCA therefore modified Ind AS 1 and Ind AS 10 so that a lender's agreement obtained before approval is treated as evidence about the year-end position.

Conditions (all must hold):

  1. the arrangement is a long-term loan (not a demand facility like cash credit);
  2. the breach occurred on or before the reporting date and made the loan payable on demand;
  3. the lender's agreement not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach is obtained after year-end and before the Board approves the financial statements;
  4. the agreement is unconditional in substance (or any conditions are satisfied by approval), and covers all lenders entitled to recall (consortium — each member or the lead bank with authority);
  5. the agreement does not itself require repayment within twelve months.

What stays current regardless: contractual instalments falling due within twelve months (current maturities) remain current borrowings.

Disclosure: disclose the breach, the waiver, its date and terms; Ind AS 107.18–19 requires disclosure of defaults and breaches of loan agreements during the period, including whether the default was remedied or terms renegotiated before approval.

IFRS group reporting: an Indian subsidiary of a foreign group reporting under IFRS will classify the same loan as current in its group-reporting pack — a reconciling item between Ind AS and IFRS.

Illustration

Hirakud Steel Ltd: term loan ₹480 crore from a four-bank consortium; ₹80 crore repayable in FY 2026-27. DSCR covenant breached at 31 March 2026 (1.05 vs 1.20), making the loan repayable on demand. Board approval of FS: 24 May 2026.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the carve-out [L2]

A company's ₹250 crore term loan (₹50 crore due within 12 months) breached its TOL/TNW covenant at 31 March 2026. The bank's letter of 30 April 2026 states it "will not recall the loan provided that the promoter pledges additional shares by 30 June 2026". The FS are approved on 15 May 2026 and the pledge is completed on 10 May 2026. Classify the loan and draft the disclosure.

💡 Hint: Check whether the condition was satisfied before the approval date.

Q2. IFRS reconciliation [L2]

Prepare the Ind AS to IFRS reconciliation of current and non-current borrowings for the Hirakud Steel first scenario, and explain the effect on the current ratio under each framework if current assets are ₹900 crore and other current liabilities are ₹620 crore.
