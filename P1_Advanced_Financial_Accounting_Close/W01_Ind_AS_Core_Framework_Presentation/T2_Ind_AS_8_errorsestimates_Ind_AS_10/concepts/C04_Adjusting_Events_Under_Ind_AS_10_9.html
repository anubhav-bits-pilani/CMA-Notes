Key concept

Adjusting Events Under Ind AS 10.9

Concept: Adjusting events: court settlement confirming obligation, customer insolvency, post-year-end sales below cost, discovery of fraud or errors (Ind AS 10.9)

Statement

An adjusting event is an event after the reporting period that provides evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period (Ind AS 10.3(a)). An entity shall adjust the amounts recognised in its financial statements to reflect adjusting events (Ind AS 10.8). Ind AS 10.9 gives examples, including:

  1. the settlement after the reporting period of a court case that confirms the entity had a present obligation at the end of the reporting period;
  2. the receipt of information indicating that an asset was impaired at the end of the reporting period — in particular the bankruptcy (insolvency) of a customer occurring after the reporting period, and the sale of inventories after the reporting period that gives evidence about their net realisable value;
  3. the determination after the reporting period of the cost of assets purchased, or the proceeds from assets sold, before the end of the reporting period;
  4. the determination after the reporting period of profit-sharing or bonus payments, if there was a present legal or constructive obligation at year-end;
  5. the discovery of fraud or errors that show that the financial statements are incorrect.

Explanation

The common thread is that the condition existed on 31 March; the post-year-end event is evidence.

Court settlement. If a judgement or settlement in April confirms an obligation arising from pre-year-end events, the provision is recognised or remeasured (Ind AS 37.16 also requires using additional evidence from events after the reporting period). A contingent liability disclosed in the draft becomes a recognised provision. Ind AS 10.10's counterpart: if the case arises from events after year-end, it is non-adjusting.

Customer insolvency. A customer admitted into CIRP in April usually was already in financial difficulty at 31 March; the ECL allowance (Ind AS 109) is updated to reflect expected recoveries. If the insolvency results from an event after year-end (a flood in May), it is non-adjusting.

Post-year-end sales below cost. Ind AS 2.30 bases NRV estimates on the most reliable evidence available at the time, taking into account price fluctuations directly relating to events after the period to the extent they confirm conditions existing at the end of the period. A clearance sale in April at prices below cost confirms obsolescence that existed at year-end.

Fraud or errors. Discovering in May that March sales were fictitious, or that stock was stolen in February, means the year-end figures are wrong; correct them before approval (this is not an Ind AS 8 prior-period error because the current-year statements are not yet approved).

Other adjusting events in Indian practice: final determination of price revisions in tariff orders relating to the year (electricity regulators' true-ups — depends on the regulatory framework), receipt of insurance claim assessment for a pre-year-end loss, crystallisation of an MSME interest liability on overdue pre-year-end dues, and the Ind AS carve-out on covenant waivers (c06).

Illustration

Yamuna Electricals Ltd, year-end 31 March 2026, approval 19 May 2026 (₹ crore):

Event Draft figure Adjustment
NCLT judgement on 6 May orders payment of ₹3.4 crore for a 2024 supply dispute; draft showed contingent liability Nil provision Recognise provision 3.4
Dealer owing 2.0 admitted into CIRP on 25 April; expected recovery 30%; existing ECL 0.2 ECL 0.2 ECL to 1.4 (+1.2)
Inventory of discontinued fan model (cost 5.0) sold in April for 3.6 less selling cost 0.2 5.0 Write down 1.6
Performance bonus for FY 2025-26 approved by NRC on 10 May at 1.8 under a policy existing at year-end; accrual 1.2 1.2 +0.6
Internal audit finds 0.9 of March sales were invoiced without dispatch Revenue overstated Reverse revenue and receivable; restore inventory

Total pre-tax profit reduction: 3.4 + 1.2 + 1.6 + 0.6 + 0.9 (less margin restored in inventory at cost) — each recorded in FY 2025-26 accounts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Adjust or not? [L1]

Year-end 31 March 2026, approval 22 May 2026: (a) on 18 April, an arbitral award of ₹7 crore against the company in a 2023 contract dispute (provision ₹4 crore); (b) on 2 May, a customer defaults after a fire at its factory on 28 April; (c) on 10 May, auditors discover that a ₹2.5 crore supplier invoice for March was not recorded; (d) April sales of a product at 20% below cost following a competitor's price cut announced on 15 April. Classify each and quantify adjustments.

💡 Hint: For (d), identify when the condition causing the low price arose.

Q2. Entry set [L2]

Pass journal entries for the Yamuna Electricals illustration, assuming the fictitious March sales of ₹0.9 crore had a cost of ₹0.65 crore (goods still in the warehouse) and GST at 18% had been charged on the invoice and included in the GSTR-1 filed. Comment on the GST correction.

Q3. Interview: why is fraud discovery adjusting? [L1]

Explain in under 120 words why discovery after year-end of a fraud committed during the year is an adjusting event, and how its treatment differs if the discovery happens after the financial statements are approved.
