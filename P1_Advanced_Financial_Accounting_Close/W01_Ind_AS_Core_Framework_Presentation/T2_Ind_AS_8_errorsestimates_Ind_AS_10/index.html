Roadmap topic: Ind AS 8 & Ind AS 10: Retrospective restatement of prior-period errors vs prospective accounting estimate changes; adjusting vs non-adjusting post-balance-sheet events

Overview

Ind AS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors and Ind AS 10 Events after the Reporting Period are the two standards that govern what happens at the edges of a reporting period. Ind AS 8 answers "something has changed or was wrong — where does the effect go?": policy changes go retrospective, estimate changes go prospective, and material prior-period errors are corrected by restating comparatives and opening balances. Ind AS 10 answers "something happened after 31 March but before the Board approved the accounts — does it change the numbers?": adjusting events (evidence of year-end conditions) do; non-adjusting events (new conditions) are disclosed; and going concern deterioration overrides both.

For the target roles, these standards are daily tools. A technical accountant writes the triage memo that decides whether a ₹40 crore revision is an estimate change through current P&L or an error restating last year; a reporting analyst builds the restated comparatives, third balance sheet and EPS for both the annual report and SEBI quarterly results; an FP&A analyst separates one-off catch-ups from run-rate changes in the forecast and knows that a final dividend recommended in May is not a March liability; an auditor runs subsequent-events procedures to the Board approval date. The Indian overlay — Sec 130/131 of the Companies Act, SEBI LODR restated columns and the Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications, and the Ind AS 1/Ind AS 10 carve-out on covenant waivers — makes this topic distinctly local.

The learning sequence: start with triage (s01) — policy vs estimate vs error, including the 2023 definition of accounting estimates. Then learn the error route: restatement mechanics (s02, c01, c03), the narrow impracticability relief (s03) and disclosures (c07). Next the estimate route: prospective recognition (c02) applied to useful lives, ECL, warranties, actuarial assumptions and contract cost revisions (s04). Then switch to Ind AS 10: the event classification and approval date (s05, c04, c05), the covenant carve-out (c06) and the going concern override (s06). Finish with the regulatory overlay: Sec 130/131 (s07) and SEBI LODR (s08).

Related concepts to study

IFRS 18 and the renamed IAS 8

IFRS 18 renames IAS 8 "Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements" and moves some IAS 1 requirements into it

When IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 (effective 1 January 2027 for IFRS reporters), IAS 8 is renamed Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements. General requirements that used to sit in IAS 1 — fair presentation and compliance with IFRS, going concern, accrual basis of accounting, and the disclosure of accounting policies — move into the renamed IAS 8, while the policy/estimate/error mechanics remain largely unchanged. For Indian preparers nothing changes until ICAI issues converged standards and MCA notifies them, but technical accountants should track the ICAI exposure drafts: paragraph references used in Indian memos (Ind AS 1.25 for going concern, 1.117 for policies) would shift, and group reporting packs for IFRS parents will use the new structure from 2027.

US GAAP ASC 250 and SAB 108

US GAAP ASC 250: "Big R" reissuance vs "little r" revision restatements; SAB 108 rollover vs iron-curtain materiality

ASC 250 requires retrospective correction of errors, similar to Ind AS 8, but US practice distinguishes two forms. A "Big R" restatement applies when the error is material to previously issued financial statements: those statements are reissued, the company files an Item 4.02 Form 8-K stating that prior statements should no longer be relied upon, and amended filings follow. A "little r" revision applies when the error is immaterial to prior periods but correcting it in the current period would be material: comparatives are revised in the next filing without non-reliance. SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin 108 requires materiality to be assessed under both the rollover approach (misstatement originating in the current-period income statement) and the iron-curtain approach (cumulative misstatement in the balance sheet at period-end); an error is corrected if material under either. Indian practice has no formal equivalent, but the dual lens is useful in Ind AS 8.41 materiality judgements.

Ind AS 34, SA 560 and SA 570

Ind AS 34 interim reporting — estimates changed in interim periods; SA 560 (subsequent events) and SA 570 (going concern)

Under Ind AS 34, estimates are revised at each interim date, and changes are not pushed back into earlier interim periods; Ind AS 34.16A(d) requires disclosure of the nature and amount of changes in estimates, and Ind AS 34.26 requires the annual statements to disclose a significant change in estimate made in the final interim period if no separate final interim report is published. SA 560 sets the auditor's responsibilities for subsequent events: procedures up to the date of the auditor's report, and responses to facts that become known after the report date but before issue, or after issue. SA 570 (Revised) covers the auditor's evaluation of management's going concern assessment, the "Material uncertainty related to going concern" section, and modified opinions when the basis is inappropriate — directly linked to s06.

Links to EPS and useful-life reassessment

→ w1_t5: EPS restatement; → w2_t1: useful-life and residual value reassessment as estimate changes

Topic 5 of this week (w1_t5) covers Ind AS 33 in depth; for restatements, Ind AS 33.64 requires basic and diluted EPS of all periods presented to be adjusted for errors and retrospective policy changes, and for bonus issues and splits — including those after the reporting date. Combine both when a restatement and a bonus issue occur together. Week 2 (w2_t1) covers Ind AS 16 and Ind AS 38 reassessment of useful lives, residual values and depreciation methods at each year-end (Ind AS 16.51, 16.61) — the most frequent estimate changes you will apply with the s04 mechanics, and a common area where reviewers question whether an "estimate change" is really an error.

Common pitfalls

Prior-period errors routed through current P&L

Routing prior-period errors through current P&L as "prior period items" (old AS 5 habit)

The mistake: a separate "prior period items" line in the current-year P&L, or the correction buried in other expenses. Why it happens: AS 5 permitted (and Tally users are used to) passing prior-period corrections as current-year entries; restating comparatives is more work. Correct treatment: material prior-period errors are corrected retrospectively (Ind AS 8.42) and excluded from current profit (8.46); opening retained earnings and comparatives are restated, with 8.49 disclosures. Detection: search the ledger for "prior period" narrations; scan journal entries in April–June posted to P&L with descriptions referencing earlier years; compare opening retained earnings with prior-year closing.

Depreciation method change treated as a policy change

Treating a change in depreciation method as a policy change — it is an estimate change (Ind AS 16.61)

The mistake: restating comparatives when moving from WDV to SLM (or vice versa), as was done under old Indian GAAP AS 6 where it was a policy change with retrospective recomputation. Why it happens: legacy AS 6 treatment and the word "method" sounding like a policy. Correct treatment: Ind AS 16.61 — the method is reviewed at least each year-end, and a change reflecting a changed expected pattern of consumption is a change in accounting estimate under Ind AS 8, applied prospectively from the date of change to the remaining carrying amount. Detection: check the policy note and the SOCIE "changes in accounting policy" row for depreciation-related restatements; verify that the depreciation recomputation starts from the current-year opening carrying amount.

Final dividend recommended after year-end accrued as a liability

Accruing a final dividend recommended after year-end as a liability

The mistake: booking "proposed dividend" as a current liability at 31 March because the Board recommended it at the accounts-approval meeting. Why it happens: pre-2016 Indian GAAP required provision for proposed dividends; Tally templates still carry the ledger. Correct treatment: dividends declared (or recommended) after the reporting period are not liabilities at year-end (Ind AS 10.12–13); disclose the amount and per-share amount (Ind AS 1.137). The liability arises on AGM declaration. Detection: look for "proposed dividend" or "dividend payable" ledgers at year-end without an interim declaration before 31 March; tie SOCIE dividends to dividends declared during the year.

Tax and EPS effects omitted on restatement

Forgetting tax effects and comparative EPS on restatement

The mistake: restating the gross pre-tax amount against retained earnings, ignoring deferred or current tax, and leaving comparative EPS as originally reported. Why it happens: the restatement is prepared by the accounting team while tax and EPS schedules are owned by different people; tight timelines. Correct treatment: compute the tax effect for each affected period (deferred if the tax base was unaffected; current if the return also needs correcting; nil for permanent differences), restate retained earnings net of tax and restate basic and diluted EPS for each prior period presented (Ind AS 8.49(b)(ii), Ind AS 33.64). Detection: reconcile the restatement workpaper: pre-tax adjustment × rate = tax adjustment; recompute EPS from restated profit and adjusted share counts; check that the deferred tax note movement includes the restatement.

Practice Questions

Q1. Year-end close triage for a pump manufacturer [L3]

Varuna Pumps Ltd (listed; FY ends 31 March 2026; Board approval 20 May 2026; tax 25.168%; 6 crore equity shares; reported FY 2024-25 PAT ₹54 crore) identifies the following during the close: (a) warranty claim rate revised from 1.2% to 1.7% of sales based on FY 2025-26 claims analytics (sales in warranty: FY 2024-25 ₹500 crore with 50% unexpired; FY 2025-26 ₹620 crore fully unexpired); (b) depreciation on a ₹36 crore foundry line capitalised on 1 April 2024 (life 12 years, SLM) was never charged in FY 2024-25; (c) the ECL model is changed from a provision matrix to a PD × LGD approach, increasing the allowance by ₹2.8 crore; (d) a customer owing ₹4.5 crore is admitted into CIRP on 28 April 2026 (expected recovery 10%; existing ECL ₹0.3 crore); (e) the Board recommends a dividend of ₹4 per share on 20 May 2026.

Required: (i) classify each item with paragraph references; (ii) pass all journal entries for FY 2025-26, including restatement entries and tax; (iii) compute restated FY 2024-25 basic EPS; (iv) state whether a third balance sheet is required; (v) draft the Ind AS 8.39, 8.49 and Ind AS 10 notes.

💡 Hint: Work out which items change FY 2025-26 profit, which change opening retained earnings, and which change nothing but disclosure.

Q2. Covenant breach, going concern and SEBI reporting [L3]

Satluj Spinners Ltd (listed) has a ₹600 crore consortium term loan (₹120 crore due in FY 2026-27). At 31 March 2026 it breaches its DSCR covenant. On 8 May 2026 three of four lenders (holding 80% of the exposure) issue no-recall letters; the fourth lender issues a recall notice on 12 May 2026. The company's 18-month cash forecast shows it can repay the fourth lender's share only if a ₹150 crore asset sale closes by September 2026. Board approval: 27 May 2026.

Required: (i) classify the loan at 31 March 2026 under Ind AS and under IFRS; (ii) assess the going concern basis and the need for a material uncertainty disclosure; (iii) identify the auditor's reporting under SA 570 and whether a SIAQ would be required; (iv) draft the borrowing and going concern notes for the annual audited results filed under Reg 33.

Q3. Restatement versus statutory revision [L3]

In January 2026, Kaveri Infra Ltd (unlisted, planning an IPO in FY 2026-27) discovers that revenue of ₹28 crore (FY 2023-24) and ₹41 crore (FY 2024-25) was recognised on EPC contracts using billing milestones rather than cost-to-cost progress; contract assets were overstated accordingly. Tax 25.168% (deferred). FY 2024-25 reported PAT ₹96 crore; 8 crore shares; opening retained earnings at 1 April 2024 ₹410 crore.

Required: (i) compute the restatement amounts for FY 2025-26 financial statements (opening RE at 1 April 2024, FY 2024-25 profit, contract assets and DTL at both dates, restated EPS); (ii) evaluate whether the company should also revise FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 statements under Sec 131, considering the IPO and SEBI ICDR restated financial information; (iii) identify whether impracticability relief could apply if FY 2023-24 project cost records were incomplete.

Q4. Interview case: estimate or error? [L2]

You join as an FP&A manager and find that in FY 2025-26 the controller booked an additional ₹15 crore of inventory obsolescence provision as a "change in estimate", explaining that a new ageing-based methodology was adopted. Internal audit notes that the same slow-moving stock had been flagged in the FY 2024-25 internal audit report (issued before the FY 2024-25 accounts were approved) and ignored. Prepare a structured answer: the Ind AS 8 analysis, the information you would gather, the accounting and disclosure under each conclusion, and how the conclusion changes the FY 2025-26 budget variance analysis.
