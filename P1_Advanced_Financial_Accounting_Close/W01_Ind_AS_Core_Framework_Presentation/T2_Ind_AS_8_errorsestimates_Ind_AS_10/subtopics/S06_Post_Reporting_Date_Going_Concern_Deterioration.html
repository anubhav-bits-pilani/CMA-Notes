Subtopic note

Post-Reporting-Date Going Concern Deterioration (Ind AS 10.14)

Scope: Going concern deterioration after reporting date (Ind AS 10.14) overriding normal non-adjusting treatment

Why it matters

Ordinarily, events after the reporting date that reflect new conditions are non-adjusting: you disclose, you do not change the numbers. Going concern is the exception. If, before the Board approves the accounts, management decides to liquidate or cease trading — or has no realistic alternative but to do so — the whole basis of preparation changes, even if the trigger arose after 31 March. Indian examples include companies whose lenders initiated CIRP under the IBC in April–May, airlines grounded after year-end, and businesses hit by regulatory bans (gaming, crypto exchanges, certain chemicals). Auditors must consider SA 570 and SA 560 together; FP&A teams supply the cash flow forecasts on which the conclusion rests.

Core notes

The rule

Ind AS 10.14: an entity shall not prepare its financial statements on a going concern basis if management determines after the reporting period either that it intends to liquidate the entity or to cease trading, or that it has no realistic alternative but to do so.

Ind AS 10.15 explains why: deterioration in operating results and financial position after the reporting period may indicate a need to consider whether the going concern assumption is still appropriate. If it is no longer appropriate, the effect is so pervasive that Ind AS 10 requires a fundamental change in the basis of accounting, rather than an adjustment to amounts recognised within the original basis.

Ind AS 10.16 links to Ind AS 1.25–26: disclosures are required when (a) the financial statements are not prepared on a going concern basis, or (b) management is aware of material uncertainties related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt upon the entity's ability to continue as a going concern — and the events or conditions requiring disclosure may arise after the reporting period.

Why it overrides the adjusting/non-adjusting split

The going concern assumption is not a line-item measurement; it is the foundation on which every carrying amount rests (assets at cost less depreciation because they will be used, liabilities split current/non-current because the entity will continue). Ind AS 10 treats the going concern assessment as made at the approval date, using all information up to that date. A post-year-end fire is normally non-adjusting; if the fire leaves the entity with no realistic alternative but to cease trading, the going concern basis is abandoned for the year-end statements.

Three outcomes

Conclusion at approval date Basis Disclosure Auditor (SA 570)
Going concern appropriate, no material uncertainty Going concern None specific; consider 1.122 judgement if close call Unmodified
Going concern appropriate, material uncertainty exists Going concern Ind AS 1.25: disclose the material uncertainty, management's plans and the events/conditions Unmodified with "Material uncertainty related to going concern" section, if disclosure adequate
Intention or no realistic alternative but to liquidate/cease trading Not going concern Disclose that fact, the basis used and the reason (1.25) Unmodified if alternative basis appropriate and disclosed (with emphasis of matter); adverse if going concern basis wrongly used

The alternative basis

Ind AS does not prescribe a "liquidation basis". Management develops a policy under Ind AS 8.10–12, typically:

  • assets measured at realisable value (expected net proceeds in an orderly or forced sale);
  • all liabilities classified as current; long-term obligations remeasured at settlement amounts, including penalties triggered by default or early termination;
  • recognition of costs of liquidation only where an obligation exists (Ind AS 37) — closure costs are provided only if a legal or constructive obligation exists at the reporting date, otherwise disclosed;
  • deferred tax assets recognised only if realisable through the wind-down;
  • no current/non-current split (or all current), but Schedule III captions retained with an explanatory note.

Comparatives remain on the going concern basis (they were prepared when it was appropriate) — the change is not retrospective.

Indicators to monitor between year-end and approval

  • Lender recall notices, invocation of guarantees, SARFAESI notices, admission of a Sec 7/9/10 IBC application;
  • loss of a licence or regulatory ban (e.g., an online-gaming ban under new legislation);
  • withdrawal of promoter or parent support; parent's own insolvency;
  • loss of a customer contributing a large share of revenue;
  • catastrophic event without adequate insurance;
  • failure of a planned fund-raise on which the going concern assessment depended;
  • Board resolution to sell all business undertakings or apply for voluntary liquidation under Sec 59 of the IBC.

Worked illustration

Sunrise Aviation Services Ltd (ground handling) at 31 March 2026: net worth ₹40 crore; term loans ₹160 crore (non-current ₹120 crore); right-of-use assets ₹70 crore; PPE ₹110 crore. On 20 April 2026, its sole airline customer (75% of revenue) suspends operations and files for insolvency. On 10 May 2026, lenders recall loans; on 22 May 2026, the Board resolves that there is no realistic alternative to ceasing operations. Board approval of accounts: 28 May 2026.

Consequences for FY 2025-26 statements:

  • going concern basis abandoned under Ind AS 10.14, despite the trigger arising after year-end;
  • PPE remeasured to estimated net realisable value (say ₹45 crore) — not merely an Ind AS 36 impairment within going concern;
  • ROU assets written down to recoverable sub-lease or nil value; lease liabilities remeasured for termination penalties if contractually triggered;
  • all borrowings presented as current;
  • receivables from the airline written down for expected recoveries under CIRP (also an adjusting event in its own right);
  • disclosure of the basis, the reasons, and the significant judgements;
  • comparatives remain on going concern basis.

Had the Board instead concluded that a new customer contract and a lender restructuring under negotiation gave a realistic alternative, the statements would remain on a going concern basis with a material uncertainty disclosure, the airline receivable ECL adjusted, and the loan recall assessed under Ind AS 1.69(d).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • "Intends" vs "no realistic alternative": a Board decision to sell the business as a going concern to a buyer is not liquidation of the business, but the entity may still cease trading — analyse at entity level.
  • Group context: a subsidiary that will be merged into its parent under a scheme of amalgamation is not ceasing trading in substance; going concern basis usually remains appropriate.
  • CIRP admission does not automatically mean the going concern basis is inappropriate — the corporate debtor continues as a going concern under the resolution professional (Sec 20 of the IBC); a resolution plan may be approved. Liquidation orders (Sec 33) generally do mean the basis is inappropriate.
  • Delaying approval to see whether a refinancing closes is common; the assessment then covers events to the actual approval date.
  • Interim results: SEBI LODR results must disclose material uncertainty in notes; auditors' limited review reports include the corresponding paragraph.

Key takeaways

  • Going concern is assessed at the approval date using all information to that date.
  • If management intends, or has no realistic alternative but, to liquidate or cease trading, the going concern basis is not used — even if the trigger arose after year-end (10.14).
  • This is a change in the basis of accounting, not an adjustment of individual amounts (10.15).
  • Material uncertainty disclosures under Ind AS 1.25 can be driven by post-year-end events (10.16).
  • CIRP admission is not liquidation; analyse the realistic alternatives carefully.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basis decision [L2]

A real estate company's lenders file a Sec 7 IBC application on 15 April 2026; it is admitted on 12 May 2026; the Board approves the FY 2025-26 accounts on 25 May 2026. Committee of creditors meetings indicate two potential resolution applicants. Should the accounts be prepared on a going concern basis? What disclosures and auditor reporting would you expect?

💡 Hint: Distinguish the effect of CIRP admission under the IBC from a liquidation order.

Q2. Remeasurement under non-going-concern basis [L2]

A company concludes that the going concern basis is inappropriate. At 31 March 2026: PPE ₹80 crore (estimated forced-sale value ₹38 crore, selling costs 3%); inventory ₹25 crore (realisable ₹17 crore net); DTA ₹6 crore; term loans ₹90 crore, of which ₹70 crore non-current, with a 2% prepayment penalty triggered on recall; gratuity liability ₹4 crore (statutory payout on closure ₹5.2 crore). Prepare the adjusted balance sheet extract and the basis-of-preparation note.

Q3. Comparing two post-year-end shocks [L3]

Company A's only plant (insured at 50%) is destroyed by fire on 10 April 2026; Company B loses its main export market on 5 May 2026 due to a foreign government's import ban. Both have Board approval dates in late May. For each, set out the analysis under Ind AS 10.14–16 and Ind AS 1.25–26, the evidence management should assemble, and the possible outcomes for the financial statements and audit report.
