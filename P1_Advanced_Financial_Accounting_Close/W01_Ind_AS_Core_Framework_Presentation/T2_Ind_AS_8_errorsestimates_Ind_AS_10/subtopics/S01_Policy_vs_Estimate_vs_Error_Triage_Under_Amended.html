Subtopic note

Policy vs Estimate vs Error Triage Under Amended Ind AS 8

Scope: Policy vs estimate vs error triage; 2023 definition of accounting estimates — changes in measurement techniques/inputs are estimate changes

Why it matters

Every year-end close throws up "changes": a revised useful life, a new ECL model, a discovered omission, a decision to move investment property to fair value disclosure. Ind AS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors routes each to a different treatment — retrospective, prospective or retrospective restatement — with very different effects on current profit, comparatives, EPS and the auditor's report. Getting the triage wrong is a recurring NFRA observation (prior-period errors dressed up as estimate changes to avoid restating). For an FP&A or reporting analyst, the triage decides whether the variance against budget sits in this year's P&L or in opening retained earnings.

Core notes

The three buckets

Bucket Definition (Ind AS 8.5) Treatment
Accounting policy The specific principles, bases, conventions, rules and practices applied in preparing and presenting financial statements Change only if required by an Ind AS or if it results in reliable and more relevant information (8.14); apply retrospectively (8.19, 8.22) unless transitional provisions say otherwise or impracticable
Accounting estimate Monetary amounts in financial statements that are subject to measurement uncertainty Change recognised prospectively in P&L of the period of change and/or future periods (8.36), or by adjusting the related asset/liability/equity (8.37)
Prior period error Omissions from, and misstatements in, financial statements for one or more prior periods arising from failure to use, or misuse of, reliable information that was available when those statements were approved and could reasonably be expected to have been obtained Material errors corrected retrospectively in the first financial statements approved after discovery (8.42)

The 2023 amendment: accounting estimates

The Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules 2023 (effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023) replaced the old definition of "change in accounting estimate" with a definition of accounting estimates themselves:

  • Accounting estimates are monetary amounts in financial statements that are subject to measurement uncertainty (8.5).
  • Accounting policies may require items to be measured at monetary amounts that cannot be observed directly and must be estimated; an entity develops accounting estimates using measurement techniques and inputs (8.32–32A). Measurement techniques include estimation techniques (for example, those used to measure a loss allowance for expected credit losses) and valuation techniques (for example, those used to measure fair value of an unquoted share).
  • The effects on an accounting estimate of a change in an input or a change in a measurement technique are changes in accounting estimates unless they result from the correction of prior period errors (8.34A).

So switching from a discounted cash flow technique to a market-multiples technique for a Level 3 fair value, changing the ECL provision matrix to a forward-looking PD × LGD model, or updating mortality tables in an actuarial valuation are all estimate changes, not policy changes. The policy is the measurement basis (fair value, amortised cost, present value of the obligation); the technique and inputs used to reach the number are the estimate.

Triage questions

Work through these in order:

  1. Was the previous treatment wrong when the financial statements were approved, given information that was available or reasonably obtainable? If yes → error. Mathematical mistakes, misapplication of policies, oversights, misinterpretation of facts and fraud are errors (8.5).
  2. Is the change in the measurement basis or in recognition/presentation principles? (Cost model to revaluation model; FIFO to weighted average; capitalising vs expensing borrowing costs in a permitted choice; presenting a class of items differently.) If yes → policy change.
  3. Is the change in a measurement technique or input used to arrive at a monetary amount under an unchanged policy? If yes → estimate change.
  4. Still unclear? Ind AS 8.35: when it is difficult to distinguish a change in policy from a change in estimate, the change is treated as a change in accounting estimate.

Not a policy change (8.16): applying a policy to transactions that differ in substance from those previously occurring, or applying a new policy to transactions that did not occur previously or were immaterial.

Hindsight and the estimate–error boundary

An estimate that later proves wrong is not an error if the estimate was reasonable based on information available at the time. A provision for a court case of ₹5 crore settled for ₹9 crore after year-end is a change in estimate (or an adjusting event if before approval), not an error. It becomes an error if management ignored a legal opinion indicating ₹9 crore that was available before approval. The test is the quality of the process and the availability of information, not the outcome.

Worked illustration

Konkan Logistics Ltd, FY 2025-26:

  • (a) Trucks costing ₹120 crore, purchased 1 April 2022, depreciated SLM over 10 years (nil residual). On 1 April 2025, based on a technical study, remaining life is revised to 4 years. Estimate change: carrying amount at 1 April 2025 = 120 − 36 = ₹84 crore; FY 2025-26 depreciation = 84 ÷ 4 = ₹21 crore (versus ₹12 crore). Additional charge ₹9 crore in current P&L; disclose under 8.39.
  • (b) In FY 2024-25, ₹6 crore of repairs were capitalised to trucks in breach of Ind AS 16.12. Error: restate FY 2024-25, adjusting PPE, depreciation, deferred tax and retained earnings.
  • (c) The company moves from WDV to SLM for computers. Estimate change, prospective, even though often described in Indian practice as a "change in policy".

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Change in estimate triggered by a new policy: the policy part is retrospective; the estimate part is prospective. Separate them.
  • Correcting an estimate that was not updated for available information (e.g., an ECL model never recalibrated despite three years of defaults) can be an error.
  • Change in presentation (e.g., moving an item between "other expenses" and "employee benefits") is reclassification under Ind AS 1.41, not Ind AS 8, unless it corrects a misclassification.
  • Voluntary policy change to align with group policy after an acquisition: acceptable only if the new policy gives reliable and more relevant information; document the relevance argument.
  • Ind AS 8.35 presumption should not be used to avoid restating an obvious error.

Key takeaways

  • Policies are measurement bases and principles; estimates are monetary amounts under measurement uncertainty; errors are failures to use available information.
  • Since the 2023 amendment, changes in measurement techniques or inputs are estimate changes unless they correct errors (8.34A).
  • Depreciation method changes are estimate changes (Ind AS 16.61).
  • When policy vs estimate is unclear, treat as an estimate (8.35); this never converts an error into an estimate.
  • Hindsight does not create errors; ignoring available information does.

Practice Questions

Q1. Triage ten changes [L1]

Classify each as policy change, estimate change, error or none, citing the paragraph: (a) change in residual value of aircraft; (b) switch from cost to fair value for FVOCI-designated equity (designation was made last year); (c) change in discount rate for gratuity; (d) omission of a ₹4 crore accrual for March power bills discovered in July; (e) moving from expensing to capitalising borrowing costs on qualifying assets; (f) change in ECL from 12-month to lifetime for a portfolio after significant increase in credit risk; (g) a new valuation technique for Level 3 unquoted investments; (h) recognising a lease previously treated as short-term in error; (i) early adoption of an amendment permitted by MCA; (j) changing the operating cycle from 12 to 24 months after entering EPC business.

Q2. Useful-life revision computation [L2]

A machine bought on 1 April 2021 for ₹48 crore (residual ₹3 crore, life 15 years, SLM) is reassessed on 1 April 2025: remaining life 6 years, residual ₹1 crore. Compute FY 2025-26 depreciation and pass the entry. Then assume the reassessment reveals that a technical report available in May 2024 (before FY 2023-24 accounts were approved) already indicated a total life of 10 years. Explain how the treatment changes.

💡 Hint: The second part turns on whether information was available when earlier financial statements were approved.

Q3. Measurement technique change [L2]

A company measures an unquoted 18% stake at fair value through OCI. Until FY 2024-25 it used DCF (fair value ₹140 crore at 31 March 2025). For FY 2025-26 it switches to an EV/EBITDA multiple because the investee is now profitable and comparable listed peers exist; fair value at 31 March 2026 is ₹175 crore (DCF would have given ₹160 crore). Classify the change, describe the accounting and disclosures, and comment on the Ind AS 113 disclosure of changes in valuation technique.

Q4. Audit committee question [L3]

The audit committee asks why the CFO can treat a revision in the ECL model that adds ₹22 crore of allowance as an estimate change in FY 2025-26, when internal audit found that the old model had not been back-tested for three years. Draft the analysis you would present, including the evidence that would tip the conclusion towards an error.
