Subtopic note

Reopening and Revising Accounts: Sec 130, Sec 131 vs Ind AS 8

Scope: Companies Act overlay: Sec 130 (reopening on NCLT/court order) and Sec 131 (voluntary revision with NCLT approval) vs Ind AS 8 restatement in next FS

Why it matters

Ind AS 8 corrects prior-period errors in the next set of financial statements, leaving the originally approved statements untouched. The Companies Act 2013 adds two routes that actually reopen or revise the earlier statements: Sec 130 (involuntary, on a Tribunal or court order) and Sec 131 (voluntary, with Tribunal approval). These routes have been used in high-profile Indian cases — the reopening of IL&FS group accounts and others following fraud investigations — and are considered when errors are pervasive or when the earlier statements are relied upon for legal purposes (tax, regulatory filings, insolvency claims). A technical accountant must know when the default Ind AS 8 route is enough and when the statute is invoked.

Core notes

Sec 130 — reopening of accounts on court or Tribunal order

A company shall not reopen its books of account and shall not recast its financial statements, unless an application is made by the Central Government, the Income-tax authorities, SEBI, any other statutory regulatory body or authority, or any person concerned, and an order is made by a court of competent jurisdiction or the Tribunal (NCLT) to the effect that:

  • (i) the relevant earlier accounts were prepared in a fraudulent manner; or
  • (ii) the affairs of the company were mismanaged during the relevant period, casting a doubt on the reliability of financial statements.

Key features:

  • The court or Tribunal must give notice to the Central Government, Income-tax authorities, SEBI or the concerned regulatory body and consider their representations before passing the order (proviso to Sec 130(1)).
  • Accounts revised or recast under Sec 130 are final (Sec 130(1) explanation).
  • No order shall be made in respect of reopening books of account relating to a period earlier than eight financial years immediately preceding the current financial year (Sec 130(2)), except where the Central Government has directed a longer retention period under Sec 128(5).
  • It is involuntary: the company does not choose it.

Sec 131 — voluntary revision of financial statements or Board's report

If it appears to the directors that the financial statements or the Board's report do not comply with Sec 129 (financial statements) or Sec 134 (Board's report), they may prepare revised financial statements or a revised report in respect of any of the three preceding financial years, after obtaining approval of the Tribunal on an application in the prescribed form.

Conditions and limits:

  • The Tribunal gives notice to the Central Government and the Income-tax authorities and considers their representations.
  • Revision is permitted not more than once in a financial year.
  • The detailed reasons for revision shall be disclosed in the Board's report in the relevant financial year in which revision is made.
  • Where the original statements or report were sent to members, filed with the Registrar or laid before the AGM, revisions are confined to correcting the respects in which they did not comply with Sec 129 or 134 and making necessary consequential alterations (Sec 131(2)).
  • The revised statements require a fresh audit report.
  • The Companies (Accounts) Rules and NCLT Rules prescribe the procedure (application, notice, filing of revised statements).

Ind AS 8 restatement — the default route

Under Ind AS 8.42, a material prior-period error is corrected retrospectively in the first financial statements approved after discovery: comparatives are restated, opening balances adjusted. The earlier statements, approved and adopted, are not withdrawn; users see the corrected comparative in the next set. No Tribunal involvement is required, and this is the route in the vast majority of Indian cases.

Interaction and practical choice

The routes are not mutually exclusive. A company that revises FY 2023-24 statements under Sec 131 will present the revised figures as comparatives in later statements; if the revision is made after the next statements were approved, Ind AS 8 principles govern how the correction flows into the subsequent comparatives. Considerations favouring Sec 131:

  • the earlier statements are the basis of a pending transaction (IPO, scheme of arrangement valuation, lender covenant certification) and must be corrected at source;
  • the error affects the Board's report (e.g., misstatement in related-party disclosures under Sec 188, CSR under Sec 135) and stakeholders need a corrected statutory document;
  • dividends were declared out of profits that did not exist (Sec 123), raising legality issues.

Considerations against: cost, time, Tribunal scrutiny, publicity, and the possibility that the Tribunal, the Central Government or tax authorities raise further questions. Errors that do not amount to non-compliance with Sec 129 (immaterial items) are not a basis for revision.

Worked illustration

In August 2025, Vasundhara Infra Ltd (unlisted) discovers that FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 revenue was overstated by ₹45 crore and ₹30 crore respectively through fictitious invoicing by a regional team. The FY 2024-25 statements were approved by the Board on 30 May 2025, sent to members and filed with the Registrar; the AGM is scheduled for 26 September 2025.

Options:

  • Ind AS 8 route: correct in FY 2025-26 statements — restate FY 2024-25 comparatives and opening balances at 1 April 2024 (third balance sheet likely). The FY 2024-25 statements already issued stand; the auditor considers SA 560 (facts that become known after the financial statements have been issued) and whether management should take steps to inform those relying on them.
  • Sec 131 route: the directors apply to NCLT to revise FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 statements because they do not give a true and fair view (non-compliance with Sec 129). Useful because the company is preparing DRHP disclosures and lenders rely on FY 2024-25 statements.
  • Sec 130 risk: SFIO or the tax authorities may separately seek reopening for fraud; the company does not control this.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Before issue vs after issue: if the error is found after Board approval but before the statements are sent to members or filed (Sec 137), the Board may re-approve amended statements (a new approval date) without Sec 131 — document the withdrawal and re-approval.
  • Listed companies: SEBI LODR requires disclosure of revisions to financial results; auditors' revised reports and Reg 30 disclosures may be needed.
  • Tax impact: revised statements do not automatically change tax assessments; revised returns are governed by Sec 139(5) time limits and updated returns under Sec 139(8A).
  • Board's report revision only (e.g., missing CSR disclosure) is possible under Sec 131 without revising the financial statements.
  • Auditor's role: CARO and Sec 143(12) fraud reporting obligations run in parallel to any revision.

Key takeaways

  • Default: correct material errors via Ind AS 8 retrospective restatement in the next FS; originals stand.
  • Sec 131: directors may revise FS or Board's report for any of three preceding FYs, with NCLT approval, once per year, confined to the non-compliance.
  • Sec 130: reopening only on a court/NCLT order on application by regulators, tax authorities or persons concerned, for fraud or mismanagement, up to eight years back; recast accounts are final.
  • Choose the statutory route when the earlier statements must be corrected at source for legal or transactional reasons.
  • Amendments before issue are handled by re-approval, not by Sec 131.

Practice Questions

Q1. Route selection [L2]

For each case, recommend the route (Ind AS 8 next-year restatement, re-approval before issue, Sec 131 or Sec 130) and justify: (a) an ₹8 crore depreciation omission in FY 2024-25 discovered in October 2025 (equity ₹600 crore); (b) a ₹220 crore fictitious revenue scheme spanning FY 2021-22 to FY 2024-25, under SFIO investigation; (c) a related-party transaction omitted from the FY 2024-25 Board's report, discovered in December 2025, with a rights issue planned on the basis of those statements; (d) an error discovered two days after Board approval and before signing of the annual report.

💡 Hint: Consider who initiates, the period limits and whether the earlier statements have been issued.

Q2. Sec 131 process memo [L2]

Draft a process memo for a Board considering a Sec 131 revision of FY 2023-24 statements: list the steps (Board resolution, application to NCLT, notices to authorities, audit, filing), the disclosure in the Board's report, the limits on scope under Sec 131(2), and the effect on FY 2025-26 comparatives.

An interviewer asks why a company would ever go through NCLT under Sec 131 when Ind AS 8 already lets it restate comparatives in the next financial statements. Give a structured answer with two Indian practical examples.
