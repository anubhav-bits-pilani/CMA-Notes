Subtopic note

Ind AS 10 Adjusting vs Non-Adjusting Events and the Approval Date

Scope: Ind AS 10 adjusting vs non-adjusting events; date approved for issue = Board approval date, not AGM adoption

Why it matters

Between 31 March and the Board meeting that approves the accounts (typically May for listed companies under SEBI's 60-day deadline, and up to September for unlisted companies), a great deal happens: customers go into insolvency, court cases are decided, dividends are recommended, plants burn down, acquisitions are signed. Ind AS 10 Events after the Reporting Period decides which of these change the numbers and which only need a note. In audit you test this with subsequent-events procedures (SA 560); in reporting you run a "subsequent events" checklist up to the approval date; in FP&A, adjusting events change the actuals your forecast starts from.

Core notes

Definitions (Ind AS 10.3)

Events after the reporting period are those events, favourable and unfavourable, that occur between the end of the reporting period and the date when the financial statements are approved for issue. Two types:

  • Adjusting events — those that provide evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period;
  • Non-adjusting events — those that are indicative of conditions that arose after the reporting period.

The question is always: did the condition exist at 31 March? The event after year-end may simply be the evidence.

Date of approval for issue (Ind AS 10.4–6)

The process for approving financial statements varies by jurisdiction. Ind AS 10.5–6 explain that where the entity is required to submit financial statements to its shareholders for approval after they have been issued, the financial statements are approved for issue on the date of approval by the Board (or the equivalent management body), not the date when shareholders approve them. Similarly, where management issues statements to a supervisory board, approval is when management authorises issue to that board.

For Indian companies:

  • Under Sec 134(1), financial statements (standalone and consolidated) are approved by the Board before being signed and submitted to the auditor for the report. That Board approval date is the "date approved for issue".
  • AGM adoption under Sec 129(2) and Sec 96 (within six months of year-end, by 30 September) is later; shareholders adopt but cannot amend the accounts (they can only refuse to adopt, which leads to the Sec 131 or re-preparation route). Events between Board approval and AGM are not Ind AS 10 events for those statements.
  • For listed entities, the Board approval of annual audited results under SEBI LODR Reg 33 (within 60 days) is often the same meeting that approves the annual financial statements; if the annual report statements are approved later, the later date is relevant to those statements.
  • Ind AS 10.17 requires disclosure of the date of approval and who gave that approval; if the owners or others have the power to amend the statements after issue, disclose that fact.

Adjusting events (Ind AS 10.8–9)

Recognise or adjust amounts to reflect adjusting events. Ind AS 10.9 examples:

Example Condition at year-end Adjustment
Settlement after the reporting period of a court case confirming a present obligation at year-end Obligation existed Recognise or adjust the provision (not just disclose a contingent liability)
Receipt of information that an asset was impaired at year-end — e.g., bankruptcy/insolvency of a customer after year-end Customer's credit-impaired state usually existed Increase ECL allowance / write off
Sale of inventories after year-end giving evidence of NRV at year-end Market conditions existed Write down to NRV
Determination after year-end of the cost of assets purchased, or proceeds of assets sold, before year-end Transaction occurred before year-end Adjust carrying amounts / gains
Determination after year-end of profit-sharing or bonus payments, where a legal or constructive obligation existed at year-end Obligation existed Accrue
Discovery of fraud or errors showing the financial statements are incorrect Misstatement existed Correct

The Indian carve-out on covenant breach waivers (lender agrees before approval not to demand repayment) is treated as an adjusting event — see c06.

Non-adjusting events (Ind AS 10.10–11, 10.21–22)

Do not adjust amounts; disclose material non-adjusting events — the nature of the event and an estimate of its financial effect, or a statement that such an estimate cannot be made (10.21). Ind AS 10.22 examples:

  • a major business combination after the reporting period, or disposal of a major subsidiary;
  • announcing a plan to discontinue an operation;
  • major purchases of assets, classification of assets as held for sale, other disposals, expropriation by government;
  • destruction of a major production plant by fire after the reporting period;
  • announcing or commencing a major restructuring;
  • major ordinary share transactions and potential ordinary share transactions (Ind AS 33.70 disclosure);
  • abnormally large changes in asset prices or foreign exchange rates;
  • changes in tax rates or tax laws enacted or announced after the reporting period that significantly affect current and deferred tax;
  • entering into significant commitments or contingent liabilities (e.g., issuing significant guarantees);
  • commencing major litigation arising solely out of events after the reporting period.

A decline in the fair value of investments after year-end is non-adjusting (10.11) — the decline reflects circumstances arising after year-end. Dividends declared after the reporting period are not liabilities at year-end (10.12–13).

Worked illustration

Raigad Chemicals Ltd, year-end 31 March 2026, Board approval 21 May 2026, AGM 18 September 2026.

Event Date Type Treatment
Customer owing ₹6 crore admitted into CIRP; RP estimates 25% recovery 2 May 2026 Adjusting (financial difficulty existed at year-end) Increase ECL by ₹4.5 crore
Labour court awards ₹1.8 crore for a dispute from FY 2024-25; ₹1.0 crore was provided 10 May 2026 Adjusting Increase provision by ₹0.8 crore
FG stock carried at ₹12 crore sold in April for ₹10.5 crore net of selling costs (prices fell in February) April 2026 Adjusting (NRV evidence) Write down ₹1.5 crore
Fire destroys warehouse (WDV ₹9 crore) 25 April 2026 Non-adjusting Disclose nature, estimated loss and insurance
Board recommends final dividend ₹5 per share 21 May 2026 Non-adjusting Disclose; no liability
Acquisition of 70% of a competitor signed 15 May 2026 Non-adjusting Disclose (Ind AS 103.59 also applies)
Raw material prices crash 30% June 2026 After approval Not an Ind AS 10 event for FY 2025-26

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Customer insolvency caused by a post-year-end event (e.g., a flood in April destroying the customer's plant): the condition arose after year-end — non-adjusting. Evidence about why the customer failed matters.
  • Price decline in inventory after year-end: adjusting if it confirms NRV at year-end (e.g., the price fall began before year-end or the goods were obsolete); non-adjusting if caused by a new event (new competitor launch in May).
  • Tax rate changes: Ind AS 12.47 measures tax at rates enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date. Whether Union Budget rates not yet passed by 31 March are "substantively enacted" is a judgement — check ICAI (ITFG) guidance for the period; rate changes enacted or announced after year-end are non-adjusting but disclosed if significant (10.22(h)).
  • Re-approval: if statements are re-approved (e.g., after the auditor requests changes), the later approval date governs and events up to it must be considered.
  • Consolidated vs standalone approved on different dates: each set has its own cut-off.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 10 window = reporting date to the date the Board approves the financial statements, not the AGM.
  • Adjusting events give evidence of conditions existing at year-end; non-adjusting events reflect new conditions.
  • Customer insolvency, court settlements, post-year-end sales below cost and discovery of fraud/errors are typical adjusting events.
  • Fire, acquisitions, restructuring announcements, market value declines and dividends declared after year-end are non-adjusting; disclose nature and financial effect.
  • Disclose the approval date and who approved (10.17).

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the events [L1]

Year-end 31 March 2026, Board approval 18 May 2026. Classify each event and state the accounting: (a) on 12 April 2026 the High Court upholds a ₹22 crore claim against the company relating to a 2023 contract (provision ₹15 crore); (b) on 30 April 2026 the company's listed investment in a group company falls 35% after a fraud is uncovered at that company that began in 2024; (c) on 5 May 2026 the government announces a cut in the export incentive rate effective 1 June 2026; (d) on 25 May 2026 a major customer defaults.

💡 Hint: For (b), think about whether the investment's fair value at 31 March reflected all information available to market participants then.

Q2. Approval date [L1]

An unlisted company's Board approves the FY 2025-26 financial statements on 28 August 2026, the auditor signs on the same day, and the AGM adopts them on 29 September 2026. On 15 September 2026 a major customer owing ₹8 crore at 31 March 2026 enters liquidation. Explain whether the financial statements must be adjusted, what disclosure is needed, and what happens if shareholders decline to adopt the accounts.

Q3. Full subsequent-events note [L2]

From the following FY 2025-26 events of a listed FMCG company (Board approval 15 May 2026), pass entries where needed and draft the Ind AS 10 note: (a) final dividend of ₹6 per share on 10 crore shares recommended on 15 May 2026; (b) a ₹40 crore acquisition of a brand completed on 2 May 2026; (c) a distributor (receivable ₹3.2 crore, ECL ₹0.2 crore) admitted to CIRP on 20 April 2026 with nil expected recovery; (d) physical verification on 8 April 2026 reveals that stock of ₹1.1 crore recorded at 31 March 2026 had been stolen in February 2026.

Q4. Interview case [L3]

A CFO argues that a 40% fall in steel prices in April 2026 should be treated as an adjusting event for FY 2025-26 because the company's steel inventory "was always going to be sold at these prices". Construct the argument for and against, cite Ind AS 2 and Ind AS 10, and identify the evidence that would decide it.
