Impracticability Relief Under Ind AS 8.43–45

Scope: Impracticability relief (Ind AS 8.43–45) and why it is rarely available

Why it matters

When a restatement is painful — records are old, systems have changed, the people who knew have left — management often reaches for "impracticable". Ind AS 8 does allow relief from full retrospective restatement, but the bar is deliberately high, and auditors and regulators challenge its use. Understanding exactly what "impracticable" means, and what the relief actually permits, prevents both over-claiming (which leads to qualified opinions) and under-using it in the genuine cases (for example, after a data loss or where prior-period fair values cannot be reconstructed without hindsight).

Core notes

The definition (Ind AS 8.5)

Applying a requirement is impracticable when the entity cannot apply it after making every reasonable effort to do so. For a particular prior period, it is impracticable to apply a change in policy retrospectively or to make a retrospective restatement to correct an error if:

  • (a) the effects of the retrospective application or restatement are not determinable;
  • (b) the retrospective application or restatement requires assumptions about what management's intent would have been in that period; or
  • (c) the retrospective application or restatement requires significant estimates of amounts and it is impossible to distinguish objectively information about those estimates that (i) provides evidence of circumstances that existed on the date(s) as at which those amounts are to be recognised, measured or disclosed, and (ii) would have been available when the financial statements for that prior period were approved — from other information.

"Every reasonable effort" is the key phrase: cost and effort alone do not make something impracticable. Reconstructing data from bank statements, vendor confirmations, tax returns or GST portal data is expected.

What the relief permits (8.43–45)

  • 8.43 — A prior-period error is corrected by retrospective restatement except to the extent that it is impracticable to determine either the period-specific effects or the cumulative effect of the error.
  • 8.44 — When it is impracticable to determine the period-specific effects on comparative information for one or more prior periods, the entity restates the opening balances of assets, liabilities and equity for the earliest period for which retrospective restatement is practicable (which may be the current period).
  • 8.45 — When it is impracticable to determine the cumulative effect, at the beginning of the current period, of an error on all prior periods, the entity restates the comparative information to correct the error prospectively from the earliest date practicable.

Equivalent provisions apply to policy changes (8.23–25). Ind AS 8.49(d) requires disclosure, where retrospective restatement is impracticable for a particular prior period, of the circumstances that led to the existence of that condition and a description of how and from when the error has been corrected.

Hindsight is prohibited (8.50–53)

Ind AS 8.53: hindsight should not be used when applying a new policy to, or correcting amounts for, a prior period — either in making assumptions about management's intentions or in estimating amounts. Ind AS 8.52 requires distinguishing information that provides evidence of circumstances that existed then and would have been available, from information that would not. If a significant estimate cannot be reconstructed without hindsight, condition (c) is met.

Why it is rarely available

  1. Information usually exists. Most errors in Indian companies involve recognition or cut-off (revenue, provisions, capitalisation) where invoices, contracts, GST returns and bank records are available for many years. Statutory retention requirements (Sec 128(5): books of account for eight years) make "records not available" hard to sustain.
  2. The relief is period-by-period, not all-or-nothing. If the cumulative effect at 1 April 2024 can be determined (even if the year-by-year split cannot), the entity must restate opening balances under 8.44 — relief only removes the period-specific split.
  3. Estimates are not automatically impracticable. Re-estimating a provision at an earlier date is impracticable only when you cannot separate evidence existing at that date from later knowledge — rare for operational provisions with documentation.
  4. Auditors must concur. SA 710 (comparative information) requires the auditor to consider whether comparatives are appropriately restated; an unsupported impracticability claim leads to modification.
  5. Regulatory review. NFRA and SEBI treat a claim of impracticability as a disclosure that invites scrutiny of internal controls.

Genuine examples

  • Data lost in a ransomware attack or fire with no backups or third-party records, where key prior-period inputs (e.g., physical stock counts) cannot be reconstructed.
  • A change in policy requiring fair values at prior dates for unquoted assets where no contemporaneous valuation was done and market data for that date is unavailable.
  • A policy requiring an assessment of management intent at an earlier date (e.g., held-for-trading designations) that was never documented.
  • An acquired subsidiary whose former owner's records were seized and are unavailable.

Worked illustration

Bharat Agro Ltd discovers in FY 2025-26 that inventory costing had included abnormal wastage for many years. Records from FY 2021-22 onward (ERP implementation) allow period-by-period quantification; earlier periods were on a legacy system that was decommissioned, but the cumulative overstatement at 1 April 2021 can be estimated from the ERP opening balance migration report.

Period Determinable? Treatment
Cumulative effect at 1 April 2024 Yes (ERP data plus migration report) Restate opening balances at 1 April 2024
FY 2024-25 period-specific effect Yes Restate comparative P&L
Years before FY 2021-22 (split by year) No Not needed — cumulative effect captured in 1 April 2024 balances

No impracticability relief is needed for the financial statements, because only the earliest period presented matters. Relief would matter only if the cumulative effect at 1 April 2024 itself could not be determined.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cost-benefit is not a ground. "It would take three months of finance-team time" does not qualify.
  • Partial impracticability: apply the relief only to the element that cannot be determined (e.g., the FY 2024-25 split of an inventory overstatement where opening and closing amounts are known but monthly flows are not).
  • Forensic investigations in progress: if the amount of fraud is not yet determinable at approval, consider whether to delay approval, disclose under 1.125, or obtain an auditor's modified opinion — impracticability is not a holding position.
  • First-time adoption (Ind AS 101) has its own exemptions and exceptions; do not confuse them with Ind AS 8 relief.
  • Policy changes under transitional provisions that prescribe modified retrospective approaches (e.g., Ind AS 116 transition) are not impracticability relief.

Key takeaways

  • Impracticable means not possible after every reasonable effort — not costly or inconvenient.
  • Three conditions: effects not determinable; requires assumptions about past intent; significant estimates impossible to separate from hindsight.
  • Relief is stepwise: restate from the earliest practicable period (8.44), or prospectively from the earliest practicable date (8.45).
  • Hindsight is prohibited in restating prior-period estimates (8.53).
  • Claims of impracticability require disclosure of circumstances (8.49(d)) and attract auditor and regulator challenge.

Practice Questions

Q1. Can relief be claimed? [L2]

For each case, decide whether impracticability relief is available and, if so, which paragraph applies: (a) a ₹14 crore unrecorded liability for GST interest from FY 2019-20 to FY 2024-25, computable from the GST portal; (b) a policy change requiring fair value of an unquoted investment at 1 April 2024, where no valuation exists but audited investee accounts for FY 2023-24 are available; (c) loss of inventory count sheets for 31 March 2024 in a warehouse fire, with sales and purchase records intact; (d) a misclassification of investments between FVTPL and FVOCI where the original designation was never documented.

💡 Hint: For each, ask what information would have been available at the relevant date and whether it can be reconstructed without hindsight.

Q2. Disclosure drafting [L2]

Draft the Ind AS 8.49 note for a company that restates opening balances at 1 April 2025 (the current period) for an error because the period-specific effects for FY 2024-25 could not be determined following a cyber-attack that destroyed FY 2024-25 ERP data. Include the circumstances, from when the error is corrected, and the effect on current-period line items.

Q3. Auditor's view [L3]

Management proposes to correct a ₹38 crore revenue cut-off error that accumulated over FY 2021-22 to FY 2024-25 prospectively in FY 2025-26, claiming impracticability because "the invoicing team has changed and old dispatch records are in physical files". As the engagement partner, prepare the evaluation you would document, including the alternative evidence you would ask for and the reporting consequences under SA 705 and SA 710 if management does not restate.
