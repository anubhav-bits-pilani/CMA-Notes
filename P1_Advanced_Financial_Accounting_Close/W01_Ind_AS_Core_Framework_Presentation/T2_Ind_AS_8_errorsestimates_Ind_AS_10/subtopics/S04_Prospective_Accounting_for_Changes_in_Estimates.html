Subtopic note

Prospective Accounting for Changes in Estimates

Scope: Prospective estimate changes: useful lives, ECL loss rates, warranty rates, actuarial assumptions, total contract cost revisions

Why it matters

Estimate changes are the most frequent "changes" in any close: every quarter, finance revises ECL rates, warranty provisions, actuarial assumptions and project cost-to-complete. They hit current profit directly, so they are prime candidates for earnings management — and also the most common source of budget variances that FP&A must explain. Each estimate has its own mechanics: some are purely prospective (depreciation), some require an immediate catch-up (ECL, warranty, EPC cumulative catch-up), and some go to OCI rather than profit (post-employment actuarial assumptions). Knowing which is which is essential for both the accounts and the forecast model.

Core notes

The general rule (Ind AS 8.36–40)

Ind AS 8.36: the effect of a change in an accounting estimate (other than one to which 8.37 applies) is recognised prospectively by including it in profit or loss in:

  • (a) the period of the change, if the change affects that period only; or
  • (b) the period of the change and future periods, if the change affects both.

Ind AS 8.37: to the extent a change in estimate gives rise to changes in assets and liabilities, or relates to an item of equity, it is recognised by adjusting the carrying amount of the related asset, liability or equity item in the period of change. "Prospective" means no restatement of comparatives — but the remeasured balance at the reporting date is fully updated, so the catch-up flows through the current period.

Disclosure (8.39–40): the nature and amount of a change that has an effect in the current period or is expected to affect future periods, unless estimating the future effect is impracticable (then disclose that fact).

1. Useful lives and residual values (Ind AS 16.51, 16.61; Ind AS 38.104)

Useful life, residual value and depreciation method are reviewed at least at each financial year-end. A revision is applied to the carrying amount at the date of change over the revised remaining life:

\[ \text{Revised annual depreciation} = \frac{\text{Carrying amount at date of change} - \text{Revised residual value}}{\text{Revised remaining useful life}} \]

Example: a CNC machine cost ₹30 crore on 1 April 2022, life 10 years, residual nil. At 1 April 2025 carrying amount is ₹21 crore; remaining life revised to 5 years, residual ₹1 crore. FY 2025-26 depreciation = (21 − 1) ÷ 5 = ₹4 crore (versus ₹3 crore). No adjustment to accumulated depreciation of earlier years. Schedule II of the Companies Act prescribes indicative useful lives; a different life is permitted with technical justification, disclosed in the notes.

2. ECL loss rates (Ind AS 109)

ECL allowances are remeasured at each reporting date. A change in loss rates (provision matrix percentages, forward-looking macro overlays, PD/LGD inputs) or in model technique is an estimate change (8.34A). Mechanically, the full allowance is recomputed at the reporting date and the difference from the opening allowance (after write-offs) is charged to P&L as impairment loss/gain — a catch-up in the current period, not spread forward.

3. Warranty provision rates (Ind AS 37.59)

Provisions are reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted to reflect the current best estimate. A revised claim rate applies to all sales still in warranty at the reporting date, so the catch-up on the opening population is recognised in current P&L:

\[ \text{Closing provision} = \sum_{\text{cohorts in warranty}} \text{Sales} \times \text{Revised claim rate} \times \text{Unexpired fraction} \times \text{Cost per claim factor} \]

Example: two-year warranty; FY 2024-25 sales ₹400 crore (half expired), FY 2025-26 sales ₹500 crore (none expired); claim rate revised from 1.0% to 1.5% of sales at 31 March 2026. Simplified closing provision = 400 × 1.5% × 50% + 500 × 1.5% × 100% = 3.0 + 7.5 = ₹10.5 crore. The increase versus the old-rate figure (₹7.0 crore) is a ₹3.5 crore estimate change in FY 2025-26.

4. Actuarial assumptions (Ind AS 19)

Discount rate (G-sec yield based in India, Ind AS 19.83), salary escalation, attrition and mortality (IALM tables) assumptions are updated at each measurement date. The accounting destination depends on the benefit type:

  • Post-employment defined benefit plans (gratuity, post-retirement medical): the effect of changes in actuarial assumptions is a remeasurement recognised in OCI (Ind AS 19.120(c), 19.128) and never reclassified to P&L.
  • Other long-term employee benefits (compensated absences/leave encashment, long-service awards): remeasurements are recognised in profit or loss (Ind AS 19.156).

These are estimate changes, but Ind AS 19 specifies the location; Ind AS 8.37 accommodates this by adjusting the liability directly.

Example: gratuity DBO ₹50 crore at old assumptions; discount rate falls from 7.2% to 6.8% and DBO rises to ₹52.8 crore. ₹2.8 crore actuarial loss goes to OCI (with deferred tax), not to employee benefits expense.

5. Total contract cost revisions (Ind AS 115.43)

For over-time contracts measured using cost-to-cost, the measure of progress is updated as circumstances change and the change is accounted for as a change in accounting estimate. Mechanically this is a cumulative catch-up:

\[ \text{Revenue this period} = \frac{\text{Cumulative cost to date}}{\text{Revised total estimated cost}} \times \text{Transaction price} - \text{Revenue recognised in earlier periods} \]

Example: EPC contract price ₹100 crore; original estimated cost ₹80 crore. At 31 March 2025: cost incurred ₹32 crore → 40% → revenue ₹40 crore recognised. In FY 2025-26, total cost estimate revised to ₹90 crore; cumulative cost at 31 March 2026 ₹63 crore → 70% → cumulative revenue ₹70 crore; FY 2025-26 revenue = ₹30 crore; FY 2025-26 cost ₹31 crore → FY 2025-26 gross loss ₹1 crore, even though the contract remains profitable overall (₹10 crore). If revised total cost exceeds the transaction price, the entire expected loss is recognised immediately under Ind AS 37 (onerous contract). Changes in the transaction price (for example, constrained variable consideration released) are allocated on the same basis as at inception and also catch up (Ind AS 115.87–90).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Change in depreciation method is an estimate change (Ind AS 16.61), applied prospectively — not restated.
  • Catch-up vs spread: ECL, warranty, decommissioning and contract revenue changes catch up in the current period; depreciation changes spread over the remaining life. Neither restates comparatives.
  • Decommissioning provision changes (Appendix A to Ind AS 16) adjust the cost of the related asset under the cost model, then depreciate prospectively.
  • Interim periods: changes made in Q3 are not pushed back into Q1–Q2 results; Ind AS 34.26 requires annual disclosure if a significant change occurs in the final interim period and no separate report is published.
  • Error disguised as estimate: a warranty rate never updated for available claims data, or a useful life inconsistent with the company's own replacement history, may be an error.
  • Tax effects: most estimate changes create or reverse temporary differences; record the deferred tax in the same location (P&L or OCI).

Key takeaways

  • Estimate changes are prospective: no restatement of comparatives, effect in current and/or future periods (8.36).
  • Balance-sheet estimates (ECL, warranty, contract revenue) are remeasured fully at the reporting date, so the catch-up hits current P&L.
  • Useful-life and residual value changes spread the remaining carrying amount over the revised remaining life.
  • Post-employment actuarial assumption changes go to OCI; other long-term benefit remeasurements go to P&L.
  • EPC total cost revisions use cumulative catch-up; expected losses are recognised immediately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Useful life and residual value [L1]

An asset purchased on 1 October 2021 for ₹18 crore (life 12 years, residual ₹1.2 crore, SLM) is reviewed on 1 April 2025: remaining life 5 years, residual ₹0.6 crore. Compute FY 2025-26 depreciation and the disclosure of the effect on current and future periods.

Q2. EPC catch-up [L2]

An EPC contract has a transaction price of ₹240 crore. Estimated total costs: ₹200 crore at 31 March 2025, ₹215 crore at 31 March 2026. Cumulative costs: ₹70 crore at 31 March 2025, ₹150.5 crore at 31 March 2026. Compute revenue and margin recognised in FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26, and show what would change if the estimate at 31 March 2026 were ₹250 crore.

💡 Hint: Compute cumulative revenue at each date first, then take the difference; check the onerous test separately.

Q3. Gratuity and leave encashment [L2]

At 31 March 2026 the discount rate falls from 7.1% to 6.7%. The actuary reports: gratuity DBO increases by ₹3.4 crore due to the change; compensated absences obligation increases by ₹0.9 crore. Tax rate 25.168%. Pass the entries and show where each appears in the Schedule III statement of profit and loss.

Q4. Forecast-model treatment [L3]

You maintain an FP&A forecast. Mid-year, the company revises (a) the warranty rate from 1.2% to 1.6%, (b) truck lives from 8 to 6 years, and (c) the ECL macro overlay upward. Explain how each change should flow into the reforecast P&L for the remaining months versus the full-year actual, and how you would present the variance bridge to the CFO so that one-off catch-ups are separated from run-rate effects.
