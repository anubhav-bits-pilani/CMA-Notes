Subtopic note

Retrospective Restatement Mechanics for Prior-Period Errors

Scope: Retrospective restatement mechanics: restate comparatives, adjust opening retained earnings of earliest period presented, third balance sheet, restated EPS

Why it matters

When a material prior-period error surfaces — an unrecorded liability, revenue recognised too early, an asset that should have been impaired — the correction must make the comparatives look as if the error had never happened. That is mechanically demanding: every affected line in two balance sheets, the comparative P&L, OCI, SOCIE, cash flow statement, EPS, tax and notes must move consistently. Restatements attract regulator, lender and investor attention (SEBI requires restated quarterly comparatives, and analysts discount management credibility). A technical accountant must be able to build the restatement workpaper; a modeller must know that historical figures in a database may be "as reported" or "as restated" and pick the right one.

Core notes

The rule (Ind AS 8.42)

An entity corrects material prior-period errors retrospectively in the first set of financial statements approved for issue after their discovery by:

  • (a) restating the comparative amounts for the prior period(s) presented in which the error occurred; or
  • (b) if the error occurred before the earliest prior period presented, restating the opening balances of assets, liabilities and equity for the earliest prior period presented.

Ind AS 8.46 adds that the correction is excluded from profit or loss for the period in which the error is discovered. Any information presented about prior periods, including historical summaries (five-year highlights), is restated as far back as practicable.

For FY 2025-26 financial statements with one comparative year:

Error originated in What is restated
FY 2024-25 FY 2024-25 P&L, OCI, cash flows and 31 March 2025 balance sheet; 1 April 2024 balance sheet unaffected
FY 2023-24 or earlier Opening balances at 1 April 2024 (retained earnings, other equity components, assets, liabilities) plus FY 2024-25 figures to the extent affected
Partly before, partly in FY 2024-25 Both: cumulative effect to 1 April 2024 in opening balances, FY 2024-25 effect in the comparative P&L
FY 2025-26 (current year, before approval) Not a prior-period error — correct in current year

Step-by-step mechanics

  1. Quantify the error by year and by line item — pre-tax and tax effects, including deferred tax and current tax (if a revised return is possible or the tax computation also reflects the error).
  2. Compute the cumulative effect at the start of the earliest period presented (1 April 2024) — this adjusts opening retained earnings (or another equity component, e.g., OCI reserve for an OCI item).
  3. Compute the effect on each comparative year line — revenue, expenses, tax, OCI.
  4. Restate the comparative balance sheet(s) at 31 March 2025 (and at 1 April 2024 if a third balance sheet is required).
  5. Restate the comparative cash flow statement — errors affecting classification or non-cash items change CFO components; cash itself does not change unless the error involved cash.
  6. Restate EPS — basic and diluted for each prior period presented (Ind AS 33.64 and 8.49(b)(ii)).
  7. Present the SOCIE with the "changes due to prior period errors" and "restated balance at the beginning" rows (Schedule III).
  8. Decide on the third balance sheet under Ind AS 1.40A — required if the restatement has a material effect on the balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period.
  9. Draft disclosures under Ind AS 8.49 and Ind AS 1.41 if classifications also changed.
  10. Pass the current-year entry — the books for FY 2025-26 are opened with corrected balances; the correction entry is to opening retained earnings, never through FY 2025-26 P&L.

Worked illustration

Indus Foods Ltd discovers in December 2025 that a long-term supply contract has been onerous since FY 2023-24 and no provision was made. Correct provision: ₹12 crore at 31 March 2024; ₹10 crore at 31 March 2025 (₹2 crore utilised in FY 2024-25 against losses charged to cost of sales); ₹7 crore at 31 March 2026. Tax rate 25.168%; provision deductible only when incurred, so a deferred tax asset arises.

Opening position 1 April 2024

Line As reported Adjustment Restated
Provisions (current) 18.0 12.0 30.0
Deferred tax asset 5.0 3.0 8.0
Retained earnings 410.0 (9.0) 401.0

\[ \text{Net effect on equity} = 12.0 \times (1 - 0.25168) = 8.98 \approx 9.0 \]

FY 2024-25 comparative P&L: the ₹2 crore of contract losses charged to cost of sales should have been charged against the provision, so cost of sales falls by ₹2.0 crore; deferred tax expense rises by ₹0.5 crore (DTA reverses); restated profit rises by ₹1.5 crore.

31 March 2025 balance sheet: provision +10.0; DTA +2.5; retained earnings −7.5 (−9.0 + 1.5).

FY 2025-26 entries: first, open the books with the correction:

Account Dr ₹ crore Cr ₹ crore
Retained earnings (opening, 1 April 2025) 7.5
Deferred tax asset 2.5
Provision for onerous contract 10.0

Then account normally for FY 2025-26: utilise ₹3 crore of provision against losses (reducing cost of sales), reverse DTA ₹0.76 crore.

Restated EPS for FY 2024-25: 4 crore shares, reported profit ₹60 crore → reported EPS ₹15.00; restated profit ₹61.5 crore → restated EPS ₹15.38.

Interaction with comparatives in multiple periods

If the entity presents more than one comparative period (voluntarily or in a prospectus), each is restated. Restated financial information in SEBI ICDR offer documents follows its own requirements (restated consolidated financial information with adjustments for errors in the year they relate to), which you will see in IPO work.

Excel workpaper design

Use a matrix: rows = affected line items; columns = "as reported FY 2024-25", "adjustment", "restated", for both balance sheet dates and the P&L. Add a tax column using a named Tax_Rate, and checks: (i) restated balance sheet balances, (ii) change in restated retained earnings = restated profit + restated OCI − dividends, (iii) cash unchanged unless the error involved cash.

=Reported + Adj_PreTax - Adj_PreTax*Tax_Rate*Is_Tax_Effected

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Error in an OCI item (e.g., actuarial loss omitted): restate the OCI reserve or retained earnings component through OCI in the comparative year and the opening balance of the relevant column, not P&L.
  • Tax effect when the tax return cannot be revised (time-barred under Sec 139(5)): the error may create a permanent difference or a current tax effect only in a future year — analyse under Ind AS 12, not mechanically at the statutory rate.
  • Error affecting a subsidiary only in the CFS: allocate the restatement between owners and NCI.
  • Interim periods already published (SEBI quarterly results): restate quarterly comparatives in the next results, labelled "restated".
  • Errors that reverse within the comparative year do not affect any balance sheet; they still require restating the comparative P&L lines and EPS if material.
  • Discovered after approval but before the AGM: the approved statements stand; correction goes into the next set (or a Sec 131 revision — see s07).

Key takeaways

  • Correct material prior-period errors in the first FS approved after discovery — never in current P&L (8.42, 8.46).
  • Restate comparatives for errors in the comparative year; restate opening balances of the earliest period for older errors.
  • Build the restatement line-by-line, including deferred/current tax, OCI, cash flow classification and EPS.
  • Third balance sheet only if the opening position of the comparative year is materially affected (Ind AS 1.40A).
  • Schedule III SOCIE requires explicit "prior period errors" and "restated balance" rows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Restatement entries [L2]

In November 2025, Sabarmati Plastics Ltd discovers that sales of ₹25 crore (cost ₹18 crore) were recognised on 31 March 2025 for goods that reached customers on 8 April 2025 under DAP terms. The goods were excluded from closing inventory. Tax rate 25.168%; the tax return for FY 2024-25 can still be revised. Pass the restatement entries, show the restated FY 2024-25 P&L lines and 31 March 2025 balance sheet lines, and state whether a third balance sheet is needed.

💡 Hint: Trace both the revenue and the inventory effect and check whether FY 2025-26 revenue is also affected.

Q2. Multi-year error [L2]

Capitalised software development costs of ₹9 crore (FY 2022-23), ₹6 crore (FY 2023-24) and ₹4 crore (FY 2024-25) did not meet Ind AS 38.57 criteria; amortisation charged was ₹1.5 crore, ₹3.0 crore and ₹3.8 crore respectively. Deferred tax applies at 25.168%. Compute the adjustment to opening retained earnings at 1 April 2024, the restated FY 2024-25 profit effect, and the carrying amount to be removed at 31 March 2025.

Q3. Restated EPS [L1]

Reported FY 2024-25 profit attributable to equity holders was ₹84 crore; weighted shares 7 crore (no potential shares). A restatement reduces FY 2024-25 profit by ₹5.6 crore and opening retained earnings by ₹12 crore. In FY 2025-26 there is a 1:1 bonus issue. Compute restated basic EPS for FY 2024-25 as it will appear in the FY 2025-26 financial statements.

Q4. Build the workpaper [L3]

Design an Excel restatement workpaper that takes the reported FY 2024-25 trial balance, a list of identified errors (each with year of origin, accounts affected, pre-tax amount and tax treatment) and produces restated balance sheets at 1 April 2024 and 31 March 2025, the restated P&L, SOCIE rows and EPS. Specify the tabs, key formulas and at least four checks.
