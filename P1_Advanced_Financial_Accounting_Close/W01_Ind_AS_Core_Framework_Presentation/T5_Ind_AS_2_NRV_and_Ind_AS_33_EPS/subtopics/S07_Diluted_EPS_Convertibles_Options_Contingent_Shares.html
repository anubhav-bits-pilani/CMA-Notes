Subtopic note

Diluted EPS: Convertibles, Options, Contingent Shares and Sequencing

Scope: Diluted EPS: convertibles (if-converted), options/warrants/ESOPs (treasury stock), contingently issuable shares; antidilution sequencing

Why it matters

Diluted EPS shows the worst-case per-share earnings if all dilutive potential shares were converted or exercised. Indian companies use a wide range of potential ordinary shares — ESOPs (very common in new-age listed companies), share warrants issued to promoters on preferential basis (SEBI ICDR Chapter V), FCCBs, CCDs, CCPS and earn-out shares in acquisitions. Analysts value companies on diluted EPS; management incentive plans often use it; and errors in ranking and antidilution testing are frequent audit findings. In valuation, the same instruments drive the fully diluted share count for per-share equity value.

Core notes

Framework (Ind AS 33.31–40)

Diluted EPS adjusts both numerator and denominator for the effects of all dilutive potential ordinary shares:

\[ \text{Diluted EPS} = \frac{\text{Basic earnings} + \text{After-tax effect of potential shares}}{\text{Basic WANS} + \text{Weighted dilutive potential shares}} \]

  • Numerator adjustments (33.33): add back after-tax dividends and interest recognised in the period on dilutive potential shares, and adjust for any other changes in income or expense that would result from conversion (e.g., employee profit-share linked to profit, effect on non-discretionary items).
  • Denominator (33.36): potential shares are deemed converted at the beginning of the period or, if later, the date of issue. Those cancelled or lapsed during the period are included only for the period outstanding; those converted during the period are included up to conversion (then in basic from conversion).
  • Potential shares are included only if dilutive — their conversion would decrease EPS or increase loss per share from continuing operations (33.41, control number — see c07).

Convertible instruments — if-converted method

For convertible debentures, FCCBs, convertible preference shares:

  • Numerator: add back interest expense (effective interest under Ind AS 109, including the unwinding of discount on the liability component of a compound instrument) net of tax; for convertible preference shares, add back the preference dividend deducted in basic EPS; for FCCBs, also adjust FX gains/losses and fair value changes recognised in P&L.
  • Denominator: add the number of shares on conversion; where there are multiple conversion rates, use the most advantageous to the holder (33.39).

Earnings per incremental share — used for ranking:

\[ \text{EPIS} = \frac{\text{After-tax earnings effect}}{\text{Incremental shares}} \]

Options, warrants and ESOPs — treasury stock method (33.45–47A)

Assume exercise of dilutive options and warrants. The assumed proceeds are deemed to buy back shares at the average market price during the period; the difference between shares issued and shares assumed bought back is treated as issued for no consideration:

\[ \text{Incremental shares} = N \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{EP} + \text{UC}}{\text{AMP}}\right) \]

where \(N\) is the number of options, \(\text{EP}\) the exercise price, \(\text{UC}\) the unrecognised future service cost per option for share-based payments (33.47A) and \(\text{AMP}\) the average market price. Options are dilutive only if \(\text{AMP} > \text{EP} + \text{UC}\). There is no numerator adjustment (EPIS = 0), so options are always the most dilutive and ranked first.

  • Written put options and forward purchase contracts (e.g., obligation to buy back own shares) are dilutive if in the money — reverse treasury stock method (33.63).
  • Purchased options (call options on own shares held) are antidilutive and excluded (33.62).
  • ESOPs with performance conditions (non-market vesting) are contingently issuable shares; time-based vesting options are options under the treasury method from grant date.

Contingently issuable shares (33.52–57)

Included in diluted EPS if the conditions would be satisfied if the end of the period were the end of the contingency period — i.e., based on the status at the reporting date:

  • Earnings target (e.g., earn-out of 10 lakh shares if cumulative PAT exceeds ₹100 crore over 3 years): include if the target would be met based on current earnings at the reporting date (33.53), without projecting future earnings.
  • Share-price target: include if the market price at period-end meets the target (33.54).
  • Other conditions (opening of 10 stores): assume the status quo continues.
  • Shares are included from the beginning of the period or from the date of the contingent share agreement, if later.

Antidilution and sequencing (33.44)

Each issue or series of potential ordinary shares is considered separately, in sequence from most dilutive to least dilutive (lowest EPIS first). Steps:

  1. Compute basic EPS from continuing operations (control number).
  2. Compute EPIS for each potential share class.
  3. Rank by EPIS ascending (options/warrants: zero).
  4. Add each class cumulatively; after each step, recompute diluted EPS from continuing operations. If it increases (or loss per share decreases), that class is antidilutive and excluded; continue with the next.
  5. Apply the resulting set to total profit and discontinued operations EPS.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Average market price for unlisted entities preparing for IPO must be estimated; for listed shares, use an average of weekly or daily closing prices (A4–A5).
  • Options granted mid-year are weighted from grant date; exercised options are included in diluted EPS up to exercise and in basic after.
  • Settlement in cash or shares at the issuer's option: presume share settlement (33.58); at the holder's option, use the more dilutive (33.60).
  • Instruments of subsidiaries convertible into subsidiary shares reduce the parent's share of subsidiary earnings (A11–A12).
  • Loss from continuing operations: all potential shares are antidilutive — diluted = basic loss per share, even if there is a profit from discontinued operations.
  • Warrants issued to promoters under SEBI ICDR (25% upfront, balance within 18 months) — treat as options in diluted EPS; the upfront amount is equity.

Key takeaways

  • If-converted for convertibles: add back post-tax interest/dividends, add conversion shares from the start of the period or issue date.
  • Treasury stock for options: incremental shares use average market price; include unrecognised ESOP cost in proceeds.
  • Contingently issuable shares are included if conditions would be met at the reporting date.
  • Rank potential shares by earnings per incremental share and add the most dilutive first.
  • Exclude any class that increases EPS from continuing operations — the control number.
  • Diluted EPS can never exceed basic EPS.

Practice Questions

Q1. Treasury stock with ESOP cost [L1]

10 lakh options outstanding all year, exercise price ₹200, unrecognised compensation cost ₹40 per option, average market price ₹320. Compute incremental shares. Recompute if the average price is ₹230.

Q2. FCCB if-converted [L2]

Profit attributable to parent ₹250 crore; basic WANS 40 crore. USD 50 million FCCBs outstanding all year, convertible at ₹500 per share (fixed exchange rate ₹83/USD). P&L charges on FCCBs: effective interest ₹28 crore and an FX loss of ₹6 crore. Tax 25.168%. Compute diluted EPS and state whether the FCCB is dilutive.

💡 Hint: Include every P&L effect that would disappear on conversion, after tax.

Q3. Full ranking with an earn-out [L3]

Basic data: continuing profit attributable ₹180 crore, discontinued loss ₹30 crore, WANS 60 crore. Potential shares: (a) 6 crore warrants at ₹40, AMP ₹60; (b) 8% CCDs ₹300 crore, convertible into 10 crore shares, effective interest ₹27 crore; (c) 12% CCPS ₹50 crore convertible into 1 crore shares, dividend ₹6 crore; (d) earn-out of 2 crore shares if FY 2025-26 PAT of an acquired subsidiary exceeds ₹40 crore — FY 2024-25 PAT ₹42 crore. Tax 25%. Compute diluted EPS for continuing, discontinued and total operations.

Q4. Interview — why can't diluted exceed basic? [L1]

Explain the antidilution rule, the role of the control number, and why a company with a net loss shows identical basic and diluted loss per share.
