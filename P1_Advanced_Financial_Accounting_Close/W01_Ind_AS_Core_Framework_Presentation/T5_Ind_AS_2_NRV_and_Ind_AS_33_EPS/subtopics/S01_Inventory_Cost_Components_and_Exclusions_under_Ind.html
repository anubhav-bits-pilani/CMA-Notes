Subtopic note

Inventory Cost Components and Exclusions under Ind AS 2

Scope: Cost components: purchase, conversion, fixed overhead on normal capacity; exclusions — abnormal wastage, storage, admin, selling costs

Why it matters

The cost of inventory is the base for gross margin, the NRV comparison and the inventory value lenders use in drawing-power statements. Over-absorbing overhead in a low-volume year inflates inventory and profit; under-absorbing excludes legitimate cost. Auditors routinely challenge overhead absorption rates, capitalisation of storage and administrative costs, and treatment of abnormal losses. In FP&A, standard cost and absorption assumptions drive product profitability analyses and the cost-volume-profit models built from CMA-style cost sheets.

Core notes

Scope reminder

Ind AS 2 applies to inventories except financial instruments, biological assets and agricultural produce at the point of harvest (Ind AS 41), and work in progress under construction contracts now within Ind AS 115 (costs to fulfil). Commodity broker-traders measuring at fair value less costs to sell are outside the measurement requirements (Ind AS 2.3(b)).

Components of cost (Ind AS 2.10)

Cost comprises all costs of purchase, costs of conversion and other costs incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and condition.

1. Costs of purchase (2.11). Purchase price + import duties and other non-recoverable taxes + freight inwards, handling and other costs directly attributable to acquisition − trade discounts, rebates and similar items. Points specific to India:

  • GST input tax credit available is excluded; GST that is not creditable (e.g., blocked credits under Sec 17(5) CGST Act or exempt supplies) is included.
  • Basic customs duty is included; IGST on imports (creditable) excluded.
  • Volume rebates from suppliers reduce cost of inventory if they relate to items still on hand.
  • Purchases on deferred settlement terms: the difference between the normal credit price and the amount paid is recognised as interest expense over the financing period (2.18).
  • Exchange differences on foreign-currency purchase payables are not part of inventory cost (they are Ind AS 21 P&L items), except where Ind AS 23 borrowing-cost rules apply to qualifying inventory.

2. Costs of conversion (2.12–2.14). Direct labour + systematic allocation of fixed and variable production overheads.

  • Variable production overheads (indirect materials, power varying with output) are allocated on actual use of production facilities.
  • Fixed production overheads (factory depreciation, maintenance, factory management) are allocated based on normal capacity — the production expected on average over a number of periods under normal circumstances, taking into account loss of capacity from planned maintenance (2.13). Actual production may be used if it approximates normal capacity.
  • In a period of low production or idle plant, the fixed overhead per unit is not increased; unallocated overhead is expensed.
  • In a period of abnormally high production, the fixed overhead per unit is decreased so that inventory is not measured above cost.
  • Joint products and by-products (2.14): allocate conversion costs on a rational and consistent basis (relative sales value at split-off or at completion); immaterial by-products may be measured at NRV and deducted from the cost of the main product.

3. Other costs (2.15). Included only to the extent incurred in bringing inventories to their present location and condition — e.g., non-production overheads or product design costs for specific customers. Borrowing costs are included only for qualifying inventories under Ind AS 23 (inventories that take a substantial period to get ready for sale, e.g., real estate, maturing whisky, shipbuilding); routinely manufactured inventories over short periods are not qualifying assets.

Exclusions (Ind AS 2.16)

Recognised as expenses when incurred:

Excluded cost Rationale Example
Abnormal amounts of wasted materials, labour or other production costs Not part of bringing goods to present condition Scrap from a machine breakdown above normal process loss
Storage costs, unless necessary in the production process before a further production stage Storage of finished goods does not add to condition Finished-goods warehouse rent; include maturation storage of wine/cheese or intermediate storage between stages
Administrative overheads not contributing to bringing inventories to present location and condition Not production-related Corporate finance, HR, head office
Selling costs Incurred after inventory is ready for sale Advertising, sales commission, outward freight to customers

Normal vs abnormal loss. Normal process loss (evaporation, inherent wastage within technical norms) is absorbed in the cost of good units; abnormal loss is expensed. Cost accountants will recognise the CAS-style distinction; Ind AS 2 adopts the same logic.

Illustration: fixed overhead absorption (₹)

A tyre plant has fixed production overhead of ₹24 crore per year, normal capacity 12 lakh tyres. FY 2024-25 actual output was 9 lakh tyres because of a two-month strike; closing finished goods 1.5 lakh tyres. Variable cost per tyre ₹2,800.

Correct (normal capacity) Incorrect (actual output)
Fixed OH rate per tyre 24 crore ÷ 12 lakh = ₹200 24 crore ÷ 9 lakh = ₹266.67
Cost per tyre ₹3,000 ₹3,066.67
Closing inventory (1.5 lakh) ₹45.00 crore ₹46.00 crore
Unabsorbed overhead expensed ₹6 crore (3 lakh × ₹200) Nil

Using actual output overstates closing inventory by ₹1 crore and defers part of the strike's cost into next year.

Standard costing and retail method

Standard costs and the retail method are allowed for convenience if results approximate cost (2.21–2.22) — covered with cost formulas in s02.

Service providers

For service providers, the old "inventory of a service provider" guidance (directly engaged labour and attributable overheads, excluding sales and administrative personnel) was removed when Ind AS 115 was issued; costs incurred to fulfil a service contract are now assessed under Ind AS 115.95–98 (costs to fulfil a contract) rather than as inventory. The same exclusion logic for sales and general administrative costs applies (115.98).

Excel implementation

Build a cost sheet with blocks for material, labour, variable OH and fixed OH. Compute the fixed OH rate as =FixedOH / MAX(NormalCapacity, ActualOutput) — this handles both the low-production rule (use normal capacity) and the high-production rule (use actual output, reducing the rate). Unabsorbed overhead = =FixedOH - Rate*ActualOutput, which goes to the P&L.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Normal capacity judgement: a new plant ramping up has no history — use designed capacity adjusted for expected ramp-up; avoid using actual low output.
  • Planned maintenance shutdowns are part of normal capacity; an unplanned two-month shutdown is not.
  • Inward freight vs outward freight: freight to bring goods to the factory or between plants (to present location) is included; freight to customers is a selling cost.
  • Packing materials: primary packing (needed to make the product saleable) is cost; secondary packing for despatch is selling.
  • Depreciation of ROU assets for factory leases (Ind AS 116) is fixed production overhead.
  • CSR, statutory audit fees, legal costs are administrative — never in inventory.

Key takeaways

  • Cost = purchase + conversion + other costs to bring inventory to present location and condition.
  • Fixed production overhead is absorbed at normal capacity; unabsorbed overhead in low-output periods is expensed.
  • Creditable GST is excluded; non-creditable taxes and duties are included.
  • Abnormal wastage, storage (unless part of production), admin overhead and selling costs are excluded.
  • Deferred-payment purchases separate the interest element; borrowing costs only for qualifying inventories.
  • Use MAX(normal capacity, actual output) as the absorption denominator in models.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the costs [L1]

State whether each is included in inventory cost: (i) customs duty on imported resin; (ii) IGST on the same import (full credit available); (iii) salary of the factory manager; (iv) CFO's salary; (v) rent of a finished-goods depot in Bhiwandi; (vi) rent of an ageing warehouse for whisky; (vii) scrap within the 2% technical norm; (viii) scrap of 7% due to operator error; (ix) outward freight to dealers; (x) depreciation of a leased factory ROU asset.

Q2. Overhead absorption in a high-volume year [L2]

A packaging plant's fixed production overhead is ₹18 crore; normal capacity 6 crore units; actual output 7.2 crore units; closing inventory 0.9 crore units; variable cost ₹12 per unit. Compute the cost per unit and closing inventory under Ind AS 2, and explain why the normal-capacity rate is not used.

💡 Hint: Inventory must not be measured above cost when production is abnormally high.

Q3. Deferred-credit purchase and supplier rebate [L2]

On 1 January 2025 a trader buys 10,000 units at ₹1,000 payable in 12 months; the cash price is ₹920. A supplier volume rebate of ₹40 per unit is earned on the same lot. At 31 March 2025, 4,000 units remain unsold. Compute the inventory carrying amount and the interest expense for FY 2024-25 (use straight-line accrual of the financing element for simplicity) and pass entries.

Q4. Cost audit vs Ind AS 2 [L3]

A company maintains cost records under the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules 2014 with CAS-based cost sheets that include a share of head-office costs and finished-goods storage in "cost of production". The statutory auditor notes that the same cost sheet values are used for financial statement inventory. Draft a memo identifying differences between the CAS cost of production and Ind AS 2 cost, the likely direction of the misstatement, and the controls needed.
