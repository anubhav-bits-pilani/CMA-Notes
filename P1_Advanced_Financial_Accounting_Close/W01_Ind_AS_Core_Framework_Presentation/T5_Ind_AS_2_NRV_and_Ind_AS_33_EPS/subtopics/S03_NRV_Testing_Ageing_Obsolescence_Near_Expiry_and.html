Subtopic note

NRV Testing: Ageing, Obsolescence, Near-Expiry and Post-Year-End Prices

Scope: NRV testing: ageing-based slow/non-moving review, obsolescence, near-expiry stock (pharma/FMCG), post-year-end selling prices as evidence

Why it matters

Inventory is carried at the lower of cost and NRV (Ind AS 2.9). Write-downs are where judgement and bias concentrate: management prefers to defer provisions, auditors look for unrecorded obsolescence, and lenders' stock statements (often prepared on cost) can diverge from the books — a difference CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) requires the auditor to report. For pharma and FMCG companies, near-expiry stock and product discontinuations are recurring; for electronics and fashion, technological and seasonal obsolescence. FP&A teams build provision matrices that feed the monthly close and the forecast.

Core notes

When cost may not be recoverable (Ind AS 2.28)

Cost may not be recoverable if inventories are damaged, have become wholly or partially obsolete, their selling prices have declined, or estimated costs to complete or to sell have increased. Writing down to NRV reflects the principle that assets should not be carried above amounts expected to be realised from their sale or use.

Measuring NRV (Ind AS 2.6, 2.30–31)

NRV = estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business − estimated costs of completion − estimated costs necessary to make the sale (detailed formula in c01). Estimates are based on the most reliable evidence available at the time the estimates are made, taking into account price or cost fluctuations directly relating to events after the period that confirm conditions existing at the end of the period (2.30). NRV is entity-specific — it is not fair value (2.7). Inventory held to satisfy firm sales contracts uses the contract price; quantities beyond contract use general selling prices (2.31).

Write-downs are normally item by item; grouping of similar or related items is allowed where they relate to the same product line, have similar purposes or end uses, are produced and marketed in the same geographical area and cannot be practicably evaluated separately (2.29). Writing down an entire class (e.g., "all finished goods") or a segment is not appropriate.

1. Ageing-based slow-moving and non-moving review

Most Indian companies use an ageing-based provision matrix as a starting point, with overlays for specific items:

Ageing since last movement / receipt Provision % (illustrative)
0–180 days 0%
181–365 days 10–25%
1–2 years 50%
> 2 years or no movement in 12 months 75–100%

The matrix is a proxy for NRV — it must be calibrated to evidence: historical realisation of aged stock (sold at what discount, scrapped how much), current demand forecasts, and specific liquidation plans. Ageing should be by date of receipt (FIFO layers) or last-movement date; "last movement" can be reset artificially by inter-warehouse transfers, so auditors look at consumption velocity (months of cover = quantity on hand ÷ average monthly consumption).

2. Obsolescence

Specific identification of items: discontinued models, superseded spares after equipment replacement, design changes, regulatory bans (e.g., single-use plastics, banned fixed-dose combinations in pharma). Obsolete items are written down to scrap value less disposal costs. Spares that are PPE under Ind AS 16 (expected to be used for more than one period) are tested under Ind AS 36, not Ind AS 2.

3. Near-expiry stock (pharma and FMCG)

Indicators: shelf life remaining below the minimum accepted by distributors (often 60–75% residual shelf life required on dispatch), stockists' return policy for expired goods, Drugs and Cosmetics Rules requirements on expired drug destruction, FSSAI rules on food. Approach:

  • bucket stock by residual shelf life (e.g., < 3 months, 3–6, 6–12, > 12);
  • estimate sales probability and price realisation per bucket from history (including liquidation channels — institutional sales, exports to markets accepting shorter shelf life);
  • provide for expected expiries and for returns from the channel (the latter interacts with Ind AS 115 refund liabilities on goods sold with return rights).

4. Post-year-end selling prices as evidence

Sales after the reporting date are the best evidence of NRV at the reporting date if they confirm conditions existing at that date (Ind AS 10.9(b)(ii) lists sale of inventories after the reporting period as an adjusting event providing evidence of NRV). Distinguish:

  • Adjusting: a price cut in April because the product was already obsolete or overstocked at 31 March; distress sales of aged stock in April.
  • Non-adjusting: a price fall due to a new competitor launch, a GST rate change or a commodity crash occurring after year-end, unrelated to conditions at the reporting date — NRV at 31 March reflects conditions then; disclose if material.

Illustration: provision computation for a pharma company (₹ lakh)

Bucket Cost Basis Expected realisation NRV Write-down
FG > 12 months shelf life 2,400 Selling price ₹3,000 less 8% selling cost 2,760 2,760 —
FG 6–12 months (normal price 800) 600 70% sold at 85% of price; 30% liquidated at 30%; selling cost 8% of realisation 548 504 96
FG < 6 months 150 Institutional sale at 20% of cost; disposal cost 2 30 28 122
Discontinued FDC (banned) 80 Destruction only; destruction cost 3 0 0 80 (+ ₹3 liability if an obligation exists)
Total 3,230 298

For the 6–12 months bucket, expected realisation is \(0.7 \times 800 \times 0.85 + 0.3 \times 800 \times 0.30 = 476 + 72 = 548\); selling costs at 8% of realisation are about 44, so NRV ≈ 504 against cost 600. Inventory cannot be carried below zero — the destruction cost of the banned product is a separate liability only if the entity has a present obligation. Always document the selling-cost base and probability weights, because small changes in assumptions move the provision materially.

Accounting and presentation

  • Write-down: Dr Cost of materials consumed / Changes in inventories (P&L), Cr Inventory (or Cr Provision for inventory obsolescence shown as a deduction). The amount of any write-down is an expense in the period it occurs (2.34).
  • Disclose: carrying amount by classification (raw materials, WIP, finished goods, stock-in-trade, stores and spares, goods in transit — Schedule III), amount of write-downs recognised as expense, reversals and the circumstances (2.36).
  • Schedule III requires the mode of valuation of inventories to be stated and goods-in-transit shown separately under each head.
  • Write-downs are typically presented within "changes in inventories" or "cost of materials consumed"; exceptional write-downs (e.g., a product ban) may be disclosed as exceptional items if material.

Model implementation

A Power Query extract of item-level stock with receipt dates and last-movement dates feeds an ageing table; Excel SUMIFS by bucket and category applies matrix percentages; a specific-provision overlay table keyed by item code adds or overrides. A check compares total NRV-basis write-down with the prior year and with actual scrap/liquidation losses (back-testing).

=SUMIFS(Stock[Cost], Stock[AgeBucket], "1-2 yrs", Stock[Category], "FG") * Matrix_1_2yrs

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Fast-moving item with a falling price: ageing shows nothing; NRV test must still compare cost with post-year-end prices.
  • Firm sales contracts at below cost: inventory for the contract is written down to contract price less selling costs; any excess loss on undelivered quantities may need an onerous contract provision (Ind AS 37) if inventory is not yet held.
  • Group-level vs entity-level NRV: intra-group transfer pricing can make inventory loss-making in a subsidiary but profitable for the group — consolidated NRV is tested against the group's external selling price.
  • Seasonal goods after season end: post-season discount history is evidence.
  • "Provision held for prudence" without an NRV basis is not permitted; over-provisioning is an error as much as under-provisioning.

Key takeaways

  • Write down to NRV when cost is not recoverable: damage, obsolescence, price falls, cost increases.
  • NRV is entity-specific, uses the most reliable evidence, and is applied item by item or similar-item groups — never by class or segment.
  • Ageing matrices are a proxy; calibrate them to realisation history and overlay specific items.
  • Near-expiry analysis uses residual shelf-life buckets, channel norms and liquidation routes.
  • Post-year-end sales are adjusting evidence only when they confirm conditions existing at the reporting date.
  • Disclose write-downs, reversals and circumstances; CARO requires reporting of differences with bank stock statements.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ageing matrix application [L1]

A consumer-electronics distributor has inventory at cost: 0–90 days ₹24 crore; 91–180 days ₹6 crore; 181–365 days ₹3 crore; >365 days ₹1.5 crore. Matrix: 0%, 5%, 30%, 80%. Additionally, ₹0.8 crore of the 91–180 bucket relates to a phone model discontinued by the OEM, saleable only at 35% of cost. Compute the provision and state any risk of double counting.

Q2. Post-year-end price evidence [L2]

A steel trader holds 5,000 tonnes of HR coils at a cost of ₹58,000 per tonne at 31 March 2025. Selling price at 31 March: ₹60,000 per tonne; selling costs ₹1,500 per tonne. On 20 April 2025 the government cuts import duty, and domestic prices fall to ₹54,000 per tonne. Separately, 800 tonnes were rusted at year-end and were sold in April at ₹40,000 per tonne. Determine the write-down at 31 March 2025 and whether disclosure is required.

💡 Hint: Separate conditions existing at the reporting date from events arising after it.

Q3. Near-expiry provisioning design [L3]

A pharma company's stockists accept goods only with at least 9 months of residual shelf life and return expired goods for credit. Inventory at the plant: ₹40 crore with >12 months, ₹6 crore with 9–12 months, ₹3 crore with 3–9 months, ₹1 crore with <3 months. Channel inventory with stockists (already sold) ₹30 crore, of which historical expiries returned are 2.5%. Design the provisioning approach for both plant inventory and channel returns, identifying the standards involved and the data you need.

Q4. CARO reporting of stock statement differences [L2]

The company's quarterly stock statement to its consortium bank for March 2025 shows inventory of ₹212 crore; books show ₹198 crore after a ₹9 crore NRV write-down and ₹5 crore of goods in transit excluded from the bank statement but included in books. Reconcile the figures and draft the CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) reporting.
