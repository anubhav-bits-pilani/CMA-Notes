Subtopic note

Cost Formulas: FIFO, Weighted Average and Specific Identification

Scope: Cost formulas: FIFO, weighted average, specific identification; LIFO prohibited; standard cost only if approximates actual

Why it matters

The cost formula decides how purchase-price movements flow into cost of goods sold and closing inventory. In inflationary commodity cycles (steel, edible oil, crude derivatives), FIFO vs weighted average can move gross margin by several percentage points and changes the "inventory gain/loss" commentary that analysts look for in results calls of Asian Paints, Pidilite or oil marketing companies. ERP configuration (SAP moving average price vs standard price, Tally FIFO/average) must be mapped to a permitted Ind AS formula. Any change of formula is a change in accounting policy requiring retrospective application under Ind AS 8.

Core notes

Specific identification (Ind AS 2.23–24)

Required for items that are not ordinarily interchangeable and for goods or services produced and segregated for specific projects — e.g., real estate units, customised machinery, jewellery with certified individual stones, aircraft, vehicles with chassis numbers in dealerships. Each item carries its actual cost. It is inappropriate for large numbers of interchangeable items because the entity could select which items remain in inventory to manipulate profit.

FIFO and weighted average (Ind AS 2.25–27)

For all other inventories, cost is assigned using FIFO or weighted average cost. The entity uses the same formula for all inventories having a similar nature and use to the entity; different formulas may be justified for inventories with a different nature or use (e.g., raw materials in one segment and traded goods in another). A difference in geographical location or tax rules alone does not justify different formulas (2.26).

  • FIFO: items purchased or produced first are sold first; closing inventory reflects the most recent costs. In rising prices, FIFO gives lower COGS and higher closing inventory than weighted average.
  • Weighted average: cost of each item is the weighted average of the cost of similar items at the beginning of the period and those purchased or produced during the period. It may be calculated on a periodic basis or as each additional shipment is received (moving/perpetual average), depending on circumstances.

LIFO prohibited

Ind AS 2 (like IAS 2 since 2005) does not permit LIFO. LIFO matches recent costs to revenue but leaves outdated costs on the balance sheet and is open to manipulation through liquidation of old layers. US GAAP permits it (see topic-level related concept).

Standard cost and retail method (Ind AS 2.21–22)

Techniques for measuring cost may be used for convenience if the results approximate cost:

  • Standard cost reflects normal levels of materials, supplies, labour, efficiency and capacity utilisation. Standards must be regularly reviewed and revised in light of current conditions. If material variances exist, allocate them between COGS and closing inventory so that inventory approximates actual cost under FIFO or weighted average.
  • Retail method (common in retail and apparel) reduces the sales value of inventory by the appropriate percentage gross margin; the percentage must reflect inventory that has been marked down below its original selling price; an average percentage for each retail department is often used.

Illustration: FIFO vs periodic and moving weighted average

Opening stock 1,000 kg at ₹100. Purchases: 5 April 2,000 kg at ₹110; 20 April 1,000 kg at ₹125. Issue on 15 April 2,500 kg; issue on 28 April 800 kg. Closing 700 kg.

Method COGS (₹) Closing inventory (₹)
FIFO 1,000×100 + 1,500×110 + 500×110 + 300×125 = 3,57,500 700×125 = 87,500
Periodic weighted average Avg = (1,00,000 + 2,20,000 + 1,25,000) ÷ 4,000 = ₹111.25; COGS = 3,300 × 111.25 = 3,67,125 700 × 111.25 = 77,875
Moving weighted average After 5 Apr: 3,20,000 ÷ 3,000 = ₹106.67; issue 2,500 = 2,66,667; balance 500 kg = 53,333. After 20 Apr: (53,333 + 1,25,000) ÷ 1,500 = ₹118.89; issue 800 = 95,111 → COGS 3,61,778 700 × 118.89 = 83,222

Total cost of goods available ₹4,45,000 is the same under all methods; only the split differs. In rising prices FIFO gives the highest closing inventory.

Standard cost variance allocation

A company carries finished goods at a standard cost of ₹500 per unit. Actual cost for the year averaged ₹540 (material price variance 6%, efficiency variance 2%). With closing inventory of 50,000 units, inventory at standard is ₹2.5 crore versus approximately ₹2.7 crore at actual. If the 8% variance is not allocated, inventory is understated by ₹20 lakh and COGS overstated. Unfavourable variances arising from abnormal inefficiency (not normal conditions) remain in the P&L — only normal variances are allocated to inventory.

Changing the cost formula

A switch from FIFO to weighted average is a change in accounting policy (Ind AS 8.14) — permitted only if it results in reliable and more relevant information — applied retrospectively with restated comparatives, opening retained earnings adjustment, and a third balance sheet if material (Ind AS 1.40A). Revising standard costs, by contrast, is a change in estimate within the same policy.

ERP and Excel implementation

  • SAP: price control "V" (moving average) maps to moving weighted average; price control "S" (standard) needs periodic variance allocation or actual costing (material ledger) to approximate actual.
  • Tally Prime: stock item costing methods include FIFO, average cost, standard cost and others — "LIFO" options exist in some software and must not be used for Ind AS financial statements.
  • Excel FIFO layer model: maintain purchase layers (date, qty, rate) and allocate issues using cumulative quantity: for each layer, quantity consumed = MAX(0, MIN(LayerCumQty, CumIssues) - (LayerCumQty - LayerQty)); closing value = SUMPRODUCT(LayerQty - Consumed, Rate).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Different formulas across group entities: consolidated statements require uniform policies for like transactions (Ind AS 110.19); subsidiaries using a different formula need adjustment.
  • Commodity traders at fair value less costs to sell (2.3(b)) are outside the formula requirement.
  • Lots with expiry dates may be physically issued FEFO (first-expiry first-out); the accounting formula can still be FIFO or average — physical flow need not match the cost formula.
  • Mixed nature inventory: same item used for both trading and manufacturing — one formula unless use differs substantively.
  • Standard costs not updated for a year of 20% inflation: inventory materially understated — treat the failure to allocate variances as an error if material.

Key takeaways

  • Specific identification for non-interchangeable or project-specific items; FIFO or weighted average for everything else.
  • Same formula for inventories of similar nature and use; location alone is not a reason to differ.
  • LIFO is prohibited under Ind AS 2.
  • Standard cost and retail method are measurement techniques allowed only if results approximate actual cost; allocate normal variances.
  • A change of cost formula is a policy change — retrospective; revising standards is an estimate change.
  • Physical flow (FEFO) and the accounting cost formula are separate questions.

Practice Questions

Q1. FIFO and weighted average computation [L1]

Opening stock 500 units at ₹200. Purchases: 10 May 1,500 at ₹220; 25 May 1,000 at ₹240. Issues: 18 May 1,200 units; 30 May 1,100 units. Compute COGS and closing inventory under FIFO, periodic weighted average and moving weighted average.

Q2. Standard cost approximation test [L2]

A cables manufacturer values finished goods at standard. FY 2024-25 standard cost of closing stock ₹42 crore; total production cost at standard ₹600 crore; actual production cost ₹648 crore, of which ₹12 crore relates to abnormal wastage from a fire. Closing stock represents 7% of production. Determine whether standard costs approximate actual, compute the variance to allocate to inventory, and pass the entry.

💡 Hint: Remove the abnormal component before allocating the remaining variance.

Q3. Change from FIFO to weighted average [L2]

An FMCG company proposes to switch from FIFO to weighted average for FY 2024-25 because its new ERP supports only moving average. Closing inventory at 31 March 2024 under FIFO ₹85 crore and under weighted average ₹82 crore; at 31 March 2025 FIFO ₹96 crore and weighted average ₹91 crore. Tax rate 25.168%. Assess whether this change is permitted, and describe the accounting and disclosure, including the effect on opening retained earnings and comparatives.

Q4. Interview — why LIFO is banned [L1]

An interviewer asks why Ind AS 2 bans LIFO while US GAAP allows it, and how LIFO would affect an Indian refinery's results in a falling crude price environment. Prepare a structured answer.
