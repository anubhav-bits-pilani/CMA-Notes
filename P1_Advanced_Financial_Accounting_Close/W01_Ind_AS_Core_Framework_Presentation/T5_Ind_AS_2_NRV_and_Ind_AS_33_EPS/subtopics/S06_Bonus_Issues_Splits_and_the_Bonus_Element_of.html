Subtopic note

Bonus Issues, Splits and the Bonus Element of Rights Issues

Scope: Bonus issues, splits, rights issue bonus element (TERP) — retrospective adjustment, including events after reporting date (Ind AS 33.64)

Why it matters

Indian companies issue bonus shares and split face value frequently — often around strong results or before index inclusion — and rights issues are common for capital raising (Reliance's 2020 partly paid rights, Bharti Airtel's rights issues). Each changes the number of shares without a proportionate change in resources, so EPS of all periods must be adjusted to stay comparable. Analysts' historical EPS series, management KPIs and ESOP strike-price adjustments all depend on the correct adjustment factor. Errors — failing to restate comparatives or ignoring a bonus approved after the year-end but before the Board approves the accounts — are classic review points.

Core notes

Principle (Ind AS 33.26–28)

The weighted average number of shares for all periods presented is adjusted for events, other than the conversion of potential ordinary shares, that have changed the number of shares outstanding without a corresponding change in resources:

  • Capitalisation or bonus issue (stock dividend) — Sec 63 Companies Act, issued out of free reserves, securities premium or capital redemption reserve;
  • Bonus element in any other issue — e.g., a rights issue at below fair value, or a bonus element in a preferential issue to existing shareholders;
  • Share split (sub-division of face value, Sec 61(1)(d));
  • Reverse share split (consolidation of shares).

The adjustment is made as if the event had occurred at the beginning of the earliest period presented (33.28). No time-weighting is applied to bonus shares themselves; they relate back to the original shares.

Events after the reporting date (Ind AS 33.64)

If the number of ordinary or potential ordinary shares outstanding increases as a result of a capitalisation, bonus issue or share split, or decreases as a result of a reverse share split, after the reporting period but before the financial statements are approved for issue, the per-share calculations for the current period and all prior periods presented are based on the new number of shares, and the fact is disclosed. The same applies to restatements for errors and retrospective policy changes (33.64).

This is a specific exception to Ind AS 10's general approach: the bonus itself is a non-adjusting event for balance sheet purposes (share capital is not increased at 31 March), but EPS is adjusted.

Other share transactions after the reporting period — fresh issues for cash, buybacks, conversions, rights issues — do not adjust EPS; they are disclosed if significant (33.70(d)). Note: a rights issue with a bonus element completed after year-end is not covered by 33.64's adjustment (only capitalisation, bonus, split, reverse split are listed), though disclosure is needed.

Bonus issue / split mechanics

Adjustment factor:

\[ F_{\text{bonus}} = \frac{\text{Shares after bonus}}{\text{Shares before bonus}} \]

A 1:2 bonus (one new share for every two held) gives \(F = 1.5\); a split from ₹10 to ₹2 face value gives \(F = 5\). All share counts before the event — in the current and prior periods — are multiplied by \(F\). Prior-period EPS is divided by \(F\).

Rights issue bonus element (Ind AS 33.A2)

A rights issue at an exercise price below fair value contains a bonus element. The adjustment factor:

\[ F_{\text{rights}} = \frac{\text{Fair value per share immediately before exercise of rights}}{\text{TERP}} \]

\[ \text{TERP} = \frac{\text{Aggregate market value of shares before exercise} + \text{Proceeds from exercise of rights}}{\text{Number of shares outstanding after exercise}} \]

The fair value immediately before exercise is the cum-rights price on the last day shares trade together with the rights (A2). If rights are traded separately from the shares, use the closing price on the last day of trading with rights.

Application:

  • Shares outstanding before the rights issue (in the current period up to the issue date, and the whole prior period) are multiplied by \(F_{\text{rights}}\).
  • Shares issued in the rights issue are time-weighted from the issue date (they brought in resources).
  • Prior-year EPS is divided by \(F_{\text{rights}}\).

Illustration: bonus and rights in the same year

Sahyadri Cement Ltd, FY ending 31 March 2025. Profit FY 2024-25 ₹300 crore; FY 2023-24 ₹240 crore. Shares at 1 April 2023 and throughout FY 2023-24: 40 crore.

  • 1 October 2024: rights issue 1 for 4 (10 crore shares) at ₹120; cum-rights price ₹220.
  • 10 May 2025 (before Board approval on 22 May 2025): 1:1 bonus.

Step 1 — TERP and rights factor.

\[ \text{TERP} = \frac{40 \times 220 + 10 \times 120}{50} = \frac{8,800 + 1,200}{50} = ₹200 \]

\[ F_{\text{rights}} = \frac{220}{200} = 1.10 \]

Interaction with diluted EPS and ESOPs

Potential shares (options, convertibles) are also adjusted for bonus and split events — conversion ratios and exercise prices are typically adjusted by the instrument terms (SEBI SBEB Regulations 2021 require fair adjustment of ESOPs for corporate actions). Treasury-stock computations use adjusted average prices.

Model implementation

Maintain a "capital events" table with a cumulative adjustment factor column. For each historical share count, the adjusted count = raw count × product of factors for events after that date (up to the approval date). In Excel: =Raw * PRODUCT(IF(EventDates > CountDate, Factors, 1)) (array formula) or a cumulative factor column computed backwards.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rights at or above market price: no bonus element; factor = 1.
  • Partly paid rights (e.g., Reliance 2020): shares count as fractions based on dividend participation; the bonus element still uses TERP on the full issue price.
  • Bonus out of securities premium is still a bonus — no resources received.
  • Preferential allotments at a discount to market to promoters: bonus element only applies to issues to all existing shareholders; a preferential issue to selected parties at a discount is treated as an issue at the price paid (no retrospective adjustment).
  • Reverse split / consolidation reduces prior-period share counts; a reverse split combined with a special dividend may be a share repurchase at fair value — then no retrospective adjustment (33.29).
  • Bonus between year-end and approval for quarterly results: SEBI results for the next quarter will show adjusted EPS for all comparative quarters.

Key takeaways

  • Bonus issues, splits, reverse splits and the bonus element in rights issues adjust shares for all periods presented, from the earliest period.
  • Events after the reporting date but before approval also adjust EPS (33.64) — a specific exception to the Ind AS 10 approach.
  • Rights factor = cum-rights fair value ÷ TERP; apply it to shares before the rights issue and to all prior periods.
  • Shares issued for cash in the rights issue are time-weighted from issue.
  • Restated comparatives must be disclosed; prior-period EPS will differ from previously published figures.
  • Model with a cumulative adjustment factor per share-count date.

Practice Questions

Q1. Bonus issue in the year [L1]

Shares at 1 April 2024: 6 crore. A 2:1 bonus (two new shares for each share held) on 1 December 2024. Profit FY 2024-25 ₹90 crore; FY 2023-24 ₹72 crore (shares 6 crore all year). Compute basic EPS for both years as presented in the FY 2024-25 financial statements.

Q2. Rights issue with TERP [L2]

On 1 July 2024, Konark Steel (25 crore shares) makes a 2-for-5 rights issue at ₹150; the cum-rights price on the last day of trading with rights is ₹260. Profit FY 2024-25 ₹410 crore; FY 2023-24 ₹350 crore. Compute TERP, the adjustment factor, WANS and EPS for both years.

💡 Hint: Only the shares in existence before the rights issue are multiplied by the factor.

Q3. Split after year-end and fresh issue after year-end [L2]

Year-end 31 March 2025; Board approves accounts on 25 May 2025. On 2 May 2025 shares are split from ₹10 to ₹1 face value; on 15 May 2025 a QIP of 1 crore (post-split) shares is completed. WANS FY 2024-25 before these events: 8 crore; FY 2023-24: 7.5 crore. Profits: ₹160 crore and ₹130 crore. Compute reported EPS for both years and list the required disclosures.

Q4. Review of an analyst's EPS series [L3]

An analyst's 5-year EPS chart for an NSE-listed company uses originally reported EPS each year. The company made a 1:1 bonus in FY 2021-22, a 1-for-10 rights issue at a 30% discount to the cum-rights price in FY 2022-23, and a split from ₹5 to ₹1 in FY 2024-25. Explain the errors in the chart, describe how to compute the adjustment factors to restate the series, and identify the source data you would use.
