Subtopic note

Basic EPS: Attributable Earnings and Time-Weighted Shares

Scope: Basic EPS: earnings attributable to equity holders of parent; time-weighted shares for fresh issues and buybacks

Why it matters

EPS is the most quoted per-share metric in Indian equity markets — it appears on the face of the Statement of Profit and Loss (Schedule III Division II requires it), in every SEBI Reg 33 quarterly result, in P/E multiples and in management incentive targets. Errors are common: using consolidated profit including NCI, forgetting preference dividends on cumulative preference shares, or using closing rather than weighted shares after a QIP or buyback. Ind AS 33's own scope covers entities whose ordinary shares are traded or are in the process of being listed, and any entity that voluntarily presents EPS; in practice Schedule III Division II requires every company preparing Ind AS financial statements to present basic and diluted EPS on the face of the Statement of Profit and Loss.

Core notes

The formula (Ind AS 33.10)

\[ \text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{Profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent}}{\text{Weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period}} \]

Computed for profit from continuing operations attributable to the parent and for total profit attributable to the parent (33.9). In consolidated financial statements, EPS is based on consolidated information; in separate (standalone) statements, on standalone information (33.4 — present only in the respective statements).

Numerator (Ind AS 33.12–18)

Start with profit or loss attributable to the parent entity (i.e., after NCI share), then deduct the after-tax amounts of preference dividends, differences arising on settlement of preference shares, and similar effects of preference shares classified as equity:

  • Cumulative preference shares classified as equity: deduct the dividend for the period whether or not declared (33.14(b)).
  • Non-cumulative preference shares: deduct only dividends declared in respect of the period (33.14(a)).
  • Preference shares classified as financial liabilities under Ind AS 32 (e.g., redeemable preference shares with a fixed dividend): the dividend is already in finance costs — no further deduction.
  • Increasing-rate preference shares: amortise the original issue discount/premium to retained earnings using the effective interest method and treat as a preference dividend (33.15).
  • Repurchase of preference shares at a premium over carrying amount: deduct the excess (33.16); induced early conversion: deduct the excess fair value (33.17); settlement below carrying amount: add (33.18).

Participating equity instruments and two-class ordinary shares require the two-class method (33.A13–A14) — rare in India except for DVR (differential voting rights) shares, which share profits differently and need allocation.

Denominator (Ind AS 33.19–29)

The weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period is the number at the beginning of the period, adjusted by shares bought back or issued during the period multiplied by a time-weighting factor — the number of days the shares are outstanding as a proportion of total days in the period (a reasonable approximation such as months is often adequate, 33.20).

\[ \text{WANS} = \sum_i N_i \times \frac{d_i}{D} \]

where \(N_i\) is the number of shares outstanding for sub-period \(i\), \(d_i\) the days in that sub-period and \(D\) the days in the reporting period.

Date shares are included (33.21): generally from the date consideration is receivable (usually the issue date):

  • issued for cash — when cash is receivable;
  • on conversion of a debt instrument — from the date interest ceases to accrue;
  • in lieu of interest or principal on other financial instruments — from the date interest ceases;
  • in exchange for settlement of a liability — from the settlement date;
  • as consideration for an asset other than cash — from the date the acquisition is recognised;
  • for services — as services are rendered;
  • in a business combination — from the acquisition date (33.22).

Partly paid shares (A15–A16): treated as a fraction of an ordinary share to the extent they are entitled to participate in dividends relative to a fully paid share.

Contingently issuable shares enter basic EPS only from the date all conditions are satisfied (33.24); shares issuable solely after the passage of time are not contingent. Mandatorily convertible instruments are included in basic EPS from the date the contract is entered into (33.23).

Treasury shares and buybacks. Shares bought back under Sec 68 of the Companies Act are extinguished (Indian law does not permit holding treasury shares, except via ESOP trusts and certain schemes); they are excluded from the date of buyback. Shares held by an ESOP trust consolidated by the company are deducted as treasury shares.

Illustration: QIP and buyback

Indus Tech Ltd, FY 2024-25 (365 days). Profit attributable to parent ₹410 crore; 8% cumulative preference shares (equity-classified) ₹100 crore, dividend not yet declared. Shares:

  • 1 April 2024: 50 crore shares
  • 1 August 2024: QIP of 5 crore shares (cash receivable that day)
  • 1 February 2025: buyback of 2 crore shares
Period Shares Days Weighted
1 Apr – 31 Jul 50 crore 122 16.712 crore
1 Aug – 31 Jan 55 crore 184 27.726 crore
1 Feb – 31 Mar 53 crore 59 8.567 crore
WANS 365 53.005 crore

Numerator: \(410 - 8 = ₹402\) crore (preference dividend deducted although undeclared, because cumulative).

\[ \text{Basic EPS} = \frac{402}{53.005} = ₹7.58 \]

Using closing shares (53 crore) would give ₹7.58 coincidentally close here; using opening shares (50 crore) would give ₹8.04 — the time-weighting matters most when issues are large and early or late in the year.

Excel implementation

Keep a share-event table: date, event type, number of shares (+/−). Weighted shares:

=SUMPRODUCT(Events[Shares], (FYEnd - Events[Date] + 1) / (FYEnd - FYStart + 1))

where opening shares are entered as an event dated FYStart. Restrict events to those within the period using (Events[Date]>=FYStart)*(Events[Date]<=FYEnd) inside SUMPRODUCT.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Loss per share is presented even if negative (33.69) — "Basic and diluted loss per share".
  • NCI with preference shares in a subsidiary: the parent's attributable profit is after the subsidiary's preference dividend attributable to outside holders.
  • Shares issued on 31 March add negligibly to WANS but fully to closing shares — ensure consistency with share capital in the SOCIE.
  • Shares issued in a scheme of arrangement under Sec 230–232 effective from an appointed date (common in Indian mergers): for common-control combinations accounted under Appendix C of Ind AS 103, comparatives are restated as if the combination occurred from the beginning of the earliest period, so shares issued are included accordingly.
  • Share application money pending allotment is not included until shares are issued (or the contract becomes a mandatorily convertible instrument).
  • Partly paid rights shares participate in dividends in proportion to paid-up value — treat as fractional shares.

Key takeaways

  • Numerator: profit attributable to the parent's ordinary equity holders after preference dividends (cumulative deducted whether or not declared).
  • Denominator: time-weighted shares, generally from the date consideration is receivable.
  • Bought-back shares are excluded from the buyback date; ESOP trust shares are treated as treasury shares.
  • Mandatorily convertible instruments count in basic EPS from the contract date; contingently issuable shares only when conditions are met.
  • Present both continuing and total basic EPS; loss per share is still presented.
  • Build a share-event table with day-count weights rather than hard-coding monthly approximations.

Practice Questions

Q1. Weighted shares with multiple events [L1]

Ganga Foods Ltd has 12 crore shares at 1 April 2024. It issues 3 crore shares for cash on 1 September 2024, buys back 1 crore shares on 1 December 2024, and issues 0.5 crore shares to acquire a brand on 1 March 2025. Profit attributable to parent ₹180 crore. Compute basic EPS using months as the weighting basis.

Q2. Preference shares and NCI [L2]

Consolidated profit for FY 2024-25 is ₹520 crore, of which NCI's share is ₹45 crore. The parent has (i) 9% cumulative preference shares of ₹150 crore classified as equity, no dividend declared; (ii) 7% redeemable preference shares of ₹80 crore classified as a liability. Weighted shares 40 crore. Compute basic EPS and explain the different treatment of the two preference instruments.

💡 Hint: Check where each instrument's dividend has already been recognised before deducting anything.

Q3. Partly paid shares and CCPS [L2]

A start-up preparing for an NSE IPO has 2 crore fully paid shares of ₹10 at 1 April 2024. On 1 October 2024 it issues 40 lakh shares of ₹10 at ₹100 (₹10 face), paid ₹50 on application, entitled to dividends in proportion to amount paid. On 1 January 2025 it issues CCPS convertible into 30 lakh equity shares on 1 January 2026 with no options for the holder. Profit ₹24 crore. Compute WANS for basic EPS.

Q4. Interview — standalone vs consolidated EPS [L1]

Explain why standalone and consolidated EPS of an Indian holding company can differ even though the share count is identical, and which figure analysts use for valuation.
