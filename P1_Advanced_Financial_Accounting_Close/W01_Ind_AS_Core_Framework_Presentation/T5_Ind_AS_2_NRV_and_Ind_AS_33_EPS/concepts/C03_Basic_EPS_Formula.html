Key concept

Basic EPS Formula

Concept: Basic EPS = (Profit attributable to equity holders − Preference dividends) ÷ Weighted average equity shares outstanding

Statement

\[ \text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\pi_{\text{parent}} - D_{\text{pref}}}{\text{WANS}} \]

where \(\pi_{\text{parent}}\) is profit or loss for the period attributable to owners of the parent (after NCI), \(D_{\text{pref}}\) is the after-tax amount of preference dividends and other effects of equity-classified preference shares for the period (cumulative dividends whether or not declared; non-cumulative only if declared), and WANS is the weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period, time-weighted from the date consideration is receivable (Ind AS 33.10, 33.12–14, 33.19–21). Computed separately for continuing operations and total profit.

Explanation

The numerator measures what belongs to ordinary shareholders of the parent: start from consolidated profit, remove NCI's share, then remove amounts belonging to preference shareholders whose shares are classified as equity. If preference shares are liabilities under Ind AS 32, their dividends are already finance costs and are not deducted again.

The denominator reflects the resources available to earn profit over the period — hence time-weighting. Shares issued for cash count from the date cash is receivable; shares bought back drop out from the buyback date; bonus and split shares are treated as outstanding from the start of the earliest period (no time-weighting). Mandatorily convertible instruments count from the contract date; contingently issuable shares only once the conditions are met.

Typical numerator adjustments beyond preference dividends:

  • premium on repurchase of preference shares above carrying amount (deduct);
  • excess fair value given on induced conversion (deduct);
  • amortisation of discount on increasing-rate preference shares (deduct).

Common mistakes: using profit including NCI; using profit before preference dividends on cumulative preference shares not declared; using closing shares; including share application money pending allotment.

Illustration

Consolidated profit FY 2024-25 ₹350 crore; NCI share ₹30 crore. Equity-classified 10% cumulative preference shares ₹200 crore (dividend not declared). Shares: 40 crore at 1 April 2024; 8 crore issued for cash on 1 January 2025.

\[ \text{Numerator} = 350 - 30 - 20 = ₹300 \text{ crore} \]

\[ \text{WANS} = 40 + 8 \times \frac{3}{12} = 42 \text{ crore} \]

\[ \text{Basic EPS} = \frac{300}{42} = ₹7.14 \]

Using closing shares (48 crore) would give ₹6.25; ignoring the undeclared cumulative dividend would give ₹7.62.

Practice Questions

Q1. Straightforward basic EPS [L1]

Profit attributable to parent ₹96 crore; non-cumulative preference dividend declared ₹4 crore; shares 20 crore at 1 April 2024, 4 crore bought back on 1 October 2024. Compute basic EPS.

Q2. Loss year with cumulative preference [L2]

Loss attributable to parent ₹25 crore; 8% cumulative preference shares (equity) ₹150 crore, no dividend declared; WANS 10 crore. Compute basic loss per share and explain why the preference dividend still affects it.

💡 Hint: Cumulative entitlement accrues regardless of declaration or profitability.

Q3. Increasing-rate preference shares [L2]

Preference shares (equity-classified) issued on 1 April 2024 for ₹90 crore carry no dividend for two years and 10% thereafter on ₹100 crore face value; the effective rate is 6% per annum. Compute the amount deducted in FY 2024-25 basic EPS and its treatment in equity.
