Key concept

Raw Material Exception and the Write-Down Reversal Cap

Concept: Raw materials not written down if finished goods will sell at or above cost (Ind AS 2.32); reversal capped at original write-down (Ind AS 2.33)

Statement

Raw material exception (Ind AS 2.32). Materials and other supplies held for use in production are not written down below cost if the finished products in which they will be incorporated are expected to be sold at or above cost. When a decline in the price of materials indicates that the cost of the finished products exceeds NRV, the materials are written down to NRV; replacement cost may be the best available measure of their NRV.

\[ \text{Write down RM only if } \text{NRV}_{FG} < \text{Cost}_{FG} \]

Reversal (Ind AS 2.33–2.34). NRV is reassessed each period. When the circumstances causing a write-down no longer exist, or NRV has clearly increased, the write-down is reversed so that the new carrying amount is the lower of cost and revised NRV:

\[ \text{Reversal} = \min\left(\text{Original write-down on items still held},\; \text{NRV}_{\text{new}} - \text{Carrying}_{\text{old}}\right), \quad \text{Reversal} \ge 0 \]

The reversal is recognised as a reduction of inventory expense in the period of reversal.

Explanation

The exception reflects the purpose of holding raw materials: they are realised through finished goods, not sold. If FG are profitable, the material's cost is recovered regardless of its own spot price. The test must be performed product by product using the bill of materials — a material used in a loss-making product line can be written down even while other lines are profitable.

The reversal rule keeps inventory at the lower of cost and current NRV. The cap means inventory never exceeds original cost: the reversal is limited to the amount previously written down on items still on hand. Items sold since the write-down carry their written-down amount into COGS; nothing is reversed for them.

Practical requirements:

  • Keep item-level (or group-level) records of write-downs so reversals can be identified and capped.
  • Present reversals within changes in inventories or cost of materials consumed — not other income — and disclose the amount and circumstances (Ind AS 2.36(f)–(g)).
  • US GAAP prohibits reversals (ASC 330); Ind AS requires them.

Illustration

A battery maker holds 500 tonnes of lead (cost ₹1,90,000/t; replacement cost at year-end ₹1,70,000/t). Batteries made from it: FG cost per unit ₹4,800 (including lead at historical cost); FG NRV ₹5,300.

  • Year 1: FG NRV > FG cost → no write-down of lead despite a ₹1 crore fall in replacement value (500 × ₹20,000).

Suppose instead FG NRV were ₹4,500 (below cost by ₹300 per unit) and the company concludes that replacement cost is the best measure of lead's NRV: write-down = 500 × ₹20,000 = ₹1 crore.

  • Year 2: 200 tonnes of the written-down lead remain; lead replacement cost recovers to ₹1,95,000/t and FG become profitable again. Carrying per tonne ₹1,70,000 → new carrying = min(₹1,90,000, ₹1,95,000) = ₹1,90,000. Reversal = 200 × ₹20,000 = ₹40 lakh — the original write-down on the items still held; the ₹5,000/t above cost is not recognised.

Connections

Expands s04 and applies the NRV formula in c01. Relevant to topic pitfall "writing down raw materials when finished goods remain profitable". Write-downs and reversals change deferred tax where tax treatment differs (Week 3) and flow into EPS (s05). In commodity-business FP&A models, the exception means raw material price shocks affect margins only when they pass through to FG prices.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the exception [L1]

Cotton stock cost ₹2.4 crore (replacement cost ₹2.0 crore). Yarn produced from it: cost ₹3.5 crore; NRV ₹3.8 crore. Determine the carrying amount of cotton and justify.

Q2. Reversal with partial sales [L2]

At 31 March 2024, 50,000 units of a component (cost ₹800) were written down to ₹650. During FY 2024-25, 30,000 units were consumed in production. At 31 March 2025, NRV of the component is ₹720. Compute the reversal and the closing carrying amount, and state the presentation.

💡 Hint: Only the remaining units are eligible for reversal, and the new carrying amount is the lower of cost and revised NRV.

Q3. Choosing between replacement cost and FG-shortfall allocation [L2]

FG cost ₹1,000 per unit (raw material ₹600, conversion ₹400); FG NRV ₹920; raw material replacement cost ₹480. Compute the raw material write-down per unit under both approaches and explain which better reflects Ind AS 2.
