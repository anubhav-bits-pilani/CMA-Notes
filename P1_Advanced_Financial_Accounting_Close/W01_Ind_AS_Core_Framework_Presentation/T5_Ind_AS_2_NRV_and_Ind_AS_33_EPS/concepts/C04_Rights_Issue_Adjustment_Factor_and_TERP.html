Key concept

Rights Issue Adjustment Factor and TERP

Concept: Rights adjustment factor = Fair value per share before exercise ÷ TERP; TERP = (Market value of shares before + Rights proceeds) ÷ Shares after

Statement

For a rights issue offered to all existing shareholders at an exercise price below fair value (Ind AS 33.27(b), A2):

\[ \text{TERP} = \frac{N_0 \times P_{\text{cum}} + N_r \times X}{N_0 + N_r} \]

\[ F = \frac{P_{\text{cum}}}{\text{TERP}} \]

where \(N_0\) is the number of shares before the rights issue, \(P_{\text{cum}}\) is the fair value per share immediately before exercise (the cum-rights price on the last day shares trade together with the rights), \(N_r\) is the number of rights shares issued, \(X\) is the rights exercise price, TERP is the theoretical ex-rights price, and \(F\) is the adjustment factor. Shares outstanding before the rights issue — in all prior periods and in the current period up to the issue date — are multiplied by \(F\); prior-period EPS is divided by \(F\).

Explanation

A rights issue at a discount is economically equivalent to an issue at full fair value plus a bonus issue. The fair-value part brought in resources and is time-weighted from issue date; the bonus part changed the share count without resources and must be applied retrospectively, as for bonus issues (Ind AS 33.26–28). The TERP is the market price that would prevail after the issue if the market valued the company at its pre-issue value plus the cash raised; the ratio of cum-rights price to TERP captures the bonus element.

Conditions:

  • Applies to issues to all existing shareholders (rights issues, and other issues with a bonus element available to all).
  • If the exercise price equals or exceeds fair value, \(F = 1\).
  • If rights are publicly traded separately before exercise, \(P_{\text{cum}}\) is the closing price on the last day shares are traded with rights.
  • Partly paid rights shares: in basic EPS they count as fractions per their dividend participation; the bonus element still uses TERP.
  • Rights issues completed after the reporting date are not adjusted retrospectively under 33.64 (only bonus, split and reverse split); disclose them.

Illustration

Pragati Ltd has 30 crore shares; 1-for-3 rights issue at ₹90 on 1 November 2024; cum-rights price ₹150. Profit FY 2024-25 ₹420 crore; FY 2023-24 ₹360 crore.

\[ \text{TERP} = \frac{30 \times 150 + 10 \times 90}{40} = \frac{4,500 + 900}{40} = ₹135 \]

\[ F = \frac{150}{135} = 1.1111 \]

WANS FY 2024-25: \(30 \times 1.1111 \times \frac{7}{12} + 40 \times \frac{5}{12} = 19.44 + 16.67 = 36.11\) crore → basic EPS ₹11.63.

Restated FY 2023-24: \(30 \times 1.1111 = 33.33\) crore → EPS ₹10.80 (originally ₹12.00, i.e., \(12.00 \div 1.1111\)).

Connections

Part of s06 (bonus elements and retrospective adjustment) and builds on c03. ESOP exercise prices and conversion ratios are usually adjusted for rights under SEBI SBEB Regulations 2021 and instrument terms, affecting s07 and c05. In valuation, TERP is the reference price for ex-rights share price analysis and for adjusting historical price series; FP&A models with planned rights issues need the factor to keep EPS comparable.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute TERP and factor [L1]

A company with 12 crore shares makes a 1-for-6 rights issue at ₹400; cum-rights price ₹580. Compute TERP and the adjustment factor.

Q2. Full EPS computation [L2]

Shares at 1 April 2024: 18 crore; 2-for-9 rights issue at ₹210 on 1 August 2024 (cum-rights price ₹300); 0.5 crore shares issued for cash at market on 1 February 2025. Profit FY 2024-25 ₹270 crore; FY 2023-24 ₹225 crore. Compute basic EPS for both years.

💡 Hint: Apply the factor only to periods before the rights issue; the February issue is time-weighted normally.

Q3. Interview — rights at a premium [L1]

Explain why a rights issue priced at the prevailing market price requires no retrospective adjustment and how deep-discount rights issues (as used by some Indian banks) affect historical EPS comparability.
