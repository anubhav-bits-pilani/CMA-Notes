Key concept

Diluted EPS Formula and Most-Dilutive-First Ranking

Concept: Diluted EPS = (Earnings + post-tax interest on convertibles) ÷ (Weighted shares + dilutive potential shares); add most dilutive first (Ind AS 33.44)

Statement

\[ \text{Diluted EPS} = \frac{E + \sum_{k \in D} \Delta E_k}{\text{WANS} + \sum_{k \in D} \Delta N_k} \]

where \(E\) is basic earnings (profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent), \(\Delta E_k\) is the after-tax effect on earnings of converting potential share class \(k\) (for convertible debt: interest × (1 − t); for convertible preference shares: the preference dividend deducted in basic EPS; plus other consequential changes), \(\Delta N_k\) is the weighted incremental shares from class \(k\), \(t\) is the tax rate, and \(D\) is the set of classes found dilutive. Classes are added in ascending order of earnings per incremental share (\(\Delta E_k \div \Delta N_k\)); options and warrants (\(\Delta E = 0\)) come first (Ind AS 33.44).

Explanation

Diluted EPS must reflect the maximum dilution. Because the inclusion of one class can change whether another is dilutive, Ind AS 33.44 requires a sequential approach: rank all classes from most to least dilutive, add them one by one, and stop including a class once it would raise EPS from continuing operations.

Numerator details:

  • Interest is the amount recognised in P&L under Ind AS 109 — effective interest, including amortisation of transaction costs and the liability-component discount for compound instruments — not the coupon.
  • Use the post-tax amount: under Sec 115BAA the effective rate is 25.168%; use the rate at which the interest is deductible.
  • For FCCBs, add back FX losses (or deduct gains) and fair value changes recognised in P&L.
  • Consequential effects (e.g., an employee profit-sharing pool that rises with profit) are included (33.35).

Denominator details: potential shares are assumed converted at the beginning of the period or, if later, the issue date; weighted for the period outstanding if lapsed or converted during the period.

Why ranking matters: adding a "weakly dilutive" class before a "strongly dilutive" one can make the strong one appear antidilutive or vice versa. Ranking by EPIS ensures the lowest diluted EPS.

Illustration

Basic: earnings ₹60 crore, WANS 20 crore → EPS ₹3.00. Tax 25.168%.

Class \(\Delta E\) \(\Delta N\) EPIS
Options 0 1.0 crore 0
9% CCDs (interest ₹18 crore) \(18 \times 0.74832 = 13.47\) 6.0 crore 2.25
7% FCCBs (interest ₹7 crore, FX loss ₹3 crore) \((7 + 3) \times 0.74832 = 7.48\) 2.0 crore 3.74
Step Earnings Shares EPS Result
Basic 60.00 20.0 3.000

Practice Questions

Q1. Pre-tax vs post-tax add-back [L1]

Basic earnings ₹45 crore; WANS 15 crore; 10% convertible debentures ₹100 crore (effective interest ₹11 crore) convertible into 4 crore shares. Tax 25.168%. Compute diluted EPS correctly and show the error from adding back pre-tax interest.

Q2. Ranking three classes [L2]

Basic earnings ₹200 crore; WANS 50 crore. Classes: (a) convertible preference shares — dividend ₹12 crore, 4 crore shares; (b) convertible bonds — post-tax interest ₹15 crore, 5 crore shares; (c) warrants — 3 crore shares at ₹50, average price ₹75. Compute EPIS, rank, and determine diluted EPS.

💡 Hint: Compute each class's EPIS first; do not test dilution in the order the instruments are listed.

Q3. Mid-year issue of convertibles [L2]

On 1 October 2024, a company issues ₹120 crore 8% CCDs (effective interest 9%) convertible into 3 crore shares. Basic earnings ₹90 crore; WANS 30 crore; tax 25.168%. Compute diluted EPS for FY 2024-25.
