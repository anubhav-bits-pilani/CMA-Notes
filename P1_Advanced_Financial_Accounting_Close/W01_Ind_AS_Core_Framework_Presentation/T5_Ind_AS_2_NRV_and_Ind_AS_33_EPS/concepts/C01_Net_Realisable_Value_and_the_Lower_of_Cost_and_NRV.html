Key concept

Net Realisable Value and the Lower of Cost and NRV Rule

Concept: NRV = Estimated selling price − Costs to complete − Costs to sell; carry at lower of cost and NRV, item-by-item or similar groups (Ind AS 2.9, 2.29)

Statement

\[ \text{NRV} = P - C_{\text{complete}} - C_{\text{sell}} \]

\[ \text{Carrying amount} = \min(\text{Cost}, \text{NRV}) \]

where \(P\) is the estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, \(C_{\text{complete}}\) the estimated costs of completion (for WIP and raw materials to be converted), and \(C_{\text{sell}}\) the estimated costs necessary to make the sale (commissions, outward freight, packing for despatch, trade discounts expected). Inventories are measured at the lower of cost and NRV (Ind AS 2.9). The comparison is made item by item, or for groups of similar or related items where appropriate (Ind AS 2.29) — not for whole classes such as "finished goods" or for an operating segment.

Explanation

NRV is the net amount an entity expects to realise from the sale of inventory in the ordinary course of business; it is entity-specific, unlike fair value which reflects market participants' price in an orderly transaction (Ind AS 2.7). NRV may therefore be lower than fair value less costs to sell (e.g., if the entity would sell through its own discount channel).

Conditions and nuances:

  • Estimates use the most reliable evidence available, including post-period price movements that confirm conditions at the reporting date (2.30).
  • Inventory held for firm sales contracts uses the contract price (2.31).
  • Grouping is allowed only for items relating to the same product line with similar purposes or end uses, produced and marketed in the same geographic area, that cannot be practicably evaluated separately (2.29).
  • Costs to sell are incremental costs directly attributable to the sale — not general administrative overhead.
  • Raw materials are subject to the exception in 2.32 (c02).

Why item-by-item matters: offsetting a loss on one product against a gain on another hides write-downs. A class-wide comparison of total FG cost with total FG NRV would show no write-down even if individual products are loss-making.

Illustration

A consumer-durables company has three finished-goods models (₹ lakh):

Model Cost Expected price Selling costs NRV Lower of cost and NRV
AC-1 400 520 30 490 400
AC-2 (old model) 250 210 20 190 190
Cooler-X (unfinished, cost to complete 40) 120 180 15 125 120
Total 770 805 710

Item-by-item write-down: ₹60 lakh on AC-2. A class-wide comparison (cost 770 vs NRV 805) would wrongly show no write-down.

Journal entry: Dr Changes in inventories of finished goods (P&L) ₹60 lakh; Cr Inventory — finished goods ₹60 lakh.

Connections

Applied in s03 (NRV testing approaches), s04 (raw material exception and reversals) and topic-level pitfalls. The cost side comes from s01–s02. Post-year-end prices link to Ind AS 10 adjusting events (Topic 2, c04). Write-downs reduce profit and EPS (s05). In FP&A, NRV assumptions sit in inventory provision matrices within the monthly close model; ICDS II also uses lower of cost and NRV for tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. NRV with costs to complete [L1]

WIP for a batch of 2,000 pumps has cost to date ₹1.6 crore; costs to complete ₹0.5 crore; expected selling price ₹11,000 per pump; dealer commission 6% of price; outward freight ₹200 per pump. Compute NRV and the carrying amount.

Q2. Grouping judgement [L2]

A garment exporter carries 40 styles of men's shirts produced in Tiruppur for the same European buyer, each with cost ₹12 crore aggregate; 12 styles are loss-making by ₹1.8 crore in total and 28 styles are profitable by ₹4.5 crore. The CFO wants to test NRV for "men's shirts" as one group. Evaluate against Ind AS 2.29 and determine the write-down.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the styles can practicably be evaluated separately.

Q3. Firm contract and excess stock [L2]

A chemical producer holds 1,500 tonnes of solvent at cost ₹80,000/t. 1,000 tonnes are committed under a firm contract at ₹85,000/t; the spot price is ₹72,000/t; selling costs ₹2,000/t. Compute the carrying amount and the write-down.
