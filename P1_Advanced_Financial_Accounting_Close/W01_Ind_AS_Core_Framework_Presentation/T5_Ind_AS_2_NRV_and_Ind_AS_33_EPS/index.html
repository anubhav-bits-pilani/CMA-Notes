Roadmap topic: Ind AS 2 & Ind AS 33: NRV write-downs on slow-moving inventory; Basic & Diluted EPS computation with convertible instruments and bonus issues

Overview

This topic pairs two standards that look mechanical but are dense with judgement. Ind AS 2 Inventories decides what goes into the cost of inventory (purchase, conversion and fixed production overhead absorbed at normal capacity), how cost is assigned (FIFO, weighted average or specific identification — never LIFO) and when inventory must be written down to net realisable value (NRV). For manufacturing, pharma, FMCG, auto components and retail companies, inventory is often the largest current asset; NRV write-downs on slow-moving, obsolete and near-expiry stock are a recurring key audit matter, and the CARO 2020 clause 3(ii) physical-verification and quarterly-returns-to-banks reporting sits alongside it. Inventory costing also drives gross margin — the line FP&A teams forecast first.

Ind AS 33 Earnings per Share converts profit into the per-share metric every equity analyst, valuation model and SEBI Reg 33 result uses. Basic EPS divides profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent by the time-weighted number of shares; bonus issues, splits and the bonus element in rights issues require retrospective adjustment of all periods presented, even if they happen after the reporting date. Diluted EPS adds potential ordinary shares — convertibles (if-converted), options, warrants and ESOPs (treasury stock method), contingently issuable shares — in a strict sequence, excluding anything antidilutive measured against the continuing-operations control number.

The two standards connect through the P&L: an NRV write-down reduces profit and therefore EPS; a restatement of inventory (Topic 2) restates EPS. They also share a modelling theme — both need clean, auditable schedules (ageing-based provision matrices for inventory; share-count roll-forwards with weights for EPS).

Learning sequence: cost components (s01) → cost formulas (s02) → NRV testing (s03) → raw-material and reversal rules (s04) → basic EPS (s05) → retrospective adjustments for bonus/split/rights (s06) → diluted EPS (s07) → presentation (s08). The concept notes give the formulas you will implement in Excel.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 102 ESOPs and assumed proceeds

Ind AS 102 ESOPs: assumed exercise proceeds include unrecognised future service cost (Ind AS 33.47A)

For share options and other share-based payment arrangements under Ind AS 102, the "assumed proceeds" in the treasury stock method include not only the exercise price but also the fair value of goods or services still to be supplied to the entity — i.e., the unrecognised portion of the grant-date fair value of unvested options (Ind AS 33.47A). Including unrecognised cost increases assumed proceeds, so fewer incremental shares are dilutive. Unvested options with performance conditions are treated as contingently issuable shares (33.48). For an Indian company with an ESOP trust, shares held by the trust are treated as treasury shares and deducted from the weighted average for basic EPS (consistent with ICAI's Guidance Note on Accounting for Share-based Payments and Ind AS 32.33).

ICDS II and Sec 145A

ICDS II inventory valuation and Sec 145A for tax computation differences

For income-tax computation, ICDS II (Valuation of Inventories) requires inventory at lower of cost and NRV, broadly aligned with AS 2, with notable differences: cost formulas allowed are FIFO and weighted average (specific identification where items are not interchangeable); standard cost and retail methods are permitted if they approximate actual. Sec 145A requires inventory to be valued inclusive of taxes, duties and cess actually paid or incurred (unless credit is available), and the method of valuation per ICDS. Ind AS adjustments such as the discounting of deferred-credit purchases (Ind AS 2.18 interest element) or NRV provisions computed on portfolio estimates may not be accepted for tax, creating deferred tax differences (Week 3). Tax audit Form 3CD clause 13 reports deviations.

US GAAP inventory differences

US GAAP: LIFO permitted, write-down reversals prohibited (ASC 330); IFRS/Ind AS require reversal

US GAAP permits LIFO (used largely because of the US tax conformity rule) — Ind AS 2 and IFRS prohibit it. For inventory measured using LIFO or the retail inventory method, ASC 330 applies lower of cost or market (replacement cost bounded by a ceiling of NRV and a floor of NRV less normal margin); for all other methods, ASU 2015-11 aligned the test to lower of cost and NRV. Crucially, US GAAP prohibits reversal of write-downs in subsequent annual periods (the written-down amount becomes the new cost basis), whereas Ind AS 2.33 requires reversal when NRV recovers, capped at the original write-down. Indian subsidiaries of US groups therefore keep dual inventory reserves for group reporting.

Ind AS 32 compound instruments feeding diluted EPS

Ind AS 32 compound instruments (CCDs, FCCBs) feeding diluted EPS → Week 3 (w3_t1)

Compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), foreign currency convertible bonds (FCCBs), compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS) and optionally convertible instruments are split under Ind AS 32 into liability and equity components (or wholly classified as equity if conversion is into a fixed number of shares with no cash obligation). The EPS treatment follows: instruments that will mandatorily convert are included in basic EPS from the date the contract is entered into (33.23); optionally convertible instruments enter diluted EPS through the if-converted method, adding back post-tax interest (including the unwinding of the liability component's effective interest) and FX gains/losses on FCCBs recognised in P&L. Week 3 covers the split accounting in detail.

Restated EPS and per-share valuation

→ w1_t2 restated EPS; later valuation work uses diluted share counts for per-share value

When prior-period errors or retrospective policy changes are corrected under Ind AS 8, basic and diluted EPS of comparatives must be restated and the impact disclosed (Ind AS 8.49(b)(ii), Ind AS 33.64). EPS is also restated for bonus issues and splits. In valuation, equity value per share in a DCF or comparable-company analysis divides equity value by a fully diluted share count — typically the treasury stock method applied using the current share price (not the period average) for options and warrants, plus if-converted shares for in-the-money convertibles. Getting the count wrong is a classic interview and model-review error.

Common pitfalls

Writing down raw materials when finished goods remain profitable

Mistake: marking down steel, chemicals or yarn to their falling market price even though the finished products into which they are incorporated still sell above cost. Why it happens: the replacement cost of inputs is visible on commodity exchanges, and conservative habits from risk-and-reward audit testing. Correct treatment: Ind AS 2.32 — materials held for use in production are not written down below cost if the finished products will be sold at or above cost; only when a decline in material prices indicates that finished-goods cost exceeds NRV are materials written down, and then replacement cost may be the best available measure of their NRV. Detection: compare the raw-material write-down schedule with finished-goods margin analysis by product line.

Using year-end shares or forgetting to restate comparatives for bonus issues

Mistake: dividing profit by closing shares, or adjusting the current year for a bonus issue but leaving the prior-year EPS unchanged. Why it happens: shareholding is pulled from the register at year-end; comparatives are copied from last year's report. Correct treatment: basic EPS uses the time-weighted average of shares outstanding (33.19–20); bonus issues, splits and the bonus element of rights issues are treated as if they occurred at the start of the earliest period presented, restating comparatives — including events between the reporting date and approval date (33.26–28, 33.64). Detection: check the EPS note's share reconciliation against the SOCIE and the bonus record date; the prior-year EPS in the current report should differ from last year's published figure after a bonus.

Mishandling options in the treasury stock method

Including out-of-the-money options or ignoring ESOP unrecognised cost in treasury method

Mistake: including options whose exercise price exceeds the average market price, or computing assumed proceeds using only the exercise price for unvested ESOPs. Why it happens: copying total options outstanding from the ESOP note. Correct treatment: options are dilutive only when the average market price exceeds the exercise price (33.47); assumed proceeds for share-based payments include the unrecognised future service cost (33.47A), which can make in-the-money options antidilutive. Detection: recompute incremental shares option tranche by tranche with the average price for the period.

Adding back pre-tax rather than post-tax interest on convertibles

Mistake: adding the full coupon/finance cost on convertible debentures to the diluted EPS numerator. Why it happens: using the interest figure from the finance cost note without considering tax. Correct treatment: the numerator adjustment is the after-tax effect of dividends and interest on dilutive potential ordinary shares, plus any other changes in income or expense from conversion (33.33) — e.g., the effective interest (not just coupon), net of tax at the applicable rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA). Detection: reconcile the numerator adjustment to the finance cost on the instrument × (1 − tax rate).

Practice Questions

Q1. Inventory write-down and its EPS impact [L3]

Medisure Pharma Ltd (FY ends 31 March 2025) has finished goods with cost ₹48 crore; of these, batches costing ₹6 crore expire within 3 months and can be sold only at 40% of cost (selling costs 5% of selling price); a product line with cost ₹10 crore is selling after year-end at ₹9.2 crore with selling costs ₹0.4 crore. Raw material API costing ₹8 crore has fallen in replacement cost to ₹6.5 crore; the finished product made from it sells at a 22% margin. Profit before the write-downs is ₹120 crore; tax rate 25.168%. Shares: 10 crore at 1 April 2024; 2 crore issued at full market price on 1 October 2024; 1:5 bonus on 15 May 2025 (financial statements approved 28 May 2025). Prior-year profit ₹95 crore, prior-year shares 10 crore throughout. (a) Compute the write-downs. (b) Compute basic EPS for both years as presented in the FY 2024-25 financial statements. (c) Draft the inventory and EPS notes' key disclosures.

💡 Hint: Test the API under the raw-material exception before writing it down, and apply the bonus to both years.

Q2. Rights issue, ESOPs and convertibles [L3]

Profit attributable to equity holders of Navratna Tech Ltd for FY 2024-25 is ₹240 crore (continuing operations ₹260 crore; loss from discontinued operations ₹20 crore). Shares at 1 April 2024: 20 crore. A 1-for-4 rights issue at ₹80 per share was completed on 1 July 2024; cum-rights fair value ₹130 per share. ESOPs: 1 crore options outstanding all year, exercise price ₹60, unrecognised compensation cost ₹15 per option; average market price ₹150. Convertible debentures: ₹200 crore, 8% coupon (effective interest 9%), convertible at 1 share per ₹100 of debentures, outstanding all year. Tax 25.168%. (a) Compute basic EPS. (b) Compute diluted EPS with full antidilution sequencing on the continuing-operations control number. (c) Compute EPS from discontinued operations.

Q3. Inventory costing system review [L2]

An auto-component manufacturer absorbs fixed production overhead (₹36 crore per year) at actual output of 60 lakh units when normal capacity is 80 lakh units; it uses standard costs last revised 3 years ago with cumulative variances of 9% of cost; and it includes ₹2 crore of finished-goods warehouse rent and ₹1.5 crore of abnormal scrap in inventory cost. Closing finished goods: 6 lakh units. Identify each error, quantify the adjustments to closing inventory, and state whether any change is a policy change, estimate change or error under Ind AS 8.

Q4. Model build — EPS schedule [L3]

Design an Excel EPS schedule for a 5-year forecast that handles: fresh issues with day-count weights, a planned 1:1 bonus in year 3, a rights issue in year 2, ESOP grants by tranche with treasury-stock dilution using a forecast average price, and an FCCB with if-converted dilution and an antidilution test. Specify input rows, calculation blocks, checks (e.g., weighted shares ≤ closing shares adjusted), and outputs.
