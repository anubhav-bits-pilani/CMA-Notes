Subtopic note

Step 4: Relative SSP Allocation, Discounts and Variable Amounts

Scope: Step 4: relative SSP allocation — adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, residual (115.79); allocating discounts and variable consideration to specific POs

Why it matters

Once POs and the transaction price are known, allocation decides how much revenue each PO carries — and therefore when revenue is recognised. In a SaaS + implementation deal, every rupee allocated to implementation is recognised in months; every rupee allocated to subscription is spread over years. In telecom handset bundles, allocation moves revenue from service to equipment. In EPC + O&M contracts, it decides construction-period margin. Auditors challenge SSP evidence intensely, because entities have incentives to allocate more to early-delivered POs.

Core notes

The allocation objective (115.73–75)

Allocate the transaction price to each PO in an amount that depicts the consideration the entity expects to be entitled to in exchange for transferring the promised goods or services. The default is allocation on a relative stand-alone selling price basis (115.74), with two exceptions: allocation of discounts (115.81–83) and of variable consideration (115.84–86).

Stand-alone selling price (115.76–78)

  • Determine SSP at contract inception for each distinct good or service and allocate in proportion (115.76). Do not reallocate for later SSP changes (115.88).
  • SSP is the price at which the entity would sell the good or service separately to a customer. The best evidence is an observable price when the entity sells it separately in similar circumstances to similar customers (115.77). A contractually stated or list price may be, but is not presumed to be, the SSP.
  • If SSP is not directly observable, estimate it, considering all reasonably available information — market conditions, entity-specific factors, customer class — and maximise the use of observable inputs, applying methods consistently in similar circumstances (115.78).

Estimation methods (115.79)

Method How Best used when
(a) Adjusted market assessment Evaluate the market and estimate the price customers would pay; can reference competitors' prices adjusted for the entity's costs and margins Competitive markets with observable competitor pricing (e.g., AMC rates, standard implementation day-rates)
(b) Expected cost plus a margin Forecast the expected costs of satisfying the PO and add an appropriate margin Customised services, new products, implementation, construction phases
(c) Residual approach SSP = total transaction price − sum of observable SSPs of other goods or services Only if the SSP is highly variable or uncertain (115.79(c)) — see c06

A combination of methods may be needed (115.80), e.g., residual for two items whose SSPs are uncertain, after which another method splits the residual.

Allocating a discount (115.81–83)

A customer receives a discount when the sum of SSPs exceeds the promised consideration. The discount is allocated proportionately to all POs (115.81) except when the entity has observable evidence that the entire discount relates to only one or more, but not all, POs. All three of these criteria in 115.82 must be met:

  • (a) the entity regularly sells each distinct good or service (or each bundle) in the contract on a stand-alone basis;
  • (b) the entity also regularly sells, on a stand-alone basis, a bundle (or bundles) of some of those distinct goods or services at a discount to their SSPs; and
  • (c) the discount in (b) is substantially the same as the discount in the contract, and analysis of the goods or services in each bundle provides observable evidence of the PO(s) to which the entire discount belongs.

If the discount is allocated entirely to specific POs, allocate it before using the residual approach (115.83).

Allocating variable consideration (115.84–86)

Variable consideration may relate to the entire contract or to a specific part. Allocate a variable amount (and subsequent changes) entirely to one PO, or to one distinct good or service in a series, if both (115.85):

  • (a) the terms of the variable payment relate specifically to the entity's efforts to satisfy that PO or transfer that distinct good or service (or to a specific outcome from doing so); and
  • (b) allocating the variable amount entirely to that PO or distinct good is consistent with the allocation objective (115.73), considering all POs and payment terms.

Otherwise, allocate on the relative SSP basis (115.86). Example: a BPO contract with a per-transaction fee — each day's fee is allocated to that day's service; a construction bonus for early completion of phase 1 only — allocated to phase 1.

Worked illustration: SaaS + implementation + training

Prakriti Cloud Ltd signs a contract for ₹2.40 crore: 3-year SaaS subscription, implementation (distinct) and training. Evidence:

  • Subscription: sold separately at ₹55–65 lakh per year to comparable customers (median ₹60 lakh) → SSP ₹1.80 crore for 3 years.
  • Implementation: not sold separately. Expected cost 2,300 hours × ₹2,600 = ₹59.8 lakh; margin 30% on cost → SSP ₹77.74 lakh (cross-check: partner rate ₹3,500/hour × 2,300 = ₹80.5 lakh).
  • Training: sold separately at ₹6 lakh.

Worked illustration: discount relating to specific POs

A company regularly sells A (SSP ₹40), B (SSP ₹55) and C (SSP ₹45) separately, and regularly sells B + C together for ₹60 (discount ₹40). A contract sells A, B and C for ₹100 (discount ₹40). The 115.82 criteria are met: the entire discount relates to B and C. Allocation: A ₹40; B and C share ₹60 in proportion to their SSPs — B ₹33, C ₹27.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • List prices with large routine discounts: list price is not SSP; use the observable discounted selling prices.
  • SSP ranges: if a contract price for an item falls within the observed SSP range, many entities use the contract price; if outside, use a point in the range (median, nearest end) — apply consistently and document.
  • Material rights (options) need an SSP (B42) and share in allocation.
  • Free goods (a "free" set-top box) are POs with an SSP; allocation gives them revenue.
  • Contract price changes after inception are allocated on the inception basis (115.87–88), unless 115.85 applies.
  • Excise/GST/TCS are excluded; Indian bundles with differing GST rates do not change the accounting allocation, though GST "composite vs mixed supply" analysis may differ.

Key takeaways

  • Allocate on relative SSP at inception; observable separate selling prices are the best evidence; contract or list prices are not automatically SSP.
  • Estimate unobservable SSPs with adjusted market assessment or expected cost plus margin; use residual only if SSP is highly variable or uncertain.
  • Discounts are allocated proportionately unless all three 115.82 criteria are met.
  • Variable consideration can be allocated to a specific PO or series period if 115.85 criteria are met.
  • Build allocation tables with SUMPRODUCT and a check that allocations sum to the transaction price.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic allocation [L1]

A telecom operator sells a handset (SSP ₹30,000) with a 24-month plan (SSP ₹750 per month) for ₹5,000 upfront and ₹999 per month. Compute the transaction price, allocate it on relative SSP and state the revenue on handset delivery and per month for the service. Ignore financing.

Q2. Choosing SSP methods [L2]

An engineering firm sells (a) a pump set, regularly sold separately at prices from ₹18–22 lakh (median ₹20 lakh); (b) installation, never sold separately, expected cost ₹1.2 lakh, typical margin on services 25%; (c) a 5-year AMC, competitors charge ₹1.5–1.8 lakh per year, the firm's costs are lower than competitors' by 10%. Contract price for all three: ₹26 lakh. Estimate each SSP, justify the method, and allocate.

💡 Hint: For the AMC, think about how 115.79(a) allows adjustment of competitor prices for the entity's own costs and margins.

Q3. Discount allocation test [L2]

A software company regularly sells Product X (₹10 lakh), Product Y (₹6 lakh) and support Z (₹2 lakh) separately; it regularly sells X + Y bundles at ₹13 lakh. A customer buys X, Y and Z for ₹15 lakh. Determine whether the 115.82 criteria are met and allocate the price.

Q4. Variable fee allocation [L3]

A 3-year IT outsourcing contract: transition services (6 months, fixed ₹3 crore) and run services (30 months, ₹40 lakh per month fixed plus ₹200 per ticket resolved). SSP: transition ₹3.5 crore; run services ₹42 lakh per month equivalent. Allocate the fixed consideration, decide whether the per-ticket fees can be allocated to the months in which tickets are resolved, and build the Excel layout (inputs, allocation, monthly revenue rows) with checks.
