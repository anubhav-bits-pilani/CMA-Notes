Subtopic note

Step 2: Identifying Distinct Performance Obligations

Scope: Step 2: distinct POs — capable of being distinct and distinct in context (115.27–29); series guidance; SaaS + implementation, hardware + AMC, EPC design-build

Why it matters

The number of performance obligations (POs) drives everything downstream: how the transaction price is split, when revenue is recognised and which disclosures apply. Splitting a SaaS deal into "implementation" and "subscription" can pull crores of revenue into the first quarter; combining them defers it over three years. Hardware-plus-AMC bundles, EPC design-build contracts, telecom handset-plus-plan offers and pharma licence-plus-supply deals all turn on this step. It is also where auditors push hardest, because the conclusion is judgement-heavy and the revenue effect is immediate.

Core notes

Identifying promises (115.22–25)

At contract inception, assess the goods or services promised and identify as a PO each promise to transfer:

  • (a) a good or service (or bundle) that is distinct; or
  • (b) a series of distinct goods or services that are substantially the same and have the same pattern of transfer to the customer (115.22(b)).

Promises can be explicit or implied by customary business practices, published policies or specific statements (115.24), e.g., free installation customarily provided, free software updates. Activities that do not transfer a good or service to the customer — set-up activities, internal administrative tasks — are not POs (115.25). Ind AS 115 has no explicit "immaterial promises" exemption as in US GAAP, but materiality applies generally.

The two-part distinct test (115.27)

A good or service is distinct if both:

  • (a) Capable of being distinct — the customer can benefit from it on its own or together with other resources that are readily available to the customer (resources sold separately by the entity or another entity, or already obtained by the customer). Indicators: the entity regularly sells it separately; it can be used, consumed or sold for more than scrap value (115.28).
  • (b) Distinct within the context of the contract — the entity's promise to transfer it is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract.

Separately identifiable — the 115.29 factors

The objective is to decide whether the nature of the promise is to transfer each item individually or to transfer a combined item to which the promised goods or services are inputs. Factors indicating that promises are not separately identifiable:

  • (a) the entity provides a significant service of integrating the goods or services with other goods or services into a bundle representing the combined output the customer has contracted for (e.g., a construction contractor integrating materials, labour and design into a building);
  • (b) one or more of the goods or services significantly modifies or customises, or is significantly modified or customised by, other goods or services in the contract;
  • (c) the goods or services are highly interdependent or highly interrelated — each is significantly affected by the others (not merely one depends on the other); e.g., the entity cannot fulfil one promise independently of the other.

If a good or service is not distinct, combine it with others until a distinct bundle is identified (115.30), which may result in the entire contract being one PO.

Series guidance (115.22(b), 115.23)

A series is accounted for as a single PO if each distinct good or service in the series would be satisfied over time (115.35) and the same method would be used to measure progress. Typical series: monthly payroll processing, daily cleaning, hourly BPO services, a year of SaaS access, electricity supply under a PPA. Consequences: one PO for disclosure and allocation, but variable consideration can be allocated to individual periods (115.84–85) — e.g., transaction-based BPO fees recognised in the month of the transactions — and modifications to a series are usually prospective (remaining periods are distinct).

Application to common bundles

SaaS + implementation. Subscription access is a stand-ready series, satisfied over time. Implementation is distinct if (i) the customer could benefit from it with readily available resources (other vendors or the customer's IT team can and do perform it; the SaaS is functional without it) and (ii) it does not significantly modify or customise the platform (configuration using standard tools, data migration, training). It is not distinct where the implementation involves significant coding that changes platform functionality, or where the SaaS cannot be used for its intended purpose without the entity's specific implementation. If not distinct, the combined PO begins when the customer can use the service (go-live) and revenue is recognised over the subscription period; implementation-phase billings are deferred.

Hardware + AMC. Equipment (e.g., medical imaging machines, DG sets) and an annual maintenance contract are usually distinct: the equipment functions without the AMC, third parties offer maintenance, and the AMC does not modify the equipment. Hardware → point in time (on installation/acceptance if installation is complex); AMC → over time (series). Embedded software essential to hardware functionality is not separate from the hardware. A "free" first-year warranty may be assurance-type (Ind AS 37) or service-type (separate PO) — see s03.

EPC design-build. Design, procurement, construction and commissioning are each capable of being distinct (others sell them separately), but in an EPC contract the contractor provides a significant integration service and bears the risk of combining them into a functioning plant (115.29(a)); design is modified by construction findings (115.29(b)–(c)). Hence typically one PO (the plant), satisfied over time if 115.35 criteria are met. Exceptions: post-commissioning O&M for several years is a separate series PO; supply of spares at the customer's option may be a separate PO or a material right.

Worked illustration

Nirmiti Software Ltd sells to a bank: (1) licence to an on-premise core banking module — right to use; (2) installation and configuration (standard tools, 400 hours, often done by partners); (3) significant customisation (12,000 hours) to integrate with the bank's legacy systems, changing the module's core functionality; (4) 3 years of post-contract support (PCS) with when-and-if-available updates.

Promise Capable of being distinct? Separately identifiable? PO
Licence Yes No — significantly customised by (3) Combined with (3)
Installation/configuration Yes Part of customised solution Combined with (1) and (3)
Customisation Yes (others could do it) No — integrates and modifies licence Combined
PCS (support + updates) Yes Yes — does not modify the customised solution Separate PO, over time

Result: two POs — (A) customised licence solution, over time if the entity's performance creates an asset with no alternative use and an enforceable right to payment exists (115.35(c)), otherwise at a point in time on acceptance; (B) PCS, ratably over 3 years.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • "Can the customer benefit with readily available resources?" — consider resources available from other vendors in the Indian market; a niche ERP with no partner ecosystem may make implementation incapable of being distinct.
  • Contractual restrictions (the customer is prohibited from engaging third parties) do not change the analysis of whether the good is capable of being distinct (IFRS 15 Basis for Conclusions); focus on the characteristics of the goods.
  • Highly dependent ≠ highly interdependent: SaaS depends on hosting, but hosting is part of the SaaS; an AMC depends on the equipment, yet they are distinct because the equipment does not depend on the AMC.
  • Shipping after control transfers is a separate PO under Ind AS 115 (no accounting policy election as in US GAAP).
  • Free upgrades or devices (telecom handset + plan): handset and service are generally distinct; allocate on relative SSP.
  • Promises to the customer's customers (e.g., loyalty points to end consumers via a distributor) can be POs to the distributor.

Key takeaways

  • A PO is a distinct good or service, a distinct bundle, or a series of substantially the same distinct services with the same pattern of transfer.
  • Distinct = capable of being distinct (115.27(a)) and separately identifiable in context (115.27(b)); 115.29 factors guide the second limb.
  • Significant integration, significant customisation and high interdependence point to a combined PO.
  • SaaS implementation is distinct only if it does not significantly customise or integrate the platform; EPC design-build is usually one PO.
  • Series treatment simplifies over-time services but still allows allocation of variable fees to specific periods.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify POs: diagnostic equipment [L1]

A company sells an MRI machine to a hospital for ₹14 crore including installation (requiring 2 weeks; other specialised firms can install it), 5 years of comprehensive AMC (the company's AMC is also sold separately for ₹40 lakh a year), operator training (3 days), and a 1-year warranty against manufacturing defects as required by the tender. Identify the POs and justify each conclusion.

Q2. SaaS implementation judgement [L2]

A SaaS HR platform signs a ₹1.5 crore contract: 3-year subscription and implementation. In scenario A, implementation involves configuration with the platform's admin tools, data migration and training; 30% of the company's customers use third-party implementers. In scenario B, implementation includes building custom payroll modules for state-specific labour codes that are embedded in the platform for this customer only, and no third party can perform it. Determine the POs and the revenue recognition pattern in each scenario.

💡 Hint: Apply 115.29(b) carefully to the scenario B facts and consider when the customer first benefits.

Q3. Series or not? [L2]

Decide whether each is a series accounted for as a single PO: (a) 36 monthly payroll processing runs; (b) delivery of 10,000 tonnes of cement in monthly lots over a year; (c) 24 hours a day IT helpdesk for 2 years with fees per ticket; (d) training sessions, each on a different module, over 6 months. Explain the consequences for variable fee allocation.

Q4. EPC design-build with O&M [L3]

A contractor signs a ₹950 crore contract to design, supply, build and commission a 100 MLD desalination plant over 30 months, and to operate and maintain it for 10 years for ₹12 crore per year. The customer can terminate O&M with 6 months' notice after year 3 without penalty. Identify the POs, the contract term for O&M, and the revenue pattern for each, citing the relevant paragraphs.
