Subtopic note

Ind AS 115.126AA: Reconciling Contracted Price to Revenue

Scope: India-specific disclosure: reconciliation of revenue recognised with contracted price showing each adjustment (Ind AS 115.126AA)

Why it matters

Paragraph 126AA is an Indian addition to IFRS 15 — every Ind AS company with material revenue adjustments presents it, and Indian analysts use it to see how much of gross invoicing or list-price value is given away in discounts, rebates, schemes, LDs and price concessions. For FMCG, pharma, auto and consumer durables companies, the gap between contracted price and revenue can be 10–25%, and the trend in that gap is a key margin signal. For EPC companies, the LDs and claims lines reveal execution problems. For the reporting team, it is a disclosure that can only be produced reliably if the revenue system or model tracks each adjustment type separately — which is why this week's exercise designs the model to output it.

Core notes

The requirement

Ind AS 115.126AA: an entity shall reconcile the amount of revenue recognised in the statement of profit and loss with the contracted price, showing separately each of the adjustments made to the contract price, for example on account of discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, performance bonuses, etc., specifying the nature and amount of each such adjustment separately.

Key interpretive points:

  • "Contracted price" — the price stated in contracts with customers before variable consideration and other adjustments (gross invoice value at list or contract rates, excluding GST). For EPC, the original contract value for work performed (contract price × progress), or gross billing — define it and apply consistently.
  • "Each adjustment" — the categories in 115.51 (variable consideration) plus other transaction-price adjustments: significant financing component adjustments (115.60), consideration payable to customers (115.70), non-cash consideration differences, changes in expected returns.
  • Nature and amount — separate line items; aggregating "discounts and others" into one figure defeats the purpose.
  • It sits with the other Ind AS 115 disclosures (115.113–129: disaggregation, contract balances, performance obligations, significant judgements, contract costs, practical expedients).

Typical format

Particulars FY 2025-26 ₹ crore FY 2024-25 ₹ crore
Revenue as per contracted price
Less: trade discounts
Less: cash discounts
Less: volume rebates and schemes
Less: sales returns and expected returns
Less: price concessions / price-downs
Less: consideration payable to customers (listing fees, display incentives treated as price reductions)
Less: liquidated damages / penalties
Add: performance bonuses / incentives earned
Add / (less): significant financing component adjustment
Revenue from contracts with customers

Revenue not from contracts with customers (e.g., export incentives under RoDTEP if presented in "other operating revenue", lease income) is shown separately so the total ties to "Revenue from operations".

Worked illustration: FMCG company

Ganga Consumer Products Ltd, FY 2025-26 (₹ crore):

Particulars Amount
Gross sales at contracted (list) price, excluding GST 2,480.0
Less: trade discounts on invoice (148.8)
Less: secondary schemes and rebates to distributors (96.5)
Less: cash discounts for prompt payment (18.2)
Less: provision for expected returns (expiry and damage) (22.4)
Less: listing and display payments to modern trade treated as price reductions (11.6)
Revenue from contracts with customers 2,182.5
Add: other operating revenue — scrap sales 6.8
Add: export incentives (RoDTEP) 4.1
Revenue from operations 2,193.4

Contracted-to-revenue leakage = (2,480.0 − 2,182.5) ÷ 2,480.0 = 12.0%.

Worked illustration: EPC company

Shreyas Infraprojects Ltd, FY 2025-26 (₹ crore):

Particulars Amount
Revenue at contracted price (contract value × progress in the year) 1,325.0
Less: liquidated damages for delay (constrained estimates and amounts levied) (21.0)
Add: price variation / escalation (constrained) 38.5
Add: approved change orders (variable consideration recognised) 17.2
Add: early-completion bonus 4.0
Less: reversal of previously recognised claims (6.3)
Revenue from contracts with customers 1,357.4

The EPC reconciliation reveals that about 3.9% of revenue (38.5 + 17.2 + 4.0 − 6.3 = 53.4 of 1,357.4) depends on judgemental upward variable consideration — a number an analyst would compare with the 115.123 significant-judgement disclosures.

Building it from the model

Design the revenue model with a separate column (or row) for each adjustment type per contract, so that:

=SUMIFS(Adj_Amount, Adj_Type, "Rebate", Period, ">="&FY_Start, Period, "<="&FY_End)
=Contracted_Price_Total + SUM(Adjustments_Range) - Revenue_Recognised     ' check = 0

Use a data-validation list for adjustment types mapped one-to-one to the disclosure lines. In ERP systems, condition types (pricing procedures in SAP; discount ledgers in Tally) should map to the same categories; a Power Query transformation can aggregate credit notes by reason code.

Relationship with other disclosures

  • Disaggregation (115.114): by product line, geography, timing (point in time vs over time), customer type; the 126AA reconciliation complements it.
  • Contract balances (115.116–118): refund liabilities for rebates and returns are not contract liabilities but are often disclosed alongside.
  • Significant judgements (115.123–126): methods for estimating variable consideration and the constraint.
  • Segment revenue (Ind AS 108): should reconcile to the same revenue figure.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Defining contracted price for services billed on time-and-materials: contracted rate × hours; volume discounts and SLA credits are adjustments.
  • Trade discounts on the face of the invoice: some entities start from invoice value net of trade discounts; if so, say so — but 126AA's purpose suggests showing them.
  • Prior-period adjustments within the reconciliation (releases of excess rebate accruals) should be shown as a separate line, not netted silently.
  • Subsidiaries in the CFS with different contracted price definitions need harmonisation.
  • Quarterly results: SEBI formats do not require 126AA, but investor presentations often show gross-to-net bridges; keep them consistent.
  • GST: contracted price excludes GST; credit notes that do not reduce GST liability (failing Sec 34 CGST conditions) still reduce revenue.

Key takeaways

  • 126AA is an India-only requirement: reconcile contracted price to revenue recognised, showing each adjustment's nature and amount.
  • Include all variable consideration items plus SFC and consideration-payable-to-customer adjustments.
  • Define "contracted price" clearly (e.g., gross invoice at list price excluding GST) and apply consistently.
  • The reconciliation is a by-product of a well-designed revenue model or ERP pricing structure.
  • Analysts use it to track gross-to-net leakage and dependence on judgemental variable consideration.

Practice Questions

Q1. Prepare the reconciliation [L1]

A pharma company's FY 2025-26 data (₹ crore, excluding GST): gross invoices at list price 1,640; trade discounts 82; chargebacks to hospitals under rate contracts 36; expected expiry returns 19 (prior-year provision of 4 released as excess); volume rebates 27; cash discounts 9. Prepare the 115.126AA reconciliation.

Q2. Spot the errors [L2]

A draft 126AA note shows: "Contracted price ₹980 crore; less: discounts and others ₹74 crore; less: GST ₹102 crore; less: bad debts written off ₹8 crore; revenue ₹796 crore." Identify every error and redraft the reconciliation, assuming discounts ₹40 crore, rebates ₹22 crore and price concessions ₹12 crore.

💡 Hint: Ask which items are adjustments to the transaction price and which belong elsewhere in the financial statements.

Q3. Model output design [L2]

For the week's SaaS + EPC exercise, specify the adjustment categories you would track to produce the 126AA reconciliation for FY 2025-26 (for example, SaaS bundle discount, EPC LDs, PVC), the columns and formulas, and the check that ties the reconciliation to the revenue waterfall.

Q4. Analyst interpretation [L3]

Two listed FMCG peers report contracted-to-revenue leakage of 11% and 17% respectively, with the second company's leakage rising from 13% the previous year. List the hypotheses an analyst should test (pricing strategy, channel mix, scheme intensity, returns, classification of trade spend), the other disclosures you would read to test them, and how you would normalise EBITDA margins for comparison.
