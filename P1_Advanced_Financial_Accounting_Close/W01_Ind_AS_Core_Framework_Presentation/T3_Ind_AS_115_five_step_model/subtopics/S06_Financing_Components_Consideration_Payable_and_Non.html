Subtopic note

Financing Components, Consideration Payable and Non-Cash Consideration

Scope: Significant financing component (115.60–65), consideration payable to customer, non-cash consideration

Why it matters

These three adjustments to the transaction price change both the amount and the character of what is reported as revenue. A real estate developer collecting large advances years before possession may need to accrue interest expense and recognise higher revenue; a consumer durables company selling on 24-month no-cost EMI may need to split revenue and interest income; FMCG companies paying slotting fees and display incentives to modern-trade retailers must usually reduce revenue, not book marketing expense; and companies receiving shares, equipment or materials from customers must measure non-cash consideration at fair value. Each affects gross margin, EBITDA and finance cost — the metrics analysts and lenders use.

Core notes

Significant financing component (SFC) — 115.60–65

Rule (115.60): in determining the transaction price, adjust the promised consideration for the effects of the time value of money if the timing of payments agreed by the parties (explicitly or implicitly) provides the customer or the entity with a significant benefit of financing the transfer of goods or services. The objective is to recognise revenue at the cash selling price — the price the customer would have paid in cash when (or as) the goods or services transfer.

Assessment factors (115.61): all relevant facts, including (a) the difference, if any, between the promised consideration and the cash selling price; and (b) the combined effect of (i) the expected length of time between transfer and payment and (ii) prevailing interest rates in the relevant market.

No SFC (115.62) if any of:

  • (a) the customer paid in advance and the timing of transfer is at the customer's discretion (prepaid cards, customer loyalty points, open-dated tickets);
  • (b) a substantial amount of consideration is variable and its amount or timing varies on a future event not substantially within the control of either party (sales-based royalty);
  • (c) the difference between promised consideration and cash selling price arises for reasons other than financing and the difference is proportional to that reason — e.g., retention to protect against non-performance, or advance payments to secure supply of scarce goods or fund the contractor's working capital as a performance protection.

Practical expedient (115.63): no adjustment is needed if, at contract inception, the entity expects the period between transfer and payment to be one year or less (see c03).

Discount rate (115.64): the rate that would be reflected in a separate financing transaction between the entity and its customer at contract inception — reflecting the credit characteristics of the party receiving financing (the customer's credit risk when the entity provides financing; the entity's own borrowing rate when the customer pays in advance). The rate is not updated for later changes in interest rates.

Presentation (115.65): present the financing effect separately from revenue — interest income (Ind AS 109, impairment applies) or interest expense (finance costs; borrowing costs may be capitalised under Ind AS 23 for qualifying assets).

Advance payments. When a customer pays significantly in advance (more than one year), the entity accrues interest expense on the contract liability, increasing the contract liability and therefore revenue recognised at transfer.

Worked illustration: real estate advance

A developer receives ₹10 crore on 1 April 2023 for a flat to be handed over on 31 March 2026 (3 years). The developer's incremental borrowing rate is 10%. Revenue is recognised at a point in time on possession (control transfers on handover).

Year Opening contract liability Interest @10% (finance cost) Closing
FY 2023-24 10.000 1.000 11.000
FY 2024-25 11.000 1.100 12.100
FY 2025-26 12.100 1.210 13.310

On possession: Dr Contract liability ₹13.31 crore, Cr Revenue ₹13.31 crore. Interest of ₹3.31 crore may be capitalised to the project inventory under Ind AS 23 if the project is a qualifying asset — in which case it flows back into cost of sales on possession. Whether the customer's advance reflects financing (versus protection against buyer default, 115.62(c)) is a judgement; payment plans offering a discount for upfront payment ("down-payment plan" vs "construction-linked plan") are strong evidence of financing.

Worked illustration: deferred payment

A consumer durables company sells a ₹1,20,000 appliance on 24 monthly instalments of ₹5,000 with no interest; the cash price is ₹1,05,000 (an implicit rate of about 1.1% per month). Revenue on delivery ₹1,05,000; interest income of ₹15,000 recognised over 24 months using the effective interest method. If the "no-cost EMI" is funded by a bank and the company receives ₹1,05,000 upfront from the bank (net of subvention), revenue is ₹1,05,000 and the subvention is a price reduction.

Consideration payable to a customer (115.70–72)

Consideration payable to a customer includes cash amounts the entity pays or expects to pay to the customer (or to others that purchase from the customer, such as retailers' customers), and credits or other items (coupons, vouchers) that can be applied against amounts owed. Account for it as a reduction of the transaction price — and therefore of revenue — unless the payment is in exchange for a distinct good or service that the customer transfers to the entity (115.70).

  • If the payment is for a distinct good or service, account for it like other purchases from suppliers; if the payment exceeds the fair value of that distinct good or service, the excess reduces the transaction price; if fair value cannot be reasonably estimated, the entire payment reduces the transaction price (115.71).
  • Timing (115.72): recognise the reduction of revenue when (or as) the later of: (a) the entity recognises revenue for the transfer of the related goods or services; and (b) the entity pays or promises to pay the consideration (including by customary practice).

Indian examples: slotting/listing fees and display charges paid to modern-trade chains (usually a revenue reduction unless a distinct, measurable advertising service at fair value exists); upfront "signing amounts" paid to hospitals or OEM customers to secure supply contracts (an asset amortised against revenue over the contract, if recoverable); cashbacks via payment apps funded by the brand; co-operative advertising reimbursements.

Non-cash consideration (115.66–69)

Measure non-cash consideration (shares, goods, services, equipment) at fair value (115.66). If fair value cannot be reasonably estimated, measure it indirectly by reference to the stand-alone selling price of the goods or services promised (115.67). If fair value varies for reasons other than the form of consideration (e.g., performance), apply the variable consideration constraint (115.68); variation due to form (share price movements after inception) is not variable consideration under Ind AS 115 — IFRS 15 does not specify the measurement date, and practice commonly uses contract inception. Customer-contributed goods or services (materials, equipment, labour) are non-cash consideration if the entity obtains control of them (115.69) — e.g., moulds and tooling provided by an OEM to a component supplier, or free-issue steel to an EPC contractor that the contractor controls.

Example: a start-up pays for ₹2 crore of consulting services with equity shares whose fair value at contract inception is ₹2.4 crore. Revenue = ₹2.4 crore (fair value of shares), recognised as services are performed; subsequent changes in share value are accounted for under Ind AS 109.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mobilisation advances in EPC: often interest-free and recovered over the project; assess whether they finance the contractor (SFC with interest expense) or protect the customer/secure performance (115.62(c)). Many Indian contractors conclude no SFC, but the conclusion must be documented; interest-bearing advances are financing liabilities in substance.
  • Retention money generally does not create an SFC (c05 of Topic 4; 115.62(c)).
  • Multi-year advance billing in SaaS (3 years upfront): SFC may exist; the practical expedient applies only where the gap per PO is ≤ 1 year.
  • Free-issue materials not controlled by the contractor (customer retains title and risk, contractor only processes): not consideration; no revenue or cost.
  • Payments to distributors' customers (consumer schemes) are consideration payable to a customer because they are made to parties purchasing from the customer.
  • GST and TDS: TDS deducted by the customer is part of consideration (receivable from tax authorities); GST is excluded from the transaction price.

Key takeaways

  • Adjust for an SFC when payment timing provides significant financing benefit; recognise revenue at the cash selling price and interest separately.
  • No SFC for customer-discretion timing, contingent variable amounts, or non-financing reasons (115.62); expedient for gaps ≤ 1 year (115.63).
  • The discount rate is the separate-financing rate at inception, reflecting the borrower's credit risk, never updated.
  • Consideration payable to customers reduces revenue unless for a distinct good/service at fair value (115.70–72).
  • Non-cash consideration is measured at fair value; customer-contributed items count only if the entity obtains control.

Practice Questions

Q1. SFC on deferred payment [L2]

A machine is sold on 1 April 2025 for ₹6 crore payable on 31 March 2028; its cash selling price is ₹4.8 crore. The customer's borrowing rate is 9%. Determine whether an SFC exists, compute the implicit rate, revenue on delivery and interest income for FY 2025-26, and state which rate (implicit or 9%) Ind AS 115.64 requires and why the two might differ.

💡 Hint: The rate must reflect a separate financing transaction with this customer; compare it with the rate implied by the cash price.

Q2. Slotting fees [L1]

An FMCG company pays a modern-trade chain ₹1.2 crore for listing 30 SKUs and ₹0.8 crore for premium end-cap display for 6 months. Comparable end-cap display advertising from independent media providers would cost ₹0.5 crore. Sales to the chain during the year are ₹40 crore. Determine how much reduces revenue and how much is marketing expense.

Q3. Developer advance with Ind AS 23 [L2]

A developer offers two payment plans for a ₹2 crore apartment: (a) 90% upfront with a 12% discount, or (b) construction-linked payments over 30 months. Possession is expected in 36 months. Explain whether plan (a) contains an SFC, how the discount rate is set, the accounting over the three years, and how Ind AS 23 capitalisation affects reported finance costs and margins.

Q4. Non-cash consideration [L2]

An IT company agrees to build an app for a start-up in return for 2% equity (fair value ₹3 crore at contract inception) plus ₹1 crore cash. During the 9-month build, the start-up raises a new round valuing the 2% stake at ₹5 crore. Determine the transaction price, the revenue pattern if the PO is satisfied over time, and the accounting for the increase in the stake's value.
