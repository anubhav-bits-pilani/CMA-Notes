Subtopic note

Warranties and Customer Options as Material Rights

Scope: Warranties: assurance-type (Ind AS 37 provision) vs service-type (separate PO); customer options as material rights (B39–B43)

Why it matters

Warranties and options are "hidden" promises: they rarely carry a separate price on the invoice, yet they can create separate performance obligations that defer revenue. Indian auto, consumer durables and electronics companies routinely sell extended warranties; two-wheeler and car makers bundle "free services"; retailers and airlines run loyalty programmes; B2B suppliers offer volume-tier discounts and renewal options. Misclassifying a service-type warranty as an Ind AS 37 provision overstates current revenue; ignoring a material right overstates it too. Both are frequent audit adjustments.

Core notes

Warranties (Ind AS 115.B28–B33)

Separately priced warranty (B29). If the customer has the option to purchase a warranty separately (it is priced or negotiated separately), the warranty is a distinct service and a separate PO; allocate part of the transaction price and recognise it over the coverage period.

Assurance-type warranty (B30). If the customer does not have that option, the warranty is accounted for under Ind AS 37 — a provision for expected repair/replacement costs — unless the warranty, or part of it, provides the customer with a service in addition to the assurance that the product complies with agreed-upon specifications.

Factors in assessing whether a warranty provides a service (B31):

  • (a) whether the warranty is required by law — if so, it indicates an assurance-type warranty (the law protects customers from defective products);
  • (b) the length of the coverage period — the longer the coverage, the more likely it includes a service;
  • (c) the nature of the tasks the entity promises to perform — tasks needed to provide assurance (return shipping of defective products) point to assurance; tasks beyond that (preventive maintenance, free periodic servicing, accidental damage cover) point to a service.

Both types (B32). If the warranty includes both, account for the service-type component as a PO (allocate price) and the assurance component under Ind AS 37. If the entity cannot reasonably account for them separately, account for both together as a single PO.

Product liability (B33). A law requiring the entity to pay compensation if its products cause harm (e.g., under consumer protection law) is not a PO — account under Ind AS 37. Promises to indemnify the customer for patent or trademark infringement claims are also generally not POs.

Warranty feature Classification
1-year manufacturing defect warranty on a refrigerator, standard industry practice Assurance — Ind AS 37
Extended warranty years 2–5 sold as an add-on for ₹4,500 Service — separate PO (B29)
Car "5 years / 1 lakh km" warranty when competitors give 2 years, including free annual inspections Assurance for defect cover in line with standard; service for extended years and inspections (judgement)
Two-wheeler "3 free services" Service — separate PO (free services are not assurance)
Accidental damage protection on a phone Service — separate PO (insurance-like; consider Ind AS 117 scope)

Customer options for additional goods or services (B39–B43)

Customer options to acquire additional goods or services for free or at a discount — sales incentives, loyalty points, contract renewal options, discounts on future purchases — give rise to a PO only if the option provides a material right that the customer would not receive without entering into the contract (B40), e.g., a discount that is incremental to the range of discounts typically given for those goods or services to that class of customer in that geographical area or market.

  • If the option provides a material right, the customer is in effect paying in advance for future goods or services; allocate part of the transaction price to the option and recognise revenue when those future goods or services are transferred or when the option expires (B40).
  • If the option is to acquire an additional good at a price that reflects its stand-alone selling price, there is no material right — it is a marketing offer, accounted for only when exercised (B41).

Estimating the SSP of the option (B42). If not directly observable, estimate it reflecting the discount the customer would obtain when exercising the option, adjusted for (a) any discount the customer could receive without exercising the option and (b) the likelihood that the option will be exercised.

Renewal options — practical alternative (B43). If the material right is for future goods or services that are similar to the original and provided in accordance with the terms of the original contract (e.g., renewal of a maintenance contract at the same discounted price), the entity may allocate the transaction price to the optional goods or services by reference to the goods or services expected to be provided and the corresponding expected consideration.

Worked illustration: loyalty points

Sahyog Retail Ltd sells goods for ₹10,00,000 in FY 2025-26 and awards 1 point per ₹100 spent (10,000 points); each point gives ₹10 off future purchases. Expected redemption 70%. Goods' SSP ₹10,00,000.

\[ \text{SSP of points} = 10{,}000 \times \text{₹}10 \times 70\% = \text{₹}70{,}000 \]

\[ \text{Allocated to goods} = 10{,}00{,}000 \times \frac{10{,}00{,}000}{10{,}70{,}000} = \text{₹}9{,}34{,}579; \quad \text{to points} = \text{₹}65{,}421 \]

If 4,500 points are redeemed by 31 March 2026 and the expected total redemption stays at 7,000, revenue from points = 65,421 × 4,500 ÷ 7,000 = ₹42,057; the balance remains a contract liability. Changes in expected redemption are estimate changes (cumulative catch-up).

Worked illustration: service-type warranty

A two-wheeler priced at ₹1,00,000 includes a statutory-style 2-year defect warranty and 3 free services (SSP ₹1,500 each); the bike's SSP alone is ₹1,00,000. Allocation: services SSP ₹4,500 → ₹1,00,000 × 4,500 ÷ 1,04,500 = ₹4,306 deferred; ₹95,694 recognised on delivery; defect warranty provided under Ind AS 37.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Breakage on unexercised rights (unused points, unused gift cards) is recognised in proportion to the pattern of exercise if the entity expects to be entitled to breakage (B46), otherwise when the likelihood of exercise becomes remote.
  • Third-party loyalty programmes (co-branded credit-card points, airline miles purchased by a retailer): consider principal vs agent for the points (Topic 4).
  • Volume-tier pricing that applies prospectively only after a threshold may be a material right (discounts on future purchases) or variable consideration (if retrospective rebates) — read the contract.
  • Warranty cost estimate changes are Ind AS 37 estimate changes (Topic 2 s04); service-type warranty revenue is recognised over the coverage period, typically straight-line or according to the expected claim pattern.
  • Statutory warranties under consumer protection law are assurance-type even if long.
  • Extended warranty sold by a dealer on behalf of an insurer: the dealer may be an agent (commission income).

Key takeaways

  • Warranties the customer can buy separately are separate POs (B29); others are assurance under Ind AS 37 unless they provide an additional service (B30–B31).
  • Legal requirement, coverage length and nature of tasks determine whether a service exists.
  • An option is a PO only if it gives a material right — a discount beyond what the customer would otherwise get (B40).
  • Estimate the option's SSP using the discount and the likelihood of exercise (B42); use B43 for renewal-type options.
  • Deferred amounts for service warranties and material rights are contract liabilities recognised as the future service is delivered or the option lapses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Warranty classification [L1]

Classify each warranty as assurance-type, service-type, or both, and state the accounting: (a) 2-year warranty on air conditioners (industry standard 1 year) covering parts and labour for manufacturing defects; (b) a ₹2,999 "extended 3-year plan" offered at the point of sale; (c) a 5-year compressor warranty mandated by BEE star-rating norms; (d) free annual servicing for 2 years included in the price.

Q2. Loyalty allocation and redemption [L2]

An airline sells tickets of ₹50 crore in Q4 FY 2025-26 and awards 2 crore miles; each mile is worth ₹0.40 on redemption, and 80% of miles are expected to be redeemed. By 31 March 2026, 30 lakh miles are redeemed. Compute the allocation, the revenue for Q4 from miles and the closing contract liability. Then recompute if expected redemption is revised to 75% at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: Revised redemption expectations change the cumulative revenue on points; compute cumulatively.

Q3. Material right or marketing offer? [L2]

A B2B chemicals supplier signs a contract for 1,000 tonnes at ₹80,000 per tonne. The contract gives the customer an option to buy a further 500 tonnes within 12 months at ₹68,000 per tonne. The supplier offers new customers of similar size a maximum 5% discount on list (₹80,000). Is there a material right? If so, estimate its SSP (expected exercise 60%) and allocate the transaction price.

Q4. Automobile bundle memo [L3]

A car maker sells a model at ₹12 lakh including: a 3-year/1 lakh km manufacturer warranty (industry norm 2 years), 4 free services in 3 years (SSP ₹3,000 each), roadside assistance for 3 years (sold separately for ₹2,500 per year), and an option to buy an extended warranty for years 4–5 at ₹8,000 (list price ₹15,000). Draft a technical memo identifying the POs, allocating the price, and setting out the revenue and provision accounting over 5 years. State the key judgements.
