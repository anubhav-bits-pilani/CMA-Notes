Key concept

Contract Modification Outcomes: Separate, Prospective, Catch-Up

Concept: Modification adding distinct goods at SSP = separate contract; else prospective (remaining distinct) or cumulative catch-up (not distinct)

Statement

For a contract modification (an approved change in scope and/or price, Ind AS 115.18):

  1. Separate contract (115.20): if the scope increases by distinct goods or services and the price increases by an amount reflecting their stand-alone selling prices (adjusted for the particular contract's circumstances) → account for the modification as a separate contract; the original contract is unaffected.
  2. Prospective (115.21(a)): otherwise, if the remaining goods or services are distinct from those already transferred → treat as termination of the old contract and creation of a new one:

\[ \text{Consideration for remaining POs} = \text{Unrecognised consideration from original contract} + \text{Consideration promised in modification} \]

  1. Cumulative catch-up (115.21(b)): if the remaining goods or services are not distinct and form part of a single PO partially satisfied at the modification date → update the transaction price and the measure of progress, and recognise the effect as an adjustment to revenue at the modification date:

\[ \text{Catch-up} = \text{Revised TP} \times \text{Revised \% complete} - \text{Revenue recognised to date} \]

  1. Mixed (115.21(c)): apply (2) and (3) to the respective parts.

Explanation

The decision tree reflects substance. A modification that adds distinct items at their normal price is economically a new sale. A price renegotiation for remaining distinct items should not change revenue already earned; spreading the new total over the remaining items is right. For a single integrated PO (a building, a customised system), there is no way to separate "before" from "after", so the revised totals are applied to progress to date.

Practical points:

  • "Unrecognised consideration" in the prospective method includes amounts received but not yet recognised (contract liabilities).
  • Unpriced but approved changes: estimate the price change as variable consideration (115.19) subject to the constraint.
  • Series contracts (monthly services) almost always fall into the prospective method because each remaining month is distinct.
  • A modification that only reduces price on already-delivered goods (e.g., a price concession for past quality issues) is a change in transaction price (115.87–90), not a modification of future scope.

Illustration

A contract for 1,200 units of a component at ₹10,000 each (SSP), delivered evenly over 12 months; 500 units delivered (₹50 lakh recognised). Three alternative modifications:

Modification Test Result
Customer adds 300 units at ₹9,800 (SSP adjusted for no new selling costs) Distinct + SSP Separate contract: 700 at ₹10,000; 300 at ₹9,800
Customer adds 300 units at ₹8,000 Distinct, not SSP Prospective: (700 × 10,000 + 300 × 8,000) ÷ 1,000 = ₹9,400 per remaining unit
Contract is an EPC building, 40% complete (TP ₹50 crore, revenue ₹20 crore); change order adds ₹5 crore price and progress falls to 38% due to added cost Not distinct Catch-up: 55 × 38% − 20 = ₹0.9 crore increase in revenue now

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the modification [L1]

A 24-month security services contract (₹1.2 lakh per month, SSP) is modified after 10 months: (a) guard count increased by 50% for the remaining 14 months at ₹0.6 lakh per month extra (SSP); (b) price reduced to ₹1.0 lakh per month for the remaining months; (c) both. Determine the method for each and the monthly revenue for months 11–24.

Q2. Catch-up computation [L2]

A ₹90 crore turnkey plant contract has costs to date ₹36 crore against an estimate of ₹72 crore. A modification adds ₹10 crore to the price and ₹6 crore to the estimated cost. Compute revenue recognised before the modification, the catch-up at the modification date, and the margin percentage on the modified contract.

💡 Hint: Recompute progress on the revised cost base before applying the revised price.

Q3. Prospective with advance [L2]

A 3-year AMC for ₹36 lakh was paid fully in advance. After 18 months (₹18 lakh recognised), the parties agree to extend the contract by 1 year for an additional ₹9 lakh (SSP for a year is ₹12 lakh). Determine the accounting and monthly revenue for the remaining 30 months.
