Key concept

When the Residual Approach Is Permitted (115.79(c))

Concept: Residual approach only when SSP is highly variable or uncertain (115.79(c))

Statement

An entity may estimate the stand-alone selling price of a good or service by reference to the total transaction price less the sum of the observable stand-alone selling prices of other goods or services promised in the contract only if one of the following criteria is met (Ind AS 115.79(c)):

  • (i) the entity sells the same good or service to different customers (at or near the same time) for a broad range of amounts — the selling price is highly variable because a representative SSP is not discernible from past transactions or other observable evidence; or
  • (ii) the entity has not yet established a price for that good or service and it has not previously been sold on a stand-alone basis — the selling price is uncertain.

\[ \text{SSP}_{\text{residual}} = \text{TP} - \sum_{j \ne r} \text{SSP}_j^{\text{observable}} \]

Explanation

The residual approach is an estimation technique of last resort, not a default allocation method. It was retained (from the legacy "residual method" for software) because some items — typically software licences sold at widely varying prices, or new products — have no reliable SSP. Conditions and safeguards:

  • The other POs must have observable SSPs; if two or more items have uncertain SSPs, the residual approach can determine their combined SSP, followed by another method to split it (115.80).
  • A discount that relates entirely to specific POs (115.82) is allocated before applying the residual approach (115.83).
  • The outcome must be reasonable: if the residual is nil or very small, it suggests the method does not reflect the allocation objective (an item with value is not normally given away), and another method must be used.
  • "Highly variable" requires evidence — a wide distribution of prices across similar customers — not just a policy of negotiating each deal.
  • Expected cost plus margin is generally unsuitable for IP such as software licences (marginal cost near nil), which is why the residual approach is used for licences in practice.

Illustration

Medhavi Analytics Ltd sells, in one contract for ₹1.50 crore: a perpetual on-premise licence (prices across recent deals ranged from ₹40 lakh to ₹1.4 crore — highly variable); 2 years of PCS (sold separately on renewal at ₹18 lakh per year — observable); and implementation (sold separately at ₹20 lakh — observable).

\[ \text{SSP}_{\text{licence}} = 150 - (36 + 20) = 94 \text{ lakh} \]

Allocation: licence ₹94 lakh (point in time on transfer of the licence, right to use); PCS ₹36 lakh (over 2 years); implementation ₹20 lakh (over the implementation period). The ₹94 lakh is within the observed range — reasonable.

Contrast: if the contract price were ₹60 lakh, the residual would be ₹4 lakh — far below any observed licence price, failing the reasonableness check; use relative SSP with an estimated licence SSP (e.g., median of observed range) instead.

Connections

s07 (all SSP methods, discount allocation order), c01 (relative SSP formula), s02 (licence + PCS + implementation PO identification), Topic 4 s07 (right-to-use licences recognised at a point in time). Pitfall: using contractual list prices as SSP without observable evidence. In models, implement the residual as a separate SSP row with a reasonableness flag against the observed range.

Practice Questions

Q1. Is residual permitted? [L1]

For each item, state whether the residual approach is permitted and why: (a) a new AI module launched this quarter, never sold separately; (b) AMC services sold at ₹2 lakh ± 5% to all customers; (c) a software licence sold at prices ranging from 30% to 90% off list depending on negotiation; (d) hardware with a published price list and minimal discounting.

Q2. Residual with specific discount [L2]

A contract for ₹2.0 crore includes A (observable SSP ₹80 lakh), B (observable SSP ₹60 lakh) and licence L (highly variable). The entity regularly sells A + B together for ₹1.2 crore. Apply 115.82–83 and the residual approach to allocate the price.

💡 Hint: Deal with the discount on the A + B bundle before computing the residual.

Q3. Reasonableness review [L2]

An auditor finds that a company applies the residual approach to licences in all contracts, and in 30% of contracts the residual licence value is below 10% of the median licence price. Explain why this is a problem, what alternative method you would propose, and how the change should be accounted for (policy, estimate or error).
