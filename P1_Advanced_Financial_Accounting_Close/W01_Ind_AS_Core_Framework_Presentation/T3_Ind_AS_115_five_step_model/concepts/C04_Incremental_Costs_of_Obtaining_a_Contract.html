Key concept

Incremental Costs of Obtaining a Contract

Concept: Incremental costs of obtaining a contract (sales commissions) capitalised and amortised; expense if amortisation period ≤1 year (115.91–94)

Statement

  • Recognition (115.91): an entity recognises as an asset the incremental costs of obtaining a contract with a customer if it expects to recover those costs.
  • Definition (115.92): incremental costs are costs that the entity would not have incurred if the contract had not been obtained — e.g., a sales commission.
  • Costs incurred regardless (115.93): costs to obtain a contract that would have been incurred regardless of whether the contract was obtained are expensed when incurred, unless explicitly chargeable to the customer regardless of whether the contract is obtained.
  • Practical expedient (115.94): an entity may expense incremental costs when incurred if the amortisation period of the asset would have been one year or less.
  • Amortisation and impairment (115.99–104): the asset is amortised on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer to the customer of the goods or services to which it relates — which may include goods or services under specific anticipated contracts (e.g., expected renewals); impairment is recognised when the carrying amount exceeds the remaining consideration expected less costs still to be incurred.

\[ \text{Annual amortisation} = \frac{\text{Capitalised commission}}{\text{Expected period of benefit (incl. anticipated renewals)}} \]

Explanation

What qualifies: commissions to employees or agents triggered by signing; success fees to brokers; bonuses paid only on contract wins; payroll taxes on those commissions. What does not: salaries of sales staff, bid costs, legal costs of negotiating, travel to pitch, costs of preparing a tender — all incurred whether or not the contract is won. Commissions contingent on future events (e.g., paid only after the customer renews, or clawed back if the customer cancels) need analysis of when the liability arises.

Amortisation period: if commissions on renewals are not commensurate with the initial commission (e.g., 8% on the initial contract, 1% on renewals), the initial commission relates partly to anticipated renewals, and the period of benefit extends to the expected customer life. If renewal commissions are commensurate, amortise the initial commission over the initial contract only.

Presentation: a separate asset ("contract costs") — in Schedule III often within other current/non-current assets, split by expected amortisation timing; amortisation is an expense (selling or employee-related), not a reduction of revenue. Disclose closing balances, amortisation and impairment by main category (115.128) and the use of the 115.94 expedient (115.129).

Tax: commissions are generally deductible when incurred under the Income-tax Act, creating a deferred tax liability on the capitalised asset.

Fulfilment costs (115.95–98) are distinct: costs that relate directly to a contract, generate or enhance resources used in satisfying POs, and are expected to be recovered (e.g., SaaS set-up costs, EPC mobilisation costs) — unless within another standard's scope (Ind AS 2, 16, 38).

Illustration

Kshitij SaaS Ltd pays a 5% commission (₹12 lakh) on a ₹2.40 crore, 36-month contract signed 1 July 2025 with go-live on 1 November 2025, plus a 1% commission on renewals. Historical data indicate customers renew on average once (6 years' total life). Sales manager salaries: ₹50 lakh per year.

  • Capitalise ₹12 lakh (incremental). Salaries are expensed.
  • Renewal commission (1%) is not commensurate with 5% → the initial commission relates to the anticipated renewal too; period of benefit ≈ 72 months from go-live.
  • Amortisation ≈ ₹12 lakh ÷ 72 = ₹16,667 per month from November 2025; FY 2025-26 amortisation = 5 months × ₹16,667 = ₹83,333.
  • If renewal commissions were 5%, amortise over 36 months: ₹33,333 per month.

Practice Questions

Q1. Capitalise or expense? [L1]

Classify each as an incremental cost to obtain (asset), a fulfilment cost, or an expense: (a) ₹8 lakh commission to an external agent on signing a 2-year contract; (b) ₹3 lakh legal fees for negotiating the contract; (c) ₹15 lakh bonus to the sales team for meeting the annual target (based on total sales); (d) ₹20 lakh for setting up customer-specific hosting environments; (e) a ₹2 lakh commission on a 9-month contract.

💡 Hint: Ask whether the cost would have been incurred had this specific contract not been obtained.

Q2. Amortisation period [L2]

An IT company pays 6% commission on initial 3-year contracts (₹30 lakh on a ₹5 crore contract) and 2% on each 3-year renewal. Customers renew on average twice. Determine the amortisation period and FY 2025-26 amortisation if the contract started 1 October 2025. How would an early termination in year 2 affect the asset?

Q3. Impairment test [L2]

A capitalised commission of ₹18 lakh (carrying amount ₹12 lakh) relates to a contract whose remaining consideration is ₹40 lakh; costs to provide the remaining services are now estimated at ₹34 lakh. Apply 115.101 and pass any entry.
