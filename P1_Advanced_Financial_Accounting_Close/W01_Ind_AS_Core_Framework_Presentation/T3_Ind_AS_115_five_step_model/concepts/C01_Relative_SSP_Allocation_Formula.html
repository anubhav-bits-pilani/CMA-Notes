Key concept

Relative SSP Allocation Formula

Concept: Allocated price to PO = Transaction price × (SSP of PO ÷ Σ SSP of all POs)

Statement

\[ \text{Allocated price}_i = \text{TP} \times \frac{\text{SSP}_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} \text{SSP}_j} \]

where \(\text{TP}\) is the transaction price determined in Step 3 (fixed consideration plus constrained variable consideration, adjusted for financing, non-cash consideration and consideration payable to the customer), \(\text{SSP}_i\) is the stand-alone selling price of performance obligation \(i\) determined at contract inception, and \(n\) is the number of POs (including material rights) in the contract (Ind AS 115.73–76).

Explanation

The formula implements the allocation objective: each PO receives revenue in proportion to what it would sell for on its own. Two consequences follow automatically:

  • Discounts are spread proportionately. If \(\sum \text{SSP}_j > \text{TP}\), each PO bears the discount in proportion to its SSP — unless the 115.82 criteria show that the discount belongs to specific POs (then allocate the discount first and apply the formula to the rest).
  • Premiums are spread too. If the contract price exceeds the sum of SSPs (rare), each PO receives a proportionate premium.

Conditions and exceptions:

  • SSPs are fixed at inception; later changes in SSP do not cause reallocation (115.88).
  • Variable consideration that relates specifically to one PO and meets 115.85 is allocated to that PO, outside the formula; the remaining (fixed) consideration is allocated by the formula.
  • Residual approach (115.79(c)) replaces SSP for a PO whose SSP is highly variable or uncertain; its "SSP" becomes TP minus the others' observable SSPs.
  • Changes in TP after inception are allocated using the same inception percentages (115.88); amounts allocated to satisfied POs are recognised immediately (catch-up).

In Excel, compute allocation percentages once, lock them as inputs for later TP changes, and check that allocations sum to TP:

=TP * SSP_i / SUM(SSP_Range)
=ROUND(SUM(Alloc_Range) - TP, 2)        ' check = 0

Illustration

A solar inverter supplier sells: inverters (SSP ₹3.60 crore), installation (SSP ₹0.40 crore) and a 5-year AMC (SSP ₹0.25 crore per year = ₹1.25 crore). Contract price ₹4.80 crore.

\[ \sum \text{SSP} = 3.60 + 0.40 + 1.25 = 5.25 \]

PO SSP Allocation % Allocated ₹ crore Recognition
Inverters 3.60 68.57% 3.291 On delivery/acceptance
Installation 0.40 7.62% 0.366 Over installation period
AMC 1.25 23.81% 1.143 ₹0.229 crore per year for 5 years
Total 5.25 100% 4.800

If, a year later, a ₹0.21 crore performance incentive becomes highly probable and relates to the whole contract, it is allocated 68.57% / 7.62% / 23.81%; the inverter and installation shares (₹0.160 crore) are recognised immediately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Allocate and recognise [L1]

A gym chain sells a 12-month membership (SSP ₹24,000) with a personal training pack of 10 sessions (SSP ₹8,000) for ₹27,000 paid upfront. Allocate the price and compute revenue for the first three months if 4 training sessions are used in that period and the membership started on day 1.

Q2. TP change after inception [L2]

Using the solar inverter illustration, suppose at the end of year 2 the customer's LD claim of ₹0.30 crore for late commissioning becomes highly probable. Compute the reallocation, the immediate revenue reduction and the revised annual AMC revenue for years 3–5.

💡 Hint: Use the inception allocation percentages; only the AMC portion relating to future years is spread forward.

Q3. Excel build [L2]

Write the Excel formulas (using named ranges) for an allocation table with a flag column identifying which POs share a specific discount under 115.82, so that the formula allocates the specific discount first and the remaining consideration on relative SSP. Include two checks.
