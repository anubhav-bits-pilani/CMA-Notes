Key concept

SFC Practical Expedient: Gaps of One Year or Less

Concept: SFC practical expedient: no adjustment if gap between payment and transfer ≤1 year (115.63)

Statement

As a practical expedient, an entity need not adjust the promised amount of consideration for the effects of a significant financing component if the entity expects, at contract inception, that the period between when the entity transfers a promised good or service to a customer and when the customer pays for that good or service will be one year or less (Ind AS 115.63).

\[ \text{Expedient available if } \lvert t_{\text{payment}} - t_{\text{transfer}} \rvert \le 12 \text{ months (expected at inception)} \]

Explanation

Purpose. Most trade credit and short advances carry negligible financing effects; the expedient avoids discounting every 90-day receivable.

How to measure the gap:

  • It is assessed at contract inception based on expected timing; it is not re-tested later if the actual gap turns out longer (though a contract modification may require reassessment).
  • It is assessed for each PO (or each distinct good in a series), comparing when that PO is satisfied with when the related consideration is paid. For a PO satisfied over time, compare the timing of payments with the pattern of transfer.
  • It applies to both advances (customer pays before transfer) and deferred payments (customer pays after transfer).

Policy and disclosure. The expedient is an accounting policy choice to be applied consistently to similar contracts; Ind AS 115.129 requires disclosure of the use of the 115.63 expedient.

Beyond one year. If the gap exceeds one year, the entity must still assess whether the financing component is significant (115.61) and whether 115.62 exclusions apply (customer-discretion timing, contingent variable consideration, non-financing reasons such as retention). A 14-month gap at 7% on a small part of the contract may not be significant.

Common Indian situations:

Situation Expedient?
Standard 30–180 day credit on goods Yes
Annual SaaS subscription billed in advance for the next 12 months Yes (gap ≤ 12 months for each month of service)
3-year SaaS fee billed upfront No for services in months 13–36; assess significance
Real estate advances collected 3–4 years before possession No; assess financing vs 115.62(c)
EPC milestone billing with 45-day payment terms Yes for billing timing; retention released after DLP is assessed separately (usually 115.62(c))
24-month EMI sale of consumer durables No for instalments beyond 12 months; assess

Illustration

A SaaS company signs a contract on 1 April 2025: 3 years of access for ₹36 lakh payable upfront; annual pricing for comparable customers is ₹13.5 lakh per year paid annually in advance. The entity's incremental borrowing rate is 10%.

  • Year-1 services (transferred within 12 months of payment): expedient available.
  • Year-2 and year-3 services: gap of 12–36 months; the difference between ₹36 lakh upfront and ₹40.5 lakh (3 × ₹13.5 lakh) suggests financing.
  • If concluded significant, the contract liability accretes interest expense at the rate reflecting the entity's credit risk, and revenue for years 2–3 is correspondingly higher; if not significant (judgement, documented), no adjustment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the expedient [L1]

For each case decide whether the 115.63 expedient is available at contract inception: (a) machinery delivered on 1 May 2025 with payment due 30 June 2026; (b) a 5-year AMC paid annually in advance; (c) a ticket sold in January for a concert in December; (d) an open-dated gift voucher valid for 3 years; (e) a 2-year construction contract with 10% advance received at signing and recovered from bills over 20 months.

💡 Hint: Measure the gap for each PO or each period of a series, and remember 115.62(a).

Q2. Significance beyond one year [L2]

A capital goods manufacturer sells a press for ₹2.10 crore payable 18 months after delivery; the cash price is ₹1.95 crore; the customer's borrowing rate is 11%. Determine whether the financing component is significant and, if so, revenue on delivery and interest income for the first 12 months.

Q3. Policy note [L1]

Draft a two-sentence accounting policy disclosure on the use of the 115.63 practical expedient for a company with 60–120 day trade credit and 3-year upfront-billed service contracts.
