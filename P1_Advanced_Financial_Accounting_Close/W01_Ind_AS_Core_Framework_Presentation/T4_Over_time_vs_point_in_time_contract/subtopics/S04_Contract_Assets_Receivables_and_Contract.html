Subtopic note

Contract Assets, Receivables and Contract Liabilities

Scope: Contract asset (unbilled revenue) vs receivable (unconditional right) vs contract liability (advances/deferred revenue); net position per contract (115.105–107)

Why it matters

Contract balances sit between the P&L and cash. Lenders test them in drawing-power computations (unbilled revenue usually gets a lower advance rate than receivables), credit analysts treat rising contract assets as an early warning of billing disputes or aggressive revenue, and Ind AS 7 working-capital movements flow from them. Misclassifying conditional unbilled revenue as receivables overstates the quality of working capital; netting across contracts hides advances. The Week 1 revenue waterfall exists largely to produce these balances correctly.

Core notes

Definitions (Ind AS 115 Appendix A, 115.105–108)

Balance Definition Typical Indian label
Contract asset Entity's right to consideration in exchange for goods or services transferred, when that right is conditioned on something other than the passage of time (e.g., future performance, milestone certification) "Unbilled revenue", "Contract assets", "Amounts due from customers"
Receivable Right to consideration that is unconditional — only the passage of time is required before payment is due (115.108) "Trade receivables" (billed; some unbilled amounts if unconditional)
Contract liability Obligation to transfer goods or services for which consideration has been received or is due from the customer "Advance from customers", "Unearned/deferred revenue", "Income received in advance", "Amounts due to customers"

A receivable can exist before revenue: if an invoice is raised for an annual subscription in advance with a non-cancellable contract and payment due in 30 days, the entity has an unconditional right — Dr Receivable, Cr Contract liability (115.108 and IE examples). If the contract is cancellable, no receivable is recognised until cash is received or the right becomes unconditional.

Net position per contract (115.105)

When either party has performed, the entity presents the contract in the balance sheet as a contract asset or a contract liability depending on the relationship between performance and payment. For each contract (after combining contracts under 115.17 where required), there is one net position: either a net contract asset or a net contract liability. Receivables are presented separately from the contract asset.

\[ \text{Net contract position} = \text{Cumulative revenue recognised} - \text{Cumulative amounts billed (or received, if unbilled advances)} \]

A positive result is a contract asset; a negative result is a contract liability. Across contracts there is no netting: total contract assets and total contract liabilities appear gross on the balance sheet, consistent with Ind AS 1.32.

Within a single contract, retention receivable, mobilisation advance and progress billing should be analysed carefully — a retention amount whose release is conditional on performance (defects liability completion) is a contract asset component; an advance repayable through deductions from bills is part of the contract liability. Practice varies on whether retention is shown within "trade receivables" (if unconditional, e.g., released on a date) or contract assets (if conditional on completion or defect-free performance).

Mechanics: three contracts at 31 March 2025 (₹ lakh)

Contract Cumulative revenue Cumulative billed Cumulative cash Contract asset Contract liability Trade receivable
A (EPC) 900 700 600 200 — 100
B (SaaS, annual in advance, non-cancellable) 250 400 300 — 150 100
C (implementation, advance received) 60 0 150 (advance) — 90 —
Total presented 200 240 200

Netting would have shown "net unbilled" of −40, concealing ₹200 lakh of conditional rights and ₹240 lakh of obligations.

Note that for Contract C the advance is a contract liability measured against revenue (150 − 60 = 90) — the formula uses cash received when no invoice has been raised.

Recognition flow

  1. Revenue recognised before billing: Dr Contract asset, Cr Revenue.
  2. Right becomes unconditional (invoice/certification): Dr Trade receivable, Cr Contract asset.
  3. Billing before performance (unconditional right): Dr Trade receivable, Cr Contract liability.
  4. Cash received in advance (no invoice): Dr Bank, Cr Contract liability.
  5. Performance against advance: Dr Contract liability, Cr Revenue.
  6. Cash collected: Dr Bank, Cr Trade receivable.

GST is charged on invoice or advance under the CGST Act time-of-supply rules (for services, advances attract GST); the GST payable is a separate liability and is not part of the contract balance or revenue.

Impairment and presentation

Receivables are financial assets measured under Ind AS 109; contract assets are not financial instruments but are brought within Ind AS 109's impairment requirements (115.107–108, Ind AS 109.5.5.15). Use the simplified approach (lifetime ECL). Impairment losses on receivables and contract assets are disclosed separately from other impairment losses (115.113(b)).

Schedule III Division II has no specific "contract asset" line. Common practice (consistent with ICAI's Guidance Note on Division II and industry disclosures):

  • Contract assets → "Other current financial assets" (unbilled revenue) or "Other current assets", with a policy note; some EPC companies show "Contract assets" as a separate line within current assets.
  • Unbilled amounts that are unconditional → "Trade receivables — unbilled" (Schedule III ageing requires unbilled receivables to be shown separately in the ageing schedule).
  • Contract liabilities → "Other current liabilities" (advances from customers, deferred revenue); non-current portion if performance is expected beyond 12 months or the operating cycle.

The 115.116–118 disclosure requires opening and closing balances of receivables, contract assets and contract liabilities, revenue recognised from opening contract liabilities, and significant changes.

Current vs non-current

Contract assets and liabilities are classified using the operating cycle test of Ind AS 1.66/1.69. EPC companies with multi-year cycles often classify the whole balance as current, disclosing the operating cycle; SaaS companies with three-year prepaid subscriptions split deferred revenue into current (next 12 months) and non-current.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Contract combination (115.17): two contracts negotiated as a package are combined, and the net position is computed for the combined contract.
  • Invoices raised in advance on cancellable contracts: do not gross up receivables and contract liabilities; disclose or recognise only upon cash receipt.
  • Unbilled revenue in IT services: time-and-materials unbilled (billed next month, no further performance) is a receivable; fixed-price unbilled pending milestone is a contract asset. Indian IT majors disclose both.
  • Refund liabilities (expected returns, rebates) are not contract liabilities — they are separate liabilities measured under 115.55.
  • Customer advances with a significant financing component: the contract liability accretes interest (Dr Finance cost, Cr Contract liability), increasing revenue.
  • Credit balances on customer ledgers from overpayments are not contract liabilities if no performance obligation remains; they are financial liabilities.

Key takeaways

  • Conditional right = contract asset; unconditional right = receivable; consideration ahead of performance = contract liability.
  • One net position per contract; no netting across contracts.
  • A receivable can exist before revenue if the invoice is non-cancellable and payment is due.
  • Both receivables and contract assets attract Ind AS 109 ECL; disclose impairment separately.
  • Schedule III has no dedicated line — present consistently, disclose policy, show unbilled receivables in ageing.
  • The roll-forward (115.116–118) is a by-product of a properly built waterfall.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the balances [L1]

For each item at 31 March 2025, state whether it is a contract asset, trade receivable, contract liability or other: (i) ₹40 lakh of T&M work for March, invoiced 3 April; (ii) ₹1.2 crore of work completed on a fixed-price contract, billable on achievement of the "factory acceptance test" in May; (iii) ₹75 lakh advance received for AMC starting 1 April 2025; (iv) ₹18 lakh customer overpayment after contract completion; (v) ₹30 lakh retention, payable on 30 September 2025 irrespective of defects.

Q2. Contract-wise net position and presentation [L2]

Vriddhi Engineering has four contracts with the same customer group at 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): Contract 1 revenue 45, billed 38, collected 30; Contract 2 revenue 12, billed 20, collected 20; Contract 3 revenue 0, advance received 8; Contract 4 revenue 22, billed 22, collected 15. Contracts 1 and 2 were negotiated together with a single commercial objective. Compute contract assets, contract liabilities and receivables for presentation, and pass the journal entries for Contract 3.

💡 Hint: Apply the combination criteria in 115.17 before calculating any net position.

Q3. Annual SaaS billing in advance [L2]

On 1 January 2025, NimbusHR Ltd invoices a customer ₹36 lakh plus 18% GST for a non-cancellable 12-month subscription starting that day; payment terms 30 days; the customer pays on 10 February 2025. Pass entries from 1 January to 31 March 2025 and show balances at 31 March. Repeat assuming the subscription can be cancelled at any time with a pro-rata refund.

Q4. Drawing-power memo [L3]

A bank's stock-and-book-debts statement allows 75% drawing power on receivables up to 90 days and excludes unbilled revenue. The company's balance sheet shows ₹110 crore "trade receivables" including ₹35 crore "unbilled — milestone pending". Draft a review memo explaining the Ind AS 115 classification, the effect on Schedule III ageing disclosure and drawing power, and the review procedures you would run.
