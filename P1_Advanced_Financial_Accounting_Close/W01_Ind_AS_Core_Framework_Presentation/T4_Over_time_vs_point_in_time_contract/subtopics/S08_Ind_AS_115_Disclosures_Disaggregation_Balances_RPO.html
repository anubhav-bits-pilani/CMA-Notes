Subtopic note

Ind AS 115 Disclosures: Disaggregation, Balances, RPO and Expedients

Scope: Disclosures: disaggregation, contract balance movements, remaining performance obligations (115.120) and practical expedients

Why it matters

Ind AS 115's disclosure objective (115.110) is to enable users to understand the nature, amount, timing and uncertainty of revenue and cash flows. For analysts, the remaining performance obligation (RPO) figure is effectively an audited order book; contract balance roll-forwards show billing discipline and advance funding; disaggregation supports segment-level forecasting. For preparers, these disclosures are where a weak revenue model is exposed — numbers must reconcile to the revenue schedule, the trial balance and the cash flow. Indian NSE-listed companies' notes are reviewed by NFRA in its audit quality reviews and by QRB of ICAI; inadequate RPO and disaggregation disclosures are common findings.

Core notes

1. Disaggregation of revenue (115.114–115, B87–B89)

Disaggregate revenue into categories depicting how economic factors affect the nature, amount, timing and uncertainty of revenue and cash flows. Categories commonly used:

  • type of good or service (products, services, licences, SaaS);
  • geographical region (India, US, Europe — export vs domestic);
  • market or customer type (government vs private, retail vs institutional);
  • type of contract (fixed price, time-and-materials, cost-plus);
  • contract duration (short-term vs long-term);
  • timing of transfer (point in time vs over time);
  • sales channel (direct, distributors, online).

Choose categories consistent with information reviewed by the CODM, presented in investor presentations and used for performance evaluation (B88). Show the relationship between the disaggregated revenue and segment revenue under Ind AS 108 (115.115).

2. Contract balances (115.116–118)

Required:

  • opening and closing balances of receivables, contract assets and contract liabilities (if not otherwise separately presented);
  • revenue recognised in the period that was included in the opening contract liability balance;
  • revenue recognised in the period from POs satisfied (or partially satisfied) in previous periods — e.g., changes in transaction price, cumulative catch-up from estimate changes;
  • explanation of how the timing of satisfaction of POs relates to typical payment timing and the effect on contract balances (115.117);
  • explanation of significant changes in contract asset and liability balances (qualitative and quantitative): business combinations, cumulative catch-up adjustments, impairment of contract assets, changes in time frame for a right to become unconditional, changes in time frame for a PO to be satisfied (115.118).

3. Performance obligations (115.119)

Describe: when the entity typically satisfies POs (on shipment, on delivery, as services are rendered, on completion); significant payment terms (when payment is due, SFC, variable consideration and constraint); nature of goods/services and whether the entity is an agent; obligations for returns and refunds; types of warranties.

4. Remaining performance obligations (115.120)

Disclose: (a) the aggregate amount of the transaction price allocated to POs that are unsatisfied (or partially unsatisfied) at the reporting date; and (b) an explanation of when the entity expects to recognise that amount as revenue — either quantitatively using time bands most appropriate for the duration of the remaining POs (e.g., within 1 year, 1–3 years, beyond 3 years), or qualitatively.

RPO includes fixed consideration and constrained variable consideration estimates, but excludes amounts constrained out of the transaction price; it is not the same as backlog or order book in management's KPI sense (which may include unawarded options or cancellable orders). Explain any difference when the company quotes "order book" in investor material.

\[ \text{RPO}_{\text{close}} = \text{RPO}_{\text{open}} + \text{New contracts and modifications} - \text{Revenue recognised} \pm \text{Transaction price changes} \]

5. Practical expedients for RPO (115.121–122)

An entity need not disclose RPO information for a PO if either: (a) the PO is part of a contract with an original expected duration of one year or less; or (b) the entity recognises revenue using the right-to-invoice expedient (B16).

If an expedient is used, disclose which one and whether any consideration is not included in the transaction price (e.g., constrained variable consideration) and therefore not in the RPO disclosure (115.122). ASC 606 offers additional optional exemptions (e.g., variable consideration allocated wholly to wholly unsatisfied distinct goods within a series, or sales/usage-based royalties) that IFRS 15 and Ind AS 115 do not contain; only (a) and (b) are available to Indian preparers, so US-parent group reporting packs often need an extra RPO computation for the Ind AS statements.

6. Other required disclosures

  • India-specific reconciliation (115.126AA): contracted price to revenue recognised, showing each adjustment (discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, performance bonuses). Covered in Topic 3.
  • Significant judgements (115.123–126): timing of satisfaction (methods used to recognise over time and why they faithfully depict transfer), determining transaction price, estimating variable consideration, constraint, allocation, measuring obligations for returns.
  • Assets from costs to obtain or fulfil a contract (115.127–128): closing balances by category, amortisation and impairment.
  • Practical expedients used for SFC (115.63) and incremental costs of obtaining a contract (115.94) — disclose (115.129).

Building disclosures from the model

The revenue waterfall should carry tags per contract row: segment, geography, contract type, timing pattern, start/end dates. Disaggregation is a SUMIFS by tag; the roll-forward uses opening balance, revenue from opening liability (the lower of opening liability and revenue in the period for that contract, computed per row), new billings in excess; RPO is transaction price less cumulative revenue, bucketed by expected recognition month using SUMIFS on the forward revenue schedule. A check row ties total disaggregated revenue to the P&L and closing balances to the balance sheet.

=SUMIFS(Rev_FY25, Tag_Geo, "India", Tag_Timing, "Over time")
=SUMPRODUCT((Fwd_Month>FYEnd)*(Fwd_Month<=EDATE(FYEnd,12))*Fwd_Rev)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cancellable contracts: RPO includes only the non-cancellable portion (the contract term under Ind AS 115 is limited by enforceable rights).
  • Constrained variable consideration (e.g., LD exposure) is excluded from RPO; disclose qualitatively.
  • Termination for convenience clauses without substantive penalty may make the contract effectively month-to-month, shrinking RPO.
  • Multi-currency RPO: translate at closing rate; explain FX effects in the RPO movement.
  • Interim reports (Ind AS 34.16A(l)) require disaggregation of revenue in interim financial statements.
  • Materiality: disclosures may be omitted if immaterial (Ind AS 1.31) — but RPO for long-cycle EPC or SaaS businesses is rarely immaterial.

Key takeaways

  • Disaggregate revenue using categories consistent with CODM reporting and link to Ind AS 108 segments.
  • Disclose opening/closing receivables, contract assets and liabilities, revenue from opening liabilities and from prior-period POs, and explain significant changes.
  • RPO = unsatisfied transaction price, in time bands; it excludes constrained amounts and differs from management "order book".
  • Only two RPO expedients exist under Ind AS 115: contracts ≤1 year and the right-to-invoice expedient — disclose their use.
  • Significant judgements, contract cost assets, expedients and the India-specific 126AA reconciliation complete the package.
  • A tagged, contract-level waterfall produces all these disclosures and their checks.

Practice Questions

Q1. Drafting the contract liability roll-forward [L2]

SaaSify Ltd's contract liabilities at 1 April 2024 were ₹42 crore. During FY 2024-25: ₹38 crore of the opening balance was recognised as revenue; ₹4 crore of the opening balance was refunded on cancellation; new billings in advance of ₹65 crore were raised on non-cancellable contracts; ₹51 crore of revenue was recognised from those new billings; a business combination added ₹6 crore. Prepare the roll-forward and the 115.116(b) disclosure sentence.

Q2. RPO with expedients [L2]

At 31 March 2025, Enviro EPC has: (i) a ₹900 crore contract, 35% complete, expected completion March 2027, with ₹40 crore of LD exposure constrained out of the price; (ii) AMC contracts of 11-month duration with ₹12 crore unsatisfied; (iii) a T&M contract billed at ₹5,000 per engineer-day under B16, with ₹8 crore expected over the next 2 years. Compute the RPO to disclose, prepare time bands (≤1 year, 1–2 years) assuming even progress on (i), and state the expedients disclosure.

💡 Hint: Decide first which contracts qualify for an expedient and whether the entity chooses to use it.

Q3. Choosing disaggregation categories [L3]

A diversified NSE-listed company has three segments: cables (domestic and export, sold via distributors and direct to EPC contractors), EPC (government and private, over time) and consumer appliances (online and retail channels). The CODM pack shows revenue by segment, geography and channel; investor presentations show order inflow and order book. Design the disaggregation table, reconcile it to segment revenue, and explain why the "order book" in the investor deck may differ from RPO.

Q4. Review of a draft revenue note [L2]

A draft note says: "Revenue is recognised when significant risks and rewards are transferred. Unbilled revenue of ₹68 crore is included in trade receivables. The company's order book is ₹4,200 crore." List the deficiencies against Ind AS 115.119–122 and 115.123–126 and redraft the note's structure (headings and required contents).
