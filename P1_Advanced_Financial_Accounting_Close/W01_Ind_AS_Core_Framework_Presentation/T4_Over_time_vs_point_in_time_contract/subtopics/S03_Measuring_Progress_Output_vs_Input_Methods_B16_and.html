Subtopic note

Measuring Progress: Output vs Input Methods, B16 and B19

Scope: Measuring progress: output (milestones, surveys, units) vs input (cost-to-cost, labour hours); uninstalled materials (B19); right-to-invoice expedient (B16)

Why it matters

Once a PO qualifies for over-time recognition, the progress measure determines how much revenue lands in each month and quarter. For an EPC company with an order book of ₹20,000 crore, a two-percentage-point difference in measured progress is ₹400 crore of revenue. Progress measures are the most common area of audit challenge in construction and project businesses, the main lever for earnings management, and the core calculation in any revenue waterfall model. Ind AS 115.39–45 and B14–B19 govern the choice.

Core notes

Objective and consistency

The objective is to depict the entity's performance in transferring control of goods or services (115.39). An entity applies a single method for each PO (not a blend), applies it consistently to similar POs in similar circumstances (115.40), and remeasures progress at each reporting date. A change in the measure of progress reflects a change in circumstances and is accounted for as a change in estimate under Ind AS 8 (115.43) — i.e., cumulative catch-up within the same contract, not restatement.

If the entity cannot reasonably measure the outcome but expects to recover costs (typically in early stages), it recognises revenue only to the extent of costs incurred (115.45) — a zero-margin approach.

Output methods (B15–B17)

Output methods measure the value to the customer of goods or services transferred to date relative to the remaining goods or services promised:

  • Surveys of performance completed — engineer-certified measurement books in civil construction, quantity surveys.
  • Appraisals of results achieved and milestones reached — but milestones are appropriate only if they faithfully depict performance; long gaps between milestones with material WIP in between distort progress.
  • Time elapsed — the natural measure for SaaS, AMC and stand-ready obligations satisfied evenly.
  • Units produced or delivered — appropriate for a series of identical units if WIP at period-end is not material. If WIP or finished goods controlled by the customer are material and not reflected, units-delivered understates performance (B15).

Output methods are conceptually closest to the objective but information may not be directly observable or may be costly to obtain.

Input methods (B18–B19)

Input methods measure the entity's efforts or inputs relative to total expected inputs: costs incurred (cost-to-cost), labour hours, machine hours, resources consumed, time elapsed. Straight-line recognition is appropriate if inputs are expended evenly.

The formula for cost-to-cost:

\[ \text{\% complete}_t = \frac{\text{Cumulative cost incurred}_t}{\text{Total estimated contract cost}_t} \]

\[ \text{Revenue}_t = \text{\% complete}_t \times \text{Transaction price}_t - \text{Cumulative revenue recognised up to } t-1 \]

The weakness: input costs that do not reflect performance must be excluded (B19):

  1. Costs that do not contribute to progress — wasted materials, labour or other resources due to significant inefficiencies not reflected in the price (e.g., rework from the entity's own errors, abnormal idle time). These are expensed as incurred and excluded from the numerator and the total cost estimate.
  2. Uninstalled materials — costs not proportionate to progress. Where all of the following hold, revenue is recognised equal to the cost of the goods (zero margin) when the customer obtains control of them, and they are excluded from the cost-to-cost calculation: - the good is not distinct; - the customer is expected to obtain control of the good significantly before receiving services related to it; - the cost of the transferred good is significant relative to total expected costs to fully satisfy the PO; - the entity procures the good from a supplier and is not significantly involved in designing and manufacturing it.

Common uninstalled items in Indian EPC: lifts, transformers, turbines, imported membranes, solar modules, elevators bought from OEMs.

Illustration: B19 uninstalled materials

Contract price ₹50 crore; total expected cost ₹40 crore, including a ₹10 crore transformer procured from an OEM and delivered to the customer's site (customer obtains control on delivery) in year 1, to be installed in year 2. Year 1 other costs ₹12 crore.

The unadjusted method front-loads ₹1.5 crore of margin purely because a supplier invoice arrived.

Labour hours and other inputs

Labour hours suit service-heavy contracts (IT implementation, consulting) where labour is the dominant input. Hours from inefficiency (e.g., a failed migration that must be redone at the entity's cost) are excluded. Subcontractor costs can be included if they reflect work performed (based on subcontractor progress, not advance payments).

Right-to-invoice practical expedient (B16)

If the entity has a right to consideration that corresponds directly with the value to the customer of performance completed to date — for example, a time-and-materials contract billing a fixed hourly rate, or a utility contract billing per unit consumed at fixed rates — it may recognise revenue in the amount it has a right to invoice. Conditions in practice:

  • the per-unit rate reflects the value of each unit (rates that step down significantly over the term, large upfront fees or back-ended rates may fail);
  • fixed minimums or volume discounts require analysis;
  • using B16 also unlocks the 115.121(b) RPO disclosure exemption.

Example: an IT services firm bills ₹4,500/hour for a senior developer for 12 months with no cap. Revenue = hours worked × ₹4,500 each month.

Selecting a method — decision table

Situation Preferred measure
Stand-ready service, even pattern (SaaS, AMC) Time elapsed (straight-line)
EPC with diverse inputs, reliable cost estimates Cost-to-cost with B19 adjustments
Civil works with engineer-certified measurement Survey of work performed (output)
Time-and-materials billing at fair rates Right-to-invoice (B16)
Labour-intensive implementation Labour hours
Series of identical units, no material WIP Units delivered

Model implementation

In the monthly waterfall, keep three input blocks: estimated total cost by month (to capture revisions), cumulative cost incurred, and excluded costs (B19 uninstalled, inefficiencies). Compute % complete with =MIN(1, (CumCost - Excluded)/(TotalCost - ExcludedTotal)) and period revenue as the difference in cumulative revenue. A check row flags any month where % complete decreases (possible only after an estimate increase, which is a legitimate cumulative catch-up and must be explained).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Milestone billing ≠ milestone method. Billing milestones in a contract are commercial; they are only a valid output measure if they depict performance.
  • Mobilisation costs (site setup) that do not transfer anything to the customer are excluded from progress; they may be capitalised as costs to fulfil a contract (115.95) and amortised.
  • Loss of reasonable measurement mid-contract (e.g., a disputed redesign) requires switching to the cost-recovery approach until an estimate is possible again.
  • Single method per PO: blending survey and cost methods for one PO is not allowed; separate POs can use different methods.
  • Estimate revisions of total cost are Ind AS 8 changes in estimates with cumulative catch-up, which can cause negative revenue in a period only through reduced % or price — losses on the whole contract go to an onerous provision instead.

Key takeaways

  • Choose one method per PO that faithfully depicts transfer of control; apply consistently and update each reporting date.
  • Output methods: surveys, milestones, units, time elapsed; input methods: cost-to-cost, labour hours.
  • Exclude inefficiency costs and uninstalled materials (B19); recognise uninstalled goods at zero margin.
  • The B16 right-to-invoice expedient fits fixed-rate T&M and usage contracts and relieves RPO disclosure.
  • Changes in progress estimates are Ind AS 8 estimate changes — cumulative catch-up.
  • Use the zero-margin cost-recovery approach when the outcome cannot yet be reasonably measured.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cost-to-cost with revised estimates [L2]

Delta Projects Ltd has a ₹80 crore contract. Year 1: costs incurred ₹20 crore, estimated total cost ₹64 crore. Year 2: cumulative costs ₹45 crore, revised total cost ₹70 crore, and an approved variation increases price to ₹86 crore (not distinct). Year 3: contract completed, total costs ₹71 crore. Compute revenue and gross margin in each year and explain how the year-2 revision is accounted for.

Q2. Uninstalled lifts in a commercial tower [L2]

A contractor building an office tower on the customer's land (price ₹200 crore, expected cost ₹160 crore) procures lifts worth ₹24 crore from an OEM; they are delivered and controlled by the customer on 15 February 2025 but installed in FY 2025-26. Other costs to 31 March 2025: ₹56 crore. Compute FY 2024-25 revenue with and without B19 adjustments and the margin difference. List the four B19 conditions and test each.

💡 Hint: Remove the lifts from both numerator and denominator and reduce the transaction price by their cost for the margin-bearing calculation.

Q3. Choosing a progress measure for an ERP rollout [L3]

TechServe Ltd signs a fixed-fee ₹6 crore ERP implementation (single over-time PO) with a 10-month plan of 12,000 hours. Costs: 70% labour, 20% third-party licences bought in month 1 and passed to the client, 10% travel. Month 4 sees 800 hours of rework because TechServe's architects misconfigured the data model. Evaluate cost-to-cost vs labour hours vs milestones, specify the adjustments needed, and design the Excel columns you would add to the waterfall.

Q4. Right-to-invoice expedient [L2]

A BPO contract charges ₹120 per transaction processed in year 1, ₹90 in year 2 and ₹60 in year 3, reflecting expected automation efficiencies passed to the customer; market rates fall similarly. A second contract charges ₹100 per transaction for all three years plus a ₹1 crore upfront "transition fee" for activities that do not transfer a service. Assess whether B16 applies to each and the consequence for RPO disclosure.
