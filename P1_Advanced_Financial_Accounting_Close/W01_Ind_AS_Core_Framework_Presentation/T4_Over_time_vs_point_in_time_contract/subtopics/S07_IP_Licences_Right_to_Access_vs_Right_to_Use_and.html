Subtopic note

IP Licences: Right to Access vs Right to Use and Royalties

Scope: IP licences: right to access (over time) vs right to use (point in time); sales/usage-based royalty exception (B63)

Why it matters

Licences of intellectual property — software, patents, brands, film and music rights, drug formulations, franchise rights — are common in Indian pharma (out-licensing to global partners), media (OTT and satellite rights for films), IT products and consumer brands (franchising). The timing answer differs sharply: a right-to-use licence can put the entire upfront fee into one quarter, while a right-to-access licence spreads it. Royalties add a second layer: sales- or usage-based royalties are excluded from the transaction price until the underlying sale occurs, regardless of how predictable they are. Analysts and auditors test these conclusions in pharma deal announcements and media rights sales.

Core notes

Step zero: is the licence distinct?

Apply the licensing guidance (B52–B63B) only to a licence that is a distinct PO or the predominant item in a combined PO. If a licence is not distinct — e.g., software that is essential to a hardware device, or a licence that only provides benefit together with an ongoing hosting service — account for the combined PO under the general over-time/point-in-time criteria (B54). SaaS where the customer cannot take possession of the software without significant penalty is a service, not a licence.

Nature of the promise (B56–B62)

A licence is a right to access the IP as it exists throughout the licence period (over time) if all three criteria in B58 are met:

  1. the contract requires, or the customer reasonably expects, that the entity will undertake activities that significantly affect the IP to which the customer has rights;
  2. the rights directly expose the customer to any positive or negative effects of those activities; and
  3. those activities do not transfer a good or service to the customer as they occur.

Activities significantly affect the IP if they are expected to change the form (design, content) or functionality of the IP, or the customer's ability to obtain benefit from the IP is substantially derived from, or dependent on, those activities — e.g., the value of a brand depends on the entity's ongoing marketing and reputation (B59A).

If the criteria are not met, the licence is a right to use the IP as it exists at the point in time the licence is granted — revenue at the point when control transfers, i.e., when the customer can use and benefit from the licence (not before the licence period begins, B61).

Type of IP Typical classification Reason
Functional IP — software (on-premise), drug compound, film, music recording, completed design Right to use Standalone functionality; licensor's later activities do not change it
Symbolic IP — brand, trade name, logo, franchise rights, sports team name Right to access Utility derives from licensor's ongoing activities and reputation
Film with promised ongoing re-edits / content changes Assess — may be right to access Activities change form/content

Restrictions of time, geography or use (B62) define the attributes of the licence; they do not determine whether it is a right to access or right to use. Guarantees to defend the patent are not a PO.

Timing details

  • Right to use: recognise at the later of transfer of control (customer can use the IP) and the start of the licence period. A film licence signed in February with a window starting 1 May is recognised in May.
  • Right to access: measure progress over the licence period, typically straight-line.
  • Renewals: revenue for a renewal of a right-to-use licence cannot be recognised before the renewal period begins (B61); Ind AS/IFRS do not contain the US GAAP explicit rule but B61's requirement has similar effect.

Sales- or usage-based royalty exception (B63–B63B)

For consideration in the form of a sales-based or usage-based royalty promised in exchange for a licence of IP, recognise revenue only when (or as) the later of:

(a) the subsequent sale or usage occurs; and (b) the PO to which some or all of the royalty has been allocated has been satisfied (or partially satisfied).

The exception applies when the royalty relates only to a licence of IP or when the licence is the predominant item to which the royalty relates (B63A). It is a mandatory override of the general variable consideration rules — the entity does not estimate and constrain the royalty; it simply waits. If the exception does not apply (e.g., the royalty relates to a sale of goods with an incidental licence), the general variable consideration model applies.

Minimum guarantees. A guaranteed minimum royalty is fixed consideration: for a right-to-use licence, recognise it upfront when control passes; the excess royalty above the minimum is recognised as sales occur. For a right-to-access licence, the treatment depends on the progress measure chosen; practice often recognises the fixed minimum ratably and the excess when earned.

Milestone payments in pharma out-licensing (regulatory approval, first commercial sale) are variable consideration not covered by the royalty exception unless they are sales-based. Development milestones are estimated under the most likely amount method and are usually fully constrained until achieved because regulatory outcomes are outside the entity's control.

Illustration: pharma out-licensing (₹ crore)

On 1 January 2025, IndoGen Pharma Ltd licenses exclusive US rights to a patented molecule to a US partner for 10 years. Consideration: upfront ₹80 crore (non-refundable), ₹40 crore on USFDA approval, and an 8% royalty on net sales. IndoGen has no obligation to undertake further R&D on the molecule. The licence period starts on signing and the partner has access to the dossier on that date.

Element Classification FY 2024-25 revenue
Upfront ₹80 crore Right to use (functional IP; no activities significantly affect it) ₹80 crore on 1 Jan 2025
Approval milestone ₹40 crore Variable consideration; constrained until approval (outcome not in IndoGen's control) Nil
8% royalty Sales-based royalty exception Royalty on partner's January–March sales only

If IndoGen also promised to conduct Phase III trials whose results improve the molecule's utility, the licence and R&D services may not be distinct, changing the pattern to over time.

Franchise illustration

A QSR brand grants a 10-year franchise for ₹50 lakh upfront plus 6% of sales; it commits to ongoing marketing and menu development. This is symbolic IP → right to access: ₹50 lakh recognised straight-line over 10 years (₹5 lakh per year), royalty as franchisee sales occur.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Customer's reasonable expectation of updates (established business practice) can make activities part of the analysis even if not contractual.
  • Software with unspecified upgrades/PCS: the licence (right to use) and the post-contract support are generally separate POs.
  • Satellite/OTT film rights with a delivery date after year-end: no revenue until the window starts and content is delivered.
  • Royalty in exchange for both a licence and services: apply B63A predominance — if the licence is predominant, the whole royalty follows the exception.
  • Sale of IP (outright assignment) is not a licence; it is a sale of an asset — point-in-time revenue (or gain under Ind AS 38 if not in ordinary activities), with sales-based contingent consideration estimated and constrained under general rules.
  • Transfer pricing and tax: royalty income timing under the Income-tax Act may follow accrual on agreement terms; Ind AS timing creates deferred tax differences.

Key takeaways

  • Apply licensing guidance only to distinct licences (or predominant licences in a combined PO).
  • Right to access (over time) requires all three B58 criteria; otherwise right to use (point in time).
  • Functional IP is usually right to use; symbolic IP (brands, franchises) is right to access.
  • Point-in-time licence revenue cannot be recognised before the licence period begins.
  • Sales/usage-based royalties on IP are recognised only when the underlying sale/usage occurs (B63) — no estimation.
  • Development milestones are variable consideration, usually constrained until achieved.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the licences [L1]

State right to access or right to use, with reasons: (i) a 5-year licence of a perpetual on-premise accounting software, no updates promised; (ii) a 3-year licence of a cricket franchise's logo for merchandise; (iii) satellite rights to a completed Hindi film for 7 years starting 1 June 2025; (iv) a licence of a patented agrochemical formulation with an obligation to conduct field trials that will modify the formulation.

Q2. Film rights with minimum guarantee [L2]

On 15 February 2025, Silverline Studios licenses OTT rights to a completed film for 5 years for a minimum guarantee of ₹12 crore plus 20% of subscription revenue attributable to views above 2 crore views. The licence window starts 1 April 2025 and the master copy is delivered on 20 March 2025. Determine revenue for FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 assuming views exceed the threshold in FY 2025-26 producing ₹3 crore of overage royalty.

💡 Hint: Check B61 before recognising the minimum guarantee.

Q3. Pharma out-licensing with R&D services [L3]

A Hyderabad-based biotech licenses a molecule for ₹150 crore upfront and up to ₹600 crore in milestones (₹100 crore on Phase III start, ₹200 crore on approval, ₹300 crore on cumulative sales reaching USD 500 million) plus tiered royalties of 8–12%. It must also conduct Phase II trials for 18 months; the licensee cannot benefit from the molecule without these trials because only the biotech has the know-how. Identify the POs, the nature of any licence, the treatment of each milestone and the royalties, and draft the significant judgement disclosure under Ind AS 115.123.

Q4. Brand licence within a group [L2]

A listed FMCG parent licenses its house brand to a subsidiary for 3% of the subsidiary's turnover. The parent undertakes national advertising. In its standalone financial statements, determine the nature of the licence and the timing of royalty income for FY 2024-25 given subsidiary turnover of ₹900 crore, of which ₹240 crore occurred in Q4.
