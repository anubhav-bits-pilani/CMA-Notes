Subtopic note

Principal vs Agent: Gross or Net Revenue (B34–B38)

Scope: Principal vs agent (B34–B38): control before transfer, primary responsibility, inventory risk, pricing discretion — marketplaces, travel, food delivery

Why it matters

India's consumer internet companies — Zomato/Eternal, Swiggy, Nykaa, MakeMyTrip, PB Fintech, FirstCry — report "GMV/GOV" in investor decks and a much smaller "revenue from operations" in financial statements. The gap is the principal-agent conclusion. It does not change profit but transforms revenue multiples, gross margin, take-rate analysis and the structure of valuation comps. Changing business models (marketplace to inventory, 1P to 3P) can flip the conclusion, and SEBI offer-document reviews, auditors and SEC staff (for dual listings) examine it closely. FP&A teams must model revenue on the reported basis while tracking GMV as a KPI.

Core notes

The two-step analysis (B34–B34A)

When another party is involved in providing goods or services to a customer, the entity determines whether its promise is to provide the specified goods or services itself (principal) or to arrange for the other party to provide them (agent):

  1. Identify the specified good or service to be provided to the customer — a good, a right to a good or service (e.g., a flight ticket, a restaurant voucher), or a service of integrating goods/services into a combined output.
  2. Assess whether the entity controls each specified good or service before it is transferred to the customer (B35).

Control is the governing test. The entity is a principal if it obtains control of:

  • a good or another asset from the other party that it then transfers to the customer;
  • a right to a service to be performed by the other party, which gives it the ability to direct that party to provide the service on its behalf; or
  • a good or service from the other party that it combines with other goods/services in providing the specified output (B35A).

Momentary legal title passing through the entity does not, by itself, prove control.

Indicators (B37)

Where control is not obvious, B37 indicators support the assessment (they are not a separate checklist that overrides control):

Indicator Principal-leaning facts Agent-leaning facts
(a) Primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise Entity is responsible for acceptability, quality, replacement; customer looks to it Supplier is responsible; platform terms disclaim responsibility
(b) Inventory risk before or after transfer Entity buys stock before a customer order, bears obsolescence, takes returns into its own stock Seller holds and owns stock; returns go back to seller
(c) Discretion in establishing price Entity sets the end price Seller sets price; platform merely displays it

Credit risk and the form of consideration (commission) were removed as indicators by the 2016 clarifications (adopted in Ind AS 115). Collecting the full amount from the customer and then remitting to the seller is a cash-flow arrangement, not control.

Measurement

  • Principal: revenue = gross consideration from the customer; payments to the supplier = cost of sales.
  • Agent: revenue = fee or commission retained (net of amounts paid to the principal), recognised when the arrangement occurs — often when the agent's PO is satisfied, which may be on booking rather than on service delivery (B36).

An entity can be principal for some specified goods/services and agent for others in the same contract — e.g., a food-delivery platform is often an agent for the food and a principal for delivery services.

Industry applications

E-commerce marketplaces. Under the FDI policy (Press Note 2 of 2018), foreign-owned e-commerce entities in India cannot hold inventory in the marketplace model; sellers own goods, set prices and are responsible for quality. The platform's specified service is facilitating the sale, so it is an agent — revenue = commission, listing fees, advertising, logistics fees. Inventory-led private-label or 1P segments (where permitted, e.g., Indian-owned entities or B2B) are principal.

Online travel agents. For air tickets, the airline provides the flight and sets fares; the OTA is an agent, recognising commission/convenience fees on booking (when its arranging service is complete). For hotel "merchant model" packages where the OTA pre-purchases room inventory at its own risk (committed allotments, non-refundable), sets the package price and bundles services, it can be a principal. Holiday packages that integrate flights, hotels and transfers into a combined itinerary may make the OTA a principal for the package.

Food delivery. Restaurants prepare the food and set menu prices; the platform is an agent for food, earning commission. For the delivery service itself, judgement depends on who the customer is (restaurant or end consumer) and who controls the delivery partners' service; Indian platforms have concluded delivery fees charged to consumers are the platform's own revenue. After the GST change effective 1 January 2022 (Sec 9(5) CGST Act), platforms pay GST on restaurant services as a deemed supplier — this tax mechanic does not make them principal for the food under Ind AS 115.

Quick commerce and dark stores. If the platform's Indian-owned entity buys and holds inventory, sets prices and takes returns, it is principal; if third-party sellers stock dark stores, agent.

Advertising networks and payment aggregators. Ad-tech: depends on whether the entity controls ad inventory before placement. Payment aggregators: MDR revenue is usually net of interchange (agent for interchange, which is set by networks and issuers) — disclosure varies.

Illustration

FoodRush Ltd, FY 2024-25 (₹ crore):

Item Amount
Gross order value (food) 8,000
Commission charged to restaurants (22%) 1,760
Delivery fees charged to consumers 600
Platform fees from consumers 150
Payout to delivery partners 900
Discounts funded by FoodRush to consumers 300

As agent for food: revenue from commission = ₹1,760 crore. Delivery fee ₹600 crore + platform fee ₹150 crore = principal services. Consumer discounts funded by the platform are consideration payable to a customer (115.70): if the consumer is the platform's customer for delivery/platform services, discounts reduce that revenue; discounts that exceed revenue from that customer may be marketing expense — a contentious judgement Indian platforms disclose. Revenue ≈ 1,760 + 600 + 150 − 300 = ₹2,210 crore versus GOV ₹8,000 crore; payouts to delivery partners ₹900 crore are expenses (delivery cost).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Specified good identified wrongly: framing the specified good as "the platform service" when the customer is really buying the product leads to the wrong conclusion.
  • Platform guarantees: offering "assured" quality or returns handling by the platform is primary-responsibility evidence but may be outweighed if sellers set prices and own stock.
  • Gift cards and wallets: a platform selling third-party vouchers is usually an agent; breakage on its own wallet balances follows B44–B47.
  • Taxes collected: GST and TCS collected under Sec 52 CGST are collected on behalf of government and excluded from revenue.
  • Change in model mid-year: the conclusion is per specified good; report segment and disaggregation notes accordingly.
  • Net revenue below discounts: negative revenue on a customer relationship signals discounts should be assessed as consideration payable to a customer vs marketing expense.

Key takeaways

  • Identify the specified good or service, then ask whether the entity controls it before transfer.
  • B37 indicators (primary responsibility, inventory risk, pricing discretion) support — but never override — the control assessment.
  • Credit risk and commission form are no longer indicators.
  • Principals report gross revenue and cost of sales; agents report net fee, often at booking.
  • An entity can be agent for one element and principal for another in the same transaction.
  • Deemed-supplier GST rules and cash collection do not determine Ind AS 115 conclusions.

Practice Questions

Q1. OTA flight and hotel bookings [L2]

TripIndia Ltd sells: (a) domestic air tickets at airline-set fares, earning ₹250 commission + ₹300 convenience fee per ticket (12 lakh tickets); (b) hotel rooms from a pool of 50,000 room-nights pre-purchased non-refundably at ₹3,000 each, resold at prices TripIndia sets — 46,000 sold at an average ₹4,200; (c) third-party hotel rooms on a pay-at-hotel basis earning 15% commission on ₹200 crore of bookings. Determine principal/agent for each, compute revenue and the treatment of unsold pre-purchased room-nights.

Q2. Marketplace vs inventory model — investor bridge [L3]

StyleKart Ltd (Indian-owned) has two segments: marketplace (GMV ₹2,400 crore, take rate 20% including commission, logistics and ads) and private label (sales ₹600 crore, COGS ₹330 crore). An analyst's model shows revenue of ₹3,000 crore and gross margin of 26%. Recompute reported revenue and gross margin, explain the conclusion for each segment, and draft the reconciliation of GMV to revenue from operations for the annual report.

💡 Hint: Gross margin must be computed on the reported revenue base, not GMV.

Q3. Food delivery — GST deemed supplier argument [L2]

A CFO argues: "Since 1 January 2022 we pay GST on restaurant supplies under Sec 9(5), so we are the supplier and should report food sales gross." Prepare a response referencing the specified good, B35–B37 and the distinction between tax-law deemed supply and control under Ind AS 115.

Q4. Interview — indicators removed in 2016 [L1]

Explain why credit risk and the form of consideration were removed from the principal-versus-agent indicators and give one example where relying on credit risk would lead to the wrong conclusion.
