Subtopic note

EPC Contracts: Advances, Retention, PVC, LDs and Loss Contracts

Scope: EPC specifics: mobilisation advances, retention money, price variation clauses, LDs, expected-loss contracts

Why it matters

Indian EPC and infrastructure companies (L&T, KEC International, Kalpataru Projects, NCC, PNC Infratech) carry large contract balances, significant variable consideration and recurring loss-contract provisions. Government contracts (NHAI, CPWD, Railways, state PWDs) follow standard forms with mobilisation advances, retention, price variation (escalation) formulas and liquidated damages. Each feature changes either the transaction price, the financing analysis or the contract balance, and each is a recurring audit and due-diligence topic. FP&A teams forecasting cash conversion for EPC businesses must model all five.

Core notes

1. Mobilisation advances

Commercial terms. Typically 5–10% of contract value paid on signing against a bank guarantee, interest-free (or at a concessional rate), recovered by deduction from running account (RA) bills, often starting when 10–20% of work is done and completed by 80–90%.

Accounting.

  • On receipt: Dr Bank, Cr Contract liability (customer advance).
  • Recovery through RA bills: the gross bill is Dr Receivable / Cr Contract asset; the deduction is Dr Contract liability / Cr Receivable.

Significant financing component (SFC). An interest-free advance received more than a year before performance could indicate a financing component (115.60–65). However, 115.62(c) says no SFC exists if the difference between promised consideration and the cash selling price arises for reasons other than financing — e.g., an advance that secures the contractor's mobilisation costs or protects the customer's interest. Most entities conclude that mobilisation advances exist to fund mobilisation and secure commitment, not to provide financing, and do not accrete interest. Where an advance is large, received years ahead, and priced to reflect a discount, accretion may be required (Dr Finance cost, Cr Contract liability).

Concessional interest-bearing advances: interest payable is a finance cost; the advance is still a contract liability (practice also treats it as a financial liability where repayable in cash irrespective of performance).

2. Retention money

Terms. 5–10% of each RA bill withheld by the customer and released after completion or the defects liability period (DLP), often replaceable by a bank guarantee.

Accounting. Revenue is recognised on progress irrespective of retention. The retained amount is either:

  • a contract asset if release depends on further performance (satisfactory completion, defect rectification); or
  • a receivable if release depends only on time (fixed date).

No SFC. 115.62(c) expressly addresses retention: withholding is to protect the customer against the contractor failing to complete obligations, not to provide financing. Retention is therefore usually not discounted. ECL still applies.

3. Price variation clauses (PVC)

PVC (escalation) clauses adjust the price for movement in indices (WPI, RBI CPI-IW, steel, cement, bitumen, labour indices) under formulae in the contract. They are variable consideration (115.50–51):

  • Estimate the expected PVC using expected value or most likely amount — for formula-based escalation linked to published indices, many entities recognise PVC as indices are published (they are a function of a known formula and observable inputs).
  • Apply the constraint (115.56): include only to the extent highly probable that a significant reversal will not occur. Forecast escalation on future index movements is usually constrained; escalation on work done using published indices is not.
  • Variable consideration attributable to a single PO or a distinct part of a series may be allocated entirely to it (115.84–85).

Claims and variations not yet approved are contract modifications (115.18–19) — recognise only when approved (enforceable); unpriced change orders may be estimated as variable consideration once the scope change is approved.

4. Liquidated damages (LDs)

LDs for delay (commonly 0.5% per week capped at 5–10% of contract value) reduce the transaction price: they are variable consideration, not a provision under Ind AS 37 (except where they compensate for a separate damage-type obligation). Estimate the most likely amount (binary: either the delay occurs or not) or the expected value (where a range of delay outcomes is possible), and constrain the revenue upside. If a delay is likely and extension of time (EOT) is not yet granted, revenue is recognised net of expected LDs; if the customer later grants EOT and waives LDs, the reversal is a cumulative catch-up increase in revenue.

LDs deducted by customers from bills but disputed: they reduce the transaction price unless the entity is highly probable to recover them.

5. Expected-loss contracts

If total estimated contract costs exceed the transaction price, Ind AS 115 does not contain loss-contract guidance (unlike AS 7.35). The loss is recognised under Ind AS 37 onerous contract provisions (Ind AS 37.66–69): the provision equals the lower of the cost of fulfilling and the cost of exiting the contract, where cost of fulfilling includes incremental costs and an allocation of other costs that relate directly to fulfilling contracts (2022 amendment, effective FY 2022-23). Before recognising a provision, the entity recognises impairment on assets dedicated to the contract (37.69), including capitalised contract costs under 115.101.

Illustration: an NHAI-style EPC contract (₹ crore)

Contract price ₹500 crore (2-year), estimated cost ₹440 crore. Mobilisation advance 10% (₹50 crore) received 1 April 2024, recovered at 12.5% from each RA bill. Retention 5% of each RA bill. Year 1: costs ₹176 crore (no B19 items). RA bills certified ₹180 crore. Expected delay of 6 weeks with LD at 0.5% per week (most likely amount: ₹15 crore). Published WPI-linked PVC on work done ₹6 crore.

Step Computation ₹ crore
Transaction price 500 + 6 (PVC on work done) − 15 (LD) 491
% complete 176 ÷ 440 40.0%
Revenue year 1 40% × 491 196.4
RA bills (gross) 180.0
Mobilisation recovered 12.5% × 180 22.5
Retention 5% × 180 9.0
Net receivable on bills 180 − 22.5 − 9 148.5
Contract asset 196.4 − 180 16.4
Retention (contract asset, conditional) 9.0
Mobilisation advance outstanding 50 − 22.5 27.5

Here the PVC is added to the transaction price and spread through the cumulative %. An alternative supported by 115.84–85 is to allocate escalation entirely to the work already performed (because it relates specifically to that work), recognising the full ₹6 crore immediately; whichever approach is chosen must be applied consistently and disclosed. The single contract's net position combines the contract asset items and the advance liability: presented under 115.105 as one net amount per contract (16.4 + 9.0 − 27.5 = net contract liability ₹2.1 crore) if retention is conditional — many Indian EPC companies instead present retention and advances gross, a presentation point to check against the policy note.

If at year 2 the cost estimate rises to ₹510 crore with the price unchanged at ₹491 crore, the contract is loss-making by ₹19 crore. Revenue is still recognised on progress; the remaining loss (₹19 crore less loss already reflected in cumulative recognition) is provided under Ind AS 37.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Arbitration awards: recognise additional consideration only when the award is final or highly probable not to reverse — awards under challenge in the High Court under Sec 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act are usually constrained (contrast Sec 36 deposit/stay mechanics).
  • Advances with 115.62(c) argument rejected: where advances fund the customer's own financing (e.g., deferred payment plans for a developer's buyers), SFC accounting is required.
  • Bank guarantees given for advances and retention are contingent liabilities/commitments to disclose; they are not contract balances.
  • EOT pending: an LD constraint released only when the EOT letter is issued.
  • Unapproved claims: not revenue; costs relating to them are expensed unless they create an asset under 115.95.
  • Joint operations in consortiums: each partner's share of contract balances is per Ind AS 111 joint operation accounting.

Key takeaways

  • Mobilisation advances are contract liabilities, usually without an SFC because their purpose is mobilisation funding (115.62(c) reasoning).
  • Retention protects against non-performance — no SFC; classify as contract asset if conditional.
  • PVC and LDs are variable consideration — estimate and constrain; LDs reduce revenue, not create provisions.
  • Unapproved claims and variations are modifications — recognise only when enforceable.
  • Expected-loss contracts are provided under Ind AS 37 onerous contracts, not as negative revenue.
  • Present one net position per contract and disclose bank guarantees separately.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mobilisation advance and retention entries [L1]

On 1 June 2024, Rachna Infra receives a ₹24 crore interest-free mobilisation advance on a ₹240 crore metro contract, recoverable at 10% from RA bills. In FY 2024-25, RA bills of ₹60 crore are certified with 5% retention. Pass journal entries for the advance, billing, recovery and retention, and show the contract liability at 31 March 2025.

Q2. LD estimate and later waiver [L2]

A ₹300 crore EPC contract carries LDs of 0.5% per week of delay, capped at 10%. At 31 March 2025 (progress 70%), management expects a 10-week delay; an EOT application covering 6 weeks is pending and historically the authority grants 60–80% of EOT claims. On 15 August 2025 the authority grants the full 10-week EOT. (a) Compute the transaction price and revenue at 31 March 2025 under most likely amount vs expected value. (b) Account for the EOT grant in FY 2025-26. (c) Is the LD a provision?

💡 Hint: Consider whether the LD outcome is binary or a range when choosing the estimation method.

Q3. PVC on a highway contract [L2]

A contract includes a bitumen PVC: price adjustment = 0.15 × value of work done in the month × (current index − base index) ÷ base index. Base index 100. Work done: Q1 ₹20 crore at index 108; Q2 ₹30 crore at index 112; management forecasts index 120 for Q3–Q4 work of ₹50 crore. Compute PVC to be included in the transaction price at the end of Q2 and explain why forecast escalation is or is not constrained.

Q4. Loss contract and onerous provision [L3]

At 31 March 2025, a ₹150 crore fixed-price contract is 60% complete (cumulative cost ₹96 crore, cumulative revenue ₹90 crore at a previous estimate). Revised total cost is ₹170 crore. The contractor has ₹3 crore of capitalised mobilisation costs (115.95) and a dedicated batching plant with carrying amount ₹5 crore and recoverable amount ₹2 crore. Compute FY 2024-25 revenue, impairment and the onerous contract provision, and pass entries. Explain why the loss is not shown as negative revenue.
