Subtopic note

Over-Time Revenue Criteria under Ind AS 115.35

Scope: Over-time criteria (115.35): simultaneous receipt/consumption (SaaS), customer-controlled WIP (EPC on customer site), no alternative use + enforceable right to payment

Why it matters

Whether a PO is satisfied over time or at a point in time is the single biggest driver of revenue phasing. For an EPC contractor with a three-year project, it is the difference between recognising revenue every quarter and recognising nothing until commissioning. For a real estate developer, it decides whether revenue follows construction or possession. Auditors treat this as a key audit matter in construction, real estate and capital-goods companies; analysts adjust forecasts when an entity changes contract terms that flip the conclusion; FP&A teams must know which criterion supports the over-time pattern because a renegotiated termination clause can remove it.

Core notes

The structure of the test

Ind AS 115.32 says revenue is recognised over time if any one of the three criteria in 115.35 is met; otherwise the PO is satisfied at a point in time (115.38). The test is applied at contract inception, per PO, and is not revisited unless the contract is modified. There is no free choice: if a criterion is met, over-time recognition is mandatory; if none is met, POC is prohibited even if the industry historically used it.

Criterion Text (paraphrased) Typical fact pattern
115.35(a) Customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits as the entity performs SaaS, BPO, cleaning, security, payroll processing, transport of goods
115.35(b) Entity's performance creates or enhances an asset the customer controls as it is created or enhanced EPC on customer's land, refurbishment of a customer's plant, software development on client servers
115.35(c) Performance does not create an asset with an alternative use to the entity and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date Specialised equipment built to customer specification, customised pharma batches under contract manufacturing, some real estate units

Criterion (a): simultaneous receipt and consumption

The benefit is consumed as it is delivered so nothing accumulates that the customer could later "receive". The application guidance (B3–B4) gives a practical test: if another entity took over the remaining obligation, would it need to substantially re-perform the work completed to date? If not, criterion (a) is met. For SaaS, the customer accesses the hosted platform each day and the benefit of yesterday's access cannot be redelivered — so a subscription is a stand-ready or series obligation recognised ratably. In performing the hypothetical test, ignore contractual or practical barriers to transfer and assume the replacement entity does not have the benefit of any asset the entity currently controls (e.g., WIP held by the entity).

Criterion (a) does not fit work that produces a deliverable at the end — a consulting report delivered at the conclusion of an engagement, for instance, typically requires analysis under (c).

Criterion (b): customer-controlled asset

The question is who controls the WIP, applying the control definition in 115.33 (ability to direct use of and obtain substantially all remaining benefits). Indicators: the asset is on the customer's land; the customer owns materials as they are incorporated; the customer can change the specification mid-way; legal title to work-in-progress passes as it is performed; the customer can terminate and keep the WIP. Most infrastructure and industrial EPC contracts in India (NHAI, state PWD, refinery EPC for PSUs) meet (b) because construction occurs on land owned or leased by the customer and fixtures become the customer's property by accession.

The asset need not be tangible: software built within a customer's environment, or enhancements to a customer's licensed system, can meet (b).

Criterion (c): no alternative use + enforceable right to payment

Both limbs must hold.

No alternative use (115.36, B6–B8). An asset lacks alternative use if the entity is restricted contractually from readily directing it to another use, or practically limited from doing so (because redirecting would cause significant economic loss — rework costs or sale only at a significant discount). The assessment is at inception and is not updated later unless there is a modification. Contractual restrictions must be substantive: a clause the customer could not enforce, or that the entity could circumvent by swapping an identical unit, does not count. A standard flat in a tower is typically interchangeable at inception; a unit specifically identified in an allotment letter with a substantive restriction may lack alternative use.

Enforceable right to payment (115.37, B9–B13). At all times throughout the contract the entity must be entitled to an amount that at least compensates it for performance completed to date if the customer terminates for reasons other than the entity's failure to perform. "Compensates" means cost plus a reasonable margin — approximating the selling price of goods or services transferred to date — not mere cost recovery or forfeiture of a deposit. Consider contract terms and legislation or precedent: in India, RERA (Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016) and state rules governing refunds, court decisions on forfeiture under Sec 74 of the Indian Contract Act, and consumer forum practice can undermine contractual payment rights. A payment schedule is not the same as a right to payment; what matters is the entitlement on termination.

Illustration: three contracts, three conclusions

Contract (a) (b) (c) Result
₹2.4 crore 3-year SaaS subscription Yes n/a n/a Over time, ratable
₹450 crore highway EPC on NHAI land No Yes — Over time, cost-to-cost
₹18 crore customised press built in vendor's factory; termination clause pays cost + 12% margin for work done No No (vendor's premises, title on delivery) Yes Over time
Same press, termination clause refunds advance only No No No Point in time, on transfer of control
Residential flat, buyer can cancel and get refund less 10% earnest money No No No (right to payment fails) Point in time, on possession

For the press with a cost-plus-margin termination right, if costs to date at 31 March are ₹9 crore of an estimated ₹15 crore, progress is 60% and revenue is \(0.60 \times 18 = ₹10.8\) crore. Without the right to payment, revenue is nil until delivery, and the ₹9 crore sits in inventory (WIP) under Ind AS 2.

Interaction with Steps 2 and 4

The criteria are applied per PO. A contract with a distinct design PO and a distinct supply PO may have one over-time and one point-in-time component. Series POs (115.22(b)) are, by definition, satisfied over time and use a single progress measure.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Partial right to payment: a right to payment that kicks in only after a milestone, or that is capped below cost plus margin during early phases, fails 115.37 because it must exist "at all times".
  • Real estate under RERA: if the agreement for sale gives the developer a right to the full consideration once construction proceeds and the buyer cannot cancel without paying it (some court-tested forms in specific states), over-time may be supportable; most standard Indian agreements fail.
  • Contract manufacturing: CDMOs producing customer-specific APIs often meet (c) — the product has no alternative use due to regulatory dossiers and the supply agreement pays cost plus margin for batches in progress.
  • Alternative use for standard inventory: if the entity could deliver a different identical unit, the asset has alternative use even if the specific unit is earmarked.
  • Stand-ready obligations (unlimited support, access to updates) normally meet (a).
  • Assessment frozen at inception: subsequent events that make an asset unsaleable elsewhere do not convert a point-in-time PO to over time.

Key takeaways

  • Any one of three criteria triggers mandatory over-time recognition; none means point in time.
  • Criterion (a) uses the "would another entity need to re-perform?" test — SaaS and routine services pass.
  • Criterion (b) depends on who controls WIP — construction on customer land typically passes.
  • Criterion (c) requires both no alternative use and an enforceable right to cost plus a reasonable margin at all times on termination.
  • Legal enforceability (RERA, Contract Act Sec 74, precedent) matters as much as contract wording.
  • Conclusions are per PO and fixed at inception unless the contract is modified.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying five Indian contracts [L1]

For each, identify which 115.35 criterion (if any) is met: (i) annual payroll-processing outsourcing contract with a fixed monthly fee; (ii) construction of a metro depot on DMRC land; (iii) a 40-page market-entry strategy report delivered at the end of an 8-week engagement, fee payable only on delivery; (iv) manufacture of 10,000 standard electric meters for a DISCOM; (v) development of a bespoke ERP module hosted on the client's servers, client owning code as written.

Q2. Right to payment in a customised equipment contract [L2]

Bharat Turbines Ltd agrees on 1 October 2024 to build a customised steam turbine for ₹30 crore, delivery in 18 months. Estimated cost ₹24 crore. The turbine is designed to the customer's plant layout; redesign for another customer would cost ₹9 crore. On termination for convenience, the customer must pay costs incurred plus 8% margin. Costs incurred to 31 March 2025: ₹6 crore. (a) Assess each limb of 115.35(c). (b) Compute FY 2024-25 revenue if over time applies, and the inventory balance if it does not. (c) How would the answer change if the termination clause paid "costs incurred only"?

💡 Hint: Compare the contractual termination margin with the contract's expected overall margin to decide whether it is "reasonable".

Q3. Real estate developer under RERA [L3]

Skyline Homes Ltd (NSE-listed, Pune) sells under-construction flats. Agreements: buyer pays 10% on booking and the rest on construction-linked milestones; buyer may cancel any time with refund of all amounts paid less 10% of the agreement value; developer may not reallocate the flat without the buyer's consent. The CFO argues POC is appropriate because "the flat is specifically identified and cannot be sold to anyone else". Write a memo evaluating each criterion, the effect of RERA and consumer forum precedent, and the presentation of costs incurred to date if point-in-time recognition is required.

Q4. Interview: SaaS vs on-premise licence [L1]

An interviewer asks: "Why is a SaaS subscription recognised over time but a perpetual on-premise licence often at a point in time, even though both give the customer the software?" Prepare a 150-word answer referencing 115.35(a) and the licensing guidance.
