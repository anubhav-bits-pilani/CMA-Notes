Point-in-Time Control Indicators and Incoterms for Exports

Scope: Point-in-time control indicators (115.38): right to payment, legal title, physical possession, risks and rewards, acceptance; Incoterms for exports

Why it matters

Most manufacturing, trading, FMCG and pharma revenue in India is point-in-time. The audit risk is cut-off: goods dispatched on 30–31 March, bill-and-hold arrangements, consignment stock and export shipments in transit at year-end. A one-day shift in the transfer date can move crores of revenue between financial years and between quarters for SEBI Reg 33 results. Under AS 9 the test was transfer of "significant risks and rewards"; under Ind AS 115 that is only one of five indicators of control, and Incoterms must be read as evidence of control transfer, not as an automatic answer.

Core notes

Control definition

Control is the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all of the remaining benefits from, the asset — including the ability to prevent others from doing so (115.33). For a PO not satisfied over time, revenue is recognised when the customer obtains control. Ind AS 115.38 lists indicators; none is individually determinative, and not all need to be present.

The five indicators (115.38(a)–(e))

Indicator What it means Practical evidence
(a) Present right to payment Customer is presently obliged to pay Invoice raised, credit period starts, no further performance conditions
(b) Legal title Customer has legal title (retention of title solely to protect against non-payment does not prevent control passing) Sale of Goods Act 1930 Sec 19–23, contract terms, e-way bill/consignee
(c) Physical possession Customer has the asset (but not in consignment, repo or bill-and-hold arrangements where possession and control differ) Proof of delivery, GRN, LR/airway bill
(d) Significant risks and rewards of ownership Customer bears loss risk and benefits from price movements Insurance responsibility, Incoterms risk point; excludes separate POs such as maintenance
(e) Acceptance Customer has accepted the asset Signed acceptance certificate, or acceptance is a formality (B83–B86)

Customer acceptance (B83–B86). If the entity can objectively determine that the goods meet agreed specifications (weight, dimensions, standard tests), acceptance is a formality and revenue can be recognised before formal acceptance. If acceptance depends on subjective criteria or on performance in the customer's environment that cannot be pre-tested, control does not transfer until acceptance. Trial or evaluation goods: control does not pass until the customer accepts or the trial period lapses.

Retention of title. Many Indian sale contracts retain title until payment. Where this is purely protective (like a security interest), control can still pass on delivery.

Arrangements where possession ≠ control

  • Consignment (B77–B78): goods with dealers who can return unsold stock and have no obligation to pay until sale — control remains with the consignor; revenue on sale by the dealer to the end customer.
  • Bill-and-hold (B79–B82): revenue before physical delivery only if all are met: the arrangement has a substantive reason (customer request, lack of space), the goods are separately identified as the customer's, ready for physical transfer, and the entity cannot use or redirect them. The entity also considers whether custodial services are a separate PO.
  • Repurchase agreements (B64–B76): a forward or call option to repurchase means control has not passed — lease or financing accounting instead. A put option at a price below original selling price with significant economic incentive to exercise is a lease; otherwise a sale with right of return.
  • Sale with right of return (B20–B27): control passes, but revenue excludes expected returns (refund liability and a right-to-recover asset).

Incoterms 2020 for exports

Incoterms define where risk of loss passes and who pays freight and insurance. They are strong evidence for indicator (d) and often (c), but control must still be assessed holistically. The key Incoterms:

Incoterm Risk passes to buyer Seller arranges/pays main carriage Usual control-transfer point
EXW At seller's premises when goods are placed at buyer's disposal No On availability at premises
FCA On delivery to buyer's carrier No Handover to carrier
FOB (sea) Once goods are on board the vessel at port of shipment No On board (bill of lading date)
CFR / CIF (sea) Once on board at port of shipment Yes (CIF also insurance) On board — shipping and insurance after that point may be a separate PO
CPT / CIP On delivery to first carrier Yes Handover to first carrier
DAP / DPU At named destination (DPU: after unloading) Yes Arrival at destination
DDP At destination, duty paid Yes, incl. import duty Arrival at destination

Shipping after control transfer. Under CIF/CFR, control usually passes at loading, while the seller still has an obligation to arrange freight (and insurance under CIF). That obligation is a service provided after control has passed and may be a separate PO, recognised as the voyage progresses. Ind AS 115 contains no US GAAP-style policy election to treat shipping as a fulfilment activity (ASC 606-10-25-18B), so Indian exporters must assess materiality; many conclude freight is immaterial and disclose that judgement.

Practical overrides. If the contract says FOB but the seller in practice replaces goods lost at sea and the customer pays only on arrival, the substance may be that control (and risk) transfer on arrival. Revenue is then deferred until delivery.

Illustration: 31 March cut-off

Kaveri Textiles Ltd exports fabric:

Shipment Value Incoterm Date on board Date of arrival FY 2024-25 revenue?
A to UK ₹2.0 crore FOB Mundra 29 Mar 2025 22 Apr 2025 Yes — on board 29 March
B to Germany ₹1.5 crore (incl. freight ₹6 lakh) CIF Hamburg 30 Mar 2025 25 Apr 2025 Goods ₹1.44 crore yes; freight PO recognised as voyage progresses (assess materiality)
C to UAE ₹0.8 crore DAP Jebel Ali 27 Mar 2025 5 Apr 2025 No — FY 2025-26

Domestic dispatches on 31 March under "ex-works, buyer's transport" are revenue on 31 March; dispatches "delivered to buyer's warehouse" with transit of 3 days are revenue in April. Shipping documents (shipping bill, bill of lading, e-way bill, LR, POD) are the audit trail.

GST and Ind AS timing differences

GST time of supply (Sec 12 CGST Act — earlier of invoice or due date of invoice) may fall in March while Ind AS revenue is in April. The GST output liability is recognised when it arises; the receivable or contract liability position follows the invoice terms. Do not drive Ind AS revenue from the GSTR-1 register.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Right of return with full refund within 30 days: control passes on delivery; revenue reduced by expected returns based on history.
  • Installation required: if installation is not distinct (complex), the combined PO may transfer only on installation; if distinct (simple, others can install), equipment revenue on delivery.
  • Delivery to customer's nominated warehouse under a third-party logistics contract: track contract wording, not who pays the freight.
  • High-sea sales and merchant trade: title transfers via endorsement of bill of lading; control on endorsement.
  • Quarter-end channel stuffing: dispatch spikes followed by April credit notes indicate that variable consideration (returns) or control transfer were misjudged.

Key takeaways

  • Point-in-time revenue arises when control passes; 115.38 indicators are evidence, weighted holistically.
  • Risks and rewards is one indicator among five — not the test itself, unlike AS 9.
  • Retention of title for credit protection does not stop control passing.
  • Consignment, bill-and-hold and repurchase arrangements break the link between possession and control.
  • Incoterms fix the risk point; under CIF/CFR, freight after loading may be a separate PO.
  • Year-end cut-off testing relies on bills of lading, PODs and shipping bills, not GST invoices.

Practice Questions

Q1. Matching Incoterms to the transfer date [L1]

Arun Exports ships four consignments of ₹50 lakh each on 30 March 2025, arriving at destination on 18 April 2025: (i) EXW Ludhiana, buyer's agent collects 30 March; (ii) FOB Nhava Sheva, loaded 30 March; (iii) CIP Frankfurt airport, handed to airline 30 March; (iv) DDP buyer's warehouse, Paris. State the FY in which revenue is recognised and the evidence you would inspect.

Q2. Bill-and-hold for a solar EPC buyer [L2]

On 25 March 2025 Surya Modules Ltd completes 2 MW of panels (₹6 crore) for a customer whose site is not ready. At the customer's written request, panels are packed, tagged with the customer's name and stored in a segregated bay; invoice is raised on 31 March with 60-day credit; Surya cannot ship them to other customers; storage until 30 June is free. Evaluate the B79–B82 criteria, decide whether revenue is recognised in FY 2024-25, and consider whether storage is a separate PO requiring allocation of the transaction price.

💡 Hint: Check every bill-and-hold criterion, then ask whether custodial service is material enough to carry a portion of the price.

Q3. Consignment vs sale-or-return in FMCG distribution [L2]

NutriFoods Ltd sends ₹12 crore of stock to super-stockists in March 2025. Contract X (60% of stock): stockist pays within 45 days regardless of onward sale; can return only expired goods (historic 1.5%). Contract Y (40%): stockist pays only when it sells to retailers; unsold stock can be returned at any time. Determine FY 2024-25 revenue, the refund liability and the right-to-recover asset (gross margin 30%), and the indicators supporting each conclusion.

Q4. Acceptance clause in capital equipment [L3]

A machine-tool manufacturer delivers a ₹4 crore CNC machine on 20 March 2025. Acceptance requires the machine to achieve a cycle time of 45 seconds on the customer's production line using the customer's raw material, tested within 30 days of installation; installation is simple and performed by the manufacturer's engineers on 28 March. Acceptance tests are completed on 12 April 2025. Analyse whether acceptance is a formality, identify the POs, and determine FY 2024-25 revenue. What changes if the same cycle-time test were performed and passed at the manufacturer's factory before dispatch?
