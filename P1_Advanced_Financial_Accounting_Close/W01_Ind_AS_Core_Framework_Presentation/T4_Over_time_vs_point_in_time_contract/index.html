Roadmap topic: Ind AS 115 Over-Time vs Point-in-Time Revenue: Input/output methods, unbilled revenue (contract assets), unearned revenue (contract liabilities), and principal vs agent analysis

Overview

Step 5 of the Ind AS 115 model answers two questions for every performance obligation (PO) identified in Steps 1–4: when does control transfer, and how fast. If any one of the three criteria in Ind AS 115.35 is met, revenue is recognised over time by measuring progress; otherwise it is recognised at a point in time, when the control indicators in 115.38 point to transfer. The answer is a contract-level, PO-level judgement — not an industry convention. A real estate developer, an EPC contractor and a SaaS provider can each land on either side depending on contract terms, especially the enforceability of a right to payment for performance completed to date.

Once timing is fixed, the second half of the topic is the balance sheet. Revenue recognised and amounts billed almost never move in lockstep. The gap creates a contract asset (performance ahead of an unconditional right to bill), a trade receivable (an unconditional right, only the passage of time remaining) or a contract liability (cash received or due ahead of performance). Ind AS 115.105–107 require a net position per contract, not per customer or per portfolio. These balances drive working-capital movements in the cash flow statement (Topic 6), ECL under Ind AS 109 and the 115.116–118 roll-forward disclosure — which is why FP&A teams model them monthly.

The third strand is gross versus net. Platform businesses — marketplaces, online travel agents, food-delivery aggregators — must decide whether they control the specified good or service before it transfers to the end customer (principal, gross revenue) or merely arrange for another party to supply it (agent, net commission). The same strand includes IP licences, where the nature of the licence (right to access vs right to use) determines over-time vs point-in-time, with a special exception for sales- and usage-based royalties.

Learning sequence: start with the over-time criteria (s01) and the point-in-time indicators (s02); then learn how to measure progress (s03); then translate progress and billing into contract balances (s04); apply all of this to EPC contracts with advances, retention, PVC and LDs (s05); then handle the special cases — principal vs agent (s06) and licences (s07); finish with the disclosures the model must produce (s08). The concept notes give the formulas and entries you will hard-code into the Week 1 revenue waterfall.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 115 vs legacy AS 7/AS 9 and real estate practice

Ind AS 115 vs legacy AS 7/AS 9 percentage-of-completion; real estate developers (DLF, Godrej Properties) largely recognise on possession, not POC

Under Indian GAAP, AS 7 mandated percentage-of-completion (POC) for construction contracts and the ICAI Guidance Note on Real Estate Transactions allowed POC once thresholds were crossed. AS 9 used a risks-and-rewards test for sales. Ind AS 115 replaced both with a control model: POC is only allowed if a 115.35 criterion is met. Most Indian residential developers sell under agreements where the buyer cannot direct the unit and the developer's right to payment on cancellation is limited (forfeiture of earnest money, RERA refund obligations), so 115.35(c) fails and revenue is recognised at handover/possession. On Ind AS transition in FY 2018-19, developers such as DLF and Godrej Properties took large opening retained earnings adjustments reversing previously recognised POC revenue. Analysts therefore track bookings and collections, not reported revenue, as the lead indicator.

Ind AS 109 ECL on contract assets and onerous contracts

Ind AS 109 ECL on contract assets → Week 3 (w3_t2); onerous contracts → Week 2 (w2_t4)

Contract assets and trade receivables are both in the scope of Ind AS 109 impairment (Ind AS 115.107–108). Entities typically use the simplified approach (lifetime ECL from day one) with a provision matrix built on ageing buckets; contract assets often get their own bucket because they carry performance risk in addition to credit risk. Impairment losses on contract balances are presented separately from other impairment losses where material (115.113(b)). Separately, when total contract costs are expected to exceed the transaction price, the contract is onerous under Ind AS 37 — the unavoidable costs (incremental costs plus an allocation of other directly related costs, per the 2022 Ind AS 37 amendment) exceed the economic benefits. Week 2 covers the provision mechanics; Week 3 covers ECL staging and provision matrices.

Excel monthly revenue waterfall

Excel monthly revenue waterfall: contract rows × EOMONTH date headers, SUMIFS roll-forwards, check rows tying billing and cash

The standard build puts one row per contract (or per PO) and one column per month, with headers generated by =EOMONTH(start,0) and =EOMONTH(prior,1). Separate blocks compute cumulative % complete, cumulative revenue, period revenue (cumulative minus prior cumulative), cumulative billing and cumulative cash. The contract asset/liability for each row is MAX(cumRev − cumBill,0) and MAX(cumBill − cumRev,0) — computed per row before any aggregation, so assets and liabilities are never netted across contracts. Roll-forwards use SUMIFS by contract type or segment. Check rows confirm cumulative revenue ≤ transaction price, closing balance = opening + revenue − billing, and receivable = billing − cash.

Contract balances in the cash flow statement

→ w1_t6: contract assets/liabilities as working-capital movements in CFO

Revenue recognised but unbilled has not generated cash; advances received have generated cash before revenue. In the indirect-method cash flow, an increase in contract assets is a deduction from operating cash flow and an increase in contract liabilities is an addition, exactly like receivables and payables. Retention receivable and mobilisation advances follow the same logic. A model that produces contract asset and liability balances per period therefore feeds the ΔWC lines in CFO directly. Topic 6 covers the sign rules and the exclusion of acquired balances arising on business combinations.

US GAAP ASC 606 and SEC focus on gross vs net

US GAAP ASC 606 same model; SEC staff focus on gross vs net in platform businesses

ASC 606 and IFRS 15/Ind AS 115 share the same over-time criteria, progress measures and principal-agent indicators, with the 2016 clarifications adopted by both boards. SEC staff comment letters repeatedly challenge gross presentation by platform and marketplace businesses (ride-hailing, online travel, ad-tech, e-commerce), asking registrants to identify the specified good or service and demonstrate control before transfer rather than relying on indicators such as credit risk. Indian unicorns preparing for NSE listings face the same questions from SEBI-registered merchant bankers and auditors, and peer comparisons of "GMV" versus "revenue from operations" need care.

Common pitfalls

Netting contract assets and liabilities across different contracts

Mistake: presenting a single net "unbilled revenue" balance per customer or for the entity, offsetting contract A's unbilled revenue against contract B's advance. Why it happens: Tally/ERP ledgers are often maintained per customer, and netting flatters both sides of working capital. Correct treatment: Ind AS 115.105 requires one net position per contract (after applying contract combination rules in 115.17); across contracts, contract assets and liabilities are presented gross, and Ind AS 1.32 prohibits offsetting. Detection: extract contract-wise balances from the revenue schedule; any customer ledger that has both a debit-balance and a credit-balance contract but shows one figure on the balance sheet is a red flag.

Including procured-but-uninstalled materials in cost-to-cost progress

Mistake: treating cement, steel, turbines or panels delivered to site as "cost incurred" in the cost-to-cost numerator. Why it happens: the cost has hit the books and the project accounting system counts it. Correct treatment: B19 says costs that do not depict progress are excluded; for uninstalled materials that are not distinct, are significant relative to the total, and where the entity is not involved in designing or manufacturing them, recognise revenue only to the extent of cost (zero margin) when the customer obtains control. Detection: compare cost-to-date with physical progress surveys; a spike in % complete coinciding with large procurement receipts indicates front-loading.

Presenting milestone-conditional unbilled revenue as trade receivables

Mistake: classifying unbilled revenue as "trade receivables — unbilled" when billing depends on achieving a milestone or certification. Why it happens: Schedule III's "unbilled" sub-caption under receivables and old AS habits. Correct treatment: a receivable exists only when the right to consideration is unconditional (115.108) — i.e., only the passage of time is required. If the entity must complete further performance (a milestone, an engineer's certificate), the balance is a contract asset presented under "other current assets" (or other financial assets as per company policy disclosed in line with ICAI guidance). Unbilled amounts that are unconditional (e.g., time-and-material work invoiced next month) can be receivables. Detection: sample unbilled balances and read the billing clause.

Grossing up marketplace revenue where the platform never controls the goods

Mistake: reporting gross merchandise value or full ticket value as revenue. Why it happens: bigger topline for valuation narratives; the platform collects the full cash and bears some credit risk. Correct treatment: under B34–B38, if the platform does not control the specified good before transfer — no inventory risk, the seller sets price, the seller is primarily responsible for fulfilment — it is an agent and recognises only its commission/fee. Collecting cash and bearing credit risk are not control indicators after the 2016 clarifications. Detection: read seller agreements and returns policy; compare revenue to GMV and to payouts to sellers.

Practice Questions

Q1. EPC contract waterfall with variation, advance and retention [L3]

Shakti Infra Ltd signs a ₹120 crore fixed-price contract on 1 April 2024 to build a water treatment plant on the customer's land (customer controls WIP). Original estimated cost ₹100 crore. Terms: mobilisation advance 10% received on 15 April 2024, recovered pro rata from each running bill; running bills monthly on engineer-certified progress; 5% retention deducted from each bill, released 12 months after completion. Costs incurred: FY 2024-25 ₹40 crore (including ₹6 crore of imported membranes delivered to site but not installed, procured from a third-party supplier, not customised by Shakti); FY 2025-26 ₹45 crore. At 31 March 2025 estimated total cost is ₹100 crore; at 31 March 2026 it is revised to ₹105 crore and an approved variation adds ₹8 crore to the price (not distinct). Certified billing (gross, before advance recovery and retention): FY 2024-25 ₹36 crore; FY 2025-26 ₹55 crore. Cash received equals net bills less 30 days' outstanding of ₹4 crore at each year-end. (a) Compute revenue for each year applying B19. (b) Compute contract asset/liability, trade receivable and retention balances at each year-end. (c) Pass summary journal entries for FY 2025-26. (d) State how the FY 2025-26 estimate revision is accounted for under Ind AS 8.

💡 Hint: Exclude the membranes from both numerator and denominator for the margin calculation, then recognise revenue equal to their cost.

Q2. Platform business — gross or net and balance sheet effects [L3]

QuickBasket Pvt Ltd runs a quick-commerce app. Model A: third-party sellers list goods, set prices, hold stock in their own dark stores; QuickBasket collects payment, charges sellers 18% commission and a ₹25 delivery fee to customers, and refunds customers for damaged goods (recovering from sellers). Model B: QuickBasket buys private-label goods outright, stores them in its own warehouses and sells at prices it sets. FY 2024-25 GMV: Model A ₹500 crore, Model B ₹120 crore (COGS ₹96 crore); 40 lakh Model A orders. (a) Determine principal/agent for each model citing B37 indicators. (b) Compute revenue from operations. (c) Explain how payables to sellers and customer wallet balances are classified. (d) Draft the disaggregation note.

Q3. SaaS + implementation — timing, progress and RPO disclosure [L2]

On 1 July 2024, CloudLedger Ltd signs a 3-year SaaS contract for ₹2.4 crore with a ₹30 lakh implementation fee. Implementation is not distinct (significant configuration and integration); it takes 3 months and the SaaS go-live is 1 October 2024. Billing: implementation fee on signing; subscription annually in advance on 1 October. (a) Determine the PO structure and the period over which the single combined PO is satisfied. (b) Compute revenue, contract asset/liability and receivable at 31 March 2025. (c) Compute the remaining performance obligations disclosure at 31 March 2025 and the time bands. (d) Can the 115.121 practical expedient be used?

💡 Hint: If implementation is not distinct, consider when the customer starts to benefit from the combined service.

Q4. Export sale under Incoterms and a licence with royalties [L2]

PharmaGlide Ltd (a) ships ₹3 crore of formulations to a US distributor under CIF New York on 25 March 2025; vessel arrives 20 April 2025; (b) ships ₹1.2 crore to a UAE buyer under DAP Dubai on 28 March 2025, delivered 6 April 2025; (c) grants a 5-year licence of a patented formulation to a Brazilian firm for an upfront ₹5 crore plus 6% royalty on the licensee's sales, with no ongoing activities by PharmaGlide affecting the IP. Licensee sales January–March 2025 are ₹10 crore. Determine FY 2024-25 revenue for each and whether shipping under CIF is a separate PO.
