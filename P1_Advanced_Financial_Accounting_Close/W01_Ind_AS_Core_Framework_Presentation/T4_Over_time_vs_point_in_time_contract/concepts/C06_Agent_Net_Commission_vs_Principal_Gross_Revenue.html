Key concept

Agent Net Commission vs Principal Gross Revenue

Concept: Agent recognises net commission/fee; principal recognises gross revenue and cost of sales

Statement

Following the principal-versus-agent analysis (Ind AS 115.B34–B38):

  • A principal controls the specified good or service before it is transferred to the customer and recognises revenue in the gross amount of consideration to which it expects to be entitled (B35B). Amounts paid to suppliers are cost of sales or other operating expenses.
  • An agent arranges for the specified good or service to be provided by another party and recognises revenue in the amount of any fee or commission to which it expects to be entitled — which may be the net amount retained after paying the other party (B36).

\[ \text{Revenue}_{\text{agent}} = \text{Gross consideration collected} - \text{Amount payable to principal} \]

\[ \text{Revenue}_{\text{principal}} = \text{Gross consideration}; \qquad \text{Gross profit} = \text{Gross consideration} - \text{Cost of goods/services from supplier} \]

Explanation

The two presentations produce the same profit but very different revenue, gross margin and asset/liability profiles. Timing can also differ: an agent's PO is satisfied when it has arranged the transaction (e.g., at booking), while a principal recognises revenue when the underlying good or service is transferred to the customer (e.g., on travel date).

Balance sheet consequences:

  • A principal recognises receivables for the gross amount and payables to suppliers; inventory if it holds stock.
  • An agent holding customer collections recognises a financial liability (payable to principals/sellers) for amounts it must remit, and its own receivable/commission income; cash collected on behalf of principals is still the agent's cash if held in its own account (with corresponding payables), unless it is a separate trust/escrow account (e.g., nodal accounts under RBI payment aggregator guidelines) — check the legal structure.

Taxes collected on behalf of government (GST, TCS under Sec 52 CGST Act or Sec 206C(1H) of the Income-tax Act, where applicable) are excluded from revenue under both models.

Common error patterns: presenting GMV as revenue, presenting commissions net of the platform's own costs (e.g., delivery partner payouts) — the agent's own costs remain expenses, they are not netted against commission — and netting customer discounts funded by the agent against seller payables instead of evaluating them as consideration payable to a customer (115.70).

Illustration

EventHub Ltd sells concert tickets. Organisers set prices; EventHub collects the ₹2,000 ticket price, keeps 10% commission and charges customers a ₹100 convenience fee; it pays ₹15 per ticket to a payment gateway. 1 lakh tickets sold in March 2025 for events in April–May 2025.

Line Principal (if it had bought tickets at risk) Agent (actual)
Revenue ₹21.00 crore (₹2,100 × 1 lakh), on event dates ₹3.00 crore (commission ₹2.00 crore + fee ₹1.00 crore), at sale
Cost of sales ₹18.00 crore (ticket cost) —
Payment gateway charges ₹0.15 crore ₹0.15 crore (expense, not netted)
Payable to organisers at 31 March — ₹18.00 crore
Profit ₹2.85 crore ₹2.85 crore

Timing of the agent's revenue depends on the agent's PO being satisfied at sale; if refund obligations for cancelled events fall on EventHub, it estimates a refund liability against its commission.

Connections

Applies the analysis in s06; affects disaggregation and the RPO disclosure (s08). Gross vs net changes revenue-based KPIs used in FP&A (take rate = net revenue ÷ GMV) and valuation multiples (EV/Revenue) — always compare peers on the same basis. Payables to sellers held by an agent influence working-capital in the cash flow (Topic 6). Related to the US GAAP/SEC focus on gross vs net in platform businesses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute revenue and gross margin both ways [L1]

A B2B platform facilitated ₹300 crore of steel sales; sellers set prices and ship directly; the platform charges 2.5% commission and pays ₹1.2 crore to logistics partners it engages for its separate delivery service (billed to buyers at ₹1.8 crore). Compute revenue and gross margin under the correct (agent) conclusion and under an incorrect gross presentation.

Q2. Timing difference for an agent [L2]

An insurance web-aggregator earns 15% commission on first-year premiums of policies sold in March 2025 (₹40 crore premiums), with clawback of commission if a policy lapses within 90 days (historic lapse rate 6%). Determine revenue for FY 2024-25 and any refund liability.

💡 Hint: The agent's PO is arranging the policy; the clawback is variable consideration.

Q3. Valuation interview [L2]

Two listed peers trade at EV/Revenue of 6x and 1.5x. One reports gross (inventory model); the other net (marketplace). Explain how you would normalise the multiples and which metric (GMV, net revenue, contribution margin) you would use for comparison.
