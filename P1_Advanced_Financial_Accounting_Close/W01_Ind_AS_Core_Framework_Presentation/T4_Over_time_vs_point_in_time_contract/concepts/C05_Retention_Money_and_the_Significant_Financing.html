Key concept

Retention Money and the Significant Financing Component

Concept: Retention money is usually not an SFC — it protects against non-performance (115.62(c))

Statement

Ind AS 115.62 lists circumstances in which a contract does not have a significant financing component (SFC). Under 115.62(c), there is no SFC where:

the difference between the promised consideration and the cash selling price arises for reasons other than the provision of finance to either the customer or the entity, and the difference is proportional to the reason for the difference.

Retention money — a percentage of each progress bill withheld by the customer until completion or the end of the defects liability period — is the standard example (Ind AS 115 IE Example 27 illustrates a construction contract with retention). The withholding protects the customer from the contractor failing to complete its obligations; it is not a loan by the contractor to the customer. Therefore the retained amount is not discounted and no interest income is imputed.

Explanation

The SFC test (115.60–61) asks whether payment timing gives either party a significant benefit of financing. Factors: the difference between promised consideration and cash selling price, the length of time between transfer and payment, and prevailing interest rates. Retention can last 12–24 months beyond completion, so on time alone it might seem to involve financing. 115.62(c) overrides this when the timing has a non-financing purpose and the size of the difference is proportionate to that purpose.

Conditions to verify before concluding "no SFC":

  • the retention percentage is commercially standard (5–10%) and tied to performance security, not to an implicit discount;
  • the retention is released on completion, certification or expiry of the DLP — i.e., it is linked to performance;
  • the contractor could not obtain a higher price by accepting no retention (or, if it could, the difference is proportionate to the protection).

If a retention is unusually large (e.g., 30% held for five years) or replaceable by a bank guarantee at negligible cost but the contractor chooses not to, consider whether part of it is financing.

Other consequences:

  • Retention is still subject to Ind AS 109 ECL — long-dated retentions from financially weak customers can carry material credit loss.
  • Presentation: a contract asset if release depends on further performance; a receivable if release is purely time-based. Non-current portion classified using the operating cycle test.
  • The same 115.62(c) logic is applied by many entities to mobilisation advances (purpose: funding mobilisation), though this needs case-specific support.
  • The practical expedient in 115.63 (≤1 year) is separate; retention often exceeds one year, so 115.62(c) — not 115.63 — is the basis for not discounting.

Illustration

A ₹100 crore water-supply contract with a state jal board: 5% retention from each RA bill, released 24 months after completion upon defects certification. Total retention at completion: ₹5 crore. Contractor's incremental borrowing rate: 10%.

Approach Revenue Finance income Retention asset at completion
Treat as SFC (incorrect for standard retention) \(100 - 5 + \frac{5}{1.10^2} = 99.13\) ₹0.87 crore over 2 years ₹4.13 crore, accreting
Apply 115.62(c) (correct) 100 Nil ₹5 crore, less ECL

If the customer is a state entity with a history of delayed releases and 15% of retention historically unrecovered, the ECL allowance (Dr Impairment loss, Cr Loss allowance) reduces the carrying amount — a credit-risk adjustment, not financing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Applying 115.62(c) [L1]

For each, state whether an SFC is likely: (i) 5% retention released on completion of a 12-month DLP; (ii) 25% of contract price payable 4 years after completion with no performance conditions, where the price is 20% above the cash price; (iii) 10% retention replaceable by a bank guarantee costing 1% per annum.

Q2. Retention with ECL [L2]

Retention receivable from a municipal corporation at 31 March 2025 is ₹12 crore, due 30 September 2026 subject to defects certification. Historical data show 8% of such retentions are never recovered and recoveries average 9 months late. Pass the entries for ECL, state the presentation (current/non-current, contract asset/receivable) and explain why the time value is not imputed as an SFC.

💡 Hint: Separate the financing question (115.62(c)) from the credit-loss question (Ind AS 109).

Q3. Interview — mobilisation advance by analogy [L2]

A reviewer argues that the 115.62(c) logic for retention also means a 10% interest-free mobilisation advance received 18 months before most of the work is performed has no SFC. Present arguments for and against and state the evidence you would collect.
