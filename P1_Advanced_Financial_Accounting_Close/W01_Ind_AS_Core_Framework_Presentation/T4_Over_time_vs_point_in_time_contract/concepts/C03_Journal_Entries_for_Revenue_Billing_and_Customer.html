Key concept

Journal Entries for Revenue, Billing and Customer Advances

Concept: Entries: Dr Contract asset Cr Revenue; on billing Dr Trade receivable Cr Contract asset; on advance Dr Bank Cr Contract liability

Statement

The three core entries under Ind AS 115 contract accounting:

Event Dr Cr
Revenue recognised for performance before an unconditional right exists Contract asset Revenue from operations
Right becomes unconditional (invoice/certification) Trade receivable Contract asset
Advance received before performance Bank Contract liability

Supporting entries complete the cycle:

Event Dr Cr
Performance satisfied against an advance Contract liability Revenue from operations
Non-cancellable invoice in advance of performance Trade receivable Contract liability
Collection Bank Trade receivable
GST on invoice/advance Trade receivable / Bank GST output payable

Explanation

Revenue is driven by performance (Step 5); balances are driven by the relationship between performance and the right to consideration. When performance comes first, the conditional right is a contract asset, reclassified to a receivable on billing. When cash comes first, the obligation to perform is a contract liability, released as performance occurs. The entries keep revenue independent of invoicing — the core shift from Tally-style "revenue = sales invoice" bookkeeping.

In ERP practice, the "unbilled revenue" accrual is often booked at month-end and reversed on the first day of the next month, with invoices posting directly to revenue. That is acceptable as a bookkeeping technique only if the net effect equals the entries above; reviewers should test that cumulative revenue per contract equals % complete × price, not invoices raised.

Points to note:

  • Advances for services attract GST at receipt; GST payable is not part of the contract liability.
  • If the advance includes an SFC (115.60–65), accrue interest: Dr Finance cost, Cr Contract liability; revenue is then measured at the accreted amount.
  • Receivables and contract assets are subject to ECL: Dr Impairment loss, Cr Loss allowance.
  • Per-contract netting applies to presentation; ledger accounts can be kept gross per contract with a presentation adjustment.

Illustration

SaaS + implementation contract signed 1 January 2025: implementation (distinct) ₹30 lakh, recognised on effort; subscription ₹60 lakh per year from 1 February 2025. Customer pays ₹20 lakh advance on signing; implementation invoiced on completion (31 March 2025); subscription invoiced annually in advance on 1 February 2025 (non-cancellable), paid 28 February 2025. Implementation 100% complete at 31 March 2025.

Date Entry Dr (₹ lakh) Cr (₹ lakh)
1 Jan Bank / Contract liability (advance) 20 20
Jan–Mar Contract asset / Revenue (implementation effort) 30 30
1 Feb Trade receivable / Contract liability (subscription billed) 60 60
28 Feb Bank / Trade receivable 60 60
Feb–Mar Contract liability / Revenue (2 months subscription) 10 10
31 Mar Trade receivable / Contract asset (implementation invoiced) 30 30
31 Mar Contract liability / Trade receivable (advance adjusted against invoice) 20 20

Closing position for this single contract: receivable ₹10 lakh; contract liability ₹50 lakh (subscription unearned); contract asset nil. Revenue FY 2024-25 = ₹40 lakh. GST entries omitted for clarity.

Practice Questions

Q1. EPC month-end entries [L1]

In March 2025 an EPC contractor recognises ₹8 crore of revenue on progress, receives engineer certification for ₹6 crore of earlier work (gross), deducts ₹0.6 crore mobilisation recovery and ₹0.3 crore retention (conditional), and receives a ₹2 crore advance on a new contract. Pass the entries.

Q2. Reversing-accrual ERP design [L2]

An ERP posts every sales invoice to revenue and requires a manual month-end unbilled revenue accrual, reversed on day 1. Over January–March, invoices on a fixed-price contract were ₹45 lakh while progress-based revenue was ₹38 lakh. Show the entries needed at 31 March to state revenue correctly and present the resulting balance.

💡 Hint: Invoices exceeding performance create a contract liability, not negative unbilled revenue.

Q3. Advance with financing component [L2]

A customer pays ₹10 crore on 1 April 2024 for equipment to be delivered on 31 March 2026; the cash selling price on delivery would be ₹11.56 crore, implying 7.5% p.a. Pass entries for FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26, and explain why revenue exceeds cash received.
