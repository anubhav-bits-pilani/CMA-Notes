Key concept

Cost-to-Cost Percentage of Completion and Cumulative Catch-Up

Concept: Cost-to-cost % complete = Cumulative cost incurred ÷ Total estimated cost; Period revenue = (% × Transaction price) − Revenue recognised to date

Statement

For a performance obligation satisfied over time and measured using an input method based on costs:

\[ p_t = \frac{C_t}{E_t} \]

\[ R_t = p_t \times TP_t - \sum_{k<t} R_k \]

where \(p_t\) is cumulative percentage complete at reporting date \(t\); \(C_t\) is cumulative contract cost incurred to date that depicts progress (excluding B19 costs — inefficiencies and uninstalled materials); \(E_t\) is the current estimate of total contract costs on the same basis; \(TP_t\) is the current transaction price (including approved variations and constrained variable consideration); \(R_t\) is revenue recognised in period \(t\); and \(\sum_{k<t} R_k\) is revenue recognised in all prior periods.

Explanation

The formula is a cumulative catch-up: each period computes total revenue earned to date on the latest estimates, then subtracts what has already been booked. Any change in \(E_t\) or \(TP_t\) is therefore absorbed entirely in the current period — this is the Ind AS 8 change-in-estimate treatment (Ind AS 115.43 treats changes in progress measures as estimate changes, and 115.88 requires transaction price changes to be allocated on the same basis as at inception, recognised in the period of change for satisfied portions). Prior periods are never restated for revised cost estimates; restatement applies only to errors.

Conditions and exceptions:

  • The PO must qualify for over-time recognition under 115.35.
  • Costs in \(C_t\) must reflect transfer of control; exclude abnormal wastage, rework from the entity's own errors, mobilisation costs that do not transfer anything, and uninstalled materials meeting B19 (which earn revenue at cost, zero margin).
  • If the outcome cannot be reasonably measured, revenue is limited to recoverable costs (115.45).
  • If \(E_t > TP_t\), the formula still drives revenue, but the expected loss on the whole contract is provided as an Ind AS 37 onerous contract provision (see c04).
  • \(p_t\) is capped at 100%; in a model use MIN(1, …).

Illustration

Contract price ₹100 crore, 3 years.

Year 1 Year 2 Year 3
Cumulative cost (₹ crore) 24 60 84
Estimated total cost 80 84 84
Transaction price 100 100 100
% complete 30.0% 71.43% 100%
Cumulative revenue 30.00 71.43 100.00
Period revenue 30.00 41.43 28.57
Period cost 24 36 24
Period margin 6.00 5.43 4.57

The Year 2 cost revision from ₹80 crore to ₹84 crore lowers the overall margin from 20% to 16%. Year 1 margin of ₹6 crore (on the 20% estimate) is not restated; Year 2 absorbs the catch-up: cumulative margin at Year 2 is \(71.43 - 60 = 11.43\), of which ₹6 crore was booked in Year 1.

In Excel, with cumulative cost in row 10, estimated total cost in row 11, transaction price in row 12:

=MIN(1, D10/D11)
=D13*D12 - SUM($C14:C14)

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-year contract with price and cost changes [L2]

A ₹60 crore contract: Year 1 cumulative cost ₹15 crore, estimate ₹48 crore; Year 2 cumulative cost ₹33 crore, estimate ₹50 crore, approved variation adds ₹4 crore (not distinct); Year 3 completed at total cost ₹51 crore. Compute revenue and margin in each year.

Q2. Spotting the model error [L2]

A model computes period revenue as =D13*D12 - C13*C12 (current % × current price minus prior % × prior price). Explain when this produces the same result as the cumulative catch-up formula and when it does not, with a numerical example where the price changes in Year 2.

💡 Hint: Compare prior cumulative revenue actually booked with prior % multiplied by the prior period's price.

Q3. Interview — why not restate? [L1]

Explain in 100 words why a revised total-cost estimate is not a prior-period error and how Ind AS 8 and Ind AS 115 combine to produce the cumulative catch-up.
