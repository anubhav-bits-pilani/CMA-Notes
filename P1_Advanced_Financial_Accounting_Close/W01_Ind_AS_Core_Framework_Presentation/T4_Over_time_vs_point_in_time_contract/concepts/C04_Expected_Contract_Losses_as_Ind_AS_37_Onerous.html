Key concept

Expected Contract Losses as Ind AS 37 Onerous Provisions

Concept: Expected contract loss recognised immediately as Ind AS 37 onerous provision, not as negative revenue

Statement

When the unavoidable costs of meeting the obligations under a contract exceed the economic benefits expected to be received, the contract is onerous and the present obligation is recognised and measured as a provision (Ind AS 37.66):

\[ P = \min(\text{CF}, \text{CE}) - \text{Loss already reflected} \]

where \(\text{CF}\) is the expected loss on fulfilling the contract (remaining unavoidable costs of fulfilment less remaining consideration expected), \(\text{CE}\) is the cost of exiting (compensation or penalties for failure to fulfil), and the loss already reflected is any part of the overall loss already absorbed through cumulative revenue/cost recognition. Costs of fulfilling include incremental costs (direct labour, materials) and an allocation of other costs that relate directly to fulfilling contracts, such as depreciation of equipment used (Ind AS 37.68A, 2022 amendment). Revenue continues to be recognised under Ind AS 115 based on progress; the loss is presented in expenses.

Explanation

Ind AS 115 has no specific loss-contract guidance — unlike AS 7.35, which required an expected loss to be "recognised as an expense immediately" within construction contract accounting. Instead, Ind AS 115 relies on Ind AS 37. Revenue continues on progress at the transaction price; it is never reduced below what performance justifies merely because costs are high. The provision covers the remaining loss on unsatisfied performance.

Sequence of steps:

  1. Recognise impairment on assets dedicated to the contract first (Ind AS 37.69) — including capitalised costs to obtain/fulfil a contract under 115.101 and dedicated PPE.
  2. Measure the remaining expected loss on fulfilment, compare with exit cost, take the lower.
  3. Recognise the provision; unwind it as the contract proceeds (Dr Provision, Cr Cost of construction) so that later periods show costs net of utilisation, producing zero margin on remaining work.
  4. Reassess at each reporting date — estimate changes under Ind AS 8.

The level of assessment is the contract (or combined contracts under 115.17), not a portfolio. Expected future operating losses of a business are not provided (Ind AS 37.63) — only contract-specific losses.

Illustration

Fixed-price contract ₹200 crore. At 31 March 2025: cumulative cost ₹120 crore; revised total cost ₹220 crore (overall loss ₹20 crore); cumulative revenue booked in prior years ₹70 crore.

Item ₹ crore
% complete 120 ÷ 220 = 54.55%
Cumulative revenue 54.55% × 200 = 109.09
FY 2024-25 revenue 109.09 − 70 = 39.09
Cumulative cost 120
Loss reflected to date through progress 120 − 109.09 = 10.91
Remaining loss to provide 20 − 10.91 = 9.09
Entry Dr Cost of construction / Onerous provision expense 9.09, Cr Provision for onerous contract 9.09

Total loss recognised to date = ₹20 crore — the full expected loss — while revenue remains ₹109.09 crore. In FY 2025-26, as the remaining ₹100 crore of cost is incurred and ₹90.91 crore revenue recognised, ₹9.09 crore of provision is utilised, giving zero margin.

Practice Questions

Q1. Provision on a road contract [L2]

A ₹90 crore contract: at 31 March 2025 cumulative cost ₹45 crore, revised total cost ₹100 crore, cumulative revenue booked in FY 2023-24 ₹30 crore. Compute FY 2024-25 revenue, the onerous provision and the total loss recognised in FY 2024-25. Pass entries.

Q2. Exit cost lower than fulfilment loss [L2]

In Q1, the contract allows the contractor to terminate on paying ₹4 crore compensation plus forfeiting retention of ₹2 crore. Determine the provision and explain the role of management intent.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 37 uses the least net cost of exiting — compare both measures.

Q3. Allocated costs after the 2022 amendment [L3]

A contractor's remaining direct costs are ₹40 crore, remaining revenue ₹42 crore, and depreciation of a tunnel boring machine used exclusively on the contract for the remaining period is ₹5 crore. Before FY 2022-23, the company applied an incremental-cost approach. Determine whether the contract is onerous now, how the transition to the amendment was accounted for, and the disclosure needed.
