Roadmap topic: Ind AS 1 & MCA Schedule III (Division II): Current vs non-current classification rules, SOCIE construction, and material disclosure notes

Overview

Ind AS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements sets the general features of Ind AS financial statements (fair presentation, going concern, accrual, materiality and aggregation, offsetting, comparatives, consistency) and the minimum content of each statement. In India, the Companies Act 2013 overlays it with Schedule III Division II, which fixes the format and order of the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss and SOCIE for Ind AS companies other than NBFCs, and adds a large set of statutory disclosures. Together they convert a trial balance into a set of financial statements that every Indian investor, lender, regulator and XBRL parser expects in the same shape.

For the roles this curriculum targets — Ind AS technical accounting, financial reporting analysis, FP&A and financial modelling — this topic is the chassis on which everything else is mounted. Technical accountants defend current/non-current and OCI classifications to auditors; reporting analysts build the SOCIE and the 2021 disclosure tables from system data; FP&A teams reconcile management reporting to Schedule III captions; modellers need the balance sheet hierarchy and current/non-current flags to build working-capital and debt schedules that match published numbers. The Week 4 TB mapping engine is a direct implementation of this topic.

The learning sequence runs from classification to presentation to disclosure. Start with the current/non-current tests — the operating cycle limb for assets (s01, c01) and the right-to-defer test with refinancing and covenant rules for liabilities (s02, c02). Then learn the face statements: the Division II balance sheet hierarchy and the financial vs non-financial split (s03), the statement of profit and loss with its OCI split, tax on OCI and exceptional items (s04, c03), and the SOCIE in Schedule III columns (s05). Next, the disclosure layer: the 2021 amendments (s06) and material accounting policy information, judgements and estimation uncertainty (s07). Finish with housekeeping and scope: rounding, regrouping of comparatives and the three Divisions (s08). The cross-cutting principles — offsetting and going concern (c04), the third balance sheet (c05), materiality (c06) and the primacy of Ind AS over Schedule III (c07) — apply throughout.

Related concepts to study

IFRS 18 and the future of the P&L

IFRS 18 (effective 2027): operating/investing/financing P&L categories, mandatory subtotals, management-defined performance measures — track ICAI convergence

IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements, issued by the IASB in April 2024, replaces IAS 1 for IFRS reporters from 1 January 2027. It requires income and expenses to be classified into operating, investing, financing, income tax and discontinued operations categories; mandates two subtotals — operating profit and profit before financing and income taxes; requires disclosure of management-defined performance measures (such as adjusted EBITDA used in public communications) with a reconciliation to the nearest IFRS subtotal; and strengthens aggregation and disaggregation guidance. For India, ICAI must issue a corresponding Ind AS and MCA must notify it; until then Schedule III's nature-based format continues. Track ICAI exposure drafts: a converged standard would require a Schedule III overhaul and would change the "exceptional items" and "other income" conventions you learn in s04.

Format freedom under IFRS and US GAAP vs Schedule III

IFRS permits liquidity-order presentation and has no prescribed format; US SEC filers follow Regulation S-X; Schedule III fixes format and order

IAS 1 specifies minimum line items but no layout: an IFRS reporter may present a liquidity-order balance sheet where that is more relevant (banks, insurers), choose between nature and function of expense, and use one or two statements of comprehensive income. US SEC registrants follow Regulation S-X, which prescribes captions (Rule 5-02 for balance sheets, 5-03 for income statements of commercial companies) with current asset and current liability totals where a classified balance sheet is presented (the norm for commercial companies). Schedule III is far more prescriptive: fixed order, nature-based expenses, single statement, current/non-current mandatory for Division II. When benchmarking an Indian company against global peers, normalise these presentation differences (for example, move depreciation into cost of sales for a function-based comparison).

SEBI LODR Reg 33 results formats

SEBI LODR Reg 33 results formats: quarterly P&L, half-yearly statement of assets & liabilities and cash flow statement

Listed entities file standalone and consolidated financial results under Reg 33: quarterly results within 45 days of quarter-end (limited-reviewed or audited) and annual audited results within 60 days of year-end. SEBI circulars prescribe a P&L format derived from Schedule III Division II with five columns (current quarter, preceding quarter, corresponding quarter of the previous year, year-to-date, previous year). A statement of assets and liabilities is required with half-yearly results (Reg 33(3)(f)) and, since FY 2019-20, a cash flow statement for the half-year. Fourth-quarter figures are the balancing figure between audited annual figures and published nine-month figures. Reporting teams therefore need Schedule III-consistent mapping at quarterly frequency, not just annually.

ICAI Guidance Note on Division II

ICAI Guidance Note on Division II – Ind AS Schedule III to the Companies Act 2013

The ICAI Guidance Note on Division II explains each Schedule III caption, how it interacts with Ind AS, and gives illustrative formats. It is the reference for questions the Schedule does not settle: where to present contract assets and unbilled revenue, how to treat current maturities and lease liabilities, how to compute the 11 ratios, how to read "promoter" and "struck-off company" disclosures, and how to handle CFS where the parent and subsidiaries fall under different Divisions. It was revised after the 2021 amendments. Guidance notes are recommendatory, but auditors and reviewers treat departures as requiring justification, so cite it in technical memos.

Common pitfalls

Current maturities left in non-current borrowings

Leaving current maturities of long-term borrowings in non-current (Schedule III requires them within current borrowings)

The mistake: the term-loan ledger is mapped wholly to non-current borrowings, or current maturities are carried in "other financial liabilities" as under pre-2021 practice. Why it happens: Tally holds one ledger per loan; the repayment schedule sits outside the accounting system; old templates are reused. Correct treatment: split each loan by contractual repayments due within twelve months of the reporting date (Ind AS 1.69(c)), present them within current borrowings and disclose separately in the borrowings note (2021 amendment). Detection: reconcile the borrowings note to sanction letters and repayment schedules; compare next year's actual repayments with this year's current maturities; analytical review of the current ratio.

OCI presented without the reclassification split

Presenting OCI without splitting reclassifiable vs non-reclassifiable items and their respective tax

The mistake: a single "Other comprehensive income (net of tax)" line, or one combined tax figure. Why it happens: only one OCI item (often actuarial loss) exists and the preparer ignores the format; consolidation adds FCTR or hedge items later. Correct treatment: Ind AS 1.82A requires grouping into items that will not be reclassified and items that will be; Schedule III requires the tax relating to each group separately (A(ii), B(ii)); Ind AS 1.90 requires tax per item in the notes. Detection: check each OCI reserve column in the SOCIE against the P&L groups; verify DTA/DTL movements recognised in OCI tie to the tax lines.

Applying a 12-month test to long-cycle working capital

Applying a 12-month test to inventory/receivables of long-cycle businesses instead of the operating cycle

The mistake: a developer or EPC contractor classifies inventory, retention receivables or customer advances realisable after twelve months as non-current. Why it happens: auditors and preparers carry over the 12-month habit from short-cycle clients; banks prefer the stricter split. Correct treatment: working-capital items realised or settled within the normal operating cycle are current (Ind AS 1.66(a), 1.68, 1.69(a), 1.70); document the cycle and disclose amounts expected after twelve months (Ind AS 1.61). Detection: compare classification policy with the documented operating cycle; scan for inventory or contract balances in non-current lines; check consistency with peers.

Boilerplate accounting policy notes

Boilerplate policy notes instead of entity-specific material accounting policy information

The mistake: policy notes that paraphrase Ind AS (including policies for transactions the company does not have) while omitting how the company actually applies judgement-heavy policies. Why it happens: template reuse, fear that deleting text invites audit questions, and the pre-2023 "significant policies" culture. Correct treatment: Ind AS 1.117–117E — disclose material accounting policy information, focused on entity-specific application, choices, changes, developed policies, judgements and complexity; remove immaterial policies that obscure. Detection: map each policy paragraph to a material balance or transaction; test whether each sentence would be true of any company (if yes, it is boilerplate); compare with KAMs in the auditor's report.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classification and presentation case — Godavari Realty Ltd [L3]

Godavari Realty Ltd, an unlisted developer with net worth ₹900 crore, documents a 5-year operating cycle. At 31 March 2026 (FS approved 22 May 2026) its balances include (₹ crore): land under development 1,400; land held with no approved plan 260; construction WIP 2,100; customer advances 1,650 (possession FY 2027-28 to FY 2029-30); retention payable to contractors 120 (due 2028); construction-finance term loan 900 (₹180 due in FY 2026-27), on which a DSCR covenant was breached at 31 March 2026 and the lender's no-recall letter is dated 10 May 2026; inter-corporate deposit placed with a group company 75 (repayable 2027); actuarial loss on gratuity 4 (pre-tax); FVOCI equity loss 10 (pre-tax). Tax rate 25.168% (equity at 14.30%).

(a) Classify each balance and justify with Ind AS 1 references. (b) Present the relevant Schedule III balance sheet lines. (c) Prepare the OCI section of the statement of profit and loss. (d) Draft the Ind AS 1.61 maturity disclosure and the 1.122 judgement note for the operating cycle and covenant conclusions.

💡 Hint: Separate working-capital items from financing items before applying the operating-cycle limb.

Q2. SOCIE with restatement and warrants — Pench Engineering Ltd [L3]

Opening balances at 1 April 2025 as previously reported (₹ crore): equity share capital 25 (2.5 crore shares of ₹10); securities premium 140; general reserve 30; retained earnings 260; FVOCI equity reserve 9; CFH reserve 2. During FY 2025-26: an error is discovered — warranty provisions for FY 2024-25 were understated by ₹8 crore (tax 25.168%); PAT ₹72 crore; actuarial gain net of tax ₹1.5 crore; FVOCI loss net of tax ₹3 crore; CFH gain net of tax ₹2 crore, of which ₹0.5 crore recycled; final dividend for FY 2024-25 ₹3 per share paid; 50 lakh preferential warrants issued at ₹400 with 25% received; bonus issue 1:5 out of securities premium on 1 February 2026.

(a) Prepare both parts of the SOCIE in Schedule III format. (b) State whether a third balance sheet is required and why. (c) List the tie-out checks to the P&L, balance sheet and cash flow statement.

Q3. 2021 disclosures and ratios — Tapti Textiles Ltd [L2]

From the following FY 2025-26 / FY 2024-25 data (₹ crore), compute the current ratio, debt-equity ratio, DSCR, ROE and net capital turnover; identify changes over 25%; and draft the explanations: current assets 410 / 380; current liabilities 330 / 250 (including current maturities 70 / 30); total debt 300 / 280; equity 520 / 490 (opening FY 2024-25 equity 460); PAT 45 / 62; depreciation 28 / 26; finance cost 26 / 22; principal repaid 30 / 28; revenue 1,240 / 1,180. Also list the information you would need from Tally and other systems to prepare the receivables, payables and CWIP ageing tables.

Q4. Policy note and materiality review [L3]

You are reviewing draft FY 2025-26 financial statements of a listed IT services company (revenue ₹3,200 crore, PBT ₹520 crore). The draft (a) contains 31 pages of policies including construction contracts, biological assets and government grants (the company has none); (b) has a one-line revenue policy despite fixed-price contracts (38% of revenue) using effort-based progress; (c) nets ₹45 crore of unbilled revenue on some contracts against ₹30 crore of deferred revenue on others; (d) presents "Revenue from operations" including ₹28 crore of interest on deposits; (e) shows rounding to ₹ thousand. Prepare a review memo identifying each issue, the requirement breached (Ind AS 1, Ind AS 115 or Schedule III) and the correction.
