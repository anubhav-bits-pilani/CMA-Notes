Key concept

Current Asset Definition: The Four Limbs of Ind AS 1.66

Concept: Current asset: realised/consumed in normal operating cycle, held for trading, realised within 12 months, or unrestricted cash/cash equivalents (Ind AS 1.66)

Statement

An entity classifies an asset as current when it meets at least one of the following (Ind AS 1.66):

  1. it expects to realise the asset, or intends to sell or consume it, in its normal operating cycle;
  2. it holds the asset primarily for the purpose of trading;
  3. it expects to realise the asset within twelve months after the reporting period; or
  4. the asset is cash or a cash equivalent (as defined in Ind AS 7) unless restricted from being exchanged or used to settle a liability for at least twelve months after the reporting period.

All other assets are classified as non-current. The operating cycle is the time between acquisition of assets for processing and their realisation in cash or cash equivalents; when not clearly identifiable it is assumed to be twelve months (Ind AS 1.68).

Explanation

The limbs are alternatives, which is why a developer's land bank and a whisky producer's maturing stock are current even though realisation is years away — they satisfy limb 1. The operating-cycle limb applies only to working-capital items (inventory, trade receivables, contract assets); a loan given or a long-term deposit does not become current because the entity's cycle is long.

The trading limb captures financial assets held for trading under Ind AS 109 (for example, a treasury portfolio of listed shares or government securities churned for short-term profit), regardless of when they will be sold. Ind AS 1.68 gives as examples financial assets that meet the held-for-trading definition in Ind AS 109. Not every FVTPL asset is held for trading (an equity investment for which the FVOCI election was simply not made is FVTPL but may be a long-term holding); such assets follow the 12-month test.

The 12-month limb covers everything else: the current portion of a long-term loan receivable, interest accrued, a fixed deposit with remaining maturity under twelve months, an advance tax refund expected within the year.

The cash limb excludes restricted balances. Cash held in an escrow account for a project due to be released in 2028, or margin money against a bank guarantee expiring in 2029, is not current. Earmarked balances such as unpaid dividend accounts remain current but are shown in "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" because they are not available for general use.

Ind AS 1.56 adds that deferred tax assets are never classified as current. Assets held for sale are presented separately under Ind AS 105.

Illustration

Narmada Pharma Ltd, operating cycle 9 months, 31 March 2026 (₹ crore):

Asset Limb met Classification
Finished goods with shelf life to 2028, expected sale in 6 months 1 Current
Receivable from a hospital, due in 15 months under a negotiated plan None (outside cycle and 12 months) Non-current trade receivable
Listed bonds held in a trading book 2 Current
₹50 crore loan to a subsidiary, ₹10 crore repayable within 12 months 3 for ₹10 crore ₹10 crore current, ₹40 crore non-current

MAT/advance tax refund expected after assessment in 2027 None Non-current (other non-current assets / tax assets)

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the assets [L1]

An EPC company with a 30-month operating cycle holds at 31 March 2026: retention receivable ₹22 crore due in 20 months; capital advance for a crane ₹6 crore; FD of ₹10 crore with original maturity 15 months and remaining maturity 8 months; margin money ₹4 crore lodged against a performance guarantee expiring in 2028; derivative forward contract (not a hedge) with fair value ₹1 crore maturing in 18 months. Classify each, naming the limb applied.

Q2. Restricted cash judgement [L2]

A company holds ₹35 crore in a debt-service reserve account that its lenders require to be maintained throughout the life of a term loan maturing in 2031. The CFO argues it is "cash" and current. Evaluate the argument under Ind AS 1.66 and Ind AS 7, and state the presentation in Schedule III.

💡 Hint: Consider both the restriction period and whether the balance meets the cash-equivalent definition.

Q3. Trading vs FVTPL [L2]

Explain why an equity investment measured at FVTPL because the entity did not elect FVOCI may still be non-current, while a portfolio of listed shares managed by the treasury desk for short-term gains is current regardless of intended holding period.
