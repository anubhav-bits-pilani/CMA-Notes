Key concept

Current Liability Definition: The Four Limbs of Ind AS 1.69

Concept: Current liability: settled in operating cycle, held for trading, due within 12 months, or no right at reporting date to defer settlement ≥12 months (Ind AS 1.69)

Statement

A liability is current when it meets at least one of the following (Ind AS 1.69):

  1. the entity expects to settle it in its normal operating cycle;
  2. it is held primarily for the purpose of trading;
  3. it is due to be settled within twelve months after the reporting period; or
  4. the entity does not have the right at the end of the reporting period to defer settlement for at least twelve months after the reporting period.

All other liabilities are non-current. Terms of a liability that could, at the counterparty's option, result in settlement by issue of equity instruments do not affect its classification (Ind AS 1.69(d)).

Explanation

Operating cycle limb (1.70): trade payables, accruals for employee and other operating costs, and customer advances form part of working capital and are current even if settled after twelve months. A developer's customer advances on 2029 possessions are current.

Trading limb (1.71): financial liabilities held for trading — derivative liabilities not designated as hedges, short positions — are current.

Due-within-twelve-months limb: current maturities of long-term borrowings, interest due, dividends payable, income tax payable and bank overdrafts (1.71). Under Schedule III (2021), current maturities are presented within current borrowings.

Right-to-defer limb: the key test for borrowings. The right must exist at the reporting date; refinancing agreed after year-end does not help (1.72) unless the entity had discretion under an existing facility to roll over for at least twelve months (1.73). A covenant breach on or before the reporting date that makes the loan repayable on demand removes the right — subject to the Ind AS 1.74 carve-out: if the lender agrees after the reporting period but before approval of the financial statements not to demand payment, the liability is not classified as current. A grace period granted by the lender by the reporting date, ending at least twelve months after it, also preserves non-current classification (1.75).

Management intent is irrelevant. A loan with a right to defer is non-current even if management plans to prepay it next quarter; a demand loan is current even if the bank has renewed it for ten years.

Deferred tax liabilities are never current (Ind AS 1.56). Provisions are split into current and non-current according to expected settlement (gratuity: current portion = expected payments within twelve months as per actuarial report).

Ujjain Textiles Ltd, operating cycle 6 months, 31 March 2026, FS approved 18 May 2026 (₹ crore):

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the liabilities [L1]

A company has at 31 March 2026: (a) ₹40 crore NCDs maturing September 2026; (b) ₹12 crore advance from a customer for delivery in 2027 (operating cycle 8 months); (c) ₹5 crore security deposit from a tenant refundable at the end of a 9-year lease, terminable by the tenant on 3 months' notice; (d) ₹25 crore 0% redeemable preference shares redeemable in 2030; (e) ₹3 crore unpaid dividend. Classify each with the limb applied.

Q2. Intent versus right [L2]

Management of a company with a ₹200 crore term loan (bullet repayment 2029, no breach) has board approval to prepay it in July 2026 from QIP proceeds received in May 2026. Separately, a ₹50 crore WCDL has been renewed every year for twelve years. Explain the classification of each at 31 March 2026 and why management intent is not determinative.

💡 Hint: Focus on what the contract entitles the company to do at the reporting date.

Q3. Carve-out conditions [L2]

List the conditions that must be satisfied for the Ind AS 1.74 carve-out to apply, and for each give one fact pattern in which it would fail.
