Key concept

Third Balance Sheet Requirement Under Ind AS 1.40A

Concept: Third balance sheet required when retrospective policy change, restatement or reclassification materially affects the opening position (Ind AS 1.40A)

Statement

An entity presents a third balance sheet as at the beginning of the preceding period, in addition to the minimum comparative statements required by Ind AS 1.38A, if (Ind AS 1.40A):

  • (a) it applies an accounting policy retrospectively, makes a retrospective restatement of items in its financial statements, or reclassifies items in its financial statements; and
  • (b) the retrospective application, restatement or reclassification has a material effect on the information in the balance sheet at the beginning of the preceding period.

For FY 2025-26 financial statements, the third balance sheet is at 1 April 2024.

Explanation

The rationale is comparability: if the opening position of the comparative year changes materially, users need to see the restated starting point to understand the restated comparative year's movements.

Key rules:

  • Both conditions must be met. A retrospective change that affects only the comparative year's P&L (for example, an error that arose and reversed within FY 2024-25) may not materially affect the balance sheet at 1 April 2024 — no third balance sheet.
  • Date (1.40D): the date of the opening balance sheet is the beginning of the preceding period, regardless of whether the entity presents comparative information for earlier periods voluntarily.
  • Notes (1.40C): the entity discloses the information required by Ind AS 1.41–44 (reclassification) and Ind AS 8 (policies, errors), but need not present the related notes to the opening balance sheet.
  • Not required for estimate changes (prospective) or for policy changes applied prospectively under transitional provisions.
  • Interim reports: Ind AS 34 does not require a third balance sheet in condensed interim statements.
  • First-time adoption: Ind AS 101 separately requires an opening Ind AS balance sheet at the date of transition — a different requirement.

Schedule III's SOCIE rows ("changes in accounting policy or prior period errors" and "restated balance at the beginning") show the equity effect; the third balance sheet shows the full asset/liability effect.

Presentation in practice: the balance sheet shows three columns — "31 March 2026", "31 March 2025 (Restated)" and "1 April 2024 (Restated)" — with a note reconciling each restated line from the previously reported amount.

Illustration

During FY 2025-26, Kalinga Metals Ltd discovers that inventory at 31 March 2024 was overstated by ₹30 crore (double counting of in-transit stock), which reversed in FY 2024-25 when the stock was consumed. Tax rate 25.168%.

Effect 1 April 2024 FY 2024-25 P&L 31 March 2025
Inventory (30.0) — —
Current tax / DTA (assume tax computation also overstated and a revised return possible) 7.6 — —
Retained earnings (22.4) — —
Cost of materials consumed FY 2024-25 — (30.0) lower —

Total equity at 1 April 2024 falls by ₹22.4 crore; at 31 March 2025 equity is unchanged because the overstatement had reversed. If ₹22.4 crore is material to the 1 April 2024 balance sheet (say equity of ₹400 crore and inventory of ₹180 crore), a third balance sheet is required even though the 31 March 2025 balance sheet is unaffected.

Practice Questions

Q1. Is a third balance sheet needed? [L2]

For FY 2025-26 financial statements, decide whether a balance sheet at 1 April 2024 is required in each case, assuming amounts are material unless stated: (a) depreciation omitted in FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 on a ₹60 crore asset; (b) a change in useful life of a fleet with effect from 1 April 2025; (c) reclassification of ₹45 crore current maturities from other financial liabilities to borrowings in both comparative balance sheets; (d) an expense of ₹2 crore wrongly capitalised in FY 2024-25 (equity ₹900 crore).

💡 Hint: Test each case against both limbs: the type of change, then materiality at the opening date.

Q2. Presentation build [L2]

Using the Kalinga Metals illustration, draft the three-column balance sheet extract (inventory, deferred/current tax, retained earnings) and the reconciliation note showing "as previously reported", "adjustment" and "restated" for 31 March 2025 and 1 April 2024. Assume reported inventory at 1 April 2024 of ₹210 crore and at 31 March 2025 of ₹235 crore, and reported retained earnings of ₹340 crore and ₹395 crore respectively.

Q3. Interview question [L1]

Explain in three sentences why Ind AS 1 requires a third balance sheet only when the opening position is materially affected, and what notes accompany it.
