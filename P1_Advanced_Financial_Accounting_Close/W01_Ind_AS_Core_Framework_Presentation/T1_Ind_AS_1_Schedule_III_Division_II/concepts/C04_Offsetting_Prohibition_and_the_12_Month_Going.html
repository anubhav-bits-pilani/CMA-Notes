Key concept

Offsetting Prohibition and the 12-Month Going Concern Horizon

Concept: Offsetting prohibited unless a standard requires/permits (Ind AS 1.32); going concern assessed for at least 12 months from reporting date (Ind AS 1.25–26)

Statement

Offsetting (Ind AS 1.32): An entity shall not offset assets and liabilities or income and expenses, unless required or permitted by an Ind AS.

Going concern (Ind AS 1.25–26): When preparing financial statements, management shall assess the entity's ability to continue as a going concern. Financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis unless management intends to liquidate the entity or cease trading, or has no realistic alternative but to do so. Material uncertainties related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt upon the entity's ability to continue as a going concern shall be disclosed. In assessing whether the assumption is appropriate, management takes into account all available information about the future, which is at least, but is not limited to, twelve months from the end of the reporting period (1.26).

Explanation

Offsetting

Gross presentation lets users see both the resources and the obligations. Measuring assets net of valuation allowances (ECL allowance on receivables, inventory obsolescence allowance) is not offsetting (1.33). Standards that require or permit offsetting include:

  • Ind AS 32.42 — financial asset and liability offset only when the entity currently has a legally enforceable right to set off and intends to settle net or realise and settle simultaneously; a master netting agreement without intent to settle net is not enough;
  • Ind AS 12.71 and 12.74 — current tax assets and liabilities offset when there is a legally enforceable right and intention to settle net; deferred tax assets and liabilities offset when they relate to the same taxation authority on the same taxable entity (or different entities intending to settle net);
  • Ind AS 19 — net defined benefit liability (obligation less plan assets);
  • Ind AS 1.34–35 — gains and losses on disposal of non-current assets shown net of disposal costs; expenditure reimbursed under a contractual arrangement (Ind AS 37) netted against the reimbursement; gains and losses from a group of similar transactions (FX gains/losses, trading gains) shown net unless material.

Indian practice issues: netting advance tax of different assessment years against provisions (permitted only where the right and intention exist, typically within the same year or settled together); netting debit and credit balances of the same customer across different contracts; netting bank overdrafts against positive balances in a different bank.

Going concern

The twelve-month horizon is a minimum: if the company has a large bond maturing in month 14 with no refinancing plan, the assessment must consider it. Indicators (SA 570 lists them) include net current liabilities, defaults on loans, NCLT applications by creditors, loss of key customers or licences, negative operating cash flows. If management concludes the entity is a going concern but material uncertainty exists, Ind AS 1.25 requires disclosure of that uncertainty; the auditor adds a "Material uncertainty related to going concern" section (SA 570). If the going concern basis is inappropriate, a different basis (for example, liquidation basis — not prescribed by Ind AS, developed under Ind AS 8) is used and that fact disclosed. Deterioration after the reporting date can override normal Ind AS 10 treatment (Topic 2, s06).

Illustration

Offsetting. A company has ₹15 crore deposits with Bank A and an overdraft of ₹9 crore with Bank B, plus a ₹6 crore FD with Bank B under a lien and set-off clause, which the company does not intend to settle net. Presentation: cash and bank balances gross; overdraft within current borrowings (or within cash and cash equivalents for the cash flow statement only if repayable on demand and integral to cash management, Ind AS 7.8); no offset of the Bank B FD because intention to settle net is missing.

Going concern. At 31 March 2026 a company has net current liabilities of ₹140 crore, a ₹300 crore NCD maturing in June 2027 (15 months), and a lender term sheet for refinancing that is not yet sanctioned. Management's forecast to June 2027 shows a funding gap without the refinancing. Even though the NCD falls outside twelve months, the assessment must include it, and a material uncertainty disclosure is likely.

Connections

Offsetting links to s03 (current tax line, contract balances net per contract only), Ind AS 115 contract-level netting in Topic 4, and Week 3 financial instruments and deferred tax. Going concern links to Topic 2 s06 (Ind AS 10.14), SA 570, covenant classification (s02), and FP&A cash forecasting — the 13-week and 18-month cash flow forecasts that support the board's going concern assessment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Offset or present gross? [L1]

State whether each may be offset and cite the standard: (a) GST input credit and GST output liability under the same GSTIN; (b) ECL allowance against gross receivables; (c) DTA of the parent against DTL of a subsidiary in the CFS; (d) receivable from and payable to the same vendor under a contract allowing set-off, with a history of net settlement; (e) insurance claim receivable against the loss provision for a fire.

Q2. Going concern memo [L3]

A listed hospitality company has at 31 March 2026: cash ₹40 crore; current liabilities exceeding current assets by ₹220 crore; ₹350 crore of term loans with ₹90 crore due within 12 months; a promoter commitment letter to infuse ₹150 crore by September 2026; and a one-time restructuring proposal pending with the consortium. Draft a going concern assessment memo covering the horizon, the evidence required, the disclosure conclusion and the auditor's likely reporting under SA 570.

💡 Hint: Separate the question of the basis of preparation from the question of whether a material uncertainty must be disclosed.

Q3. Overdraft netting [L2]

A company presents a ₹12 crore overdraft with ICICI netted against ₹20 crore balances with HDFC as "cash and cash equivalents ₹8 crore" on the face of the balance sheet. Explain what is wrong under Ind AS 1.32 and Ind AS 32.42 and how the Ind AS 7.8 treatment differs for the cash flow statement.
