Key concept

Definition of Material Under Ind AS 1.7

Concept: Material = omitting, misstating or obscuring could reasonably influence primary users' decisions (Ind AS 1.7)

Statement

Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial statements make on the basis of those financial statements, which provide financial information about a specific reporting entity (Ind AS 1.7, aligned with Ind AS 8.5).

Primary users are existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors who cannot require reporting entities to provide information directly to them.

Explanation

The definition has four working parts:

  1. Omitting, misstating or obscuring. "Obscuring" was added by the amended definition: burying a material item in a mass of immaterial detail, scattering related information across notes, aggregating dissimilar items, or using vague language can make information effectively missing.
  2. Could reasonably be expected. The test is objective — a reasonable user — not whether any user could conceivably care.
  3. Influence decisions of primary users. Decisions about buying, selling or holding equity and debt, providing or settling loans, and exercising voting rights on management's actions.
  4. Specific reporting entity. Materiality is entity-specific: the same ₹5 crore is immaterial for Reliance and material for a small-cap.

Materiality is both quantitative (size relative to profit, revenue, total assets, equity) and qualitative (nature — related-party transactions, illegal acts, breaches of covenants, items that turn a profit into a loss or affect a trend, items affecting compliance with regulatory thresholds such as Sec 135 CSR applicability or MSME disclosures). An item can be material by nature even when small.

Uses in Ind AS:

  • Ind AS 1.29–31: aggregation and separate presentation; an entity need not provide a specific disclosure required by an Ind AS if the information is not material;
  • Ind AS 1.117: material accounting policy information;
  • Ind AS 8.41: errors, including immaterial errors made intentionally to achieve a particular presentation, make financial statements non-compliant;
  • Ind AS 1.40A: third balance sheet triggered by material effect.

Auditors use SA 320 benchmarks (for example, 5% of profit before tax, 0.5–1% of revenue or 1–2% of total assets) to set performance materiality; these are audit planning tools, not accounting rules — management must make its own materiality judgements.

Illustration

Saraswati Chemicals Ltd: revenue ₹1,800 crore, PBT ₹90 crore, total assets ₹1,500 crore.

Item Amount Quantitative Qualitative Conclusion
Unrecorded accrual for freight ₹1.2 crore 1.3% of PBT None Likely immaterial; correct anyway if simple
Loan to a director's relative not disclosed as related party ₹0.4 crore Trivial Related party, Sec 185 compliance Material by nature
Misclassification of ₹60 crore term loan as non-current ₹60 crore 4% of total assets Affects current ratio covenant of 1.25 (ratio drops from 1.31 to 1.12) Material
Policy notes of 22 pages hiding a change in revenue recognition for export incentives ₹14 crore 15% of PBT Obscured Material — obscuring

Connections

Underpins s07 (material accounting policy information), c05 (third balance sheet), Topic 2 (material errors and restatement; SAB 108 rollover vs iron-curtain approaches in US GAAP), and audit sampling. In FP&A, materiality thresholds decide which variances are explained in management reporting and which ratio changes require the Schedule III 25% variance note.

Practice Questions

Q1. Quantitative vs qualitative [L2]

A listed company with PBT of ₹40 crore discovers three uncorrected items: (a) ₹1.5 crore of revenue recognised in March for goods delivered in April; (b) ₹0.3 crore paid to a vendor owned by the CFO's spouse, not disclosed as a related-party transaction; (c) ₹2.5 crore of FD interest presented in revenue from operations. Assess materiality of each individually and in aggregate, giving reasons.

💡 Hint: Consider trends, ratios, regulatory disclosures and the effect of aggregation, not just percentages of PBT.

Q2. Obscuring [L1]

Give four examples of how information can be "obscured" in an Indian annual report and, for each, the presentation change that would fix it.

Q3. Interview: materiality and intentional errors [L2]

An interviewer asks: "If an error is below materiality, can the company leave it uncorrected?" Answer by reference to Ind AS 8.41 and the definition in Ind AS 1.7, including the position on intentional immaterial misstatements.
