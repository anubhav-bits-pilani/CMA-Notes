Key concept

Total Comprehensive Income and Its Attribution to Owners and NCI

Concept: Total comprehensive income = Profit for the period + OCI (net of tax); CFS splits both between owners of parent and NCI

Statement

\[ \text{TCI} = \text{Profit or loss for the period} + \text{OCI}_{\text{net of tax}} \]

where profit or loss is the total of income less expenses excluding OCI items, and \(\text{OCI}_{\text{net of tax}}\) is the sum of items that will not be reclassified to profit or loss and items that will be reclassified, each net of its related income tax (Ind AS 1.7, 1.81A, 1.82A, 1.91).

In consolidated financial statements, Ind AS 1.81B requires both profit or loss and TCI to be allocated:

\[ \text{Profit} = \text{Profit}_{\text{owners of parent}} + \text{Profit}_{\text{NCI}}, \qquad \text{TCI} = \text{TCI}_{\text{owners of parent}} + \text{TCI}_{\text{NCI}} \]

Schedule III Division II additionally requires OCI attributable to owners and NCI to be shown in the consolidated statement.

Explanation

TCI captures every change in equity during the period other than transactions with owners in their capacity as owners (Ind AS 1.7). Profit is the subset that users rely on for performance and EPS; OCI holds remeasurements that Ind AS deliberately keeps outside profit (actuarial gains and losses, FVOCI movements, hedge reserves, translation differences, revaluation surplus).

Attribution to NCI follows present ownership interests (Ind AS 110.B94): profit and each OCI component are allocated in proportion to the NCI's share in the subsidiary that generates them, even if this results in a deficit balance for NCI (Ind AS 110.B94). Where a subsidiary has cumulative preference shares classified as equity held by NCI, the parent computes its share after adjusting for preference dividends whether or not declared (110.B95).

Common mistakes: allocating OCI to NCI using the group's overall NCI percentage rather than the specific subsidiary's; omitting NCI's share of FCTR on a foreign subsidiary; and computing EPS on TCI rather than on profit attributable to owners of the parent (Ind AS 33 uses profit, not TCI).

In the SOCIE, TCI attributable to owners is spread across the equity columns (retained earnings for profit, each OCI reserve for its OCI item) and TCI attributable to NCI goes to the NCI column.

Connections

Links to s04 (OCI presentation and tax) and s05 (SOCIE columns). Ind AS 110 attribution, NCI measurement and changes in ownership come later in consolidation weeks. EPS (Topic 5) uses profit attributable to owners. In three-statement models, retained earnings roll forward with profit to owners less dividends, while OCI reserves roll forward separately — mixing them breaks the equity schedule.

Practice Questions

Q1. Attribution exercise [L2]

A parent holds 70% of S (domestic) and 55% of T (Singapore). FY 2025-26 (₹ crore): parent PAT 90; S PAT 30 and FVOCI equity gain net of tax 4; T loss (12) and FCTR loss (6); parent's own actuarial gain net of tax 2. Compute profit, OCI and TCI attributable to owners of the parent and to NCI, and show the face presentation required by Schedule III.

Q2. Deficit NCI [L1]

A 51% subsidiary has accumulated losses such that NCI's share of equity is negative ₹8 crore. In FY 2025-26 the subsidiary incurs a further loss of ₹20 crore. How much is attributed to NCI, and why does Ind AS 110 not cap NCI at zero?

💡 Hint: Look at the wording of Ind AS 110.B94 on deficit balances.

Q3. Model design [L2]

In a consolidated three-statement model, describe how you would structure the equity schedule so that TCI flows correctly into retained earnings, each OCI reserve and NCI, and list two checks that would catch an attribution error.
