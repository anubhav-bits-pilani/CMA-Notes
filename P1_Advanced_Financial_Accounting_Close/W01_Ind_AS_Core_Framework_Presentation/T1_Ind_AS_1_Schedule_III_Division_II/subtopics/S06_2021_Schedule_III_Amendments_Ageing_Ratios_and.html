Subtopic note

2021 Schedule III Amendments: Ageing, Ratios and Other Disclosures

Scope: 2021 Schedule III amendments: promoter shareholding, receivable/payable/CWIP ageing, title deeds not in company name, 11 ratios with >25% variance explanation, struck-off companies, crypto

Why it matters

MCA's notification of 24 March 2021 amended Divisions I, II and III of Schedule III with effect from 1 April 2021 (FY 2021-22 onwards). The amendments turned the notes into a governance and fraud-risk dashboard: who the promoters are and whether they are selling, how old receivables and payables are, whether projects in CWIP are stuck, whether the company even holds title to its properties, and whether it deals with shell companies or crypto. CARO 2020 clauses mirror many of these items, so auditors test them twice. For an analyst, the ageing schedules and the ratio note give quick signals of stress; for an FP&A or reporting team, they require system-level data (due dates, project start dates, title records) that Tally ledgers do not capture by default.

Core notes

Promoter shareholding

Shares held by promoters at the end of the year must be disclosed in the share capital note:

S. No. Promoter name No. of shares % of total shares % change during the year

"Promoter" follows Sec 2(69) of the Companies Act (and the promoter group disclosed under SEBI regulations for listed entities). Disclosure is per class of shares. The "% change" column captures the change in the promoter's holding during the year; practice varies between percentage-point change and change relative to the opening holding, so state the basis and show holdings at both dates. Promoter pledges are not part of this table but are separately disclosed under SEBI SAST for listed entities.

Trade receivables ageing

Ageing is from the due date of payment (where no due date is specified, from the date of transaction):

Particulars Not due < 6 months 6 months – 1 year 1–2 years 2–3 years > 3 years Total
(i) Undisputed — considered good
(ii) Undisputed — significant increase in credit risk
(iii) Undisputed — credit impaired
(iv) Disputed — considered good
(v) Disputed — significant increase in credit risk
(vi) Disputed — credit impaired

The "not due" column and an "unbilled" line are added in practice (ICAI Guidance Note) so the table reconciles to the balance sheet. The credit-risk categories link to Ind AS 109 ECL staging: companies using the simplified approach still need a basis for the "significant increase in credit risk" and "credit impaired" rows. Separate tables are given for non-current and current receivables. "Disputed" requires a documented dispute (customer has contested the debt, legal notice, arbitration).

Trade payables ageing

Particulars Not due < 1 year 1–2 years 2–3 years > 3 years Total
(i) MSME
(ii) Others
(iii) Disputed dues — MSME
(iv) Disputed dues — Others

MSME status must be as per the MSMED Act 2006 registration (Udyam). The MSME payables also link to the MSMED Act Sec 22 disclosures (principal and interest unpaid, interest paid under Sec 16) and, from FY 2023-24, to Sec 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, under which payments to micro and small enterprises beyond the Sec 15 limit (45 days with agreement, 15 days without) are deductible only on payment.

CWIP and intangible assets under development ageing

Ageing by the period for which amounts have been in CWIP:

CWIP < 1 year 1–2 years 2–3 years > 3 years Total
Projects in progress
Projects temporarily suspended

For projects whose completion is overdue or has exceeded cost compared to its original plan, a completion schedule is required showing the amount to be completed in < 1, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years, project-wise. Projects whose activity has been suspended must be disclosed separately. The same tables apply to intangible assets under development (for example, internally developed software or product development under Ind AS 38).

Title deeds of immovable property not held in the company's name

For each immovable property (other than properties where the company is lessee and the lease agreement is duly executed in its favour) whose title deed is not held in the company's name:

Relevant BS line Description of item Gross carrying value Title deeds held in the name of Whether holder is a promoter, director, relative or employee Period held (date range) Reason for not being held in company's name (indicate if in dispute)

Typical causes: properties acquired through amalgamation where mutation is pending; land allotted by state industrial corporations with conveyance pending; property registered in a director's name. The table covers PPE, investment property, PPE retired from active use and held for disposal, and other immovable property line items. CARO 2020 clause 3(i)(c) requires a matching auditor report.

The 11 analytical ratios

Division II requires these ratios for the current and previous year, with an explanation for any change of more than 25% compared with the preceding year:

Ratio Common formula
Current ratio Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Debt-equity ratio Total debt ÷ Shareholders' equity
Debt service coverage ratio Earnings available for debt service ÷ Debt service (interest + lease payments + principal repayments)
Return on equity Net profit after tax − Preference dividend ÷ Average shareholders' equity
Inventory turnover Cost of goods sold (or sales) ÷ Average inventory
Trade receivables turnover Net credit sales ÷ Average trade receivables
Trade payables turnover Net credit purchases ÷ Average trade payables
Net capital turnover Net sales ÷ Working capital
Net profit ratio Net profit ÷ Net sales
Return on capital employed EBIT ÷ Capital employed (tangible net worth + total debt + deferred tax liability)
Return on investment Income generated from investments ÷ Time-weighted average investments

Schedule III names the ratios but does not define numerators and denominators exactly; companies must disclose their formulas and apply them consistently. ICAI's Guidance Note gives suggested definitions (for example, earnings available for debt service = net profit after tax + non-cash operating expenses + interest + other adjustments such as loss on sale of fixed assets). Division III NBFCs instead disclose capital-to-risk-weighted-assets ratio, Tier I and Tier II CRAR and liquidity coverage ratio.

Illustration of the variance test: DSCR fell from 1.80 to 1.26 in FY 2025-26.

\[ \text{Change} = \frac{1.26 - 1.80}{1.80} = -30\% \]

Because the change exceeds 25%, the note must explain it (for example, "higher principal repayments following the bullet repayment of NCDs and lower operating profit due to raw material price increase").

Relationship with struck-off companies

Where the company has transactions with companies struck off under Sec 248 of the Companies Act 2013 or Sec 560 of the Companies Act 1956:

Name of struck-off company Nature of transactions (investments in securities; receivables; payables; shares held by struck-off company; other outstanding balances) Balance outstanding Relationship with the struck-off company, if any

The data source is the MCA struck-off list matched against vendor, customer and shareholder masters — a data-matching exercise best done with Power Query fuzzy merge on CIN and name.

Crypto currency or virtual currency

Where the company has traded or invested in crypto or virtual currency during the year: the profit or loss on transactions involving crypto, the amount held at the reporting date, and deposits or advances from any person for trading or investing in crypto. The accounting itself is not prescribed by Schedule III: crypto held is usually an intangible asset (Ind AS 38) or, for a broker-trader, inventory (Ind AS 2). Taxation is under Sec 115BBH (30% on income from transfer of virtual digital assets) and TDS under Sec 194S.

Other 2021 additions (complete the picture)

Revaluation of PPE and intangibles — whether based on a registered valuer's report; loans or advances to promoters, directors, KMPs and related parties repayable on demand or without specified terms; benami property proceedings; quarterly returns/statements of current assets filed with banks in agreement with books (or reconciliation); wilful defaulter status; registration of charges or satisfaction with ROC beyond the statutory period; compliance with the number of layers under Sec 2(87); compliance with approved schemes of arrangement; utilisation of borrowed funds and share premium through intermediaries; undisclosed income surrendered in tax assessments; CSR details; current maturities of long-term borrowings within current borrowings; lease liabilities as a separate line; SOCIE rows for prior period errors.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Receivables with no credit terms are aged from invoice date; a long "not due" column on retention or milestone receivables is acceptable if contractually not due.
  • ECL allowance is shown as a deduction after the ageing table total to reconcile to net carrying amount.
  • CWIP of a project re-planned with board-approved revised timelines: completion schedule compares to the original plan; disclosure is still required.
  • Leasehold land from MIDC/GIDC under a registered lease deed in the company's name is excluded from the title deeds table.
  • Ratios in a loss year (negative ROE) still require computation and explanation.
  • Struck-off company later restored by NCLT under Sec 252: disclose based on status during the year, with a note.

Key takeaways

  • The 2021 amendments apply from FY 2021-22 to Divisions I, II and III.
  • Ageing schedules (receivables from due date; payables; CWIP from capitalisation start) must reconcile to the balance sheet.
  • Title deeds not in the company's name, promoter shareholding and struck-off company relationships are governance red flags tested again under CARO 2020.
  • Eleven ratios are required, with explanations for changes above 25%; disclose the formulas used.
  • Crypto holdings and transactions must be disclosed; accounting follows Ind AS 38 or Ind AS 2.

Practice Questions

Q1. Receivables ageing table [L1]

Gross trade receivables at 31 March 2026 are ₹840 lakh: not due ₹410 lakh; overdue < 6 months ₹250 lakh; 6–12 months ₹80 lakh; 1–2 years ₹60 lakh (of which ₹45 lakh disputed, credit impaired); > 3 years ₹40 lakh (undisputed, credit impaired). ECL allowance is ₹115 lakh. Prepare the Schedule III ageing table and reconcile to the balance sheet figure.

Q2. Ratio variance note [L2]

Using the following (₹ crore) compute DSCR, ROCE and trade receivables turnover for FY 2025-26 and FY 2024-25 and identify which changes exceed 25%: PAT 62 / 80; depreciation 30 / 28; finance cost 24 / 18; principal repaid 70 / 40; lease payments 6 / 6; EBIT 118 / 132; tangible net worth 520 / 470; total debt 260 / 300; DTL 20 / 18; revenue (all credit) 1,450 / 1,320; average receivables 310 / 220.

💡 Hint: State your formula definitions first; the 25% test compares the ratio values, not the underlying amounts.

Q3. Title deeds case [L2]

A company acquired a subsidiary by amalgamation in FY 2023-24; three factory plots (gross value ₹46 crore) remain registered in the transferor company's name, and one office flat (₹3 crore) is registered in the managing director's name. Draft the Schedule III disclosure and list the CARO 2020 implications.

Q4. Data pipeline design [L3]

Design a Power Query and Excel workflow that produces the receivables ageing, payables ageing (with MSME split) and struck-off company disclosures from Tally Prime exports (ledger masters, bill-wise outstanding, vendor masters) and the MCA struck-off list. Specify the queries, key joins, the checks that ensure totals tie to the TB, and how you would handle missing due dates.
