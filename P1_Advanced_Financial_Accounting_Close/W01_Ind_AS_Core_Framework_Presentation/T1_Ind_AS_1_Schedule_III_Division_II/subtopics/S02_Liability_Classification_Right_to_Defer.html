Subtopic note

Liability Classification: Right to Defer, Refinancing and Covenants

Scope: Liability classification: right to defer settlement ≥12 months at reporting date, refinancing after year-end, covenant-breach carve-out when lender waives before FS approval

Why it matters

Misclassifying a term loan as non-current is one of the most frequent findings in NFRA and peer-review observations on Indian listed company accounts. The classification feeds the current ratio, the net working capital in drawing-power computations, debt-maturity analysis in credit appraisal, the "current maturities" line reviewers scan for refinancing risk, and the going-concern assessment. In FP&A and modelling work, the debt schedule's current/non-current split must match the audited balance sheet, and covenant headroom analysis at quarter-end decides whether a loan is reclassified to current in the next set of results.

Core notes

The four limbs of Ind AS 1.69

A liability is current when the entity:

  • (a) expects to settle it in its normal operating cycle;
  • (b) holds it primarily for trading;
  • (c) is due to settle it within twelve months after the reporting period; or
  • (d) does not have the right at the end of the reporting period to defer settlement for at least twelve months after the reporting period.

Limb (d) is the decisive test for borrowings. Three features matter:

  1. Right, not intention. What counts is whether the entity has the right at the reporting date. Management's intention or expectation to repay early, or to refinance, does not change a classification supported by the contractual right.
  2. At the reporting date. The right must exist on 31 March. Events between 31 March and the date of approval (refinancing, waivers, grace periods) are generally non-adjusting for classification (Ind AS 1.72, 1.76) — with the Indian carve-out discussed below.
  3. Equity-settlement terms. Ind AS 1.69(d) states that terms which could, at the counterparty's option, result in settlement by issue of equity instruments do not affect classification (relevant for convertibles whose conversion option is an equity component).

Refinancing after year-end

Ind AS 1.72 requires financial liabilities due to be settled within twelve months to be classified as current even if (a) the original term exceeded twelve months, and (b) an agreement to refinance or reschedule on a long-term basis is completed after the reporting period and before the financial statements are approved. Ind AS 1.76 lists such refinancing as a non-adjusting event to be disclosed under Ind AS 10.

Contrast Ind AS 1.73: if the entity expects, and has the discretion, to refinance or roll over an obligation for at least twelve months under an existing loan facility, the obligation is non-current even if it would otherwise be due within twelve months. The discretion must exist under a facility in place at the reporting date, with the same lender on the same or similar terms. A commercial-paper programme backed by a standby line from a different bank does not qualify; a revolving credit facility sanctioned for three years under which the entity can roll each drawdown does.

Situation at 31 March 2026 Classification
₹200 crore NCD maturing 15 Nov 2026; refinanced by new 5-year NCD issue on 20 April 2026 Current (1.72); disclose refinancing as non-adjusting event
₹150 crore drawn under a 3-year committed revolving facility (expiring 2028), each drawdown 6 months, entity may roll over Non-current (1.73)
₹80 crore working-capital demand loan, renewed annually by practice Current — no right to defer; renewal is at the bank's discretion
Cash credit ₹40 crore, repayable on demand Current

Covenants

Loan agreements in India commonly carry financial covenants (DSCR ≥ 1.2, TOL/TNW ≤ 3, FACR, current ratio ≥ 1.33). If a covenant is breached on or before the reporting date and the breach makes the loan payable on demand, the entity no longer has the right to defer settlement at the reporting date.

IAS 1 position (IFRS). IAS 1.74 requires such a liability to be classified as current even if the lender agrees after the reporting period, and before approval, not to demand repayment. A waiver is effective for classification only if obtained by the reporting date and provides a period of grace ending at least twelve months after the reporting date (IAS 1.75, mirrored in Ind AS 1.75).

Ind AS 1.74 carve-out. Ind AS modifies this: where a breach of a material provision of a long-term loan arrangement on or before the reporting date makes the liability payable on demand, the entity does not classify the liability as current if the lender agreed, after the reporting period and before the approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach. Correspondingly, Ind AS 10 treats such an agreement as an adjusting event (see Topic 2, c06). This reflects the Indian banking reality that waivers from consortium lenders are rarely obtained by 31 March.

Conditions to apply the carve-out:

  • the breach relates to a long-term arrangement (not a demand facility);
  • the lender's agreement is obtained before the Board approves the financial statements (Ind AS 10 approval date);
  • the agreement covers the consequence of the breach (not merely a promise to "consider" a waiver); a conditional waiver whose conditions remain unmet is not sufficient;
  • the agreement does not itself make the loan due within twelve months.

IASB amendments of 2020 and 2022. IASB amended IAS 1 (effective for IFRS reporters from 1 January 2024): covenants that must be complied with only after the reporting date do not affect classification at that date, while covenants tested on or before the reporting date do; entities disclose information about non-current liabilities subject to covenants within twelve months (carrying amount, covenant details, facts indicating difficulty). The amendments also clarified that settlement includes transfer of the entity's own equity instruments, except where the conversion option is an equity component. Verify the notified Ind AS 1 text for your reporting period: Indian amendments follow MCA notification, and the 1.74 carve-out has been retained in Ind AS. For multinational groups reporting under IFRS, the same breached loan may be current in the IFRS pack and non-current in the Ind AS accounts.

Current maturities

The portion of a long-term borrowing repayable within twelve months is current under 1.69(c). After the 2021 Schedule III amendments, current maturities of long-term borrowings are presented within current borrowings (and disclosed separately in the borrowings note), not within "other financial liabilities". Lease liabilities are split similarly into current and non-current "Lease liabilities" lines.

Worked illustration

Ganga Steel Ltd has a ₹300 crore term loan from a consortium, quarterly instalments of ₹15 crore, final maturity March 2031. DSCR covenant ≥ 1.25 tested on annual audited results; actual DSCR for FY 2025-26 is 1.10, and the breach permits the lead bank to recall the loan. Board approval of FS: 22 May 2026.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Subjective acceleration clauses ("material adverse change" allowing recall): if not triggered at the reporting date, the right to defer generally exists; if the lender has invoked it, the loan is current.
  • Cross-default: a breach on one facility that triggers cross-default on others affects every linked loan; check all sanction letters.
  • Breach discovered after year-end but existing at 31 March (e.g., audited DSCR computed in May): the breach existed at the reporting date; the carve-out is available only if the waiver precedes approval.
  • Covenant test after year-end (half-yearly test on 30 September 2026): under the IASB amendments this does not affect classification at 31 March; disclose the risk.
  • One-time restructuring under RBI frameworks: revised repayment schedules effective only on implementation; until implemented, contractual dues apply.
  • Interest accrued and due on NCDs is current; interest accrued but not due follows the timing of payment.

Key takeaways

  • The test is a right at the reporting date to defer settlement by at least 12 months; intention is irrelevant.
  • Post-year-end refinancing does not rescue classification (1.72) unless rollover discretion existed under an existing facility (1.73).
  • Ind AS 1.74 carve-out: a lender's agreement not to demand repayment, obtained after year-end but before Board approval, keeps a breached long-term loan non-current; IFRS does not allow this.
  • Current maturities of long-term borrowings belong in current borrowings under Schedule III (2021 amendment).
  • Document covenant compliance at every reporting date; check cross-defaults and conditional waivers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Refinanced bullet NCD [L1]

A company has ₹500 crore of listed NCDs maturing on 31 January 2027. On 15 April 2026 it issues ₹500 crore of new 7-year NCDs and uses the proceeds to redeem the old NCDs early. FS are approved on 10 May 2026. Classify the NCDs at 31 March 2026 and state the disclosure required.

Q2. Waiver timing [L2]

Narmada Chemicals Ltd has a ₹180 crore term loan (₹36 crore repayable each year from FY 2026-27). Its TOL/TNW covenant was breached at 31 March 2026, making the loan repayable on demand. The bank issues a no-recall letter on (i) 28 April 2026, (ii) 5 June 2026. The Board approves the FS on 20 May 2026 and the AGM is on 15 September 2026. For each case, present the borrowings in Schedule III format and pass any reclassification entry. How would the answer differ in an IFRS group reporting pack?

💡 Hint: Identify the approval date first, then compare the Ind AS 1.74 wording with IAS 1.74.

Q3. Revolving facility vs demand loan [L2]

At 31 March 2026 a company has: (a) ₹100 crore drawn under a 3-year committed RCF expiring March 2028 with the right to roll 3-month drawdowns; (b) ₹60 crore commercial paper due June 2026, backed by an undrawn standby line from a different bank; (c) ₹25 crore WCDL renewed annually. Classify each and justify with Ind AS 1 paragraph references.

Q4. Conditional waiver memo [L3]

A consortium waiver letter dated 10 May 2026 (FS approval 25 May 2026) states that the lenders "will not recall the facility provided the promoter infuses ₹75 crore of equity by 31 July 2026 and DSCR at 30 September 2026 is at least 1.2". Write a judgement memo on classification at 31 March 2026, the Ind AS 10 treatment, going-concern implications and the disclosures you would require as auditor.

Q5. Debt schedule build [L2]

Specify a quarterly debt schedule in Excel that automatically splits each loan into current and non-current at each reporting date, including a covenant-breach flag and a "waiver before approval" input that overrides the split. List the inputs, the key formulas and two checks.
