Subtopic note

Operating Cycle Test for Current vs Non-Current Classification

Scope: Operating cycle test for current classification (Ind AS 1.66) — >12-month cycles in real estate, EPC, shipbuilding; 12-month default when cycle not identifiable (Ind AS 1.68)

Why it matters

Current/non-current classification drives the current ratio, net working capital, the "net capital turnover" ratio in the Schedule III ratio note, bank drawing-power calculations, and covenant tests such as minimum current ratio of 1.33 in Indian working-capital sanctions. For long-cycle businesses — real estate developers, EPC contractors, shipbuilders, aircraft MRO, distillers maturing spirits — the choice between a 12-month test and the operating-cycle test can move hundreds of crores between current and non-current. Auditors look at the documented operating cycle, and analysts reading an Indian real estate balance sheet need to know why inventory of a project completing in 2029 sits in current assets.

Core notes

The operating cycle limb of Ind AS 1.66

Ind AS 1.66 classifies an asset as current when any one of four limbs is met:

  1. it is expected to be realised, or intended to be sold or consumed, in the entity's normal operating cycle;
  2. it is held primarily for trading;
  3. it is expected to be realised within twelve months after the reporting period; or
  4. it is cash or a cash equivalent (Ind AS 7) unless restricted from being exchanged or used to settle a liability for at least twelve months.

All other assets are non-current. The limbs are alternatives: an asset that fails the 12-month limb can still be current under the operating cycle limb. Ind AS 1.68 confirms that current assets include inventories and trade receivables "that are sold, consumed or realised as part of the normal operating cycle even when they are not expected to be realised within twelve months after the reporting period". The same logic applies on the liability side under Ind AS 1.69(a) and 1.70: trade payables and accruals for employee and other operating costs are current even if settled more than twelve months later, because they form part of working capital used in the normal operating cycle.

Defining the operating cycle

Ind AS 1.68: the operating cycle is the time between the acquisition of assets for processing and their realisation in cash or cash equivalents. When the normal operating cycle is not clearly identifiable, it is assumed to be twelve months.

Practical points:

  • The cycle is determined for the entity's business, not for each individual asset. An entity with two distinct businesses (say, a cement plant and a real estate division) may have two operating cycles, and the ICAI Guidance Note on Division II accepts that classification is made with reference to the cycle of the relevant business.
  • The cycle is measured from acquisition of inputs (for a developer, land or development rights) to cash realisation (customer collections, including the final instalment on possession).
  • It is an estimate: changes in the documented cycle, driven by a change in the business, are accounted for prospectively; the comparative is reclassified only if the earlier classification was an error.
  • Document it: management should approve an operating-cycle policy (for example, "the Company's operating cycle for residential projects is 4–5 years, being the period from land acquisition to final realisation"), and disclose it in the accounting policies note. (Division I of Schedule III, for Indian GAAP companies, contains the same 12-month default in its own definitions.)

Assets and liabilities that follow the operating cycle

Only working-capital items follow the operating cycle limb: inventories, trade receivables, contract assets, unbilled revenue, trade payables, advances from customers (contract liabilities), operating accruals and short-term operating provisions. Financial items that are not part of the cycle — loans given, deposits placed, borrowings, deferred consideration on a business disposal — are classified using the 12-month and right-to-defer tests even in a long-cycle entity. Ind AS 1.71 makes this explicit for liabilities: some current liabilities are not settled as part of the operating cycle but are due within twelve months (bank overdrafts, current portion of non-current borrowings, dividends payable, income taxes).

Long-cycle industries

Industry Typical cycle Effect
Residential real estate 3–7 years (land → approvals → construction → possession → collection) Land bank intended for development, WIP and customer advances current even if possession in 2029
EPC / infrastructure 2–4 years per project; cycle often set at project duration Retention receivables, contract assets and mobilisation advances current
Shipbuilding 3–5 years Hull WIP and progress billings current
Aged spirits, tobacco leaf, seed producers 2–12 years ageing Maturing inventory current (Ind AS 1.68)
FMCG / trading 30–120 days Cycle < 12 months, so 12-month limb is decisive

Where the cycle is shorter than twelve months, the operating cycle limb rarely changes the answer because the 12-month limb catches everything realised within a year anyway. The limb matters for cycles longer than twelve months.

Real estate: land held for development

A developer holding land to construct and sell residential units classifies it as inventory (Ind AS 2) and current, even if development will start in three years — provided the intention to develop and sell in the ordinary course is supported by board approvals and plans. Land held for capital appreciation or undetermined future use is investment property (Ind AS 40) and non-current. Customer advances on under-construction units are contract liabilities, current under 1.69(a).

Disclosure of amounts beyond twelve months

Ind AS 1.61 requires, for each line combining amounts expected to be recovered/settled within and after twelve months, disclosure of the amount expected after more than twelve months. A developer with ₹12,000 crore of inventory therefore discloses how much is expected to be realised after March 2027. This is the mechanism that protects users when the operating cycle pushes long-dated items into current.

Worked illustration

A Bengaluru developer, 31 March 2026, operating cycle documented at 5 years:

Item ₹ crore Classification Basis
Land for project launching FY 2027-28 1,800 Current inventory 1.66(a): consumed in operating cycle
WIP — Tower B, possession Dec 2028 2,400 Current inventory 1.66(a), 1.68
Customer receivable, final 10% due on possession 2028 150 Current operating cycle
Security deposit with landowner under JDA, refundable 2030 200 Non-current financial asset not a working-capital item; 12-month test fails
Loan to group company repayable 2027 90 Non-current 12-month test only
Construction-finance term loan, bullet 2029 600 Non-current borrowing right to defer ≥ 12 months (1.69(d))

Current ratio including operating-cycle items might be 1.6; on a strict 12-month basis it could fall below 1.0 — which is why lenders to developers often ask for a 12-month maturity analysis alongside Schedule III figures.

Excel / model implementation

In a TB mapping table, carry a CNC_Basis column with values OpCycle, 12M, Trading, Cash and a Months_to_realise column. A formula such as

=IF(OR(CNC_Basis="OpCycle",CNC_Basis="Trading",CNC_Basis="Cash"),"Current",IF(Months_to_realise<=12,"Current","Non-current"))

keeps the logic auditable; Week 4's mapping engine reuses these flags.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cycle not identifiable. A diversified conglomerate with many short and long cycles may conclude that no single cycle is identifiable for a segment; the 12-month default then applies to that segment's working-capital items.
  • Spare parts and stores. Consumable stores used within the cycle are inventory and current; major spares meeting the PPE definition (Ind AS 16.8) are non-current PPE. Classification follows the asset's nature, not the purchase ledger.
  • Retention money in non-EPC entities. A manufacturer that occasionally accepts retention on capital equipment supply typically has a short cycle; retention due beyond 12 months is then non-current.
  • Deferred revenue for multi-year AMCs. A SaaS company with a 12-month cycle classifies the portion of contract liabilities relating to services beyond 12 months as non-current — unlike a developer, whose advances are cycle-based.
  • Change in cycle. If a developer shifts from plotted development (2 years) to integrated townships (6 years), the reclassification is prospective; consider disclosure under 1.61 and Ind AS 8.39.
  • Consolidation. A group may present a subsidiary's long-cycle inventory as current in the CFS even if the parent's own cycle is 12 months; consistency is per business, not per group.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 1.66 limbs are alternatives: working-capital items realised within the normal operating cycle are current even beyond 12 months.
  • Operating cycle = acquisition of assets for processing to realisation in cash; 12 months is assumed only when the cycle is not clearly identifiable (1.68).
  • The operating cycle limb applies to working-capital items only; loans, deposits and borrowings follow the 12-month and right-to-defer tests.
  • Long-cycle industries (real estate, EPC, shipbuilding, maturing inventory) must document and disclose their cycle.
  • Ind AS 1.61 maturity disclosure of amounts beyond 12 months compensates for cycle-based classification.

Practice Questions

Q1. Distillery maturing stock [L1]

A whisky producer holds barrelled malt spirit with a carrying amount of ₹420 crore that will mature over 3–8 years before bottling and sale. It has never documented its operating cycle. Explain how the spirit should be classified at 31 March 2026 and what disclosure Ind AS 1.61 requires.

Q2. Developer balance sheet classification [L2]

A Pune developer (operating cycle documented at 4 years) has at 31 March 2026: land for a township approved by the board for development from 2027 ₹650 crore; land held with no development plan ₹120 crore; WIP ₹900 crore; customer advances ₹780 crore (possession 2028); retention payable to contractors ₹45 crore (due 2028); inter-corporate deposit placed ₹60 crore repayable in 2028; construction loan ₹400 crore repayable in 2029. Classify each item, identifying the limb of Ind AS 1.66 or 1.69 applied and the Ind AS standard governing the land.

💡 Hint: Separate working-capital items from financing and investing items before applying any limb.

Q3. Cycle change mid-year [L2]

An EPC company documented a 24-month operating cycle until FY 2024-25. From FY 2025-26 it wins 5-year metro contracts and revises the cycle to 60 months. Retention receivables of ₹85 crore due in FY 2027-28 were non-current at 31 March 2025. How should they be presented at 31 March 2026 and in the comparative column? Is this a policy change, an estimate change or an error?

Q4. Lender reviewing current ratio [L3]

A bank's credit team argues that the Schedule III current ratio of a shipbuilder (2.1) is misleading because 70% of current assets will be realised after 12 months. Draft a short analyst note explaining why the classification complies with Ind AS 1, how to compute an adjusted 12-month current ratio using Ind AS 1.61 disclosures, and which covenant definition the bank should negotiate.

Q5. Mapping flag design [L2]

Design a TB mapping column set (field names and allowed values) that captures the operating-cycle basis for each ledger and write an Excel formula that returns "Current" or "Non-current". Explain how you would test it on 500 ledgers for errors.
