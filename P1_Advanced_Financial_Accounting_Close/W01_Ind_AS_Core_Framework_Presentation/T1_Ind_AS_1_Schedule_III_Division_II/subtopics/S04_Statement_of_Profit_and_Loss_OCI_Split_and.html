Subtopic note

Statement of Profit and Loss, OCI Split and Exceptional Items

Scope: Statement of Profit and Loss incl. OCI: items that will vs will not be reclassified to P&L (Ind AS 1.82A), exceptional items line, tax on OCI

Why it matters

Indian Ind AS companies present a single statement of profit and loss that ends with total comprehensive income (TCI). Analysts use PAT, not TCI, for EPS and valuation, so the split between profit and OCI matters; the split within OCI tells users which gains will later hit profit (hedge reserves, FVOCI debt) and which never will (actuarial losses, FVOCI equity). "Exceptional items" is a line managements love and regulators scrutinise because it shifts costs below "profit before exceptional items". FP&A teams reconciling management EBITDA to reported numbers, and modellers forecasting OCI reserves in the SOCIE, both rely on this structure.

Core notes

The Division II format

Schedule III Division II prescribes a single statement (the two-statement option in Ind AS 1.10A is not used):

Ref Caption
I Revenue from operations
II Other income
III Total income (I + II)
IV Expenses: cost of materials consumed; purchases of stock-in-trade; changes in inventories of finished goods, stock-in-trade and work-in-progress; employee benefits expense; finance costs; depreciation and amortisation expense; other expenses
V Profit/(loss) before exceptional items and tax (III − IV)
VI Exceptional items
VII Profit/(loss) before tax (V − VI)
VIII Tax expense: (1) current tax; (2) deferred tax
IX Profit/(loss) for the period from continuing operations
X–XII Profit/(loss) from discontinued operations; tax expense of discontinued operations; profit/(loss) from discontinued operations after tax
XIII Profit/(loss) for the period (IX + XII)
XIV Other comprehensive income: A (i) items that will not be reclassified to profit or loss, (ii) income tax relating to those items; B (i) items that will be reclassified to profit or loss, (ii) income tax relating to those items
XV Total comprehensive income for the period (XIII + XIV)
XVI–XVIII Earnings per equity share: continuing; discontinued; continuing and discontinued — basic and diluted

Expenses are classified by nature — the function-of-expense option in Ind AS 1.99 is not available on the face. In the consolidated statement, profit and OCI and TCI are each attributed to owners of the parent and NCI (Ind AS 1.81B).

OCI: will not vs will be reclassified (Ind AS 1.82A)

Ind AS 1.82A requires OCI items to be grouped by whether they will be reclassified (recycled) to profit or loss when specific conditions are met.

Will not be reclassified Will be reclassified
Remeasurements of defined benefit plans (Ind AS 19.120(c)) Exchange differences on translating foreign operations (Ind AS 21.48, on disposal)
Fair value changes of equity instruments designated at FVOCI (Ind AS 109.5.7.5); on derecognition the cumulative amount may be transferred within equity, not to P&L Fair value changes of debt instruments at FVOCI (Ind AS 109.4.1.2A) — recycled on derecognition
Revaluation surplus changes (Ind AS 16, 38) Effective portion of cash flow hedges (Ind AS 109.6.5.11) — recycled when the hedged item affects P&L
Own credit risk changes on financial liabilities designated at FVTPL (Ind AS 109.5.7.7) Cost of hedging reserve (time value of options, forward points)
Share of OCI of associates/JVs that will not be reclassified Share of OCI of associates/JVs that will be reclassified

Reclassification adjustments — amounts recycled from OCI to P&L in the current period — must be disclosed (Ind AS 1.92–93), either on the face or in the notes. Transfers within equity (for example, revaluation surplus to retained earnings as the asset is depreciated, or FVOCI equity reserve to retained earnings on sale) are not reclassification adjustments and never pass through OCI or P&L.

Tax on OCI

Ind AS 1.90 requires disclosure of the income tax relating to each item of OCI. Ind AS 1.91 allows OCI to be presented either net of tax or before tax with one tax line for each group; Schedule III adopts the latter, with tax shown separately for each of the two groups (A(ii) and B(ii)). Deferred and current tax on items recognised in OCI is itself recognised in OCI (Ind AS 12.61A). For Indian companies:

  • Actuarial loss on gratuity: tax deduction is available under Sec 43B only on payment, so a deferred tax asset arises; the DTA on the OCI portion goes to OCI.
  • FVOCI equity gains on listed shares: deferred tax at the capital-gains rate applicable (for example LTCG on listed equity under Sec 112A at 12.5% from 23 July 2024, with the exemption threshold — check the rate applicable to your reporting date), not at the corporate rate.
  • Cash flow hedge reserve: deferred tax at the normal corporate rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA, including surcharge and cess).

Worked illustration

Malabar Foods Ltd, FY 2025-26, tax rate 25.168% (capital gains on equity at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, taken as 14.30% for illustration):

OCI item Gross ₹ lakh Tax ₹ lakh Group
Actuarial loss on gratuity (120) 30.2 A
FVOCI equity gain 300 (42.9) A
Cash flow hedge gain (effective portion) 80 (20.1) B
Translation gain on UAE subsidiary (CFS only) 50 — B

Presentation: A(i) 180, A(ii) (12.7); B(i) 130, B(ii) (20.1) → OCI net 277.2. If PAT is ₹2,400 lakh, TCI is ₹2,677.2 lakh. No deferred tax arises on the translation reserve where the parent does not expect the temporary difference to reverse in the foreseeable future (Ind AS 12.39).

Exceptional items

Neither Ind AS 1 nor Schedule III defines "exceptional items"; Ind AS 1.97 requires separate disclosure of the nature and amount of material items of income or expense, and 1.98 lists circumstances: write-downs of inventories to NRV or PPE to recoverable amount and their reversals; restructurings and reversal of restructuring provisions; disposals of PPE; disposals of investments; discontinued operations; litigation settlements; other reversals of provisions. Ind AS 1.87 prohibits extraordinary items. Schedule III places "Exceptional items" between "profit before exceptional items and tax" and "profit before tax", so exceptional items remain within profit before tax.

Policy points:

  • Define exceptional items in the accounting policy (size, nature or incidence) and apply consistently, both for gains and losses.
  • Recurring items (annual restructuring, routine impairments) are not exceptional.
  • Tax on exceptional items stays in the tax expense line; disclose the tax effect in the note.
  • SEBI's results format also carries an exceptional items line; analysts compare quarter-on-quarter consistency.

Other income vs revenue from operations

Revenue from operations covers sale of products, sale of services and other operating revenues (scrap sales, export incentives, government grants related to operations such as PLI incentives where policy treats them as operating). Other income carries interest, dividends, gains on investments and non-operating rentals. Misclassification inflates "revenue" and distorts margin analysis.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • FVOCI equity sold: the cumulative gain is transferred to retained earnings within equity; it is not recycled and is not a reclassification adjustment.
  • Hedge reserve basis adjustment (hedging a forecast purchase of inventory): removed from the reserve and included in the inventory cost — not a reclassification adjustment, not through OCI (Ind AS 109.6.5.11(d)(i)).
  • Deferred tax on actuarial losses where the company is in a tax-loss position and DTA is not recognised: OCI is presented gross with nil tax; disclose.
  • Exceptional gains: profit on sale of land by a manufacturer is typically exceptional; for a developer it is revenue.
  • Discontinued operations are presented in X–XII, not as exceptional items; their OCI remains in OCI.
  • Income from investments in an investment company may be revenue from operations; the business model decides.

Key takeaways

  • Division II requires a single statement, expenses by nature, ending with TCI and EPS.
  • OCI is split into items that will not and will be reclassified, each with its own tax line (Ind AS 1.82A, 1.91).
  • Remeasurements, FVOCI equity and revaluation surplus are never recycled; CFH, FVOCI debt and FCTR are.
  • Exceptional items sit above PBT; extraordinary items are prohibited (Ind AS 1.87).
  • Tax on OCI items follows the item into OCI (Ind AS 12.61A) at the rate applicable to that item.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the OCI section [L1]

A company's FY 2025-26 data (₹ lakh): actuarial gain on gratuity 45; FVOCI equity loss 160; FVOCI debt gain 70 (including 20 recycled to P&L on sale); CFH effective loss 35; revaluation surplus on land 400. Tax rate 25.168% for all items except equity investments and land (14.30% each, being the capital-gains rate with surcharge and cess). Prepare the OCI section in Schedule III format, including reclassification adjustment disclosure.

Q2. Exceptional or not? [L2]

Decide whether each is an exceptional item for a listed cement company and justify: (a) ₹80 crore VRS cost in a one-time plant closure; (b) ₹12 crore inventory write-down that recurs annually; (c) ₹150 crore gain on sale of surplus land; (d) ₹30 crore reversal of an excise duty provision after a Supreme Court ruling; (e) ₹45 crore impairment of a subsidiary in standalone accounts.

💡 Hint: Test size, nature and incidence against the company's own policy, and check consistency with prior years.

Q3. Equity sale and transfer within equity [L2]

An FVOCI equity investment bought for ₹10 crore was carried at ₹18 crore at 31 March 2025 (FVOCI reserve ₹6.9 crore net of tax). It is sold on 30 November 2025 for ₹21 crore. Pass all entries for FY 2025-26 and show how they appear in OCI and the SOCIE.

Q4. Management EBITDA reconciliation [L3]

The CFO wants "Adjusted EBITDA" in the investor presentation to exclude an ₹18 crore restructuring cost presented as exceptional, a ₹6 crore ESOP charge and ₹4 crore of FX loss. Draft the reconciliation from the Schedule III statement and set out the governance and disclosure issues, including how IFRS 18's management-defined performance measures would change this in future.
