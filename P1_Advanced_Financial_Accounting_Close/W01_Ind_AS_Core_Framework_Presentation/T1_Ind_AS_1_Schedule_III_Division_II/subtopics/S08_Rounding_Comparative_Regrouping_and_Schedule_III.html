Subtopic note

Rounding, Comparative Regrouping and Schedule III Divisions I, II, III

Scope: Rounding rules, comparative regrouping, Division II vs Division III (NBFCs) vs Division I (Indian GAAP companies)

Why it matters

Rounding and regrouping look clerical, but they generate a surprising number of audit points and investor queries: casting differences in notes, a comparative column that silently changed, an NBFC subsidiary whose balance sheet has no current/non-current split being consolidated into a manufacturing parent. Group reporting teams in India routinely consolidate entities under Division I (Indian GAAP subsidiaries for statutory standalone purposes), Division II and Division III, and must convert formats. Models that pull reported numbers need a consistent unit and an awareness that last year's comparative may differ from last year's published figure.

Core notes

Rounding off (Schedule III General Instructions)

Depending on the total income of the company, figures are rounded off as follows:

Total income Permitted rounding
Less than ₹100 crore To the nearest hundreds, thousands, lakhs or millions, or decimals thereof
₹100 crore or more To the nearest lakhs, millions or crores, or decimals thereof

Once a unit is selected it must be used uniformly throughout the financial statements, including notes (comparatives in the same unit). A company is free to present in absolute rupees. The unit is stated in the heading of each statement and note ("All amounts in ₹ crore, unless otherwise stated"). Ind AS 1.51(e) also requires disclosure of the level of rounding.

Practical issues:

  • Casting differences: rounded line items may not add to rounded totals. Common practice is to round each line independently and footnote "Figures may not add due to rounding", or to adjust the largest line by the rounding difference within each note (a documented "plug" policy). Never force-fit totals that then disagree with the face of the statement.
  • Zero and near-zero amounts: amounts below the rounding unit appear as "0.00" or "—"; disclose a footnote such as "0.00 represents amounts below ₹50,000" where significant (common for related-party balances).
  • EPS is presented in rupees and paise, not in the reporting unit.

In Excel, use ROUND(x,2) on the presentation layer only, never in calculation chains; build a rounding check:

=ROUND(SUM(Note_Lines_Unrounded),2)-SUM(ROUND(Note_Lines_Unrounded,2))

Comparative information and regrouping

Ind AS 1.38 requires comparative information for all amounts reported in the current period, and for narrative information where relevant. Ind AS 1.38A requires at minimum two balance sheets, two statements of profit and loss, two cash flow statements, two SOCIEs and related notes.

Reclassification of comparatives (Ind AS 1.41): when presentation or classification is changed, comparatives are reclassified unless impracticable, and the entity discloses (a) the nature of the reclassification, (b) the amount of each item or class reclassified, and (c) the reason. If impracticable, disclose the reason and the nature of adjustments that would have been made (1.42). Schedule III's General Instructions require that "except in the case of the first financial statements laid before the company after incorporation, the corresponding amounts for the immediately preceding reporting period are to be disclosed".

The Indian annual-report footnote "Previous year's figures have been regrouped/reclassified wherever necessary" is not sufficient on its own when the reclassification is material — Ind AS 1.41 demands nature, amount and reason. Distinguish:

Change Treatment
Moving current maturities from other financial liabilities to current borrowings (2021 amendment) Reclassification, disclose under 1.41; third balance sheet only if material to opening position (1.40A)
Correcting a misclassification of a loan as non-current in the prior year Error under Ind AS 8 (presentation error) — restate, disclose under 8.49
Change in policy (e.g., cost to revaluation model for land) Policy change, but Ind AS 8.17 requires it to be dealt with as a revaluation under Ind AS 16, not by retrospective restatement
New line item added for a new business No regrouping needed unless comparatives contain similar items

Consistency of presentation (Ind AS 1.45) requires the same presentation and classification from one period to the next unless a significant change in operations makes another presentation more appropriate, or an Ind AS requires a change.

Division I vs Division II vs Division III

Feature Division I Division II Division III
Applies to Companies following Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules 2021 (Indian GAAP) Companies following Ind AS, other than NBFCs NBFCs following Ind AS (Companies (Ind AS) Rules phases for NBFCs)
Balance sheet format Equity and liabilities first: shareholders' funds, share application money, non-current liabilities, current liabilities; then assets Assets first; equity and liabilities next; current/non-current split Liquidity order: financial assets, then non-financial assets; financial liabilities, non-financial liabilities, equity — no current/non-current split on face
Reserves "Reserves and surplus"; P&L debit balance shown as negative in surplus "Other equity" with OCI reserves "Other equity" with OCI reserves
P&L No OCI; extraordinary items and prior period items lines exist OCI with reclassification split; no extraordinary items OCI; revenue lines include interest income, dividend income, fees and commission, net gain on fair value changes
SOCIE Not required (changes shown in reserves note) Required Required
Ratios (2021) 11 analytical ratios 11 analytical ratios CRAR, Tier I CRAR, Tier II CRAR, liquidity coverage ratio
Maturity Current/non-current on face Current/non-current on face Maturity analysis in notes (Ind AS 1.61)

The 2021 amendments added common disclosures across all three divisions (promoter shareholding, title deeds, struck-off companies, crypto, ageing where relevant). Division III also prescribes NBFC-specific lines ("Derivative financial instruments", "Receivables" split into trade and other receivables, "Debt securities", "Borrowings (other than debt securities)", "Deposits", "Subordinated liabilities").

Consolidating across Divisions

A Division II parent consolidating a Division III NBFC subsidiary presents the CFS in Division II format (the parent's format), mapping the NBFC's liquidity-order balances into current/non-current using the NBFC's maturity analysis. ICAI guidance on Divisions II and III indicates that the CFS follows the division applicable to the parent, so an NBFC parent presents a Division III CFS even with manufacturing subsidiaries. An Indian GAAP subsidiary of an Ind AS parent prepares its own Division I statements but reports Ind AS-adjusted figures for consolidation.

Worked illustration: regrouping disclosure

FY 2024-25 published balance sheet showed "Other current financial liabilities" ₹184 crore including current maturities of ₹62 crore and interest accrued ₹4 crore. In FY 2025-26, the company correctly presents current maturities within current borrowings and restates the comparative.

Line (31 March 2025) As reported Reclassification As reclassified
Current borrowings 95 62 157
Other current financial liabilities 184 (62) 122

Disclosure: "Current maturities of long-term borrowings of ₹62 crore have been reclassified from other financial liabilities to current borrowings to comply with Schedule III; there is no effect on total current liabilities, equity or profit."

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Changing the rounding unit (₹ lakh to ₹ crore) when total income crosses ₹100 crore: permitted; restate comparatives in the new unit.
  • Interim results under SEBI formats follow the same rounding unit as the annual statements for consistency.
  • Reclassification that changes current ratio across a covenant threshold: still a presentation change, but highlight for lenders.
  • Holding company that is a Core Investment Company (an NBFC) consolidating manufacturing subsidiaries: CFS in Division III format, with maturity analysis.
  • Comparatives of first Ind AS financial statements follow Ind AS 101 (opening balance sheet at transition date).

Key takeaways

  • Rounding depends on total income: below ₹100 crore any of hundreds/thousands/lakhs/millions; at or above ₹100 crore only lakhs/millions/crores; one unit throughout.
  • Reclassified comparatives require nature, amount and reason (Ind AS 1.41); a generic regrouping footnote is insufficient for material changes.
  • Distinguish reclassification (presentation change) from error correction (Ind AS 8) when comparatives move.
  • Division I is Indian GAAP; Division II is Ind AS non-NBFC; Division III is Ind AS NBFC with liquidity-order presentation.
  • CFS follows the parent's division; map subsidiary formats accordingly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rounding choice [L1]

A company's total income was ₹96 crore in FY 2024-25 and ₹112 crore in FY 2025-26. It has always presented in ₹ thousands. What rounding options are available for FY 2025-26 and how must the comparative be presented?

Q2. Regroup or restate? [L2]

For each change identified in FY 2025-26 accounts, decide whether it is a reclassification under Ind AS 1.41 or a correction of an error under Ind AS 8, and draft the disclosure: (a) security deposits from dealers of ₹28 crore, refundable on demand, were shown as non-current in FY 2024-25; (b) the company now presents export incentives (₹14 crore) within "other operating revenue" instead of "other income" to align with peer practice; (c) CSR expense of ₹3 crore was netted against revenue last year.

💡 Hint: Ask whether the earlier presentation complied with the framework at the time it was made.

Q3. Converting an NBFC subsidiary [L2]

An NBFC subsidiary's Division III balance sheet shows (₹ crore): loans 1,200 (maturity < 12 months: 450); investments 300 (all FVTPL, held for trading); debt securities 700 (due < 12 months: 150); borrowings other than debt securities 400 (due < 12 months: 220); deposits 50 (all > 12 months). Map these into the Division II consolidated balance sheet of its manufacturing parent.

Q4. Rounding plug policy [L3]

An auditor objects that your notes have rounding differences of up to ₹0.02 crore between note totals and face figures. Design a written rounding policy and an Excel mechanism that guarantees every note total equals the face figure while keeping line-level accuracy, and explain the trade-offs.
