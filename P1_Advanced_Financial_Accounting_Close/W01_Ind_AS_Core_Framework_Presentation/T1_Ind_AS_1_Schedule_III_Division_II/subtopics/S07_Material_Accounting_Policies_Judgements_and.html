Subtopic note

Material Accounting Policies, Judgements and Estimation Uncertainty

Scope: Material accounting policy information (Ind AS 1.117, 2023 amendment), significant judgements (1.122) and sources of estimation uncertainty (1.125)

Why it matters

Policy notes in Indian annual reports have historically run to twenty pages of text copied from the standards. The 2023 amendment to Ind AS 1 — notified by MCA through the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Amendment Rules 2023, effective for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023 — replaced "significant accounting policies" with material accounting policy information. Alongside, Ind AS 1.122 (judgements) and 1.125 (estimation uncertainty) are the disclosures that tell users where the numbers are soft. NFRA's audit quality reviews and SEBI's observations repeatedly flag generic disclosures. In an FP&A or technical accounting role, you will draft or review these notes; in valuation work, the 1.125 note is where you find the assumptions (discount rates, growth rates, useful lives) that drive impairment and fair values.

Core notes

Material accounting policy information (Ind AS 1.117–117E)

Ind AS 1.117 now requires an entity to disclose its material accounting policy information. Accounting policy information is material if, when considered together with other information in the financial statements, it can reasonably be expected to influence decisions that primary users make on the basis of those statements.

  • 117A — Accounting policy information relating to immaterial transactions, other events or conditions is immaterial and need not be disclosed. Information may nevertheless be material because of the nature of the related transactions even if amounts are immaterial.
  • 117B — Accounting policy information is likely to be material if it relates to material transactions and, for example: (a) the entity changed its policy during the period resulting in a material change; (b) it chose the policy from options permitted by Ind AS (cost vs revaluation model, FVOCI election, NCI measurement); (c) it developed the policy under Ind AS 8.10–11 in the absence of a specific Ind AS; (d) it relates to an area requiring significant judgements or assumptions disclosed under 1.122 or 1.125; or (e) the accounting is complex and users would otherwise not understand the transactions (for example, multiple standards applied to one class of transactions).
  • 117C — Information that focuses on how the entity has applied the requirements to its own circumstances is more useful than standardised information or a summary of Ind AS requirements.
  • 117D — If immaterial policy information is disclosed, it must not obscure material information.
  • 117E — A conclusion that policy information is immaterial does not affect related disclosure requirements of other Ind AS.

IFRS Practice Statement 2 Making Materiality Judgements is a useful reference, although it is not notified and not mandatory in India. A practical four-step process: identify material transactions and balances; identify policies relating to them; assess whether each policy meets 117B indicators; draft entity-specific text and delete standard-restating paragraphs.

Boilerplate vs entity-specific: an example

Boilerplate (delete) Entity-specific (keep)
"Revenue is recognised when control of goods or services is transferred to the customer at an amount that reflects the consideration expected." "Revenue from EPC contracts for solar plants is recognised over time using cost-to-cost, excluding the cost of modules and inverters until installation; module costs are recognised at zero margin on delivery to site. Liquidated damages for expected delays are deducted from the transaction price when the delay is assessed as more likely than not."
"Inventories are valued at lower of cost and NRV." "Finished goods of seasonal apparel are written down to 40% of cost after one season and to 10% after two seasons, based on realisation data from outlet sales."

Significant judgements (Ind AS 1.122)

Ind AS 1.122 requires disclosure, along with the policy information or elsewhere, of the judgements, apart from those involving estimations, that management has made in applying accounting policies and that have the most significant effect on the amounts recognised. Examples (1.123): whether financial assets are held-to-collect; when substantially all significant risks and rewards of ownership of financial assets and lease assets are transferred; whether sales are financing arrangements; whether contractual terms give rise to solely payments of principal and interest. Indian practice examples:

  • control of a 50:50 entity (subsidiary vs joint venture under Ind AS 110/111);
  • principal vs agent for an e-commerce marketplace;
  • whether implementation services are distinct from SaaS;
  • lease term judgement for renewable rental agreements (Ind AS 116);
  • operating cycle determination for a developer;
  • classification of a breached loan as non-current under the Ind AS 1.74 carve-out;
  • going concern conclusion where material uncertainty was considered but not concluded.

Sources of estimation uncertainty (Ind AS 1.125)

Ind AS 1.125 requires disclosure of the assumptions about the future, and other major sources of estimation uncertainty at the end of the reporting period, that have a significant risk of resulting in a material adjustment to the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities within the next financial year. For those assets and liabilities, disclose (a) their nature and (b) their carrying amount at the end of the period. Ind AS 1.129 lists the types of disclosures: nature of the assumption or uncertainty; sensitivity of carrying amounts to methods, assumptions and estimates, including reasons; expected resolution and range of reasonably possible outcomes within the next year; and changes to past assumptions if the uncertainty remains unresolved. Ind AS 1.128 exempts assets and liabilities measured at fair value based on quoted prices in active markets. Ind AS 1.130 does not require budget or forecast disclosure.

Key words: "significant risk", "material adjustment" and "within the next financial year". A 30-year decommissioning provision whose discount rate could change materially next year qualifies; a judgement about something that cannot change next year does not belong in 1.125.

Worked illustration: sensitivity disclosure

Goodwill of ₹640 crore on the acquisition of a pharma formulations business; recoverable amount (value in use) ₹710 crore; headroom ₹70 crore. Pre-tax discount rate 13.5%, terminal growth 5%.

Assumption change Effect on headroom
Discount rate +1% headroom falls by ₹92 crore → impairment of ₹22 crore
Terminal growth −0.5% headroom falls by ₹38 crore
EBITDA margin −2% headroom falls by ₹55 crore

Ind AS 36.134(f) separately requires the change in key assumptions that would make recoverable amount equal carrying amount. Disclosing both satisfies 1.125 and Ind AS 36.

Placement and cross-referencing

Policy information sits in Note 2 or 3 ("Material accounting policy information"), judgements and estimates in a dedicated note ("Critical accounting judgements and key sources of estimation uncertainty"), with cross-references to the detailed notes (revenue, impairment, employee benefits, provisions, tax). Avoid restating the same estimate in three places.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Changing from "significant" to "material" is not a change in accounting policy; it is a change in disclosure — no Ind AS 8 restatement.
  • Immaterial amounts, material nature: a related-party policy or a policy on crypto holdings may be material even if amounts are small (117A).
  • Judgement vs estimate overlap: lease term involves judgement (reasonably certain) and estimation; disclose under the dominant character and cross-refer.
  • Key audit matters in the auditor's report (SA 701) that are not reflected in 1.122/1.125 notes signal a disclosure gap.
  • Interim reports (Ind AS 34) normally state that the same policies, judgements and estimates apply as in the annual statements, plus changes.
  • Sensitivity that cannot be quantified — disclose the range and reasons instead of omitting.

Key takeaways

  • Since the 2023 amendment, Ind AS 1.117 requires material accounting policy information — entity-specific, not standard-restating.
  • Policy information is likely material when it involves a choice, a change, a developed policy, significant judgement or complexity (117B).
  • Ind AS 1.122 covers judgements other than estimates with the most significant effect on recognised amounts.
  • Ind AS 1.125 covers estimation uncertainty with significant risk of material adjustment within the next year; disclose nature, carrying amount and sensitivity.
  • Boilerplate must not obscure material information (117D); review notes against KAMs and material balances.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rewrite a policy note [L2]

A SaaS company's revenue policy reads: "Revenue is measured at the fair value of consideration received or receivable, net of discounts and GST, and is recognised when control is transferred." Its contracts include 3-year subscriptions billed annually in advance, implementation services (judged distinct) priced at 20% of year-one fees, and a usage-based overage fee. Rewrite the note as material accounting policy information, identifying which 117B indicators apply.

Q2. Judgement or estimate? [L1]

Classify each item as a 1.122 judgement, a 1.125 estimation uncertainty, both, or neither: (a) determining that a 26% investee is an associate; (b) the ECL loss rate for receivables over 180 days; (c) useful life of telecom towers; (d) deciding a breached loan stays non-current under the carve-out; (e) the actuarial discount rate for gratuity; (f) fair value of quoted equity investments.

💡 Hint: For 1.125, ask whether the carrying amount could change materially within the next financial year.

Q3. Sensitivity drafting [L2]

A company carries a warranty provision of ₹48 crore based on a claim rate of 1.8% of sales and an average cost per claim of ₹4,200. Sales covered by warranty are ₹2,600 crore. Draft the 1.125 disclosure with a sensitivity table for ±0.2% in claim rate and ±10% in cost per claim.

Q4. Policy note audit [L3]

You are the incoming financial controller of a listed auto ancillary. The existing policy note is 28 pages long. Describe how you would apply Ind AS 1.117–117E to reduce it, the evidence you would assemble for the audit committee, and how you would ensure that the reduction does not remove information required by other Ind AS.
