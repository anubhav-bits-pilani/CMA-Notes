Subtopic note

SOCIE Construction in Schedule III Division II Format

Scope: SOCIE (Ind AS 1.106) in Schedule III format: equity share capital and other equity columns — securities premium, retained earnings, OCI reserves, FCTR, share warrants

Why it matters

The statement of changes in equity (SOCIE) is where restatements, dividends, buybacks, ESOP charges and OCI movements become visible. It is the statement most often built last and checked least — and therefore where tie-out errors hide: OCI in the SOCIE not matching OCI in the P&L, dividends not matching the cash flow statement, or securities premium used for purposes Sec 52 does not permit. In a three-statement model, the SOCIE is the bridge that rolls equity forward; an analyst reconstructing distributable reserves or dividend capacity reads it column by column.

Core notes

Ind AS 1.106 requirements

Ind AS 1.106 requires the SOCIE to present:

  • (a) total comprehensive income for the period, showing separately the totals attributable to owners of the parent and to NCI;
  • (b) for each component of equity, the effects of retrospective application or retrospective restatement recognised under Ind AS 8;
  • (d) for each component of equity, a reconciliation between the opening and closing carrying amounts, separately disclosing changes from (i) profit or loss, (ii) OCI, and (iii) transactions with owners in their capacity as owners, showing separately contributions by and distributions to owners and changes in ownership interests in subsidiaries that do not result in loss of control.

Ind AS 1.106A requires an analysis of OCI by item for each component of equity (in the SOCIE or notes). Ind AS 1.107 requires dividends recognised as distributions and the related amount per share (in the SOCIE or notes).

Schedule III format — Part A: equity share capital

Schedule III (as amended in 2021) prescribes two tables, for the current and the previous reporting period:

Balance at the beginning Changes in equity share capital due to prior period errors Restated balance at the beginning Changes in equity share capital during the year Balance at the end

"Changes during the year" covers fresh issues, bonus issues, ESOP allotments, conversions and buybacks (with extinguishment of shares under Sec 68).

Part B: other equity — the columns

The columns for other equity are:

  1. Share application money pending allotment
  2. Equity component of compound financial instruments
  3. Reserves and surplus: capital reserve; securities premium; other reserves (specify nature — general reserve, capital redemption reserve, debenture redemption reserve, share-based payment reserve); retained earnings
  4. Debt instruments through OCI
  5. Equity instruments through OCI
  6. Effective portion of cash flow hedges
  7. Revaluation surplus
  8. Exchange differences on translating the financial statements of a foreign operation (FCTR)
  9. Other items of OCI (specify nature)
  10. Money received against share warrants
  11. Total

The rows are: balance at the beginning of the reporting period; changes in accounting policy or prior period errors; restated balance at the beginning; total comprehensive income for the year; dividends; transfer to retained earnings; any other change (to be specified); balance at the end.

Columns may be omitted when nil in both periods; the CFS adds "attributable to owners of the parent" and "non-controlling interests" totals.

Securities premium

Under Sec 52 of the Companies Act 2013, premium received on issue of shares is transferred to securities premium and may be applied only for: issuing fully paid bonus shares; writing off preliminary expenses; writing off expenses of, or commission paid or discount allowed on, any issue of shares or debentures; providing for the premium payable on redemption of preference shares or debentures; and buying back shares under Sec 68. Under Ind AS, share issue costs (net of tax) are deducted from equity (Ind AS 32.37) — usually from securities premium. Sec 52(3) empowers the Central Government to confine a prescribed class of companies (whose financial statements comply with notified standards) to a narrower list — bonus shares, share-issue expenses and buybacks; check whether any class has been prescribed for your reporting date, because that restriction would bite on Ind AS companies.

Retained earnings

Retained earnings absorb profit for the period, remeasurements of defined benefit plans (many Indian companies show remeasurements as a separate "other items of OCI" column, others route them directly into retained earnings — both are seen; disclose the policy), dividends, transfers from OCI reserves on disposal of FVOCI equity, transfers from revaluation surplus, and restatement adjustments. Under Ind AS, the transition adjustments on first-time adoption also sit here (with separate disclosure of the Ind AS 101 effect in some companies).

OCI reserves and FCTR

Each OCI reserve column moves only through the OCI row (net of tax), reclassification to P&L (also via OCI, as a negative OCI amount), or transfers within equity. FCTR arises only in consolidated (or branch) financial statements under Ind AS 21.39(c); it is recycled to P&L on disposal of the foreign operation (21.48) and partially attributed to NCI. Indian companies that elected the Ind AS 101 D13 exemption reset cumulative translation differences to zero at transition.

Money received against share warrants

Under SEBI ICDR preferential issue rules, at least 25% of the warrant price is paid upfront and the balance on exercise within 18 months. Upfront money is equity (warrants for a fixed number of shares at a fixed price meet the Ind AS 32 fixed-for-fixed test), shown in its own column; on exercise it moves to share capital and securities premium; on lapse it is forfeited — typically transferred to capital reserve.

Excel implementation

Build the SOCIE as a matrix: rows = movement types, columns = components. Each cell links to a source schedule (P&L, OCI schedule, dividend register, share register). Checks:

=SOCIE_OCI_Total - PL_OCI_Line              ' must be 0
=SOCIE_Closing_Total + Share_Capital_Close - BS_Total_Equity   ' must be 0
=SOCIE_Dividends + CFF_Dividends_Paid + Change_Unpaid_Dividend ' must be 0

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Interim dividend declared and paid in the year is shown in the dividends row; a final dividend proposed after year-end is not (Ind AS 10.12).
  • Buyback: reduce share capital (face value), utilise securities premium or free reserves for the premium, create a capital redemption reserve equal to face value of shares bought back out of free reserves (Sec 69); buyback tax treatment depends on the date — buybacks after 1 October 2024 are taxed in shareholders' hands as deemed dividend, before that under Sec 115QA in the company's hands.
  • ESOP: share-based payment reserve credited through P&L; on exercise transferred to securities premium; on lapse after vesting, transferred to general reserve or retained earnings (policy).
  • Transactions with NCI without loss of control go to equity (Ind AS 110.23) — a separate "changes in ownership" row.
  • Dividend distribution tax no longer applies (abolished from 1 April 2020); older comparatives may show it.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 1.106 requires TCI, retrospective effects and a full reconciliation of each equity component.
  • Schedule III fixes columns (securities premium, retained earnings, each OCI reserve, FCTR, share warrants) and rows (including a prior period errors row).
  • Securities premium can be used only for Sec 52 purposes; share issue costs reduce equity under Ind AS 32.37.
  • OCI reserves move through OCI or transfers within equity — never directly through P&L.
  • Tie the SOCIE to the P&L (OCI), balance sheet (closing equity) and cash flow statement (dividends, share issues).

Practice Questions

Q1. Full SOCIE build [L2]

Opening balances at 1 April 2025 (₹ crore): share capital 30; securities premium 90; capital redemption reserve 5; retained earnings 140; FVOCI equity reserve 7; FCTR 3. FY 2025-26: PAT 48 (owners 44, NCI 4); actuarial loss net of tax 2; FVOCI gain net of tax 6; FCTR gain 4 (owners 3, NCI 1); bonus issue 1:3 from securities premium; interim dividend ₹1.50 per share on post-bonus shares (face value ₹10); share issue expenses ₹0.8 crore pre-tax on bonus issue. Prepare both parts of the SOCIE in Schedule III format for the consolidated statements.

💡 Hint: Compute the post-bonus share count first; remember the NCI column in the CFS.

Q2. Warrants lapse [L1]

A company received ₹40 crore as 25% upfront money on preferential warrants. Holders of 30% of the warrants did not exercise within 18 months. Pass entries for exercise and lapse and show the SOCIE rows affected.

Q3. Tie-out failure [L2]

A reviewer finds that OCI in the SOCIE is ₹4.2 crore but the P&L shows OCI of ₹5.0 crore, and dividends in the SOCIE (₹18 crore) differ from dividends paid in the cash flow statement (₹17.4 crore). List possible causes for each difference and the evidence you would collect.

Q4. Use of securities premium [L3]

The board of an Ind AS company proposes to use ₹25 crore of securities premium to (a) write off unamortised expenses of a share issue, (b) fund a buyback premium, (c) absorb accumulated losses and (d) pay a special dividend. Evaluate each against Sec 52, Sec 68 and any capital-reduction route under Sec 66, and state the SOCIE presentation for the permitted uses.
