How this week fits

This is the opening week of Phase 1: Advanced Financial Accounting & Close Mastery. Your audit and Tally background means you already know how a voucher becomes a ledger balance; this week moves one level up, to how ledger balances become Ind AS financial statements — classified, presented, restated where necessary — and how the single largest line in those statements, revenue, is measured under Ind AS 115.

The week runs in a deliberate order. Topic 1 (Ind AS 1 and Schedule III Division II) gives you the "chassis": the balance sheet hierarchy, current/non-current tests, OCI split and SOCIE. Topic 2 (Ind AS 8 and Ind AS 10) gives you the correction and cut-off toolkit used at every year-end close. Topics 3 and 4 (Ind AS 115) build the revenue engine, from contract identification to contract balances. Topic 5 covers inventory NRV and EPS, and Topic 6 closes with the indirect cash flow statement that proves the other statements tie together.

Prerequisites: CMA Final Corporate Financial Reporting, working Excel. What comes next: Week 2 adds leases, PPE and provisions (useful-life reassessment, onerous contracts), Week 3 adds financial instruments and deferred tax (ECL on contract assets, ICDS timing differences), and Week 4 builds a TB-to-Schedule III mapping engine that reuses the line items and current/non-current flags set up this week.

Refresh before starting

CMA Final CFR: framework, Ind AS 1/8/10 basics, consolidation

Recall the Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting under Ind AS (ICAI): the objective is useful information for primary users (existing and potential investors, lenders, other creditors); fundamental qualitative characteristics are relevance (incl. materiality) and faithful representation; enhancing ones are comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability. Elements: asset (present economic resource controlled as a result of past events), liability (present obligation to transfer an economic resource), equity (residual), income, expenses. Recall the Ind AS 1 general features — fair presentation, going concern, accrual, materiality and aggregation, offsetting, frequency, comparatives, consistency. Recall Ind AS 8's three buckets (policy, estimate, error) and Ind AS 10's adjusting/non-adjusting split. For consolidation, recall Ind AS 110 control (power, exposure to variable returns, ability to use power), NCI measurement choices, elimination of intra-group balances and unrealised profit, and that the CFS is prepared under Sec 129(3).

Indian GAAP AS 5, AS 7, AS 9, AS 3 — what to unlearn

  • AS 5: prior-period items were passed through the current P&L as a separate line. Under Ind AS 8.42 material errors are corrected retrospectively via opening retained earnings and restated comparatives — never in current P&L.
  • AS 7: percentage-of-completion with contract combining/segmenting rules and recognition of foreseeable losses. Under Ind AS 115 you first ask whether there is a contract, how many performance obligations exist, and whether control transfers over time (115.35); cost-to-cost is only one measure of progress, and uninstalled materials are excluded.
  • AS 9: revenue on transfer of significant risks and rewards. Ind AS 115 uses transfer of control; risks and rewards survive only as one indicator in 115.38.
  • AS 3: the cash flow statement logic is similar, but Ind AS 7 adds the financing-liabilities reconciliation (7.44A–44E), explicit treatment of bank overdrafts as cash equivalents (7.8) and Ind AS 116 lease flows.

Companies (Ind AS) Rules 2015 applicability; Sec 129 and Sec 133

Phase I (FY beginning 1 April 2016): companies (listed or unlisted) with net worth ≥ ₹500 crore, plus their holding, subsidiary, joint venture and associate companies. Phase II (1 April 2017): all companies listed or in the process of listing on a stock exchange in India or outside (other than SME exchanges), unlisted companies with net worth ≥ ₹250 crore, plus their holding/subsidiary/JV/associate companies. NBFCs followed separate phases from 1 April 2018 and 1 April 2019; banks and insurers remain deferred. Net worth is per Sec 2(57), on a standalone basis. Voluntary adoption is permitted but irreversible. Sec 129 requires financial statements to give a true and fair view, comply with notified accounting standards and be in the Schedule III form; Sec 133 empowers the Central Government to prescribe standards recommended by ICAI after examination by NFRA.

Cross-cutting concepts

Revenue, contract balances and working capital as one chain

Every rupee of revenue recognised under Ind AS 115 has a balancing entry that is either cash, a trade receivable, a contract asset or a reduction of a contract liability. Revenue recognised ahead of an unconditional right to bill creates a contract asset; billing converts it into a receivable; cash collection extinguishes the receivable. Billing or cash ahead of performance creates a contract liability. In the indirect cash flow statement, the increase in contract assets and receivables is deducted from profit and the increase in contract liabilities is added. The practical consequence: if your revenue schedule is right but your contract balances are wrong, the cash flow statement will not tie to closing cash. Build the chain in one model so that a single change (for example, a revised total cost estimate) flows to revenue, balance sheet and CFO simultaneously.

\[ \text{Opening contract asset} + \text{Revenue recognised} - \text{Transfers to receivables} = \text{Closing contract asset} \]

Policy, estimate and error lens applied to revenue

Ind AS 8 classification decides where a revenue change lands. A revision of total estimated contract cost in an EPC project is a change in accounting estimate: the measure of progress changes, and Ind AS 115.43 and Ind AS 8.36 require the effect to be recognised in the period of revision through a cumulative catch-up — revenue to date is recomputed at the new percentage and the difference hits current P&L. Switching from output to input methods for a class of contracts is generally a change in estimate too (Ind AS 115.43 treats updates to the measure of progress as estimate changes), whereas a change from recognising revenue at a point in time to over time without a change in facts is usually an error correction. Prior-year revenue recognised on a contract that never met 115.9 is an error, corrected retrospectively with restated comparatives and, if material to the opening position, a third balance sheet.

SaaS exercise: SSP split of ₹2.4 Cr

The SaaS contract bundles a subscription (stand-ready access, satisfied over time on a straight-line basis) and implementation. First test whether implementation is distinct (115.27): if a third party could perform it and it does not significantly customise or integrate the platform, it is a separate PO recognised by effort (hours incurred ÷ total estimated hours). Allocate the ₹2.40 Cr on relative SSP — subscription SSP from observable standalone renewals, implementation SSP by expected cost plus margin — and allocate the bundle discount proportionately unless 115.82 is met. If implementation significantly customises the software, combine the two into one PO recognised ratably from go-live; implementation-period revenue is nil and billings are deferred.

EPC exercise: cost-to-cost, billing, retention, advance, LD, onerous test

An EPC contract on the customer's site is typically an over-time PO under 115.35(b). Progress is measured cost-to-cost, excluding uninstalled materials (B19). Milestone billing seldom matches progress, producing contract assets or liabilities. Retention money withheld is a receivable (or contract asset if conditional on performance) and is usually not a significant financing component (115.62(c)). The mobilisation advance is a contract liability recovered against bills; assess whether it carries a financing component. Liquidated damages are variable consideration that reduce the transaction price, subject to the constraint. At each reporting date test whether total estimated costs exceed the transaction price; if so, recognise the entire expected loss immediately as an Ind AS 37 onerous provision.

Disclosures driven by the model

A well-built waterfall produces disclosures as by-products. Disaggregation (115.114, B87–B89): revenue by type (subscription, implementation, EPC), timing (over time vs point in time) and geography. 126AA reconciliation (India-specific): contracted price → adjustments (discounts, rebates, LDs, bonuses, price concessions) → revenue recognised. Contract balance roll-forward (115.116–118): opening contract assets/liabilities, revenue recognised from opening contract liability, transfers to receivables, cash received, closing balances. Remaining performance obligations (115.120) come from the unrecognised transaction price. Designing the model so these tables are formula-driven avoids the classic year-end exercise of reverse-engineering disclosures from journal entries.

Model hygiene

Separate Inputs (blue font, contract terms, SSPs, budgets, toggles), Calculations (one monthly timeline, consistent columns, no hard-codes) and Outputs (journal entries, BS/P&L extracts, disclosures). Use a single date row built with EOMONTH so every schedule shares columns. Build explicit checks: cumulative revenue ≤ transaction price (per contract and in total); contract asset/liability balance = cumulative revenue − cumulative billing; receivable = cumulative billing − cumulative cash; total of PO allocations = transaction price; allocation percentages sum to 100%. Roll the checks into one master flag (=IF(SUM(Check_Range)=0,"OK","ERROR")) shown on every output tab.
