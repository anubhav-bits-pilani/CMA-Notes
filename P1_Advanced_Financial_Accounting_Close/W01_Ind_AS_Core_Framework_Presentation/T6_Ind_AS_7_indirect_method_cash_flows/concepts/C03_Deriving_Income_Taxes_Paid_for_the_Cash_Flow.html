Key concept

Deriving Income Taxes Paid for the Cash Flow Statement

Concept: Taxes paid = Opening net current tax liability + Current tax expense (P&L) − Closing net current tax liability (exclude deferred and OCI tax)

Statement

\[ T_{\text{paid}} = \text{NCTL}_0 + CT_{\text{P\&L}} - \text{NCTL}_1 \;(\pm \text{adjustments}) \]

where \(\text{NCTL}\) is the net current tax liability (current tax liabilities less current tax assets / advance tax and TDS receivable, including non-current income-tax assets such as disputed tax paid under protest), \(CT_{\text{P\&L}}\) is current tax expense recognised in profit or loss (including adjustments for earlier years), subscript 0 denotes opening and 1 closing. Deferred tax expense and tax recognised in OCI or directly in equity are excluded, except where current tax recognised in OCI/equity is actually paid — then include it consistently. Adjustments include tax balances acquired/disposed with subsidiaries, interest on tax refunds (classified per policy) and FX on foreign tax balances.

Taxes paid are classified as operating unless specifically identified with investing or financing activities (Ind AS 7.35–36).

Explanation

The P&L tax expense is an accrual (current + deferred). Cash tax is what was actually paid (advance tax, TDS deducted by customers, self-assessment tax, tax on regular assessment) less refunds received. A roll-forward of the net current tax position isolates it: opening liability + charge for the year − closing liability = cash paid.

In India, the net current tax position typically comprises:

  • Advance tax and TDS/TCS receivable (current tax assets), often netted against provision for tax where the right of set-off exists (Ind AS 12.71) — same tax authority, same entity, intent to settle net;
  • Provision for income tax for current and earlier years;
  • Income-tax paid under protest / deposits for disputed demands — non-current tax assets (part of the tax roll-forward);
  • Refunds receivable for earlier years.

Deferred tax never touches cash. Current tax in OCI (e.g., on realised FVOCI gains taxed currently) is rare; most OCI tax is deferred.

Why it matters: using total tax expense overstates or understates CFO by the deferred tax charge and by timing differences between accrual and payment. For a company switching to Sec 115BAA, deferred tax remeasurement can be large and non-cash.

Illustration

Krishna Auto Ltd, FY 2024-25 (₹ crore):

Item Opening Closing
Provision for income tax 140 170
Advance tax and TDS (128) (162)
Income-tax paid under protest (non-current asset) (10) (14)
Net current tax liability 2 (6)

P&L: current tax ₹48 crore (including ₹2 crore for earlier years); deferred tax charge ₹9 crore; total tax expense ₹57 crore. OCI deferred tax credit ₹1 crore.

\[ T_{\text{paid}} = 2 + 48 - (-6) = ₹56 \text{ crore} \]

Using total tax expense (₹57 crore) would be wrong by coincidence of ₹1 crore here; using current tax alone (₹48 crore) would understate taxes paid by ₹8 crore. The ₹4 crore increase in tax paid under protest is cash paid to the department and is included.

Connections

A component of the CFO formula (c01) and a topic pitfall ("using total tax expense as taxes paid"). Current and deferred tax mechanics are covered in Week 3 (Ind AS 12); the offsetting criteria determine how balances are presented. In FP&A, cash tax forecasts use advance tax instalments (15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December, 100% by 15 March) — the same roll-forward drives the quarterly cash forecast. Valuation FCFF uses cash taxes (or operating taxes on EBIT), not book tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic derivation [L1]

Opening provision for tax ₹30 crore and advance tax ₹26 crore; closing provision ₹45 crore and advance tax ₹41 crore. Current tax expense ₹19 crore; deferred tax credit ₹3 crore. Compute taxes paid.

Q2. Refunds and acquired balances [L2]

Net current tax liability: opening ₹12 crore, closing ₹8 crore. Current tax expense ₹60 crore including reversal of ₹5 crore excess provision of earlier years. A subsidiary acquired during the year brought a current tax liability of ₹3 crore. A refund of ₹7 crore relating to FY 2020-21 was received with interest of ₹0.8 crore (credited to other income). Compute taxes paid and state where the interest received is classified.

💡 Hint: Adjust the roll-forward for balances that arrived with the acquisition before solving for cash.

Q3. Model formula [L2]

Write the Excel formula for taxes paid using named ranges for the opening and closing tax balances and current tax expense, and design a check that compares the result with the sum of advance tax challans and TDS credits per Form 26AS/AIS less refunds received.
