Key concept

Cash Capex: From Asset Additions to Investing Outflow

Concept: Capex cash = PPE/CWIP/intangible additions − Non-cash additions (ROU, decommissioning) − ΔCapex creditors + ΔCapital advances

Statement

\[ \text{Capex}_{\text{cash}} = \text{Add}_{\text{PPE+CWIP+Intang}} - \text{NC} - \Delta CC + \Delta CA \]

where \(\text{Add}\) is total additions to PPE, capital work-in-progress and intangible assets (including intangibles under development) per the asset roll-forward notes, avoiding double counting of CWIP capitalised into PPE; \(\text{NC}\) is non-cash additions — ROU assets under Ind AS 116 (if presented within PPE), decommissioning/restoration assets (Ind AS 16.16(c)), assets acquired through business combinations, assets acquired for shares or by assuming liabilities, capitalised non-cash items (e.g., unrealised FX on borrowings capitalised, share-based payment capitalised) and, depending on policy, capitalised borrowing costs presented within interest paid; \(\Delta CC\) is the increase in capex creditors (payables for capital goods); and \(\Delta CA\) is the increase in capital advances. The result is presented as "Purchase of property, plant and equipment and intangible assets" in investing activities (Ind AS 7.16(a)).

Explanation

The asset note records additions when assets are recognised; the cash flow records payments. Three gaps arise:

  1. Non-cash additions — assets recognised with no cash paid (ROU assets, decommissioning assets, assets acquired with a subsidiary or for shares).
  2. Timing of payment — capex creditors. An asset added in March but paid in May increases capex creditors; cash paid this year is lower than additions. Last year's capex creditors paid this year increase cash capex. Hence subtract the increase in capex creditors.
  3. Timing of payment — capital advances. Advances paid to suppliers before delivery are cash outflows not yet in additions. Hence add the increase in capital advances.

CWIP double counting: if CWIP additions are taken and then PPE additions include transfers from CWIP, the same spend is counted twice. Use either PPE additions excluding CWIP transfers plus CWIP additions, or gross block movement plus disposals.

Capitalised borrowing costs: Ind AS 7.32 requires total interest paid to be disclosed whether expensed or capitalised. If the policy shows capitalised interest in interest paid (financing), remove it from capex; if in capex (investing), do not include it in interest paid.

Proceeds from disposal are presented separately (gross) — never netted against capex.

Illustration

Mahanadi Cement Ltd, FY 2024-25 (₹ crore):

Item Amount
PPE additions (excluding transfers from CWIP) 140
CWIP additions during the year 220
Intangible additions 10
Total additions 370
Of which: ROU assets within PPE (25)
Of which: mine restoration asset (12)
Of which: capitalised borrowing costs (shown in interest paid) (18)
Capex creditors: opening 45, closing 70 → increase (25)
Capital advances: opening 30, closing 22 → decrease (8)
Purchase of PPE and intangibles (investing) 282

\[ \text{Capex}_{\text{cash}} = 370 - 25 - 12 - 18 - 25 + (-8) = 282 \]

Using note additions (₹370 crore) would overstate investing outflows by ₹88 crore; ₹25 crore of capex creditors would wrongly sit in operating payables, overstating CFO.

Connections

Implements the exclusions in s02 (capex creditors, capital advances) and s06 (non-cash ROU and decommissioning additions), and the interest policy in s03. It is the second topic pitfall ("ROU additions or unpaid capex as cash investing outflows"). In FP&A, capex budgets are tracked on a commitment, capitalisation and cash basis — the same bridge reconciles them. FCFF uses cash capex.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute cash capex [L1]

Additions: PPE ₹90 crore (excluding CWIP transfers), CWIP ₹60 crore, intangibles ₹5 crore. ROU additions in PPE ₹15 crore. Capex creditors increased by ₹12 crore; capital advances increased by ₹7 crore. Compute cash capex.

Q2. Gross block approach [L2]

Gross block (PPE + CWIP + intangibles): opening ₹2,000 crore, closing ₹2,380 crore. Disposals at cost ₹70 crore. Assets acquired with a subsidiary ₹110 crore. FX translation of a foreign subsidiary's gross block +₹15 crore. ROU additions ₹40 crore. Capex creditors fell by ₹18 crore; capital advances rose by ₹9 crore. Compute cash capex.

💡 Hint: Rebuild additions from the gross block movement before removing non-cash items.

Q3. Review query [L2]

A draft cash flow shows purchase of PPE ₹510 crore and an increase in trade payables of ₹95 crore in CFO. You find that trade payables include capex creditors that increased by ₹40 crore, and PPE additions include ₹30 crore of a decommissioning asset. Prepare the correcting adjustments and the net effect on CFO and CFI.
