Key concept

Cash Equivalents: The Three-Month and Insignificant-Risk Tests

Concept: Cash equivalents: short-term, highly liquid, insignificant value risk — typically ≤3 months maturity from acquisition (Ind AS 7.6–7)

Statement

Cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash and which are subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value (Ind AS 7.6). They are held for the purpose of meeting short-term cash commitments rather than for investment or other purposes (7.7). An investment normally qualifies as a cash equivalent only when it has a short maturity of, say, three months or less from the date of acquisition. Equity investments are excluded unless they are, in substance, cash equivalents (e.g., preference shares acquired close to a specified redemption date) (7.7).

All four conditions must hold:

  1. short-term (≈ ≤3 months original maturity);
  2. highly liquid / readily convertible;
  3. to a known amount of cash;
  4. insignificant risk of changes in value — and held for cash-management purposes.

Explanation

The three-month guide is measured from the date of acquisition, not from the reporting date. A 12-month FD bought 11 months ago does not become a cash equivalent because only one month remains; it stays in "other bank balances". This prevents reclassification games around year-end and keeps the cash boundary stable.

"Known amounts of cash" and "insignificant risk of changes in value" exclude instruments whose value fluctuates with market rates or credit — equity shares, most debt mutual funds, long-dated government securities. Liquid and overnight mutual funds in India are debated: they are redeemable at T+1 with low volatility but at NAV (not a known amount), so many Indian companies classify them as current investments at FVTPL; others treat overnight funds as cash equivalents with a disclosed policy. FDs that can be broken prematurely with a penalty remain classified by original maturity.

Purpose matters: an investment with a 2-month maturity held as part of a long-term investment strategy (e.g., rolling T-bills in a treasury portfolio that is never used for operating needs) may not be a cash equivalent. Restricted balances (lien-marked FDs, escrow) fail the cash-management purpose test.

Classification consequences:

  • Purchases and redemptions of cash equivalents are not cash flows — they are movements within C&CE (7.9).
  • Purchases and maturities of non-cash-equivalent deposits and investments are investing cash flows.
  • A change in the composition policy (e.g., including liquid funds) is a change in accounting policy under Ind AS 8 (7.47).

Illustration

Tungabhadra Ltd, 31 March 2025:

Instrument Acquired Maturity Classification
FD ₹10 crore 15 Jan 2025 15 Apr 2025 (90 days) Cash equivalent
FD ₹25 crore 1 Jun 2024 31 May 2025 (12 months) Other bank balance — not C&CE although maturing in 2 months
91-day T-bill ₹8 crore 10 Feb 2025 12 May 2025 Cash equivalent (if held for cash management)
Overnight fund ₹15 crore Rolling — Policy choice — typically current investment (FVTPL); disclose
Listed equity shares ₹5 crore — — Not a cash equivalent
FD ₹4 crore under lien for BG 1 Mar 2025 31 May 2025 Other bank balance (restricted)

C&CE = cash and bank current accounts + ₹10 crore + ₹8 crore (+ overnight fund only if the policy includes it).

Connections

Defines the boundary used in s05 (overdrafts, earmarked balances) and the cash proof in c06. Movements across the boundary appear in investing (s05) — e.g., "investment in bank deposits not considered cash equivalents". In treasury and FP&A, the classification affects reported "cash" in investor decks versus "cash and investments" in net-debt calculations — always reconcile the two in analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify deposits [L1]

At 31 March 2025, classify: (i) FD of ₹6 crore placed on 5 February 2025 for 60 days; (ii) FD of ₹9 crore placed on 1 December 2024 for 180 days; (iii) a 364-day T-bill bought on 20 March 2025; (iv) sweep-in deposits from a current account redeemable on demand; (v) commercial paper of a AAA corporate bought 2 March 2025 maturing 30 May 2025.

Q2. Liquid fund policy [L2]

A company holds ₹200 crore in liquid mutual funds used for daily working-capital management; NAV volatility over the past year has been below 0.1% per month, but the fund holds commercial paper and T-bills of up to 91 days. Evaluate the 7.6–7.7 criteria and recommend a classification policy with disclosure wording.

💡 Hint: Focus on whether redemption gives a "known amount" and on the purpose of holding.

Q3. Year-end window dressing [L2]

A company breaks ₹50 crore of 1-year FDs on 30 March 2025 and places ₹50 crore in 30-day FDs on 31 March 2025, then reverses the move in April. Analyse the cash flow statement effect, the reasons this might be done, and what an auditor should consider.
