Key concept

Working Capital Sign Rule in the Indirect Method

Concept: ΔWC sign rule: increase in operating asset = outflow; increase in operating liability = inflow

Statement

For each operating working-capital balance \(B\) with opening value \(B_0\) and closing value \(B_1\) (after removing acquired/disposed balances, FX translation and non-cash reclassifications):

\[ \text{CFO effect of operating asset} = -(A_1 - A_0) \]

\[ \text{CFO effect of operating liability} = +(L_1 - L_0) \]

An increase in an operating asset (receivables, contract assets, inventories, prepaid expenses, operating advances) is a use of cash and is deducted; a decrease is added. An increase in an operating liability (trade payables, contract liabilities, provisions, statutory dues, customer advances) is a source of cash and is added; a decrease is deducted.

Explanation

Profit is measured on accruals. If revenue of ₹100 is recognised but receivables rise by ₹30, only ₹70 was collected — so the increase is subtracted. If purchases of ₹80 are expensed (through COGS) but payables rise by ₹20, only ₹60 was paid — so the increase is added. Inventory works through COGS: if inventory rises, cash was spent on goods not yet expensed — subtract.

The rule follows from the balance sheet identity: since cash = liabilities + equity − non-cash assets, any increase in a non-cash asset (holding other things equal) reduces cash, and any increase in a liability increases cash.

Conditions:

  • Apply only to operating balances — capex creditors, capital advances, intercompany loans, interest accrued, dividend payable and tax balances are excluded (they have their own lines).
  • Use movements net of non-cash changes — acquisitions/disposals of subsidiaries, FX translation of foreign operations, reclassifications (e.g., current maturities), OCI remeasurements of employee benefit liabilities.
  • For allowance-based balances, be consistent with the non-cash adjustment (ECL add-back with gross receivables; no add-back with net receivables).
  • In a model with positive = inflow convention, assets use =-(Close-Open) and liabilities =(Close-Open).

A useful mnemonic: assets and cash move in opposite directions; liabilities and cash move in the same direction.

Illustration

Chambal Textiles Ltd (₹ crore):

Balance Opening Closing Movement CFO effect
Trade receivables (gross) 120 145 +25 asset (25)
Contract assets 10 6 −4 asset 4
Inventories 200 185 −15 asset 15
Prepaid expenses 5 7 +2 asset (2)
Trade payables (excl. capex creditors) 90 112 +22 liability 22
Advances from customers 14 9 −5 liability (5)
Provision for warranty (movement approach) 6 8 +2 liability 2
Net ΔWC effect 11

The positive ₹11 crore means working capital released cash during the year.

Connections

Applied in s02 (which balances are operating and what to exclude) and within the CFO formula (c01). The sign rule also drives FP&A working-capital forecasting: rising receivable days (DSO) or inventory days (DIO) reduce cash; rising payable days (DPO) increase it — the cash conversion cycle is the same rule expressed in days. In the revenue waterfall (Topic 4), contract asset increases are outflows and contract liability increases are inflows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the rule [L1]

Opening/closing (₹ crore): receivables 80/70; inventories 60/95; trade payables 50/58; GST input credit 6/9; employee benefit liabilities 12/15 (including OCI remeasurement loss ₹2 crore); customer advances 20/14. Compute each CFO effect and the net working-capital effect.

💡 Hint: Strip the OCI remeasurement out of the employee benefit movement first.

Q2. Days to cash [L2]

Revenue ₹1,460 crore and COGS ₹1,095 crore (365-day year). DSO rises from 45 to 55 days, DIO falls from 60 to 50 days, DPO rises from 40 to 48 days. Compute the closing balances and the cash effect of each working-capital change.

Q3. Interview — negative working capital [L1]

Explain why a company like an FMCG major or a SaaS business with large customer advances can report CFO above EBITDA, using the sign rule, and when this signals risk rather than strength.
