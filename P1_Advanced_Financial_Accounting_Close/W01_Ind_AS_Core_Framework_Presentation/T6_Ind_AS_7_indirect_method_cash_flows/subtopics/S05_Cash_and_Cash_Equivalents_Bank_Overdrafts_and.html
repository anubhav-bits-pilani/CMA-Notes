Subtopic note

Cash and Cash Equivalents, Bank Overdrafts and Other Bank Balances

Scope: Cash and cash equivalents definition, bank overdrafts (7.8), earmarked/unpaid dividend balances in "other bank balances"

Why it matters

The cash flow statement explains the change in one specific number — cash and cash equivalents (C&CE). Where the boundary is drawn determines whether a flow is a cash flow at all: buying a 6-month fixed deposit is an investing outflow, buying a 2-month one is a movement within cash. Indian balance sheets split "cash and cash equivalents" from "bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" (Schedule III), and the cash flow statement must reconcile C&CE to the balance sheet, including bank overdrafts that are deducted for cash flow purposes but presented as borrowings. Treasury, audit and FP&A teams deal with this boundary every close.

Core notes

Definitions (Ind AS 7.6–7.9)

  • Cash comprises cash on hand and demand deposits.
  • Cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash and which are subject to an insignificant risk of changes in value (7.6). They are held to meet short-term cash commitments rather than for investment (7.7). An investment normally qualifies only when it has a short maturity of, say, three months or less from the date of acquisition (c05). Equity investments are excluded unless they are, in substance, cash equivalents (e.g., preference shares acquired within a short period of maturity with a specified redemption date).

Typical Indian items:

Item Usually C&CE? Notes
Cash on hand, current accounts, savings accounts Yes
Fixed deposits with original maturity ≤ 3 months Yes Measured from acquisition, not from reporting date
FDs with original maturity > 3 months but ≤ 12 months No — "other bank balances" Even if maturing within 3 months of reporting date
FDs with remaining maturity > 12 months No — "other financial assets (non-current)"
Liquid / overnight mutual fund units Judgement — many classify as current investments (FVTPL), not C&CE NAV fluctuates; Ind AS 7.7 "known amounts" test; some entities treat overnight funds as cash equivalents — disclose policy
Treasury bills ≤ 91 days Yes, if held for cash management
Cheques in hand / remittances in transit Yes
Earmarked balances (unpaid dividend accounts) No — "other bank balances" Restricted use
Margin money / FDs under lien for bank guarantees, LCs No — "other bank balances" or other financial assets Restricted
Escrow balances (e.g., from QIP proceeds, RERA 70% account) Generally no — restricted Disclose; RERA accounts are restricted to project use

Bank overdrafts (Ind AS 7.8)

Bank borrowings are generally financing activities. However, in some jurisdictions, bank overdrafts repayable on demand form an integral part of an entity's cash management; in these circumstances, bank overdrafts are included as a component of cash and cash equivalents. A characteristic is that the bank balance often fluctuates from positive to overdrawn.

In India:

  • Cash credit (CC) and overdraft facilities repayable on demand that are used for day-to-day cash management are commonly included as a deduction from C&CE in the cash flow statement.
  • Working capital demand loans (WCDL), short-term loans with fixed tenors and packing credit are borrowings — financing activities.
  • On the balance sheet, overdrafts remain within current borrowings (Schedule III; offsetting with cash is prohibited under Ind AS 32.42 unless conditions are met). The cash flow statement therefore needs a reconciliation from C&CE in the cash flow to the balance sheet line (7.45).

Judgement: if the CC account is persistently overdrawn and functions as a revolving loan (never in credit), many argue it is a financing borrowing, not part of cash management. Policies differ; disclose and apply consistently.

Other bank balances (Schedule III)

Schedule III Division II requires "Bank balances other than (iii) above" (i.e., other than cash and cash equivalents) to be shown separately under current financial assets, with sub-disclosure of:

  • earmarked balances with banks (e.g., unpaid dividend accounts — Sec 124 of the Companies Act requires unpaid dividends to be transferred to a special Unpaid Dividend Account within 7 days of the 30-day payment period; after 7 years transfer to IEPF under Sec 125);
  • balances with banks to the extent held as margin money or security against borrowings, guarantees, other commitments;
  • repatriation restrictions, if any, in respect of cash and bank balances;
  • bank deposits with more than 12 months' maturity (presented separately).

Movements between C&CE and other bank balances are investing cash flows (e.g., "investment in / redemption of bank deposits not considered as cash equivalents"). The unpaid dividend account transfer is typically shown in financing together with dividends paid, or as a movement in other bank balances.

Reconciliation and disclosure (Ind AS 7.45–48)

  • Disclose components of C&CE and reconcile amounts in the cash flow statement to the balance sheet (7.45).
  • Disclose the policy for determining the composition of C&CE (7.46); a change in policy is a change in accounting policy under Ind AS 8 (7.47).
  • Disclose significant C&CE balances not available for use by the group, with management commentary (7.48) — e.g., cash in a subsidiary in a country with exchange controls, cash in RERA project accounts.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • FD purchased on 1 February with a 120-day tenor is not a cash equivalent at 31 March even though it matures within 60 days — the test is from acquisition.
  • Sweep-in accounts (auto FDs from savings balance) are usually C&CE because they are redeemable on demand.
  • Cash held by an ESOP trust consolidated by the company — C&CE but restricted; disclose.
  • Cash collected on behalf of others (platform nodal accounts, escrow for sellers) — assess control and restriction; may be excluded from C&CE with a corresponding liability.
  • Book overdraft (cheques issued exceeding bank balance, not a bank overdraft) — a liability (payables), not a reduction in C&CE.
  • Foreign currency cash — translated at closing rate; the FX effect is reported separately (s08).

Key takeaways

  • C&CE = cash + short-term, highly liquid investments with insignificant value risk, typically ≤3 months original maturity.
  • Bank overdrafts repayable on demand that are integral to cash management are deducted from C&CE in the cash flow (7.8) but presented as borrowings in the balance sheet.
  • WCDL and fixed-tenor short-term loans are financing, not cash.
  • Earmarked balances (unpaid dividends), margin money and longer FDs are "other bank balances" — outside C&CE; movements are investing.
  • Reconcile cash flow C&CE to the balance sheet and disclose restricted balances (7.45–48).
  • Apply and disclose a consistent policy for liquid mutual funds and CC accounts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify bank items [L1]

Classify at 31 March 2025: (i) FD opened 10 January 2025 for 90 days; (ii) FD opened 1 March 2025 for 100 days; (iii) unpaid dividend account; (iv) margin money against a bank guarantee; (v) overnight mutual fund units; (vi) cash credit overdrawn ₹12 crore, regularly swinging into credit; (vii) WCDL of 30 days.

Q2. Reconcile C&CE [L2]

Balance sheet: cash and cash equivalents ₹64 crore; bank balances other than C&CE ₹23 crore; current borrowings ₹210 crore including CC (repayable on demand, part of cash management) ₹35 crore and WCDL ₹100 crore. Prior year: C&CE ₹48 crore; CC ₹50 crore. Compute opening and closing C&CE for the cash flow statement and draft the 7.45 reconciliation note.

💡 Hint: The same overdraft policy must apply to both opening and closing balances.

Q3. Restricted cash in a real estate company [L2]

A developer holds ₹180 crore in RERA-designated project accounts (withdrawals permitted only for project land and construction costs, certified by engineer, architect and CA), ₹40 crore in free current accounts and ₹25 crore in FDs (2-month original maturity). Discuss the classification of the RERA accounts for cash flow and balance sheet purposes and the 7.48 disclosure.

Q4. Policy change for liquid funds [L3]

A company that previously classified overnight and liquid mutual funds (₹300 crore) as current investments proposes to include them in cash equivalents from FY 2024-25. Analyse whether they meet the 7.6–7.7 criteria, whether the change is permissible, and the accounting and disclosure consequences, including restated comparatives.
