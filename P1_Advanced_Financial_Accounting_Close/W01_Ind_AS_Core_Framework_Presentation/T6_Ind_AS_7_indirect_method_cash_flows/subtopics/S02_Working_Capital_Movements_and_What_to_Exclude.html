Subtopic note

Working Capital Movements and What to Exclude

Scope: Working capital movements: receivables, contract assets/liabilities, inventories, payables, provisions — excluding capex creditors, IC loans, acquired balances

Why it matters

Working-capital movements are usually the largest reconciling items between EBITDA and operating cash flow, and the most common place where classification errors hide. Including capex creditors in trade payables flatters CFO and understates capex; including balances acquired in a business combination distorts both CFO and investing; treating intercompany loans as working capital moves financing into operations. Lenders, rating agencies and equity analysts focus on CFO/EBITDA conversion, so these errors change credit and valuation conclusions. FP&A teams forecast working capital with days ratios that rely on the same definitions.

Core notes

Which balances are operating working capital

Operating activities are the principal revenue-producing activities of the entity and other activities that are not investing or financing (Ind AS 7.6, 7.14). Working-capital lines are the balance-sheet items whose movements arise from those activities:

Asset side (increase = outflow) Liability side (increase = inflow)
Trade receivables (gross or net — see ECL pitfall) Trade payables (for goods and services)
Contract assets (unbilled revenue) Contract liabilities (advances from customers, deferred revenue)
Inventories (net of write-downs if write-downs are not added back) Other operating payables (employee dues, statutory dues — GST, TDS payable)
Loans and advances to employees, prepaid expenses Provisions (warranty, employee benefits, onerous contracts)
Other operating current assets (GST input credit, export incentives receivable) Refund liabilities
Security deposits for operating leases (rent, utilities) Security deposits from dealers/customers

The sign rule (c02): an increase in an operating asset absorbs cash and is deducted; an increase in an operating liability provides cash and is added.

Contract assets and liabilities

Contract assets behave like receivables — increases are deductions; contract liabilities behave like payables/advances — increases are additions. For an EPC or SaaS business, these lines can dominate CFO: a SaaS company collecting annual subscriptions in advance shows strong CFO relative to profit because deferred revenue keeps growing. Retention receivable and mobilisation advances are also operating.

Provisions

Provisions charged to P&L and settled in cash are operating. Two approaches: add back the charge in the non-cash block and deduct utilisation; or include the provision's net movement within working capital. Either is acceptable; use one consistently. Remove non-P&L movements: OCI remeasurement of defined benefit obligations, provisions acquired, and decommissioning provisions capitalised into PPE (non-cash investing, 7.43).

Exclusions — balances that look like working capital but are not

Balance Correct classification Why
Capex creditors (payables for PPE, CWIP, intangibles) Investing — adjust capex outflow (c04) Cash flow relates to acquisition of long-term assets (7.16(a))
Capital advances Investing Prepayment for PPE
Intercompany / related-party loans given or taken Investing (given) / Financing (taken) Not trade transactions (7.16(e), 7.17(c))
Interest accrued on borrowings / deposits Financing / Investing Part of interest paid/received
Dividend payable / unpaid dividend Financing Dividends paid (7.34)
Current maturities of long-term borrowings Financing Borrowings
Payables for share buyback, deposits for share application Financing Equity transactions
Balances acquired in a business combination Within "acquisition of subsidiary, net of cash" (7.39) Movement not from operations
Balances of subsidiaries disposed Within "disposal of subsidiary, net of cash" As above
Income tax assets/liabilities (current) Separate "taxes paid" line 7.35
Deferred tax Non-cash Not a cash flow
Lease liabilities Financing (principal) Ind AS 116 / 7.17(e)
Investments in mutual funds/FDs > 3 months Investing Not cash equivalents

Acquired balances: the "organic movement"

When a subsidiary is acquired, the consolidated closing receivables include the acquiree's receivables. The working-capital movement must exclude the acquired amount:

\[ \Delta \text{WC}_{\text{CF}} = \text{Closing} - \text{Opening} - \text{Acquired} + \text{Disposed} \pm \text{FX translation of foreign operations} \]

Illustration (₹ crore)

Consolidated receivables: opening 200, closing 290; receivables of a subsidiary acquired during the year 40; translation gain on foreign subsidiaries' receivables 5. Trade payables: opening 150, closing 210, including capex creditors opening 12, closing 30; payables acquired 15.

Line Computation CFO effect
Receivables \(290 - 200 - 40 - 5 = 45\) increase (45)
Trade payables (excl. capex creditors) \((210 - 30) - (150 - 12) - 15 = 27\) increase 27
Capex creditors Increase 18 Reduces capex outflow in investing

Using raw balances would show receivables (90) and payables +60 — a net (30) instead of the correct (18) — and the ₹18 crore increase in capex creditors would sit wrongly in CFO instead of reducing the capex outflow in investing.

Data sources and model implementation

The trial balance must separate capex creditors, intercompany balances and statutory dues into their own ledgers — a design point for the Week 4 mapping engine. In the model, each working-capital line pulls closing and opening balances, then subtracts acquisition and FX columns from a "non-cash movements" matrix:

=-(Close_AR - Open_AR - Acq_AR - FX_AR)

A check confirms that every BS line appears in exactly one CF bucket.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Supplier finance / reverse factoring: payables under supplier-finance arrangements may need to be reclassified as borrowings if their nature changes; the IASB's 2023 amendments to IAS 7 and IFRS 7 require disclosure of supplier finance arrangements, their terms and their effects on liabilities and cash flows — check whether and from which date MCA has notified the corresponding Ind AS 7/Ind AS 107 amendments before relying on them; even without them, Ind AS 1 and 107 liquidity-risk disclosures apply.
  • Bill discounting with recourse: receivables remain on the balance sheet; cash received is financing, and the receivable's later collection is a non-cash settlement of the borrowing.
  • Factoring without recourse (derecognition): proceeds are operating (sale of receivables).
  • Security deposits for office premises leases (outside Ind AS 116 short-term) — operating; deposits for long-term leases are often shown as investing by practice — be consistent.
  • Advances to suppliers of capital goods recorded within "other current assets" — reclassify to capital advances.
  • GST input credit on capex purchases sits in operating GST receivable but relates to capex — most entities treat it as operating for practicality; disclose if material.

Key takeaways

  • Working capital = operating receivables, contract balances, inventories, payables, provisions and other operating items.
  • Increase in an operating asset is an outflow; increase in an operating liability is an inflow.
  • Exclude capex creditors, capital advances, intercompany loans, interest/dividend payables, tax balances, borrowings and lease liabilities.
  • Strip out balances acquired or disposed with subsidiaries and FX translation of foreign operations.
  • Provisions can be handled as add-back-and-utilise or as a movement — not both.
  • Ledger design (separate capex creditor and IC ledgers) is a prerequisite for accurate cash flows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the balances [L1]

Classify the movement of each into operating WC, investing, financing, taxes or non-cash: (i) GST payable; (ii) payable to a machinery supplier; (iii) loan to a subsidiary; (iv) advance from a customer; (v) unclaimed dividend payable; (vi) deferred tax liability; (vii) interest accrued on NCDs; (viii) provision for warranty; (ix) capital advance for a new plant; (x) refund liability for expected returns.

Q2. Organic working capital with acquisition [L2]

Consolidated inventories: opening ₹320 crore, closing ₹410 crore; inventories of an acquired subsidiary at acquisition ₹55 crore; inventory write-down in the year ₹6 crore (not separately added back). Trade payables: opening ₹260 crore, closing ₹330 crore; including capex creditors ₹20 crore and ₹35 crore; payables acquired ₹28 crore. Compute the CFO effect of each line.

💡 Hint: Decide whether the write-down needs any separate adjustment given how the inventory movement is computed.

Q3. Provision handling consistency [L2]

Warranty provision: opening ₹14 crore, charged to P&L ₹9 crore, utilised in cash ₹7 crore, closing ₹16 crore. Gratuity liability: opening ₹22 crore, current service + interest cost ₹4 crore, OCI remeasurement loss ₹3 crore, paid ₹2 crore, closing ₹27 crore. Show two acceptable presentations of these in CFO and explain why the OCI remeasurement must be excluded.

Q4. Supplier finance arrangement [L3]

An auto OEM's payables of ₹600 crore include ₹240 crore under a bank-led supplier finance programme: suppliers are paid by the bank at 30 days; the OEM pays the bank at 120 days (normal credit terms are 60 days). Evaluate whether these remain trade payables or become borrowings, the cash flow presentation of the bank's payment to suppliers and the OEM's repayment, and the disclosures required.
