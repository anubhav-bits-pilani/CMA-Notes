Subtopic note

Indirect Method from Profit Before Tax: Non-Cash and Reclassification Adjustments

Scope: Indirect method starting from profit before tax (Indian practice): non-cash adjustments and reclassification of investing/financing items

Why it matters

The indirect method is the reconciliation analysts read to judge earnings quality — the gap between profit and operating cash flow. In India, the standard presentation starts from profit before tax, adds back non-cash expenses, removes investing and financing items embedded in profit, adjusts for working capital and then deducts taxes paid. Getting the first block right (non-cash and reclassification adjustments) determines whether CFO is clean; errors here (missing share-based payment add-back, leaving dividend income in CFO, double-counting FX) are among the most common review findings in audit and in model reviews.

Core notes

The method (Ind AS 7.18(b), 7.20)

Under the indirect method, net cash flow from operating activities is determined by adjusting profit or loss for the effects of: (a) changes during the period in inventories and operating receivables and payables; (b) non-cash items such as depreciation, provisions, deferred taxes, unrealised foreign currency gains and losses, and undistributed profits of associates; and (c) all other items for which the cash effects are investing or financing cash flows.

Ind AS 7.20 refers to "profit or loss"; Indian practice — following the format illustrated in Ind AS 7's appendix and ICAI guidance — starts with profit before tax so that taxes can be shown separately as "income taxes paid" (7.35). Some companies start from profit after tax and add back tax expense — both reach the same CFO.

Block 1: non-cash adjustments (add back expenses, deduct income)

Item Adjustment Why
Depreciation and amortisation (incl. ROU depreciation) Add Allocation of past capex
Impairment of PPE, goodwill, intangibles, investments Add Non-cash write-down
ECL / bad debts provision charge Add (if receivables movement is gross) Non-cash — see topic pitfall
Inventory write-downs Usually none separately — embedded in inventory movement if inventory is net Avoid double count
Unrealised FX loss/(gain) on operating monetary items and borrowings Add / deduct Translation, not cash
Share-based payment expense (equity-settled) Add Settled in shares
Provisions (warranty, employee benefits) charged Either add back or capture via provision movement — not both
Fair value changes on derivatives/FVTPL investments Deduct gains / add losses Unrealised
Share of profit of associates/JVs (equity method) Deduct (dividends received are investing) Not cash
Unwinding of discount on provisions Add (part of finance cost) Non-cash
Government grant amortisation (deferred income) Deduct Cash received earlier
Liabilities no longer required written back Deduct Non-cash income

Block 2: reclassification of investing and financing items

These are income or expense in profit but their cash flows belong elsewhere:

Item in P&L Adjustment in CFO Cash flow shown in
Finance costs (interest, lease interest, unwinding) Add back Financing — interest paid (7.33)
Interest income Deduct Investing — interest received
Dividend income Deduct Investing — dividends received
Profit on sale of PPE / investments Deduct Investing — proceeds from sale (gross)
Loss on sale of PPE / investments Add Investing
Gain on derecognition of lease (modification) Deduct Non-cash
Gain on bargain purchase or remeasurement of previously held interest Deduct Non-cash

Exceptional items: classify the cash effect by nature — a voluntary retirement scheme payment is operating; insurance proceeds for a destroyed plant are investing (7.16 in substance); a settlement with lenders is financing.

The subtotal "operating profit before working capital changes" is not required by Ind AS 7 but is the Indian convention and useful as a cash-EBITDA proxy. Working-capital movements (s02) and taxes paid (c03) follow.

Model implementation

In a model, each adjustment should be sourced from a note schedule, not typed: depreciation from the fixed-asset roll-forward, ECL from the allowance roll-forward, FX from the borrowing schedule, SBP from the ESOP schedule. Build the CFO block as a column of links, with a check that the sum of finance cost add-back equals the P&L finance cost line.

=PBT + DandA + Impairment + ECL + UnrealFX + SBP - FVGain - ShareAssoc + FinCost - IntInc - DivInc - GainOnSale

Direct method (for completeness)

Ind AS 7.19 encourages the direct method (cash receipts from customers, cash paid to suppliers and employees); it is rarely used in India but is useful for treasury forecasting — the 13-week cash flow forecast is effectively a direct-method statement.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Realised vs unrealised FX: realised FX on trade payables settled is operating cash flow already within payments; only the unrealised element is adjusted. FX on foreign-currency cash balances goes to the 7.28 line (s08), not CFO.
  • Provisions: if the employee-benefit liability movement is in working capital, do not also add back the gratuity expense. OCI remeasurements on gratuity do not touch profit — but they change the liability, so remove them from the liability movement.
  • Profit on sale of a subsidiary — deduct in CFO; proceeds net of cash disposed go to investing (s07).
  • Capitalised borrowing costs are not in finance cost in P&L; their cash is part of interest paid in financing (with disclosure) or capex in investing — be consistent and disclose (7.32).
  • Government grants related to assets received in cash are investing or financing depending on policy — not operating.
  • Expected credit loss on loans given to related parties (investing) — the cash flow effect sits in investing; adjust in CFO, not working capital.

Key takeaways

  • Indian convention: start from PBT, add non-cash expenses, remove investing/financing items, then working capital and taxes paid.
  • Non-cash adjustments include D&A, impairment, ECL, unrealised FX, SBP, fair value changes, equity-accounted results, write-backs.
  • Finance cost is added back and interest/dividend income deducted because their cash flows are financing/investing under Ind AS 7.33.
  • Gains/losses on disposals are removed; gross proceeds are shown in investing.
  • Avoid double counting: each non-cash item is adjusted once — either as an add-back or within a balance movement.
  • Source each adjustment from a note schedule in the model and check totals to the P&L.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the pre-working-capital block [L1]

PBT ₹75 crore after: depreciation ₹18 crore; amortisation ₹2 crore; ECL ₹1.5 crore; gratuity expense ₹2 crore (liability movement included in working capital); finance cost ₹12 crore; interest income ₹3 crore; gain on sale of investments ₹4 crore; unrealised FX gain on trade receivables ₹0.6 crore; government grant amortisation ₹1 crore. Prepare the operating profit before working capital changes.

Q2. Associates and FVTPL [L2]

A holding company's consolidated PBT of ₹240 crore includes: share of profit of associates ₹22 crore (dividends received ₹6 crore); FVTPL gain on mutual funds ₹5 crore of which ₹2 crore realised on redemption; impairment of goodwill ₹15 crore; liabilities written back ₹3 crore. Show the CFO adjustments and where each related cash flow appears.

💡 Hint: Realised fair value gains still belong to investing because the redemption proceeds are investing cash flows.

Q3. Exceptional items [L2]

PBT includes three exceptional items: (i) VRS expense ₹30 crore, of which ₹20 crore paid during the year; (ii) insurance claim ₹12 crore received for a fire-damaged warehouse; (iii) profit on sale of a business division ₹40 crore (sale consideration ₹110 crore received, division cash ₹5 crore). Explain the CFO adjustment and classification of each cash flow.

Q4. Interview — PBT vs PAT start [L1]

Explain why Indian companies start the indirect method from PBT, what changes if you start from PAT, and why CFO is identical either way.
