Subtopic note

Financing Liability Reconciliation and Subsidiaries Acquired or Disposed

Scope: Changes in liabilities from financing activities reconciliation (7.44A–44E); subsidiaries acquired/disposed net of cash (7.39–42)

Why it matters

The 7.44A reconciliation is effectively an audited net-debt roll-forward — the first schedule a credit analyst or lender builds. It shows how much of the change in borrowings and lease liabilities came from cash and how much from acquisitions, FX, fair value changes, new leases and conversions. The acquisition/disposal rules (7.39–42) prevent consolidated working capital and investing lines from being distorted by balances that arrived or left with subsidiaries. Both are central to consolidated cash flows of Indian groups that make frequent acquisitions or divestments.

Core notes

Part A — Changes in liabilities arising from financing activities (Ind AS 7.44A–44E)

An entity provides disclosures that enable users to evaluate changes in liabilities arising from financing activities, including both changes arising from cash flows and non-cash changes (7.44A). To the extent necessary, disclose (7.44B):

(a) changes from financing cash flows; (b) changes arising from obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries or other businesses; (c) the effect of changes in foreign exchange rates; (d) changes in fair values; and (e) other changes (e.g., new leases, interest accretion, conversions, EIR amortisation).

Liabilities arising from financing activities are liabilities for which cash flows were, or future cash flows will be, classified as financing (7.44C). Also include changes in financial assets that hedge financing liabilities (e.g., cross-currency swaps on ECBs) (7.44C). A tabular reconciliation between opening and closing balances is one way to satisfy the requirement (7.44D); if included, it is shown separately from changes in other assets and liabilities (7.44E).

Typical columns: non-current borrowings (incl. current maturities), current borrowings (excl. overdrafts in C&CE), lease liabilities, interest accrued, derivative liabilities/(assets) hedging borrowings, unpaid dividends (some include).

Part B — Obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries or other businesses (Ind AS 7.39–42B)

Presentation (7.39): aggregate cash flows arising from obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries or other businesses are presented separately and classified as investing activities.

Net of cash (7.42): the aggregate amount of cash paid or received as consideration is reported in the cash flow statement net of cash and cash equivalents acquired or disposed of.

\[ \text{Net cash outflow on acquisition} = \text{Cash consideration paid in the period} - \text{C\&CE of acquiree at acquisition} \]

Disclosures (7.40) for both obtaining and losing control, in aggregate: (a) total consideration paid or received; (b) portion of consideration consisting of cash and cash equivalents; (c) amount of C&CE in the subsidiaries or businesses over which control is obtained or lost; (d) amount of assets and liabilities other than C&CE, summarised by major category.

Investment entities (Ind AS 110) need not apply 7.40(c)–(d) to subsidiaries measured at FVTPL (7.40A).

Changes in ownership without loss of control (7.42A–42B): purchases or sales of NCI stakes are financing activities (transactions with owners), not investing.

Consequential effects:

  • Working-capital movements exclude acquired/disposed balances (s02).
  • PPE acquired through the acquisition is not "purchase of PPE".
  • Borrowings acquired appear in the 7.44A column "acquisition of subsidiary", not as proceeds.
  • Deferred or contingent consideration paid in later periods: payments up to the acquisition-date fair value are generally investing; excess over that amount (remeasurement) may be operating — practice varies; deferred consideration with a financing character may be financing. Disclose.
  • Acquisition-related costs expensed (Ind AS 103.53) are operating cash flows.

Illustration — acquisition and disposal (₹ crore)

Group acquires 80% of Alpha Ltd on 1 September 2024: cash consideration ₹240 crore paid on completion; deferred consideration ₹30 crore payable in 2026; Alpha's C&CE ₹35 crore and overdraft (part of cash management) ₹5 crore. Group sells 100% of Beta Ltd on 1 January 2025 for ₹90 crore cash; Beta's C&CE ₹12 crore. Group buys an additional 10% of Gamma Ltd (already a subsidiary) for ₹20 crore.

Line Computation Section
Acquisition of subsidiary, net of cash acquired 240 − (35 − 5) = 210 outflow Investing
Disposal of subsidiary, net of cash disposed 90 − 12 = 78 inflow Investing
Acquisition of NCI in Gamma 20 outflow Financing
Deferred consideration Non-cash in FY 2024-25; disclose —

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Asset acquisitions (not businesses) under Ind AS 103's optional concentration test: cash paid is "purchase of PPE / investment", not acquisition of subsidiary — but if the acquiree is a legal subsidiary holding mainly an asset, many still present it as acquisition of subsidiary; disclose.
  • Step acquisitions: cash paid for the controlling stake is investing; previously held interest remeasurement gain is non-cash.
  • Loss of control while retaining an associate stake: proceeds for the stake sold net of cash of the subsidiary derecognised; the retained interest is non-cash.
  • Common-control mergers under Appendix C of Ind AS 103 with restated comparatives — the transferor's cash flows are included as if combined from the beginning of the earliest period; no "acquisition" cash flow arises.
  • Overdrafts acquired are deducted from cash acquired only if they are part of C&CE under the group policy.
  • Cash held by subsidiary acquired after year-end — not included; disclose as non-adjusting event.

Key takeaways

  • 7.44A requires a reconciliation of financing liabilities separating cash flows from non-cash changes (acquisitions, FX, fair value, other).
  • Include financial assets that hedge financing liabilities; ensure cash rows tie to CFF.
  • Aggregate cash flows from obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries are investing, presented net of C&CE acquired or disposed.
  • Disclose total consideration, cash portion, cash acquired/disposed and summarised assets and liabilities.
  • Changes in ownership without loss of control are financing.
  • Exclude acquired and disposed balances from working capital, capex and borrowings lines.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a 7.44A table [L2]

Opening: term loans ₹500 crore, lease liabilities ₹80 crore. During the year: term loans drawn ₹200 crore, repaid ₹120 crore; FX loss on ECB ₹9 crore; EIR amortisation ₹2 crore; term loans of an acquired subsidiary ₹45 crore; lease payments ₹22 crore (interest ₹7 crore); new leases ₹18 crore; lease terminations ₹4 crore. Prepare the reconciliation and identify the CFF lines it must agree with.

Q2. Acquisition with mixed consideration [L2]

On 1 November 2024, a group acquires 100% of Delta Ltd for ₹400 crore: ₹250 crore cash at completion, ₹100 crore in shares, ₹50 crore contingent consideration (fair value) payable in 2026. Delta has C&CE ₹30 crore, borrowings ₹60 crore, receivables ₹45 crore, inventory ₹38 crore. Present the investing line, the 7.40 disclosures and the effect on the 7.44A table and working-capital lines.

💡 Hint: Separate the cash, share and contingent elements before computing the net outflow.

Q3. Disposal with retained associate [L2]

A group sells 60% of its 100% subsidiary Omega Ltd for ₹180 crore cash, retaining 40% (fair value ₹120 crore). Omega's C&CE at disposal ₹25 crore. Determine the investing cash flow, the treatment of the retained interest and the gain recognised (net assets ₹200 crore), and the 7.40 disclosures.

Q4. Interview — NCI purchase classification [L1]

Explain why buying out minority shareholders of an existing subsidiary is a financing cash flow while acquiring a new subsidiary is investing, and how this affects FCFF comparisons.
