Subtopic note

Effect of Exchange Rate Changes on Foreign-Currency Cash (Ind AS 7.28)

Scope: Effect of exchange rate changes on foreign-currency cash (7.28)

Why it matters

Indian exporters, IT services companies and groups with foreign subsidiaries hold significant USD, EUR and GBP balances in EEFC accounts and overseas bank accounts. When the rupee moves, the rupee value of those balances changes without any cash flow. Ind AS 7.28 requires this effect to be shown as a separate reconciling line — neither operating, investing nor financing — so that the three activity sections reflect only real cash flows. Misplacing FX on cash in CFO (where it often lands via "unrealised FX gain" adjustments) is a common error; in consolidated statements, translation of foreign subsidiaries' cash adds a second layer.

Core notes

The rule (Ind AS 7.25–28)

  • Cash flows arising from transactions in a foreign currency are recorded in the entity's functional currency by applying the exchange rate at the date of the cash flow (7.25); a rate approximating the actual (e.g., a weighted average for the period) may be used (7.27, Ind AS 21).
  • Cash flows of a foreign subsidiary are translated at the exchange rates at the dates of the cash flows (or an average approximation) (7.26).
  • Unrealised gains and losses arising from changes in exchange rates are not cash flows (7.28).
  • However, the effect of exchange rate changes on C&CE held or due in a foreign currency is reported in the statement of cash flows in order to reconcile C&CE at the beginning and end of the period. This amount is presented separately from operating, investing and financing activities and includes the differences, if any, had those cash flows been reported at end-of-period exchange rates (7.28).

Components of the FX line

\[ \text{FX effect} = \text{Closing C\&CE (₹)} - \text{Opening C\&CE (₹)} - \text{Net cash flows translated at transaction/average rates} \]

The line captures two things:

  1. Remeasurement of opening foreign-currency cash at the closing rate (the opening balance's FX gain/loss).
  2. The difference between translating the year's net foreign-currency cash flows at transaction/average rates and at the closing rate.

For a standalone entity, the FX gain/loss on foreign-currency bank balances recognised in P&L (Ind AS 21.23, monetary items) is an unrealised item included in PBT — it must be deducted/added back in CFO and then shown in the separate FX line. For consolidated statements, the translation difference on foreign subsidiaries' cash recognised in OCI (FCTR) is likewise not a cash flow — it appears in the FX line, and the OCI does not otherwise enter the cash flow.

Illustration — standalone exporter

Kaveri IT Ltd holds an EEFC account. Opening balance USD 2.0 million at ₹82.00 = ₹16.40 crore. During the year, net inflow of USD 3.0 million (receipts USD 10 million less payments USD 7 million), translated at transaction rates averaging ₹83.50 = ₹25.05 crore (within CFO). Closing balance USD 5.0 million at ₹85.50 = ₹42.75 crore.

\[ \text{FX effect} = 42.75 - 16.40 - 25.05 = ₹1.30 \text{ crore} \]

Decomposition:

  • Opening balance remeasured: USD 2.0 million × (85.50 − 82.00) = ₹0.70 crore.
  • Year's flows at closing vs average: USD 3.0 million × (85.50 − 83.50) = ₹0.60 crore.
  • Total ₹1.30 crore.

In the P&L, the FX gain on the EEFC balance (₹1.30 crore, if all unrealised on cash) is in PBT; in CFO it is deducted as an unrealised FX gain; in the cash flow statement it appears as "Effect of exchange rate changes on cash and cash equivalents ₹1.30 crore".

Note that FX on trade receivables (operating monetary items) is a different matter: realised FX is embedded in cash receipts (operating); unrealised FX on closing receivables is removed in CFO (either as a non-cash adjustment or through the receivable movement), and never goes in the 7.28 line.

Illustration — consolidated group with a US subsidiary

US subsidiary's cash: opening USD 10 million (₹82.00 → ₹82.0 crore); net cash flows USD 4 million (CFO USD 6 million, CFI −USD 2 million) translated at average ₹83.80 = ₹33.52 crore; closing USD 14 million at ₹85.50 = ₹119.70 crore.

\[ \text{FX effect} = 119.70 - 82.00 - 33.52 = ₹4.18 \text{ crore} \]

The subsidiary's CFO and CFI lines in the consolidated statement use ₹83.80; the ₹4.18 crore is in FCTR (OCI) and in the FX line.

Presentation

The standard Indian presentation places the FX line after the net increase/decrease in C&CE:

Line ₹ crore
Net cash from operating activities A
Net cash used in investing activities B
Net cash used in financing activities C
Net increase/(decrease) in C&CE (A + B + C) D
C&CE at beginning of the year E
Effect of exchange rate changes on C&CE F
C&CE at end of the year (D + E + F) G

Some companies present F before E; both are acceptable. The line must include FX on overdrafts in foreign currency if those are within C&CE.

Model implementation

Track foreign-currency cash in currency units and rupees. Compute flows in currency × average rate; compute the closing balance at the closing rate; the FX line is the plug that must also equal the P&L unrealised FX on cash (standalone) or FCTR on cash (consolidated). A check row compares the plug with an independent computation (opening FC balance × rate change + net FC flows × (closing − average)).

=ClosingFC*ClosingRate - OpeningFC*OpeningRate - NetFlowsFC*AvgRate

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Hyperinflationary subsidiaries (Ind AS 29) — cash flows are restated and translated at closing rates; the FX line includes monetary gains/losses.
  • Foreign-currency FDs not within C&CE — FX on them is a non-cash item removed in CFO; it does not appear in the 7.28 line.
  • Derivatives hedging cash balances — the derivative's fair value changes are not cash flows; settlements are classified by the hedged item's nature.
  • Very volatile currencies — average rates may not approximate transaction rates; use monthly averages or actual rates.
  • Gross presentation errors: do not present FX gains on cash within "other income" in CFO — the gain must be removed from CFO and shown separately.
  • Opening C&CE in a newly acquired subsidiary is acquired cash (investing net line); FX thereafter enters the 7.28 line.

Key takeaways

  • Unrealised FX on foreign-currency cash is not a cash flow; show it as a separate reconciling line (7.28).
  • The FX line = closing C&CE − opening C&CE − net cash flows at transaction/average rates.
  • It captures remeasurement of opening FC cash and the difference between average and closing rates on the year's flows.
  • Remove the corresponding unrealised gain/loss from CFO (standalone) — FCTR on cash in consolidation is handled through the same line.
  • FX on receivables, payables, borrowings and non-cash-equivalent deposits never goes in the 7.28 line.
  • Track FC cash in currency units in the model and reconcile the FX plug independently.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the FX line [L1]

Opening EUR balance €1.5 million at ₹89.00; receipts €4.0 million at an average ₹90.20; payments €3.2 million at an average ₹90.60; closing rate ₹92.00. Compute the closing balance in rupees and the FX effect, and decompose it.

Q2. Separate FX on cash from FX on receivables [L2]

An exporter's P&L shows net FX gain ₹6.4 crore: ₹2.1 crore on USD bank balances (unrealised), ₹3.0 crore realised on collections and ₹1.3 crore unrealised on closing receivables. Show how each piece is treated in CFO and the FX line, and state the effect on CFO.

💡 Hint: Only one of the three pieces belongs in the separate 7.28 line.

Q3. Consolidated FX line with two subsidiaries [L2]

A group has a UK subsidiary (opening £3 million at ₹104; net outflow £1 million at average ₹106; closing rate ₹108) and a UAE subsidiary (opening AED 20 million at ₹22.3; net inflow AED 5 million at average ₹22.7; closing rate ₹23.3). Compute the consolidated FX effect on C&CE and explain where the translation difference appears in the financial statements.

Q4. Review case — FX in CFO [L2]

A draft cash flow shows "Unrealised FX gain ₹4 crore" deducted in CFO but no FX line, and closing cash does not tie by ₹4 crore. The gain relates entirely to USD bank balances. Explain the error, correct the statement and describe a model check that would catch it automatically.
