Subtopic note

Ind AS 116 Lease Cash Flows in the Cash Flow Statement

Scope: Ind AS 116 lease cash flows: principal and interest in financing; short-term, low-value and variable payments in operating

Why it matters

Ind AS 116 (effective FY 2019-20) moved most lease payments of lessees out of operating cash flows. For retailers (DMart, Trent), QSR chains (Jubilant FoodWorks, Devyani), airlines (IndiGo) and telecom tower users, CFO jumped and CFF absorbed the lease payments — EBITDA and CFO became non-comparable with pre-2019 years and with companies that buy rather than lease. Analysts now routinely compute "post-lease" CFO or FCF. For preparers, the cash flow must split lease payments correctly and keep ROU additions out of investing. FP&A teams forecasting cash need the same split to avoid double counting rent.

Core notes

Lessee classification (Ind AS 116.50, Ind AS 7.17(e))

Lease cash flow Classification
Cash payments for the principal portion of the lease liability Financing
Cash payments for the interest portion of the lease liability Per Ind AS 7.33 for interest paid — financing for non-financial entities
Short-term lease payments (≤12 months, recognition exemption, 116.6) Operating
Low-value asset lease payments (exemption) Operating
Variable lease payments not included in the lease liability (e.g., revenue-share rent above fixed minimum) Operating
Payments of initial direct costs or lease incentives received at commencement Generally investing/operating depending on nature (incentives reduce ROU — practice varies)
Prepaid lease payments made before commencement Investing (in practice, as part of acquiring the ROU asset) — judgement, disclose
Security deposits paid to lessors Investing or operating by nature — consistent policy

The ROU asset addition at commencement is a non-cash transaction (Ind AS 7.43) — the lessee recognises an asset and a liability without cash. It appears neither in investing nor financing; disclose it as a non-cash transaction (s06). Subsequent remeasurements and modifications of lease liabilities are also non-cash.

Indirect method adjustments

In CFO (starting from PBT):

  • add back ROU depreciation (within D&A);
  • add back lease interest (within finance cost);
  • deduct gains on lease modifications/terminations (non-cash);
  • short-term, low-value and variable lease expenses remain in profit — they are already operating cash outflows (adjusted for accruals through working capital).

In CFF:

  • show "payment of lease liabilities — principal" and "interest on lease liabilities" (or combined "payment of lease liabilities" with interest disclosed).

Rent concessions and subleases

  • COVID-19-related rent concessions (practical expedient in FY 2020-21 and FY 2021-22) recognised in P&L as negative variable payments — the cash effect is a lower lease payment in financing; the P&L gain is non-cash and deducted in CFO.
  • Lessor perspective: lease receipts for operating leases are operating; for finance leases, receipts reduce the net investment in the lease — typically investing for non-financial lessors (receivable principal) and interest received investing (7.33); for manufacturer/dealer lessors, operating (principal activities).
  • Sublease (intermediate lessor): receipts classified by the same logic as lessor; payments to the head lessor as lessee.

Analyst adjustments

"Pre-Ind AS 116 equivalent" CFO = reported CFO − total lease payments (principal + interest). Lease-adjusted net debt includes lease liabilities; some lenders exclude them in covenant definitions ("frozen GAAP" clauses). In FCFF models, either treat leases as debt (deduct lease liabilities in the equity bridge and leave lease payments out of FCFF) or treat them as operating (deduct lease payments from FCFF and exclude lease liabilities from debt) — never mix.

Tax angle

For income-tax, lease rent paid is deductible as per the Income-tax Act (Ind AS 116 depreciation and interest are not), so current tax is computed on rent; the deferred tax on the ROU asset and lease liability (Ind AS 12 amendment on single-transaction deferred tax, effective FY 2023-24) is non-cash. Taxes paid in CFO remain operating.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Prepaid lease of land (long-term lease premium paid upfront to a state industrial corporation such as MIDC): an ROU asset acquired for cash — the payment is an investing outflow, not a non-cash addition.
  • Lease payments made before commencement of a lease capitalised into ROU: investing by practice; disclose.
  • Variable payments that are in-substance fixed are part of the liability — financing.
  • Sale-and-leaseback with a transfer that is a sale: proceeds are investing (sale of asset) to the extent of the asset sold at fair value; any excess is financing (the financial liability).
  • Lease liabilities settled through set-off against receivables from the lessor — non-cash; disclose.
  • Interest on lease liabilities capitalised into CWIP (during construction) — classified consistently with other capitalised interest.

Key takeaways

  • Lessee: lease principal → financing; lease interest → financing under Ind AS 7.33 for non-financial entities.
  • Short-term, low-value and variable payments not in the liability → operating.
  • ROU additions and lease remeasurements are non-cash — excluded from investing and disclosed under 7.43.
  • Upfront premiums for leasehold land are cash investing outflows.
  • Lease liabilities feed the 7.44A financing liability reconciliation.
  • For analysis and FCFF, treat leases consistently as either debt or operating cost.

Practice Questions

Q1. Lease lines in the statement [L1]

A company's lease payments are ₹48 crore (interest ₹14 crore); ROU depreciation ₹30 crore; new ROU additions ₹65 crore; short-term lease rent ₹5 crore; variable rent ₹7 crore. State every line affected in CFO, CFI, CFF and the non-cash disclosure.

Q2. Lease liability roll-forward for 7.44A [L2]

Opening lease liabilities ₹300 crore; new leases ₹55 crore; interest accretion ₹26 crore; lease payments ₹80 crore; lease terminations ₹12 crore; FX translation of a foreign subsidiary's lease liability +₹4 crore. Compute the closing balance and prepare the 7.44A reconciliation row, separating cash and non-cash changes.

💡 Hint: Interest accretion is a non-cash change; its cash payment is part of the cash flow column.

Q3. Leasehold land premium [L2]

A manufacturer pays ₹40 crore upfront for a 95-year lease of industrial land from a state development corporation, plus annual lease rent of ₹1 lakh. Explain the ROU asset recognition, the cash flow classification of the premium and the annual rent, and the effect on CFO, CFI and CFF.

Q4. Pre-Ind AS 116 comparison for a retailer [L3]

A retailer reports EBITDA ₹1,200 crore, CFO ₹1,050 crore, lease principal ₹260 crore and lease interest ₹140 crore (both financing), capex ₹300 crore. A competitor owns most of its stores. Construct a comparable "post-lease" EBITDA, CFO and FCF, and explain how you would align net debt and EV/EBITDA multiples.
