Subtopic note

Non-Cash Investing and Financing Transactions (Ind AS 7.43)

Scope: Non-cash transactions excluded (7.43): ROU additions, debt-to-equity conversion, share-settled acquisitions

Why it matters

Many large investing and financing transactions involve no cash: assets acquired under leases, debentures converted into shares, acquisitions paid in shares, assets swapped, liabilities settled by issuing equity. Including them in the cash flow inflates both investing outflows and financing inflows and breaks the link to cash. Ind AS 7.43 requires their exclusion from the statement and separate disclosure. In India, CCD conversions, share-swap mergers under Sec 230–232 schemes, debt-to-equity conversions in resolution plans under the IBC, and Ind AS 116 ROU additions make this a frequent judgement area.

Core notes

The rule (Ind AS 7.43–44)

Investing and financing transactions that do not require the use of cash or cash equivalents are excluded from the statement of cash flows. Such transactions are disclosed elsewhere in the financial statements in a way that provides all relevant information about them (7.43). Many investing and financing activities do not have a direct impact on current cash flows although they affect the capital and asset structure (7.44). Examples in the standard:

  • acquisition of assets either by assuming directly related liabilities or by means of a lease;
  • acquisition of an entity by means of an equity issue;
  • conversion of debt to equity.

Common Indian examples

Transaction Why non-cash Effect on cash flow preparation
ROU asset additions (Ind AS 116) Asset and lease liability recognised simultaneously Exclude from capex; exclude from financing proceeds; lease payments later are financing
Capitalised decommissioning / restoration provision (Ind AS 16.16(c)) Asset and provision recognised together Exclude from capex additions
Conversion of CCDs, FCCBs, CCPS into equity Liability/equity reclassified Exclude from repayment of borrowings and proceeds from issue of shares
Debt-to-equity conversion in restructuring (RBI framework) or IBC resolution Lenders receive shares for loans Exclude; disclose
Acquisition of a subsidiary/business paid in shares (share swap) Consideration is equity Only the cash component (net of cash acquired) appears in investing
Bonus issue Reserves capitalised Not a cash flow at all
Assets acquired on deferred credit (vendor finance) Liability assumed Exclude at acquisition; later payments: investing (capex) per practice — or financing if the arrangement is in substance financing; disclose policy
Asset swaps / barter Exchange of non-cash assets Exclude; disclose
Dividends settled by distribution of non-cash assets (Appendix A to Ind AS 10 / distributions) No cash Exclude
Share-based payment (equity-settled) Services paid in shares Non-cash adjustment in CFO (s01)
Set-off of receivables against payables or loans Netting without cash Exclude both legs; disclose if material
Government grants received as non-monetary assets (land at nominal price) Asset received Exclude

Partly cash transactions

Where a transaction is partly cash, include only the cash component in the cash flow statement and disclose the non-cash portion. Example: a machine costing ₹50 crore paid ₹20 crore in cash with ₹30 crore deferred vendor credit — investing outflow ₹20 crore; disclose ₹30 crore non-cash; subsequent payments of the vendor credit are classified by substance (many companies show them as capex in investing; if the vendor credit carries interest and is structured as financing, payments are financing — Ind AS 7 does not prescribe, so disclose).

Preparing the statement: removing non-cash movements

In a balance-sheet-driven cash flow, every BS movement that includes a non-cash component must be adjusted:

\[ \text{Cash capex} = \Delta\text{Gross PPE} + \text{Disposals at cost} - \text{ROU additions} - \text{Decommissioning additions} - \text{Non-cash acquisitions} - \Delta\text{Capex creditors} + \Delta\text{Capital advances} \]

\[ \text{Proceeds from borrowings (net)} = \Delta\text{Borrowings} + \text{Conversions to equity} - \text{Non-cash FX/EIR changes} - \text{Borrowings acquired} \]

\[ \text{Proceeds from issue of shares} = \Delta(\text{Share capital} + \text{Securities premium}) - \text{Shares issued on conversion} - \text{Shares issued for acquisitions} - \text{Bonus} - \text{ESOP reserve transfers} \]

Disclosure format

Most Indian companies include a short note after the cash flow statement: "Non-cash investing and financing transactions" — listing ROU additions, conversions, share-settled acquisitions with amounts, and cross-referencing to the 7.44A reconciliation (where non-cash changes in financing liabilities also appear).

Illustration (₹ crore)

Sindhu Energy Ltd, FY 2024-25:

  • Gross PPE additions per note: ₹480 crore (including ROU ₹60 crore, decommissioning asset ₹15 crore, a wind farm acquired by issuing 2 crore shares at ₹100 = ₹200 crore).
  • ₹100 crore of CCDs converted into 1 crore shares at ₹100.
  • Capex creditors increased by ₹25 crore; capital advances increased by ₹10 crore.
  • New term loans drawn in cash ₹150 crore.
Line Computation ₹ crore
Purchase of PPE (investing) 480 − 60 − 15 − 200 − 25 + 10 (190)
Proceeds from issue of shares Nil (both share issues non-cash) 0
Proceeds from borrowings 150 (CCD conversion excluded) 150

If the wind farm is a business (Ind AS 103), it would be an acquisition of a subsidiary/business with only cash consideration net of cash acquired in investing (s07).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • IBC resolution plans: lenders' haircut and conversion of residual debt into equity — the haircut is a non-cash gain (deduct in CFO), the conversion non-cash; only cash paid by the resolution applicant to lenders is a financing outflow (repayment).
  • Merger by absorption under a scheme with an appointed date earlier than the effective date: the transferor's cash becomes the transferee's cash — presented as cash acquired, not as an inflow from operations.
  • Purchase consideration paid by the parent on behalf of a subsidiary — in the subsidiary's standalone statements, non-cash (capital contribution or loan).
  • Supplier-financed capex where a bank pays the supplier directly and the company owes the bank: the company never handles cash — capex is non-cash on drawdown; repayments are financing; disclose (some show a "gross-up" of investing and financing — not permitted for non-cash, though widely debated).
  • Rights issue proceeds received in escrow and released after year-end — the cash flow occurs when the entity obtains control of cash; restricted cash may need separate disclosure.

Key takeaways

  • Exclude investing and financing transactions that do not use cash; disclose them elsewhere (7.43).
  • ROU additions, decommissioning assets, conversions of debt to equity and share-settled acquisitions are the common Indian examples.
  • For partly cash transactions, include only the cash portion.
  • Cash capex, borrowing proceeds and share issue proceeds must each be derived by stripping out non-cash movements.
  • Non-cash changes in financing liabilities also appear in the 7.44A reconciliation.
  • Classify deferred settlements of capex consistently and disclose the policy.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify non-cash items [L1]

From the following FY 2024-25 events, list the non-cash investing/financing transactions and their disclosure amounts: (i) 5 crore CCPS converted into equity (₹250 crore); (ii) land worth ₹40 crore received from a state government as a grant; (iii) ROU additions ₹35 crore; (iv) QIP ₹500 crore cash; (v) set-off of ₹12 crore payable to a supplier against a loan given to it.

Q2. Cash capex derivation [L2]

Gross block (PPE + CWIP + intangibles): opening ₹1,200 crore, closing ₹1,520 crore. Disposals at cost ₹60 crore. Additions include ROU ₹45 crore and a capitalised restoration provision ₹8 crore. Capitalised borrowing costs ₹12 crore (paid in cash, classified in financing per policy). Capex creditors: opening ₹40 crore, closing ₹22 crore. Capital advances: opening ₹30 crore, closing ₹55 crore. Compute cash capex.

💡 Hint: Be explicit about whether capitalised interest is counted in capex or in interest paid — not both.

Q3. Share-swap acquisition with cash top-up [L2]

A listed company acquires 100% of a start-up for ₹600 crore: ₹150 crore cash and the balance in its own shares. The start-up's cash at acquisition is ₹40 crore. Show the investing line and the non-cash disclosure, and explain how share capital and securities premium movements are handled in the financing section.

Q4. IBC resolution — acquirer and target perspective [L3]

An acquirer pays ₹1,000 crore in cash to financial creditors of a corporate debtor under an approved resolution plan, of which ₹700 crore is infused by the acquirer as equity into the corporate debtor and ₹300 crore as a loan; lenders' admitted claims of ₹4,000 crore are settled for this ₹1,000 crore plus equity of 10% of the corporate debtor. Prepare the cash flow treatment in the corporate debtor's financial statements, distinguishing cash, non-cash and gain elements.
