Subtopic note

Interest and Dividend Classification under Ind AS 7.33–7.34

Scope: Ind AS 7.33 for non-financial entities: interest paid → financing; interest and dividends received → investing; dividends paid → financing (7.34)

Why it matters

Interest classification is the most visible Indian carve-out in Ind AS 7. It changes CFO materially for leveraged companies: a manufacturer with ₹500 crore of interest paid reports ₹500 crore more CFO under Ind AS than it would under US GAAP. Analysts comparing Indian and global peers, lenders computing CFO-based coverage ratios and FP&A teams building FCFF must know which line interest sits in. Misclassification is a recurring finding in NFRA and peer reviews, often caused by IFRS-based group reporting templates.

Core notes

The IAS 7 position and the Ind AS carve-out

IAS 7.31–34 require separate disclosure of cash flows from interest and dividends received and paid, classified consistently from period to period as operating, investing or financing. IAS 7.33 allowed a choice: financial institutions usually classify interest paid and interest and dividends received as operating; for other entities, there was no consensus and the choice was free.

Ind AS 7.33 removes the choice for entities other than financial institutions:

In case of other entities, interest paid and interest and dividends received are classified as financing activities and investing activities respectively, because they are costs of obtaining financial resources or returns on investments.

For financial institutions (banks, NBFCs, insurers), interest paid and interest and dividends received are usually classified as operating cash flows — their principal revenue-producing activities.

Dividends paid (7.34): classified as financing activities, being a cost of obtaining financial resources. (IAS 7.34 also allowed dividends paid to be operating; Ind AS 7.34 specifies financing.) Dividend distribution tax no longer exists after 1 April 2020; TDS on dividends deducted and paid on behalf of shareholders is part of dividends paid.

Practical consequences for the indirect method

  • Finance costs in the P&L are added back in CFO; interest paid (cash) appears in financing.
  • Interest income and dividend income are deducted in CFO; interest received and dividends received (cash) appear in investing.
  • Interest paid is the cash amount — finance cost adjusted for accruals, unwinding of discount (non-cash), amortisation of transaction costs (non-cash), and lease interest:

\[ \text{Interest paid} = \text{Finance cost (P\&L)} - \text{Non-cash components} + \text{Opening interest accrued} - \text{Closing interest accrued} + \text{Interest capitalised paid} \]

Non-cash components include the unwinding of discount on provisions (decommissioning, deferred consideration), the EIR amortisation of upfront fees, and interest on income tax (if presented in finance costs — though many classify it within taxes paid by nature).

Capitalised interest (Ind AS 7.32)

The total interest paid is disclosed whether it is recognised as an expense or capitalised under Ind AS 23. Practice in India: capitalised interest paid is included in interest paid under financing, with disclosure; some entities include it within capex (investing) as part of the asset's cost. Choose consistently and disclose. IFRS 18-era IAS 7 amendments will move to a single classification for interest paid in financing for most entities, consistent with the Ind AS approach.

Interest on taxes, MSME interest and others

  • Interest on delayed payment of income tax or GST: practice varies; classify by nature — many show it in financing as interest paid, others within taxes paid/operating. Disclose policy.
  • Interest on delayed payment to MSME suppliers (MSMED Act Sec 16) is a finance cost — financing.
  • Interest received on income-tax refunds — investing by 7.33's logic, though some present it in operating along with tax refunds. Be consistent.
  • Dividend from subsidiaries in standalone statements — investing; eliminated in consolidation.
  • Dividends received from equity-accounted associates — investing in consolidated statements.

Tapti Steel Ltd: finance cost in P&L ₹96 crore, comprising interest on term loans ₹70 crore, lease interest ₹9 crore, unwinding of decommissioning provision ₹2 crore, EIR amortisation of processing fees ₹3 crore, interest on MSME dues ₹1 crore, interest on bill discounting ₹11 crore. Interest accrued but not due: opening ₹6 crore, closing ₹10 crore. Interest capitalised (paid) on CWIP ₹8 crore. Interest income ₹4 crore (accrued interest receivable opening ₹1 crore, closing ₹2 crore). Dividend income from mutual funds ₹0.5 crore received. Dividends paid ₹40 crore.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Holding companies whose principal activity is investing (core investment companies registered with RBI) are financial institutions for this purpose — interest and dividends received are operating.
  • Conglomerates with an NBFC subsidiary: in consolidated statements, classification follows the nature of each activity — the NBFC's interest flows are operating; the parent's are financing/investing. Disclose the policy.
  • Interest on customer advances with an SFC: a finance cost — interest paid is non-cash (accretion), so no cash line until revenue/settlement.
  • Interest received on deposits held as margin money for borrowings — investing.
  • Interest paid on lease liabilities of short-term or low-value leases does not arise (no liability).
  • Group reporting to IFRS parents with "interest paid in operating" — maintain a reconciliation for Ind AS statutory accounts.

Key takeaways

  • Non-financial entities under Ind AS: interest paid → financing; interest and dividends received → investing; dividends paid → financing.
  • Financial institutions classify interest paid and interest/dividends received as operating.
  • Finance cost is added back in CFO; cash interest paid is derived after removing non-cash components and accrual movements.
  • Total interest paid includes capitalised interest — disclose and classify consistently.
  • Ind AS removes the IAS 7 choice; US GAAP puts interest in operating — adjust CFO when comparing peers.
  • IFRS 18-era IAS 7 converges towards the Ind AS approach from 2027.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify interest and dividend flows [L1]

For (a) a cement manufacturer and (b) an NBFC, classify: interest paid on NCDs; interest received on fixed deposits; dividend received from a subsidiary; dividend paid to shareholders; interest received on loans to customers.

Q2. Derive interest paid [L2]

Finance cost ₹54 crore includes: interest on term loans ₹38 crore, lease interest ₹6 crore, unwinding of discount on deferred consideration for an acquisition ₹4 crore, processing fee amortisation ₹1 crore, interest on income-tax ₹2 crore, interest on working capital loans ₹3 crore. Interest accrued: opening ₹4 crore, closing ₹7 crore. Interest capitalised ₹5 crore (paid). Compute interest paid and state your policy on the income-tax interest.

💡 Hint: Remove every component that is not settled in cash during the year before adjusting accruals.

Q3. Peer comparison adjustment [L2]

An Indian specialty chemicals company reports CFO ₹640 crore, interest paid ₹85 crore (financing), interest received ₹12 crore (investing). A US-listed peer reports under ASC 230. Restate the Indian company's CFO on a US GAAP basis and explain the impact on CFO/EBITDA conversion comparisons.

Q4. Holding company classification [L3]

An NSE-listed holding company has 80% of its income from dividends and interest on loans to group companies, and is registered as a CIC with RBI. Its auditors question whether interest and dividends received should be investing. Prepare a memo applying Ind AS 7.33's distinction between financial institutions and other entities, the "principal revenue-producing activities" definition, and the required disclosures.
