Roadmap topic: Ind AS 7 Statement of Cash Flows: Indirect method operating cash flow adjustments separating non-cash working capital movements from investing/financing items

Overview

The statement of cash flows is the reconciliation that ties the P&L and the balance sheet to the one number that cannot be manipulated by accruals — cash. Ind AS 7 permits the direct or indirect method for operating activities; Indian companies overwhelmingly use the indirect method, starting from profit before tax (a presentation convention reflected in Schedule III practice and the ICAI Guidance Note) and adjusting for non-cash items, investing and financing items included in profit, working-capital movements and taxes paid. Investing and financing activities are presented gross (major classes of receipts and payments), subject to limited net-presentation exceptions.

For FP&A and valuation roles, the cash flow is the bridge from EBITDA to free cash flow; for audit and reporting roles, it is where errors in classification — capex creditors, ROU additions, ECL double counts, interest classification — surface. India has specific features: Ind AS 7.33 removes the IAS 7 policy choice for interest and dividends for non-financial entities (interest paid is financing; interest and dividends received are investing), bank overdrafts repayable on demand form part of cash and cash equivalents, and earmarked balances such as unpaid dividend accounts sit in "other bank balances", outside cash and cash equivalents.

The subtopics follow the build order of a cash flow statement: start from PBT and strip out non-cash and non-operating items (s01); compute working-capital movements, excluding non-operating balances (s02); classify interest and dividends (s03) and lease flows (s04); define the cash boundary (s05); exclude non-cash transactions (s06); reconcile financing liabilities and treat acquisitions/disposals of subsidiaries (s07); and isolate the effect of exchange-rate changes (s08). The concept notes give the formulas you will implement in a balance-sheet-driven cash flow model with a closing-cash check.

Related concepts to study

IAS 7 policy choice, IFRS 18 and US GAAP classification

IAS 7 allows a policy choice for interest/dividends (removed by IFRS 18 from 2027); US GAAP ASC 230 classifies interest paid as operating

IAS 7.31–34 let entities classify interest paid and interest/dividends received as operating, investing or financing, applied consistently. IFRS 18 (effective 1 January 2027) amends IAS 7: for entities without a specified main business activity (e.g., not banks or investment entities), interest paid and dividends paid become financing, and interest and dividends received become investing; the indirect method will start from "operating profit" rather than profit or loss. Ind AS 7 already removed the choice for non-financial entities via 7.33 — so Indian practice is closer to the IFRS 18 outcome. ASC 230 requires interest paid and interest/dividends received to be operating, and dividends paid financing — a major source of CFO differences when comparing Indian and US peers.

FCFF and FCFE from Ind AS 7 lines

FCFF/FCFE derived from Ind AS 7 lines for valuation and debt capacity work

Free cash flow to the firm is cash available to all capital providers. Because Ind AS 7 places interest paid in financing, Indian CFO is already pre-interest, but taxes paid in CFO are after the interest tax shield; so \(\text{FCFF} = \text{CFO} - \text{Interest paid} \times t - \text{Capex}\), i.e., FCFF ≈ CFO − capex (with care over lease principal and interest classified in financing — analysts often deduct lease payments to make FCFF comparable to pre-Ind AS 116 figures). FCFE = FCFF − interest × (1 − t) + net borrowing. Lenders use CFO-based ratios (CFO/debt, DSCR) for debt capacity. Reliable FCF requires clean classification — capex net of creditors, taxes paid rather than tax expense, and exclusion of non-cash ROU additions.

SEBI LODR half-yearly cash flow statement

SEBI LODR Reg 33 half-yearly cash flow statement in results

Since the 2019 amendment to the LODR Regulations (Reg 33(3)(g), applicable from the half-year ended 30 September 2019), listed entities must disclose a statement of cash flows for the half-year as part of the standalone and consolidated financial results for the half-year ending 30 September, alongside the statement of assets and liabilities. The format follows Ind AS 7; the indirect method is common. This makes September cut-off quality for working capital, capex creditors and borrowings as important as March, and means FP&A teams must produce an auditable half-yearly cash flow within 45 days.

Lease cash flows and TB tagging

→ Week 2 (w2_t2) lease cash flows; → Week 4 (w4_t5) tagging every TB account with a CF category

Week 2 covers Ind AS 116 measurement — lease liabilities, ROU assets, modifications — which drive the lease lines in the cash flow (s04). Week 4's TB-mapping engine tags every ledger with a Schedule III line, a current/non-current flag and a cash flow category (operating WC, non-cash add-back, investing, financing, cash). That tagging lets a cash flow be generated automatically from two trial balances: movements are bucketed by tag, non-cash items are reversed from the relevant movement (e.g., ROU additions out of investing), and a check confirms that the sum of all categories equals the change in cash.

Excel: balance-sheet-driven cash flow

Excel: balance-sheet-driven cash flow with sign conventions and a closing-cash check flag

A robust model derives the cash flow from the balance sheet: every BS line (except cash) is mapped to a CF line; the CF line equals the negative of the change in an asset or the change in a liability, adjusted for non-cash movements sourced from the notes (depreciation, ECL, FX, acquisitions). Sign convention: positive = inflow. The check flag is =ROUND(OpeningCash + CFO + CFI + CFF + FX - ClosingCash, 0) = 0, with conditional formatting on failure. Every BS line must be used exactly once — a second check sums the absolute use of each line.

Common pitfalls

Adding back ECL and also using gross receivable change (double count)

Mistake: adding back the ECL/bad-debt provision as a non-cash item while computing the receivable movement on gross (pre-allowance) balances, or vice versa leaving out the add-back while using net balances. Why it happens: the impairment note shows the allowance, and preparers mechanically add back every provision. Correct treatment: choose one approach — either (a) add back ECL expense and use gross receivables movement adjusted for write-offs, or (b) do not add back ECL and use the net receivables movement. The common Indian presentation adds back the ECL charge and uses the movement in gross receivables — then write-offs against the allowance must be reflected consistently. Detection: recompute CFO both ways; they must give the same answer.

Treating ROU additions or unpaid capex as cash investing outflows

Mistake: taking "additions to PPE" from the fixed asset note (including ROU assets, capitalised decommissioning costs and capex still payable) as "purchase of PPE". Why it happens: the note is the easiest source. Correct treatment: only cash paid for capex is investing (7.16); ROU additions are non-cash (7.43), decommissioning assets are non-cash; adjust for the movement in capital creditors and capital advances (c04). Detection: reconcile capex outflow to additions less non-cash items less increase in capex creditors plus increase in capital advances.

Classifying interest paid as operating for a manufacturing company under Ind AS

Mistake: presenting interest paid within operating activities, copying IAS 7/US GAAP practice. Why it happens: global templates and IFRS-reporting parent packs. Correct treatment: Ind AS 7.33 — for non-financial entities, interest paid is a financing cash flow, interest and dividends received are investing; dividends paid are financing (7.34). Financial entities (banks, NBFCs) classify interest as operating. Borrowing costs capitalised under Ind AS 23 are still interest paid — financing (practice) rather than investing, with disclosure. Detection: check that finance cost is added back in CFO and interest paid appears in CFF.

Using total tax expense (incl. deferred) as taxes paid

Mistake: deducting the P&L tax expense (current + deferred) or OCI tax as "income taxes paid". Why it happens: shortcut from P&L. Correct treatment: taxes paid = opening net current tax liability + current tax expense in P&L − closing net current tax liability (c03), excluding deferred tax and tax charged to OCI/equity (unless current tax in OCI is paid, which is then included consistently); classified as operating unless specifically identified with investing/financing (7.35–36). Detection: reconcile the tax paid line to TDS/advance tax challans and refunds received.

Q1. Full cash flow from two balance sheets [L3]

Saffron Polymers Ltd (FY ending 31 March 2025). P&L: revenue ₹900 crore; PBT ₹110 crore after depreciation ₹45 crore (including ROU depreciation ₹6 crore), ECL ₹4 crore, unrealised FX loss on trade payables ₹2 crore, finance cost ₹28 crore (including lease interest ₹3 crore), interest income ₹5 crore, dividend from mutual funds ₹1 crore, profit on sale of land ₹8 crore, share-based payment expense ₹3 crore. Current tax ₹26 crore; deferred tax ₹3 crore. Balance sheet movements (₹ crore, opening → closing): gross trade receivables 140 → 175 (write-offs against allowance ₹2 crore); contract assets 20 → 32; inventories 160 → 150; trade payables 120 → 138 (incl. FX loss); capital creditors 10 → 18; current tax liability (net) 6 → 9; provisions for employee benefits 15 → 18; PPE additions ₹70 crore (incl. ₹12 crore ROU, ₹3 crore decommissioning asset); land sold carrying amount ₹12 crore; borrowings 220 → 250; lease liabilities 30 → 33 (lease payments ₹12 crore including interest ₹3 crore); dividends paid ₹15 crore; cash and cash equivalents (net of overdraft ₹10 crore) 25 → ?. (a) Prepare the cash flow statement. (b) Derive closing cash and prove it. (c) Prepare the 7.44A reconciliation.

💡 Hint: Work line by line through non-cash adjustments before touching working capital, and keep ROU and decommissioning additions out of investing.

Q2. Group cash flow with acquisition and FX [L3]

The group's USD 10 million deposit was translated at ₹82 at 1 April 2024 and ₹85.5 at 31 March 2025, with no flows. (a) Compute the net cash outflow on acquisition. (b) Compute the working capital movement in receivables. (c) Show the FX effect line. (d) List non-cash disclosures.

Q3. Review of a draft statement [L2]

Identify each error, quantify corrections and state the corrected CFO effect.
