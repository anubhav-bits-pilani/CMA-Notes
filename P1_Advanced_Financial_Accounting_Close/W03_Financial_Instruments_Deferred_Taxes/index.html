Objective: Execute technical accounting for financial assets/liabilities, Expected Credit Loss (ECL), deferred tax balance-sheet proofs, and acquisition accounting.

How this week fits

Week 3 closes the technical-accounting core of Phase 1 (Advanced Financial Accounting & Close Mastery). Weeks 1 and 2 built the presentation layer (Schedule III, Ind AS 1/7/8, Ind AS 115 revenue) and the long-dated measurement schedules (Ind AS 116 leases, Ind AS 19 gratuity, Ind AS 36 impairment, Ind AS 37 provisions). This week adds the three areas where Indian reporting teams most often get qualified audit comments or restate numbers: financial instruments (Ind AS 109/32/107/113), deferred tax (Ind AS 12 under the 115BAA regime) and group accounting (Ind AS 103/110).

The sequence matters. Classification and EIR (t1) produce amortised-cost schedules; ECL (t2) produces loss allowances; together with the Week 2 lease and gratuity schedules these become the carrying amounts that deferred tax (t3) compares with tax bases. Acquisition accounting (t4) then creates fair value step-ups and goodwill that carry their own deferred tax, and consolidation (t5) layers eliminations, NCI and consolidation-level deferred tax on top. Fair value hierarchy and risk disclosures (t6) wrap the whole instrument set in Ind AS 107/113 notes.

Prerequisites: the CMA Final CFR syllabus on Ind AS 109/32 and consolidation, business income computation under the Income-tax Act, and comfort with IRR/NPV in Excel. What comes next: Week 4 turns these schedules into a month-end close (tax provisioning step, intercompany reconciliation as a pre-consolidation control), and from Phase 2 onwards the same schedules feed the debt, tax and working-capital blocks of a 3-statement model.

Refresh before starting

CMA Final CFR: Ind AS 109/32 basics, consolidation and business combinations

  • Ind AS 32 defines a financial liability by the contractual obligation to deliver cash; equity is the residual. Fixed-for-fixed conversion keeps a conversion feature in equity; variable-number settlement makes it a liability.
  • Ind AS 109 has three measurement buckets for debt assets (amortised cost, FVOCI with recycling, FVTPL) and an irrevocable FVOCI election for non-trading equity (no recycling).
  • Consolidation mechanics from CFR: cost of control/goodwill table, pre- vs post-acquisition profit split, NCI at proportionate share, elimination of intra-group sales and unrealised profit in closing stock.
  • Ind AS 103 differs from AS 14/AS 21: acquisition date fair values, acquisition costs expensed, bargain purchase to capital reserve, common control combinations at book values (Appendix C).

Re-solve two CFR past-paper consolidation problems before Day 4.

Business income: Sec 32, 36, 37, 40, 43B; MAT and 115BAA

  • Sec 32: block-of-assets WDV depreciation (15% plant, 10% buildings, 40% computers, 25% intangibles other than goodwill); half rate if put to use for less than 180 days; additional depreciation under Sec 32(1)(iia) not available under 115BAA.
  • Sec 36(1)(vii): bad debts allowed only on write-off in the books. Sec 37: general allowance for revenue expenditure; provisions for contingencies are not allowed.
  • Sec 40(a)(ia): 30% of expenditure disallowed for TDS default, allowed in the year the TDS is paid. Sec 40A(7): gratuity provision disallowed unless paid to an approved fund.
  • Sec 43B: statutory dues, bonus, leave encashment, interest to banks/NBFCs and (from AY 2024-25) MSME dues under Sec 43B(h) allowed only on actual payment.
  • MAT (Sec 115JB) at 15% of book profit for old-regime companies; MAT credit carried forward 15 years. 115BAA: 22% base rate, 10% surcharge, 4% cess, no MAT, no specified deductions, irrevocable.

IRR/DCF maths and basic probability

  • IRR solves \( \sum_{t} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^{t}} = 0 \); XIRR uses actual dates with a 365-day year. Sign convention: inflow positive, outflow negative.
  • Present value of a single amount \( PV = \frac{CF}{(1+r)^{t}} \); annuity factor for level repayments.
  • Expected value \( E[X] = \sum p_i x_i \) with \( \sum p_i = 1 \); conditional probability for roll rates (probability of moving to the next bucket given current bucket).
  • Scenario weighting: ECL is the probability-weighted average of losses across scenarios, not the loss in the most likely scenario.

Week 2 lease and gratuity schedules as deferred tax inputs

  • Ind AS 116 schedule gives closing lease liability and closing ROU asset; tax base of both is nil when the lease rent is deductible on payment, so the liability creates a deductible difference and the ROU asset a taxable difference.
  • Ind AS 19 gratuity schedule gives the closing defined benefit obligation and the split of cost between P&L (service cost, net interest) and OCI (remeasurements). Tax base is nil (deduction on payment under Sec 40A(7)/43B), so DTA arises, with the OCI part of the movement going to OCI.
  • Ind AS 37 decommissioning provisions and Ind AS 36 impairment losses also feed the temporary-difference table.

Keep the closing balances from your Week 2 workbook; they are inputs to this week's DT exercise.

Cross-cutting concepts

Amortised-cost logic across instruments and provisions

One engine sits behind the EIR term loan, the discounted security deposit, the lease liability and the unwinding decommissioning provision: a carrying amount that starts at a present value, accretes interest at a constant rate, and is reduced by cash flows. In every case the closing balance is Opening + Interest at the effective rate − Cash paid, and the balance must reach zero (or the final settlement amount) on the last date. The rate differs by standard: the EIR for Ind AS 109 instruments (fixed at inception, including fees), the incremental borrowing rate or rate implicit in the lease for Ind AS 116, and a pre-tax risk-free-adjusted rate reset at each reporting date for Ind AS 37 provisions. Recognising this shared structure lets you build one reusable Excel block (dates, opening, interest, cash, closing, check) and swap only the rate logic. It also makes audit review faster: any schedule whose final balance is not zero, whose interest does not equal opening × rate × period, or whose cash column does not reconcile to the bank book is broken. In a 3-statement model the same block drives finance cost in the P&L, the debt line on the balance sheet and the financing section of the cash flow.

Tag every schedule with carrying amount and tax base

Deferred tax is only as good as the inventory of balances behind it. The practical discipline is to add two columns to every schedule built in Weeks 1–3: the Ind AS carrying amount at the reporting date and the tax base under the Income-tax Act. The PPE register carries book WDV and tax block WDV; the lease schedule carries ROU and lease liability against nil tax base; the gratuity and leave schedules carry the obligation against nil; the ECL schedule carries the allowance against nil (deduction on write-off); the EIR loan schedule carries the net-of-fees borrowing against the gross principal tax base; Ind AS 115 contract balances carry revenue timing differences where ICDS differs. A single DT sheet then pulls these with SUMIFS by a "DT category" tag, multiplies by the enacted rate for the reversal period, and splits movement between P&L, OCI and equity. This converts deferred tax from a year-end guess into a mechanical, auditable output with a clear trail from each schedule.

The fair value thread across Ind AS 103, 36, 109 and 113

Fair value appears in four places this phase and is always defined by Ind AS 113: an exit price in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. In Ind AS 103 it measures identifiable assets and liabilities in the PPA (plant via cost approach, brands via relief-from-royalty, customer relationships via MEEM) and contingent consideration. In Ind AS 36 it is one leg of recoverable amount (fair value less costs of disposal, FVLCD). In Ind AS 109 it is the measurement basis for FVTPL and FVOCI instruments and the starting point for initial recognition of everything. Ind AS 113 then requires classification of each recurring measurement into Level 1, 2 or 3 and, for Level 3, a roll-forward and sensitivity. The connecting skill is the same valuation toolkit (market, income, cost approaches) applied with market-participant, not entity-specific, assumptions. A Level 3 input used in a PPA (say a customer attrition rate) should be consistent with the inputs used in the subsequent goodwill impairment test and disclosed consistently in the hierarchy note.

EIR exercise: fees, moratorium, repayment profile, XIRR, DT on EIR

The ₹10 Cr term loan exercise combines every EIR input: processing fee and stamp duty deducted from proceeds, a moratorium period where interest is paid but principal is not, a repayment profile (equal quarterly instalments after moratorium) and actual dates. XIRR on the net cash flows (net proceeds positive, interest and principal negative) gives the EIR; the amortised-cost schedule applies EIR × day-count to opening balance, deducts actual cash, and must close at zero. The difference between EIR interest and contractual interest is the fee unwind. For tax, interest is deductible on the contractual accrual basis (subject to Sec 43B where the lender is a bank or NBFC) and the processing fee is normally claimed under Sec 36(1)(iii) when incurred, because Sec 2(28A) includes a service fee or other charge on borrowed money within "interest" (capitalisation rules apply where the loan funds an asset not yet put to use); the tax base of the borrowing is therefore the gross principal, and the unamortised fee is a taxable temporary difference that reverses over the loan life. The schedule should output the closing temporary difference for the DT sheet.

DT exercise: temporary-difference table, roll-forward and ETR

The deferred tax exercise asks for a table listing each temporary difference (PPE, ROU asset, lease liability, gratuity, ECL allowance, loan fees) with carrying amount, tax base, difference, nature (taxable or deductible) and DT at 25.168%. The roll-forward then proves: Opening net DTL + P&L charge + OCI charge ± acquisitions ± rate-change effect = Closing net DTL. The P&L and OCI split comes from the source: the actuarial remeasurement on gratuity moves through OCI (Ind AS 12.61A), everything else here moves through P&L. The ETR reconciliation starts with PBT × 25.168%, adds back the tax effect of permanent items (CSR expenditure disallowed under Sec 37 Explanation 2, penalties, 50% of donations where eligible), adjusts for income at different rates (LTCG on investments), unrecognised DTA and prior-year true-ups, and lands on total tax expense. Every number must trace back to a schedule; the closing DTL on the table must equal the balance sheet figure.

Consolidation-level deferred tax over standalone DT

Standalone deferred tax compares each entity's own carrying amounts with its own tax bases. Consolidation creates new carrying amounts that exist only in the group financial statements, and deferred tax must be recomputed on them. Two sources dominate. First, PPA fair value step-ups on plant and intangibles increase group carrying amounts while the subsidiary's tax base is unchanged, so a DTL is recognised at acquisition (increasing goodwill) and unwinds as the step-up is depreciated or amortised. Second, unrealised profit on intra-group inventory reduces the group carrying amount of inventory below the holding entity's tax base, creating a DTA at the tax rate of the entity holding the inventory (Ind AS 12 applies the buyer's rate because the buyer's tax base is cost to it). Other layers include undistributed profits of subsidiaries (Ind AS 12.39, usually exempt where the parent controls dividend timing) and dividend distribution effects. Model these as a separate consolidation-adjustment DT schedule rather than editing entity numbers.

Hands-on exercise brief

Roadmap exercise: Construct an EIR debt amortization model for a ₹10 Cr term loan with upfront transaction costs, plus an Ind AS 12 Deferred Tax reconciliation table proving opening-to-closing DTL.

Inputs and assumptions

Item Assumption
Borrower Vardhan Components Ltd, unlisted manufacturer, FY ending 31 March, opted 115BAA from FY 2020-21
Loan ₹10,00,00,000 term loan from a scheduled bank, drawn 1 April 2025
Contractual rate 9.75% p.a. fixed, interest payable quarterly on 30 June, 30 September, 31 December, 31 March
Moratorium 12 months on principal; interest paid throughout
Repayment 16 equal quarterly principal instalments of ₹62.5 lakh from 30 June 2026 to 31 March 2030
Transaction costs Processing fee 1% + 18% GST (GST input credit available), stamp duty ₹5 lakh, legal and valuation fees ₹3 lakh
Day count Actual/365 for both contractual interest and EIR
Tax treatment Interest deductible on accrual (paid within due dates, so Sec 43B satisfied); transaction costs claimed as deduction in FY 2025-26
Tax rate 25.168%

For the DT table use these 31 March 2026 balances (opening 1 April 2025 in brackets): PPE book WDV ₹48.0 Cr (₹45.0 Cr), tax WDV ₹39.5 Cr (₹38.2 Cr); ROU asset ₹6.2 Cr (₹7.1 Cr), lease liability ₹6.9 Cr (₹7.5 Cr); gratuity obligation ₹3.4 Cr (₹2.9 Cr), of which ₹0.2 Cr of the increase is actuarial loss in OCI; ECL allowance ₹1.1 Cr (₹0.8 Cr); loan transaction costs as computed in your EIR schedule (nil opening). PBT for FY 2025-26 is ₹22.0 Cr; CSR expense ₹0.45 Cr; penalty for delayed compliance ₹0.05 Cr; current tax per computation to be derived; prior-year current tax short provision ₹0.12 Cr.

Required tabs

  1. Inputs — every assumption above in one place, named ranges, no hard-codes elsewhere.
  2. Cash flows — dated schedule of drawdown, transaction costs, interest and principal; XIRR-based EIR and a cross-check by IRR on equal-period flows.
  3. EIR schedule — date, days, opening amortised cost, EIR interest, contractual interest, fee unwind (difference), cash paid, closing amortised cost, current/non-current split at each 31 March.
  4. Journal entries — initial recognition and the first two quarters' entries, plus year-end classification.
  5. DT table — carrying amount, tax base, temporary difference, taxable/deductible, DT at 25.168% for opening and closing.
  6. DT roll-forward — opening net DTL, P&L charge, OCI charge, closing net DTL, tie-out to the DT table.
  7. ETR reconciliation — PBT × statutory rate to total tax expense, with the effective rate as a percentage.
  8. Checks — closing amortised cost = 0 at 31 March 2030; total EIR interest = total contractual interest + total transaction costs; DT roll-forward closing = DT table closing; ETR reconciliation total = P&L tax expense; all checks feeding a single TRUE/FALSE flag.

Deliverable

One Excel workbook with the tabs above, formulas visible, input cells shaded, and a one-page memo explaining the EIR, the year-end current maturity of borrowings and the three largest drivers of the deferred tax movement.

Saturday assessment paper

Roadmap assessment: Saturday 3-Hour Case Test: Prepare a consolidated Balance Sheet and P&L for a Parent + 80% Subsidiary acquired mid-year with fair value step-up on plant and inter-company inventory.

Instructions: Time 3 hours. Total 100 marks. Answer all questions. Work in Excel or on paper; show workings. Tax rate 25.168% for both entities unless stated. Amounts in ₹ crore. FY ends 31 March 2026.

Q1. Acquisition-date PPA and goodwill (20 marks)

Pratham Industries Ltd (P) acquired 80% of Sagar Polymers Ltd (S) on 1 October 2025 for cash ₹96 Cr plus contingent consideration with fair value ₹6 Cr (payable if S's FY 2026-27 EBITDA exceeds ₹30 Cr). Acquisition-related legal and due-diligence costs were ₹1.5 Cr. S's book net assets at 1 October 2025 were ₹85 Cr (share capital ₹20 Cr, retained earnings ₹65 Cr). Fair value of S's plant exceeded its carrying amount by ₹16 Cr (remaining useful life 8 years); an unrecognised customer relationship had fair value ₹8 Cr (life 5 years). Tax bases are unaffected by the acquisition. NCI is measured at proportionate share of identifiable net assets. (a) Compute identifiable net assets including deferred tax on step-ups. (b) Compute goodwill. (c) Pass the acquisition-date consolidation entries, including treatment of acquisition costs.

Q2. Individual financial statements for consolidation (10 marks)

Summarised FY 2025-26 P&L (full year): P — Revenue ₹420 Cr, cost of sales ₹300 Cr, other expenses ₹55 Cr, finance cost ₹8 Cr, tax ₹14.5 Cr. S — Revenue ₹180 Cr, cost of sales ₹120 Cr, other expenses ₹30 Cr, finance cost ₹4 Cr, tax ₹6.5 Cr. S's profits accrue evenly. Balance sheets at 31 March 2026: P — PPE ₹210 Cr, investment in S ₹102 Cr (including contingent consideration recognised by P), inventory ₹60 Cr, receivables ₹70 Cr, cash ₹25 Cr, share capital ₹100 Cr, other equity ₹230 Cr, borrowings ₹80 Cr, payables ₹51 Cr, contingent consideration liability ₹6 Cr. S — PPE ₹70 Cr, inventory ₹30 Cr, receivables ₹40 Cr, cash ₹15 Cr, share capital ₹20 Cr, other equity ₹80 Cr, borrowings ₹30 Cr, payables ₹25 Cr. Split S's profit into pre- and post-acquisition and identify which P&L lines are consolidated for the period 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2026.

Q3. Intra-group inventory (15 marks)

From 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2026 S sold goods to P for ₹24 Cr at a markup of 25% on cost. At 31 March 2026 P held one-third of these goods. P owed S ₹5 Cr at year-end. Compute the unrealised profit, the deferred tax effect and the NCI share, and pass elimination entries for sales, purchases, balances and unrealised profit.

Q4. Step-up depreciation and NCI (15 marks)

Compute post-acquisition depreciation and amortisation on the plant and customer relationship step-ups, the related deferred tax unwind, the adjusted post-acquisition profit of S, and NCI's share of profit for the year and NCI at 31 March 2026.

Q5. Contingent consideration remeasurement (10 marks)

At 31 March 2026 the fair value of the contingent consideration is ₹7.2 Cr due to a revised EBITDA forecast; the change is not a measurement-period adjustment. State where the ₹1.2 Cr change is recognised, pass the entry, and explain how the answer would differ if the information had existed at the acquisition date and been obtained by 31 March 2026.

Q6. Consolidated Balance Sheet and P&L (25 marks)

Using Q1–Q5, prepare the consolidated Balance Sheet at 31 March 2026 and the consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss for FY 2025-26 in Schedule III Division II format, showing profit attributable to owners and to NCI, and a check that total assets equal total equity and liabilities.

Q7. Reviewer's note (5 marks)

In not more than 150 words, list the five review checks a group financial controller should perform before signing off this consolidation.

💡 Hint: Think about tie-outs that would catch an error in goodwill, NCI, intra-group balances, deferred tax and the pre/post-acquisition split.
