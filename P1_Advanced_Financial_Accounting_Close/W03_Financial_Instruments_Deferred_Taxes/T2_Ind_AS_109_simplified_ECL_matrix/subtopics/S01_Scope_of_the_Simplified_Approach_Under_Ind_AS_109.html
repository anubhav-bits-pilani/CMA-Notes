Subtopic note

Scope of the Simplified Approach Under Ind AS 109.5.5.15

Scope: Scope: trade receivables and contract assets without SFC (mandatory simplified), lease receivables and SFC receivables (policy choice) (5.5.15)

Why it matters

Before building a matrix, you must know which balances belong in it. A typical Indian manufacturer has trade receivables, unbilled revenue (contract assets), retention money, security deposits, employee loans, inter-corporate deposits and loans to subsidiaries — and they are not all measured the same way. Mixing general-approach assets into the simplified matrix (or vice versa) misstates the allowance and the Ind AS 107 disclosures, and is a common audit comment. The scope decision is also a policy choice that must be disclosed and applied consistently.

Core notes

The impairment scope (5.5.1)

Ind AS 109's ECL requirements apply to: financial assets at amortised cost; debt instruments at FVOCI; lease receivables (Ind AS 116); contract assets (Ind AS 115); loan commitments not at FVTPL; and financial guarantee contracts not at FVTPL. Equity investments and FVTPL assets are outside ECL.

Three approaches

  • General approach (5.5.3–5.5.5): 12-month ECL at initial recognition (Stage 1), lifetime ECL if credit risk has increased significantly (Stage 2) or the asset is credit-impaired (Stage 3). Requires tracking credit risk from initial recognition.
  • Simplified approach (5.5.15): loss allowance always at lifetime ECL; no SICR tracking.
  • Purchased or originated credit-impaired (5.5.13): cumulative changes in lifetime ECL since initial recognition, with a credit-adjusted EIR.

What 5.5.15 requires

The entity shall always measure the loss allowance at an amount equal to lifetime ECL for:

Asset Treatment
Trade receivables or contract assets within Ind AS 115 that do not contain a significant financing component (or where the 115.63 practical expedient for ≤ 1 year is applied) Mandatory simplified approach (5.5.15(a)(i))
Trade receivables or contract assets that contain a significant financing component Policy choice — simplified or general (5.5.15(a)(ii))
Lease receivables (finance and operating) Policy choice (5.5.15(b)); may be chosen separately for finance and operating lease receivables (5.5.16)

The policy choices are applied independently to (a)(ii) and (b) assets, and must be applied consistently to all such assets within the category (5.5.16).

Why "mandatory" for short-term receivables

For short-term receivables without significant financing, the difference between 12-month and lifetime ECL is negligible (the life is usually under a year), and staging would add cost without benefit. Mandating lifetime ECL removes the SICR tracking burden.

What is outside 5.5.15 — general approach applies

  • Security deposits (rent, electricity, utilities) — financial assets at amortised cost; general approach, usually Stage 1 with low ECL.
  • Loans to employees, ICDs, loans to subsidiaries — general approach.
  • Retention money receivable on construction contracts: usually a trade receivable or contract asset depending on whether the right is unconditional. If it is a contract asset with significant financing (multi-year retention) the policy choice applies.
  • Other receivables such as insurance claims, export incentives receivable from government (MEIS, RoDTEP scrips are non-financial), GST refunds (statutory, not contractual — outside Ind AS 109 entirely) — these need scoping carefully. Statutory receivables like GST input credit, income-tax refunds and duty drawback are not financial assets (no contract) and are outside ECL; they are assessed under other standards.
  • Bank balances and fixed deposits — general approach; ECL is usually immaterial for scheduled banks (low credit risk simplification 5.5.10).

Significant financing component — when it arises

Ind AS 115.60–65: a contract has a significant financing component if the timing of payment agreed provides a significant benefit of financing. Indicators in India: deferred payment schemes for capital equipment (12–36 months), extended credit periods to DISCOMs or government departments under contract, instalment sales. Where the gap between transfer of goods and payment is a year or less, the practical expedient (115.63) allows the entity to ignore the financing effect, keeping the receivable in mandatory simplified scope.

Chetan Machines Ltd, 31 March 2026:

Trade receivables, 60–90 day credit 145 Ind AS 115 receivable, no SFC Simplified — mandatory

GST input credit 9 Statutory, non-financial Outside Ind AS 109

The accounting policy note states: "The Company applies the simplified approach to trade receivables, contract assets and lease receivables, including those with a significant financing component." If the company had elected the general approach for the deferred-payment receivables, it would need SICR tracking for those balances.

Practical consequences of the policy choice

  • Simplified approach → lifetime ECL from day one, so the allowance is higher at origination, especially for long-dated receivables; but no staging data is needed.
  • General approach → 12-month ECL at origination for long receivables (lower allowance) but requires PD term structures and SICR monitoring.
  • Most Indian non-financial companies elect simplified for all receivables and lease receivables for operational simplicity.

Disclosure

Ind AS 107.35F requires the credit risk management practices and how they relate to recognition and measurement; 107.35N allows a provision matrix to be presented for simplified-approach assets. The accounting policy should state the approach chosen for each category.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unbilled revenue in IT services — if invoicing depends only on passage of time, it is a receivable (unconditional right), not a contract asset; ECL approach is the same, but Schedule III presentation differs.
  • Receivables sold under factoring with recourse remain on balance sheet and remain in scope.
  • Receivables from group companies are trade receivables and fall in mandatory simplified scope; the matrix rate may be adjusted for parental support (s06).
  • Bill discounting with recourse — the receivable stays; the bank liability is separate.
  • Lessors applying simplified to operating lease receivables (rent receivable) — straightforward; for finance lease receivables of a leasing NBFC, the general approach is more common to align with loan books.

Key takeaways

  • ECL applies to amortised-cost and FVOCI debt assets, lease receivables, contract assets, loan commitments and financial guarantees.
  • Simplified (lifetime ECL) is mandatory for trade receivables and contract assets without a significant financing component.
  • For receivables/contract assets with SFC and for lease receivables, simplified is a policy choice made separately for each category.
  • Deposits, loans and ICDs follow the general approach; statutory receivables are outside Ind AS 109.
  • Scope decisions must be documented in the accounting policy and reflected in Ind AS 107 disclosures.

Practice Questions

Q1. Scoping a mixed receivables ledger [L1]

A company's "Sundry debtors and other receivables" ledger includes: trade debtors ₹80 Cr (45-day credit); retention money ₹12 Cr receivable on completion of performance guarantee period of 2 years; income-tax refund ₹3 Cr; rent deposit ₹1.5 Cr; staff advances ₹0.5 Cr; rental receivable under an operating lease ₹0.8 Cr. State the ECL approach for each, with reasons.

💡 Hint: Check whether each item arises from a contract, whether it is Ind AS 115 or Ind AS 116 related, and whether it has a significant financing component.

Q2. Policy choice impact [L2]

A capital-equipment maker has ₹50 Cr of 3-year instalment receivables with a significant financing component. Lifetime PD is 6%, 12-month PD 2%, LGD 45%. Compute the allowance under the simplified approach and under the general approach at initial recognition (ignore discounting), and discuss the operational trade-offs of each choice.

Q3. Contract asset vs receivable [L2]

An EPC contractor has billed ₹40 Cr on milestones (customer must pay within 60 days) and has ₹15 Cr of work done but not yet certified. Classify each balance, state the ECL approach and explain whether a single loss rate can be applied to both.

Q4. Audit observation [L3]

During audit, you find the company has applied a 2% matrix rate to its ₹25 Cr security deposits with state electricity boards and landlords, along with trade receivables. Draft the audit observation and the recommended corrected approach.
