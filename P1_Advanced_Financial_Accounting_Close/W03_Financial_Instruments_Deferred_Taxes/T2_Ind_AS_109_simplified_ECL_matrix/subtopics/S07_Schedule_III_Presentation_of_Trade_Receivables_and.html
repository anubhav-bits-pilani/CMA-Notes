Subtopic note

Schedule III Presentation of Trade Receivables and Ageing

Scope: Schedule III presentation: considered good–secured/unsecured, significant increase in credit risk, credit impaired; ageing incl. disputed

Why it matters

The 2021 amendments to Schedule III (effective FY 2021-22) made trade receivables one of the most heavily formatted notes in Indian financial statements. Lenders, rating agencies and investors read the ageing schedule to spot old and disputed balances; NFRA and peer reviewers check consistency between the ageing, the credit risk categories and the ECL allowance. A provision matrix that cannot be mapped to the Schedule III categories creates last-minute reworking at year-end. For the target roles, preparing this note — and building the Power Query/Excel pipeline that produces it from the ERP ageing — is routine close work.

Core notes

Classification categories (Division II, Part I, Note on trade receivables)

Trade receivables are sub-classified as:

  1. Trade receivables considered good — Secured
  2. Trade receivables considered good — Unsecured
  3. Trade receivables which have significant increase in credit risk
  4. Trade receivables — credit impaired

Allowance for bad and doubtful debts is disclosed under the relevant heads separately. Debts due by directors or other officers of the company, or any of them severally or jointly with any other person, or debts due by firms or private companies in which any director is a partner, a director or a member, are separately stated. Trade receivables are split into non-current (Note under non-current financial assets) and current.

Secured means backed by security such as letters of credit, bank guarantees, security deposits held (to the extent available for set-off) or collateral. Credit insurance is generally not "security" in the Schedule III sense, though it affects ECL.

Mapping ECL categories to Schedule III

Under the simplified approach, entities do not track SICR for measurement. But Schedule III still requires the SICR category. The ICAI Guidance Note on Division II indicates that entities need to evaluate the categories for presentation. A practical mapping many companies adopt:

Schedule III category Practical criteria
Considered good Not past due or past due within the rebuttable 30-day window, no adverse indicators
SICR Past due beyond 30 days (5.5.11 presumption) or on watch list, not credit impaired
Credit impaired Past due beyond 90 days (B5.5.37 presumption) where not rebutted, or individually impaired (IBC, liquidation, serious distress)

Some companies, with justification, use longer thresholds for specific segments (e.g., government receivables that are routinely 180 days late but not credit-impaired). The criteria must be disclosed and consistently applied.

The ageing schedule (2021 amendment)

Trade receivables ageing — outstanding for the following periods from due date of payment:

Particulars Not due < 6 months 6 months–1 year 1–2 years 2–3 years > 3 years Total
(i) Undisputed — considered good
(ii) Undisputed — SICR
(iii) Undisputed — credit impaired
(iv) Disputed — considered good
(v) Disputed — SICR
(vi) Disputed — credit impaired

Notes:

  • Where no due date of payment is specified, ageing is from the date of the transaction.
  • Unbilled dues are disclosed separately.
  • The "Not due" column is commonly added (Schedule III columns start at "less than 6 months" from due date; companies add not-due balances separately so the total reconciles).
  • The schedule is presented gross; the allowance is shown separately below to reconcile to the net carrying amount.
  • Comparative figures are required.

Disputed means the customer has formally disputed the amount (correspondence, arbitration, litigation) — not merely that collection is slow. Disputed balances that are price or quantity disputes may also need Ind AS 115 adjustments (s05).

Building the note

Power Query pipeline:

  1. Import customer-invoice ageing from ERP with due date, amount, dispute flag, security flag, segment.
  2. Compute days past due \( = \text{ReportingDate} - \text{DueDate} \); assign Schedule III bucket.
  3. Merge credit category (good/SICR/impaired) from the credit-risk table (DPD rules plus watch list overrides).
  4. Pivot by category × bucket; append unbilled dues; reconcile to GL.
= Table.AddColumn(Source, "DPD", each Duration.Days(ReportDate - [DueDate]), Int64.Type)
= Table.AddColumn(#"Added DPD", "Bucket", each if [DPD] <= 0 then "Not due" else if [DPD] <= 182 then "<6m" else if [DPD] <= 365 then "6m-1y" else if [DPD] <= 730 then "1-2y" else if [DPD] <= 1095 then "2-3y" else ">3y")

Division III (NBFCs)

NBFCs do not use the trade receivables ageing for loans; Division III requires loans to be classified by type, security, and sector (public/others), with Ind AS 107 staging disclosures. NBFCs present receivables (trade and other) with the same categories (considered good, SICR, credit impaired) but the main credit disclosures are stage-wise.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Credit-impaired but undisputed vs disputed — a customer in IBC is credit impaired; if it also disputes the claim before the resolution professional, show as disputed.
  • SICR category and simplified approach — explain the criteria in the accounting policy to avoid the appearance that the company is applying staging for measurement.
  • Netting of advances received from the same customer is not permitted in the ageing unless Ind AS 32.42 offset criteria are met.
  • Retention money — age from the date it becomes due under the contract; until then it may be a contract asset (disclosed outside trade receivables).
  • Related parties — disclose amounts from directors and director-interested entities separately; Ind AS 24 related-party balances are disclosed in the RPT note.
  • Ageing from invoice date vs due date — Schedule III specifies from due date; using invoice date overstates old buckets.

Key takeaways

  • Four classification categories: good–secured, good–unsecured, SICR, credit impaired; allowance shown under relevant heads.
  • The ageing schedule splits undisputed and disputed balances across good, SICR and credit impaired, with buckets from due date: <6 m, 6 m–1 y, 1–2 y, 2–3 y, >3 y.
  • Unbilled dues are disclosed separately; not-due balances are commonly shown in an added column.
  • Define and disclose the criteria used to map simplified-approach balances to SICR and credit impaired.
  • Automate the note from invoice-level ERP data and reconcile to the GL.

Practice Questions

Q1. Prepare the ageing schedule [L2]

From the following invoice-level data at 31 March 2026, prepare the Schedule III ageing and classification note: (a) ₹30 Cr not due; (b) ₹10 Cr 1–150 days past due, of which ₹4 Cr over 30 days; (c) ₹3 Cr 200–300 days past due, all from a customer on the watch list; (d) ₹2 Cr 800 days past due from a customer in liquidation; (e) ₹1.5 Cr 400 days past due, disputed on quality, customer solvent; (f) unbilled ₹2.5 Cr; LC-backed balances within (a) ₹6 Cr. Allowance ₹4.4 Cr. Use the 30/90 DPD criteria unless rebutted.

💡 Hint: Classify each line by credit category and dispute status first, then bucket by days past due.

Q2. Rebutting presumptions for presentation [L2]

A power-equipment maker's DISCOM receivables are routinely paid 120–200 days late but always in full with LPS interest. Explain whether these should be shown as SICR or credit impaired in Schedule III, what evidence supports rebutting the presumptions, and how the ECL is measured.

List five errors you would look for when reviewing a draft Schedule III trade receivables note.

Q4. Automating the note [L3]

Design an Excel/Power Query workflow for a group of four companies that produces the Schedule III ageing note, the classification note and an ECL reconciliation, with check rows that tie each to the trial balance. Specify inputs, transformations, outputs and checks.
