Subtopic note

Forward-Looking Overlays and Probability-Weighted Scenarios

Scope: Forward-looking overlays: GDP, sector stress, commodity prices, DISCOM payment behaviour; scenario weighting

Why it matters

The forward-looking element is what makes ECL "expected" rather than "incurred". Regulators (SEBI, NFRA) and auditors repeatedly flag provision matrices that simply reuse historical rates. For Indian businesses, the most powerful drivers are sector-specific — infrastructure and power-sector payment cycles, commodity prices for steel or agri-processing customers, MSME stress, state government fiscal positions — rather than headline GDP. A well-built overlay links each driver to loss rates, uses probability-weighted scenarios and is consistent with the forecasts used elsewhere (budgets, impairment tests, going concern).

Core notes

The requirement

ECL must reflect reasonable and supportable information that is available without undue cost or effort at the reporting date about past events, current conditions and forecasts of future economic conditions (5.5.17(c)). When using historical loss rates, the entity adjusts them on the basis of current observable data to reflect the effects of current conditions and forecasts that did not affect the historical period, and removes effects of past conditions not relevant to the future (B5.5.52). The estimate must be probability-weighted, considering at least the possibility that a loss occurs and that it does not (5.5.18, B5.5.42).

Choosing the drivers

Driver Relevance Data source
Real GDP growth Broad demand and payment capacity MoSPI, RBI Monetary Policy Report, IMF
Sector stress indices Steel, textiles, real estate, MSME credit stress RBI Financial Stability Report, rating agency sector outlooks, SIDBI MSME Pulse
Commodity prices Customers whose margins depend on input/output prices (steel, cotton, edible oil) LME, NCDEX, MCX, CMIE
Interest rates / liquidity Working capital stress of dealers and SMEs RBI repo, credit growth data
DISCOM payment behaviour Power, EPC and coal suppliers PRAAPTI portal (payment ratification), Late Payment Surcharge Rules 2022 instalment status, state budget allocations
Customer-specific signals Rating downgrades, covenant breaches, GST filing defaults CRISIL/ICRA/CARE, MCA filings, GST network

Linking drivers to loss rates

Three approaches, in increasing sophistication:

  1. Management overlay (qualitative): a judgemental percentage uplift to rates in stressed segments, justified by indicators. Acceptable when data is limited; must be documented and challenged.
  2. Scalar approach: compute a historical relationship (e.g., loss rate rises 12% for each 1 percentage point fall in GDP growth) and apply forecast GDP to derive the adjustment factor.
  3. Regression model: regress segment loss rates (or roll rates) on macro variables over the history; forecast loss rates under each scenario.

The adjustment is expressed as (c03):

\[ LR^{\text{adj}}_i = LR^{\text{hist}}_i \times (1 + FLA) \]

where \( FLA \) is the forward-looking adjustment for that segment and scenario.

Scenario weighting

Define at least two (commonly three) scenarios — base, downside, upside — each with a macro narrative, driver values and a probability weight summing to 100%. Compute ECL in each scenario and weight:

\[ ECL = \sum_{s} w_s \times ECL_s \]

Because losses are usually non-linear (downside losses rise faster than upside losses fall), probability-weighted ECL exceeds ECL under the base scenario alone. Using only the most likely scenario violates 5.5.17(a).

Illustration: DISCOM and private customers

Gautam Cables Ltd supplies private industrial customers and state DISCOMs. Historical lifetime loss rates for private customers: not due 0.6%, 1–90 DPD 2.5%, 91–180 DPD 12%, >180 DPD 45%. Balances: ₹80 Cr, ₹30 Cr, ₹8 Cr, ₹4 Cr.

Scenarios:

Scenario GDP growth Steel price index FLA on private rates Weight
Base 6.5% Stable +5% 60%
Downside 5.0% −15% +40% 30%
Upside 7.5% +5% −10% 10%

Historical ECL = 80(0.6%) + 30(2.5%) + 8(12%) + 4(45%) = 0.48 + 0.75 + 0.96 + 1.80 = ₹3.99 Cr.

Scenario ECL: base 3.99 × 1.05 = 4.19; downside 3.99 × 1.40 = 5.59; upside 3.99 × 0.90 = 3.59.

Weighted ECL = 0.6(4.19) + 0.3(5.59) + 0.1(3.59) = 2.51 + 1.68 + 0.36 = ₹4.55 Cr.

For DISCOM receivables of ₹120 Cr, credit loss is expected to be negligible but collection timing is the risk. Base: collected 9 months after due date; downside: 18 months (state fiscal stress, LPS instalment schedule). With an effective rate of 9% and no compensating interest:

  • Base shortfall ≈ 120 × \( (1 - 1.09^{-0.75}) \) = ₹7.51 Cr
  • Downside shortfall ≈ 120 × \( (1 - 1.09^{-1.5}) \) = ₹14.55 Cr
  • Weighted (70/30) ≈ ₹9.62 Cr

If the late payment surcharge under the Electricity (Late Payment Surcharge and Related Matters) Rules 2022 is contractually receivable and expected to be collected, those cash flows reduce the shortfall — include them only if collection is probable on the evidence.

Governance

  • Scenarios and weights approved by a management committee (CFO, credit head) each reporting period.
  • Consistency check with the budget, Ind AS 36 impairment cash flows and going-concern assessment.
  • Sensitivity disclosure: ECL under 100% downside, and effect of ±10% on loss rates (useful for Ind AS 1.125 estimation uncertainty disclosure).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Overlay with no historical correlation. If statistics show no link to GDP, use sector-specific or customer-specific drivers rather than forcing a macro overlay.
  • Post-model adjustments for events not in the model (e.g., a sudden export ban affecting a customer segment) should be separately tracked and released when captured in data.
  • Upside scenarios should not be given unreasonable weights to offset the downside.
  • Interim reporting — under Ind AS 34 the overlay should be refreshed for significant changes in outlook each quarter.
  • Undue cost or effort — an entity need not undertake exhaustive search for information, but must consider information available, including industry reports and credit agency outlooks.

Key takeaways

  • Historical rates must be adjusted for current conditions and reasonable, supportable forecasts (5.5.17(c), B5.5.52).
  • Choose drivers that actually explain losses in each segment — sector and payer-specific indicators often beat GDP.
  • Use at least two probability-weighted scenarios; weighted ECL usually exceeds base-case ECL.
  • For government and DISCOM receivables, time value of delayed collection is often the main ECL component.
  • Document scenarios, weights, governance and sensitivity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Scenario-weighted ECL [L2]

Historical ECL for a segment is ₹6.2 Cr. Management defines base (FLA +8%, weight 55%), downside (FLA +45%, weight 35%) and upside (FLA −12%, weight 10%). Compute probability-weighted ECL and the difference from base-only ECL, and explain why using only the base case would breach Ind AS 109.

💡 Hint: Weight the scenario outcomes, not the adjustment factors, when losses are non-linear.

Q2. DISCOM collection delay [L2]

An EPC company has ₹200 Cr receivable from a state DISCOM, due 31 March 2026. Expected collection: 40% after 6 months, 40% after 12 months and 20% after 24 months, without interest. The original effective rate is 10%. Compute the ECL from time value alone and discuss how an agreed instalment plan under the LPS Rules 2022 would change your estimate.

Q3. Choosing drivers [L3]

A company supplies packaging to edible-oil refiners and FMCG companies. Over five years, loss rates for refiners moved sharply with palm oil price swings, while FMCG loss rates were flat. Propose segment-specific forward-looking drivers, the data sources, how you would quantify the link and what you would present to the audit committee.

Q4. Interview: overlay challenge [L1]

An interviewer asks: "Your historical loss rate is 1.2%, and you have added 30% for forward-looking factors. How would you defend that 30% to an auditor?" Outline your answer.
