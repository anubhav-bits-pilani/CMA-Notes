Subtopic note

General Approach: 12-Month vs Lifetime ECL and Stages 1, 2, 3

Scope: General approach contrast: 12-month vs lifetime, SICR, stages 1/2/3 for loans and NBFCs

Why it matters

Corporates use the simplified approach for receivables, but the general approach governs their loans to subsidiaries, ICDs, employee loans, security deposits, investments in bonds at amortised cost or FVOCI, and financial guarantees given for group borrowings. For NBFCs and HFCs the general approach is the core of the P&L. FP&A professionals moving into lending businesses need to understand staging because credit cost, NIM and capital all depend on it; technical-accounting roles must defend SICR criteria and stage transfers to auditors and RBI inspectors.

Core notes

The three stages

Stage Condition Allowance Interest revenue basis
Stage 1 No significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition 12-month ECL (5.5.5) EIR × gross carrying amount
Stage 2 SICR since initial recognition, not credit-impaired Lifetime ECL (5.5.3) EIR × gross carrying amount
Stage 3 Credit-impaired Lifetime ECL EIR × amortised cost (net of allowance) (5.4.1(b))

12-month ECL is the portion of lifetime ECL resulting from default events possible within 12 months after the reporting date (Appendix A) — not the cash shortfalls expected in the next 12 months, but the lifetime loss on the exposure weighted by the 12-month probability of default.

Lifetime ECL covers all possible default events over the expected life.

Significant increase in credit risk (SICR)

At each reporting date, compare the risk of default over the expected life at the reporting date with the risk at initial recognition (5.5.9). Key features:

  • It is a relative test (change from origination), not an absolute level — a loan originated as BB and still BB stays in Stage 1.
  • Use reasonable and supportable forward-looking information (5.5.11); past-due information alone may be used only if forward-looking information is not available without undue cost.
  • Rebuttable presumption: credit risk has increased significantly when contractual payments are more than 30 days past due (5.5.11), unless the entity has reasonable and supportable information demonstrating otherwise.
  • Low credit risk exemption (5.5.10): an instrument with low credit risk at the reporting date (e.g., investment grade, BBB− or above) may be assumed not to have SICR.
  • Indicators (B5.5.17): changes in internal price indicators, external credit ratings, internal ratings, business/financial/economic conditions, operating results, collateral value, borrower behaviour; for NBFCs, restructuring, SMA-1/SMA-2 classifications under RBI early warning framework.

Default and credit-impaired

Default is not defined in Ind AS 109; entities define it consistently with internal credit risk management, but there is a rebuttable presumption that default does not occur later than 90 days past due (B5.5.37). Stage 3 aligns broadly with RBI's NPA classification (90 DPD), though RBI's upgrade rules (from November 2021, NPAs upgraded only on clearing all arrears) may differ from Ind AS cure criteria.

Movement between stages

Movement is symmetric: if SICR no longer exists, the asset returns to Stage 1 and 12-month ECL. Many NBFCs apply cure periods (e.g., 3–6 months of regular payments) before moving back, consistent with credit risk management. Modified/restructured loans are assessed against the original-recognition risk (5.5.12).

ECL measurement in the general approach

\[ ECL = \sum_{t} PD_t \times LGD_t \times EAD_t \times DF_t \]

with PD term structure (12-month for Stage 1, lifetime marginal PDs for Stage 2), LGD reflecting collateral, EAD including expected drawdowns for commitments, and discounting at the EIR (c01).

Illustration: corporate's loan to a subsidiary

Kavya Holdings Ltd lends ₹50 Cr to a subsidiary for 5 years at 10%. At origination the subsidiary is rated internally BBB; 12-month PD 1.0%, lifetime PD 6.0%, LGD 40%.

  • Stage 1 ECL at origination ≈ 50 × 1.0% × 40% = ₹0.20 Cr.
  • Two years later the subsidiary's rating is downgraded to BB−, lifetime PD now 18% (from 6%) — SICR → Stage 2: lifetime ECL ≈ 50 × 18% × 40% = ₹3.6 Cr (ignoring discounting and amortisation).
  • If the subsidiary later defaults → Stage 3; interest income then on the net amount.

Under the simplified approach, the same receivable would have carried lifetime ECL from day one.

NBFC context

NBFCs (Division III) present loans by stage with ECL by stage, and the Ind AS 107.35H reconciliation by stage (transfers between stages, new assets, derecognitions, write-offs). RBI requirements overlay: Impairment Reserve where Ind AS ECL < IRACP provision (13 March 2020 circular), board-approved ECL policy, and disclosure of comparison between IRACP and Ind AS by asset classification. Model governance: PD models from internal rating migration or DPD-based roll rates; LGD from recovery data; macro overlays; management overlays (e.g., COVID-era, sectoral stress) documented and periodically released.

Financial guarantees and loan commitments

A parent's financial guarantee for a subsidiary's bank loan is initially at fair value (often the guarantee commission saved) and subsequently at the higher of ECL under the general approach and the amount initially recognised less cumulative income (4.2.1(c)). EAD is the guaranteed amount expected to be called.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Low credit risk exemption is often used for bank deposits and G-secs; it cannot be applied to all intra-group loans by default.
  • Origination date for revolving facilities (cash credit lines) — use the date the facility was granted, adjusted for renewals.
  • Restructured loans — in Stage 2 or 3 depending on whether credit-impaired; RBI restructuring also triggers NPA classification unless permitted under a resolution framework.
  • Collective SICR assessment where individual data is limited (B5.5.1–B5.5.6) — e.g., shifting a sector portfolio to Stage 2 when the sector is stressed.
  • Purchased credit-impaired assets (bought at a deep discount, e.g., from ARCs) — no staging; credit-adjusted EIR.

Key takeaways

  • General approach: Stage 1 12-month ECL; Stage 2 lifetime ECL after SICR; Stage 3 lifetime ECL with interest on net.
  • SICR is a relative test against origination risk, presumed at >30 DPD; default presumed at >90 DPD.
  • Simplified approach skips staging — always lifetime ECL — for receivables and contract assets.
  • Corporates apply the general approach to loans, ICDs, deposits, bonds and financial guarantees.
  • NBFCs layer RBI IRACP requirements (Impairment Reserve) on top of Ind AS ECL.

Practice Questions

Q1. Staging an ICD book [L2]

A company has three ICDs of ₹20 Cr each: (a) to an AAA-rated group company, current; (b) to a BBB-rated third party, originally A-rated, 15 days past due; (c) to a BB-rated company, originally BB, 45 days past due due to a technical delay in a bank transfer that has since been resolved. 12-month PDs: 0.1%, 2%, 5%; lifetime PDs: 0.5%, 9%, 20%; LGD 50%. Determine the stage and ECL for each, explaining any rebuttal of presumptions.

💡 Hint: SICR is relative to origination; consider whether the 30 DPD presumption is rebutted in (c).

Q2. Interest revenue in Stage 3 [L1]

An NBFC loan with gross carrying amount ₹10 Cr and EIR 14% moves to Stage 3 on 1 April 2025 with lifetime ECL ₹4 Cr. Compute interest revenue for FY 2025-26 and explain why it differs from Stage 2.

Q3. Financial guarantee for a subsidiary [L2]

A parent guarantees a ₹100 Cr bank loan of its subsidiary without charging a fee. A bank would have charged 0.75% p.a. for 5 years. Two years later the subsidiary's credit risk increases significantly; lifetime PD 15%, LGD 60%. Explain initial measurement, subsequent measurement and the entries in the parent's separate financial statements.

Q4. Simplified vs general comparison [L3]

A finance team proposes applying the general approach to its trade receivables to reduce the allowance at origination. Explain why this is not permitted, which balances could use the general approach by policy choice, and the operational implications of SICR tracking.
