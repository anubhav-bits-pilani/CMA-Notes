Subtopic note

Specific Provisions for Credit-Impaired Customers Beside the Matrix

Scope: Specific provisions for credit-impaired customers (IBC/NCLT admissions, disputed dues) alongside the matrix

Why it matters

A provision matrix assumes customers within a segment and bucket share credit risk. Some do not: a customer admitted into the corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, a customer disputing an invoice in arbitration, or a customer whose promoter has absconded. These need individual assessment. Indian audit files routinely contain "specific provision" lists, and the interaction with the matrix — avoiding double counting and keeping the historical data consistent — is a frequent review finding. Credit-impaired balances also drive the Schedule III "credit impaired" column and the Ind AS 107 disclosures.

Core notes

Credit-impaired: the definition

A financial asset is credit-impaired when one or more events that have a detrimental impact on estimated future cash flows have occurred (Ind AS 109 Appendix A). Evidence includes: significant financial difficulty of the debtor; a breach of contract such as default or past-due event; the lender granting a concession for reasons of the borrower's financial difficulty; it becoming probable that the borrower will enter bankruptcy or other financial reorganisation; disappearance of an active market; purchase at a deep discount reflecting incurred losses. Under the simplified approach, credit-impaired receivables are still measured at lifetime ECL, but on an individual basis using the specific expected recovery.

When to assess individually

Individual assessment is appropriate where:

  • IBC/NCLT: a petition under Sec 7, 9 or 10 IBC is admitted and a moratorium under Sec 14 applies; the operational creditor files a claim with the resolution professional. Recovery depends on the resolution plan; operational creditors historically recover a small fraction of admitted claims, and under Sec 30(2)(b) they receive at least the liquidation value due to them.
  • Liquidation orders or winding-up petitions under the Companies Act.
  • Disputed dues: customer disputes quality, quantity or pricing; matter under arbitration or MSME Facilitation Council.
  • Known financial distress: credit rating downgraded to D, cheques bounced (Sec 138 NI Act proceedings), customer has stopped operations.
  • Large individual exposures where matrix rates are unrepresentative.

Measurement for individually assessed customers

ECL = Gross balance − PV of expected recoveries (discounted at the original effective rate; for short-term receivables without financing, approximated using a suitable rate). Probability-weight recovery scenarios:

\[ ECL_{\text{specific}} = EAD - \sum_{s} w_s \times PV(\text{Recovery}_s) \]

Inputs: expected realisation under the resolution plan, liquidation value, security (bank guarantee, letter of credit, collateral, security deposits held), credit insurance, set-off rights, time to recovery.

Disputed dues — distinguish credit loss from revenue

A dispute about price or quantity is often not a credit loss: if it relates to the amount of consideration (e.g., expected credit notes, liquidated damages, price variation disputes), it is variable consideration under Ind AS 115 and reduces revenue and the receivable (or is a refund liability). Only the portion representing the customer's inability to pay is ECL. Document the nature of each dispute.

Avoiding double counting with the matrix

The mechanics:

  1. Identify specifically assessed customers at the reporting date.
  2. Remove their entire balance from the matrix population before applying bucket rates.
  3. Apply specific ECL to them.
  4. For consistency, remove the same type of events from historical roll-rate data, or else the historical loss rates already embed losses from such customers — in which case applying both overstates ECL. Most practitioners exclude specifically provided customers from both the current population and the historical data.
  5. Reconcile: total receivables = matrix population + specific population.

Illustration

Harsh Chemicals Ltd, 31 March 2026, gross receivables ₹150 Cr. Two customers are individually assessed:

Customer Balance Facts Expected recovery Specific ECL
Alpha Dyes Ltd ₹6.0 Cr Admitted to CIRP Dec 2025; claim filed; recovery expected 15% in 18 months PV of 0.9 Cr at 10% ≈ ₹0.78 Cr ₹5.22 Cr
Beta Textiles Ltd ₹4.0 Cr Disputes ₹1.0 Cr on quality (credit note expected); balance ₹3.0 Cr undisputed but customer in distress; ₹1.0 Cr bank guarantee held ₹1.0 Cr is revenue reversal; of ₹3.0 Cr, 60% expected recovery (incl. BG) ₹1.20 Cr ECL + ₹1.0 Cr revenue reduction

Matrix population = 150 − 6 − 4 = ₹140 Cr; matrix ECL computed on ₹140 Cr only. Total allowance = matrix ECL + ₹6.42 Cr; plus ₹1.0 Cr reduction of receivable/revenue.

Practical controls

  • Monthly legal update from the legal team on NCLT/arbitration status.
  • Credit control "watch list" with triggers (cheque bounces, rating to D, GST registration cancelled, MCA strike-off).
  • Approval matrix for specific provisions and reversals.
  • Track post-balance-sheet events: an NCLT admission in April relating to conditions existing at 31 March is adjusting evidence (Ind AS 10).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • IBC resolution plan approved: once the NCLT approves a plan, the claim is extinguished beyond what is payable under the plan; write off the excess (s06).
  • Sec 14 moratorium stops recovery and set-off actions; set-off rights against payables to the same customer may not be enforceable during CIRP.
  • MSME supplier claims under the MSMED Act carry compound interest at three times the bank rate; customers who are MSMEs may receive protection that affects your recovery timing.
  • Advances received from the customer or security deposits held reduce LGD only if legally available for set-off.
  • Specific provision on a related party — group support considerations may justify lower ECL, but evidence is needed (s06).
  • Reversal — if a customer emerges from CIRP and pays, reverse the allowance through impairment gain in P&L.

Key takeaways

  • Customers with objective evidence of impairment (IBC admission, liquidation, serious distress) are assessed individually.
  • Specific ECL = exposure − probability-weighted PV of recoveries, considering security, insurance and set-off.
  • Price/quality disputes are often Ind AS 115 variable consideration, not credit loss.
  • Remove specifically assessed balances from the matrix population — and treat historical data consistently.
  • Reconcile matrix and specific populations to total receivables.

Practice Questions

Q1. IBC admission after year-end [L2]

A customer owing ₹12 Cr defaulted in February 2026; an operational creditor's Sec 9 petition was admitted on 20 April 2026, before the board approved the FY 2025-26 accounts on 15 May 2026. The matrix rate for the >60 DPD bucket is 18%. Determine whether the admission is an adjusting event, how to measure ECL if expected recovery is 12% in 2 years at a 9% discount rate, and the effect on the matrix.

💡 Hint: Ask whether the condition existed at the reporting date and remove the customer from the matrix population.

Q2. Dispute or credit loss [L2]

A cement company has ₹8 Cr receivable from a contractor who disputes ₹2.5 Cr as price escalation not agreed and ₹1 Cr as delay penalties under the contract; the contractor is solvent. Explain the Ind AS 115 and Ind AS 109 treatment of each component.

Q3. Overlap in historical data [L3]

A company's historical roll-rate data over four years includes two large customers that went into NCLT and were 100% provided. This year three customers are individually assessed and carved out of the matrix. Explain the risk of double counting, the alternatives to fix the historical data, and how to document your choice for audit.

Q4. Security and set-off [L1]

A customer owes ₹5 Cr and has been admitted to CIRP. You hold a ₹1.5 Cr security deposit from the customer and owe it ₹0.8 Cr for purchases. Discuss which amounts reduce your ECL and why the Sec 14 moratorium matters.
