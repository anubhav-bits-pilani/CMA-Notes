Subtopic note

Historical Loss Rates by Roll-Rate and Migration Analysis

Scope: Historical loss rates: roll-rate/migration analysis across ageing buckets over 3–5 years

Why it matters

The historical loss rate is the base of every matrix cell. An auditor's first question on a provision matrix is "where do these percentages come from?" — and "management estimate" is not an answer. Roll-rate analysis turns ordinary ERP ageing reports into a defensible, data-driven estimate of how much of each bucket will eventually be lost. Done over 3–5 years, it captures a credit cycle and supports the forward-looking overlay. The same analysis gives FP&A teams collection curves for cash forecasting.

Core notes

Two main methods

  1. Roll-rate (migration) method. Measures the proportion of each ageing bucket that moves ("rolls") into the next older bucket over one period. The loss rate for a bucket is the product of roll rates from that bucket through to write-off.
  2. Vintage (cohort) method. Tracks each month's sales cohort to final collection or write-off; lifetime loss rate = cumulative losses ÷ original cohort. Useful where invoices have uniform terms; applied to the unpaid portion of each cohort.

Both are acceptable under B5.5.35 provided they reflect 5.5.17. Roll-rate is most common in Indian corporates because ageing reports are readily available.

Roll-rate mechanics

Choose a period consistent with bucket width (monthly for 30-day buckets; quarterly for 90-day buckets). For each period:

\[ \text{Roll rate}_{i \to i+1} = \frac{\text{Balance in bucket } i+1 \text{ at } t+1 \text{ that came from bucket } i \text{ at } t}{\text{Balance in bucket } i \text{ at } t} \]

Without invoice-level tracking, approximate with "balance in bucket \( i+1 \) at \( t+1 \) ÷ balance in bucket \( i \) at \( t \)", since nothing can enter bucket \( i+1 \) except from bucket \( i \).

The final bucket "rolls" to write-off: the rate is write-offs in the period ÷ opening balance of the last bucket (or an assumed recovery rate).

Loss rate for bucket \( i \):

\[ LR_i = \prod_{j=i}^{N} RR_j \]

where \( RR_N \) is the roll into write-off from the last bucket.

Averaging and period selection

  • Compute roll rates for each period over 3–5 years (12–20 quarters or 36–60 months).
  • Average them (simple or balance-weighted) — weighted averages reduce the effect of small balances.
  • Exclude periods distorted by one-off events only if justified, and capture those effects in the forward-looking overlay instead.
  • Remove specifically provided customers (s05) from the data if they are carved out of the current matrix.

Illustration

Esha Textiles Ltd uses quarterly 90-day buckets. Average balances (₹ Cr) at quarter-ends and transitions over 12 quarters give these average roll rates:

Transition Average roll rate
Not due → 1–90 DPD 30%
1–90 → 91–180 25%
91–180 → 181–270 40%
181–270 → 271–365 55%
271–365 → written off 70%

Loss rates (product of subsequent roll rates):

Bucket Computation Historical loss rate
271–365 70% 70.0%
181–270 55% × 70% 38.5%
91–180 40% × 55% × 70% 15.4%
1–90 25% × 40% × 55% × 70% 3.85%
Not due 30% × 25% × 40% × 55% × 70% 1.16%

Applied to 31 March 2026 balances of ₹60 Cr, ₹25 Cr, ₹6 Cr, ₹3 Cr and ₹2 Cr:

\[ ECL = 60(1.16\%) + 25(3.85\%) + 6(15.4\%) + 3(38.5\%) + 2(70\%) = 0.70 + 0.96 + 0.92 + 1.16 + 1.40 = 5.14 \text{ Cr} \]

Partial recoveries and LGD

If written-off debts are partly recovered later, the effective loss is lower. Adjust the final roll: net loss = write-offs − subsequent recoveries. Alternatively compute a separate LGD from recovery data and apply it to the final roll.

Data preparation steps

  1. Extract customer-level ageing (by due date) at each period end for 3–5 years; reconcile totals to the GL.
  2. Map customers to segments (s02) consistently across years.
  3. Remove unapplied receipts and credit notes; remove intra-group balances if treated separately.
  4. Compute transitions (ideally invoice-level; otherwise bucket-level approximations).
  5. Calculate roll rates per period and averages; chart them to detect trends.
  6. Calculate loss rates; compare with actual write-offs (back-test).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Roll rates above 100% arise when new invoices are booked directly into overdue buckets (e.g., late-booked invoices, re-ageing). Investigate data quality; cap or exclude.
  • Re-ageing on partial payment. Some ERPs reset ageing when part is paid; ensure ageing is by original due date of each invoice (FIFO allocation of receipts).
  • Short history. A company with only two years of data can supplement with industry or peer data and widen overlays; document the limitation.
  • Changing credit terms. If credit days changed from 30 to 60, roll rates from before the change are not comparable for early buckets.
  • COVID-period data (FY 2020-21) may inflate roll rates; keep it if it represents a plausible stress, but weight it consistently with the scenario design (s04).
  • Seasonality (festive-season sales for consumer durables) — use average across all quarters, not one quarter.

Key takeaways

  • Roll rate = portion of bucket \( i \) that migrates to bucket \( i+1 \) over one period; the final bucket rolls to write-off.
  • Bucket loss rate = product of roll rates from that bucket onwards.
  • Use 3–5 years to capture a cycle; average roll rates (preferably balance-weighted).
  • Reconcile data to the GL and age by original due date.
  • Back-test the resulting rates against actual write-offs annually.
