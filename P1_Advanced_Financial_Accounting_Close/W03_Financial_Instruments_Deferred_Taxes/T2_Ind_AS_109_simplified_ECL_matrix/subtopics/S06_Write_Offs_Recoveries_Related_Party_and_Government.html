Subtopic note

Write-Offs, Recoveries, Related-Party and Government Receivables

Scope: Write-off policy, recoveries, related-party and government receivables, time value on delayed collections

Why it matters

The ECL allowance is only one half of credit accounting; the other half is deciding when to remove a balance from the books, how to account for later recoveries, and how to treat receivables that the matrix handles poorly — group companies and government bodies. Indian companies often carry large old balances from state governments, PSUs and DISCOMs that are "good" but slow, and intra-group balances that are never really collected. Write-off decisions also drive the tax deduction under Sec 36(1)(vii). Getting these right affects the allowance, the deferred tax asset, Schedule III ageing and SEBI-mandated related-party disclosures.

Core notes

Write-off policy

An entity shall directly reduce the gross carrying amount of a financial asset when it has no reasonable expectations of recovering the asset in its entirety or a portion of it (Ind AS 109.5.4.4). A write-off is a derecognition event. Indicators of no reasonable expectation: completion of IBC liquidation with no distribution, approved resolution plan paying less than the claim, time-barred debts under the Limitation Act 1963 (3 years) without acknowledgement, failure of legal recovery, or uneconomic further enforcement.

Write-off can occur while enforcement activity continues (amounts written off but still subject to enforcement must be disclosed, Ind AS 107.35L). Policy disclosure is required under 107.35F(e).

The write-off is normally against an existing allowance:

Entry Dr Cr
Write-off of fully provided balance ECL allowance Trade receivables
Write-off exceeding allowance ECL allowance; Impairment loss (excess) Trade receivables

Recoveries

Cash collected on a balance previously written off is recognised in P&L as a reversal of impairment loss (or "bad debts recovered" within other income — presentation policy must be consistent). Dr Bank, Cr Impairment loss on financial assets (reversal). For tax, recovery of a debt previously allowed under Sec 36(1)(vii) is taxable under Sec 41(4).

Recoveries reduce the net loss in historical data; loss rates should use net write-offs (s03).

Related-party receivables

Receivables from subsidiaries, associates, fellow subsidiaries and promoter entities are trade receivables in the simplified approach scope. Considerations:

  • Parental support and group cash pooling may make expected loss minimal; but a subsidiary with negative net worth and no realistic repayment capacity may need a provision even if the parent supports it — support must be evidenced (letters of support, history of capital infusion).
  • Quasi-equity balances — long-outstanding intra-group trade balances that are never settled may be in substance an investment; consider reclassification only with clear evidence; otherwise assess ECL.
  • Time value — balances outstanding much longer than terms without interest have a time-value loss.
  • Disclosure: Ind AS 24.18(b)–(d) requires outstanding balances, terms, guarantees and provisions for doubtful debts related to related-party balances and the expense recognised. SEBI LODR Reg 23 and Schedule V require related-party disclosures; Schedule III requires disclosure of receivables from directors and firms/private companies in which directors are interested.
  • Consolidation eliminates both the receivable and the ECL; the ECL in the standalone books is reversed on consolidation.

Government and PSU receivables

Government departments, PSUs and DISCOMs rarely default outright, but delays are common and often uncompensated. ECL must reflect time value (5.5.17(b), B5.5.44): the shortfall between contractual cash flows and the expected cash flows discounted at the original effective interest rate. Credit losses include delayed payments even if the full amount is eventually received (Appendix A definition of credit loss).

\[ ECL_{\text{TV}} = \text{Balance} - \sum_{k} \frac{CF_k}{(1 + r)^{t_k}} \]

where \( CF_k \) are expected collections at times \( t_k \) (in years from the reporting date) and \( r \) is the original effective rate (for receivables without a financing component, commonly approximated by the entity's cost of short-term borrowing or a rate implicit in similar credit sales, consistently applied).

Illustration. Ishita Infra Ltd has ₹90 Cr receivable from a state roads authority, all past due. Collection expectations: ₹30 Cr in 6 months, ₹30 Cr in 15 months, ₹30 Cr in 30 months; no interest. Effective rate 9.5%.

Tranche PV factor PV (₹ Cr)
30 at 0.5 yr \( 1.095^{-0.5} \) = 0.9556 28.67
30 at 1.25 yr \( 1.095^{-1.25} \) = 0.8927 26.78
30 at 2.5 yr \( 1.095^{-2.5} \) = 0.7970 23.91

Arbitration awards and claims

Claims under arbitration (common in EPC) that are not yet receivables (no unconditional right) are contract assets or contingent assets; once an award is passed and not challenged, it may become a receivable. Awards under challenge in court (Sec 34 Arbitration Act) — recognition and ECL require judgement on collection probability and timing.

Presentation

Under Schedule III, allowance for bad and doubtful debts is shown as a deduction from receivables under each relevant head, and receivables from related parties and directors are disclosed. The write-off and allowance movement form the Ind AS 107.35H reconciliation.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Write-off vs allowance timing — a fully provided balance can remain on the books for years; tax deduction requires a write-off, so companies sometimes write off for tax while recovery efforts continue — permissible if consistent with 5.4.4.
  • Partial write-off — write off the portion with no reasonable expectation (e.g., the haircut in an approved IBC plan).
  • Acknowledgement of debt extends limitation (Sec 18 Limitation Act); time-bar alone may not justify write-off if the debtor acknowledges.
  • Government counter-claims (penalties, liquidated damages) should be assessed as variable consideration rather than ECL.
  • Subsidiary receivable with negative net worth — auditors expect either a provision or strong support evidence.

Key takeaways

  • Write off when there is no reasonable expectation of recovery (5.4.4); a write-off is derecognition and may precede the end of enforcement.
  • Recoveries of written-off amounts go to P&L and are taxable under Sec 41(4).
  • Related-party receivables need ECL assessment supported by evidence of support; they eliminate on consolidation.
  • Government and PSU receivables carry ECL for time value of delayed, uncompensated collection.
  • Disclose write-off policy, amounts under enforcement and related-party provisions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write-off and recovery entries [L1]

A company's allowance includes ₹2.4 Cr for a customer whose IBC resolution plan was approved in December 2025, paying operational creditors 8% of claims. The balance is ₹3.0 Cr. The plan amount of ₹0.24 Cr is received in March 2026. In FY 2026-27, ₹0.10 Cr is unexpectedly recovered from the customer's promoter guarantee. Pass all entries and state the tax treatment.

💡 Hint: Write off the portion beyond the plan payout; the later recovery is income.

Q2. Government receivable time value [L2]

A defence supplier has ₹150 Cr receivable from a central ministry, due 31 March 2026. Expected collection: 50% in 9 months, 50% in 21 months, no interest. Effective rate 8.5%. Compute ECL, and explain how the answer changes if the contract provides 6% interest on delayed payments that the ministry historically pays.

Q3. Struggling subsidiary [L3]

A parent has ₹45 Cr trade receivable from a wholly owned subsidiary with negative net worth of ₹30 Cr, outstanding for 3 years; the parent has issued a comfort letter to the subsidiary's bankers but no support letter covering intra-group dues. Discuss ECL in the parent's separate financial statements, the consolidation effect, and the related-party disclosures.

Q4. Write-off policy drafting [L2]

Draft a write-off policy for a mid-sized FMCG company with 5,000 distributors, covering triggers, approvals, tax considerations, enforcement after write-off and disclosure under Ind AS 107.35F and 35L.
