Key concept

Rebuttable Presumptions: SICR at 30 DPD and Default at 90 DPD

Concept: Rebuttable presumptions: SICR at >30 days past due (5.5.11); default at >90 days past due (B5.5.37)

Statement

  • SICR presumption (Ind AS 109.5.5.11): there is a rebuttable presumption that the credit risk on a financial asset has increased significantly since initial recognition when contractual payments are more than 30 days past due. It may be rebutted if the entity has reasonable and supportable information that demonstrates credit risk has not increased significantly even though payments are more than 30 days past due.
  • Default presumption (Ind AS 109.B5.5.37): default does not occur later than when a financial asset is 90 days past due, unless the entity has reasonable and supportable information to demonstrate that a more lagging default criterion is more appropriate.

Days past due \( DPD = \text{Reporting date} - \text{Contractual due date} \) of the oldest unpaid instalment.

Explanation

Past-due status is a lagging indicator; Ind AS 109 prefers forward-looking information for SICR (5.5.11). The 30-day presumption acts as a backstop: whatever the model says, by 30 DPD credit risk is presumed to have increased. Similarly, 90 DPD is a backstop for the definition of default, aligning broadly with RBI's NPA norm for lenders.

Where they matter:

  • General approach (loans, ICDs, NBFC books): 30 DPD → Stage 2 (lifetime ECL); 90 DPD → Stage 3 (credit-impaired, interest on net).
  • Simplified approach (trade receivables): no staging for measurement, but the thresholds are commonly used to map balances to Schedule III's SICR and credit-impaired categories (s07), and to define default in the historical loss data.

Rebuttal requires evidence, not convenience. Acceptable examples:

  • Administrative delays: payments late due to an operational lapse (e.g., a customer's ERP migration) with no deterioration in creditworthiness, evidenced by subsequent prompt payment.
  • Government or DISCOM counterparties that systematically pay 60–180 days late but historically always pay in full — default (credit loss due to non-payment) does not occur at 90 DPD, though the time-value shortfall is still ECL.
  • Specific sectors where historical data shows that 90+ DPD balances cure at very high rates.

The entity should apply rebuttals consistently to segments and document the evidence; Ind AS 107.35F(a)–(b) requires disclosure of how SICR and default are determined, including whether the presumptions have been rebutted.

Neeraj Agro Exports Ltd's receivables at 31 March 2026:

Customer group DPD Evidence Conclusion
Domestic distributors 35–60 Rising overdues, two cheque bounces SICR presumption holds; show as SICR in Schedule III

Connections

  • s07 uses the thresholds for Schedule III categories; s08 for staging.
  • s03 defines default in historical roll-rate data — must be consistent with this presumption.
  • s06: government receivables often rebut default but still carry time-value ECL.
  • RBI SMA-0/1/2 classifications (1–30, 31–60, 61–90 DPD) and NPA at 90 DPD for NBFCs.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rebuttal evidence [L2]

A company wants to rebut the 90 DPD default presumption for its hospital customers, who routinely pay 100–150 days past due. What evidence would you require, what would you disclose, and how would ECL still be measured?

💡 Hint: Think about cure rates, write-off history and time value.

Q2. Staging an employee loan book [L1]

A company's ₹8 Cr employee housing loan book has ₹0.4 Cr more than 30 DPD (employees on unpaid leave) and ₹0.1 Cr more than 90 DPD (employees who have resigned). Apply the presumptions and state the stage of each portion.

Q3. Presumption vs forward-looking information [L2]

A corporate borrower is current on payments, but its rating has been downgraded from A to BB and it has breached a financial covenant. Explain whether the loan can remain in Stage 1 because it is not 30 DPD, citing the relevant requirement.
