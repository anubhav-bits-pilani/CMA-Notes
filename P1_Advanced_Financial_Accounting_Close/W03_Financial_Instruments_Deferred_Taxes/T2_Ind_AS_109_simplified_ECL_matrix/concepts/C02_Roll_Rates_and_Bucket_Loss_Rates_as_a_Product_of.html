Key concept

Roll Rates and Bucket Loss Rates as a Product of Migrations

Concept: Roll rate (bucket i → i+1) = Balance migrating to next bucket ÷ Opening balance of bucket i; bucket loss rate = product of subsequent roll rates

Statement

For ageing buckets \( i = 0, 1, \ldots, N \) (0 = not due, \( N \) = oldest), and a period equal to the bucket width:

\[ RR_{i} = \frac{M_{i \to i+1}}{B_{i,\text{open}}} \]

where \( M_{i \to i+1} \) is the balance that was in bucket \( i \) at the start of the period and is in bucket \( i+1 \) at the end (i.e., not collected), and \( B_{i,\text{open}} \) is the opening balance of bucket \( i \). For the oldest bucket, \( RR_N \) is the proportion written off (net of recoveries). The lifetime loss rate of bucket \( i \) is:

\[ LR_i = \prod_{j=i}^{N} RR_j \]

Explanation

Roll rates are conditional probabilities: given that a rupee is in bucket \( i \) today, the probability it is still unpaid one period later. A rupee in the not-due bucket is lost only if it rolls through every subsequent bucket and is then written off; so its loss rate is the product of all roll rates from there onward. A rupee already in the oldest bucket has only one step left — the write-off rate.

Conditions for validity:

  • Bucket width must equal the measurement period (30-day buckets with monthly snapshots; 90-day buckets with quarterly snapshots).
  • Ageing must be by original due date with receipts applied to specific invoices (or FIFO), so that partial payments do not reset ageing.
  • Use averages over a cycle (3–5 years), preferably balance-weighted, and adjust for forward-looking factors (c03).
  • Where invoice-level tracking is unavailable, approximate \( M_{i \to i+1} \) by the closing balance of bucket \( i+1 \), since only bucket \( i \) can feed it — valid only if no new invoices are booked directly into overdue buckets.

Illustration

Monthly 30-day buckets for Kunal Distributors Pvt Ltd, average roll rates over 36 months:

From → To Roll rate
Current → 1–30 DPD 20%
1–30 → 31–60 35%
31–60 → 61–90 50%
61–90 → 90+ 60%
90+ → write-off 75%

Loss rates: 90+ = 75%; 61–90 = 60% × 75% = 45%; 31–60 = 50% × 45% = 22.5%; 1–30 = 35% × 22.5% = 7.875%; Current = 20% × 7.875% = 1.575%.

Balances ₹50 Cr, ₹10 Cr, ₹4 Cr, ₹2 Cr, ₹1.5 Cr:

\[ ECL = 0.79 + 0.79 + 0.90 + 0.90 + 1.125 = 4.50 \text{ Cr} \]

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute from snapshots [L2]

Month-end ageing (₹ lakh) for two consecutive months: Month 1 — current 800, 1–30 DPD 200, 31–60 DPD 90, 61–90 DPD 40, 90+ DPD 30; Month 2 — current 850, 1–30 DPD 180, 31–60 DPD 70, 61–90 DPD 45, 90+ DPD 28; write-offs in Month 2 ₹18 lakh from the 90+ bucket. Compute roll rates, loss rates and ECL on Month 2 balances.

💡 Hint: The 90+ bucket at Month 2 contains both the roll-in from 61–90 and the unpaid remainder of the old 90+ balance; decide how to treat it consistently.

Q2. Roll rate above 100% [L1]

A roll rate from 31–60 to 61–90 DPD comes out at 118% for one month. Give three possible causes and how to correct the analysis.

Q3. Collection forecast from roll rates [L2]

Using the illustration's roll rates, forecast how much of a ₹10 Cr current balance will be collected in each of the next four months and what will remain in 90+ after four months, assuming no new sales.
