Key concept

ECL Formulas: PD × LGD × EAD and the Matrix Sum

Concept: General: ECL = PD × LGD × EAD (discounted); matrix: ECL = Σ (Bucket balance × Adjusted loss rate)

Statement

General (component) approach:

\[ ECL = \sum_{t=1}^{T} PD_t \times LGD_t \times EAD_t \times \frac{1}{(1 + EIR)^{t}} \]

where \( PD_t \) is the marginal probability of default in period \( t \) (12 months for Stage 1, over the expected life for Stage 2/3), \( LGD_t \) the loss given default as a percentage of exposure after collateral and recoveries, \( EAD_t \) the exposure at default, and \( EIR \) the effective interest rate used for discounting (Ind AS 109 B5.5.44).

Provision matrix (simplified approach):

\[ ECL = \sum_{s} \sum_{b} B_{s,b} \times LR^{\text{adj}}_{s,b} \]

where \( B_{s,b} \) is the gross balance of segment \( s \) in ageing bucket \( b \) and \( LR^{\text{adj}}_{s,b} \) the lifetime loss rate for that cell after forward-looking adjustment.

Explanation

The two formulas measure the same thing — the probability-weighted present value of cash shortfalls (Appendix A "credit loss") — at different levels of granularity. The component approach separates the likelihood of default (PD), the severity (LGD) and the amount at risk (EAD), which suits loans with ratings, collateral and long tenors. The matrix combines all three into one empirical loss rate per bucket: a historical loss rate already embeds the probability that a balance in that bucket defaults and how much of it is lost. Discounting in the matrix is usually immaterial for short receivables but becomes material for slow-paying government balances (s06).

PD, LGD and EAD must be forward-looking and consistent with each other: if a downside scenario raises PD, collateral values (LGD) typically fall at the same time. For receivables, EAD is the balance at the reporting date (no further drawdowns); for loan commitments, EAD includes expected drawdowns (B5.5.31).

Illustration

Component approach. ₹20 Cr two-year bullet loan, EIR 10%, lifetime marginal PDs 3% (year 1) and 4% (year 2), LGD 45%, EAD ₹20 Cr throughout (interest ignored for simplicity).

\[ ECL = \frac{0.03 \times 0.45 \times 20}{1.10} + \frac{0.04 \times 0.45 \times 20}{1.10^2} = 0.245 + 0.298 = 0.543 \text{ Cr} \]

12-month ECL (Stage 1) would be only the first term, ₹0.245 Cr.

Matrix approach. Segment balances ₹40 Cr, ₹12 Cr, ₹5 Cr, ₹2 Cr with adjusted rates 0.5%, 2.5%, 12%, 50% → ECL = 0.20 + 0.30 + 0.60 + 1.00 = ₹2.10 Cr.

Practice Questions

Q1. Component ECL with amortising exposure [L2]

A ₹30 Cr loan amortises ₹10 Cr per year over three years, EIR 11%. Marginal PDs: 2%, 3%, 3.5%; LGD 40%. Assume default occurs at the start of each year on the opening exposure. Compute 12-month and lifetime ECL.

💡 Hint: EAD falls each year with amortisation; discount each year's expected loss separately.

Q2. Reconciling a matrix rate to PD × LGD [L2]

A segment's 91–180 DPD bucket has an empirical lifetime loss rate of 15%. Recovery analysis shows that 40% of amounts in default are recovered. What implied PD does this suggest for the bucket, and why might this decomposition help when designing forward-looking overlays?

Q3. Matrix ECL across segments [L1]

Compute ECL for two segments: Segment A balances ₹25 Cr, ₹6 Cr, ₹2 Cr at rates 0.3%, 1.5%, 20%; Segment B balances ₹10 Cr, ₹4 Cr, ₹3 Cr at rates 1%, 6%, 40%.
