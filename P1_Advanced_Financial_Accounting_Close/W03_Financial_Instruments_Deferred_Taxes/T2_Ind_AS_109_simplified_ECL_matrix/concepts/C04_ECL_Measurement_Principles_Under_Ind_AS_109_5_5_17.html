Key concept

ECL Measurement Principles Under Ind AS 109.5.5.17

Concept: ECL is unbiased, probability-weighted, reflects time value and reasonable, supportable forward-looking information (5.5.17)

Statement

Ind AS 109.5.5.17 requires an entity to measure expected credit losses in a way that reflects:

  1. an unbiased and probability-weighted amount determined by evaluating a range of possible outcomes;
  2. the time value of money; and
  3. reasonable and supportable information that is available without undue cost or effort at the reporting date about past events, current conditions and forecasts of future economic conditions.

Formally, with scenarios \( k \), probabilities \( p_k \) and present value of cash shortfalls \( S_k \):

\[ ECL = \sum_{k} p_k \times PV(S_k), \qquad \sum_{k} p_k = 1 \]

Explanation

Unbiased and probability-weighted. ECL is neither a best-case nor a worst-case estimate, nor the single most likely outcome. It must consider at least the possibility that a credit loss occurs and the possibility that none occurs, even if the loss probability is very low (5.5.18, B5.5.42). The entity need not identify every possible scenario (B5.5.42), but must reflect non-linearity: if downside outcomes are more severe than upside outcomes are favourable, a single central scenario understates ECL. "Unbiased" also rules out deliberate conservatism — a "prudent" overlay with no evidential basis is as non-compliant as optimism.

Time value of money. ECL is the present value of cash shortfalls, discounted at the EIR determined at initial recognition, or an approximation thereof (B5.5.44). For short-term receivables the effect is small; for government or DISCOM receivables paid late without interest it can dominate (s06). A credit loss arises even if all cash is eventually received, if it is received later than contractually due (Appendix A).

Reasonable and supportable information. Includes internal historical loss data, current customer-specific information (payment behaviour, ratings, legal status), and macroeconomic and sector forecasts. Entities need not undertake an exhaustive search (B5.5.49), but must not ignore information that is reasonably available. Forecasts beyond the reasonable and supportable horizon may be extrapolated from historical trends (B5.5.50). Historical data must be adjusted for current conditions (B5.5.52), and estimates should be back-tested (B5.5.52 requires regular review of methodology and assumptions to reduce differences between estimates and actual experience).

Illustration

A ₹10 Cr receivable from a private hospital chain is 120 days past due. Scenarios:

Scenario Probability Outcome PV of shortfall at 9%
Pays in full after 3 more months 60% ₹10 Cr in 0.25 yr 10 − 10/1.09^0.25 = ₹0.21 Cr
Pays 70% after 12 months 30% ₹7 Cr in 1 yr 10 − 7/1.09 = ₹3.58 Cr
Insolvency, 10% recovery in 2 years 10% ₹1 Cr in 2 yrs 10 − 1/1.09^2 = ₹9.16 Cr

\[ ECL = 0.6(0.21) + 0.3(3.58) + 0.1(9.16) = 0.13 + 1.07 + 0.92 = 2.12 \text{ Cr} \]

The "most likely" scenario alone would give ₹0.21 Cr — an order of magnitude lower.

Connections

  • s04 operationalises scenario weighting and forward-looking information; s06 the time value for delayed collections.
  • c01 and c03 are the formulas that must embody these principles.
  • Pitfall: zero ECL on government/PSU receivables ignoring time value.
  • Ind AS 1.125 requires disclosure of key sources of estimation uncertainty — ECL assumptions often qualify.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-scenario customer ECL [L2]

A ₹25 Cr receivable from a real-estate developer: 50% chance of full payment in 6 months; 35% chance of 60% payment in 18 months; 15% chance of 20% payment in 3 years. Discount rate 10%. Compute ECL and compare with the most-likely-outcome estimate.

💡 Hint: Compute the PV shortfall for each scenario before weighting.

Q2. Bias in the estimate [L1]

A CFO wants to add a "prudent" 25% overlay across all segments "to be safe". Explain why this may breach 5.5.17 and what would make an overlay acceptable.

Q3. Undue cost or effort [L2]

A mid-sized company has no internal model linking losses to macro variables and argues that forward-looking information is unavailable without undue cost. Discuss what information is reasonably available in India and how it could be used.
