Key concept

Lowest Significant Input Determines the Hierarchy Level

Concept: Level of whole measurement = lowest level input significant to the entire measurement (113.73)

Statement

\[ L(\text{FV}) = \max \{\, L(x_i) : x_i \text{ is significant to the entire measurement} \,\} \]

where \(L(\cdot)\) maps an input to its level (1 = quoted price for the identical item in an active market, 2 = other observable, 3 = unobservable), "max" picks the lowest-priority (highest-numbered) level, and \(x_i\) ranges over the inputs of the valuation technique, including adjustments. An input is significant if it materially affects the fair value measurement in its entirety, assessed with judgement considering factors specific to the asset or liability (113.73). Adjustments to arrive at measurements based on fair value (e.g., costs to sell for FVLCS) are not considered in determining the level.

Explanation

The hierarchy classifies a measurement, not each input separately — so a model mixing Level 2 curves and one significant Level 3 input is a Level 3 measurement. Practical approach:

  1. List all inputs and adjustments to the valuation.
  2. Classify each input's level.
  3. Assess significance of unobservable inputs — commonly by sensitivity: how much would fair value change across a reasonably possible range of the unobservable input, relative to the total fair value? Many entities set internal thresholds (e.g., 10% of the instrument's fair value), but the standard requires judgement, not a bright line.
  4. Consider the range of the unobservable input and the interaction of inputs.
  5. Classify the whole measurement at the lowest level among significant inputs.
  6. Reassess at each reporting date — as observability or significance changes, the level may change (transfers; disclose policy under 113.95).

Points to note:

  • Using a valuation technique doesn't by itself make a measurement Level 3; a DCF using only observable inputs is Level 2.
  • An adjustment to a Level 1 price (e.g., for a post-close event) moves the measurement to Level 2 or 3 (113.79).
  • A Level 2 input adjusted using a significant unobservable input becomes Level 3 (113.75, 113.84).
  • An entity cannot "average" levels or split one instrument into parts at different levels.

Illustration

A 7-year uncollateralised IRS with a corporate counterparty (BBB, no traded CDS):

Input Level Effect on FV (₹ Cr)
MIBOR OIS curve to 5 years 2 Base MTM 30.0
Swap curve years 6–7 (extrapolated, thinly quoted) 2/3 ±0.4 across reasonable range
CVA using proxy credit spread (sector curve adjusted) 3 (4.5); reasonable range (3.0)–(6.5)

Fair value = 30.0 − 4.5 = ₹25.5 Cr. The CVA range moves fair value by up to ₹1.5–2.0 Cr either side (6–8% of FV), and the CVA itself is 15% of the base MTM. If the entity concludes the CVA is significant to the whole measurement, the swap is Level 3; if CVA were ₹0.3 Cr on a ₹30 Cr MTM, Level 2 would be appropriate.

Similarly, an unlisted equity valued on an EV/EBITDA multiple from listed peers (observable) with a 25% size discount and 15% DLOM (unobservable, significant) is Level 3.

Practice Questions

Q1. Significance assessment [L2]

A corporate bond is valued using a matrix: AA curve from FBIL (observable) plus an issuer-specific spread adjustment of 80 bps estimated from internal analysis (unobservable). Modified duration 4.2; FV ₹100 Cr before the adjustment. The reasonable range of the adjustment is 50–110 bps. Assess whether the measurement is Level 2 or 3 and explain your significance threshold.

💡 Hint: Convert the spread range into a rupee range using duration.

Q2. Adjusted quoted price [L1]

A listed share's closing price on NSE on 31 March is ₹400; at 8 pm the company announces a major fraud, and the ADR in New York falls 30% overnight. The entity adjusts its fair value to ₹290. Determine the level and justify with 113.79.

Q3. Interview: why not Level 2? [L2]

A CFO argues that a start-up investment is Level 2 because "all inputs are from a funding round two months ago". The round was for a senior preference class with a 1.5× liquidation preference; the entity holds ordinary shares. Explain why the measurement is likely Level 3 using 113.73.
