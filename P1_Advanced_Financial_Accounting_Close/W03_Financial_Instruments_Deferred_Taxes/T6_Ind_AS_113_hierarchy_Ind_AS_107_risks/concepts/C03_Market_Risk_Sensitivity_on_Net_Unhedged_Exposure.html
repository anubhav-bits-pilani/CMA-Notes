Key concept

Market Risk Sensitivity on Net Unhedged Exposure

Concept: Sensitivity: ΔProfit before tax = Net unhedged exposure × ΔRate (e.g., ±1% INR/USD, ±50 bps interest)

Statement

For each market risk variable \(r\) (FX rate, interest rate, equity price):

\[ \Delta \text{PBT} = E_{\text{net}} \times \Delta r \]

where:

  • \(E_{\text{net}}\) = net exposure of financial instruments at the reporting date whose P&L is affected by \(r\) — e.g., for FX: foreign-currency monetary assets − monetary liabilities ± notional of derivatives not in hedge relationships, excluding items whose FX effects go to OCI;
  • \(\Delta r\) = reasonably possible change in the risk variable (e.g., ±1% or ±5% INR/USD; ±50 bps in interest rates; ±10% equity prices) (Ind AS 107.40(a)).

A separate equity (OCI) sensitivity is computed for exposures whose fair value changes go to OCI — cash flow hedging instruments, FVOCI investments.

Explanation

The sensitivity disclosure answers: "If this market variable moved by a reasonably possible amount at the reporting date, what would happen to profit and equity?" Key rules:

  • Reporting-date instruments only — forecast sales or purchases are excluded (they are not financial instruments yet), even if hedged.
  • Currency risk arises only on monetary items in a currency other than the entity's functional currency; translation risk on foreign subsidiaries' net assets is not currency risk for Ind AS 107 purposes (B23).
  • Hedges included — an FX forward that economically hedges a USD receivable offsets it in \(E_{\text{net}}\); if the forward is a designated cash flow hedge of a forecast transaction, its FV change goes to OCI (equity sensitivity), so it is excluded from the P&L sensitivity.
  • Interest rate risk — floating-rate borrowings and deposits affect P&L via interest expense/income (\(\Delta\text{interest} = \text{Floating balance} \times \Delta r\), typically annualised); fixed-rate instruments at amortised cost do not affect P&L; FVTPL/FVOCI debt investments change in value (\(\approx -D \times \text{FV} \times \Delta r\)).
  • Reasonably possible — based on economic environment and historical volatility; not worst-case.
  • Tax — Ind AS 107 refers to profit or loss; many Indian companies disclose pre-tax and post-tax effects.

Illustration

Indian exporter, 31 March 2026 (₹85.0/USD):

USD exposure (USD million) Amount Treatment
Trade receivables 30.0 P&L
Trade payables (8.0) P&L
ECB borrowing (25.0) P&L, but USD 15 million hedged by a cross-currency swap designated as a cash flow hedge
CCS (hedging USD 15 million of ECB) 15.0 Hedged item and hedging instrument offset in P&L; CFH reserve affected by basis/interest elements
FX forwards sold USD 10 million (not designated) (10.0) P&L
Net unhedged P&L exposure 2.0

\[ \Delta \text{PBT} = \text{USD } 2.0 \text{ mn} \times 85.0 \times 1\% = \text{₹ } 1.7 \text{ mn} = \text{₹ } 0.17 \text{ Cr} \]

for a 1% depreciation of INR (gain), and −₹0.17 Cr for 1% appreciation. Computing on gross receivables alone (USD 30 million) would show ₹2.55 Cr — overstating the risk 15-fold (pitfall).

Interest rate: floating-rate rupee term loans ₹600 Cr, of which ₹200 Cr swapped to fixed (cash flow hedge). Net floating ₹400 Cr × 0.50% = ₹2.0 Cr p.a. impact on PBT for +50 bps; the swap's FV change (say ₹3.1 Cr) goes to OCI — equity sensitivity.

Connections

Links to s06 (107.40 requirements and qualitative disclosures), related item on hedge accounting (cash flow hedge reserve), w3_t1 (floating-rate EIR loans), and FP&A practice — the same exposure maths drives FX/rate scenario analysis in budgets, although budgets also include forecast transactions excluded here. Pitfall: running sensitivity on gross exposure while ignoring hedges.

Practice Questions

Q1. FX sensitivity [L2]

At 31 March 2026 (₹85/USD; €1 = ₹92): USD receivables USD 18 million, USD payables USD 22 million, EUR loan €5 million, undesignated USD forward purchases USD 6 million. Compute the P&L sensitivity to ±2% movement of INR against each currency, stating assumptions.

💡 Hint: Treat each currency separately and determine the direction of each item.

Q2. Interest rate sensitivity with hedges [L2]

Borrowings: ₹500 Cr repo-linked floating; ₹300 Cr fixed-rate NCDs at amortised cost; ₹150 Cr floating swapped to fixed in a designated cash flow hedge. Investments: ₹100 Cr G-secs at FVOCI with modified duration 5. Compute the P&L and OCI sensitivity to +50 bps.

Q3. Review a sensitivity note [L1]

A draft note shows FX sensitivity of ₹40 Cr computed on forecast FY27 exports of USD 47 million plus year-end receivables. Explain what is wrong under Ind AS 107 and how to correct it.
