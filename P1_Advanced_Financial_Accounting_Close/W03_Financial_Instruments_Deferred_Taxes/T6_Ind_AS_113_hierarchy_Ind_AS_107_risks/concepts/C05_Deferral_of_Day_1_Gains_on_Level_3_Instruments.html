Key concept

Deferral of Day-1 Gains on Level 3 Instruments

Concept: Day-1 gains on Level 3-based instruments deferred (Ind AS 109 B5.1.2A)

Statement

At initial recognition, the best evidence of the fair value of a financial instrument is normally the transaction price (Ind AS 109 B5.1.2A). If fair value differs from the transaction price:

  • (a) if fair value is evidenced by a quoted price in an active market for an identical instrument (Level 1) or based on a valuation technique that uses only data from observable markets, the difference is recognised as a gain or loss in P&L at initial recognition;
  • (b) in all other cases (i.e., the valuation uses significant unobservable — Level 3 — inputs), the instrument is measured at fair value adjusted to defer the difference. After initial recognition, the deferred difference is recognised as a gain or loss only to the extent it arises from a change in a factor (including time) that market participants would take into account when pricing the instrument.

\[ \text{Initial carrying amount} = \text{FV}_{\text{model}} - D_{0}, \quad D_{0} = \text{FV}_{\text{model}} - \text{Transaction price} \]

Explanation

The rule prevents entities from booking "model profits" on day one: if a bank sells a bespoke derivative to a corporate for ₹10 Cr and its in-house Level 3 model says the derivative is worth ₹12 Cr, the ₹2 Cr gain is not recognised immediately because it rests on unobservable assumptions. Instead, the difference is deferred and released as the model inputs become observable, as time passes, or as the instrument is settled.

Practical aspects:

  • Release policy — commonly straight-line over the instrument's life, or when inputs become observable, or on derecognition. The policy must be disclosed (Ind AS 107.28).
  • Ind AS 107.28 disclosures — accounting policy for recognising the difference in P&L; the aggregate difference yet to be recognised at the beginning and end of the period and a reconciliation; why the entity concluded the transaction price was not the best evidence of FV.
  • Interaction with calibration (Ind AS 113.64) — where the transaction price is fair value, the model is calibrated to it and no day-1 difference arises; B5.1.2A applies where FV is deemed different from price but cannot be evidenced by observable inputs.
  • Not for related-party/subsidised loans — where the difference arises because the transaction includes another element (e.g., interest-free loans to subsidiaries/employees), the day-1 difference is accounted for according to its substance (investment in subsidiary, employee benefit) — the FV is based on observable market rates, so B5.1.2A(a) applies (w3_t1 c04).
  • Level 1/2 evidence — if observable, recognise the gain/loss immediately.

Illustration

On 1 April 2025, a corporate treasury buys a structured note from a bank for ₹50 Cr. The note pays a coupon linked to a basket of Indian equities with a 5-year tenor; the bank's model values it at ₹50 Cr, but the corporate's independent valuer (Level 3 inputs: long-dated implied correlations and volatilities) values it at ₹53 Cr. The corporate classifies it at FVTPL.

  • Day-1 difference ₹3 Cr — Level 3 → deferred. Initial carrying amount ₹50 Cr (₹53 Cr less deferred ₹3 Cr).
  • Policy: release straight-line over 5 years (₹0.6 Cr per year), plus immediate release if inputs become observable.
  • At 31 March 2026, model FV ₹55.2 Cr: carrying amount = 55.2 − (3.0 − 0.6) = ₹52.8 Cr; P&L gain FY26 = 52.8 − 50.0 = ₹2.8 Cr (of which ₹0.6 Cr is the deferred difference released).

Disclosure: deferred difference reconciliation — opening nil, new transactions ₹3.0 Cr, recognised in P&L ₹0.6 Cr, closing ₹2.4 Cr.

Practice Questions

Q1. Recognise or defer? [L1]

For each, state whether the day-1 difference is recognised or deferred: (a) a listed bond bought off-market at ₹98 when NSE quotes ₹100; (b) an OTC FX option priced by the bank at ₹4 Cr while the entity's model using observable volatilities gives ₹4.3 Cr; (c) an unlisted CCPS bought at ₹20 Cr valued by a DCF at ₹26 Cr; (d) an interest-free loan to an employee.

💡 Hint: Ask whether fair value is evidenced only by observable data.

Q2. Release schedule [L2]

A 4-year Level 3 derivative asset has a ₹6 Cr deferred day-1 gain released straight-line; at the end of year 2 its key input becomes observable. Model fair values at the end of years 1 and 2 are ₹30 Cr and ₹27 Cr (transaction price ₹25 Cr). Compute the carrying amounts and P&L for years 1 and 2.

Q3. Disclosure draft [L2]

Draft the Ind AS 107.28 disclosure for a portfolio of structured notes with deferred day-1 gains of ₹8 Cr opening, new trades ₹5 Cr, releases ₹3.5 Cr, and ₹1 Cr released on early redemption.
