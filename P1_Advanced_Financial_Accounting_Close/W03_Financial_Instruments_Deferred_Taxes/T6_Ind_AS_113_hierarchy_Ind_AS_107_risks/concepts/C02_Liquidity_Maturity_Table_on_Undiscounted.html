Key concept

Liquidity Maturity Table on Undiscounted Contractual Flows

Concept: Liquidity table = contractual undiscounted cash flows (principal + interest) by bucket (<1 yr, 1–5 yrs, >5 yrs); won't tie to carrying amounts

Statement

For each class of financial liability \(j\) and time band \(b\):

\[ M_{j,b} = \sum_{t \in b} \left( P_{j,t} + I_{j,t} \right) \]

where \(P_{j,t}\) and \(I_{j,t}\) are the contractual principal and interest cash outflows due at date \(t\), undiscounted, with floating-rate interest estimated using conditions at the reporting date (Ind AS 107.B11D), amounts with a counterparty demand option placed in the earliest band in which payment can be required (B11C(a)), and issued financial guarantees at the maximum amount in the earliest band they could be called (B11C(c)). Because \(M\) includes future interest and is undiscounted, \(\sum_b M_{j,b} \neq \text{Carrying amount}_j\) in general.

Explanation

The purpose of 107.39(a) is to show when cash leaves the entity — not what is on the balance sheet. A ₹100 Cr 5-year bond at 9% carries at ~₹100 Cr but requires ₹145 Cr of cash. Including interest makes the table useful for liquidity planning, and lease liabilities (undiscounted lease payments), trade payables, derivative liabilities (gross for gross-settled contracts), and guarantees complete the picture.

Mechanics:

  • Buckets are a judgement (B11): common Indian practice is "on demand / <1 year / 1–5 years / >5 years", sometimes with "<3 months" and "3–12 months" splits.
  • Total column shows undiscounted totals; many entities add a carrying amount column to aid reconciliation — the difference is future interest and discount unwinding.
  • Fees already deducted in the EIR carrying amount (w3_t1) also create a difference: the carrying amount is below the principal repayable.
  • Floating rate: use the rate prevailing at the reporting date (e.g., repo-linked rate on 31 March) for all future periods unless the contract specifies otherwise.
  • Revolving facilities and demand loans: earliest band, even if expected to roll over.

Illustration

At 31 March 2026, Prakash Ltd has:

  • Term loan ₹300 Cr (carrying ₹297.6 Cr after unamortised fees), repayable in 5 equal annual instalments from 31 March 2027, interest 9% p.a. on outstanding, paid annually;
  • Lease liabilities: carrying ₹40 Cr; undiscounted payments ₹12 Cr p.a. for 5 years;
  • Trade payables ₹150 Cr (due within 60 days);
  • Corporate guarantee to an associate's lender: ₹50 Cr.

Term loan cash flows: year 1: 60 + 27.0 = 87.0; year 2: 60 + 21.6 = 81.6; year 3: 60 + 16.2 = 76.2; year 4: 60 + 10.8 = 70.8; year 5: 60 + 5.4 = 65.4.

The ₹152.2 Cr difference = future interest on the loan (₹81 Cr) + unamortised fees (₹2.4 Cr) + future lease interest (₹20 Cr) + guarantee at maximum vs carrying (₹48.8 Cr).

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a maturity table [L2]

At 31 March 2026: NCDs ₹200 Cr bullet on 31 March 2029, coupon 8.4% annual; working capital demand loan ₹80 Cr at 9.25%; ECB USD 10 million repayable in two equal instalments on 31 March 2028 and 2030, SOFR + 1.8% (SOFR 4.3% at the reporting date), interest annual, ₹85/USD; lease payments ₹5 Cr p.a. for 7 years. Prepare the maturity table with <1, 1–5 and >5 year buckets and a carrying amount column (assume carrying amount equals principal except leases at ₹27 Cr).

💡 Hint: Floating interest uses the reporting-date rate; demand loans go in the earliest bucket.

Q2. Explain the reconciliation [L1]

List the reconciling items between the total of a maturity table and the carrying amounts of financial liabilities, and give an example of each.

Q3. Derivative liabilities [L2]

The company has gross-settled USD/INR forward purchase contracts (pay ₹ 425 Cr, receive USD 5 million in 6 months) and a pay-fixed IRS in a cash flow hedge (net settlement; MTM liability ₹3 Cr). Explain how each is presented in the 107.39 maturity analysis.
