Key concept

Gearing Ratio and the Net Debt Definition

Concept: Gearing = Net debt ÷ Equity (or ÷ Total capital); net debt definition entity-specific and disclosed

Statement

\[ \text{Gearing}_{E} = \frac{\text{ND}}{E} \qquad \text{Gearing}_{TC} = \frac{\text{ND}}{\text{ND} + E} \]

where:

  • \(\text{ND}\) = net debt = gross debt (non-current and current borrowings including current maturities, and, by policy, lease liabilities, interest accrued, debt-hedging derivative liabilities) − cash and cash equivalents − other liquid assets (bank deposits, liquid MF units, by policy);
  • \(E\) = total equity (or equity attributable to owners, or equity excluding specified reserves such as the cash flow hedge reserve — by policy);
  • \(\text{ND} + E\) = total capital.

Ind AS 1.135 requires the entity to describe what it manages as capital and give summary quantitative data — so the definitions of ND and E are entity-specific and must be disclosed, applied consistently, and changes explained (1.135(c)).

Explanation

There is no single gearing ratio in Ind AS; it is a management measure. The two common forms relate simply: if \(g = \text{ND}/E\), then \(\text{ND}/(\text{ND} + E) = g/(1 + g)\). A net debt/equity of 0.5× equals 33.3% of total capital.

Definition choices and their effects:

  • Lease liabilities — post Ind AS 116, including them raises net debt (often materially for retailers, airlines, QSR chains). Covenants may use "frozen GAAP" excluding them; disclose.
  • Liquid investments — Indian companies often park surplus cash in liquid/overnight MF units (FVTPL); including them as cash-like reduces net debt; be consistent with the cash flow statement and internal reporting.
  • Restricted cash (margin money, escrow, unpaid dividend accounts) — usually excluded from deductions.
  • Equity — some exclude OCI reserves (hedge reserve) or NCI; some use tangible net worth (excluding goodwill and intangibles) when covenants require.
  • Perpetual bonds / CCDs classified as equity — affect equity rather than debt; disclose.

Gearing also links to the Schedule III debt-equity ratio disclosure (2021 amendments), which uses its own definition (total debt ÷ shareholders' equity) and requires explanation for changes over 25%; the two may differ.

Illustration

Annapurna Retail Ltd, 31 March 2026 (₹ Cr):

Component Amount
Borrowings (non-current + current) 800
Lease liabilities 1,200
Cash and cash equivalents (150)
Liquid MF units (100)
Total equity 2,000
Definition Net debt Gearing (ND/E) Gearing (ND/TC)
Excluding leases 550 0.28× 21.6%
Including leases 1,750 0.88× 46.7%

The same balance sheet shows "low" or "moderate" gearing depending on the definition — hence the disclosure requirement. If a loan covenant caps net debt/equity at 0.75× on "frozen GAAP", the company complies (0.28×) even though the Ind AS-based measure is 0.88×.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both gearing forms [L1]

Borrowings ₹1,250 Cr; lease liabilities ₹180 Cr; cash ₹210 Cr; fixed deposits with 9-month maturity ₹90 Cr; margin money deposits ₹25 Cr; total equity ₹2,400 Cr including cash flow hedge reserve of ₹(30) Cr. Compute gearing (ND/E and ND/TC) under two definitions you specify, and state which items you excluded and why.

💡 Hint: Decide treatment of restricted cash and the hedge reserve before computing.

Q2. Covenant vs disclosure [L2]

A loan agreement defines "Total Debt" to exclude lease liabilities and "Tangible Net Worth" as equity less goodwill and intangibles. Using Q1 figures plus goodwill ₹300 Cr and intangibles ₹120 Cr, compute the covenant ratio and explain how the capital management note should present both the internal gearing measure and covenant compliance.

Q3. Interview: choosing a definition [L2]

You are the new FP&A head of a QSR chain with heavy leases. Recommend a net debt definition for the capital management note and internal targets, considering Ind AS 116, rating agency methods, covenants and comparability with peers.
