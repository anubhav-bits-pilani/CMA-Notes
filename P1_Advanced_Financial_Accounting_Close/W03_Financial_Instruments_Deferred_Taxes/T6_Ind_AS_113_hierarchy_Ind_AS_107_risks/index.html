Roadmap topic: Ind AS 113 & 107: Level 1/2/3 Fair Value hierarchy and liquidity/credit risk disclosure tables

Overview

This topic closes the week's technical accounting by turning measurements into disclosures. Ind AS 113 defines fair value as a market-based exit price, sets out how to measure it (principal market, market-participant assumptions, highest and best use, liabilities with non-performance risk, valuation techniques and calibration) and requires a three-level hierarchy with extensive Level 3 disclosures. Ind AS 107 requires disclosure of financial instruments by category and class, their fair values, and the nature and extent of credit, liquidity and market risks — the ECL reconciliation, the maturity analysis of liabilities on undiscounted contractual cash flows, and sensitivity analyses. Ind AS 1.134–135 adds the capital management disclosure with gearing and covenant compliance.

These notes are among the most-read sections of an annual report by lenders, rating agencies and analysts, and they are high-risk areas for auditors and NFRA reviewers. For a CMA moving into reporting or FP&A, the skill is to translate models you already built this week — EIR schedules (w3_t1), ECL matrices (w3_t2), PPA valuations and earn-outs (w3_t4) — into compliant tables that tie to the balance sheet, and to explain them: why the maturity table does not tie to borrowings, why a Level 3 investment moved, what a 50 bps rate change does to profit. FP&A teams use the same data for covenant headroom, cash forecasting and scenario analysis.

The learning sequence: start with the definition of fair value (s01), then two special applications — highest and best use for non-financial assets and liabilities with non-performance risk (s02). Learn the valuation techniques and calibration (s03), then classify measurements into the hierarchy (s04) and prepare Level 3 disclosures (s05). Move to Ind AS 107 for categories, fair values, credit, liquidity and market risk (s06), and finish with capital management and covenants (s07). The concept notes give the level rule, the liquidity table, the sensitivity formula, the Level 3 roll-forward, the day-1 gain rule and the gearing ratio.

Related concepts to study

Registered valuers and ICAI Valuation Standards

Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules 2017; ICAI Valuation Standards for Level 3 work

Sec 247 of the Companies Act 2013 requires valuations "required to be made in respect of any property, stocks, shares, debentures, securities or goodwill or any other assets or net worth of a company or its liabilities" under the Act to be carried out by a registered valuer, registered with IBBI under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, in specified asset classes (land and building, plant and machinery, securities or financial assets). Valuations for Ind AS purposes are not always "under the Act", but in practice Level 3 measurements, PPAs and scheme valuations are commonly done by registered valuers. The ICAI Valuation Standards 2018 (issued by ICAI's Valuation Standards Board; mandatory for ICAI members acting as valuers) cover definitions, bases of value, approaches (market, income, cost), and specific assets (intangibles, financial instruments); they align closely with Ind AS 113 terminology and support the 113.93(g) valuation-process disclosure.

US GAAP ASC 820 and the NAV practical expedient

US GAAP ASC 820 converged hierarchy; NAV practical expedient excluded from levels

ASC 820 and Ind AS 113 (IFRS 13) were developed jointly and share the definition of fair value, the principal-market concept, the three-level hierarchy and most disclosures. Key differences: ASC 820 permits a NAV practical expedient for investments in investment companies (e.g., private equity funds, hedge funds) — fair value may be measured at the fund's reported NAV, and such investments are excluded from the hierarchy table (shown as a reconciling item). Ind AS 113 has no such expedient; NAV may be used only if it represents fair value, and such investments must be levelled (often Level 3 for AIF units). Private companies under US GAAP have some disclosure relief. Indian subsidiaries reporting to US parents need to map AIF/PE fund holdings accordingly.

Hedge accounting basics and the cash flow hedge reserve

Ind AS 109 hedge accounting basics (cash flow hedge reserve) for FX-forward disclosures

Ind AS 109 Chapter 6 allows designation of hedging relationships when there is an economic relationship, credit risk does not dominate, and the hedge ratio is consistent with risk management. In a cash flow hedge (e.g., FX forwards hedging forecast USD export receipts, or an IRS hedging floating-rate debt), the effective portion of the hedging instrument's fair value change goes to OCI as the cash flow hedge reserve and is reclassified to P&L when the hedged item affects P&L (or included in the cost of a non-financial asset — basis adjustment). Ineffectiveness goes to P&L. For disclosures, this affects market-risk sensitivity (OCI vs P&L), the maturity analysis of derivative liabilities, the fair value hierarchy (FX forwards usually Level 2) and the hedge accounting disclosures in Ind AS 107.21A–24G.

Links to PPA, FVLCD and ECL disclosures

→ w3_t4 PPA, w2_t3 FVLCD, w3_t2 ECL disclosures

Fair value measurement and disclosure tie together several topics. PPA fair values (w3_t4) are non-recurring Ind AS 113 measurements, mostly Level 3, and contingent consideration is a recurring Level 3 liability requiring the 113.93(e) roll-forward and sensitivity. FVLCD in impairment testing (w2_t3) must be measured under Ind AS 113, with the level and key assumptions disclosed under Ind AS 36.134(e). ECL (w3_t2) feeds the Ind AS 107.35H loss-allowance reconciliation, 35M exposures by grade and 35N provision matrix tables. Building disclosures from the same schedules used for measurement ensures that numbers tie and that narrative explanations match the models.

Excel maturity ladder and sensitivity tables

Excel: maturity ladder via SUMIFS on cash-flow dates; sensitivity tables

Build a cash-flow register: one row per contractual cash flow (instrument, type — principal/interest/lease, date, amount, currency). The maturity ladder then uses SUMIFS on dates relative to the reporting date for each bucket, with a check that principal flows sum to the contractual principal. Sensitivity tables use an exposure register (instrument, currency, amount, hedge designation) with SUMIFS by currency and P&L/OCI flag, multiplied by shock cells; Excel Data Tables (What-If Analysis) run the valuation model for Level 3 sensitivities.

=SUMIFS(CF[Amount],CF[Date],">"&EDATE(RepDate,12),CF[Date],"<="&EDATE(RepDate,60))
=SUMIFS(Exp[INR],Exp[Ccy],"USD",Exp[Flag],"P&L")*Shock_USD

Common pitfalls

Putting carrying amounts instead of undiscounted contractual flows in the maturity table

The mistake: presenting borrowings and payables at carrying amounts by maturity bucket. Why it happens: carrying amounts are readily available from the balance sheet, and preparers want the table to tie. Correct treatment: Ind AS 107.39(a) and B11D require contractual undiscounted cash flows, including future interest; floating interest at reporting-date rates; demand items in the earliest bucket; guarantees at maximum amount. Detection: if the table total equals the balance sheet total, it is almost certainly wrong; check that interest is included and reconcile the difference.

Labelling unlisted equity at last funding-round price as Level 2 without adjustment analysis

The mistake: classifying start-up investments as Level 2 because a recent round price exists. Why it happens: the round price looks "observable", and Level 2 avoids the Level 3 roll-forward and sensitivity disclosures. Correct treatment: assess whether the round instrument is identical (rights, liquidation preferences), whether the round was orderly and recent, and whether adjustments (time, market movements, rights differences, DLOM) are significant; significant unobservable adjustments make it Level 3 (113.73, 113.84). Detection: review the valuation workings for adjustments; check the time elapsed since the round and the instrument class held vs issued.

Omitting lease liabilities and future interest from liquidity analysis

The mistake: maturity tables showing only principal on borrowings and excluding lease liabilities. Why it happens: legacy templates from before Ind AS 116; treasury systems track borrowings only. Correct treatment: lease liabilities are financial liabilities within Ind AS 107; include undiscounted lease payments; include contractual interest on all interest-bearing liabilities. Detection: compare the table's lease line with the Ind AS 116 maturity analysis; recompute interest on the loan schedule (w3_t1 EIR model) and compare with the table.

Running sensitivity on gross exposure while ignoring hedges

The mistake: computing FX or interest sensitivity on gross receivables or gross floating debt, ignoring forwards, swaps and natural offsets. Why it happens: exposure data is extracted from AR/AP ledgers, while derivatives sit in a separate treasury system. Correct treatment: include all financial instruments at the reporting date — hedged items, hedging instruments and natural offsets; separate P&L effects from OCI (cash flow hedges, FVOCI); exclude forecast transactions (c03). Detection: reconcile the sensitivity exposure to the hedging register and FX position report; a sensitivity far larger than the year's actual FX gain/loss relative to rate moves is a red flag.

Practice Questions

Q1. Fair value hierarchy and Level 3 note [L3]

At 31 March 2026 a listed holding company has: NSE-listed equity (FVOCI) ₹640 Cr; liquid MF units ₹380 Cr; FBIL-valued SDLs (FVOCI) ₹210 Cr; FX forwards (asset) ₹14 Cr; IRS (liability) ₹6 Cr; unlisted equity (FVTPL) ₹290 Cr (opening ₹250 Cr; purchases ₹40 Cr; an investment with opening FV ₹35 Cr listed on NSE on 1 December 2025 at a then FV of ₹48 Cr, policy: date of event; FV gains on the remaining Level 3 portfolio ₹35 Cr); contingent consideration liability ₹55 Cr (issued in FY26 at ₹48 Cr; FV loss ₹7 Cr). (a) Prepare the hierarchy table. (b) Prepare Level 3 roll-forwards for assets and liabilities. (c) Draft the significant unobservable inputs table for the unlisted equity (DCF and multiples) and the earn-out. (d) Describe the quantitative sensitivity you would disclose.

💡 Hint: Check that the unlisted equity roll-forward closes at the hierarchy table's Level 3 figure.

Q2. Liquidity and credit risk tables [L3]

Using the following, prepare the Ind AS 107.39 maturity table (<1, 1–5, >5 years) and the 35H/35N trade receivables tables: term loan ₹400 Cr (carrying ₹396 Cr after fees), 8 equal semi-annual instalments starting 30 September 2026, 8.75% p.a. on outstanding paid semi-annually; cash credit ₹120 Cr at 9.5%; lease payments ₹18 Cr p.a. for 8 years (carrying ₹98 Cr); trade payables ₹260 Cr; corporate guarantee ₹75 Cr for a JV's loan. Trade receivables: not due ₹500 Cr (0.4%), 1–90 days ₹220 Cr (1.8%), 91–180 ₹70 Cr (9%), 181–365 ₹30 Cr (30%), >365 ₹20 Cr (75%); opening ECL ₹30 Cr; write-offs ₹6 Cr.

Q3. Market-risk sensitivity and hedges [L2]

An auto-component exporter at 31 March 2026 (₹85/USD; ₹92/EUR): USD receivables USD 45 million; EUR payables €12 million; USD ECB USD 60 million, of which USD 40 million is hedged by a cross-currency swap designated as a cash flow hedge; undesignated USD forward sales USD 20 million; floating-rate rupee loans ₹700 Cr, ₹300 Cr swapped to fixed (cash flow hedge; swap DV01 ₹0.12 Cr per bp); FVOCI listed equity ₹150 Cr. Prepare the 107.40 sensitivity disclosure for ±1% INR, ±50 bps rates and ±10% equity, separating P&L and OCI.

Q4. Fair value of land and liabilities in a PPA [L3]

An acquirer's PPA includes (i) a 20-acre industrial site in Thane with current-use value ₹180 Cr and potential residential redevelopment (GDV ₹1,200 Cr; construction ₹520 Cr; premiums ₹140 Cr; developer profit 18% of GDV; finance/time ₹90 Cr) subject to a rezoning application that the state has granted for three neighbouring plots; (ii) the target's ₹300 Cr listed NCDs trading at 96.5; (iii) an earn-out. Determine the HBU and fair value of the land, the fair value of the NCDs and its level, the measurement basis for the earn-out, and the disclosures under 113.93(i) and 113.97.

Q5. Capital management and covenant breach [L3]

A company has net debt/EBITDA covenant ≤ 3.25× (frozen GAAP excluding leases) and DSCR ≥ 1.2×. At 31 March 2026: borrowings ₹1,600 Cr; leases ₹350 Cr; cash and liquid MFs ₹220 Cr; Ind AS EBITDA ₹520 Cr (of which ₹70 Cr is due to Ind AS 116 rent reversal); equity ₹2,100 Cr; DSCR 1.15×. The lender grants a waiver on 28 April 2026; the board approves the financial statements on 15 May 2026. (a) Compute gearing and covenant ratios. (b) Determine the classification of the borrowings under Ind AS 1.74 (and contrast with IAS 1). (c) Draft the capital management note including compliance and consequences. (d) Explain how the breach affects the 107.39 maturity table.
