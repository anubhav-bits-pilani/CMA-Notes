Capital Management Disclosure, Gearing and Covenants

Scope: Capital management disclosure (Ind AS 1.134–135): gearing, covenant compliance

Why it matters

The capital management note is where an entity explains, in its own terms, how it funds itself and what leverage it targets — and whether it has complied with externally imposed capital requirements such as loan covenants or regulatory capital. For Indian companies with bank term loans, NCDs and ECBs, covenant compliance (net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, TOL/TNW, interest cover) is a live issue every quarter; a breach changes debt classification under Ind AS 1 and can trigger cross-default. For NBFCs and banks, RBI capital adequacy (CRAR) is the external requirement. FP&A owns the covenant forecast; the controller owns the disclosure; both must tell the same story.

Core notes

The requirement (Ind AS 1.134–136)

An entity discloses information that enables users to evaluate its objectives, policies and processes for managing capital (1.134). Specifically (1.135):

  • (a) Qualitative information about objectives, policies and processes, including:
  • (i) a description of what it manages as capital;
  • (ii) when it is subject to externally imposed capital requirements, the nature of those requirements and how they are incorporated into capital management;
  • (iii) how it is meeting its objectives for managing capital.
  • (b) Summary quantitative data about what it manages as capital (some entities include certain financial liabilities such as subordinated debt; others exclude components of equity such as cash flow hedge reserves).
  • (c) Changes in (a) and (b) from the previous period.
  • (d) Whether during the period it complied with any externally imposed capital requirements.
  • (e) When it has not complied, the consequences of such non-compliance.

Disclosures are based on information provided internally to key management personnel. If the entity manages capital differently across parts (e.g., a regulated NBFC subsidiary), separate disclosure may be needed (1.136).

Gearing ratio

The most common quantitative measure:

\[ \text{Gearing} = \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{Total equity}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{Net debt} + \text{Total equity}} \]

Net debt is entity-specific and must be defined: typically borrowings (current and non-current, including current maturities) + lease liabilities (policy choice post-Ind AS 116) + interest accrued − cash and cash equivalents − other bank balances − liquid investments (e.g., liquid MF units). Some include derivative liabilities hedging debt or exclude restricted cash.

Covenant compliance

Typical Indian lender covenants (term loans, NCD trust deeds, ECB agreements):

Covenant Definition (illustrative) Typical threshold
Net debt / EBITDA Net debt ÷ trailing 12-month EBITDA ≤ 3.0×
DSCR (PAT + depreciation + interest) ÷ (interest + principal due) ≥ 1.25×
Interest cover EBITDA ÷ finance cost ≥ 3.0×
TOL/TNW Total outside liabilities ÷ tangible net worth ≤ 2.5×
Current ratio Current assets ÷ current liabilities ≥ 1.1×
Fixed asset coverage (secured NCDs) Value of secured assets ÷ secured debt ≥ 1.25×

Definitions matter. Covenants are computed per the loan agreement — often on standalone financials, sometimes pre-Ind AS 116 ("frozen GAAP") or excluding exceptional items. The disclosure should state whether the entity complied and, if not, the consequences.

Breach consequences and classification (Ind AS 1.74)

If a covenant breach at or before the reporting date makes a long-term liability payable on demand, it is classified as current unless the lender agreed before the reporting date to provide a grace period ending at least 12 months after it (Ind AS 1.75). Ind AS carve-out: Ind AS 1.74 provides that if the lender agrees, after the reporting period but before the approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach, the liability is not classified as current — a departure from IAS 1. Disclose the breach, the waiver and its terms. (Check also whether MCA has notified the IAS 1 amendments on non-current liabilities with covenants; their disclosure of covenant risk aligns with this note.)

Schedule III (2021 amendments) links

Division II now requires disclosure of key ratios with explanations for changes of more than 25% (current ratio, debt-equity, DSCR, ROE, inventory turnover, trade receivables turnover, trade payables turnover, net capital turnover, net profit ratio, ROCE, return on investment), wilful defaulter status, reconciliation of quarterly returns/statements filed with banks for working capital limits secured on current assets with books, and utilisation of borrowed funds for the stated purpose. These dovetail with the capital management narrative.

Regulatory capital (NBFCs and banks)

NBFCs (Schedule III Division III) disclose capital adequacy under RBI's Scale-Based Regulation — CRAR (e.g., minimum 15% for NBFC-ML/UL, with Tier 1 ≥ 10%), leverage ratios, and compliance — in the capital management note.

FP&A linkage

Covenant headroom forecasting is a monthly FP&A deliverable: project EBITDA, net debt and DSCR under base and downside cases; compute headroom; flag breaches 2–3 quarters ahead to negotiate waivers before the reporting date, which avoids current classification without relying on the post-period carve-out.

=IF(NetDebt/EBITDA_LTM>Covenant_Max,"BREACH",TEXT(Covenant_Max-NetDebt/EBITDA_LTM,"0.00")&"x headroom")

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Lease liabilities in net debt — be consistent with covenant definitions; disclose the policy.
  • Liquid investments — include in net debt only if readily convertible and held for liquidity, consistent with internal reporting.
  • Covenants tested after year-end on year-end numbers — if the test date is after the reporting date, IAS 1's newer guidance focuses on covenants applicable at or before the reporting date; document the analysis.
  • Group-level vs standalone covenants — disclose the basis; a group may comply while a subsidiary breaches.
  • Cross-default clauses — a breach in one facility may make others callable.
  • Equity definition — excluding OCI reserves (e.g., hedge reserve) for capital management is acceptable if consistent with internal reporting; disclose.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 1.134–135 requires qualitative and quantitative disclosure of what is managed as capital, external requirements, compliance and consequences.
  • Gearing = net debt ÷ equity (or ÷ total capital); net debt is entity-specific and must be defined.
  • Covenant compliance must be stated; breaches affect current/non-current classification.
  • Ind AS 1.74 carve-out: a post-period, pre-approval lender waiver avoids current classification.
  • Schedule III ratio and borrowing disclosures complement the capital note.
  • FP&A should forecast covenant headroom to manage breaches before the reporting date.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute gearing and covenants [L2]

Given: non-current borrowings ₹900 Cr; current maturities ₹150 Cr; working capital loans ₹220 Cr; lease liabilities ₹120 Cr; cash ₹90 Cr; liquid MF units ₹60 Cr; total equity ₹1,600 Cr; EBITDA ₹420 Cr; finance cost ₹105 Cr; principal repayments due next year ₹150 Cr; PAT ₹160 Cr; depreciation ₹110 Cr. Compute net debt/equity, net debt/EBITDA, interest cover and DSCR, defining net debt with and without lease liabilities.

💡 Hint: State your net debt definition before computing.

Q2. Covenant breach and classification [L2]

A ₹600 Cr term loan (repayable over 6 years) has a covenant of net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0×. At 31 March 2026 the ratio is 3.4×; the bank issues a waiver letter on 25 April 2026; the board approves the financial statements on 20 May 2026. Determine the classification under Ind AS 1 and draft the capital management disclosure, contrasting with the IAS 1 answer.

Q3. Draft the capital management note [L3]

Draft a capital management note for an NSE-listed manufacturer with a target net debt/EBITDA of below 2.5×, NCD covenants (DSCR ≥ 1.3×, fixed asset cover ≥ 1.25×), a planned ₹1,000 Cr capex programme funded 60:40 by debt and internal accruals, and a dividend policy of 25–30% of PAT. Include the quantitative table structure.

Q4. FP&A headroom forecast [L3]

Design a quarterly covenant headroom model for the next 8 quarters: inputs, EBITDA and net debt drivers, covenant calculations per the loan agreement definitions, downside scenario, and a dashboard flag. Explain how you would handle Ind AS 116 if the covenant uses frozen pre-Ind AS 116 GAAP.
