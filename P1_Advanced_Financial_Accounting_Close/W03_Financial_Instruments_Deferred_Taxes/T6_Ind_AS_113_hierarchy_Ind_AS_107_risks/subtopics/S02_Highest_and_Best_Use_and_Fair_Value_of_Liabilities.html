Highest and Best Use and Fair Value of Liabilities

Scope: Non-financial assets: highest and best use (113.27); liabilities and non-performance risk

Why it matters

Two of the most judgement-heavy applications of fair value in Indian practice are land and liabilities. Industrial land in Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru or the NCR often has a higher value for residential or commercial redevelopment than as a factory site, and Ind AS 113's highest-and-best-use (HBU) principle requires valuers to consider it — in PPAs, investment property disclosures and FVLCD tests. For liabilities, the fair value of an earn-out, a derivative liability or a FVTPL-designated bond must reflect the entity's own credit risk (non-performance risk), producing the counter-intuitive result that a deterioration in credit quality reduces the liability and creates a gain — which Ind AS 109 then routes through OCI for FVO liabilities.

Core notes

Highest and best use (113.27–30)

A fair value measurement of a non-financial asset takes into account a market participant's ability to generate economic benefits by using the asset in its highest and best use or by selling it to another market participant who would use it in its highest and best use (113.27). The HBU must be:

  • Physically possible — size, shape, location, soil conditions;
  • Legally permissible — zoning, FSI/FAR norms, environmental permissions, land-use conversion rules (e.g., industrial-to-residential conversion policies under state development control regulations); a use that is reasonably probable to become legally permissible (e.g., a pending rezoning that market participants would price) can be considered;
  • Financially feasible — generates adequate income or cash flows (including conversion costs) to produce an investment return market participants would require.

HBU is determined from market participants' perspective, even if the entity intends a different use (113.29). The current use is presumed to be HBU unless market or other factors suggest a different use would maximise value.

Valuation premise (113.31–32):

  • In combination with other assets/liabilities (e.g., a plant used with its land, buildings and workforce) — value assumes the other assets are available to market participants;
  • On a stand-alone basis — e.g., vacant land valued for sale.

Illustration. An NSE-listed textile company owns 12 acres in central Mumbai, used as a mill (current use). A PPA requires fair value.

Basis Inputs Value (₹ Cr)
Current use (industrial, in combination with plant) Industrial land rates, plant DRC 520
Alternative: residential redevelopment Permissible FSI under current DCR, residual method: GDV ₹2,600 Cr less construction ₹1,100 Cr, approvals/premiums ₹350 Cr, developer profit ₹420 Cr, finance and time ₹180 Cr 550

If the conversion is legally permissible (or reasonably probable) and financially feasible, HBU is redevelopment and land fair value is ₹550 Cr, even though the acquirer intends to continue the mill. The plant's value in the combination may then fall (it would be demolished), and the overall fair value must reflect a single, consistent HBU for the group of assets. Disclose when the HBU differs from current use (113.93(i)).

Liabilities and own equity (113.34–47)

Fair value of a liability assumes it is transferred to a market participant at the measurement date — it remains outstanding, and the transferee is required to fulfil the obligation; it is not settled with the counterparty or extinguished (113.34).

Measurement hierarchy (113.37–41):

  1. Quoted price for the transfer of an identical or similar liability (rarely available).
  2. If not available but the identical item is held by another party as an asset (e.g., a listed bond), measure from the holder's perspective — quoted price of the asset in an active market, or other observable inputs, or another valuation technique (113.37–39), adjusted for factors specific to the asset not applicable to the liability (e.g., third-party credit enhancement).
  3. If no corresponding asset (e.g., decommissioning obligations, contingent consideration liabilities), use a technique from the perspective of a market participant that owes the liability — typically a PV technique including the compensation a market participant would require for taking on the obligation (risk premium) (113.40–41, B31–B33).

Non-performance risk (113.42–44)

Fair value of a liability reflects non-performance risk — the risk that the entity will not fulfil the obligation — which includes, but is not limited to, the entity's own credit risk. It is assumed to be the same before and after the transfer (113.42).

  • Third-party credit enhancements (a bank guarantee, parent guarantee) inseparable from the liability are excluded from the issuer's measurement (113.44); the issuer's own credit risk is used.
  • Effect: a widening of the entity's credit spread reduces the fair value of its liabilities and produces a fair-value gain.

Ind AS 109 consequence. For financial liabilities designated at FVTPL (fair value option), the change in fair value attributable to changes in own credit risk is presented in OCI (not reclassified), with the remainder in P&L, unless that creates or enlarges an accounting mismatch (Ind AS 109.5.7.7). For held-for-trading liabilities and derivatives, all changes go to P&L.

Illustration. An entity has issued a ₹500 Cr 5-year bond designated at FVTPL (coupon 8.5%). Over the year, risk-free yields rise 30 bps and its credit spread widens 120 bps. Approximate modified duration 3.5.

  • Total change in FV ≈ −3.5 × 1.50% × 500 = −₹26.3 Cr (gain).
  • Attributable to own credit: −3.5 × 1.20% × 500 = −₹21.0 Cr → OCI.
  • Attributable to benchmark rates: −₹5.3 Cr → P&L.

Restrictions and demand features

  • A restriction preventing transfer of a liability is not a separate input; it is already reflected in other inputs (113.45–46).
  • Demand deposits — fair value is not less than the amount payable on demand, discounted from the first date payment could be required (113.47).

Own equity instruments

Measured from the perspective of a market participant holding the instrument as an asset (113.37) — e.g., equity-classified contingent consideration at acquisition date.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Speculative rezoning — only if market participants would price it; document evidence (draft development plans, precedent conversions, premium policies).
  • Heritage or CRZ restrictions — legal constraints override physically possible uses.
  • HBU for a group of assets — avoid valuing land at redevelopment value and plant at continuing-use value simultaneously.
  • Derivative liabilities and DVA — debit valuation adjustments reflect own credit; Indian corporates often ignore DVA on small FX forward portfolios — assess materiality.
  • Contingent consideration liabilities — include own credit in the discount rate, often a modest effect over short horizons.
  • Credit deterioration "gains" — ensure the OCI split is applied for FVO liabilities and explain it clearly to the board.

Key takeaways

  • Non-financial assets are valued at their highest and best use: physically possible, legally permissible, financially feasible.
  • HBU is a market-participant concept; current use is presumed HBU unless evidence suggests otherwise.
  • Liabilities are valued on a transfer (not settlement) basis, often from the holder's perspective.
  • Non-performance risk, including own credit, is included; third-party credit enhancements are excluded.
  • Under Ind AS 109, own-credit changes on FVO liabilities go to OCI without recycling.
  • Disclose when HBU differs from current use.

Practice Questions

Q1. HBU for industrial land [L2]

A company's 5-acre factory site in Pune (current use value ₹140 Cr) could, under the current development control regulations, be converted to commercial use on payment of a premium. A residual valuation for commercial use gives GDV ₹620 Cr, construction cost ₹260 Cr, premiums and approvals ₹70 Cr, developer profit 20% of GDV, and finance/time costs ₹45 Cr. Determine the HBU and fair value, and describe the effect on the factory plant's fair value within the same group of assets.

💡 Hint: Compute the residual land value and compare it with current-use value.

Q2. Own credit split [L2]

An entity designated a ₹300 Cr bond at FVTPL. At year-end, its fair value fell from ₹300 Cr to ₹284 Cr. A benchmark-rate approach attributes ₹6 Cr of the decrease to market interest rates. Pass the journal entry and explain the OCI presentation, including what happens on maturity.

Q3. Liability without a corresponding asset [L3]

Describe how you would measure the fair value of a decommissioning obligation for an offshore platform assumed in a business combination, using the market-participant compensation approach in 113.B31–B33. List the inputs, their sources and the level classification.

Q4. Third-party guarantee [L1]

A subsidiary issues ₹200 Cr NCDs guaranteed by its AAA-rated parent; the subsidiary's standalone rating is A−. Explain which credit risk the subsidiary uses to measure the NCDs at fair value (if designated at FVTPL) and why.
