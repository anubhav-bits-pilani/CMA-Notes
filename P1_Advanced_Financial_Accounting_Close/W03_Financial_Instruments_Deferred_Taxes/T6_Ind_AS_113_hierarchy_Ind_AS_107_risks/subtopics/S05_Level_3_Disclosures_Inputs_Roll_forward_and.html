Subtopic note

Level 3 Disclosures: Inputs, Roll-forward and Sensitivity

Scope: Level 3 disclosures (113.93): techniques, significant unobservable inputs, roll-forward, sensitivity, transfers between levels

Why it matters

Level 3 is where management's judgement drives reported numbers. Ind AS 113.93 therefore requires the most granular disclosures for Level 3: what technique was used, which unobservable inputs matter and their values, how the balance moved, how sensitive it is to reasonably possible changes, and what governance surrounds the valuations. Indian listed companies with large unlisted portfolios (holding companies, conglomerates with start-up investments, insurers, NBFCs) and acquirers with earn-outs produce these notes every year; auditors, NFRA reviewers and analysts read them for evidence of aggressive valuations. A well-structured disclosure also forces the finance team to understand its own models.

Core notes

What 113.93 requires (by class of asset/liability)

For recurring and non-recurring fair value measurements:

  • (a) The fair value at the end of the period; for non-recurring, the reasons for the measurement.
  • (b) The level within the hierarchy.
  • (c) For recurring measurements held at the end of the period, transfers between Level 1 and Level 2, the reasons, and the entity's policy for when transfers are deemed to occur (113.95).
  • (d) For Level 2 and Level 3, a description of valuation techniques and inputs; any change in technique and the reasons. For Level 3, quantitative information about the significant unobservable inputs used (values or ranges and weighted averages), unless those inputs are not developed by the entity (e.g., prior-transaction prices used without adjustment).
  • (e) For recurring Level 3, a reconciliation from opening to closing balances, disclosing separately: total gains/losses in P&L (and the line items), total gains/losses in OCI (and the line items), purchases, sales, issues and settlements (each separately), and transfers into or out of Level 3 with reasons and the transfer policy.
  • (f) For recurring Level 3, the amount of total gains/losses for the period in (e) included in P&L that are attributable to unrealised gains/losses relating to assets and liabilities held at the end of the period, and the line items.
  • (g) For Level 3 (recurring and non-recurring), a description of the valuation processes (policies, procedures, how decisions are made, who performs valuations — e.g., a valuation committee, registered valuers).
  • (h) For recurring Level 3:
  • (i) a narrative description of the sensitivity to changes in unobservable inputs if a change might result in a significantly higher or lower fair value, and of interrelationships between inputs;
  • (ii) for financial assets and liabilities, if changing one or more unobservable inputs to reasonably possible alternative assumptions would change fair value significantly, state that fact and disclose the effect (quantitative sensitivity), and how it was calculated.
  • (i) If the highest and best use of a non-financial asset differs from its current use, disclose that fact and why.

Also 113.97: for items not measured at FV but whose FV is disclosed (e.g., borrowings, loans at amortised cost), disclose (b), (d) and (i) — the level, the technique and inputs (but not the quantitative unobservable input table or roll-forward).

Sensitivity (113.93(h))

Narrative — describe how changes in each significant unobservable input affect fair value and any interrelationships (e.g., higher growth often accompanies higher discount rates).

Quantitative (financial instruments) — show the effect of reasonably possible alternative assumptions:

Instrument Input Change Increase in FV (₹ Cr) Decrease in FV (₹ Cr)
ABC Pvt Ltd WACC ±1% 11.5 (WACC −1%) (9.8) (WACC +1%)
ABC Pvt Ltd Terminal growth ±0.5% 5.2 (4.6)
XYZ Tech Revenue multiple ±10% 7.0 (7.0)
Contingent consideration Probability ±10 pp Liability +11.6 Liability (11.6)

"Reasonably possible" is entity- and period-specific; the alternative should be plausible given market conditions at the reporting date, not stress scenarios.

Valuation process disclosure (113.93(g))

Describe: who performs valuations (internal team, external registered valuers under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017), frequency, review by a valuation committee or CFO, audit committee oversight, use of ICAI Valuation Standards, back-testing against subsequent transactions, and how changes in fair value are analysed.

Excel implementation

  • Maintain a register per instrument with columns: class, level, technique, each significant input, opening FV, movements, closing FV, sensitivity results.
  • Build the roll-forward with SUMIFS by movement type and level; the closing balance must equal the hierarchy table's Level 3 total.
  • Run sensitivity with data tables (What-If Analysis) on the valuation model's input cells.
=SUMIFS(Mvmt[Amount],Mvmt[Level],3,Mvmt[Type],"Purchase",Mvmt[Class],B6)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Inputs not developed by the entity — e.g., a recent unadjusted round price, or third-party pricing — the quantitative input table may be omitted, but the entity must not ignore reasonably available information.
  • Grouping into classes — classes are determined by nature, characteristics and risks, and level; aggregating start-ups with mature companies can hide risk.
  • Realised vs unrealised — the 113.93(f) figure excludes gains on items sold during the year.
  • Transfers timing — inconsistent transfer policy between in and out distorts the roll-forward.
  • Sensitivity to multiple inputs — if inputs are correlated, a combined sensitivity may be more meaningful; disclose the approach.
  • Non-recurring Level 3 (impairment FVLCD, held for sale) — roll-forward and quantitative sensitivity not required, but technique and inputs are.

Key takeaways

  • Level 3 disclosures cover: technique, significant unobservable inputs (values/ranges), roll-forward, unrealised gains, valuation processes, sensitivity.
  • Roll-forwards separate P&L, OCI, purchases, sales, issues, settlements and transfers in/out.
  • Quantitative sensitivity is required for Level 3 financial instruments with significant reasonably possible effects.
  • Disclose transfer policy and HBU differences.
  • Items at amortised cost with FV disclosed need level and technique but not the roll-forward.
  • Build the disclosure from a single valuation register so that tables tie.

Practice Questions

Q1. Prepare the Level 3 roll-forward [L2]

Opening Level 3 unlisted equity ₹340 Cr. During FY26: purchases ₹60 Cr; a stake sold for ₹95 Cr (carrying amount at last valuation ₹80 Cr); one investment transferred out on NSE listing (carrying ₹55 Cr at the transfer date under a "date of event" policy); FV gains recognised in P&L on the remaining portfolio ₹42 Cr; an FVOCI-equity stake (Level 3) lost ₹12 Cr in OCI. Prepare the roll-forward and the unrealised gains line.

💡 Hint: The gain on the stake sold belongs in the P&L line but not in the unrealised line.

Q2. Sensitivity disclosure [L2]

An unlisted investment valued at ₹150 Cr by DCF (WACC 14%, terminal growth 5%, terminal value 70% of EV) is to be disclosed with sensitivity to ±1% WACC and ±0.5% growth. Describe the calculation steps in Excel and the narrative disclosure, including interrelationships.

Q3. Draft the inputs table [L3]

For a group holding (i) Series B CCPS in a fintech valued by OPM back-solve to a recent round, (ii) an earn-out liability, and (iii) investment property disclosed at fair value using a capitalisation approach, draft the significant unobservable inputs table with plausible ranges and the direction of each relationship.

Q4. Review findings [L2]

A Level 3 note shows sensitivity only to ±0.25% WACC for an investment whose fair value fell 40% during the year, and the valuation-process description says only "valued by an independent valuer". Identify the deficiencies against 113.93(g)–(h) and suggest improvements.
