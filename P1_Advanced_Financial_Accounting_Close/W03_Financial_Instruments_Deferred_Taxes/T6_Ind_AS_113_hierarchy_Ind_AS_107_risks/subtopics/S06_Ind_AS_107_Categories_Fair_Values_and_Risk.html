Ind AS 107: Categories, Fair Values and Risk Disclosures

Scope: Ind AS 107: categories and fair values (107.8, 107.25), credit risk (35H, 35M), liquidity maturity analysis (107.39, B11), market-risk sensitivity (107.40)

Why it matters

Ind AS 107 is the disclosure counterpart to Ind AS 109 and 32: it tells users what financial instruments the entity holds, how they are measured, what their fair values are, and how exposed the entity is to credit, liquidity and market risks. For Indian listed companies, the financial instruments note is often the longest in the annual report, and lenders, rating agencies and analysts use the maturity table and sensitivity numbers directly. Preparing it well requires data from treasury, the ECL model (w3_t2), the EIR schedules (w3_t1), lease liabilities (w2) and FX exposure reports — and an understanding of what each table is meant to show.

Core notes

Categories of financial instruments (107.8)

Disclose the carrying amounts of each category, in the balance sheet or notes:

Financial assets Financial liabilities
FVTPL — mandatorily measured FVTPL — held for trading
FVTPL — designated (FVO) FVTPL — designated (FVO)
Amortised cost Amortised cost
FVOCI — debt (recycling)
FVOCI — equity (designated, no recycling)

Typical Indian table: investments (MF units at FVTPL, equity at FVOCI, bonds at amortised cost), trade receivables, loans, cash and bank balances, other financial assets; borrowings, lease liabilities, trade payables, derivative liabilities, other financial liabilities.

Fair values (107.25–30)

For each class of financial assets and liabilities, disclose the fair value in a way that permits comparison with carrying amount (107.25). Exemptions (107.29): when carrying amount is a reasonable approximation of fair value (short-term trade receivables/payables, cash), and for lease liabilities. Fair values disclosed must also show the hierarchy level and techniques under Ind AS 113.97. Example: fixed-rate NCDs at amortised cost ₹500 Cr with fair value ₹486 Cr (Level 2) after a rise in yields.

Also: deferred day-1 differences (107.28) — the accounting policy for recognising the deferred difference, the aggregate yet to be recognised and a reconciliation, and why the transaction price was not the best evidence of FV.

Credit risk (107.35A–38)

Objective (35B): explain credit risk management practices and how they relate to ECL; provide quantitative and qualitative information to evaluate amounts arising from ECL; and show the entity's credit risk exposure.

  • Credit risk management practices (35F–35G): how SICR is determined (including the 30 days past due presumption and any rebuttal), definition of default and reasons (90 dpd presumption), how instruments are grouped, how forward-looking information is incorporated, write-off policy, modifications.
  • Loss-allowance reconciliation (35H): for each class, a table from opening to closing loss allowance, showing separately:
  • 12-month ECL;
  • lifetime ECL for instruments that are not credit-impaired (stage 2), credit-impaired (stage 3), purchased or originated credit-impaired;
  • lifetime ECL for trade receivables, contract assets and lease receivables under the simplified approach (5.5.15);
  • movements: new assets originated, derecognitions, transfers between stages, write-offs, changes in models/risk parameters, FX and other.
  • Gross carrying amount changes (35I) explaining changes in the loss allowance.
  • Credit risk exposure (35M): gross carrying amount of financial assets (and exposure on loan commitments/financial guarantees) by credit risk rating grades, separately for 12-month, lifetime and credit-impaired, and simplified-approach items.
  • Simplified approach (35N): for trade receivables, contract assets and lease receivables, information may be based on a provision matrix — disclose the ageing buckets with gross amounts, loss rates and allowance.
  • Maximum exposure (36): amount best representing maximum exposure without collateral; collateral and credit enhancements; written-off amounts still subject to enforcement (35L).

Liquidity risk (107.39, B10A–B11F)

Disclose:

  • (a) a maturity analysis for non-derivative financial liabilities (including issued financial guarantee contracts) showing the remaining contractual maturities;
  • (b) a maturity analysis for derivative financial liabilities, including remaining contractual maturities for those derivatives where contractual maturities are essential for understanding the timing of cash flows (e.g., IRS in a cash flow hedge of floating-rate debt, loan commitments) (B11B);
  • (c) a description of how the entity manages liquidity risk (undrawn facilities, liquid assets held, diversification of funding).

Rules for the maturity table (B11–B11D):

  • Amounts are contractual undiscounted cash flows — principal and interest — which differ from carrying amounts (B11D); floating-rate interest is estimated from conditions at the reporting date (B11D).
  • Time bands are chosen by judgement (B11), e.g., <1 year, 1–5 years, >5 years, or finer near term (on demand, <3 months, 3–12 months).
  • When a counterparty can choose when an amount is paid, the liability is placed in the earliest period in which the entity can be required to pay (B11C(a)) — demand loans "on demand"; financial guarantees at the maximum guaranteed amount in the earliest period the guarantee could be called (B11C(c)).
  • Include lease liabilities (undiscounted lease payments).
  • Gross-settled derivatives (FX forwards): show gross outflows (and the corresponding inflows if relevant to understanding) (B11D(d)).

Market risk sensitivity (107.40–42)

Unless the entity uses VaR-type analysis (107.41), disclose:

  • (a) a sensitivity analysis for each type of market risk (currency, interest rate, other price risk) to which the entity is exposed at the reporting date, showing how profit or loss and equity would have been affected by reasonably possible changes in the relevant risk variable;
  • (b) methods and assumptions used;
  • (c) changes from the previous period in methods/assumptions and reasons.

Practical points (B17–B28): sensitivity is computed on financial instruments at the reporting date (not forecast transactions); currency risk arises only on monetary items in a currency other than the functional currency; hedged exposures and hedging instruments are both included (so the net unhedged exposure drives P&L sensitivity, while cash flow hedges affect equity via the hedge reserve). Floating-rate borrowings drive interest-rate P&L sensitivity; fixed-rate debt at amortised cost has no P&L sensitivity. Equity price risk applies to FVTPL/FVOCI equity.

Other qualitative disclosures (107.33–34)

For each type of risk: exposures and how they arise; objectives, policies and processes for managing the risk; methods used to measure it; and summary quantitative data based on information provided internally to key management personnel; plus concentrations of risk (e.g., a single DISCOM or OEM customer).

Hedge accounting disclosures (107.21A–24G)

Risk management strategy, the amount/timing/uncertainty of future cash flows, and the effects of hedge accounting on financial position and performance (hedging instruments' notional and carrying amounts, hedge ineffectiveness, cash flow hedge reserve movements).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Trade payables to MSMEs — include in the maturity table at contractual (45-day MSMED Act) timing; delays are relevant to liquidity and to Sec 43B(h) tax (w3_t3).
  • Covenant breaches — debt callable on demand at the reporting date goes to the earliest bucket; link to Ind AS 1.74 classification and capital-management disclosure (s07).
  • Supplier finance arrangements — the IASB's 2023 amendments add disclosures (IAS 7/IFRS 7); check whether and when MCA notifies equivalent Ind AS amendments.
  • Sensitivity of FVOCI investments — goes to equity (OCI), not P&L; show both columns.
  • Netting/offsetting (107.13A–13F) — disclose rights of set-off for derivatives under ISDA/CSA agreements.
  • Financial guarantees given to subsidiaries — in standalone statements, include the full guaranteed amount in the earliest bucket.

Key takeaways

  • 107.8: carrying amounts by measurement category; 107.25: fair value by class vs carrying amount (exemptions for short-term items and lease liabilities).
  • Credit risk: management practices, 35H loss-allowance reconciliation, 35M exposure by grade, 35N provision matrix for simplified approach.
  • Liquidity: undiscounted contractual cash flows including interest, in time bands, earliest possible date for demand features and guarantees.
  • Market risk: sensitivity on reporting-date financial instruments, showing P&L and equity effects of reasonably possible changes.
  • Include hedges in sensitivity and disclose concentrations.
  • The note draws on ECL, EIR, lease and treasury data — build it from a single instrument register.

Practice Questions

Q1. Categories and fair values table [L1]

Prepare the 107.8/107.25 table for: MF units ₹250 Cr (FVTPL); listed equity ₹80 Cr (FVOCI); 5-year bonds held to collect ₹150 Cr (FV ₹146 Cr); trade receivables ₹400 Cr; term loans ₹600 Cr at amortised cost (fixed rate; FV ₹612 Cr); lease liabilities ₹90 Cr; trade payables ₹310 Cr; FX forward liability ₹6 Cr. State which fair values may be omitted and why.

Q2. 35H reconciliation [L2]

Opening ECL on trade receivables (simplified approach) ₹18 Cr. During the year: new receivables added ECL ₹7 Cr; receivables collected released ₹3 Cr; change in forward-looking overlay ₹2.5 Cr; write-offs ₹4 Cr; recoveries of previously written-off amounts ₹0.6 Cr (recognised in P&L). Prepare the 35H reconciliation and explain the treatment of recoveries.

💡 Hint: Recoveries of written-off amounts don't pass through the allowance.

Q3. Liquidity disclosure judgement [L2]

A company has: a ₹200 Cr working capital demand loan (rolled over annually); a ₹500 Cr term loan with a financial covenant breached at 31 March 2026 (waiver received 20 April 2026); a corporate guarantee of ₹150 Cr for an associate's borrowing; and an IRS (pay fixed/receive floating) in a cash flow hedge. Explain how each appears in the 107.39 maturity analysis.

Q4. Sensitivity design [L3]

Treasury reports: USD receivables USD 40 million, USD payables USD 15 million, USD ECB USD 50 million of which USD 30 million is hedged by a cross-currency swap designated as a cash flow hedge; floating-rate rupee borrowings ₹800 Cr; FVOCI listed equity ₹120 Cr. Design the 107.40 sensitivity disclosure (inputs, assumptions, P&L vs equity columns) for ±2% INR/USD, ±50 bps rates and ±10% equity prices.

Q5. Interview: why won't it tie? [L1]

Explain to a credit analyst why the total of the liquidity maturity table (₹1,840 Cr) is higher than the borrowings and lease liabilities on the balance sheet (₹1,520 Cr).
