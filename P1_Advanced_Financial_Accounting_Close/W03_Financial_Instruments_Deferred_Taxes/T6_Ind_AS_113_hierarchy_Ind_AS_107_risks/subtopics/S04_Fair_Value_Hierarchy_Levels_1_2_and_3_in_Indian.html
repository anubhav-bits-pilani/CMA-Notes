Subtopic note

Fair Value Hierarchy Levels 1, 2 and 3 in Indian Practice

Scope: Hierarchy (113.72–90): Level 1 NSE/BSE quoted equity; Level 2 FX forwards, IRS, FBIL-valued G-secs; Level 3 unlisted equity, contingent consideration

Why it matters

The fair value hierarchy tells users how much of a balance sheet's fair value rests on observable market prices and how much on management's models. Analysts focus on Level 3 because it carries estimation risk; auditors treat Level 3 measurements as significant risks requiring specialist involvement; regulators (SEBI, NFRA) have commented on misclassification. In Indian groups, typical portfolios include NSE-listed equity, mutual fund units, G-secs and corporate bonds in treasury, FX forwards and swaps for hedging, and unlisted strategic stakes and earn-outs. Classifying each correctly drives the extent of disclosure under Ind AS 113.93.

Core notes

The hierarchy (113.72–75)

Inputs to valuation techniques are categorised into three levels, giving the highest priority to unadjusted quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities (Level 1) and the lowest to unobservable inputs (Level 3). The level of a fair value measurement as a whole is determined by the lowest level input that is significant to the entire measurement (113.73; → c01). The hierarchy categorises inputs, not techniques — a DCF can be Level 2 if all significant inputs are observable.

Level 1 (113.76–80)

Quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities that the entity can access at the measurement date.

  • Active market — transactions occur with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing basis.
  • A Level 1 price is used without adjustment whenever available, except in limited cases (113.79): large numbers of similar assets valued by matrix pricing as a practical expedient; a quoted price that doesn't represent fair value at the measurement date (significant events after market close); a liability quoted as an asset with adjustments for asset-specific factors. Any adjustment moves the measurement to a lower level.
  • Blockage factor not permitted — FV = quoted price × quantity (113.80), even for a 10% stake that would depress the price if sold at once.

Indian examples: NSE/BSE-listed equity shares with regular trading; exchange-traded derivatives (NSE index futures and options); actively traded G-secs on NDS-OM; ETFs with active trading. Mutual fund units — redemption at the daily published NAV: Indian companies commonly classify these as Level 1 (NAV is a quoted price at which the fund transacts daily) though some classify as Level 2; disclose the policy consistently.

Level 2 (113.81–85)

Inputs other than quoted prices included in Level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly:

  • quoted prices for similar assets/liabilities in active markets;
  • quoted prices for identical or similar items in markets that are not active;
  • observable inputs other than quoted prices: interest rate and yield curves at commonly quoted intervals, implied volatilities, credit spreads;
  • market-corroborated inputs.

Adjustments to Level 2 inputs that are significant and use unobservable inputs may push the measurement into Level 3 (113.84).

Indian examples:

FX forwards (USD/INR) FBIL reference rate, forward premia, OIS discount curve All observable; not quoted for the identical contract
Interest rate swaps (MIBOR OIS, MIFOR) Observable swap curves, CSA discounting Curve inputs observable

Corporate bonds (illiquid) Matrix pricing using FBIL/FIMMDA-type spread matrices by rating and tenor; valuation agency prices (e.g., CRISIL/ICRA for MF industry) Observable spreads

Note: CVA/DVA adjustments on derivatives using unobservable credit spreads may be significant enough to push to Level 3 for large uncollateralised positions.

Level 3 (113.86–90)

Unobservable inputs, used to the extent relevant observable inputs are not available. They reflect assumptions market participants would use, developed using the best information available — which may include the entity's own data, adjusted if reasonable available information indicates market participants would use different data (113.89).

Indian examples:

Instrument Significant unobservable inputs
Unlisted equity shares (strategic stakes, start-up CCPS) Forecast cash flows, WACC, terminal growth, DLOM, revenue multiples adjusted for size, OPM volatility
Contingent consideration (earn-out) Probability of targets, forecast EBITDA, risk-adjusted discount rate
Investment property (FV disclosure under Ind AS 40) Rental growth, capitalisation rate, comparable land rates adjusted
PPA intangibles (non-recurring FV) Royalty rates, attrition, CACs
Unquoted debt with company-specific credit Credit spread derived from internal ratings
Long-dated FX options with extrapolated volatility Volatility beyond observable tenor

Recurring vs non-recurring measurements

  • Recurring — measured at fair value at each reporting date (FVTPL/FVOCI instruments, derivatives, earn-outs).
  • Non-recurring — measured at fair value in particular circumstances (held-for-sale assets at FVLCTS, impaired assets at FVLCD, PPA). Disclosure requirements differ (113.93).
  • Disclosed-only fair values — for items at amortised cost (borrowings, loans), the level must still be disclosed (113.97).

Transfers between levels (113.93(c), 113.95)

Movements occur when observability changes — e.g., an unlisted investee lists on NSE (Level 3 → Level 1), a bond stops trading (Level 1 → Level 2), a derivative's tenor moves within observable range (Level 3 → Level 2). The policy for determining when transfers are deemed to occur (e.g., beginning of period, end of period, date of event) must be disclosed and applied consistently to transfers in and out.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • SME platform or illiquid NSE shares — infrequent trading may mean the market is not active; consider Level 2.
  • Suspended shares — last traded price adjusted → Level 2 or 3.
  • Broker quotes — indicative quotes not backed by transactions are not Level 1; assess whether corroborated.
  • Funding-round prices — Level 2 only if the round is recent, the instrument is identical (same rights) and no significant adjustment is needed; otherwise Level 3 (pitfall).
  • NAV of AIFs or unlisted funds — not a quoted price in an active market; unless redeemable at NAV at the measurement date, often Level 3 (and US GAAP excludes NAV practical expedient from the levels altogether).
  • Pricing services — the entity remains responsible for the classification; understand the vendor's methodology.

Key takeaways

  • Level 1: unadjusted quoted prices in active markets for identical items; no blockage factor.
  • Level 2: observable inputs other than Level 1 prices — curves, spreads, FBIL prices, similar-item quotes.
  • Level 3: significant unobservable inputs — unlisted equity, earn-outs, model-derived spreads.
  • The level is set by the lowest-level input significant to the whole measurement.
  • Disclose levels for recurring, non-recurring and disclosed-only fair values, and the transfer policy.
  • Indian treasury portfolios are mostly Level 1/2; strategic investments and earn-outs are Level 3.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the portfolio [L1]

Classify into Level 1, 2 or 3 with reasons: (a) shares of an NSE Nifty 50 company; (b) a 7-year SDL valued at FBIL price; (c) a 6-month USD/INR forward; (d) CCPS of an unlisted D2C start-up valued using the latest funding round less a 20% adjustment for liquidation preference; (e) a liquid mutual fund; (f) an earn-out liability; (g) shares of a company suspended from trading for 3 months.

Q2. Transfer between levels [L2]

An unlisted investment (Level 3, carrying ₹90 Cr at 1 April 2025) lists on NSE on 15 November 2025; at 31 March 2026 its quoted price gives ₹160 Cr. The entity's policy is to recognise transfers at the end of the reporting period. Explain how the investment appears in the Level 3 roll-forward and the hierarchy table, and the disclosure of the transfer.

💡 Hint: The transfer-date policy decides where the gain between November and March is shown.

Q3. Derivative with CVA [L2]

An uncollateralised 10-year cross-currency swap with a BBB-rated counterparty has a mark-to-market of ₹45 Cr before CVA; CVA computed using a proxy spread (no CDS market for the counterparty) is ₹7 Cr. Discuss whether the measurement is Level 2 or Level 3, using the significance assessment in 113.73.

Q4. Hierarchy table review [L3]

A draft hierarchy note classifies all unlisted equity investments as Level 2 "based on recent transactions" and shows no Level 3 roll-forward. List the questions you would ask management and the evidence needed to sustain or change the classification.
