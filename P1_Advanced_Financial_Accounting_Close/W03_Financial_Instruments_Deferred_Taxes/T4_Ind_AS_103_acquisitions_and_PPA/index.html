Roadmap topic: Ind AS 103 Business Combinations: Acquisition method, Purchase Price Allocation (PPA), intangible asset recognition, Goodwill vs Bargain Purchase (Capital Reserve)

Overview

Ind AS 103 governs how an acquirer accounts for obtaining control of a business. The acquisition method has four steps (103.5): identify the acquirer; determine the acquisition date; recognise and measure identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any NCI; and recognise and measure goodwill or a gain from a bargain purchase. The purchase price allocation (PPA) is the practical exercise that delivers step three: a valuation of every identifiable asset and liability at fair value, including intangibles the target never recognised, followed by deferred tax on the step-ups. Goodwill is the residual. Ind AS departs from IFRS 3 in two important places — bargain purchase gains go to capital reserve (not P&L), and common control combinations are mandatorily accounted under pooling of interests (Appendix C).

For a CMA moving into technical accounting, FP&A or transaction services, Ind AS 103 is where valuation, tax and consolidation meet. Deal models must forecast PPA amortisation, the unwinding of PPA deferred tax, earn-out remeasurements and acquisition costs; controllers must defend the PPA to auditors and audit committees; FP&A teams must restate budgets and comparatives after common control mergers. The Indian context adds NCLT schemes under Sections 230–232, IBC resolution-plan acquisitions (a common source of bargain purchases), SEBI disclosure requirements for listed acquirers, and tax rules that make goodwill non-depreciable from AY 2021-22.

The learning sequence follows the acquisition method. Start with scope (s01): is the acquired set a business, who is the acquirer, is it a reverse acquisition? Then measure the consideration (s02), including deferred and contingent elements. Next, build the identifiable net assets at fair value with the measurement exceptions (s03), and the PPA intangibles with their valuation methods (s04). Choose the NCI basis (s05), then compute goodwill or a bargain purchase (s06). Layer the special situations: step acquisitions, measurement period and costs (s07), and finally common control combinations where none of the acquisition method applies (s08). The six concept notes capture the formulas you will use repeatedly: the goodwill equation, PPA deferred tax, bargain purchase measurement, the common-control capital reserve, contingent consideration remeasurement, and MEEM.

Related concepts to study

US GAAP ASC 805 differences

US GAAP ASC 805: bargain purchase gain in P&L; private-company goodwill amortisation alternative; ASC 805-50 common control

ASC 805 is largely converged with IFRS 3 on the acquisition method but differs from Ind AS in three practical ways. First, a bargain purchase, after reassessment, is a gain in net income — Ind AS routes it to capital reserve. Second, NCI must be measured at fair value (full goodwill only); Ind AS allows the proportionate method. Third, private companies may elect the Private Company Council alternative to amortise goodwill over up to 10 years and test impairment only on triggering events. For common control, ASC 805-50 requires transfers at the parent's historical carrying amounts with retrospective presentation, similar in spirit to Appendix C pooling. Indian subsidiaries of US groups need GAAP-difference adjustments for each of these in reporting packages.

Goodwill impairment and valuation links

Annual goodwill impairment → w2_t3; valuation techniques and hierarchy → w3_t6

Goodwill recognised under Ind AS 103 is not amortised; it is allocated to CGUs expected to benefit from synergies and tested annually under Ind AS 36 (Week 2, w2_t3). The PPA's assumptions — revenue growth, margins, discount rates — become the benchmark against which later impairment tests are compared, so aggressive PPA forecasts often reappear as impairment a few years later. Proportionate-NCI goodwill must be grossed up for testing. The fair values in the PPA are measured under Ind AS 113 (w3_t6): most intangibles and earn-outs are Level 3, requiring disclosure of techniques, unobservable inputs and sensitivity, and use of market, income and cost approaches consistently with the valuation techniques covered there.

Tax treatment of goodwill and the 12.15(a) exemption

Tax: goodwill not depreciable from AY 2021-22; no DTL on goodwill initial recognition (12.15(a))

The Finance Act 2021 amended Sec 32 and Sec 2(11) so that goodwill of a business or profession is not a depreciable asset from AY 2021-22 (FY 2020-21 onwards); goodwill already in a block was removed from the block, with a capital-gains mechanism on transfer. Consequently, purchased goodwill has a tax base of nil in most Indian situations. Ind AS 12.15(a) nevertheless prohibits recognising a DTL on the initial recognition of goodwill, because the DTL would increase goodwill, which would increase the DTL, and so on. Any subsequent difference arising (e.g., on tax-deductible goodwill in another jurisdiction) is assessed normally. The exemption does not extend to intangibles recognised in the PPA, which do attract DTL.

Reading PPA disclosures of NSE acquirers

Read PPA notes of NSE acquirers (e.g., Zomato/Eternal–Blinkit, FY23) for intangibles, goodwill and NCI disclosures

Zomato Ltd (renamed Eternal Ltd in 2025) acquired Blink Commerce (Blinkit) in FY23 through an all-share deal, having previously held a minority stake — a step acquisition. Its FY23 consolidated financial statements disclose the consideration, the fair value of the previously held interest and the remeasurement effect, the identified intangibles (such as brand, technology and customer-related assets) with their valuation methods and lives, the deferred tax on them, and the resulting goodwill. Read the note alongside the Ind AS 103.B64 disclosure checklist: acquisition date, reasons, consideration by class, contingent consideration, receivables' gross and fair values, the goodwill rationale, and revenue/profit since acquisition. Comparing two or three such notes (e.g., listed pharma or IT acquirers) builds judgement about typical intangible splits.

Excel PPA bridge

Excel: PPA bridge — purchase price → book net assets → FV step-ups → DTL → goodwill/capital reserve

A PPA bridge is a single-page schedule that every deal file should contain: start with consideration (by component, at fair value), add NCI and the FV of any previously held interest; subtract book net assets; subtract each fair value step-up (PPE, inventory, each intangible) and add step-downs; add back the DTL (and deduct DTAs) computed as step-up × reversal-period tax rate; the balancing figure is goodwill, or a capital reserve if negative. Build it with named inputs, a sign convention, and checks: total step-ups tie to the valuation report; DTL equals Σ step-ups × rate; goodwill equals the consolidation-entry balancing figure. Chart it as a waterfall for audit committee packs.

=Consideration+NCI+Prev_Held-Book_NA-SUM(StepUps)+SUMPRODUCT(StepUps,DT_Rates)

Common pitfalls

Capitalising transaction costs into goodwill

The mistake: adding banker, legal, valuation and due-diligence fees to consideration, inflating goodwill. Why it happens: standalone books capitalise these costs into the investment under Ind AS 27, and old Indian GAAP practice capitalised them in goodwill; the consolidation team copies the investment figure. Correct treatment: expense acquisition-related costs in the consolidated P&L as incurred (103.53); debt issue costs go into EIR (Ind AS 109), equity issue costs to equity (Ind AS 32). Detection: reconcile the goodwill working's consideration line to the SPA and completion statement; trace any difference between standalone investment cost and consideration.

Ignoring DTL on intangible step-ups

The mistake: recognising brands and customer relationships at fair value but no deferred tax, understating goodwill and later overstating ETR. Why it happens: the intangibles were never in the target's books, so they don't appear in its DT working; teams think "it's goodwill-like". Correct treatment: in a share acquisition, tax bases are unchanged, so DTL = step-up × reversal-period rate (103.24, Ind AS 12.66); only goodwill's initial recognition is exempt. Detection: check that Σ(step-ups) × rate equals the PPA DTL; analyse whether post-deal consolidated ETR exceeds the statutory rate by the tax effect of amortisation.

Applying acquisition accounting to group reorganisations under common control

The mistake: fair-valuing assets and recognising goodwill when merging fellow subsidiaries or a subsidiary into its parent. Why it happens: the scheme includes a registered-valuer exchange ratio and "purchase consideration", which looks like an arm's-length deal; IFRS 3 is silent on common control, and teams trained on IFRS apply the acquisition method. Correct treatment: Ind AS 103 Appendix C — pooling at carrying amounts, reserves preserved, capital reserve for the share-capital difference, comparatives restated. Detection: map the ultimate controlling party before and after; any scheme between group companies should trigger an Appendix C check; the auditor's certificate under Sec 232(3) should reference Appendix C.

Recognising bargain purchase gain in P&L (IFRS/US GAAP habit)

The mistake: crediting the excess of net assets over consideration to P&L as "gain on acquisition". Why it happens: IFRS 3 and ASC 805 do so; Big-4 global methodology documents and training material are IFRS-based. Correct treatment: after reassessment (103.36), credit OCI and accumulate in capital reserve if there is clear evidence of the reasons; otherwise credit capital reserve directly (103.34–36A). Never P&L. Detection: search the P&L for "gain on bargain purchase" or "negative goodwill"; review equity movements for a capital reserve credit and the B64(n) disclosure.

Practice Questions

Q1. End-to-end PPA and goodwill [L3]

On 1 October 2025, Pragya Consumer Ltd (NSE-listed, 115BAA) acquires 80% of Saral Foods Pvt Ltd (115BAA). Consideration: ₹480 Cr cash; 20 lakh Pragya shares (NSE close ₹650 on 1 October; ₹600 on signing); deferred ₹60 Cr payable 1 October 2027 (Pragya borrowing rate 9%); earn-out of ₹50 Cr if FY27 EBITDA ≥ ₹120 Cr (FV ₹21 Cr, liability). Pragya previously held 10% at FVOCI-equity (FV ₹75 Cr at 1 October). Transaction costs ₹7 Cr. Saral's book net assets ₹400 Cr (tax bases equal book values). Fair value findings: PPE step-up ₹90 Cr; brand ₹150 Cr (relief-from-royalty); customer relationships ₹110 Cr (MEEM); inventory step-up ₹12 Cr; contingent liability ₹18 Cr (deductible when paid); gratuity remeasured under Ind AS 19 increases the liability by ₹6 Cr. NCI fair value for the remaining 10% is ₹82 Cr. (a) Compute consideration transferred. (b) Prepare the PPA bridge with deferred tax. (c) Compute goodwill under both NCI methods. (d) State the treatment of the transaction costs and the FVOCI stake remeasurement. (e) List the post-acquisition P&L lines this deal will create in FY26.

Q2. IBC acquisition with bargain purchase [L3]

Dridh Infra Ltd acquires 100% of a stressed power-equipment maker under an NCLT-approved resolution plan for ₹900 Cr cash. The provisional valuation shows identifiable net assets of ₹1,380 Cr before DT, with ₹500 Cr of step-ups (tax bases unchanged; rate 25.168%). Reassessment identifies: ₹160 Cr economic obsolescence on plant, ₹45 Cr of unprovided warranty claims, and a ₹25 Cr indemnity from the resolution applicant's consortium partner against a specific customs demand of ₹25 Cr (recognised at FV ₹25 Cr). (a) Compute the provisional and reassessed bargain purchase. (b) Pass the acquisition entry. (c) Explain the Ind AS route and the disclosure. (d) Explain the difference in reported profit versus an IFRS reporter.

💡 Hint: Work through each reassessment item's DT effect before netting.

Q3. Group reorganisation scheme [L2]

Promoter-owned Holdco controls Alpha Ltd (listed, 72%) and Beta Pvt Ltd (100%) since 2018. A scheme merges Beta into Alpha with appointed date 1 April 2025, effective 20 January 2026. Alpha issues 2 crore shares of ₹10 (exchange ratio based on a registered valuer's fair value of ₹1,400 Cr for Beta). Beta's balance sheet: share capital ₹50 Cr, securities premium ₹30 Cr, retained earnings ₹210 Cr, FVOCI reserve ₹15 Cr; net assets ₹305 Cr; Beta's brand (unrecognised) valued at ₹400 Cr. (a) Determine the accounting method. (b) Pass the entries in Alpha's books. (c) Explain the restatement of FY25 comparatives. (d) Identify three disclosure items and the auditor's certificate requirement.

Q4. Step acquisition, measurement period and earn-out [L3]

Tara Ltd held 30% of Megh Ltd (associate; carrying ₹180 Cr; share of FCTR in OCI ₹6 Cr). On 1 January 2026 it acquires a further 30% for ₹270 Cr plus an earn-out liability (FV ₹30 Cr); FV of the previously held 30% is ₹240 Cr. Provisional FV of Megh's identifiable net assets is ₹780 Cr; NCI proportionate. In June 2026 the final valuation (acquisition-date data) increases a technology intangible by ₹40 Cr (life 5 years; DT 25.168%). At 31 March 2027 the earn-out FV is ₹45 Cr after strong FY27 trading. (a) Compute the FY26 gain on the previously held interest and provisional goodwill. (b) Account for the measurement-period adjustment, including the revised FY26 comparatives. (c) Account for the earn-out at 31 March 2027. (d) Draft the FP&A commentary explaining FY27 reported vs adjusted PAT.

Q5. Reverse acquisition through a listed shell [L3]

Unlisted Vega Tech (8 lakh shares, FV ₹2,500 per share) reverse-merges into listed Orbit Ltd (40 lakh shares, quoted ₹30; net identifiable assets FV ₹9 Cr; dormant with no employees or processes). Orbit issues 1.6 crore shares to Vega's owners. (a) Determine whether Orbit is a business and the consequence for the accounting. (b) Compute the deemed consideration and the amount recognised in P&L or goodwill. (c) State the share capital and comparatives presented in the combined entity's FS. (d) Explain the EPS computation for the comparative year.
