Key concept

Common Control Capital Reserve and Restated Comparatives

Concept: Common control: difference between consideration and transferor's share capital → capital reserve; comparatives restated from start of earliest period

Statement

Under the pooling of interests method in Ind AS 103 Appendix C:

\[ \text{Capital reserve} = \text{SC}_{T} - (\text{SC}_{\text{issued}} + \text{OC}) \]

where \(\text{SC}_{T}\) is the share capital of the transferor, \(\text{SC}_{\text{issued}}\) the amount recorded as share capital issued by the transferee (at nominal value) and \(\text{OC}\) any other consideration in cash or other assets (including cancellation of an existing investment). A positive result is a credit to capital reserve; a negative result is a debit (presented within other equity, separately from other capital reserves). Financial information for prior periods is restated as if the combination had occurred from the beginning of the preceding period presented, or from the date common control was established if later.

Explanation

  • No goodwill, no fair values. Assets and liabilities come across at carrying amounts; only accounting-policy harmonisation adjustments are allowed. The "price" in the scheme (share exchange ratio based on a valuation) has no accounting effect beyond the number of shares issued.
  • Reserves keep their identity. The transferor's retained earnings, securities premium, general reserve, FVOCI reserve and so on appear as such in the transferee. Only the share capital is replaced by the transferee's issued capital and the difference goes to capital reserve.
  • Separate presentation. The capital reserve on common control is distinct from capital reserve on a bargain purchase (s06) and from other capital reserves.
  • Restatement. Because common control means the economic group did not change, the combined entity presents itself as if always combined. Comparatives are restated from the start of the preceding period — even if the NCLT appointed date or effective date falls later — unless common control started later (then from that date).
  • Which carrying amounts. For a subsidiary merging into its parent, ITFG guidance uses the carrying amounts in the parent's consolidated financial statements (including goodwill and PPA balances).

Illustration

Fellow subsidiaries Kalpa Ltd and Vriksha Ltd (both 100% owned by Mool Holdings since 2019) merge: Vriksha into Kalpa, appointed date 1 April 2025, effective 10 February 2026. Kalpa issues 3 crore equity shares of ₹10 to Mool and pays ₹5 Cr cash. Vriksha's balance sheet: share capital ₹40 Cr, securities premium ₹12 Cr, retained earnings ₹88 Cr; net assets ₹140 Cr.

Capital reserve = 40 − (30 + 5) = ₹5 Cr credit.

Kalpa's FY26 financial statements present FY25 comparatives restated to include Vriksha from 1 April 2024 (beginning of the preceding period), since common control existed throughout.

Connections

Links to s08 (Appendix C scope and NCLT schemes), s06 (bargain-purchase capital reserve — different origin), and the pitfall of applying acquisition accounting to group reorganisations. Week 1 Schedule III other-equity presentation applies. In FP&A, restated comparatives change the base year for growth metrics and budget variance analysis — models must be re-based on the pooled history, not the pre-merger standalone history.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the capital reserve [L1]

Transferor's share capital is ₹60 Cr. The transferee issues 4 crore shares of ₹10 each (valued at ₹85 per share in the scheme) and cancels an existing 10% holding carried at ₹18 Cr. Compute the capital reserve and state whether it is a debit or a credit.

Q2. Restatement date [L2]

H acquired control of B on 1 July 2024 from a third party; A has been H's subsidiary since 2015. B merges into A in FY26 (appointed date 1 April 2025). From what date are A's FY25 comparatives restated? Explain how the B acquisition in July 2024 from the third party is reflected.

💡 Hint: Restatement cannot precede the establishment of common control.

Q3. Review a scheme's accounting clause [L3]

A scheme clause states: "The excess of the fair value of net assets of the transferor over the shares issued shall be credited to General Reserve, and the transferor's FVOCI reserve shall be transferred to retained earnings." Identify the Ind AS non-compliances and redraft the clause.
