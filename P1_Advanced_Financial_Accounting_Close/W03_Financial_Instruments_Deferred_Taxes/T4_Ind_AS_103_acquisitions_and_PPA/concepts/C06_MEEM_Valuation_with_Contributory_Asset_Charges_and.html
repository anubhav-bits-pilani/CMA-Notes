Key concept

MEEM Valuation with Contributory Asset Charges and TAB

Concept: MEEM = PV of after-tax cash flows attributable to the intangible after contributory asset charges (TAB only if tax-amortisable)

Statement

\[ \text{FV}_{\text{MEEM}} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\left[\text{EBITA}_{t} \times (1 - T)\right] - \text{CAC}_{t}}{(1 + k)^{t - 0.5}} \times \text{TAF} \]

where:

  • \(\text{EBITA}_{t}\) = earnings before interest, tax and amortisation from the existing customer base (or the primary intangible) in year \(t\), after attrition and excluding costs of acquiring new customers;
  • \(T\) = tax rate (25.168% for a 115BAA company);
  • \(\text{CAC}_{t}\) = contributory asset charges — returns on (and where applicable of) working capital, fixed assets, assembled workforce, brand, technology and other assets used to generate the cash flows;
  • \(k\) = discount rate for the intangible (typically WACC plus an intangible-specific premium);
  • \(t - 0.5\) = mid-year discounting convention (use \(t\) for year-end flows);
  • \(\text{TAF}\) = tax amortisation factor \(= 1 \div (1 - T \times \text{PV of annuity of tax amortisation per rupee})\), applied only where a market participant would obtain a tax-deductible basis; otherwise \(\text{TAF} = 1\).

Explanation

MEEM isolates the value of one intangible — usually the one that drives the business (customer relationships in B2B services, technology in software, licences in telecom). The idea: a business's cash flows are produced jointly by many assets. Charge the cash flow for the use of each other asset as if it were rented, and what remains — the excess earnings — belongs to the primary intangible.

  • Attrition — revenue from the acquisition-date customer cohort decays (e.g., 12% p.a.) based on historical churn; the life of the intangible ends when the cohort's revenue becomes immaterial.
  • CACs — return on working capital at a short-term debt rate; return on and of fixed assets (depreciation proxy + required return); workforce at its replacement-cost value × required return; brand/technology at their royalty rates (consistent with RFR valuations, avoiding double counting).
  • Discount rate — higher than WACC for riskier intangibles; checked through the weighted average return on assets (WARA) reconciliation to the deal IRR.
  • TAB — the extra value from tax deductions on amortising the intangible. In Indian share deals, there is no new tax base, so TAB is excluded; in asset purchases where an intangible qualifies under Sec 32(1)(ii) (25% WDV), TAB may be included. Customer relationships and goodwill are generally not depreciable for tax.

Illustration

Customer relationships of a Chennai IT services target (₹ Cr; year-end flows; \(k\) = 14%; no TAB in a share deal):

Year Revenue (attrition 20%) EBITA @25% After-tax @25.168% CAC (8% of revenue) Excess earnings PV @14%
1 300.0 75.0 56.1 (24.0) 32.1 28.2
2 240.0 60.0 44.9 (19.2) 25.7 19.8
3 192.0 48.0 35.9 (15.4) 20.5 13.8
4 153.6 38.4 28.7 (12.3) 16.4 9.7
5 122.9 30.7 23.0 (9.8) 13.2 6.8

Five-year value ≈ ₹78.3 Cr; extending to year 10 adds a further tail. With no TAB the full FV becomes the step-up and generates a DTL at 25.168% (→ c02). The same cash flows must not also support a separate customer-list or backlog value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Extend the MEEM [L2]

Using the illustration's assumptions, extend the schedule to year 8 and compute the total fair value. Then compute the effect on the value if attrition is 15% instead of 20%.

💡 Hint: Set the model up with attrition and discount rate as input cells so the sensitivity is a single change.

Q2. CAC design [L3]

A target's assets: working capital ₹40 Cr (required return 9%), fixed assets ₹120 Cr (required return 11%, annual depreciation ₹15 Cr), assembled workforce ₹18 Cr (return 15%), brand valued by RFR at 2.5% royalty. Revenue year 1 ₹400 Cr. Compute the year-1 CAC and explain whether a return of fixed assets should also be charged when the excess-earnings model starts from EBITA rather than EBITDA.

Q3. TAB in an Indian context [L2]

Explain when the TAB should be included in a MEEM for an Indian acquisition, giving one example where it would apply and one where it would not, with reference to Sec 32(1)(ii) and the treatment of goodwill from AY 2021-22.
