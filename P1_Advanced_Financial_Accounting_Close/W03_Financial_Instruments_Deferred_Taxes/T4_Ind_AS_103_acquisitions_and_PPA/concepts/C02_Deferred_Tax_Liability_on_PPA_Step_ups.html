Key concept

Deferred Tax Liability on PPA Step-ups

Concept: PPA DTL = (Fair value − Tax base) × Tax rate on step-ups; increases goodwill

Statement

For each asset or liability recognised in a business combination:

\[ \text{DTL}_{i} = (\text{FV}_{i} - \text{TB}_{i}) \times t \]

where \(\text{FV}_{i}\) is the carrying amount recognised in the PPA (fair value, or the Ind AS 103 exception amount), \(\text{TB}_{i}\) is the tax base in the hands of the acquiree after the transaction, and \(t\) is the tax rate enacted or substantively enacted for the period of reversal (Ind AS 12.47). Because DTL reduces identifiable net assets \(A\), and goodwill \(= C + N + P - A\), every rupee of DTL increases goodwill by one rupee (or reduces a bargain purchase).

Explanation

  • Why it arises. In a share acquisition in India, the target's tax WDV and tax bases do not change — the buyer acquires shares, not assets. The PPA steps up book values to fair value (PPE, brands, customer relationships, inventory) while tax bases stay put, creating taxable temporary differences (Ind AS 12.19, 12.66).
  • No initial recognition exemption. The IRE in 12.15(b) does not apply to business combinations, so DT must be recognised on step-ups.
  • Intangibles with nil tax base. Customer relationships, brands created internally by the target and order backlog typically have a tax base of nil → DTL on the full fair value.
  • Exception for goodwill. No DTL on goodwill's initial recognition (12.15(a)).
  • Rate. Use the target's expected rate on reversal: 25.168% for a 115BAA company; 34.944% for an old-regime company with income above ₹10 Cr; if the target plans to switch to 115BAA before reversal, use the new rate for the differences expected to reverse after the switch.
  • DTAs too. Liabilities stepped up (contingent liabilities, onerous contracts, gratuity measured under Ind AS 19) generate deductible differences → DTA, if recoverable (12.24, 12.66). Target's unused losses may become recognisable as DTA because of the group's profit outlook.
  • Asset deals differ. In an asset purchase or slump sale where the buyer gets a stepped-up tax base, differences may be small or nil.
  • Reversal. As step-ups are depreciated/amortised, the DTL unwinds through deferred tax credit in P&L, so post-deal ETR looks "normal" despite non-deductible amortisation.

Illustration

Sparsh Ltd acquires 100% of Kiran Ltd (a 115BAA company) for ₹600 Cr.

₹ Cr Book Tax base Fair value Temp. difference DT @25.168%
PPE 220 160 280 120 30.2 DTL
Brand – – 90 90 22.7 DTL
Customer relationships – – 70 70 17.6 DTL
Inventory 50 50 56 6 1.5 DTL
Contingent liability (GST dispute) – – (12) (12) 3.0 DTA
Other net assets 80 80 80 – –

Note: the existing book-vs-tax difference on PPE (220 − 160 = 60) already carried a DTL of ₹15.1 Cr in Kiran's books; the PPA recomputes the total DTL on FV vs tax base (₹30.2 Cr), so the incremental DTL from step-ups is ₹15.1 Cr.

Net assets before DT = 280 + 90 + 70 + 56 − 12 + 80 = ₹564 Cr. Net DT = 30.2 + 22.7 + 17.6 + 1.5 − 3.0 = ₹69.0 Cr DTL. Identifiable net assets = ₹495 Cr. Goodwill = 600 − 495 = ₹105 Cr (versus ₹36 Cr if DT were wrongly ignored).

Connections

Links to s03 (deferred tax exception, 103.24–25), s04 (intangible step-ups), c01 (goodwill equation), and to w3_t3 Ind AS 12 mechanics (temporary differences, 12.47 rate selection, 12.66–68 business combinations). At consolidation (w3_t5) the PPA DTL and its unwind are layered over the subsidiary's standalone DT. In FP&A models, PPA amortisation and the matching DT credit should be shown as separate lines so that adjusted PAT and cash tax forecasts reconcile.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute PPA deferred tax [L2]

Target (old regime, 34.944%) has PPE carrying ₹300 Cr, tax WDV ₹210 Cr, fair value ₹380 Cr; an unrecognised technology valued at ₹60 Cr (tax base nil); gratuity under Ind AS 19 ₹14 Cr (deductible on payment). The acquirer plans to move the target to 115BAA from FY28, by which time 40% of the PPE difference and 50% of the technology difference will have reversed. Compute the net DT in the PPA.

💡 Hint: Split each difference by expected reversal period before applying rates.

Q2. Effect on goodwill and future ETR [L2]

A brand step-up of ₹150 Cr is amortised over 10 years with nil tax base at 25.168%. Show the effect of the PPA DTL on goodwill at acquisition and the annual P&L effect (amortisation and DT) for FY27, and explain why the consolidated ETR is unaffected by the non-deductible amortisation.

Q3. Asset deal vs share deal [L3]

Compare the PPA DT outcome when the same business is bought (a) as 100% of shares, (b) by a slump sale under Sec 50B where the buyer allocates the lump sum to assets for tax. Discuss the goodwill, DT and future cash tax implications, and how the deal model should reflect them.
