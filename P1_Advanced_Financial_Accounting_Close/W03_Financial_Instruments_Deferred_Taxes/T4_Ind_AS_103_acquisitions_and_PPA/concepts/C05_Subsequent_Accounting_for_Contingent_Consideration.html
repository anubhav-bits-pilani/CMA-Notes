Key concept

Subsequent Accounting for Contingent Consideration

Concept: Contingent consideration: equity-classified not remeasured; liability remeasured through P&L (103.58)

Statement

After the acquisition date, changes in the fair value of contingent consideration that are not measurement-period adjustments are accounted for as follows (103.58):

Classification at acquisition (103.40, Ind AS 32) Subsequent treatment
Equity Not remeasured; subsequent settlement accounted for within equity
Financial liability or asset within Ind AS 109 Measured at fair value at each reporting date; changes recognised in P&L
Liability/asset outside Ind AS 109 (rare) Per the applicable Ind AS (e.g., Ind AS 37)

Measurement-period adjustments (new information about facts existing at the acquisition date, within 12 months) adjust goodwill retrospectively (103.45, 103.58).

Explanation

  • Why P&L. Once control is obtained, the earn-out is a financial instrument of the group; post-acquisition events (target hits EBITDA, regulatory approval, share price moves) are performance outcomes, not part of the purchase price for the business acquired. Goodwill is frozen at the acquisition date.
  • The counter-intuitive result. If the acquired business outperforms, the earn-out liability rises and the acquirer books a loss; if it underperforms, the liability falls and the acquirer books a gain — while potentially also impairing goodwill. Analysts typically strip these remeasurements from adjusted earnings.
  • Equity classification. An obligation to issue a fixed number of the acquirer's shares (fixed-for-fixed) is equity and is never remeasured. A variable number of shares to a fixed value, or cash, is a liability.
  • Components of P&L movement. Practice splits the movement into time-value unwind (often presented as finance cost) and fair value changes (other gains/losses); the policy must be consistent and disclosed.
  • Fair value hierarchy. Earn-outs are usually Level 3; changes feed the Level 3 roll-forward (Ind AS 113.93(e)).
  • Tax. The remeasurement is not taxable/deductible; it is a capital item relating to cost of shares → typically no current tax; DT considered under outside-basis rules.

Illustration

On 1 April 2025, Arjun Tech acquires Bhima Software; earn-out of ₹50 Cr payable on 31 March 2027 if FY27 revenue ≥ ₹200 Cr. Acquisition-date FV: probability 40%, discount rate 12% → \(50 \times 0.40 \div 1.12^{2} = 15.94\) Cr (liability).

At 31 March 2026, probability revised to 75% after a large contract win (post-acquisition event); one year remains:

\[ \text{FV} = \frac{50 \times 0.75}{1.12} = 33.48 \text{ Cr} \]

₹ Cr Amount
Unwind: 15.94 × 12% 1.91 (finance cost)
Fair value change: 33.48 − 15.94 − 1.91 15.63 (P&L loss)
Goodwill adjustment Nil

If the earn-out were 1 lakh Arjun shares (fixed number), it would be recorded in equity at ₹15.94 Cr-equivalent fair value at acquisition and left unchanged; on issue, transferred within equity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Remeasure an earn-out [L2]

Acquisition-date FV of a cash earn-out was ₹22 Cr (payable in 2 years, maximum ₹40 Cr, discount rate 13%). At the next year-end, the target has missed its first-year milestone and the probability of payment falls to 20%. Compute the year-end FV, split the movement into unwind and fair value change, and pass the journal entry.

💡 Hint: Unwind the opening balance for one year before computing the fair value change.

Q2. Classification decides volatility [L2]

Compare the P&L effect over two years of an earn-out settled in (a) a fixed 5 lakh acquirer shares, (b) acquirer shares worth ₹40 Cr, if the acquirer's share price doubles and the target meets its targets. Explain the Ind AS 32 reasoning.

Q3. Measurement period or not? [L3]

Four months after acquisition, the acquirer's valuer revises the earn-out FV upward by ₹6 Cr. The reasons: (i) ₹4 Cr from correcting the revenue base the valuer had mis-keyed from the target's FY25 audited accounts; (ii) ₹2 Cr from strong trading in the four months since acquisition. Determine the accounting for each component.
