Common Control Combinations and NCLT Schemes

Scope: Common control combinations (Appendix C): pooling of interests at carrying amounts; NCLT schemes under Sec 230–232

Why it matters

A large share of Indian "mergers" are group reorganisations: a promoter group merges a sister company into the listed flagship, folds a wholly owned subsidiary into its parent, or demerges a division into a new listed entity. These are common control combinations, outside the acquisition method. Ind AS 103 Appendix C requires the pooling of interests method — no fair values, no new goodwill, comparatives restated. Because most such reorganisations are effected through NCLT-sanctioned schemes under Sections 230–232 of the Companies Act 2013, the accounting treatment in the scheme must comply with Ind AS and the statutory auditor must certify it. Mistakes here (fair-valuing assets, creating goodwill, using the court's appointed date blindly) are among the most frequently qualified areas in Indian reporting.

Core notes

Definition and scope (Appendix C, paras 1–7)

A common control business combination is one in which all the combining entities or businesses are ultimately controlled by the same party or parties both before and after the combination, and that control is not transitory.

  • The controlling party can be an entity, an individual, or a group of individuals acting together under a contractual arrangement (e.g., a promoter family with a shareholders' agreement).
  • The extent of NCI in each combining entity before and after is not relevant to whether it is common control.
  • Transitory control (e.g., a Newco briefly controlled before an IPO-related combination) is excluded — use the acquisition method.
  • Appendix C applies to businesses; transfers of assets that are not businesses are accounted as asset transfers.

Typical Indian fact patterns: merger of a WOS into its parent; merger of two fellow subsidiaries; demerger of a division of a listed company into another group company; hive-down of a business to a WOS by slump sale.

Pooling of interests method (Appendix C, para 9)

  1. Assets and liabilities of the combining entities are reflected at their carrying amounts.
  2. No adjustments to fair value and no new assets or liabilities (e.g., no brands not already recognised); the only adjustments permitted are to harmonise accounting policies.
  3. Financial information for prior periods is restated as if the combination had occurred from the beginning of the preceding period in the financial statements, irrespective of the actual date of combination — but if common control was established only after that date, restatement starts from the date common control was established.
  4. The balance of the retained earnings of the transferor is aggregated with that of the transferee (or transferred to general reserve, if any).
  5. The identity of the reserves is preserved; they appear in the transferee's financial statements in the same form as in the transferor's.
  6. The difference, if any, between the amount recorded as share capital issued plus any additional consideration in the form of cash or other assets, and the amount of share capital of the transferor, is transferred to capital reserve and presented separately from other capital reserves (para 12).

Whose carrying amounts? ICAI's Ind AS Transition Facilitation Group (ITFG) has clarified that where a subsidiary is merged into its parent, the carrying amounts to be used are those appearing in the consolidated financial statements of the parent (which include goodwill and PPA step-ups from the original acquisition), not the subsidiary's standalone carrying amounts. For fellow-subsidiary mergers, the carrying amounts in the transferor's own statements (or the ultimate parent's CFS, as justified) are used — document the basis.

Illustration

Parent P Ltd (listed) owns 100% of Sub S Ltd. Under an NCLT scheme with appointed date 1 April 2024, S merges into P; the scheme becomes effective on 15 January 2026. P cancels its investment in S (₹50 Cr). S's balance sheet (from P's CFS): net assets ₹180 Cr (incl. goodwill ₹20 Cr); share capital ₹40 Cr; securities premium ₹10 Cr; general reserve ₹30 Cr; retained earnings ₹100 Cr.

Account Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Assets less liabilities of S (incl. goodwill from P's CFS) 180
Capital reserve (debit: 50 − 40) 10
Investment in S cancelled 50
Securities premium of S (identity preserved) 10
General reserve (identity preserved) 30
Retained earnings 100
Total 190 190

The consideration is the cancellation of P's investment (₹50 Cr; no new shares are issued). S's share capital of ₹40 Cr is eliminated; the ₹10 Cr excess of consideration over S's share capital is a debit to capital reserve, presented separately. S's reserves (securities premium, general reserve, retained earnings) are carried over with their identity preserved. Some schemes instead set off the transferor's securities premium against the debit — the scheme must state the treatment and it must be Ind AS-compliant. A debit balance in capital reserve arising from common control combinations is presented within other equity (ITFG has accepted such presentation).

P's FY26 standalone statements present FY25 comparatives restated as if merged from 1 April 2024 (beginning of preceding period), since common control existed throughout.

Fellow subsidiary merger with share issue

X Ltd and Y Ltd are both 100% owned by H Ltd. Y merges into X; X issues 2 crore shares of ₹10 (₹20 Cr) to H. Y's share capital is ₹35 Cr. Capital reserve = \(35 - 20 = 15\) Cr (credit).

NCLT schemes under Sections 230–232

  • Sec 230 — compromise or arrangement with creditors/members; Sec 232 — merger and amalgamation (including demergers); Sec 233 — fast-track merger for small companies, holding–WOS mergers (and, after the 2024 CAA amendment rules, certain cross-border inbound mergers of a foreign holding company into its Indian WOS), approved by the Regional Director rather than NCLT.
  • Process: board approval → valuation report (registered valuer, Sec 247) and fairness opinion (for listed companies, SEBI Master Circular on schemes and stock exchange no-objection under LODR Reg 37) → NCLT application → meetings of shareholders/creditors (may be dispensed) → notices to regulators (RD, ROC, Income-tax, RBI/SEBI as applicable) → sanction order → filing with ROC (effective date).
  • Accounting treatment certificate — Sec 230(7) and 232(3) proviso: no scheme shall be sanctioned unless a certificate by the company's auditor confirms that the accounting treatment proposed is in conformity with the accounting standards under Sec 133.
  • Appointed date vs effective date — MCA General Circular 09/2019 clarified that the appointed date may be a specific calendar date (including a past date) or tied to an event; if more than one year in the past, justification is required. For Ind AS accounting, the key question is when the combination is reflected: for common control, Appendix C restatement from the beginning of the preceding period (or from when common control was established) applies regardless of the appointed date. Where NCLT approval is a substantive condition and is received after the reporting date, ICAI guidance generally treats the scheme as not yet effective at the reporting date (non-adjusting under Ind AS 10), though practice and ITFG clarifications should be checked for the specific facts.

Tax interplay

Amalgamation as defined in Sec 2(1B) is tax-neutral (Sec 47(vi)/(vii)); accumulated losses and unabsorbed depreciation may carry forward under Sec 72A for qualifying companies; demergers under Sec 2(19AA). Pooling carrying amounts usually equal to tax WDV continuity, but book–tax differences carried over bring their DT balances with them.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Promoter control through a group of individuals — needs a contractual arrangement (shareholders' agreement) for common control; informal family influence may not suffice.
  • Combination with partly independent shareholders — NCI in the transferor does not prevent common control; the NCI's shares in the merged entity are consideration issued.
  • Business purchased from a fellow subsidiary for cash at fair value — still pooling under Appendix C; the premium over carrying amounts is a debit to capital reserve, not goodwill.
  • Demerger into a Newco listed for value unlocking — common control if the same shareholders control both before and after; FV accounting is prohibited.
  • Policy harmonisation — e.g., transferor uses WDV depreciation, transferee SLM; harmonise retrospectively within the pooled balances.
  • Ind AS 10 and NCLT timing — document whether sanction is substantive.

Key takeaways

  • Common control = same ultimate controller before and after, not transitory; group of individuals needs a contractual arrangement.
  • Pooling: carrying amounts, no fair values, no new goodwill or intangibles; only policy harmonisation.
  • Comparatives restated from the beginning of the preceding period (or from establishment of common control).
  • Reserves keep their identity; consideration vs transferor share capital → capital reserve shown separately.
  • NCLT schemes need an auditor's certificate that the accounting complies with Sec 133 standards.
  • Appointed date in a scheme does not override Ind AS requirements on timing and restatement.

Practice Questions

Q1. Is it common control? [L1]

For each case, state whether Appendix C applies: (a) two companies 100% owned by the same listed parent merge; (b) a company 60% owned by Promoter A and 40% by public merges into another company 70% owned by Promoter A's brother, with no shareholders' agreement; (c) a Newco formed by a PE fund acquires two unrelated companies and lists within 3 months; (d) a parent buys a division from its 75% subsidiary for cash.

Q2. Pooling entries with share issue [L2]

A Ltd and B Ltd are fellow subsidiaries of H Ltd. B merges into A with appointed date 1 April 2025. A issues 1.5 crore equity shares of ₹10 at a "fair value" of ₹120 to H. B's balance sheet: share capital ₹25 Cr, securities premium ₹15 Cr, retained earnings ₹60 Cr, FVOCI reserve ₹8 Cr; net assets ₹108 Cr. Pass the journal entries in A's books and compute the capital reserve.

💡 Hint: Shares are recorded at nominal value under pooling; fair value is irrelevant.

Q3. Restatement of comparatives [L2]

Common control of X and Y by H was established on 1 October 2024 (H bought Y from a third party then). X and Y merge in FY26 with appointed date 1 April 2025. Determine the date from which X's FY25 comparatives are restated and explain why.

Q4. Scheme review as auditor [L3]

A draft NCLT scheme for merging a 100% subsidiary into a listed parent proposes to record the subsidiary's assets at fair value (₹600 Cr vs carrying amount ₹420 Cr) and to credit the excess to "amalgamation reserve" available for dividends. List the deviations from Ind AS 103 Appendix C and the Companies Act, and state what the auditor's certificate under Sec 232(3) must address.

Q5. Whose carrying amounts? [L2]

P acquired 100% of S three years ago, recognising goodwill of ₹80 Cr and a brand of ₹50 Cr (now ₹35 Cr) in its CFS. S now merges into P. Explain which carrying amounts P uses in its standalone books under pooling, and the effect on P's standalone balance sheet compared with using S's own book values.
