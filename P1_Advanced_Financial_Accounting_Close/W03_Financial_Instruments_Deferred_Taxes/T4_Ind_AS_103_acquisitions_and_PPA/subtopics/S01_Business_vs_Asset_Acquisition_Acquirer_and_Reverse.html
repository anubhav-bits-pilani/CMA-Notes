Subtopic note

Business vs Asset Acquisition, Acquirer and Reverse Acquisitions

Scope: business vs asset acquisition, optional concentration test (B7A), identifying the acquirer, reverse acquisitions

Why it matters

The first question in every deal memo is not "what is the goodwill?" but "is this a business combination at all?". If the acquired set is a business, Ind AS 103 applies: fair value everything, recognise goodwill or capital reserve, record deferred tax on step-ups, expense transaction costs. If it is an asset acquisition, you allocate cost to assets on relative fair values, recognise no goodwill, capitalise transaction costs and (because of the initial recognition exemption in Ind AS 12.15(b) and 12.24) usually record no deferred tax. The two answers can differ by hundreds of crores of goodwill and a materially different P&L profile. Auditors challenge this conclusion first; FP&A teams need it to forecast post-deal amortisation, impairment testing and EPS. The second question — who is the acquirer — matters in share-swap mergers and listings via shell companies, where the legal acquirer may be the accounting acquiree (reverse acquisition).

Core notes

Definition of a business

Ind AS 103 (as amended by MCA with effect from 1 April 2020, aligning with the IFRS 3 2018 amendment) defines a business as an integrated set of activities and assets capable of being conducted and managed to provide goods or services to customers, generate investment income or other income from ordinary activities (Appendix A). The guidance in B7–B12D sets three elements:

  • Inputs — economic resources that create outputs when processes are applied (PPE, intangibles, rights to use, employees, intellectual property).
  • Substantive processes — systems, standards, protocols, conventions or rules that, applied to inputs, create outputs. An organised workforce with the necessary skills is the usual evidence.
  • Outputs — goods/services to customers, investment income, other income. Outputs are not required for a set to be a business, but the bar is higher without them.

To be a business, the set must include, at a minimum, an input and a substantive process that together significantly contribute to the ability to create outputs (B8). The assessment is from a market participant's viewpoint; whether the seller operated it as a business or the buyer intends to is irrelevant (B11).

Substantive process tests (B12A–B12D):

Set has outputs? Process is substantive if…
No outputs (e.g., pre-revenue biotech, a factory under construction) It is critical to developing/converting inputs into outputs and the inputs include an organised workforce with the skills to perform it plus other inputs the workforce can develop into outputs
Has outputs It is critical to continuing to produce outputs and the set includes an organised workforce with the necessary skills; or it significantly contributes to continuing outputs and is unique/scarce or cannot be replaced without significant cost, effort or delay

Acquired contracts (e.g., an outsourced O&M contract) are not substantive processes by themselves, though they may give access to an organised workforce (B12C).

Optional concentration test (B7A–B7B)

The concentration test is a transaction-by-transaction election that gives a quick "not a business" answer. If substantially all of the fair value of the gross assets acquired is concentrated in a single identifiable asset or a group of similar identifiable assets, the set is not a business and no further assessment is needed. If the test fails, you must still perform the full B8–B12D analysis — failing the test never proves it is a business.

Mechanics (B7B):

  • Gross assets exclude cash and cash equivalents, deferred tax assets, and goodwill resulting from the effects of deferred tax liabilities.
  • Fair value of gross assets includes any consideration transferred (plus FV of NCI and previously held interest) in excess of the FV of net identifiable assets — i.e., implicit goodwill is in the denominator.
  • A single identifiable asset includes an asset that would be recognised together as one (e.g., land and building on it; a lease and the leasehold improvements).
  • Assets are not "similar" if they have significantly different risk characteristics — e.g., a tangible and an intangible, or different classes of tangible assets such as inventory and plant.

"Substantially all" is not quantified in the standard; practice uses a threshold around 85–90%, consistent with other Ind AS usage, documented as a policy.

Illustration. A Mumbai REIT sponsor buys an SPV holding one Grade-A office tower with tenant leases for ₹1,250 Cr. Fair values: building and land ₹1,150 Cr, in-place lease intangibles ₹60 Cr, cash ₹40 Cr. Gross assets excluding cash = ₹1,210 Cr. Building + land + in-place leases (recognised together with the property and not separable in practice) = ₹1,210 Cr = 100%. The test is met → asset acquisition. Allocate ₹1,250 Cr less cash ₹40 Cr across the identifiable assets on relative fair values; capitalise stamp duty and legal costs.

Asset acquisition accounting (103.2(b))

  • Identify and recognise individual assets acquired and liabilities assumed.
  • Allocate the cost of the group to them on the basis of relative fair values at the purchase date.
  • No goodwill, no bargain purchase gain.
  • Transaction costs are capitalised (Ind AS 16/38).
  • Deferred tax: if the tax base differs from the allocated cost, the initial recognition exemption (12.15(b)/12.24) usually prevents DT, because the transaction is not a business combination and affects neither accounting nor taxable profit. Note the 2023 amendment (12.22A) only removes the exemption where equal taxable and deductible differences arise — rarely relevant to plain asset purchases.
  • Contingent consideration in asset acquisitions is not explicitly covered; policy choice (recognise when probable vs at fair value) — disclose it.

Identifying the acquirer (103.6–7, B13–B18)

Ind AS 110 control guidance identifies the acquirer first. If it is not clear, the indicators in B14–B18 apply:

  • Cash/asset transfer — the entity transferring cash or incurring liabilities is usually the acquirer (B14).
  • Equity exchange — usually the issuer, but consider: relative voting rights in the combined entity after the deal; existence of a large minority voting interest; composition of the governing body; composition of senior management; terms of the exchange (who pays a premium) (B15).
  • Relative size — the entity whose assets, revenue or profit is significantly larger (B16).
  • With more than two entities — who initiated the combination and relative sizes (B17).
  • A new entity formed to issue shares is not the acquirer; one of the pre-existing entities is identified as acquirer (B18). A Newco paying cash may be the acquirer.

Reverse acquisitions (B19–B27)

A reverse acquisition occurs when the entity issuing securities (the legal acquirer) is identified as the accounting acquiree. Typical Indian setting: an unlisted operating company merges into, or is acquired by, a listed shell via share swap so that its promoters end up controlling the listed entity.

  • Consideration (B20) is deemed: the number of equity interests the legal subsidiary (accounting acquirer) would have had to issue to give the owners of the legal parent the same percentage ownership, times its fair value per share. If the legal parent's shares are more reliably measured (listed), that fair value may be used.
  • Consolidated financial statements are issued in the name of the legal parent but are a continuation of the legal subsidiary's financial statements (B21): its assets/liabilities at pre-combination carrying amounts; the legal parent's identifiable net assets at fair value; retained earnings and comparatives of the legal subsidiary; equity structure (number and type of shares) of the legal parent, with the legal subsidiary's issued equity plus deemed consideration as the equity amount (B22).
  • NCI — owners of the legal subsidiary who do not exchange their shares are NCI measured at their share of the pre-combination carrying amounts (B23–B24).
  • EPS (B25–B27) — pre-acquisition weighted shares = legal subsidiary's weighted shares × exchange ratio.
  • If the legal parent (the shell) is not a business, the transaction is not a business combination; it is a share-based payment (Ind AS 102) for the listing service, with the excess of deemed consideration over net assets expensed.

Worked numbers. Listed L has 10 lakh shares (market value ₹80 each; net assets FV ₹6 Cr, a small trading business). Private P has 6 lakh shares (FV ₹400 each). L issues 15 lakh new shares to P's owners for 100% of P. P's owners hold 15/25 = 60% of L. To give L's owners 40%, P would have issued \(6 \div 0.6 - 6 = 4\) lakh shares; deemed consideration = 4 lakh × ₹400 = ₹16 Cr. Goodwill = ₹16 Cr − ₹6 Cr = ₹10 Cr (if L is a business). Share capital presented is L's 25 lakh shares; equity amount = P's pre-deal issued equity + ₹16 Cr.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Pre-revenue set with key employees — a biotech with an assembled scientific team and in-process R&D is usually a business; a licence without people is not.
  • Concentration test denominator — forgetting to include implicit goodwill makes a set look more concentrated than it is.
  • Real estate with outsourced property management — if the acquirer takes over the management contract but no workforce, the process is often not substantive.
  • Multiple entities under common control — scope out to Appendix C before applying acquirer indicators.
  • Shell with a dormant business — whether the listed shell itself is a business decides between goodwill and a listing expense under Ind AS 102.
  • Tax — in India the business/asset distinction also has tax analogues (slump sale under Sec 50B vs itemised sale); these do not drive the accounting answer.

Key takeaways

  • Decide business vs asset acquisition first; it drives goodwill, DT, transaction costs and future P&L.
  • Minimum for a business: an input and a substantive process that significantly contribute to ability to create outputs.
  • The B7A concentration test is optional and one-directional: pass = asset acquisition; fail = do the full test.
  • Asset acquisitions: relative fair value allocation, no goodwill, costs capitalised, IRE usually blocks DT.
  • Identify the acquirer using Ind AS 110 control, then B14–B18 indicators; Newcos issuing shares are not acquirers.
  • Reverse acquisitions: deemed consideration from the legal subsidiary's shares; CFS continue the legal subsidiary's history.

Practice Questions

Q1. Concentration test on a solar portfolio [L2]

Surya Ltd acquires 100% of an SPV for ₹540 Cr. Fair values: 3 operating solar plants in Rajasthan (similar risk) ₹470 Cr, PPA-related intangible with a state DISCOM ₹35 Cr, trade receivables ₹25 Cr, cash ₹20 Cr, DTA ₹5 Cr; liabilities assumed ₹30 Cr. The SPV has 12 plant technicians and an O&M process. Compute the concentration ratio, conclude whether the test is met using an 85% threshold, and state what further analysis (if any) is required.

💡 Hint: Remember which items are excluded from gross assets and that any excess consideration enters the denominator.

Q2. Who is the acquirer? [L2]

Alpha Ltd (revenue ₹2,400 Cr) and Beta Ltd (revenue ₹1,800 Cr) merge into Newco Ltd, which issues shares: Alpha's holders get 48%, Beta's holders 52%. Beta's promoter family will hold 30% (largest block); Newco's board has 5 Alpha nominees out of 9; the CEO and CFO come from Alpha; Alpha's shareholders received a 15% premium over pre-announcement market value. Identify the acquirer with reasons, citing the indicators.

Q3. Reverse acquisition consideration and equity [L3]

Listed Shell Ltd has 20 lakh shares (quoted ₹50) and net identifiable assets at FV of ₹7 Cr comprising a small but operating distribution business. Private Op Ltd has 8 lakh shares (FV ₹600 per share) and pre-deal issued equity of ₹8 Cr. Shell issues 60 lakh shares for all of Op's shares. Compute the deemed consideration and goodwill, state the number of shares and the equity amount to be presented in the consolidated balance sheet, and explain how EPS for the comparative year is computed.

Q4. Asset acquisition with deferred tax [L2]

Rasayan Ltd pays ₹90 Cr plus ₹1.2 Cr stamp duty for a company whose only asset is a chemical licence (FV ₹88 Cr) and land (FV ₹4 Cr); no workforce or processes transfer. The tax base of the assets in the acquired company is ₹10 Cr. Prepare the initial measurement table and explain whether deferred tax is recognised, citing Ind AS 12.

Q5. Interview: why the amendment matters [L1]

Explain to a deal team why the 2020 amendment to the definition of a business (substantive process tests and concentration test) is likely to move more real-estate and single-asset deals into asset-acquisition accounting, and what that does to reported EPS in the following three years.
