Subtopic note

Step Acquisitions, Measurement Period and Acquisition Costs

Scope: Step acquisitions (previously held interest remeasured, gain in P&L), measurement period ≤12 months (103.45), acquisition costs expensed (103.53)

Why it matters

Indian groups often build control in stages — an investor takes 26% (blocking stake), then an associate stake, then crosses 50% after a regulatory approval or an open offer under the SEBI Takeover Regulations. Crossing the control threshold is treated as if the old stake were sold and repurchased at fair value, producing a P&L gain or loss that can dominate the quarter's results. PPAs also rarely finish by the first reporting date; the measurement-period rules decide whether later valuation changes adjust goodwill or hit P&L. And transaction costs — banker fees, due diligence, stamp duty on share transfer — are expensed, a frequent source of "exceptional item" disclosure and EBITDA adjustment.

Core notes

Step acquisitions (103.41–42)

A business combination achieved in stages occurs when the acquirer held an equity interest immediately before obtaining control. On the acquisition date the acquirer:

  1. Remeasures its previously held equity interest to acquisition-date fair value.
  2. Recognises the resulting gain or loss in P&L or OCI, as appropriate: - previously an associate/JV (equity method) → gain in P&L; amounts in OCI (e.g., share of FCTR, share of FVOCI-debt reserve) reclassified to P&L or transferred within equity on the same basis as if the interest were disposed of directly; - previously an FVTPL investment → gain in P&L (it was already at fair value, so usually small); - previously an FVOCI-equity investment (5.7.5 election) → remeasurement gain in OCI; cumulative OCI not recycled, may be transferred within equity to retained earnings.
  3. Includes the FV of the previously held interest in the goodwill computation (103.32).

Illustration. Dhruv Ltd holds 30% of Akash Ltd (associate; equity-method carrying amount ₹210 Cr including ₹8 Cr share of FCTR in OCI). On 1 December 2025 it buys a further 40% for ₹520 Cr; FV of the 30% stake on that date = ₹360 Cr (control premium excluded). FV of identifiable net assets = ₹1,050 Cr; NCI 30% at proportionate share = ₹315 Cr.

₹ Cr
Gain on remeasurement = 360 − 210 150 (P&L)
Reclassification of FCTR from OCI to P&L 8
Goodwill = 520 + 315 + 360 − 1,050 145

Dhruv's consolidated P&L for Q3 FY26 shows ₹158 Cr of gains — typically presented as an exceptional item — even though no cash was received.

Control premium and FV of the previously held interest

The FV of the old stake is measured under Ind AS 113 as a non-controlling block; it is generally not the per-share price paid for the controlling block. Using the acquisition price per share overstates the remeasurement gain.

Measurement period (103.45–50)

If the initial accounting is incomplete at the end of the reporting period in which the combination occurs, provisional amounts are reported. During the measurement period — which ends when the acquirer receives the information it was seeking, and cannot exceed one year from the acquisition date — the acquirer:

  • retrospectively adjusts provisional amounts to reflect new information about facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition date that, if known, would have affected measurement (103.45);
  • recognises additional assets or liabilities on the same basis;
  • offsets adjustments against goodwill (or capital reserve on a bargain purchase) (103.48);
  • revises comparative information as if the accounting had been completed at the acquisition date, including depreciation, amortisation and DT effects (103.49).

Distinguishing factors (103.47): information obtained shortly after the acquisition date is more likely to reflect acquisition-date circumstances; information about events after that date (e.g., a customer loss in month 7 due to a new competitor) is not a measurement-period adjustment.

After the measurement period, the accounting is revised only to correct an error under Ind AS 8 (103.50).

Illustration. Acquisition on 1 October 2025; FY26 statements show provisional PPE FV ₹400 Cr (useful life 10 years). Valuation finalised in July 2026: PPE FV ₹460 Cr at acquisition date. Adjustment: PPE +₹60 Cr, DTL +₹15.1 Cr (25.168%), goodwill −₹44.9 Cr. FY26 comparatives are revised: extra depreciation for 6 months ₹3 Cr, DTL reversal ₹0.76 Cr. The FY27 statements present the revised FY26 balance sheet and P&L.

Acquisition-related costs (103.53)

Costs the acquirer incurs to effect a business combination are expensed in the periods incurred and services received:

  • finder's fees, investment banker advisory fees;
  • legal, accounting, valuation, due diligence and other professional fees;
  • general administrative costs, including an internal M&A department;
  • stamp duty on transfer of shares acquired (in India, 0.015% on delivery-based transfer of securities under the Indian Stamp Act as amended in 2020; stamp duty on conveyance of assets in an asset deal follows state laws).

Exceptions: costs to issue debt are included in the EIR of the borrowing under Ind AS 109 (→ w3_t1); costs to issue equity are deducted from equity net of tax under Ind AS 32.35–37.

Standalone vs consolidated. In the parent's separate financial statements, the investment in subsidiary is carried at cost under Ind AS 27.10, and directly attributable transaction costs are typically included in the cost of the investment (Ind AS 27 does not define cost; practice and ICAI guidance commonly capitalise). On consolidation, those costs are reversed out of goodwill and expensed. This standalone/CFS difference is a frequent review point.

Journal (consolidated, ₹ Cr):

Account Dr Cr
Acquisition-related costs (P&L — other expenses / exceptional) 6.5
Investment in subsidiary (reverse capitalised cost before goodwill computation) 6.5

Tax notes

  • Acquisition costs on a share purchase are part of the cost of acquisition for capital gains on eventual sale; not deductible as business expenditure → in CFS, the expense has no current tax deduction (permanent in nature for P&L, although an outside-basis difference on the investment may exist; DT under 12.39/12.44 usually not recognised).
  • Remeasurement gains on previously held interests are not taxable until shares are actually sold; in the CFS they increase the outside basis difference.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Control obtained without buying shares (e.g., buy-back by investee, lapse of minority veto rights, contractual agreement) — still a business combination (103.43); consideration may be nil and the previously held interest is remeasured.
  • Partial payment of fees before signing — expense when services are received, even if the deal later fails.
  • Success fees contingent on completion — expensed when completed; accrue when the obligation arises.
  • Measurement-period adjustments to contingent consideration — only if they reflect acquisition-date facts; otherwise 103.58 P&L.
  • Interim reporting — Indian listed acquirers report quarterly under SEBI LODR Reg 33; provisional PPA amounts are common in Q-results and must be flagged.
  • Previously held FVOCI-equity interest — no P&L gain; analysts expecting a gain from news reports will be surprised.

Key takeaways

  • Crossing into control = deemed disposal of the old stake at fair value; gain in P&L (or OCI for FVOCI-equity) plus OCI reclassification for associates.
  • FV of the previously held interest excludes the control premium.
  • Measurement period ≤12 months; adjustments for acquisition-date facts go to goodwill with comparatives revised.
  • Post-measurement-period changes are corrected only as errors under Ind AS 8.
  • Acquisition costs are expensed in CFS; debt and equity issue costs follow Ind AS 109 and Ind AS 32.
  • Standalone investment cost may include transaction costs that CFS expenses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Step acquisition gain [L2]

Mahi Ltd held 25% of Ganga Ltd as an associate with carrying amount ₹140 Cr (including ₹5 Cr share of FVOCI-debt reserve and ₹3 Cr share of actuarial losses in OCI). It acquires a further 35% for ₹280 Cr on 1 August 2025; FV of the 25% stake is ₹190 Cr. FV of Ganga's net identifiable assets is ₹720 Cr; NCI at fair value ₹300 Cr. Compute the P&L gain, the OCI reclassifications/transfers and goodwill.

💡 Hint: Actuarial losses are never recycled; the debt reserve is.

Q2. Measurement period adjustment [L2]

An acquisition on 1 November 2025 recorded provisional customer relationships of ₹80 Cr (life 8 years). In May 2026 the valuer finalises ₹110 Cr based on churn data existing at the acquisition date. In December 2026 a major customer is lost due to a competitor's launch. Explain the accounting for each event and quantify the revision to FY26 comparatives at 25.168% tax.

Q3. Classify the costs [L1]

Classify each cost in the consolidated statements: investment banker fee ₹4 Cr; stamp duty on share transfer ₹0.15 Cr; arranger fee on acquisition term loan ₹2 Cr; merchant banker fee for preferential issue of acquirer shares ₹1.2 Cr; internal M&A team salaries ₹0.9 Cr; post-merger integration consultants ₹3 Cr.

Q4. Standalone vs consolidated reconciliation [L2]

The parent capitalised ₹7.5 Cr of due diligence and legal fees in its standalone investment. Explain the consolidation adjustment, its effect on goodwill and consolidated PBT, and how this would appear in the reconciliation between standalone and consolidated equity.

Q5. Control without a purchase [L3]

An investor holds 45% of a company; the company buys back 12% of shares from other shareholders, taking the investor to 51% with board control. Analyse whether a business combination has occurred, identify the consideration, and describe the measurement of the previously held interest and goodwill.
