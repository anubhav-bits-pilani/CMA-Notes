Subtopic note

PPA Intangibles: Identification and Valuation Methods

Scope: PPA intangibles: customer relationships (MEEM), brands (relief-from-royalty), technology, order backlog, non-competes; contractual-legal or separability criteria

Why it matters

In consumer, tech, pharma and services deals, intangibles are typically 30–70% of the purchase price. Every rupee allocated to a finite-life intangible is amortised through EBIT (hitting reported EPS but not EBITDA), every rupee left in goodwill is tested annually for impairment instead. Lenders and analysts watch "PPA amortisation" as a separate adjusting item; FP&A teams must forecast it for the next 5–15 years. Valuations are prepared by registered valuers but owned by management, so a CMA in a finance team must understand the methods well enough to challenge royalty rates, attrition curves and contributory asset charges.

Core notes

Identifiability criteria (Ind AS 38.12; 103.B31–B34)

An intangible is recognised separately from goodwill if it is identifiable, meaning it meets either:

  • Contractual-legal criterion — arises from contractual or other legal rights, regardless of whether transferable or separable (e.g., a licence, a supply contract, a trademark registration, a non-compete agreement); or
  • Separability criterion — capable of being separated and sold, transferred, licensed, rented or exchanged, individually or with a related contract, asset or liability, even if the acquirer does not intend to do so (e.g., customer lists, unpatented technology, databases).

Probability of inflows and reliable measurement are assumed satisfied for intangibles acquired in a business combination (Ind AS 38.33). Assembled workforce is not identifiable and is subsumed in goodwill (B37), but it is still valued as a contributory asset in MEEM.

Categories and typical examples

Category Examples Criterion usually met
Marketing-related Brands, trademarks, domain names, non-compete agreements Contractual-legal
Customer-related Customer contracts and relationships, order backlog, customer lists, non-contractual relationships Contractual (contracts, backlog); separability (lists)
Contract-based Licences, franchises, favourable supply contracts, broadcast rights, mining rights Contractual-legal
Technology-based Patented and unpatented technology, software, process know-how, IPR&D Contractual-legal (patents) or separability
Artistic Film libraries, music, books Contractual-legal

Multi-period excess earnings method (MEEM) — customer relationships

MEEM isolates the cash flows of the primary intangible (usually customer relationships in services/distribution, technology in software) by deducting a return on every other asset needed to generate those cash flows:

  1. Forecast revenue from existing customers only, applying an attrition rate (e.g., 10% p.a. derived from historical churn).
  2. Deduct operating costs (excluding costs of acquiring new customers) to get EBITDA, then tax at the expected rate.
  3. Deduct contributory asset charges (CACs) — a return on (and where relevant of) working capital, fixed assets, assembled workforce, brand and technology, each at its required rate of return.
  4. Discount the excess earnings at a rate above WACC reflecting intangible-specific risk (typically WACC + 1–3%).
  5. Add the tax amortisation benefit (TAB) only if the intangible's cost is tax-amortisable for a market participant.

TAB in India: customer relationships and goodwill are not depreciable for tax (goodwill excluded from Sec 32 from AY 2021-22). Specified intangibles (know-how, patents, copyrights, trademarks, licences, franchises "or any other business or commercial rights of similar nature") are depreciable at 25% WDV under Sec 32(1)(ii) when acquired as such — typically in an asset purchase. In a share acquisition, there is no new tax base, so no TAB; a DTL arises on the full fair value.

Mini illustration (₹ Cr). Existing-customer revenue Year 1 = 100, attrition 15%, EBITDA margin 22%, tax 25.168%, CAC 6% of revenue, discount rate 15%.

Year Revenue EBITDA Tax After-tax CAC Excess earnings PV factor PV
1 100.0 22.0 (5.5) 16.5 (6.0) 10.5 0.870 9.1
2 85.0 18.7 (4.7) 14.0 (5.1) 8.9 0.756 6.7
3 72.3 15.9 (4.0) 11.9 (4.3) 7.6 0.658 5.0

Continue until the cohort runs off (often 8–12 years); the sum is the fair value before TAB.

Relief-from-royalty (RFR) — brands, technology

The intangible's value equals the PV of after-tax royalties the owner avoids paying because it owns, rather than licenses, the asset:

\[ \text{FV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{Revenue}_t \times \text{Royalty rate} \times (1 - T)}{(1 + k)^{t}} + \text{TV} + \text{TAB} \]

where \(T\) is the tax rate, \(k\) the discount rate and TV the terminal value for indefinite-life brands. Royalty rates come from comparable licensing agreements (commercial royalty databases, transfer-pricing benchmarking studies, disclosed related-party brand royalties of listed Indian subsidiaries; in India FMCG brand royalties of 1–5% of sales are common). A cross-check is the profit split: the royalty should leave the licensee a reasonable share of operating profit (the "25% rule" is a sanity check only).

Illustration. Brand revenue ₹200 Cr growing 6%, royalty 3%, tax 25.168%, discount 14%, indefinite life. After-tax royalty year 1 = \(200 \times 0.03 \times 0.748 = 4.49\) Cr. Gordon value = \(4.49 \div (0.14 - 0.06) = 56.1\) Cr (no TAB in a share deal).

Technology

Use RFR when licensing comparables exist (software, process know-how), or MEEM if technology is the primary asset. Key judgement: obsolescence curve — revenue attributable to existing technology declines as new versions replace it. Cost approach (reproduction/replacement cost of developer effort plus developer's profit and opportunity cost) for internal-use software.

Order backlog

Contracted but unfulfilled orders at the acquisition date. Valued using a short-horizon MEEM: margin on backlog revenue after CACs, discounted over the delivery period (often 6–24 months). Separate from the ongoing customer relationship — avoid double counting by excluding backlog revenue from the relationship's MEEM. Amortised as the backlog is delivered, so it depresses margins in the first post-deal year.

Non-compete agreements

Valued by the with-and-without method: PV of cash flows with the non-compete minus PV without it (sellers competing), weighted by the probability that the sellers would actually compete and their ability to do so. Amortised over the non-compete term. Payments to sellers for a non-compete may be separate consideration (taxable to the seller under Sec 28(va)) — but the PPA still values the intangible.

Useful lives and subsequent accounting

Finite-life intangibles are amortised over the pattern of benefits (customer relationships often 5–12 years, technology 3–7, backlog over delivery, non-compete over its term); brands may be indefinite-life if no foreseeable limit (Ind AS 38.88), tested annually for impairment. PPA amortisation and the related DTL reversal create a predictable P&L and ETR pattern that FP&A must model.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Customer relationships with no contracts — still identifiable via separability if similar relationships are exchanged (e.g., customer lists sold in the market), B31 and Illustrative Examples.
  • Double counting — the same cash flow should not support both brand (RFR) and customer relationships (MEEM); the CAC for the brand in MEEM equals the RFR royalty.
  • Circularity — the CAC for assembled workforce uses its own value; build the model iteratively.
  • Discount-rate reconciliation (WARA) — the weighted average return on assets (including goodwill) should approximate the deal IRR/WACC; large gaps indicate mis-specified rates.
  • Indian regulatory overlay — valuations under the Companies Act for certain purposes must be by a registered valuer (Sec 247); ICAI Valuation Standards 2018 guide method choice.
  • TAB in a slump sale — under Sec 50B, the buyer's tax allocation of lump-sum price to intangibles is contested; take advice before including TAB.

Key takeaways

  • Intangibles are recognised if contractual-legal or separable; assembled workforce is never separately recognised.
  • MEEM values the primary intangible after contributory asset charges; RFR values brands and technology from avoided royalties.
  • Order backlog and non-competes are short-lived intangibles with distinct methods.
  • TAB is added only where a market participant would get tax amortisation — usually not in Indian share deals.
  • Step-ups create DTL in share deals; that DTL increases goodwill.
  • Reconcile intangible discount rates to WACC and deal IRR (WARA test).

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify the intangibles [L1]

A Bengaluru acquirer buys a B2B logistics company with: a registered brand; 3-year contracts with 40 corporate clients; a proprietary route-optimisation algorithm (unpatented); 1,200 trained drivers; a ₹45 Cr order book for the next 9 months; and a 3-year non-compete from the founders. Classify each item as a separately recognised intangible or part of goodwill, citing the criterion met.

Q2. Relief-from-royalty valuation [L2]

A skincare brand generates ₹150 Cr revenue in FY27, growing 10% for 4 years then 5% in perpetuity. Royalty rate 4%, tax 25.168%, discount rate 15%, no TAB, cash flows at year-end. Value the brand and state how the value would change if the deal were an asset purchase where the brand is depreciable at 25% WDV.

💡 Hint: Build explicit years 1–4, then a Gordon terminal value at the end of year 4.

Q3. MEEM contributory asset charges [L3]

Draft the CAC schedule for a MEEM valuation of customer relationships: working capital ₹30 Cr (return 9%), fixed assets ₹80 Cr (return on 11% plus return of via depreciation ₹10 Cr p.a.), assembled workforce ₹12 Cr (return 14%), brand (royalty 2% of revenue). Revenue is ₹250 Cr. Compute the total CAC in year 1 and explain why the brand charge is computed as a royalty rather than a return on value.

Q4. Backlog vs relationship [L2]

Explain how you would prevent double counting between the order backlog and the customer relationship valuation, and the P&L effect in the first post-acquisition year of a ₹12 Cr backlog intangible delivered over 9 months starting 1 January (FY ends 31 March).

Q5. Interview: PPA challenge [L3]

A valuer assigns 85% of the excess purchase price to goodwill and only 15% to intangibles for a D2C brand acquisition, with no value for customer relationships. As group financial controller, list the questions you would raise and the evidence you would ask for before signing off the PPA.
