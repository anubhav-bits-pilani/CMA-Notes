Subtopic note

Consideration Transferred: Cash, Shares, Deferred and Contingent

Scope: Consideration: cash, shares at fair value, deferred and contingent consideration (liability at FVTPL under Ind AS 109)

Why it matters

Consideration transferred is the first line of the goodwill equation, and in Indian deals it is rarely a single cash cheque. Promoter-led acquisitions commonly mix upfront cash, a share swap, holdbacks paid after 12–24 months, and earn-outs tied to EBITDA or customer retention. Each component has its own measurement date, discounting and post-deal accounting. Getting it wrong misstates goodwill on day one and creates P&L volatility later: an earn-out liability remeasured through P&L can swing reported profit by more than the target's own earnings. FP&A teams must model these liabilities and their unwinding in the post-deal forecast and covenant tests.

Core notes

General measurement rule (103.37–38)

Consideration transferred is measured at acquisition-date fair value, being the sum of:

  • assets transferred by the acquirer (cash, non-cash assets, a business or subsidiary of the acquirer);
  • liabilities incurred by the acquirer to former owners of the acquiree; and
  • equity interests issued by the acquirer.

If the acquirer transfers non-cash assets whose carrying amount differs from fair value, it remeasures them to fair value and recognises the gain or loss in P&L — unless the assets remain within the combined entity after the deal (e.g., transferred to the acquiree itself), in which case they stay at carrying amount (103.38).

Cash

Straightforward, but watch for: amounts paid into escrow that are still consideration (they are paid to former owners); amounts paid to settle the acquiree's debt (part of consideration only if it is the seller's obligation being settled on their behalf — otherwise it is assumption of a liability); and working-capital adjustments that true-up the price (part of consideration, often measured as a contingent element until finalised).

Shares issued at fair value

Equity instruments are measured at fair value on the acquisition date (the date control is obtained, 103.8), not the agreement or announcement date. For NSE/BSE-listed acquirers this is the closing quoted price on the acquisition date × shares issued. Consequences:

  • A rise in share price between announcement and completion increases consideration and goodwill.
  • Costs of issuing the shares (merchant banker, stamp duty on issue, SEBI/exchange fees) are not consideration and not expensed — they are deducted from equity under Ind AS 32.37.
  • If the acquiree's equity has a more reliably measurable fair value than the acquirer's (e.g., unlisted acquirer, listed target), goodwill is measured using the acquiree's fair value instead (103.33).

Deferred consideration

A fixed amount payable later is a financial liability measured at present value using a market rate for the acquirer's credit risk at the acquisition date. It is then carried at amortised cost under Ind AS 109, with the unwind charged to finance cost — not to goodwill.

\[ \text{PV} = \frac{\text{Deferred amount}}{(1 + r)^{n}} \]

where \(r\) is the acquirer's incremental borrowing rate for similar tenor and \(n\) the years to payment.

Contingent consideration (103.39–40, 103.58)

Contingent consideration is an obligation to transfer additional assets or equity if specified future events occur (earn-outs, milestone payments), or a right to recover consideration (clawbacks, recognised as an asset).

Initial recognition: at acquisition-date fair value, which already embeds the probability of payment — there is no "probable" threshold. Typical techniques: probability-weighted scenarios discounted at a risk-adjusted rate, or Monte Carlo / option-pricing models for non-linear earn-outs.

Classification (103.40): as a financial liability or equity using Ind AS 32.11 — a fixed number of shares for a fixed amount is equity; a variable number of shares worth a fixed amount, or cash, is a liability.

Subsequent measurement (103.58):

Cause of change Treatment
Additional information about facts and circumstances existing at acquisition date, within the measurement period Measurement-period adjustment — adjust goodwill retrospectively (103.45)
Events after acquisition date (target met, share price moves, milestone reached) — equity-classified Not remeasured; settlement accounted within equity
Same — liability-classified (financial instrument within Ind AS 109) Remeasured to fair value at each reporting date, changes in P&L (it is effectively FVTPL)

Because the earn-out liability is FVTPL, its valuation is usually Level 3 under Ind AS 113 (unobservable forecast inputs), requiring the Level 3 roll-forward and sensitivity disclosures (→ w3_t6).

Payments that are not consideration

Ind AS 103.51–52 and B50–B62 require separating transactions that are not part of the exchange:

  • Payments to selling shareholders who stay as employees — if contingent payments are automatically forfeited when employment terminates, they are remuneration for post-combination services (B55(a)), expensed over the service period. Other indicators: duration of required employment vs earn-out period, level of remuneration, incremental payments to employee-shareholders vs non-employee-shareholders.
  • Settlement of pre-existing relationships (B51–B53) — e.g., a supply contract between acquirer and acquiree at off-market rates; the off-market element is a settlement gain/loss in P&L.
  • Replacement share-based payment awards (B56–B62) — split into pre-combination service (consideration) and post-combination service (expense).
  • Acquisition-related costs — expensed (103.53; → s07).

Illustration

Kaveri Foods Ltd acquires 100% of Masala Co on 1 October 2025:

Component Terms Acquisition-date FV (₹ Cr)
Cash Paid on completion 200.00
Shares 10 lakh Kaveri shares; NSE close on 1 Oct ₹520 (announcement-date price ₹480) 52.00
Deferred consideration ₹30 Cr payable 1 Oct 2027; Kaveri's 2-year borrowing rate 10% 24.79
Earn-out ₹40 Cr if FY27 EBITDA ≥ ₹60 Cr; 45% probability; risk-adjusted rate 14%, ~1.5 years 15.40 (approx.)
Total consideration 292.19

Earn-out FV ≈ \(40 \times 0.45 \div 1.14^{1.5} \approx 14.8\); a scenario model with partial payouts gives ₹15.4 Cr — use the model. Deferred PV = \(30 \div 1.10^{2} = 24.79\).

Entries at 31 March 2026 (6 months later):

Entry Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Finance cost (deferred consideration unwind ≈ 24.79 × (1.10^0.5 − 1)) 1.21
Deferred consideration liability 1.21
Fair value loss on contingent consideration (P&L) — revised FV ₹19.0 Cr after strong trading 3.60
Contingent consideration liability 3.60

Goodwill is not touched by the post-acquisition earn-out revision, because it results from post-acquisition performance.

Tax and presentation

  • Indian tax: the cost of acquisition of shares for capital gains (when the acquirer later sells) includes the contingent amount only when paid/crystallised; the accounting liability therefore has a tax base of nil → temporary difference, but in a share deal it relates to investment cost; DT is usually considered under 12.39/12.44 (investments in subsidiaries) in standalone books.
  • Present the earn-out within other financial liabilities (current/non-current by expected settlement date); disclose the arrangement, basis for determining payment, and range of outcomes (103 B64(g)).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Earn-out payable in a variable number of shares — liability (fails fixed-for-fixed), so P&L remeasurement applies even though settlement is in equity.
  • Measurement-period vs post-acquisition change — an updated valuation in month 4 using information that existed at the acquisition date adjusts goodwill; a revision because the target beat its budget is P&L. Document which.
  • Holdbacks for warranty claims — if released to the seller absent claims, they are consideration; the indemnity side may give an indemnification asset (→ s03).
  • Share price between signing and completion — large movements can make goodwill materially different from the deal model; FP&A should flag this before completion.
  • Earn-out tied to the seller's continued employment — almost always compensation, not consideration; this is a frequent audit finding.

Key takeaways

  • All consideration is at acquisition-date fair value; shares use the completion-date price.
  • Deferred consideration is discounted; its unwind is finance cost.
  • Contingent consideration is recognised at fair value on day one with no probability threshold.
  • Liability-classified contingent consideration is FVTPL under Ind AS 109; equity-classified is never remeasured (103.58).
  • Only measurement-period information about acquisition-date facts adjusts goodwill.
  • Strip out employee remuneration, pre-existing relationship settlements and acquisition costs from consideration.

Practice Questions

Q1. Measure total consideration [L2]

Nirmal Pharma acquires 100% of Vayu Labs on 1 January 2026. It pays ₹150 Cr cash, issues 5 lakh shares (quoted ₹1,200 on 1 January; ₹1,050 on signing date 1 November 2025), agrees to pay ₹50 Cr on 1 January 2028 (Nirmal's borrowing rate 9.5%) and an earn-out of ₹60 Cr if a drug approval is obtained by December 2027 (probability 40%, discount rate 15%, expected payment in 2 years). Share-issue costs are ₹0.8 Cr. Compute consideration transferred and state the treatment of the issue costs.

Q2. Earn-out remeasurement journals [L2]

Continuing Q1, at 31 March 2026 the probability of approval rises to 70% on positive Phase III data released in February 2026, and the deferred consideration has accrued one quarter of interest. Pass the journal entries for 31 March 2026 and explain why goodwill is not adjusted.

💡 Hint: Ask whether the Phase III data is information about facts that existed on 1 January 2026.

Q3. Consideration or compensation? [L3]

The two founders of a SaaS target (each holding 35%) will receive an extra ₹25 Cr each over three years if ARR targets are met and they remain employed; the 30% financial investor receives nothing contingent. Founders' current salary is ₹40 lakh, below market of ₹1.2 Cr. Analyse the arrangement under B54–B55 and state the accounting in the acquirer's consolidated statements.

Q4. Equity vs liability earn-out [L2]

An earn-out pays the sellers either (a) a fixed 2 lakh acquirer shares if FY27 PAT exceeds ₹80 Cr, or (b) shares worth ₹30 Cr at the then market price. Classify each under Ind AS 32 and describe the subsequent accounting for each under 103.58.

Q5. Model build: earn-out schedule [L3]

Build an Excel schedule for a three-scenario earn-out (low ₹0, base ₹20 Cr, high ₹45 Cr; probabilities 30/50/20; payment in 24 months; discount rate 13%) that computes acquisition-date FV, quarterly remeasurement with updated probabilities, and splits each quarter's movement into unwind and fair value change for disclosure. Specify the inputs, formulas and checks.
