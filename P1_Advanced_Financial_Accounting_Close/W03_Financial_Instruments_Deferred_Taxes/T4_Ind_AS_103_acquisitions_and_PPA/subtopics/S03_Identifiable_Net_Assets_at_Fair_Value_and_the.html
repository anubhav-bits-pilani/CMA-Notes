Subtopic note

Identifiable Net Assets at Fair Value and the Measurement Exceptions

Scope: Identifiable assets/liabilities at fair value; exceptions — deferred tax, employee benefits, indemnification assets, leases

Why it matters

Goodwill is a residual: whatever is not allocated to identifiable net assets falls into it. The quality of a PPA therefore depends on how rigorously the acquirer identifies and fair-values every asset and liability, including those the acquiree never recognised (brands, customer relationships, contingent liabilities). Ind AS 103 then carves out specific items that are not measured at fair value, because another standard's measurement basis is needed to keep post-deal accounting coherent. Auditors test the completeness of identification and the application of these exceptions; FP&A needs the resulting step-ups because they drive post-deal depreciation, amortisation, inventory unwind in gross margin, and deferred tax.

Core notes

Recognition principle (103.10–14)

At the acquisition date the acquirer recognises, separately from goodwill, the identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any NCI. To qualify:

  • each item must meet the definitions of asset and liability in the Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting under Ind AS at the acquisition date (103.11) — so expected future restructuring costs the acquirer intends but is not obliged to incur are not liabilities;
  • it must be part of what was exchanged, not a separate transaction (103.12, 103.51).

Items the acquiree never recognised can and should be recognised: internally generated brands, customer lists, patents, in-process R&D (103.13).

Classification at acquisition date (103.15–17)

Financial assets are classified (amortised cost/FVOCI/FVTPL), hedges designated and embedded derivatives assessed on conditions at the acquisition date — the acquirer does not inherit the acquiree's choices. Exceptions: lease classification where the acquiree is lessor and insurance contract classification follow terms at inception (103.17).

Measurement principle (103.18) and practical fair values

All identifiable assets and liabilities are measured at acquisition-date fair value (Ind AS 113 exit price):

Item Typical fair value approach
Land and buildings Market approach (comparable sales, circle rates as cross-check); registered valuer report
Plant and machinery Cost approach — depreciated replacement cost, adjusted for functional and economic obsolescence
Finished goods inventory Selling price less costs to dispose and a reasonable profit for the selling effort
Work-in-progress Selling price of finished goods less costs to complete, dispose and profit on remaining effort
Raw materials Current replacement cost
Receivables Present value of amounts expected to be received; no separate ECL allowance carried over (B41)
Borrowings Present value at current market rates for the acquiree's credit risk
Contingent liabilities Recognised if a present obligation from past events and FV is reliably measurable, even if outflow is not probable (103.23) — an explicit departure from Ind AS 37

The exceptions

Exception Para Rule Why
Contingent liabilities 103.22–23 Recognised if present obligation + reliable FV; possible obligations not recognised Ind AS 37 probability threshold overridden
Income taxes 103.24–25 DT measured under Ind AS 12 (undiscounted, enacted rates), not fair value Ind AS 12 prohibits discounting; FV of a tax asset is not the model
Employee benefits 103.26 Measured under Ind AS 19 (DBO less plan assets) Actuarial measurement keeps post-deal accounting consistent
Indemnification assets 103.27–28 Recognised and measured on the same basis as the indemnified item, subject to a collectability allowance Mirrors the liability it protects
Leases where acquiree is lessee 103.28A–28B Lease liability = PV of remaining lease payments as if a new lease at acquisition date; ROU = lease liability adjusted for favourable/unfavourable terms vs market Avoids separate off-market intangibles; aligns with Ind AS 116
Reacquired rights 103.29 Valued on remaining contractual term only (no renewals)
Share-based payment awards 103.30 Ind AS 102 market-based measure
Assets held for sale 103.31 FV less costs to sell (Ind AS 105)

Deferred tax exception in detail

The acquirer recognises DT on temporary differences between the new carrying amounts (fair values) and the tax bases, plus the acquiree's tax losses and credits that become recognisable. In a share acquisition in India, the tax base of the target's assets does not change — so every step-up creates a DTL at the rate expected on reversal (25.168% for a 115BAA company, 34.944% for an old-regime company with income above ₹10 Cr). The acquirer may also recognise DTAs on the target's losses that the combined group's prospects now make probable (12.66–67); if the acquirer's own pre-existing DTA becomes recognisable because of the deal, that is recognised in the acquirer's P&L, not in goodwill (12.67).

Employee benefits exception

Gratuity and leave encashment obligations are measured by an actuary under Ind AS 19 at the acquisition date using current discount rates and the acquirer's assumptions. Plan amendments or curtailments the acquirer decides post-deal are post-combination events.

Indemnification assets

Sellers frequently indemnify against specific tax demands or litigation (e.g., a pending GST show-cause notice). If the related liability is recognised at fair value, the indemnification asset is also at fair value; if the liability falls under an exception (e.g., an uncertain income-tax position measured under Ind AS 12 Appendix C), the asset follows that measurement, capped by collectability. Subsequently, the asset is remeasured on the same basis as the liability, again capped (103.57).

Leases (acquiree as lessee)

Illustration. Target leases a Pune warehouse with 6 years remaining at ₹1.2 Cr per year payable in arrears; market rent is ₹1.5 Cr; acquirer's incremental borrowing rate 9%.

\[ \text{Lease liability} = 1.2 \times \frac{1 - 1.09^{-6}}{0.09} = 1.2 \times 4.486 = 5.38 \text{ Cr} \]

Favourable terms = \((1.5 - 1.2) \times 4.486 = 1.35\) Cr. ROU asset = ₹5.38 Cr + ₹1.35 Cr = ₹6.73 Cr. No separate favourable-lease intangible is recognised. The DT effect: DTA on lease liability ₹5.38 Cr and DTL on ROU ₹6.73 Cr (the initial recognition exemption in Ind AS 12.15/12.24 never applies to a business combination, so both are recognised: DTA ₹1.35 Cr and DTL ₹1.69 Cr at 25.168%, assuming nil tax bases).

Short-term leases (≤12 months remaining) and low-value leases may be left unrecognised (103.28B). Where the acquiree is a lessor under an operating lease, no separate asset/liability for off-market terms is recognised; the terms are reflected in the fair value of the underlying asset (B42).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Restructuring provisions — only if the acquiree had a present obligation at the acquisition date; a plan conditional on the deal is post-combination.
  • Contingent liabilities subsequently — measured at the higher of the amount under Ind AS 37 and the initial amount less cumulative Ind AS 115 income (103.56) until settled or expired.
  • Contingent assets — never recognised in a business combination.
  • Acquiree's existing goodwill and DT — derecognised; new amounts computed.
  • Assets the acquirer does not intend to use (defensive brands) — still recognised at market-participant fair value (B43).
  • Ind AS 19 exception and day-1 remeasurements — no remeasurement gain in OCI on acquisition; the opening DBO is just the Ind AS 19 number.

Key takeaways

  • Recognise everything that meets the Framework definitions at the acquisition date, including unrecognised intangibles and contingent liabilities with reliable FV.
  • Classification and designation are made afresh at the acquisition date, except lessor lease classification and insurance contracts.
  • DT (Ind AS 12) and employee benefits (Ind AS 19) are measured under their own standards, not fair value.
  • Indemnification assets mirror the indemnified item, subject to collectability.
  • Lessee leases use the new-lease measurement; off-market terms adjust the ROU asset.
  • In share deals, step-ups almost always create DTL that increases goodwill.

Practice Questions

Q1. Fair value of inventory [L1]

Target holds finished goods with book cost ₹12 Cr and expected selling price ₹18 Cr; costs to sell are 5% of selling price; a normal profit for the selling effort is 8% of selling price. Compute the acquisition-date fair value and explain the effect on post-acquisition gross margin when the inventory is sold.

Q2. Contingent liability and indemnity [L2]

On acquisition, the target faces a GST demand of ₹20 Cr; legal advice puts the outflow probability at 30%; the probability-weighted fair value is ₹6.5 Cr. The seller has indemnified the acquirer for 100% of any payment, but only ₹4 Cr of the seller's escrow is available. Pass the PPA entries for the contingent liability and the indemnification asset, and explain subsequent measurement if the demand is settled for ₹15 Cr.

💡 Hint: The indemnification asset is capped by collectability.

Q3. Lease acquired in a business combination [L2]

The acquiree leases a retail store: 4 years remaining, rent ₹80 lakh per year in arrears, market rent ₹65 lakh, IBR 10%. Compute the lease liability and ROU asset under 103.28A–28B and the DT on each at 25.168% assuming tax base nil.

Q4. Restructuring and post-deal plans [L3]

The acquirer plans to close two of the target's depots (estimated cost ₹9 Cr) and announces this to staff three weeks after completion. The target's board had approved a separate ₹3 Cr VRS scheme and communicated it to employees one month before the deal closed. Determine which amounts are recognised in the PPA and justify with reference to 103.11.

Q5. Gratuity in the PPA [L2]

The target's books show a gratuity provision of ₹6 Cr computed on the Payment of Gratuity Act formula; the acquirer's actuary values the DBO at ₹8.4 Cr with plan assets at ₹2.1 Cr. State the amount recognised in the PPA, the governing paragraph, and the DT consequence given that gratuity is deductible only on payment (Sec 43B).
