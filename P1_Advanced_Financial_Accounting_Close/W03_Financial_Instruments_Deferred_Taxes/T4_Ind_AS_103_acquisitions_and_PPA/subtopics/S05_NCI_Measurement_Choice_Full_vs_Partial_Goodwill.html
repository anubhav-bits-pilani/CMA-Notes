Subtopic note

NCI Measurement Choice: Full vs Partial Goodwill

Scope: NCI choice per transaction: fair value (full goodwill) vs proportionate share of net assets (103.19)

Why it matters

Most Indian acquisitions of less than 100% (promoter retains 20–49%, or a PE investor stays in) require an NCI measurement choice at the acquisition date. The choice changes the amount of goodwill, total equity, subsequent impairment losses, and the accounting when the acquirer later buys out the minority. Because the choice is per transaction, two acquisitions in the same group can be measured differently — analysts comparing goodwill across groups must know which basis was used. In consolidation workbooks, the choice sets the opening NCI balance that every subsequent NCI roll-forward builds on (→ w3_t5).

Core notes

The rule (103.19)

For each business combination, the acquirer measures components of NCI that are present ownership interests entitling holders to a proportionate share of the entity's net assets in the event of liquidation at either:

  • Fair value at the acquisition date (the "full goodwill" method); or
  • the present ownership instruments' proportionate share of the recognised amounts of the acquiree's identifiable net assets (the "partial" or "proportionate" method).

All other components of NCI (e.g., CCPS with preference on liquidation, equity components of convertible instruments, vested share options of the acquiree) are measured at acquisition-date fair value, unless another Ind AS requires a different basis (e.g., Ind AS 102 market-based measure for share-based payments).

The choice is made transaction by transaction — not an accounting policy applied consistently across deals — and must be disclosed (103.B64(o)).

Fair value of NCI (B44–B45)

Fair value of NCI may be measured from quoted prices of the shares not held by the acquirer (if listed), or by other valuation techniques. The per-share fair value of NCI can be lower than the acquirer's per-share price because the acquirer's price includes a control premium; equivalently, NCI fair value may reflect a discount for lack of control. Do not simply gross up the acquirer's price.

Effect on goodwill

\[ \text{Goodwill} = \text{Consideration} + \text{NCI} + \text{FV of previously held interest} - \text{FV of identifiable net assets} \]

Under the proportionate method, NCI = NCI% × identifiable net assets, so goodwill represents only the parent's share. Under fair value, goodwill includes the NCI's share as well.

Illustration

Pragati Ltd acquires 75% of Tarang Ltd on 1 July 2025 for ₹900 Cr cash. Identifiable net assets at FV (after PPA DTL) = ₹1,000 Cr. Tarang is unlisted; a valuer estimates the FV of the 25% NCI at ₹270 Cr (₹10.8 per share vs Pragati's ₹12.0 price per share, reflecting a 10% control premium).

Subsequent consequences

1. Impairment (Ind AS 36 Appendix C). When NCI is measured at the proportionate share, the recognised goodwill excludes the NCI's share. For impairment testing of the CGU, goodwill is notionally grossed up to include the NCI portion (C4), the CGU carrying amount compared with recoverable amount, and only the parent's share of any goodwill impairment is recognised. Under the fair value method, goodwill is already full; impairment losses are allocated between parent and NCI on the same basis as profit or loss (C6), unless goodwill was not allocated proportionately.

Example: Proportionate method goodwill ₹150 Cr, parent 75%. Grossed-up goodwill = \(150 \div 0.75 = 200\). If the CGU impairment is ₹60 Cr (all against goodwill), recognised impairment = \(60 \times 0.75 = 45\) Cr.

2. Later purchase of NCI (Ind AS 110.23). A buy-out after control is an equity transaction; the difference between consideration and the NCI carrying amount goes to equity attributable to owners. The fair value method carries a higher NCI, so the equity debit on a later buy-out at a given price is smaller. Goodwill never changes on such transactions.

3. Profit and loss attribution. NCI shares in post-acquisition profit, OCI, PPA amortisation and depreciation on step-ups (the NCI is measured on the consolidated carrying amounts, including fair value adjustments), under either method.

4. Loss of control. Under either method, the whole goodwill and NCI are derecognised; the gain differs only in the composition of carrying amounts derecognised.

Choosing between the methods — practitioner view

Factor Points to proportionate Points to fair value
Valuation effort No NCI valuation needed Requires separate Level 3 valuation if unlisted
Plan to buy out NCI soon — Higher NCI → smaller equity debit on buy-out
Impairment risk Lower recognised goodwill; smaller absolute impairment Larger goodwill, more exposure to impairment
Comparability with peers / US GAAP — US GAAP (ASC 805) requires fair value NCI
Ratios Lower total equity and assets → higher ROE/ROCE Higher equity and goodwill

Indian listed groups predominantly elect the proportionate method; global groups reporting under US GAAP or those with near-term buy-out options often use fair value.

Put options over NCI

Where the parent writes a put to the NCI shareholders (common in Indian JVs and PE exits), Ind AS 32.23 requires a financial liability at the PV of the redemption amount, regardless of NCI measurement. Groups then choose whether to continue recognising NCI (and debit "other equity" for the liability) or to derecognise NCI (anticipated-acquisition method); the policy must be applied consistently and disclosed.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • NCI holds preference shares — redeemable preference shares classified as liabilities are not NCI at all; equity-classified CCPS with liquidation preference are "other components" at fair value.
  • Negative goodwill under one method, positive under the other — possible where NCI fair value exceeds its proportionate share; the bargain purchase test uses the elected NCI amount.
  • Listed acquiree — NCI fair value from the quoted price is Level 1, but the per-share price of the acquirer's controlling block is not a valid proxy.
  • Indirect NCI in multi-tier groups — computed on effective interests; the choice is applied at the level where the combination occurs.
  • Disclosure — the measurement basis, the amount of NCI and, for fair value, the valuation techniques and key inputs (103.B64(o)).

Key takeaways

  • The NCI choice is per transaction and applies only to present ownership interests; other NCI components are at fair value.
  • Fair value NCI gives "full goodwill"; proportionate NCI gives parent-only goodwill.
  • NCI fair value may be below the acquirer's per-share price because of the control premium.
  • For impairment, proportionate-method goodwill is grossed up for testing, but only the parent's share is recognised.
  • Later NCI purchases go through equity — goodwill is fixed at the acquisition date.
  • Disclose the basis used and the inputs for fair value NCI.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute goodwill both ways [L1]

Sagar Ltd acquires 60% of Nadi Ltd for ₹480 Cr. FV of identifiable net assets is ₹650 Cr. Nadi's shares are listed; the market price implies ₹300 Cr for the 40% NCI. Compute goodwill and NCI under both methods and the goodwill attributable to NCI under the fair value method.

Q2. Impairment under the proportionate method [L2]

Goodwill recognised (proportionate NCI, 70% parent) is ₹210 Cr. The CGU's carrying amount excluding goodwill is ₹1,100 Cr and its recoverable amount is ₹1,250 Cr. Compute the notional grossed-up goodwill, the impairment loss for the CGU, and the amount recognised in the consolidated P&L, splitting it between owners and NCI.

💡 Hint: Gross up first, compare, then recognise only the parent share against recognised goodwill.

Q3. Buy-out of NCI [L2]

Continuing Q1, two years later Sagar buys the remaining 40% for ₹420 Cr when NCI carrying amount is ₹290 Cr (proportionate method) or ₹340 Cr (fair value method). Pass the journal entry under each method and explain why goodwill is unchanged.

Q4. Judgement: which method to elect [L3]

A listed Indian group acquires 51% of an unlisted EV-component maker; the sellers hold a put option exercisable in 3 years at fair value, and the group expects to exercise its call earlier. Recommend an NCI measurement basis, considering valuation effort, impairment exposure, put-option accounting and covenant ratios (debt/equity covenant of 1.5×).

Q5. Other components of NCI [L2]

The acquiree has, besides ordinary shares, CCPS held by a PE fund (FV ₹85 Cr; on liquidation entitled to the higher of capital back and as-converted share) and vested ESOPs (Ind AS 102 market-based measure ₹6 Cr). The acquirer elects the proportionate method. Explain how each component of NCI is measured.
