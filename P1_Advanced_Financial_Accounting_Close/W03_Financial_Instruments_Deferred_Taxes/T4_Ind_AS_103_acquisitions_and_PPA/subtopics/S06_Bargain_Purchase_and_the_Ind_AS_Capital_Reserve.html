Subtopic note

Bargain Purchase and the Ind AS Capital Reserve Carve-out

Scope: Bargain purchase — Ind AS carve-out: after reassessment, gain via OCI to capital reserve (clear evidence) else directly to capital reserve

Why it matters

Distressed acquisitions under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) resolution plans, forced sales by lenders and promoter exits under regulatory pressure regularly produce purchase prices below the fair value of identifiable net assets. Under IFRS 3 and US GAAP, that excess is a gain in P&L. Ind AS 103 departs from IFRS: the gain never touches P&L — it goes to capital reserve, either via OCI or directly. This is one of the formal carve-outs from IFRS, listed in the Ind AS 103 appendix comparing it with IFRS 3. Analysts reading an Indian acquirer's accounts, and auditors reviewing an IBC acquisition, need to know which route applies and whether the reassessment was rigorous.

Core notes

When a bargain purchase arises (103.34)

A bargain purchase exists when the acquisition-date amount of identifiable net assets (measured under Ind AS 103, including exceptions) exceeds:

\[ \text{Consideration transferred} + \text{NCI} + \text{FV of previously held equity interest} \]

Mandatory reassessment (103.36)

Before recognising anything, the acquirer must reassess whether it has correctly identified all assets acquired and liabilities assumed, and review the procedures used to measure:

  • the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed;
  • NCI, if any;
  • the previously held equity interest, for a step acquisition; and
  • the consideration transferred.

The objective is to ensure measurements reflect all available information at the acquisition date. Practical reassessment checklist:

  • Were contingent liabilities (tax demands, environmental, litigation) fully identified and fair-valued?
  • Were employee liabilities, onerous contracts, and decommissioning obligations captured?
  • Do asset fair values reflect economic obsolescence — a distressed target's plant is rarely worth its depreciated replacement cost if the business earns below its cost of capital?
  • Is DTL on step-ups recognised?
  • Is contingent or deferred consideration included at fair value?
  • Are any elements of the deal separate transactions (e.g., lenders' haircuts recognised as settlement of pre-existing relationships)?

If an excess remains after reassessment, it is a bargain purchase.

The Ind AS treatment (103.34–36A)

Condition Recognition
Clear evidence of the underlying reasons for a bargain purchase exists (forced sale, distress, regulatory compulsion, seller under liquidity pressure) Gain recognised in OCI on the acquisition date and accumulated in equity as capital reserve
No clear evidence of the underlying reasons Excess recognised directly in equity as capital reserve (not through OCI)

Either way: no gain in P&L, and the capital reserve is not available for distribution as dividend (it is not a realised profit under Sec 123 of the Companies Act 2013). The OCI amount is not reclassified to P&L subsequently.

Why the carve-out exists

The Indian standard-setters considered that recognising a day-1 gain in P&L on an acquisition would allow profit to be manufactured from asset fair-value estimates and could encourage aggressive valuations; it also mirrors the pre-Ind AS practice (AS 14 / AS 21) of crediting negative goodwill to capital reserve.

Illustration

Sthir Steel Ltd acquires 100% of Durbal Metals Ltd under an NCLT-approved IBC resolution plan on 1 September 2025 for ₹1,400 Cr. Initial PPA:

₹ Cr Amount
PPE (depreciated replacement cost) 2,100
Inventory 260
Receivables 140
Liabilities assumed (operational creditors under plan) (450)
Contingent liabilities (40)
DTL on step-ups (25.168% × ₹600 Cr step-up) (151)
Net assets — initial 1,859

Excess = ₹1,859 Cr − ₹1,400 Cr = ₹459 Cr.

Reassessment: The valuer applies an economic obsolescence adjustment of ₹220 Cr to PPE (capacity utilisation 55%); DTL falls by 25.168% × 220 = ₹55 Cr; an additional ₹30 Cr environmental remediation obligation is identified (DTA ₹7.6 Cr).

Revised net assets = \(1{,}859 - 220 + 55 - 30 + 7.6 = 1{,}671.6\) Cr. Bargain = ₹271.6 Cr.

Clear evidence exists (IBC process, creditors' committee forced sale). Entry:

Account Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Identifiable net assets (line by line) 1,671.6
Bank 1,400.0
Gain on bargain purchase (OCI) 271.6
Closing of OCI: OCI — bargain purchase 271.6
Capital reserve (other equity) 271.6

Tax considerations

  • Bargain purchase gain is not taxable income under the Income-tax Act in a share acquisition (it is an accounting construct).
  • MAT (Sec 115JB) for companies in the old regime: OCI items that will never be reclassified to P&L are included in book profit through the Ind AS transition/OCI adjustment rules in 115JB(2A) — taxpayers should examine whether a bargain-purchase OCI credit is caught (the treatment of capital-reserve credits not routed through OCI differs). Companies under 115BAA are outside MAT.
  • No DT arises on the capital reserve itself; DT is computed on the underlying assets and liabilities.

Presentation and disclosure

  • Within other equity as "Capital reserve" (separately identified from capital reserve arising on common control combinations, which has a different origin — see s08).
  • Disclose the amount, the line item where recognised and a description of the reasons the transaction resulted in a gain (103.B64(n)), and whether it was recognised in OCI or directly in equity.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bargain from NCI measurement — choosing fair value NCI may eliminate a bargain that appears under proportionate NCI; the choice is still free per transaction.
  • Consideration includes contingent elements — subsequent increases in earn-out liabilities go to P&L, not capital reserve; the bargain amount is fixed at the acquisition date (subject to measurement period adjustments, which do adjust the capital reserve).
  • IBC plans with lender haircuts — debt extinguished as part of the plan may be part of the identifiable liabilities assumed at revised (FV) amounts; careful identification of what is "assumed" matters.
  • Step acquisitions — remeasuring the old stake to fair value can turn goodwill into bargain or vice versa.
  • "Clear evidence" — management must document it; absent documentation, auditors will expect the direct-to-equity route.
  • Comparatives in IFRS-reporting parents — an Indian subsidiary's Ind AS capital reserve becomes a P&L gain in a foreign parent's IFRS package; group reporting adjustments are required.

Key takeaways

  • A bargain purchase exists only after a documented reassessment of every measurement (103.36).
  • Ind AS carve-out: never a P&L gain.
  • Clear evidence of reasons → OCI then capital reserve; no clear evidence → directly to capital reserve.
  • Capital reserve on bargain purchase is not distributable.
  • DTL on step-ups and economic obsolescence are the usual reassessment levers.
  • Disclose the amount, route and reasons (B64(n)).

Practice Questions

Q1. Recognise a bargain purchase [L1]

Acquirer pays ₹320 Cr for 80% of a target with FV of identifiable net assets ₹450 Cr; NCI measured at proportionate share. Compute the bargain purchase, and state the accounting route if (a) the seller was a stressed promoter forced to sell by lenders, (b) no specific reason can be identified.

Q2. Reassessment changes the answer [L2]

An IBC acquisition shows FV of net assets ₹980 Cr vs consideration ₹800 Cr. Reassessment identifies: PPE economic obsolescence of ₹120 Cr (tax base unchanged), unrecorded gratuity obligation of ₹15 Cr, and a missed DTL on a brand step-up of ₹80 Cr. Tax rate 25.168%. Recompute the bargain purchase and pass the acquisition entry.

💡 Hint: Each adjustment has a DT consequence; apply them one by one.

Q3. Bargain or goodwill with step acquisition [L2]

The acquirer previously held 20% (carrying amount ₹60 Cr under equity method, FV at acquisition date ₹95 Cr) and pays ₹300 Cr for a further 60%. FV of identifiable net assets is ₹520 Cr; NCI measured at proportionate share. Determine goodwill or bargain purchase and the P&L effect of the previously held interest.

Q4. Compare with IFRS [L3]

A UK-listed parent consolidates the Indian subsidiary from Q1(a). Explain the reporting differences between the Indian statutory consolidated statements (Ind AS) and the IFRS group reporting package, and the adjustments the group reporting team must make.

Q5. Interview: why not P&L? [L1]

In an interview for a technical accounting role, explain in under two minutes why Ind AS 103 departs from IFRS 3 on bargain purchases and why the capital reserve is not distributable.
