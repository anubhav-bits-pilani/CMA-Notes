Key concept

Measuring DT at the Rate Enacted for the Reversal Period

Concept: DTL/DTA = Temporary difference × Tax rate enacted/substantively enacted for the reversal period (12.47)

Statement

\[ DT = \sum_{t} TD_t \times r_t \]

where \( TD_t \) is the portion of the temporary difference expected to reverse in period \( t \) and \( r_t \) the tax rate expected to apply in that period, based on tax rates and laws enacted or substantively enacted by the end of the reporting period (Ind AS 12.47). If a single rate applies to all reversal periods, \( DT = TD \times r \). The measurement also reflects the manner in which the entity expects to recover or settle the carrying amount (12.51) and is not discounted (12.53).

Explanation

DT represents future tax consequences, so it uses the rate that will apply when the difference reverses, not necessarily the current rate. "Substantively enacted" in India is generally taken to mean that the Finance Bill has been passed by both Houses of Parliament (even before Presidential assent), since the remaining steps are formalities; a rate announced in the Budget speech but not passed is not substantively enacted at a 31 March reporting date if Parliament has not passed it. (The Finance Bill is usually passed in late March, so the timing matters for 31 March year-ends.)

Rate selection depends on:

  • Regime: 25.168% (115BAA) vs old-regime rates with surcharge bands; a planned switch to 115BAA changes the rate for later reversals (s05).
  • Character of income on reversal: business income vs capital gains (LTCG 12.5%, STCG 20%, plus surcharge and cess) — e.g., DT on FVOCI equity measured at the LTCG rate if sale is expected (12.51).
  • Tax holidays: differences reversing during a holiday period (SEZ Sec 10AA for old-regime companies) carry lower or nil rates.
  • Graduated surcharge: for an old-regime company expecting income below ₹10 Cr, the 7% band applies; where different rates apply to different levels of income, Ind AS 12.49 requires the average rate expected to apply to taxable profit in the reversal periods.

When the rate changes, opening DT is remeasured; the effect goes to P&L except for items originally recognised outside P&L (12.60, 12.61A).

Illustration

Xavier Polymers Ltd (old regime, income above ₹10 Cr) decides in March 2026 to opt for 115BAA from FY 2027-28 because it wants to use its remaining MAT credit in FY 2026-27. At 31 March 2026 its taxable PPE difference of ₹30 Cr reverses ₹5 Cr in FY 2026-27 and ₹25 Cr thereafter. It also holds FVOCI listed shares with an unrealised gain of ₹8 Cr expected to be sold in 3 years.

\[ DTL_{\text{PPE}} = 5 \times 34.944\% + 25 \times 25.168\% = 1.747 + 6.292 = 8.039 \text{ Cr} \]

\[ DTL_{\text{shares}} = 8 \times 12.5\% \times 1.10 \times 1.04 = 1.144 \text{ Cr (to OCI)} \]

The shares' rate assumes the sale occurs after the switch (115BAA surcharge 10%), and the ₹1.25 lakh Sec 112A exemption is ignored as immaterial.

Practice Questions

Q1. Blended reversal rate [L2]

A company's deductible difference on gratuity of ₹6 Cr reverses ₹1 Cr a year. It will be in the old regime (34.944%) for two more years and then opt for 115BAA. Compute the DTA and explain the treatment of the portion arising from OCI remeasurements (₹1.5 Cr of the ₹6 Cr).

💡 Hint: Allocate the reversal by year before applying rates.

Q2. Substantive enactment timing [L1]

A Finance Bill proposing a change in the capital gains rate is presented on 1 February 2026 and passed by both Houses on 25 March 2026; assent is given on 30 March 2026. Another proposal is announced on 1 February 2027 for a year-end of 31 December 2026 (a subsidiary with a December year-end). Explain which rate each entity uses.

Q3. Manner of recovery [L2]

A company holds land with carrying amount ₹40 Cr (revalued) and tax cost ₹15 Cr. Management may either use it for a factory (no depreciation for tax) or sell it within two years. Explain how the expected manner of recovery affects the DTL and compute it under each expectation at 115BAA rates.
