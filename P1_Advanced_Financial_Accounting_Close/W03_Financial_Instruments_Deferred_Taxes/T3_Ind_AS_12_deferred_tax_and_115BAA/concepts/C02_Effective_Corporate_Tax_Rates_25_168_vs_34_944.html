Key concept

Effective Corporate Tax Rates: 25.168% vs 34.944%

Concept: 115BAA effective rate = 22% × 1.10 × 1.04 = 25.168%; old regime (income > ₹10 Cr) = 30% × 1.12 × 1.04 = 34.944%

Statement

\[ r_{\text{eff}} = r_{\text{base}} \times (1 + s) \times (1 + c) \]

where \( r_{\text{base}} \) is the base rate, \( s \) the surcharge rate and \( c \) the health and education cess (4%).

\[ r_{115BAA} = 22\% \times 1.10 \times 1.04 = 25.168\% \]

\[ r_{\text{old, income} > 10 \text{ Cr}} = 30\% \times 1.12 \times 1.04 = 34.944\% \]

Explanation

Surcharge is levied on income-tax and cess on income-tax plus surcharge, so the factors multiply. Under 115BAA the surcharge is a flat 10% whatever the income, giving a single rate. In the old regime, surcharge depends on total income: nil up to ₹1 Cr, 7% above ₹1 Cr up to ₹10 Cr, and 12% above ₹10 Cr (with marginal relief at the thresholds). Base rate is 30%, or 25% for companies whose turnover or gross receipts in the specified earlier year did not exceed ₹400 Cr.

Regime ≤ ₹1 Cr ₹1–10 Cr > ₹10 Cr
Old, 30% base 31.200% 33.384% 34.944%
Old, 25% base 26.000% 27.820% 29.120%
115BAA, 22% 25.168% 25.168% 25.168%
115BAB, 15% (eligible new manufacturers) 17.160% 17.160% 17.160%
MAT (old regime), 15% of book profit 15.600% 16.692% 17.472%

Capital gains for a 115BAA company: LTCG at 12.5% → 14.30%; STCG under Sec 111A at 20% → 22.88%. For an old-regime company with income above ₹10 Cr: LTCG 14.56%, STCG 23.30%.

These are the rates applied in deferred tax (c01) and in the ETR reconciliation (c04). For old-regime companies near a surcharge threshold, use the average rate expected for the reversal years (Ind AS 12.49).

Illustration

Yash Foods Ltd has taxable business income of ₹50 Cr and LTCG of ₹4 Cr in FY 2025-26.

  • Under 115BAA: tax = 50 × 25.168% + 4 × 14.30% = 12.584 + 0.572 = ₹13.156 Cr.
  • Under old regime (30% base, surcharge 12%): tax = 50 × 34.944% + 4 × 14.56% = 17.472 + 0.582 = ₹18.054 Cr, before any deductions available only in the old regime, and subject to MAT if higher.

The ₹4.9 Cr gap is the value of the switch, against which forgone incentives and MAT credit must be weighed.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rate computation by band [L1]

Compute the effective tax rate for: (a) an old-regime company with turnover ₹300 Cr in the relevant earlier year and income ₹8 Cr; (b) the same company with income ₹15 Cr; (c) a 115BAA company with income ₹0.8 Cr; (d) MAT for a company with book profit ₹12 Cr.

💡 Hint: Check the base-rate condition, then the surcharge band, then cess.

Q2. Regime comparison with MAT [L2]

An old-regime company has taxable income ₹30 Cr after a ₹10 Cr Sec 80-IA deduction, and book profit ₹45 Cr. Compute tax payable under the old regime (normal vs MAT) and under 115BAA (no 80-IA deduction), and the MAT credit generated, if any.

Q3. WACC tax shield [L2]

A valuation analyst uses 34.944% as the tax rate for the debt shield of an NSE-listed company that has opted for 115BAA. Explain the error and quantify its effect on the after-tax cost of debt if the pre-tax cost is 9.5%.
