Key concept

Reviewing the Carrying Amount of DTA at Each Reporting Date

Concept: DTA carrying amount reviewed each reporting date (12.56)

Statement

The carrying amount of a deferred tax asset shall be reviewed at the end of each reporting period. An entity shall reduce the carrying amount of a DTA to the extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profit will be available to allow the benefit of part or all of that DTA to be utilised. Any such reduction shall be reversed to the extent that it becomes probable that sufficient taxable profit will be available (Ind AS 12.56). Separately, at the end of each reporting period the entity reassesses unrecognised DTAs and recognises them to the extent it has become probable that future taxable profit will allow recovery (12.37).

\[ DTA_{\text{recognised}} = \min\left(DTA_{\text{gross}},\; r \times \text{Probable taxable amounts available for utilisation}\right) \]

where the probable taxable amounts include reversals of taxable temporary differences (same authority and entity, in the right periods, 12.28) and probable future taxable profit (12.29), and \( r \) is the rate expected on utilisation.

Explanation

DTA recognition is not a one-time judgement. Every reporting date — including quarterly reporting dates for listed companies — management must ask whether the evidence that supported recognition still holds: forecasts, order book, DTL reversal profile, loss expiry, changes in tax law (115BAA switch, Income-tax Act 2025 transition), and events such as stake sales (Sec 79). The review covers DTAs on deductible temporary differences as well as on losses and credits (MAT credit under the old regime).

Key points:

  • Same tests as initial recognition (12.24–12.31, 12.34–12.36), including the heightened evidence requirement after recent losses (12.35).
  • Character of income: a DTA on capital losses requires probable capital gains; business profits do not help.
  • Excluding originating deductible differences when forecasting taxable profit (12.29(a)(i)) to avoid circularity.
  • Write-down and reversal both go through P&L, unless the DTA originally arose on items in OCI or equity (backward tracing, 12.61A, 12.63).
  • Disclosure: amount and nature of evidence supporting DTAs whose utilisation depends on future profits in loss-making entities (12.82); unrecognised deductible differences, losses and credits with expiry dates (12.81(e)).
  • Ind AS 34: changes in recoverability judgements are generally recognised in the interim period in which they occur.

Illustration

Bhavya Hotels Ltd (115BAA) recognised a DTA of ₹12.58 Cr on ₹50 Cr of business losses at 31 March 2025, based on forecasts of ₹15 Cr taxable profit a year from FY 2025-26. Actual FY 2025-26 taxable profit is ₹4 Cr (₹4 Cr of losses used; ₹46 Cr remain, of which ₹18 Cr expire after FY 2027-28). Revised forecasts: ₹6 Cr and ₹8 Cr for the next two years and ₹10 Cr a year thereafter; taxable temporary differences reversing within the expiry windows: ₹5 Cr.

  • Near-expiry losses (₹18 Cr): utilisable by FY 2027-28 from profits ₹14 Cr + DTL reversals in that window, say ₹1 Cr = ₹15 Cr → ₹3 Cr will lapse.
  • Remaining ₹28 Cr (longer expiry): forecasts of ₹10 Cr a year for the following years, with weak track record, support perhaps ₹20 Cr (management judgement) plus ₹4 Cr DTL reversals = ₹24 Cr.
  • Recognisable losses = ₹15 + ₹24 = ₹39 Cr → DTA ₹9.82 Cr.
  • Opening DTA after utilisation: 46 × 25.168% = ₹11.58 Cr. Write-down: ₹1.76 Cr charged to P&L, disclosed in the ETR reconciliation as derecognition of DTA; ₹7 Cr of losses disclosed as unrecognised with expiry dates.

Practice Questions

Q1. Quarter-end review [L2]

A listed company carries a DTA of ₹20 Cr on losses, recognised on the basis of a large export contract. In Q2 the customer cancels the contract. Revised forecasts support utilisation of only 55% of the losses before expiry. Compute the write-down at 25.168%, state where and when it is recognised, and how Q2 and year-to-date ETR are affected.

💡 Hint: Treat the write-down as a discrete item in the interim period of the change.

Q2. Reversal of an earlier write-down [L1]

A company wrote down ₹4 Cr of DTA two years ago. It now has a long-term supply agreement and three consecutive profitable years. Explain the conditions for reinstating the DTA and the accounting.

Q3. Review checklist [L2]

Draft a year-end DTA review checklist (8–10 points) for a group with five Indian subsidiaries, covering evidence, tax-law changes, Sec 79, expiry profiles and disclosures.
