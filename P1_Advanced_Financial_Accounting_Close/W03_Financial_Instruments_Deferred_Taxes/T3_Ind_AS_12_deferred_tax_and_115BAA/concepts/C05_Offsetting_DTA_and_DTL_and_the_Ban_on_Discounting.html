Key concept

Offsetting DTA and DTL and the Ban on Discounting

Concept: Offset DTA and DTL only with legally enforceable right and same tax authority/entity (12.74); DT never discounted (12.53)

Statement

Offset (Ind AS 12.74). An entity offsets deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities if, and only if:

  1. the entity has a legally enforceable right to set off current tax assets against current tax liabilities; and
  2. the DTA and DTL relate to income taxes levied by the same taxation authority on either (a) the same taxable entity, or (b) different taxable entities that intend either to settle current tax liabilities and assets on a net basis, or to realise the assets and settle the liabilities simultaneously, in each future period in which significant amounts of DT are expected to be settled or recovered.

No discounting (Ind AS 12.53). Deferred tax assets and liabilities shall not be discounted.

Explanation

Offset. In India, each company is a separate taxable entity under the Income-tax Act; there is no consolidated tax return or group relief. So within one company, DTA and DTL under the Income-tax Act are normally offset and presented as a single net amount (Schedule III shows "Deferred tax assets (net)" or "Deferred tax liabilities (net)"). Across group companies, offset is generally not permitted in consolidated financial statements: a DTA of a loss-making subsidiary cannot be netted against the parent's DTL. A group with foreign subsidiaries cannot offset across jurisdictions. Gross amounts by type of temporary difference are still disclosed in the notes (12.81(g)), and MAT credit (where applicable) is often shown within the DT note.

The same logic applies to current tax (12.71): advance tax/TDS and provision for tax are offset only where there is a legally enforceable right and intention to settle net — in practice, per assessment year for the same entity. Many Indian companies present "Income tax assets (net)" and "Current tax liabilities (net)" by netting within each assessment year.

No discounting. Reliable discounting would require detailed scheduling of each reversal, which is often impracticable and would add subjectivity; for comparability Ind AS 12 prohibits it. This holds even where reversals are decades away (e.g., PPE with long lives). Note that the carrying amounts on which temporary differences are measured may themselves be discounted (e.g., a decommissioning provision, lease liability) — this does not breach 12.53, which concerns the DT amount.

Illustration

A group at 31 March 2026 (₹ Cr):

Entity DTL DTA Presentation
Parent (India) 22.0 (PPE) 6.5 (gratuity, ECL, leases) Net DTL 15.5
Indian subsidiary A 1.2 4.0 (losses, recognised) Net DTA 2.8
UK subsidiary 0.0 0.9 DTA 0.9
Consolidation adjustments (PPA step-up DTL in A; DTA on unrealised profit in parent's inventory) 3.0 (A) 0.4 (parent) Allocated to the entity that will pay or recover the tax

Consolidated balance sheet: DTL 15.1 (parent 15.5 − 0.4) plus A's position: A's net 2.8 DTA less PPA DTL 3.0 → net DTL 0.2 for A. Presentation: Deferred tax liabilities (net) 15.3 (parent 15.1 + A 0.2); Deferred tax assets (net) 0.9 (UK). No netting across entities.

Connections

  • s07: business-combination DT and unrealised-profit DT must be allocated to the correct taxable entity before offsetting.
  • c03: the roll-forward is prepared gross by category, then offset for presentation.
  • w3_t5 consolidation: DTA on eliminated profit uses the tax rate and entity of the inventory holder.
  • s03: leases produce gross DTA and DTL that are typically offset within the entity.
  • Valuation: undiscounted DTLs on long-lived PPE overstate the economic liability; analysts sometimes treat them as quasi-equity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Presentation decision [L1]

A company has DTL ₹10 Cr under the Income-tax Act and DTA ₹3 Cr relating to a foreign branch's tax losses under the branch country's law. Determine the presentation.

💡 Hint: Check whether both relate to the same taxation authority.

Q2. Consolidated offset [L2]

Parent P has net DTL ₹25 Cr; subsidiary S (India) has net DTA ₹6 Cr; the group files separate returns. The CFO wants to present net DTL of ₹19 Cr. Explain why this is not permitted and what, if anything, would allow it.

Q3. Discounting debate [L2]

An analyst argues that a ₹200 Cr DTL that will reverse over 25 years "is worth only ₹50 Cr in present value" and the balance sheet overstates liabilities. Explain the Ind AS 12 position and how you would treat the DTL in an enterprise valuation.
