Subtopic note

PPE Deferred Tax: Book SLM vs Block WDV and Additional Depreciation

Scope: PPE: book SLM vs block WDV (Sec 32), additional depreciation — DTL build-up and reversal

Why it matters

For a capital-intensive Indian company, PPE is usually the largest single source of deferred tax. Books depreciate on straight-line over Schedule II or technically assessed useful lives, while the Income-tax Act uses written-down value rates on blocks of assets. The result is a DTL that builds in early years and reverses later — and that keeps growing while the company invests. Analysts model it to forecast cash taxes; reviewers test the tax WDV reconciliation and the handling of block-level rules (sales, transfers, Sec 50). Additional depreciation and the 115BAA choice change the profile materially.

Core notes

Book depreciation

Ind AS 16 requires depreciation over the useful life reflecting the pattern of consumption; most Indian companies use SLM with Schedule II to the Companies Act 2013 useful lives (e.g., plant and machinery 15 years, general; buildings 30–60 years; computers 3 years; vehicles 8–10 years) or technically assessed lives with disclosure of justification. Component accounting, residual value (usually ≤5% under Schedule II unless justified) and revaluation (Ind AS 16.31) all affect carrying amounts.

Tax depreciation under Sec 32

  • Block of assets (Sec 2(11)): assets of the same class with the same rate are pooled. Depreciation is on the WDV of the block, not individual assets.
  • Rates (Appendix I to the Income-tax Rules, from AY 2018-19): buildings 10% (residential 5%), furniture 10%, plant and machinery 15% (general), motor cars 15% (30% for vehicles used in hiring business), computers and software 40%, intangibles (know-how, patents, copyrights, licences) 25%. Goodwill is excluded from depreciable assets from AY 2021-22.
  • Half rate: if an asset is acquired during the year and put to use for less than 180 days in that year, depreciation is allowed at half the rate (second proviso to Sec 32(1)).
  • Sale of assets: sale proceeds reduce the block's WDV; if proceeds exceed the block's WDV or the block ceases to exist, short-term capital gains arise under Sec 50, regardless of holding period.
  • Unabsorbed depreciation carries forward indefinitely (Sec 32(2)) — see s06.

Additional depreciation (Sec 32(1)(iia))

A company engaged in manufacture or production (or generation/distribution of power) is entitled to additional depreciation of 20% of the actual cost of new plant and machinery acquired and installed (35% under the proviso for notified backward areas of Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar and West Bengal, for machinery acquired up to 31 March 2020 — now largely historical). If put to use for less than 180 days, half is allowed in that year and the balance in the next year. Excluded: ships, aircraft, second-hand machinery, office appliances, road vehicles, assets installed in office or residential premises, assets fully deductible in one year.

Under 115BAA, additional depreciation is not allowed (Sec 115BAA(2)(i)), and the company cannot claim deductions such as Sec 32AD, 33AB, 35(1)(ii)/(iia)/(iii), 35AD, 35CCC, 35CCD and most of Chapter VI-A. The normal WDV rate continues.

How the DTL builds and reverses

For a single asset, the temporary difference is book carrying amount − tax WDV. Because WDV front-loads deductions, the tax WDV falls faster early on, building a DTL; later the book depreciation overtakes and the difference reverses. For a block, new additions keep adding fresh front-loaded differences, so a growing company's DTL rarely reverses in aggregate.

Ruhi Auto Parts Ltd (115BAA, 25.168%) buys a machine for ₹10 Cr on 1 April 2025 (put to use immediately). Book: SLM over 15 years, nil residual. Tax: 15% WDV, no additional depreciation.

The DTL peaks around year 5 and becomes a DTA by year 15 because WDV never reaches zero on an individual basis — in practice the residual stays in the block until sale or the block ceases.

Old regime comparison. With 20% additional depreciation, year-1 tax deduction is 35% (₹3.5 Cr): tax WDV ₹6.5 Cr; temporary difference ₹2.833 Cr; DTL at 34.944% ≈ ₹0.99 Cr. The regime choice therefore affects both the rate and the size of the difference.

Building the PPE DT schedule in Excel

  1. Fixed asset register with book cost, accumulated depreciation, Ind AS adjustments (decommissioning, borrowing costs capitalised, component splits).
  2. Tax block register: opening WDV, additions (split ≥180 / <180 days), deletions (sale proceeds), depreciation by rate, closing WDV.
  3. Map book assets to tax blocks; reconcile total book carrying amount to the balance sheet and total tax WDV to the tax computation (Form 3CD Clause 18 data for audited companies).
  4. Temporary difference by block; flag items with no tax base (e.g., goodwill, leasehold land premium if not deductible) and items with special treatment (capital subsidies reducing tax cost under Explanation 10 to Sec 43(1)).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ind AS 16 capitalisations not recognised for tax (decommissioning asset; exchange differences adjusted to asset cost under Sec 43A differing from the Ind AS 21 treatment; borrowing costs under ICDS IX vs Ind AS 23) create differences in the asset's tax base.
  • Government grants: Ind AS 20 deferred income vs tax reduction in actual cost (Explanation 10 to Sec 43(1)) — two separate differences, on the asset and the deferred income.
  • Revaluation: DTL on revaluation surplus charged to OCI (12.61A); subsequent excess depreciation transfer within equity is net of tax.
  • Land: freehold land is not depreciable for tax; on sale, capital gains (indexation removed for transfers after 23 July 2024 for companies) — DT on revalued land at the capital gains rate.
  • Assets held for sale — expected manner of recovery is sale; check if Sec 50 STCG at normal rates applies.
  • Impairment losses reduce book carrying amount without tax effect → reduce the DTL.

Key takeaways

  • PPE temporary difference = book carrying amount − tax WDV of the related block.
  • WDV front-loads deductions, building a DTL that reverses later; continuous capex keeps the aggregate DTL growing.
  • Half-rate for assets used < 180 days and additional depreciation (20%/35%) increase early differences.
  • 115BAA removes additional depreciation and lowers the rate, reducing the DTL.
  • Reconcile book and tax registers to the balance sheet and tax computation before computing DT.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-year DT schedule with half-rate [L2]

A 115BAA company buys plant for ₹6 Cr on 15 November 2025 (put to use on that date). Book depreciation: SLM over 12 years, residual 5%, pro rata from date of use. Tax: 15% WDV. Compute book carrying amounts, tax WDV, temporary differences and DTL at 31 March 2026, 2027 and 2028.

💡 Hint: The asset is used for fewer than 180 days in FY 2025-26, so tax depreciation is at half rate in that year only.

Q2. Additional depreciation under the old regime [L2]

Repeat Q1 for an old-regime manufacturing company eligible for 20% additional depreciation, with a 34.944% rate, and quantify the difference in DTL at each date.

Q3. Block sale and Sec 50 [L2]

A company's 15% block has opening WDV ₹2.4 Cr. During the year it sells all machines in the block for ₹3.1 Cr (book carrying amount ₹3.5 Cr). Compute the tax consequence and the effect on the deferred tax balance for that block.

Q4. Forecasting the PPE DTL [L3]

Design a model block that forecasts the PPE DTL for 5 years given annual capex of ₹50 Cr growing 10%, book useful life 15 years, tax rate 15% WDV and 25.168%. Explain why the DTL keeps growing and what happens if capex stops in year 4.
