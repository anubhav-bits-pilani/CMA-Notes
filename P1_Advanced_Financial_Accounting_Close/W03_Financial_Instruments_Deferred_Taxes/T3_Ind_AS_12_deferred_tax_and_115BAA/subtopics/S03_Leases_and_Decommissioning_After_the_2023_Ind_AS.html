Subtopic note

Leases and Decommissioning After the 2023 Ind AS 12 Amendment

Scope: Leases and decommissioning: 2023 amendment narrowing initial recognition exemption — DTA on lease liability, DTL on ROU

Why it matters

Ind AS 116 put lease liabilities and ROU assets on the balance sheet of every Indian lessee — retail chains, telecom tower tenants, airlines, hospital groups, IT companies with large office leases. Tax law ignores the split and allows rent as paid. Before 2023, practice diverged on whether the initial recognition exemption (IRE) prevented deferred tax on these balances. The Companies (Ind AS) Amendment Rules 2023, notified on 31 March 2023 and effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023, settled the issue: deferred tax is recognised on both. The same logic applies to decommissioning and restoration obligations capitalised into PPE. Gross DTA and DTL now appear in the notes, and the ETR effect of leases must be understood by FP&A teams modelling store or tower roll-outs.

Core notes

The problem the amendment solved

At lease commencement a lessee recognises a lease liability and an ROU asset of equal amount (ignoring initial direct costs and prepaid rent). The transaction affects neither accounting profit nor taxable profit. Under the original IRE (12.15(b), 12.24), some preparers concluded no DT should be recognised on either — which then produced ETR distortions as the balances diverged over the lease term. Others applied an integrally-linked net approach.

The amendment (12.15(b)(iii), 12.22A, 12.24(c))

The IRE does not apply to transactions that, at the time of the transaction, give rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences. Consequently, at commencement of a lease (and on initial recognition of a decommissioning liability capitalised into an asset), the entity recognises:

  • a DTA on the deductible temporary difference relating to the lease liability (subject to the recoverability test in 12.24), and
  • a DTL on the taxable temporary difference relating to the ROU asset.

12.22A clarifies two points. First, the exemption is lost only where the differences are equal at the time of the transaction; if applicable tax law makes them unequal at inception (for example, because part of the asset cost is deductible differently), the exemption continues to apply to the transaction. Second, even where equal differences arise, the DTA is still subject to the recoverability test in 12.24, so the recognised DTA and DTL can end up unequal.

Transition: the amendment applied to transactions occurring on or after the beginning of the earliest comparative period presented; for leases and decommissioning obligations existing at that date, DTA and DTL were recognised with the cumulative effect as an adjustment to opening retained earnings (or other equity) at that date. Many Indian companies already applied a gross or net approach before the amendment, so the effect was often limited to disclosure.

Tax treatment in India

  • Lessee claims lease rent as a deduction under Sec 37 (or Sec 30 for premises) on accrual/payment as per the agreement. Neither ROU depreciation nor lease interest is deductible.
  • Tax base of the ROU asset = nil (no future deductions attached to the asset itself).
  • Tax base of the lease liability = carrying amount − future deductions = nil (all future payments are deductible).
  • Upfront lease premium or prepaid rent may have its own tax treatment (capital vs revenue).

Lease illustration

Riya Retail Ltd (115BAA) signs a 5-year store lease on 1 April 2025, annual rent ₹2.5 Cr in arrears, IBR 10%. Lease liability = ROU = ₹9.477 Cr.

31 March 2026 Amount (₹ Cr) Tax base Difference DT at 25.168%
ROU asset (9.477 − 1.895 depreciation) 7.582 0 7.582 taxable DTL 1.908
Lease liability (9.477 + 0.948 − 2.5) 7.925 0 7.925 deductible DTA 1.995
Net 0.343 deductible DTA 0.086

P&L year 1: depreciation 1.895 + interest 0.948 = 2.843 vs rent deductible 2.5. Book expense exceeds tax deduction by 0.343 → deferred tax credit of 0.086 in P&L. At commencement, DTA = DTL = 2.385 (both recognised, net nil, no P&L effect). Over the lease, the net DTA builds in early years (front-loaded interest) and reverses to nil at the end.

Offset: if DTA and DTL relate to the same taxation authority and the entity has a legally enforceable right to set off current tax (12.74), they are presented net on the balance sheet; the gross amounts are disclosed in the deferred tax note by type of temporary difference (12.81(g)).

Decommissioning illustration

Sahil Power Ltd commissions a solar plant on 1 April 2025 with an obligation to restore land after 25 years; PV of restoration cost ₹4 Cr at 8% is capitalised into PPE and recognised as a provision. Tax deduction is available only when restoration expenditure is actually incurred.

At recognition: DTL on the decommissioning component of PPE ₹4 Cr × 25.168% = ₹1.007 Cr; DTA on provision ₹1.007 Cr. Year 1: asset component depreciated by ₹0.16 Cr (to ₹3.84 Cr); provision unwinds by ₹0.32 Cr (to ₹4.32 Cr). Net deductible difference ₹0.48 Cr → net DTA ₹0.121 Cr, credited to P&L. Changes in estimate of the provision (Ind AS 16 Appendix A — decommissioning liabilities adjust the asset) adjust both the asset and the provision, and the DT on each.

Excel implementation

Add two columns — tax base and DT — to the Week 2 lease schedule for each lease, sum by category, and feed a "Leases — ROU" and "Leases — lease liability" line in the DT table. For portfolios of hundreds of leases, compute at portfolio level using the lease register's closing balances.

=SUMIFS(ROU_Closing, Entity, $B$2) * TaxRate
=SUMIFS(LL_Closing, Entity, $B$2) * TaxRate

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • DTA recoverability: the DTA on the lease liability is subject to 12.24; normally the matching DTL on the ROU supports it (12.28), but if the ROU is impaired the DTL falls while the DTA remains — assess recoverability.
  • Lease modifications and remeasurements change both balances; recompute DT on both.
  • Short-term and low-value leases under the Ind AS 116.5 exemptions have no balances and no DT.
  • Sale and leaseback — the gain deferred and the ROU measured at retained right of use; tax may tax the full gain on sale, creating a DTA.
  • Lessor accounting — finance lease receivable vs tax treatment (lessor may claim depreciation for tax if it remains owner under tax law) creates differences on the lessor side.
  • Decommissioning provisions discounted in books; tax deduction on payment of undiscounted cost — DT is computed on the carrying amount (discounted), as DT is never discounted itself (12.53).

Key takeaways

  • From FY 2023-24, the IRE no longer applies where a transaction creates equal taxable and deductible temporary differences — leases and decommissioning are the key cases.
  • Recognise a DTL on the ROU asset (or decommissioning component of PPE) and a DTA on the lease liability (or provision).
  • Tax bases are nil because Indian tax allows rent or restoration cost only when paid/incurred.
  • The net DT builds in early years and reverses by the end of the lease or restoration.
  • Present net where 12.74 permits; disclose gross by type in the DT note.

Practice Questions

Q1. Lease DT schedule [L2]

A 115BAA company enters into a 4-year office lease on 1 April 2025 with annual rent ₹1.2 Cr payable in advance on 1 April each year, IBR 9%. Prepare the lease schedule, the DT on ROU and lease liability at each 31 March and the P&L deferred tax credit or charge each year.

💡 Hint: Rent in advance changes the opening liability; tax deduction follows the rent paid each year.

Q2. Transition to the 2023 amendment [L2]

A company with lease liabilities of ₹120 Cr and ROU assets of ₹105 Cr at 1 April 2022 had recognised DT only on the net difference. Explain what changed on adopting the amendment for FY 2023-24 (with comparatives), the transition entries, and the disclosure effect.

Q3. Decommissioning estimate change [L2]

A mining company's restoration provision is ₹15 Cr at 31 March 2026 (asset component ₹11 Cr). The estimate is revised upward by ₹3 Cr. Pass the entries for the revision and the related deferred tax at 25.168%.

Q4. ETR effect of a store roll-out [L3]

A retail chain plans to open 150 stores over three years, each with a 9-year lease. The FP&A team asks how leases will affect reported ETR and the DT balance. Explain the pattern and build a high-level model outline that forecasts the lease-related DTA/DTL.
