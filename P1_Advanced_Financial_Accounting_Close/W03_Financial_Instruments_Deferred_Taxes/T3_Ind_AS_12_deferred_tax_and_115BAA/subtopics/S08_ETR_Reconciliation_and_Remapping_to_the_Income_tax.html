Subtopic note

ETR Reconciliation and Remapping to the Income-tax Act 2025

Scope: ETR reconciliation (12.81(c)); Income-tax Act 2025 (from 1 Apr 2026) renumbers provisions — remap legacy section references

Why it matters

The effective tax rate (ETR) reconciliation is the reader's single check on whether the tax charge makes sense. Analysts use it to identify one-off items (MAT credit write-offs, rate changes, DTA recognition on losses) and to forecast a sustainable tax rate; auditors use it as an analytical procedure over the whole tax computation. In India, the reconciliation must now also be read alongside a structural change: the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the Income-tax Act 1961 from 1 April 2026, with new section numbering and the "tax year" concept. Policies, schedules, tax-base mappings and disclosures that cite legacy sections need remapping from FY 2026-27.

Core notes

The disclosure requirement (Ind AS 12.81(c), (d))

An entity discloses an explanation of the relationship between tax expense (income) and accounting profit in either or both of the following forms:

  1. A numerical reconciliation between tax expense and the product of accounting profit multiplied by the applicable tax rate(s), disclosing the basis on which the applicable rate(s) is (are) computed; or
  2. A numerical reconciliation between the average effective tax rate and the applicable tax rate, disclosing the basis of the applicable rate.

12.81(d) requires an explanation of changes in the applicable tax rate(s) compared with the previous period. For Indian companies the applicable rate is normally the domestic statutory rate (25.168% under 115BAA, or 34.944%/other old-regime rates); groups with foreign subsidiaries may reconcile using the parent's domestic rate and show foreign rate differentials, or aggregate separate reconciliations.

Structure of the reconciliation

A large "others" line is a warning sign; good practice keeps it immaterial and explains every item.

Building it in Excel

Compute the reconciliation independently of the tax charge (bottom-up from items) and compare with the P&L tax expense booked from the tax computation and DT roll-forward. The difference must be within rounding.

=PBT*StatRate
=SUMPRODUCT(PermItems,StatRate)

Income-tax Act 2025 — remapping

The Income-tax Act 2025 received Presidential assent in August 2025 and applies from 1 April 2026, replacing the Income-tax Act 1961. Key features relevant to reporting:

  • The "previous year/assessment year" construct is replaced by a single "tax year" (the financial year in which income is earned).
  • Provisions are consolidated and renumbered; the Act was framed largely as a simplification with limited policy change, but individual provisions should not be assumed identical — read the new text.
  • Savings and transitional provisions preserve rights such as brought-forward losses, unabsorbed depreciation, MAT credit and pending proceedings.

Implications for financial reporting:

  1. Deferred tax at 31 March 2026: because the new Act was enacted before the reporting date, DT on differences reversing on or after 1 April 2026 should be measured under the new Act's provisions (12.47). Where provisions are substantively unchanged, the measurement is unaffected; identify any differences (e.g., changes in timing of deductions) and assess the effect.
  2. Accounting policies, notes and tax-base schedules that cite Sec 32, 36(1)(vii), 40(a)(ia), 43B, 72, 115BAA, 115JB etc. need a mapping column: legacy section → new section. Use the official mapping tables published by the CBDT/Income Tax Department rather than unofficial lists, and footnote the source.
  3. Uncertain tax treatments under the old Act continue to be assessed under the old Act for earlier years; Appendix C analysis must reference the correct law for each year.
  4. Tax audit and ERP masters — update GL mapping for disallowance tags (Sec 43B, 40(a)(ia) equivalents) so the DT table continues to pull data.
  5. Disclosures for FY 2026-27 comparatives may need to explain renumbering where references change.

In these notes, legacy (1961 Act) section numbers are used because they remain the reference for FY 2025-26 and earlier; remap when preparing FY 2026-27 workings.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Consolidated ETR with loss-making subsidiaries — unrecognised DTA on subsidiary losses often explains a high group ETR; show separately.
  • Choice of applicable rate — a company in the old regime with income below ₹10 Cr uses its actual surcharge band; disclose the basis.
  • Interim ETR (Ind AS 34) — the annual estimate excludes discrete items, which are shown in the quarter they arise.
  • Pillar Two top-up tax (for large MNE groups) appears as a separate line of current tax; no deferred tax due to the mandatory exception, subject to MCA notification status.
  • Rate reconciliation in valuation — the normalised ETR for DCF should exclude one-offs (MAT credit write-off, DTA recognition, prior-year adjustments).

Key takeaways

  • 12.81(c) requires a numerical reconciliation from PBT × applicable rate (or rate-to-rate) to tax expense, with the basis of the rate.
  • Typical Indian reconciling items: CSR, penalties, capital gains rate differences, 80JJAA, unrecognised DTA, rate changes, MAT credit, prior-year true-ups.
  • Build the reconciliation bottom-up and tie it to the booked tax expense; keep "others" immaterial.
  • The Income-tax Act 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 with a "tax year" concept and new numbering — measure DT under the enacted law and remap section references using official tables.
  • Normalise ETR for forecasting by stripping one-off items.

Q1. Prepare an ETR reconciliation [L2]

A 115BAA company reports PBT ₹150 Cr, tax expense ₹39.4 Cr. Items: CSR ₹2.5 Cr; interest on delayed income-tax ₹0.4 Cr; STCG on listed shares ₹5 Cr taxed at 20% × 1.10 × 1.04; DTA not recognised on capital loss of ₹3 Cr (rate 14.3%); prior-year over-provision ₹1.1 Cr; remeasurement of opening DTL on FVOCI investments due to change in capital gains rate, ₹0.2 Cr charge (to OCI). Prepare the reconciliation and identify any unexplained difference.

💡 Hint: Remember which items affect P&L tax expense and which affect OCI.
