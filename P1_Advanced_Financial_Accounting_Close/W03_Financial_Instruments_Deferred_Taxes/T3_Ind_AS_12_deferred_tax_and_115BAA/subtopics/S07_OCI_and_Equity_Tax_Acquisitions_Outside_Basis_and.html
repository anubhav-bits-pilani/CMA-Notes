Subtopic note

OCI and Equity Tax, Acquisitions, Outside Basis and Tax Uncertainty

Scope: OCI/equity items (12.61A), business-combination DT (→ w3_t4), undistributed subsidiary profits (12.39), Appendix C uncertain tax treatments

Why it matters

The four areas in this note sit outside the routine P&L deferred tax computation and are where senior reviewers find most errors. Tax on OCI and equity items determines whether the P&L tax charge is clean. Business-combination DT changes goodwill. Outside-basis differences on subsidiaries and associates can create large DTLs in consolidated accounts if ignored. Uncertain tax treatments — very common in India given the volume of litigation before CIT(A), ITAT and High Courts — require a structured Appendix C analysis rather than a contingent liability note alone.

Core notes

Items outside profit or loss (12.61A–12.65)

Current and deferred tax is recognised outside P&L if the tax relates to items recognised, in the same or a different period, outside P&L (12.61A):

Item Where recognised Where its tax goes
Actuarial gains/losses on defined benefit plans (Ind AS 19) OCI OCI
FVOCI equity and FVOCI debt fair value changes OCI OCI
Cash flow hedge reserve OCI OCI
Revaluation surplus on PPE (Ind AS 16) OCI OCI
Exchange differences on translating foreign operations OCI OCI
Equity component of compound instruments (Ind AS 32) Equity Equity (12.23)
Transaction costs of equity issue (Ind AS 32.35) Equity Equity
Excess tax deduction over cumulative ESOP expense (12.68C) — Equity
Opening retained earnings adjustments (Ind AS 8 retrospective changes, Ind AS 101) Equity Equity

Where it is difficult to determine the amount relating to items outside P&L (e.g., a rate change on DT whose origin is mixed), use a reasonable pro rata allocation (12.63). Subsequent rate changes on DT originally recognised in OCI are also recognised in OCI (backward tracing, 12.60).

Illustration — gratuity. Opening gratuity obligation ₹8 Cr; closing ₹10 Cr; increase comprises service cost and interest ₹1.4 Cr (P&L), actuarial loss ₹1.0 Cr (OCI), less benefits paid ₹0.4 Cr. DTA increases by ₹2 Cr × 25.168% = ₹0.503 Cr. Allocation: OCI credit ₹1.0 × 25.168% = ₹0.252 Cr; P&L credit ₹(1.4 − 0.4) × 25.168% = ₹0.252 Cr. (Benefits paid are deductible currently and reduce the provision; the net P&L-driven movement is ₹1.0 Cr.)

Business combinations (12.19, 12.66–12.68; → w3_t4)

  • Fair value step-ups on identifiable assets with unchanged tax base create taxable temporary differences; the DTL is recognised as part of the acquired identifiable net assets, which increases goodwill (or reduces bargain purchase gain/capital reserve).
  • No DTL on the initial recognition of goodwill (12.15(a)) — goodwill is not depreciable for tax in India from AY 2021-22. Subsequent reductions in an unrecognised DTL on goodwill are also not recognised (12.21A).
  • Acquiree's unrecognised DTAs (e.g., losses) may be recognised by the acquirer if recoverable in the group context — affects goodwill. Changes within the measurement period from new information about facts at acquisition date reduce goodwill; other changes go to P&L (12.68).
  • The acquirer's own DTA that becomes recoverable because of the combination is recognised in P&L, not goodwill (12.67).
  • In a tax-neutral amalgamation under Sec 2(1B)/47(vi), the tax bases of the transferor's assets carry over; under a slump sale (Sec 50B), tax bases may change — model accordingly.

Investments in subsidiaries, branches, associates and JVs — outside basis (12.38–12.45)

The outside-basis temporary difference is the difference between the carrying amount of the investment (in consolidation: the parent's share of net assets plus goodwill) and its tax base (usually cost). In consolidated statements it typically equals undistributed post-acquisition profits plus FCTR and other OCI.

12.39: an entity recognises a DTL for all taxable temporary differences associated with such investments, except to the extent that both (a) the parent, investor or venturer is able to control the timing of the reversal, and (b) it is probable that the difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future.

Indian application:

  • Since 1 April 2020 dividends are taxable in the shareholder's hands (DDT abolished). A parent receiving dividends from an Indian subsidiary is taxed on them, with a Sec 80M deduction to the extent it redistributes them to its own shareholders by one month before the return due date. If the parent controls dividend policy and does not plan distributions, the exemption applies.
  • Associates: the investor does not control dividend policy, so a DTL is normally recognised unless there is an agreement that profits will not be distributed (12.42); measured at the rate expected on recovery (dividend vs sale — capital gains).
  • Joint ventures: depends on whether the venturer can prevent distributions (12.43).
  • Foreign subsidiaries: dividends received are taxable in India at normal rates with credit for withholding tax; consider foreign withholding tax as part of the DTL if distributions are planned.
  • DTA on deductible outside-basis differences only if the reversal is probable in the foreseeable future and taxable profit will be available (12.44).

Uncertain tax treatments — Ind AS 12 Appendix C

Appendix C applies when there is uncertainty over whether the tax authority will accept a tax treatment. Steps:

  1. Decide whether to consider each uncertain treatment separately or together (whichever better predicts resolution).
  2. Assume the tax authority will examine amounts it has a right to examine with full knowledge of all relevant information (detection risk ignored).
  3. If it is probable that the authority will accept the treatment, measure tax consistently with the return.
  4. If not probable, reflect the uncertainty using either the most likely amount or the expected value, whichever better predicts the resolution.
  5. Reassess when facts and circumstances change (new judgements, expiry of limitation, assessment orders).

Indian context: disputes over Sec 14A disallowance, transfer pricing adjustments, 80-IA/10AA claims, characterisation of capital vs revenue expenditure, and depreciation on intangibles. Precedent from the jurisdictional High Court or ITAT on identical facts is relevant evidence. Uncertain tax liabilities are presented as current tax liabilities (or as reductions of DTAs where they relate to losses), not as Ind AS 37 provisions; interest and penalties follow a disclosed policy (Ind AS 12 if they are income taxes in nature, otherwise Ind AS 37).

Illustration. A company claimed ₹10 Cr deduction for a disputed item; the assessing officer has disallowed it; the CIT(A) ruled for the department; the matter is before ITAT, which has ruled in favour of other taxpayers on similar facts but the High Court has not. Management assesses 60% probability of success. Since acceptance is "probable" (more likely than not), no liability is recognised under Appendix C, but disclosure of the judgement is required (Ind AS 1.122). If probability were 40%, a most-likely-amount approach would recognise ₹10 Cr × 25.168% = ₹2.52 Cr plus interest under Sec 234B/234C per policy.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rate changes on OCI-originated DT — backward tracing to OCI, even years later.
  • Reclassification of FVOCI-debt gains to P&L — the related tax is also reclassified from OCI to P&L.
  • Slump sale or demerger in the group can reset tax bases and crystallise outside-basis differences.
  • Undistributed profits of a subsidiary planned for an IPO exit — the expected manner of recovery (sale, capital gains) determines the rate; if a sale is planned, the 12.39 exemption fails.
  • Vivad se Vishwas 2024 or similar settlement schemes — the decision to settle changes the measurement of uncertain positions.
  • Appendix C and DTAs — if an uncertain deduction relates to a loss carry-forward, reduce the DTA rather than recognise a liability.

Key takeaways

  • Tax follows the item: OCI items' tax goes to OCI, equity items' to equity (12.61A), including later rate changes.
  • Business-combination DT on step-ups increases goodwill; no DTL on goodwill itself.
  • Outside-basis DTL on subsidiaries is exempt only if the parent controls reversal and reversal is not probable; associates usually need a DTL.
  • Appendix C: assume full examination; recognise if acceptance is not probable, using most likely amount or expected value.
  • Reassess uncertain positions at each assessment, appeal and limitation milestone.

Practice Questions

Q1. Allocating DT between P&L and OCI [L2]

A 115BAA company's FVOCI equity investment rose from ₹20 Cr to ₹26 Cr (cost ₹15 Cr; LTCG rate 14.3%); its gratuity obligation rose by ₹1.5 Cr including ₹0.6 Cr actuarial loss; and it revalued land by ₹10 Cr (capital gains rate 14.3%). Compute the DT movements and allocate them between P&L and OCI.

💡 Hint: Each item's tax follows the location of its accounting entry.

Q2. Outside-basis DTL in consolidation [L2]

A parent holds (a) 100% of an Indian subsidiary with undistributed post-acquisition profits of ₹80 Cr and no plan to distribute; (b) 30% of an associate with ₹40 Cr undistributed share of profits and a policy of distributing 50% of profits; (c) 100% of a Singapore subsidiary expected to pay a ₹10 Cr dividend next year (Singapore withholding nil). Determine the outside-basis DTL in consolidated financial statements.

Q3. Acquiree's losses in a business combination [L2]

On acquisition, a target has unrecognised losses of ₹30 Cr that the acquirer expects to use via an amalgamation under Sec 72A within two years. Explain the effect on goodwill at acquisition, and how a later conclusion (after 14 months) that only ₹15 Cr will be used is accounted for.

Q4. Appendix C assessment [L3]

A company has three uncertain positions: transfer pricing adjustment of ₹12 Cr (ITAT ruled in favour last year; department appealed to the High Court), Sec 14A disallowance of ₹2 Cr (consistently lost at ITAT), and an 80-IA claim of ₹20 Cr (High Court ruled in favour of a peer on identical facts). Explain how you would assess and measure each under Appendix C, and draft the disclosure.
