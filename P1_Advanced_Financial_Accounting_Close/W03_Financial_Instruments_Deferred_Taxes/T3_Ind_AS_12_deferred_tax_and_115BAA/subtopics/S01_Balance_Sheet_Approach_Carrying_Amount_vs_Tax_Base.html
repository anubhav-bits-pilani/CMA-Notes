Subtopic note

Balance-Sheet Approach: Carrying Amount vs Tax Base

Scope: Balance-sheet approach: carrying amount vs tax base for every asset/liability; taxable vs deductible temporary differences vs permanent differences

Why it matters

Deferred tax under Ind AS 12 is not a P&L reconciliation exercise but a balance-sheet inventory: every asset and liability is compared with its tax base. This is the single most important conceptual shift from AS 22 for Indian preparers, and the reason Ind AS deferred tax picks up items such as FVOCI gains, business-combination step-ups and lease ROU assets. Auditors test completeness by walking the balance sheet line by line. In modelling, the method explains why DT balances move with balance-sheet growth (capex, leases) even when profit is flat.

Core notes

Definitions (Ind AS 12.5)

  • Temporary differences are differences between the carrying amount of an asset or liability in the balance sheet and its tax base.
  • Taxable temporary differences will result in taxable amounts in determining taxable profit of future periods when the carrying amount is recovered or settled → DTL.
  • Deductible temporary differences will result in amounts deductible in future periods → DTA (subject to probability, 12.24).
  • Tax base is the amount attributed to an asset or liability for tax purposes.

Tax base of an asset (12.7)

The amount that will be deductible for tax purposes against any taxable economic benefits that flow to the entity when it recovers the carrying amount. If those economic benefits will not be taxable, the tax base equals the carrying amount (no temporary difference).

Examples:

  • Machinery cost ₹100 lakh, tax WDV ₹61 lakh → tax base ₹61 lakh (future depreciation deductions).
  • Trade receivable ₹50 lakh, revenue already taxed → tax base ₹50 lakh.
  • Receivable net of ECL allowance ₹45 lakh; allowance not deducted until write-off → tax base ₹50 lakh; deductible difference ₹5 lakh.
  • Interest receivable ₹3 lakh taxed on receipt basis (rare) → tax base nil; taxable difference.
  • Dividend receivable from a subsidiary where dividends are taxable → tax base nil if the income is taxed on receipt; in India dividends are taxable to the recipient company on declaration/receipt, so analyse timing.

Tax base of a liability (12.8)

Carrying amount less any amount that will be deductible for tax purposes in respect of that liability in future periods. For revenue received in advance, tax base is the carrying amount less any amount of revenue that will not be taxable in future.

Examples:

  • Gratuity provision ₹20 lakh, deductible on payment → tax base nil; deductible difference ₹20 lakh.
  • Lease liability ₹80 lakh; lease rent deductible as paid → tax base nil (all future payments deductible) → deductible difference ₹80 lakh.
  • Trade payable for an expense already deducted → tax base = carrying amount.
  • Borrowing with unamortised fees (carrying ₹9.8 Cr, principal ₹10 Cr) where fees were deducted upfront → tax base ₹10 Cr; the ₹0.2 Cr is a taxable temporary difference (on settlement of ₹10 Cr, the extra ₹0.2 Cr of accounting expense has no further deduction).
  • Advance from customer taxed on receipt (rare in India because revenue is taxed on accrual under ICDS) → tax base nil.

Items with a tax base but no carrying amount (12.9)

Some items are not recognised as assets or liabilities but have a tax base — e.g., expenditure disallowed now but deductible later under Sec 35D (preliminary expenses amortised over 5 years) when already expensed in books; or Sec 94B carried-forward interest. These create deductible temporary differences.

Permanent differences

Differences between accounting profit and taxable profit that never reverse are not temporary differences and carry no DT — they appear in the ETR reconciliation (s08). Common Indian examples:

Item Reason
CSR expenditure Not deductible (Sec 37(1) Explanation 2)
Penalties and fines for offences Disallowed (Sec 37(1) Explanation 1)
Donations to charities Sec 80G is a Chapter VI-A deduction not available under 115BAA (only 80JJAA and 80M survive), so the whole donation is permanent
Interest on delayed TDS/income tax Not deductible
Exempt income (e.g., agricultural income, Sec 10 items) and related expenses under Sec 14A Permanent
Goodwill amortisation/impairment — goodwill not depreciable from AY 2021-22 No tax base; no DTL on initial recognition (12.15(a))

The initial recognition exemption (12.15(b), 12.24)

No DT is recognised on the initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction that is not a business combination, affects neither accounting nor taxable profit, and (from the 2023 amendment) does not give rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences. Example: a non-depreciable asset with no tax deduction ever (e.g., certain land acquired where cost will not be deductible). The 2023 amendment carved leases and decommissioning out of this exemption (s03).

Step-by-step method

  1. Take the balance sheet (standalone) at the reporting date.
  2. For each line, determine the tax base.
  3. Compute the temporary difference: for an asset, carrying amount above tax base is taxable (below is deductible); for a liability, carrying amount above tax base is deductible (below is taxable).
  4. Identify exemptions (goodwill, IRE, outside basis under 12.39/12.44).
  5. Multiply by the rate expected on reversal (c01).
  6. Assess DTA recoverability (12.24, 12.27–31).
  7. Allocate the movement between P&L, OCI and equity (12.58, 12.61A).
  8. Offset where permitted (12.74).

Pooja Instruments Ltd, 31 March 2026 (₹ lakh), 115BAA:

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Dual manner of recovery (use vs sale): tax base and rate depend on expected manner of recovery (12.51) — e.g., land held for use but expected to be sold (capital gains).
  • Assets whose recovery is not taxable (e.g., certain tax-exempt investments) — tax base equals carrying amount.
  • Items in tax computation but not in books — e.g., Sec 35D amortisation; MAT credit (treated as DTA).
  • Foreign branches — two tax bases may exist; the Indian tax base applies to Indian tax.
  • Revaluation of PPE (Ind AS 16 revaluation model) — DTL charged to OCI even without tax revaluation.

Key takeaways

  • Temporary difference = carrying amount vs tax base, for every asset and liability.
  • Taxable differences → DTL; deductible differences → DTA if probable.
  • Tax base of an asset = future deductions; of a liability = carrying amount less future deductions.
  • Permanent differences (CSR, penalties, exempt income) do not create DT — they sit in the ETR reconciliation.
  • Apply exemptions (goodwill, IRE, outside basis) deliberately and document them.

Practice Questions

Q1. Tax bases for a mixed balance sheet [L1]

Determine the tax base and temporary difference for: (a) machine carrying ₹40 lakh, tax WDV ₹28 lakh; (b) bonus provision ₹12 lakh to be paid in June 2026 before the return due date; (c) ECL allowance ₹6 lakh; (d) advance from customer ₹15 lakh where revenue is taxed on accrual; (e) leave encashment provision ₹9 lakh; (f) CSR expense ₹4 lakh paid.

💡 Hint: For (b), consider whether payment before the return due date under Sec 43B makes the deduction available in the current year.

Q2. DT table from a trial balance [L2]

From the following 31 March 2026 balances of a 115BAA company (₹ Cr), prepare the temporary-difference table and net DT: PPE 85 (tax WDV 64); ROU asset 12 and lease liability 13.5; gratuity 4.2; ECL 2.1; FVTPL mutual funds carrying 30, cost 26 (expected tax at 25.168% as debt MF gains are taxed at normal rates); unamortised loan fees 0.25; MSME dues outstanding beyond 45 days ₹0.8 Cr (expense booked).

Q3. Permanent vs temporary [L2]

Classify as temporary or permanent, giving reasons: penalty for GST late filing; interest under Sec 234B; provision for warranty; impairment of goodwill; depreciation on a revalued building; donation to a political party; 30% disallowance under Sec 40(a)(ia).

Q4. Interview question: AS 22 vs Ind AS 12 [L2]

Explain to an interviewer, with two examples, why the balance-sheet approach can produce DT where the AS 22 income-statement approach would not.
