Subtopic note

Section 115BAA: Rate Mechanics, Conditions and DT Remeasurement

Scope: Section 115BAA: 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess, no MAT, no specified deductions, irrevocable once opted; remeasure DT on switch

Why it matters

Section 115BAA, introduced by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act 2019 with effect from AY 2020-21, is now the default regime for most profitable Indian companies. Its interaction with deferred tax is significant: the switch from a 34.944% to a 25.168% effective rate remeasures every DT balance, MAT credit is forfeited, and losses or depreciation attributable to forgone incentives lapse. The timing of the switch is a tax-planning decision that drives financial reporting — the DT remeasurement is recognised when the decision is made, which may precede the year of the switch. FP&A and valuation models must reflect the regime in forecast ETR and cash taxes.

Core notes

The rate

\[ 22\% \times (1 + 10\%) \times (1 + 4\%) = 25.168\% \]

The 10% surcharge applies irrespective of the level of income; the health and education cess is 4%. Compare the normal regime for a domestic company:

Base rate Income ≤ ₹1 Cr ₹1–10 Cr (7% surcharge) > ₹10 Cr (12% surcharge)
30% 31.200% 33.384% 34.944%
25% (turnover/gross receipts ≤ ₹400 Cr in the specified earlier year) 26.000% 27.820% 29.120%
22% (115BAA) 25.168% 25.168% 25.168%

Capital gains are taxed at their special rates (Sec 111A, 112, 112A) with the 10% surcharge and 4% cess under 115BAA.

Conditions (Sec 115BAA(2))

Total income must be computed:

  • without deductions under Sec 10AA (SEZ units), 32(1)(iia) (additional depreciation), 32AD, 33AB, 33ABA, 35(1)(ii), (iia), (iii), 35(2AA), 35(2AB), 35AD, 35CCC, 35CCD, and Chapter VI-A deductions other than Sec 80JJAA, 80M (and, for IFSC units, 80LA(1A) as later allowed);
  • without set-off of any loss or allowance for unabsorbed depreciation carried forward from earlier years if attributable to the above deductions — such losses/depreciation are deemed to have been given full effect and cannot be carried forward (an adjustment to the WDV of the block is permitted for unabsorbed additional depreciation as prescribed);
  • with depreciation under Sec 32 (other than additional depreciation) as prescribed;
  • without set-off of losses from units covered by the above deductions.

No MAT, MAT credit lapses

Sec 115JB (MAT) does not apply to a company exercising the 115BAA option (115JB(5A)), and brought-forward MAT credit under Sec 115JAA cannot be set off. Under Ind AS the MAT credit asset is presented as a DTA (per ICAI guidance) and must be written off to P&L when the company decides to opt — a strong reason to defer opting until MAT credit is largely utilised.

Exercise of option and irrevocability

The option must be exercised in Form 10-IC on or before the due date for filing the return under Sec 139(1) for the first year of opting; once exercised it applies to subsequent years and cannot be withdrawn (115BAA(5)). If conditions are not satisfied in any year, the option becomes invalid for that year and subsequent years and the normal provisions apply.

Deciding when to switch

Model both regimes over the planning horizon:

Factor Pushes towards staying Pushes towards switching
Large MAT credit balance Utilise it first —
SEZ/80-IA type deductions still available Keep incentives Incentives expiring
Additional depreciation on heavy capex Keep for capex years Capex phase over
Losses attributable to incentives Lapse on switch Losses small
High profitability — Lower rate saves more

Deferred tax remeasurement

Ind AS 12.47 requires DT to be measured at rates expected to apply when the asset is realised or liability settled, based on rates enacted or substantively enacted. The 115BAA rate is enacted; which rate applies depends on the company's decision. ICAI's FAQ on 115BAA (2020) indicates that a company that has decided (or expects) to opt in a future year should measure DT reversing after the expected switch at the 115BAA rate. Practical steps:

  1. Identify the date of the decision (board approval of the tax plan is good evidence).
  2. Schedule reversal of temporary differences by year: those reversing before the switch at the old rate, those after at 25.168%.
  3. Remeasure; recognise the effect in P&L, except for DT relating to items originally recognised in OCI or equity, which follows the item (12.60, 12.61A — backward tracing).
  4. Write off MAT credit no longer usable.
  5. Reassess DTA on losses/unabsorbed depreciation that will lapse because they are attributable to forgone deductions.
  6. Disclose the rate change effect in the ETR reconciliation (12.81(c), (d)) and, for listed entities, in quarterly results (Ind AS 34 — recognise in the quarter of decision).

Illustration

Tanvi Steels Ltd, old regime, at 31 March 2026: temporary differences — PPE ₹80 Cr taxable, gratuity ₹10 Cr deductible (of which DT on ₹3 Cr originated in OCI), ECL ₹6 Cr deductible; MAT credit ₹7 Cr. In March 2026 the board decides to opt for 115BAA from FY 2026-27. All differences reverse after FY 2025-26.

Item Difference At 34.944% At 25.168% Change
PPE (DTL) 80 27.955 20.134 (7.821)
Gratuity (DTA) (10) (3.494) (2.517) 0.977
ECL (DTA) (6) (2.097) (1.510) 0.587
Net DTL 64 22.364 16.107 (6.257)

The net remeasurement is a ₹6.257 Cr reduction in the DTL, split by source. The ₹0.977 Cr reduction in the gratuity DTA includes ₹0.293 Cr (₹3 Cr × 9.776 percentage points) relating to the OCI-originated remeasurements; that part is charged to OCI. The P&L therefore receives a credit of ₹6.550 Cr and OCI a charge of ₹0.293 Cr, netting to ₹6.257 Cr. MAT credit write-off ₹7 Cr charged to P&L tax expense. Net P&L deferred tax effect: ₹0.45 Cr charge.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Decision not yet made but likely — if the switch is probable based on the tax plan, measure at the expected rate; document the basis.
  • Group with multiple entities — each entity decides separately; consolidated DT uses entity-specific rates.
  • Companies with SEZ units in their tax holiday — may stay in the old regime; units may get DTA/DTL on differences reversing after the holiday (12.47 reversal-period rate).
  • Losses attributable to additional depreciation lapse; partial lapse requires a computation of the attributable portion.
  • Interim periods — the remeasurement and MAT credit write-off are discrete items in the quarter of the decision, not spread through the annual effective rate.
  • Income-tax Act 2025 — the concessional regime continues under a new section number from 1 April 2026; update references (s08).

Key takeaways

  • 115BAA effective rate is 25.168% at all income levels; the top old-regime rate is 34.944%.
  • Opting removes MAT and MAT credit, additional depreciation, specified deductions and losses attributable to them.
  • The option is irrevocable and exercised by the return due date (Form 10-IC).
  • Remeasure DT to the rate expected on reversal once the company decides to switch; trace OCI/equity items back.
  • Write off MAT credit and lapsing loss DTAs; show the effect separately in the ETR reconciliation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Regime decision model [L3]

A company's projected taxable income (old regime, before incentives) is ₹40 Cr, ₹48 Cr and ₹55 Cr for the next three years; it has ₹9 Cr MAT credit, Sec 80-IA deduction of ₹10 Cr a year expiring after year 2, and no additional depreciation. Build a comparison of total tax under the old regime (with MAT) and under 115BAA from year 1, year 2 or year 3, and recommend the timing.

💡 Hint: Compute normal tax versus MAT each year and the MAT credit utilisation before comparing with 25.168%.

Q2. DT remeasurement with OCI items [L2]

At 31 March 2026 a company decides to opt for 115BAA from FY 2026-27. Its temporary differences are: PPE ₹45 Cr taxable; FVOCI equity gain ₹12 Cr (LTCG at 12.5%: 14.56% effective in the old regime with 12% surcharge, 14.3% under 115BAA); gratuity ₹8 Cr deductible (₹2 Cr OCI-originated). Compute the remeasurement and its split between P&L and OCI.

Q3. Failure of conditions [L1]

A 115BAA company inadvertently claims a Sec 35(2AB) deduction in its return. Explain the consequences under Sec 115BAA and the deferred tax implications.

Q4. Quarterly results presentation [L2]

A listed company decides in Q3 FY 2025-26 to opt for 115BAA from FY 2025-26 itself. Explain how the current tax, DT remeasurement and MAT credit write-off are presented in Q3 results and the year-to-date ETR.
