Subtopic note

Payment-Basis and Write-Off-Basis Deductions as Deferred Tax Assets

Scope: Payment/write-off-basis items: gratuity, leave, bonus (Sec 43B), MSME dues (Sec 43B(h)), ECL (Sec 36(1)(vii)), Sec 40(a)(ia) disallowances

Why it matters

After PPE, the most common Indian deferred tax items are expenses that the books recognise on accrual but the Income-tax Act allows only on payment or write-off. Employee benefit provisions, statutory dues, MSME payables, ECL allowances and TDS defaults together often create a DTA larger than any single item. Since 1 April 2023 (AY 2024-25) Sec 43B(h) has added a new, volatile item for companies buying from micro and small enterprises. The tax audit report (Form 3CD Clauses 21, 26) contains much of the data needed, so the DT working should reconcile to it. Distinguishing temporary items from those that become permanent (employee PF contributions deposited late) is a typical review point.

Core notes

The mechanism

Each item is a liability (provision, payable) or contra-asset (allowance) whose carrying amount reflects an expense already recognised in P&L, while the tax deduction comes later. Tax base of the liability = carrying amount − future deductions = nil (or the portion already allowed). This is a deductible temporary difference → DTA, recognised if probable (12.24).

Gratuity (Ind AS 19 defined benefit)

  • Unfunded gratuity: provision not deductible (Sec 40A(7)); deduction only on actual payment to employees. Tax base nil.
  • Funded via an approved gratuity fund (LIC/insurer): contributions are deductible under Sec 36(1)(v), subject to Sec 43B(b) (actual payment by the return due date). The net defined benefit liability (obligation less plan assets) has tax base nil, because future contributions will be deductible.
  • Split of movement: service cost and net interest go to P&L; remeasurements (actuarial gains/losses, return on plan assets excluding interest) go to OCI. The DT on remeasurements goes to OCI (12.61A) — see s07.

Leave encashment

Provision for leave encashment is deductible only on payment (Sec 43B(f)); the provision's constitutional validity was upheld by the Supreme Court in Union of India v. Exide Industries (2020). Tax base nil; DTA on the whole provision. Leave is usually an "other long-term employee benefit" under Ind AS 19 with remeasurements in P&L (not OCI), so the DT on leave goes entirely to P&L.

Bonus and other 43B items

Sec 43B allows certain sums only in the year of actual payment, but the first proviso allows the deduction in the year of accrual if paid on or before the due date of filing the return under Sec 139(1):

Clause Item
43B(a) Taxes, duties, cess, fees under any law
43B(b) Employer contributions to PF, superannuation, gratuity or other welfare funds
43B(c) Bonus or commission to employees
43B(d), (da), (e) Interest on loans from public financial institutions, state financial corporations, scheduled banks, co-operative banks, deposit-taking and systemically important non-deposit-taking NBFCs
43B(f) Leave encashment
43B(g) Payments to Indian Railways for use of railway assets
43B(h) Sums payable to micro or small enterprises beyond the time limit in Sec 15 MSMED Act

So a bonus provision at 31 March 2026 that is paid in September 2026 (before the 31 October 2026 return due date for audited companies) is deductible in FY 2025-26 — no temporary difference. If paid after the due date, it is deductible only in the year of payment — a temporary difference at the year-end. The DT working therefore depends on facts after year-end up to the return filing date; at the time of finalising accounts (May), estimate based on payments expected before the due date.

MSME dues — Sec 43B(h)

From AY 2024-25, any sum payable by the assessee to a micro or small enterprise (as defined in the MSMED Act 2006) beyond the time limit specified in Sec 15 of that Act — 15 days where there is no written agreement, and at most 45 days where there is one — is allowed as a deduction only in the year of actual payment. The first proviso (payment before the return due date) does not apply to 43B(h). Medium enterprises are not covered; traders are generally outside Sec 15 protection (Udyam registration of traders is for priority-sector lending purposes), but check facts.

DT effect: expenses booked for MSME purchases unpaid beyond the time limit at 31 March create a deductible temporary difference on the related payable portion (to the extent the expense is in P&L — for purchases of capitalised assets, the analysis follows the asset). Because companies now pay MSMEs before year-end to protect deductions, the balance may be small, but it must be tracked supplier-by-supplier using the MSME master and payment dates.

ECL and bad debts — Sec 36(1)(vii)

The ECL allowance is not deductible; bad debts are deductible only when written off as irrecoverable in the accounts (TRF Ltd, SC 2010). DTA on the allowance; reversal on write-off. ICDS does not change this.

Sec 40(a)(ia) — TDS defaults

30% of any sum payable to a resident on which tax is deductible but not deducted, or deducted but not paid by the return due date, is disallowed; the amount is allowed in the year in which the tax is deducted and paid (proviso). This is a temporary difference — a DTA, as the 30% becomes deductible later. For non-residents, Sec 40(a)(i) disallows 100% until the TDS is paid. Recognise DTA only if the company expects to deduct and deposit the TDS (otherwise permanent).

Illustration

Samar Pharma Ltd (115BAA), 31 March 2026 (₹ lakh):

Item Carrying Tax base Difference DTA at 25.168%
Unfunded gratuity 420 0 420 105.7
Leave encashment 180 0 180 45.3
Bonus (₹150 paid in Sept 2026, ₹30 to be paid in Dec 2026) 180 150 30 7.6
MSME payables beyond 45 days (revenue expenses) 65 0 65 16.4
ECL allowance 240 0 240 60.4
40(a)(ia): TDS not deducted on ₹200 professional fees; to be deducted and paid in FY 2026-27 (30% disallowed now) 60 0 60 15.1
Total 995 250.4

Note the 40(a)(ia) row: had the TDS been deducted in FY 2025-26 and deposited before the return due date, there would be no disallowance — again a post-balance-sheet fact to confirm before finalising. Because the TDS will be deducted and paid only in FY 2026-27, the 30% is disallowed in FY 2025-26 and allowed in FY 2026-27, a temporary difference.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Employee contributions to PF/ESI (deducted from salary) are income of the employer under Sec 2(24)(x) and deductible only if deposited by the due date under the PF/ESI Acts (Sec 36(1)(va)); after Checkmate Services (SC, 2022), late deposits are permanently disallowed — a permanent difference, not a DTA.
  • Gratuity paid to trust after year-end but before return due date — deductible in the current year; reduces the temporary difference.
  • Provision for warranties and onerous contracts — not deductible until incurred (Sec 37), unless scientifically estimated (Rotork Controls, SC 2009, allows warranty provisions made on a scientific basis) — DT depends on the tax position taken.
  • 43B on GST/customs dues unpaid at year-end — deductible on payment before due date.
  • Interest to banks converted into a loan (funded interest) is not "actual payment" (Explanation 3C to Sec 43B) — DTA until paid.
  • Income-tax Act 2025 renumbers these provisions from 1 April 2026; update references (s08).

Key takeaways

  • Payment- and write-off-basis items create deductible temporary differences → DTA, subject to recoverability.
  • Sec 43B items paid before the return due date create no temporary difference; 43B(h) MSME dues have no such grace.
  • Gratuity and leave provisions are deductible only on payment (40A(7), 43B(f)); gratuity remeasurement DT goes to OCI.
  • ECL allowance → DTA until write-off under Sec 36(1)(vii).
  • 40(a)(ia) disallowances reverse when TDS is paid; late employee PF deposits are permanent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Year-end DTA working [L2]

At 31 March 2026 a 115BAA company has: gratuity obligation ₹5.2 Cr (unfunded); leave provision ₹2.1 Cr; bonus payable ₹1.6 Cr (₹1.2 Cr paid on 20 October 2026, ₹0.4 Cr in January 2027; return due 31 October 2026); MSME dues outstanding beyond 45 days ₹0.9 Cr (expenses) paid in April 2026; ECL allowance ₹3.3 Cr; professional fees of ₹1 Cr on which TDS was not deducted (to be deducted and paid in FY 2026-27). Compute the DTA.

💡 Hint: Check each item for whether payment before the return due date restores the deduction in FY 2025-26.

Q2. MSME tracking design [L2]

Design an Excel or Power Query control that identifies, at each quarter-end, MSME payables at risk under Sec 43B(h), and feeds both the tax computation and the deferred tax schedule. Specify inputs, logic and outputs.

Q3. Funded gratuity split [L2]

A company's funded gratuity plan has obligation ₹12 Cr and plan assets ₹9 Cr at year-end (opening: ₹10 Cr and ₹8 Cr). Current service cost ₹1.1 Cr, net interest ₹0.15 Cr, actuarial loss ₹0.9 Cr, return on plan assets excluding interest ₹0.1 Cr gain, contributions ₹0.8 Cr, benefits paid ₹0.6 Cr from the fund. Compute the DT movement and split it between P&L and OCI at 25.168%.

Q4. Permanent or temporary? [L1]

Classify: (a) employees' PF contribution deposited 10 days late; (b) employer's PF contribution deposited after the due date under the PF Act but before the return due date; (c) TDS on rent deducted in March but deposited in November after the return due date; (d) interest to a scheduled bank converted into a funded interest term loan.
