Subtopic note

DTA on Tax Losses and Unabsorbed Depreciation (Ind AS 12.34–36)

Scope: Losses and unabsorbed depreciation: DTA only if probable future taxable profit (12.34–36); 8-year loss carry-forward, unabsorbed depreciation indefinite

Why it matters

DTA on tax losses is the most judgemental deferred tax item and the one most often challenged by auditors, NFRA and analysts. Start-ups, turnaround companies, newly commissioned plants and companies emerging from IBC frequently carry large losses. Recognising a DTA boosts net worth and reduces the reported loss; derecognising it later creates a large tax charge. Indian law distinguishes business losses (8 years) from unabsorbed depreciation (unlimited), which changes the recoverability analysis. Financial models need the loss schedule to forecast cash taxes.

Core notes

Recognition criteria (Ind AS 12.34–36)

A DTA is recognised for the carry-forward of unused tax losses and unused tax credits to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profit will be available against which they can be utilised (12.34). The criteria are the same as for deductible temporary differences, but 12.35 adds: the existence of unused tax losses is strong evidence that future taxable profit may not be available. Therefore, when an entity has a history of recent losses, it recognises a DTA arising from unused losses only to the extent that it has sufficient taxable temporary differences or there is convincing other evidence that sufficient taxable profit will be available.

12.36 lists the criteria to assess probability:

  1. Whether the entity has sufficient taxable temporary differences relating to the same taxation authority and the same taxable entity, which will result in taxable amounts against which the losses can be utilised before they expire.
  2. Whether it is probable that the entity will have taxable profits before the losses expire.
  3. Whether the unused losses result from identifiable causes which are unlikely to recur.
  4. Whether tax planning opportunities (12.30) are available that will create taxable profit in the period in which the losses can be utilised.

To the extent it is not probable, the DTA is not recognised; the unrecognised amount is disclosed (12.81(e)) and reassessed each reporting date (12.37).

Indian carry-forward rules

Item Provision Carry-forward Set-off
Business loss (non-speculative) Sec 72 8 assessment years following the loss year Against business income only; return must be filed by due date (Sec 80)
Speculation loss Sec 73 4 years Speculation income only
Unabsorbed depreciation Sec 32(2) Indefinite Against any head (other than salary) in subsequent years; added to the next year's depreciation allowance
Long-term capital loss Sec 74 8 years LTCG only
Short-term capital loss Sec 74 8 years STCG or LTCG
Loss of specified business (Sec 35AD) Sec 73A Indefinite Only against specified business income

Order of set-off: current-year depreciation, then brought-forward business loss, then unabsorbed depreciation (Sec 72(2) with 32(2)) — so business losses, which expire, are used before unabsorbed depreciation.

Change in shareholding (Sec 79): for a closely held company, brought-forward business losses lapse if shares carrying at least 51% of voting power are not beneficially held by the same persons on the last day of the loss year and of the set-off year (exceptions for start-ups and IBC resolution plans approved under Sec 79(2)(c)). Unabsorbed depreciation is not affected. A planned stake sale must be considered in the DTA assessment.

115BAA interplay: losses and unabsorbed depreciation attributable to forgone deductions (e.g., additional depreciation) lapse on opting (s05).

Amalgamation (Sec 72A): accumulated loss and unabsorbed depreciation of an amalgamating company may be carried forward by the amalgamated company if conditions are met (industrial undertaking, continuity of business and assets).

Evidence assessment

Positive evidence Negative evidence
Firm orders/contracts (e.g., long-term offtake agreements) History of losses in recent years
Taxable temporary differences (PPE DTL) reversing in the loss window Losses expiring unused in the past
Loss due to an identifiable non-recurring cause (one-off write-off, start-up phase of a plant) Going-concern uncertainty
Tax planning: sale of appreciated assets, change in depreciation claims (claim lower depreciation to shift deductions) Industry downturn, unprofitable business model
Recent profitable quarters and approved budgets with a track record of meeting them Forecasts repeatedly missed

Illustration

Uma Textiles Ltd (115BAA) at 31 March 2026: business losses ₹40 Cr (₹15 Cr usable up to AY 2029-30, i.e., FY 2028-29; ₹25 Cr up to AY 2033-34); unabsorbed depreciation ₹30 Cr; taxable temporary difference on PPE ₹20 Cr reversing evenly over 10 years. Forecast taxable profits before using losses and excluding the reversal of the existing PPE difference (to avoid double counting with 12.28): ₹4 Cr, ₹6 Cr, ₹8 Cr, ₹10 Cr in the next four years, management considers years beyond four not reliably forecastable. Recent history: losses in 3 of the last 4 years, mainly due to a demerged loss-making division (identifiable, non-recurring).

Analysis:

  • Forecast profits over 4 years ₹28 Cr; business losses are used first. ₹15 Cr of near-expiry loss is used in years 1–3 (cumulative profits ₹18 Cr by year 3). Remaining ₹13 Cr of the 4-year profit absorbs part of the ₹25 Cr later-expiring loss.
  • PPE DTL reversal provides ₹2 Cr a year of taxable amounts; within the expiry window of the later losses (8 years), ₹16 Cr; this supports further utilisation.
  • Unabsorbed depreciation (indefinite) can be supported by PPE DTL reversals beyond the forecast horizon.

A reasonable conclusion: recognise DTA on ₹28 Cr of losses supported by forecasts plus ₹12 Cr of remaining loss supported by DTL reversals within the expiry window (₹40 Cr total, ₹10.07 Cr DTA), and on unabsorbed depreciation only to the extent of DTL reversals not already used (here ₹20 Cr total DTL reversal less ₹12 Cr used = ₹8 Cr → ₹2.01 Cr DTA). Unrecognised: unabsorbed depreciation ₹22 Cr (₹5.54 Cr), disclosed. The 115BAA and Sec 79 facts must be checked.

Measurement

DTA on losses uses the rate expected when the losses are utilised (12.47) — 25.168% for 115BAA companies. It is not discounted (12.53). Losses taxed at special rates (capital losses) use the relevant capital gains rate.

Presentation and disclosure

Ind AS 12.82 requires disclosure of the DTA amount and the nature of evidence supporting its recognition when utilisation depends on future taxable profits in excess of DTL reversals and the entity has suffered a loss in the current or preceding period. Disclose unrecognised losses with expiry dates (12.81(e)).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Start-ups with Sec 80-IAC or Sec 79 relaxation — check eligibility, but a history of losses usually prevents DTA recognition beyond DTL reversals.
  • Group relief does not exist in India — losses of one company cannot shelter profits of another; consolidated DTA must be assessed entity by entity.
  • IBC acquisitions — resolution plans may waive liabilities (taxable under Sec 28/41 unless exempt) and Sec 79 relief applies; DTA analysis must reflect the post-resolution tax position, and Sec 115JB relief for IBC companies.
  • Tax planning opportunities must be feasible and within management's control; selling a profitable division only to use losses may qualify if management would do so.
  • MAT credit under the old regime is a tax credit with a 15-year limit; assess recoverability the same way.
  • Interim periods — reassess DTA recognition when circumstances change; changes are usually recognised in the quarter of change.

Key takeaways

  • DTA on losses only to the extent probable; recent losses demand convincing evidence (12.35).
  • Use the 12.36 criteria: DTL reversals, forecast profits before expiry, non-recurring causes, tax planning.
  • Business losses expire after 8 years (Sec 72); unabsorbed depreciation is indefinite (Sec 32(2)); set-off order uses business losses first.
  • Sec 79 shareholding change and 115BAA switch can make losses lapse.
  • Disclose unrecognised losses with expiry and the evidence supporting recognised DTAs (12.81(e), 12.82).

Practice Questions

Q1. DTA recognition analysis [L3]

A 115BAA company has business losses of ₹60 Cr (₹20 Cr expiring in 2 years, ₹40 Cr in 6 years) and unabsorbed depreciation of ₹25 Cr. Its PPE DTL arises from ₹30 Cr of taxable temporary differences reversing over 6 years. Board-approved forecasts show taxable profits of ₹5 Cr, ₹9 Cr and ₹14 Cr over the next three years; the company has missed its budget in each of the last three years. Determine the DTA to recognise and the disclosures, explaining your use of 12.35–36.

💡 Hint: Start with DTL reversals, then consider how much weight the forecasts can bear given the track record and expiry profile.

Q2. Shareholding change [L2]

A closely held company with ₹18 Cr brought-forward business losses and ₹12 Cr unabsorbed depreciation is acquired by a new investor who buys 70% of the shares in FY 2025-26. Explain the effect on the DTA under Sec 79 and Ind AS 12.

Q3. Set-off order [L1]

A company has FY 2025-26 business income of ₹10 Cr before depreciation, current-year depreciation ₹4 Cr, brought-forward business loss ₹5 Cr (expiring this year) and unabsorbed depreciation ₹8 Cr. Show the set-off order and closing carry-forward balances.

Q4. Derecognition of DTA [L2]

A company recognised ₹15 Cr of DTA on losses at 31 March 2025. In FY 2025-26 a key customer contract is cancelled and forecasts fall by half. Explain how the DTA is reassessed, where the reversal is recognised, and how this appears in the ETR reconciliation.
