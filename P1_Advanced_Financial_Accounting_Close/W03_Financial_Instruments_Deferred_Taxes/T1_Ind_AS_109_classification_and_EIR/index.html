Roadmap topic: Ind AS 109 Financial Instruments Classification: Business model & SPPI test for Amortized Cost, FVOCI, and FVTPL; Effective Interest Rate (EIR) loan amortization with processing fees

Overview

Ind AS 109 decides two things for every financial asset and liability: which measurement basis applies (amortised cost, fair value through OCI, or fair value through profit or loss) and, for amortised-cost instruments, how interest is recognised over time through the effective interest rate. For a debt asset the answer comes from two tests applied in order — the business model in which the asset is managed (Ind AS 109.4.1.1(a)) and whether its contractual cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal outstanding (SPPI, 4.1.1(b)). Equity instruments default to FVTPL with an irrevocable FVOCI election for non-trading holdings (5.7.5). Financial liabilities are at amortised cost unless held for trading, designated under the fair value option, or caught by specific exceptions (4.2.1).

For the target roles this is daily work. A technical-accounting analyst classifies treasury investments (liquid funds, bonds, CCDs, strategic stakes) and documents the conclusions for auditors. A reporting accountant builds the EIR schedule for every term loan, NCD and ECB, and makes sure processing fees and stamp duty are not expensed on day one. An FP&A or modelling analyst uses the same schedule to forecast finance cost and debt balances, and must understand why reported finance cost exceeds the coupon. Classification errors move gains between P&L and OCI, change EBITDA-adjacent metrics that lenders test, and create deferred tax differences.

The learning sequence follows the instrument's life. Start with the classification flow (s01) and the four measurement categories with the fair value option (s02); then study the common SPPI failures in Indian portfolios (s03) and the liability side including Ind AS 32 debt/equity splits (s04). Move to initial measurement and day-1 differences (s05), the inputs and revisions of the EIR (s06), what happens when a loan is restructured or refinanced (s07), and finally the narrow reclassification rule (s08). The six key concepts give the formulas and entries you will reuse: the EIR definition and Excel method, the amortised-cost roll-forward, net-of-fee initial recognition, interest-free intra-group loans, the 10% test and the floating-versus-fixed revision rule.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 32 convertibles and Ind AS 107 disclosures

Ind AS 32 split of convertibles into liability and equity components; Ind AS 107 disclosures → w3_t6

For the issuer, a convertible bond that converts into a fixed number of shares for a fixed amount is a compound instrument. Ind AS 32.31–32 require the liability component to be measured first at the fair value of a similar non-convertible bond (present value of coupons and principal at the market rate for straight debt), with the residual going to equity; the equity component is never remeasured. Transaction costs are allocated in proportion to the two components. The liability then runs on an EIR schedule exactly like a term loan. Ind AS 107 requires disclosure of carrying amounts by category (107.8), fair values (107.25), and for compound instruments with multiple embedded derivatives, the existence of those features. Week 3 t6 builds the disclosure tables from the classification decisions made here.

US GAAP debt and equity securities comparison

US GAAP: ASC 320 debt securities (HTM/AFS/trading), ASC 321 equity at FVNI, debt issuance costs deducted from liability

US GAAP classifies debt securities by intent rather than business model and SPPI: held-to-maturity (amortised cost, requires positive intent and ability, with tainting on sales), available-for-sale (fair value through OCI) and trading (fair value through net income). There is no SPPI test for securities; embedded derivatives are bifurcated under ASC 815 instead. ASC 321 requires equity securities with readily determinable fair values to be measured at fair value through net income, with no OCI election (a measurement alternative exists for equity without readily determinable fair value). Since ASU 2015-03, debt issuance costs are presented as a direct deduction from the debt liability and amortised as interest — the same economic result as Ind AS 109's net-of-fees EIR. Indian subsidiaries of US groups often maintain both views in group reporting packs.

Tax on EIR interest and Sec 94B

Tax: interest deductible per contractual accrual, not EIR → temporary difference (w3_t3); Sec 94B interest limitation on non-resident AE debt

The Income-tax Act allows interest under Sec 36(1)(iii) on the contractual accrual basis (subject to Sec 43B for interest payable to banks and specified lenders), and an upfront processing fee is generally "interest" under Sec 2(28A) and deductible when incurred. Books spread the fee over the loan life through EIR. The borrowing's carrying amount (net of unamortised fee) is therefore lower than its tax base (gross principal), giving a taxable temporary difference that unwinds as the fee is amortised — see w3_t3. Separately, Sec 94B restricts deduction of interest paid or payable to a non-resident associated enterprise (or debt guaranteed by one) to 30% of EBITDA where such interest exceeds ₹1 crore; disallowed interest carries forward for eight assessment years. A Sec 94B carry-forward is a deductible temporary difference whose DTA requires a probability assessment.

Financing cash flows for transaction costs and interest

→ w1_t6: transaction costs and interest in financing cash flows

In the Ind AS 7 cash flow statement, proceeds from borrowings are reported as a financing inflow. Transaction costs paid to obtain the borrowing are also financing cash flows; many Indian companies present proceeds net of fees because the bank disburses net. Interest paid can be classified as operating or financing for non-financial entities (Ind AS 7.33), but the classification must be consistent and most listed Indian companies present it in financing. The non-cash fee unwind in finance cost is added back in the indirect-method operating section (it is included in finance cost, which is added back in full and then the cash interest is shown in financing). Ind AS 7.44A–44E require a reconciliation of liabilities from financing activities, in which the EIR unwind appears as a non-cash movement.

Excel XIRR, day-count and zero-balance check

Excel: XIRR with irregular dates, actual/365 day-count, check that amortised cost = 0 at maturity

XIRR computes the annual rate for irregular dates assuming actual/365 compounding, which matches a schedule where interest for each period is computed as opening × \( ((1+\text{EIR})^{d/365} - 1) \). If you instead compute simple interest as opening × EIR × d/365, the schedule will not close exactly to zero; use the compounding form or convert XIRR to a periodic rate. Always add a check cell that the final closing balance is zero (within ₹1), that total interest equals total contractual cash outflow minus net proceeds, and that the cash columns reconcile to the loan agreement's repayment schedule.

=XIRR(CashFlows, Dates)
=Opening*((1+EIR)^((ThisDate-PrevDate)/365)-1)
=ABS(INDEX(Closing,ROWS(Closing)))<1

Common pitfalls

Expensing processing fees upfront

Expensing processing fees upfront instead of including them in EIR

The mistake is debiting finance cost (or "bank charges") with the processing fee, stamp duty and legal fees on drawdown. It happens because Tally/ERP books the fee from the bank statement and the tax treatment allows an immediate deduction. Ind AS 109 B5.4.1–B5.4.3 treat fees that are an integral part of generating the instrument as part of the EIR; the borrowing is recognised net (5.1.1) and the fee unwinds through finance cost over the expected life. Detect it in review by comparing the loan's carrying amount with the sanction letter principal: a carrying amount equal to principal on a loan that carried fees indicates the fees were expensed. Also scan the bank charges ledger for large one-off debits around drawdown dates.

FVOCI election on mutual fund units

Applying FVOCI-equity election to mutual fund units (puttables fail equity definition for the holder)

Companies park surplus cash in debt or equity mutual funds and, wanting to keep volatility out of P&L, elect FVOCI as if the units were equity. But the 5.7.5 election is available only for investments in equity instruments as defined in Ind AS 32. Mutual fund units are puttable (the holder can redeem at NAV), and the puttable exception in Ind AS 32.16A–16B applies only to the issuer's classification. For the holder the units are debt-type instruments whose cash flows are not SPPI, so they are measured at FVTPL. Detect it by listing all FVOCI-equity holdings and checking each is an ordinary share or a genuine equity instrument; any MF, AIF unit or REIT/InvIT unit needs scrutiny.

Treating every interest reset as a modification

Treating every interest reset as a modification

Floating-rate loans linked to MCLR, repo or T-bill benchmarks reset periodically under their original terms. Some preparers run a 10% test on each reset, or rebuild the schedule as a new loan. A reset under existing contractual terms is not a modification; B5.4.5 requires the EIR to be re-estimated prospectively to reflect the new market rate, with no gain or loss. A modification is a change in contractual terms negotiated between the parties (tenor, spread, principal, covenants). Detect it by checking whether the schedule shows modification gains/losses in periods where only benchmark resets occurred, and by reading the loan agreement for whether the change was contractual.

Charging coupon instead of EIR interest

Charging coupon instead of EIR interest to P&L

The finance cost booked monthly from the bank's interest debit advice equals the coupon, not EIR interest. If fees were correctly netted from the liability but the fee unwind is never booked, the liability never accretes back to principal and finance cost is understated each year until a large catch-up on repayment. Correct treatment is a monthly or quarterly entry Dr Finance cost (EIR interest) Cr Borrowings for the difference between EIR interest and coupon. Detect it by testing whether the closing carrying amount on the EIR schedule matches the ledger and whether the ledger balance converges to principal over time.

Practice Questions

Q1. Treasury portfolio classification and measurement memo [L3]

Kaveri Chemicals Ltd holds at 31 March 2026: (i) ₹25 Cr of 7.1% G-secs held by the treasury desk, which sells about 30% of holdings each year to fund capex and maintain liquidity; (ii) ₹12 Cr in a liquid mutual fund; (iii) ₹8 Cr of CCDs of an unlisted start-up convertible at a fixed ratio after 3 years; (iv) a 4% stake in a listed supplier held for strategic reasons, cost ₹5 Cr, fair value ₹7.4 Cr; (v) an interest-free loan of ₹10 Cr to a wholly owned subsidiary, repayable in 5 years, market rate 10%. For each, determine the business model and SPPI conclusion (where relevant), the measurement category, the initial-recognition entry and where subsequent gains/losses go. Prepare a one-page memo suitable for the statutory auditor.

Q2. Term loan with fees, moratorium and refinancing [L3]

Navya Foods Ltd drew a ₹20 Cr term loan on 1 April 2025 at 10% p.a., interest payable annually on 31 March, principal repayable in four equal annual instalments from 31 March 2027. Processing fee 0.75% and stamp duty ₹10 lakh were paid on drawdown. On 1 April 2027, after the first instalment, the bank agreed to reduce the rate to 8.5% and extend the remaining tenor by two years for a fee of ₹12 lakh. (a) Compute the EIR and prepare the amortised-cost schedule to 31 March 2027. (b) Apply the 10% test on 1 April 2027. (c) Pass the entries for the restructuring under your conclusion. (d) State the current and non-current classification at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: Discount the revised cash flows and the new fee at the original EIR, not the new rate, for the 10% test.

Q3. Preference shares and compound instruments for the issuer [L2]

Ruchi Retail Ltd issued on 1 April 2025: (a) 10 lakh 8% cumulative redeemable preference shares of ₹100 each, redeemable at par in 5 years; (b) 5 lakh 0% CCPS of ₹100 each, each converting into 2 equity shares after 3 years; (c) ₹50 Cr of 6% convertible debentures, convertible at the holder's option into a fixed number of shares after 4 years, market rate for similar non-convertible debt 9.5%. Classify each under Ind AS 32, compute the liability and equity components where relevant, and prepare the FY 2025-26 finance cost entries.

Q4. Interview: explaining finance cost above coupon [L2]

The CFO of a listed NBFC asks why reported finance cost on a ₹500 Cr NCD issue is ₹45.6 Cr when the coupon is 8.5%, and whether the EIR model should be rebuilt each quarter because the repo rate changed twice. Write the answer you would give in an interview, covering EIR, transaction costs, the difference between a fixed-rate NCD and a repo-linked bank loan, and the related deferred tax consequence.
