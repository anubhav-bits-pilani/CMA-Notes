Subtopic note

Financial Liabilities and Ind AS 32 Debt vs Equity Classification

Scope: Liabilities: amortised cost vs FVTPL, own credit in OCI under FVO; Ind AS 32 debt/equity for preference shares, CCPS, compound instruments

Why it matters

Indian companies fund themselves with term loans, NCDs, redeemable preference shares, CCPS issued to private equity investors, and convertible debentures. Whether each is debt or equity changes net worth, debt-equity ratios tested in loan covenants, the finance cost line, and ROE. Ind AS 32 often overrides legal form: redeemable preference shares that are "share capital" under the Companies Act are financial liabilities under Ind AS, and CCPS with variable conversion may also be liabilities. Schedule III requires the Ind AS classification to be reflected, so a company's balance-sheet equity can differ sharply from its paid-up capital under company law.

Core notes

Classification of financial liabilities under Ind AS 109

Ind AS 109.4.2.1 requires all financial liabilities to be measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method, except:

  • financial liabilities at FVTPL — held for trading (including derivatives that are liabilities) or designated under the FVO;
  • liabilities arising when a transfer of a financial asset does not qualify for derecognition or under continuing involvement;
  • financial guarantee contracts and below-market loan commitments (measured at the higher of ECL and the amount initially recognised less income recognised);
  • contingent consideration of an acquirer in a business combination (FVTPL).

Unlike assets, hybrid liabilities with non-closely-related embedded derivatives are bifurcated (4.3.3): the host liability stays at amortised cost and the derivative goes to FVTPL — unless the whole hybrid is designated at FVTPL (4.3.5).

FVO for liabilities and own credit in OCI

A liability may be designated at FVTPL at initial recognition when (4.2.2): it eliminates or significantly reduces an accounting mismatch; a group of liabilities (or assets and liabilities) is managed and evaluated on a fair value basis under documented risk management; or the contract contains embedded derivatives (4.3.5).

For a liability designated under the FVO, the change in fair value attributable to changes in the entity's own credit risk is presented in OCI, and the remainder in P&L (5.7.7), unless that treatment would create or enlarge an accounting mismatch in P&L (5.7.8). OCI amounts are never recycled to P&L; they may be transferred within equity (B5.7.9). The logic: a company whose credit deteriorates should not report a gain in profit because the fair value of its own debt falls.

Own credit change is typically measured as the change in fair value not attributable to changes in market conditions (benchmark rates) — B5.7.18 describes a default method using the instrument's IRR less the observed benchmark rate.

Ind AS 32: the debt/equity principle

An instrument is a financial liability if it contains a contractual obligation to deliver cash or another financial asset, or to exchange financial instruments under potentially unfavourable conditions, or will or may be settled in the entity's own equity instruments and is a non-derivative obligating delivery of a variable number of own shares, or a derivative other than fixed-for-fixed (Ind AS 32.11). It is equity only if none of these apply (32.16). Substance governs over legal form (32.15, 32.18). The issuer's ability to avoid paying cash is the test — economic compulsion or intentions do not create a liability (32.AG26 for dividends at the issuer's discretion).

Under the Companies Act (Sec 55) preference shares must be redeemed within 20 years (30 years for infrastructure projects), so most Indian preference shares are liabilities in substance. Dividends on liability-classified preference shares are interest in P&L (32.35) but remain "dividends" for company law and tax (no deduction; no TDS as interest).

CCPS

Compulsorily convertible preference shares, common in PE deals, convert into equity shares. Classification depends on the conversion ratio and dividend terms:

  • Fixed number of equity shares, discretionary or nil dividends → equity in full (fixed-for-fixed).
  • Fixed number of shares, cumulative mandatory dividend → compound: PV of mandatory dividends = liability; residual = equity.
  • Variable number of shares (e.g., conversion ratio adjusts to a future valuation or IPO price so the holder receives a fixed value — down-round protection, IRR-based ratchet) → financial liability because the issuer delivers a variable number of shares worth a fixed amount (32.11(b)(i)). The conversion feature may be an embedded derivative.
  • Investor put options or exit rights (e.g., promoter or company must buy back if no IPO by a date) → liability if the company has an obligation it cannot avoid (32.25 contingent settlement provisions).

Compound instruments

A compound instrument (e.g., a convertible debenture converting into a fixed number of shares) is split at initial recognition (32.28–32.32):

  1. Measure the liability component at the fair value of a similar liability without the conversion option — PV of contractual cash flows at the market rate for straight debt.
  2. Equity component = Proceeds − Liability component (residual).
  3. Allocate transaction costs in proportion to the allocation of proceeds (32.38).
  4. The liability is subsequently at amortised cost; the equity component is not remeasured.
  5. On conversion, the liability carrying amount transfers to equity; no gain or loss (32.AG32). On early redemption or repurchase, allocate consideration between components using fair values at that date (32.AG33–AG34).

Illustration. Kiran Infra Ltd issues ₹50 Cr of 5% convertible debentures on 1 April 2025, maturing in 4 years, convertible at the holder's option into 25 lakh shares. Market rate for similar non-convertible debt is 10%.

\[ \text{Liability} = 2.5 \times \frac{1-(1.10)^{-4}}{0.10} + \frac{50}{(1.10)^{4}} = 7.92 + 34.15 = 42.07 \text{ Cr} \]

Equity component = ₹50 − 42.07 = ₹7.93 Cr. FY 2025-26 interest = 10% × 42.07 = ₹4.21 Cr; cash coupon ₹2.50 Cr; closing liability ₹43.78 Cr.

Presentation

Schedule III Division II places liability-classified preference shares and the liability component of convertibles within Borrowings, with the note disclosing terms and rights. The equity component sits in Other equity ("Equity component of compound financial instruments"). The Statement of Changes in Equity shows it separately.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bonus-style conversion at fair value at IPO is variable-number settlement — a liability, even if conversion is "compulsory".
  • Contingent settlement on events outside both parties' control (change in law, IPO not achieved) generally creates a liability unless the contingency is not genuine or arises only on liquidation (32.25).
  • Foreign-currency convertible bonds (FCCBs) with a fixed number of shares for a fixed foreign-currency amount would fail fixed-for-fixed under IAS 32 (conversion option a derivative liability at FVTPL). Ind AS 32.11 contains a carve-out: an equity conversion option embedded in a foreign-currency convertible bond to acquire a fixed number of own equity instruments for a fixed amount in any currency is classified as equity. Groups reporting under both frameworks need a reconciling adjustment.
  • Group perspective: an instrument that is equity in the subsidiary's books may be a liability in consolidation if the parent guarantees redemption.
  • Own credit gains in OCI on FVO liabilities are rare in India outside banks, but a structured NCD issued by an NBFC and designated FVTPL needs the split.

Key takeaways

  • Liabilities default to amortised cost; embedded derivatives in liabilities may be separated.
  • Under the FVO, own-credit fair value changes go to OCI and never recycle.
  • Ind AS 32 classifies by contractual obligation to deliver cash or a variable number of shares, not by legal form.
  • Most Indian redeemable preference shares are liabilities; CCPS are equity only if fixed-for-fixed and without mandatory dividends.
  • Compound instruments: liability at PV of straight-debt cash flows, residual to equity, DTL on the split charged to equity.
  • Covenant definitions of "debt" and "net worth" must be read against the Ind AS classification.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compound instrument split and schedule [L2]

On 1 April 2025 Devika Cements Ltd issues ₹80 Cr of 6% convertible debentures with annual coupons, maturing on 31 March 2030, convertible at the holder's option into a fixed number of shares. Market rate for similar straight debt is 9.5%. Transaction costs are ₹1.2 Cr. Compute the liability and equity components, allocate costs, compute the EIR of the liability, prepare the schedule for two years and pass the deferred tax entry on initial recognition at 25.168%.

💡 Hint: Allocate transaction costs pro rata to the components before computing the liability's EIR.

Q2. CCPS classification for a PE deal [L3]

A PE fund subscribes ₹200 Cr of CCPS in Zara Health Pvt Ltd. Terms: 0.001% non-cumulative dividend; conversion after 5 years at a ratio that ensures the fund receives shares worth at least a 15% IRR, based on the IPO price or a valuation; if no IPO by year 6, the promoters (not the company) must buy the shares. Analyse the classification in the company's separate financial statements and in the promoter-controlled consolidated group.

Q3. Preference shares under company law and Ind AS [L1]

A company has ₹25 Cr of 8% cumulative preference shares redeemable in 2031. Explain where they appear in the Schedule III balance sheet, how the dividend is presented in P&L, and why the treatment for income-tax purposes differs.

Q4. Own credit under the FVO [L2]

An NBFC designated a ₹100 Cr structured NCD at FVTPL. During the year its fair value fell from ₹100 Cr to ₹94 Cr; benchmark rate changes explain ₹2.5 Cr of the fall. Pass the entries and explain the presentation, and when the 5.7.8 exception would apply.
