Subtopic note

Reclassification of Financial Assets on Business Model Change

Scope: Reclassification only on change in business model — prospective and rare

Why it matters

Management sometimes wants to "move" an investment between categories — to stop FVTPL volatility after a market fall, or to lock in amortised cost for a bond book. Ind AS 109 permits reclassification only in a narrow circumstance, and never for liabilities or equity elections. Auditors treat any reclassification as a high-risk area because it can be used to manage earnings. Understanding the rule, the mechanics on the reclassification date and the disclosures helps you push back on such requests and correctly handle the genuine cases, such as an NBFC exiting a business line or a corporate winding down its treasury desk.

Core notes

The rule

When, and only when, an entity changes its business model for managing financial assets, it reclassifies all affected financial assets (Ind AS 109.4.4.1). Financial liabilities are never reclassified (4.4.2). Changes in SPPI conclusions do not cause reclassification because SPPI is assessed only at initial recognition (a contractual change that results in derecognition creates a new asset, which is classified afresh).

Business model changes are expected to be very infrequent. They are determined by senior management as a result of external or internal changes, must be significant to the entity's operations and demonstrable to external parties (B4.4.1). Examples:

  • An entity acquires, disposes of or terminates a business line — e.g., a corporate shuts its treasury trading desk and transfers the bond portfolio to a hold-to-collect liquidity book; or an NBFC acquires a company whose loan portfolio was managed for sale and now manages it to collect.
  • A financial services entity decides to stop originating loans for securitisation and to hold them instead.

What is not a change in business model (B4.4.3)

  • A change in intention related to particular financial assets, even in circumstances of significant changes in market conditions.
  • The temporary disappearance of a particular market for financial assets.
  • A transfer of financial assets between parts of the entity with different business models.

So a decision to "hold for now" a bond portfolio because the market has fallen is not a business model change.

Timing: the reclassification date

The reclassification date is the first day of the first reporting period following the change in business model (Appendix A). For an entity reporting quarterly under SEBI LODR Reg 33, a change decided in August 2025 (Q2 of FY 2025-26) leads to reclassification on 1 October 2025. The entity must not engage in activities consistent with the old business model after the date of change (B4.4.2).

Prospective application (5.6.1)

Reclassification is applied prospectively from the reclassification date. Previously recognised gains, losses (including impairment) or interest are not restated.

From → To Measurement on reclassification date Effect
Amortised cost → FVTPL (5.6.2) Fair value at reclassification date Difference between amortised cost and fair value to P&L
FVTPL → Amortised cost (5.6.3) Fair value becomes new gross carrying amount EIR determined on reclassification date; ECL from that date as initial recognition
Amortised cost → FVOCI (5.6.4) Fair value Difference to OCI; EIR and ECL unchanged
FVOCI → Amortised cost (5.6.5) Fair value, with cumulative OCI removed from equity and adjusted against the fair value As if always at amortised cost; EIR and ECL unchanged
FVTPL → FVOCI (5.6.6) Fair value EIR set on reclassification date
FVOCI → FVTPL (5.6.7) Fair value Cumulative OCI reclassified to P&L

The FVOCI-equity election and FVO designations are irrevocable; they cannot be reclassified.

Illustration

Hemant Tyres Ltd's treasury desk managed ₹80 Cr of corporate bonds on a fair value basis (FVTPL). In September 2025 the board resolves to close the desk and hold the bonds to fund a 2028 capex programme, collecting coupons. The change is approved, communicated to the lenders and evidenced by the desk's closure. Reclassification date: 1 October 2025 (quarterly reporting).

On 1 October 2025 the fair value is ₹77.6 Cr. The bonds become amortised cost with gross carrying amount ₹77.6 Cr; the EIR is the yield that discounts remaining contractual cash flows to ₹77.6 Cr; 12-month ECL is recognised as if on initial recognition (Stage 1). Fair value changes recognised in P&L up to 30 September 2025 stay in P&L.

Deferred tax: carrying amount ₹77.6 Cr vs tax base (cost) say ₹80 Cr remains a deductible temporary difference, now reversing through EIR accretion rather than sale; reassess the rate and manner of recovery (Ind AS 12.51).

Disclosures (Ind AS 107.12B–12D)

For each reclassification: the date, a detailed explanation of the change in business model and a qualitative description of its effect on the financial statements, and the amount reclassified into and out of each category. For reclassification out of FVTPL into amortised cost or FVOCI: the EIR determined on the reclassification date and interest revenue recognised. For reclassifications out of FVOCI into amortised cost, or out of FVTPL: the fair value at the end of the period and the fair value gain or loss that would have been recognised had it not been reclassified, in each period until derecognition.

Practical process

  1. Board or KMP resolution documenting the change and its operational effects.
  2. Evidence of the change being implemented (closure of desk, change in mandates, systems).
  3. Identify the reclassification date (first day of next reporting period).
  4. Value all affected assets at fair value on that date.
  5. Compute new EIR (if moving out of FVTPL) and ECL staging.
  6. Pass the entries and update the classification register and deferred tax schedule.
  7. Prepare the Ind AS 107 disclosures.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Sale of a subsidiary whose loans are transferred to the group's collection model does not itself change the parent's business model in standalone accounts; assess entity by entity.
  • Business model change in consolidated vs separate statements can differ — an asset may be reclassified in one set and not the other.
  • Covid-type market dislocations are not business model changes, even if the entity stops selling for a year.
  • Error vs reclassification. If an asset was wrongly classified at initial recognition, that is a prior-period error under Ind AS 8, corrected retrospectively — not a reclassification.
  • Ind AS 101 transition — classification was assessed on facts at transition date; later changes follow 4.4.1.

Key takeaways

  • Only a significant, demonstrable change in business model triggers reclassification — and it must reclassify all affected assets.
  • Liabilities and irrevocable elections (FVOCI-equity, FVO) are never reclassified.
  • Reclassification date is the first day of the next reporting period; application is prospective.
  • Moving out of FVTPL sets a new EIR and fresh ECL staging from the reclassification date.
  • Changes in intention, temporary market closure and intra-entity transfers are not business model changes.
  • Wrong initial classification is an Ind AS 8 error, not a reclassification.

Practice Questions

Q1. Is this a business model change? [L2]

For each case state whether reclassification is permitted: (a) after a 15% fall in bond prices, a company decides to stop selling its FVOCI bonds until prices recover; (b) an NBFC sells its securitisation business to another NBFC and will hold remaining loans to collect; (c) a parent transfers bonds from its treasury trading book to a liquidity buffer within the same entity; (d) a company discovers that a CCD investment was wrongly carried at amortised cost last year.

💡 Hint: Separate intentions and market conditions from changes in how a business is run, and remember Ind AS 8 for errors.

Q2. Amortised cost to FVTPL entries [L2]

On 1 January 2026 (reclassification date), an entity reclassifies a bond portfolio from amortised cost (gross carrying amount ₹40 Cr, ECL ₹0.3 Cr) to FVTPL. Fair value on that date is ₹39.2 Cr. Pass the entries and explain the deferred tax treatment at 25.168% assuming the tax base is ₹40 Cr.

Q3. FVOCI to amortised cost [L2]

A bond with amortised cost ₹25 Cr and fair value ₹24.1 Cr (cumulative OCI loss ₹0.9 Cr, ECL ₹0.05 Cr held in OCI) is reclassified from FVOCI to amortised cost. Show the carrying amount immediately after reclassification and the entries.

Q4. Board memo on reclassification request [L3]

The CFO of a listed company wants to reclassify ₹150 Cr of debt mutual fund units from FVTPL to amortised cost to "reduce P&L volatility" because the company now intends to hold them for three years. Write a memo to the Audit Committee explaining whether this is possible and what alternatives exist.
