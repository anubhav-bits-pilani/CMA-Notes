Subtopic note

Modification vs Extinguishment of Loans: The 10% Test

Scope: Modification vs extinguishment: 10% test for liabilities, refinancing and restructured term loans

Why it matters

Indian borrowers frequently renegotiate loans — rate cuts after rating upgrades, tenor extensions, one-time restructuring under RBI frameworks, refinancing by a new lender or NCD refinancing of bank debt. Each event raises the question whether the old liability is extinguished (derecognised with a gain or loss and a new liability recognised at fair value) or merely modified (carrying amount adjusted and fees carried forward). The answer changes finance cost, can create a large one-off P&L item, and affects EBITDA-based covenants and deferred tax. Auditors test the 10% computation and the qualitative assessment on every material restructuring.

Core notes

The derecognition rule for liabilities

A financial liability is derecognised when it is extinguished — the obligation is discharged, cancelled or expires (Ind AS 109.3.3.1). An exchange between an existing borrower and lender of debt instruments with substantially different terms, or a substantial modification of the terms of an existing liability, is accounted for as an extinguishment of the original liability and recognition of a new one (3.3.2). The difference between the carrying amount extinguished and the consideration paid (including any non-cash assets or liabilities assumed) is recognised in P&L (3.3.3).

The quantitative 10% test (B3.3.6)

Terms are substantially different if the discounted present value of the cash flows under the new terms, including any fees paid net of any fees received, discounted using the original effective interest rate, is at least 10% different from the discounted present value of the remaining cash flows of the original liability (B3.3.6). In practice the PV of remaining original cash flows at the original EIR equals the current amortised cost.

\[ \text{Difference} = \frac{\left\lvert PV_{\text{new at original EIR}} - \text{Carrying amount} \right\rvert}{\text{Carrying amount}} \]

(\(PV_{\text{new}}\) includes fees paid to the lender on the modification; per the 2022 annual improvement adopted in Ind AS, only fees paid or received between the borrower and the lender, including those paid on the other's behalf, are included.)

The qualitative assessment

Even if the quantitative difference is below 10%, a modification can be substantial for qualitative reasons, for example: change in currency of the loan (INR to USD), conversion from fixed to floating in a way that changes risk profile significantly, introduction of an equity conversion feature, change of borrower (debtor substitution), or a change from secured to unsecured with materially different covenants. Many practitioners perform the qualitative test first for obvious cases and the 10% test for the rest.

Accounting outcomes

Extinguishment (≥10% or qualitatively substantial):

  1. Derecognise the old carrying amount (including unamortised fees).
  2. Recognise the new liability at fair value (Ind AS 113, using a market rate for the new terms).
  3. Fees and costs incurred are part of the gain or loss on extinguishment (B3.3.6).
  4. Gain or loss in P&L = carrying amount of old − fair value of new − fees paid.
  5. Compute a new EIR for the new liability.

Modification (not substantial):

  1. Recalculate the amortised cost as PV of modified cash flows at the original EIR.
  2. Recognise the difference as a modification gain or loss in P&L (the IFRS Interpretations Committee in 2017 confirmed this applies to liabilities, consistent with 5.4.3 for assets).
  3. Fees and costs incurred adjust the carrying amount and are amortised over the remaining term (B3.3.6).
  4. Continue at the original EIR (adjusted for the capitalised costs).

Illustration

Farhan Packaging Ltd has a term loan with carrying amount ₹9.89 Cr at 1 April 2026 (principal ₹10 Cr, original EIR 10.2%, contractual rate 9.75%, remaining tenor 3 years, bullet). The bank agrees to cut the rate to 8.0% and extend maturity to 5 years; modification fee ₹10 lakh.

New cash flows at original EIR 10.2%:

\[ PV = 0.80 \times \frac{1-(1.102)^{-5}}{0.102} + \frac{10}{(1.102)^{5}} + 0.10 = 3.02 + 6.15 + 0.10 = 9.27 \text{ Cr} \]

Difference = (9.89 − 9.27) ÷ 9.89 = 6.3% → not substantial (and no qualitative trigger). Modification accounting:

  • New amortised cost excluding fee = ₹9.17 Cr; modification gain ₹0.72 Cr (Dr Borrowings 0.72, Cr Finance cost / other income — modification gain 0.72).
  • Fee ₹0.10 Cr: Dr Borrowings 0.10, Cr Bank 0.10 (reduces carrying amount to ₹9.07 Cr); new EIR solved on ₹9.07 Cr against revised cash flows.

If the rate had been cut to 6.0%, PV of new flows would fall to about ₹8.52 Cr including the fee (14% difference) → extinguishment: derecognise ₹9.89 Cr, recognise the new loan at fair value (say ₹10 Cr if 6% is at-market for a rating-upgraded borrower), expense the ₹0.10 Cr fee within the extinguishment loss.

Refinancing with a new lender

Repayment of loan A from proceeds of loan B with a different lender is not a modification at all — loan A is extinguished (settled in cash) and loan B is a new liability. Any unamortised fees on A are written off to finance cost; prepayment penalties paid to A are part of the loss. If the same bank syndicate refinances (an "exchange"), apply the 10% test lender by lender, considering whether lenders who continue are exchanging instruments.

Restructured term loans

Under RBI resolution frameworks (the 2019 Prudential Framework, the 2020 COVID-19 Resolution Framework 1.0/2.0), restructuring may involve tenor extension, interest conversion into funded interest term loans (FITL), conversion of part debt into equity or OCDs, and changes in security. For the borrower:

  • Debt-for-equity conversion: Ind AS 109 and Appendix D of Ind AS 109 (Extinguishing Financial Liabilities with Equity Instruments, IFRIC 19 equivalent) — equity is measured at fair value and the difference with the extinguished carrying amount goes to P&L.
  • FITL and tenor extension: run the 10% test on the combined revised cash flows.
  • A waiver of interest or principal is generally an extinguishment of that portion, with a gain in P&L.

For the lender (asset side), modification is assessed under 5.4.3 and 3.2 derecognition; the 10% test is not prescribed for assets, but many entities use it as a policy benchmark. Credit-impaired restructuring stays in Stage 3 for ECL.

Tax

Waiver of loan principal taken for trading purposes can be taxable under Sec 28(iv) or Sec 41(1) depending on use; the Supreme Court in Mahindra & Mahindra (2018) held waiver of a loan for capital assets not taxable under 28(iv) or 41(1). Modification gains recognised under Ind AS are not automatically taxable — they create temporary differences until the cash flows reverse. Check MAT book profit adjustments only for companies not under 115BAA.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Several small modifications over time — assess each against the then carrying amount; a series of linked amendments may need to be assessed together.
  • Modification of a floating-rate loan — use the current reset EIR as the "original EIR" for the test.
  • Fees to third parties (legal counsel) are not in the 10% test but are treated as costs of the modification or extinguishment.
  • Covenant waivers alone usually do not change cash flows; but a waiver fee and a spread increase together may.
  • Classification at year-end: a refinancing or rescheduling agreed after the reporting date does not make a loan due within 12 months non-current (Ind AS 1.72). For a covenant breach, however, Ind AS 1.74 carries an Indian carve-out from IAS 1: if the lender agrees after the reporting period but before approval of the financial statements not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach, the liability is not classified as current.

Key takeaways

  • Substantial modification or exchange = extinguishment of old liability and recognition of a new one at fair value.
  • The 10% test compares PV of new cash flows (including lender fees) at the original EIR with the carrying amount.
  • Qualitative factors (currency, conversion feature, borrower change) can force extinguishment below 10%.
  • Non-substantial modification: recompute amortised cost at the original EIR, gain/loss to P&L, fees adjust carrying amount.
  • Refinancing with a new lender is always an extinguishment; unamortised fees are written off.
  • Debt-for-equity swaps are measured at the fair value of equity issued.

Practice Questions

Q1. 10% test on a rate cut and extension [L2]

Gauri Steel Ltd's loan has carrying amount ₹49.05 Cr at 1 April 2026 (principal ₹50 Cr, 9.5% coupon, original EIR 10.1%, 4 years remaining, bullet). The lender agrees to a 7.75% coupon and a 6-year remaining term for a ₹25 lakh fee paid to the lender. Perform the 10% test, conclude, and pass the entries.

💡 Hint: Include the lender fee in the new cash flows and discount everything at 10.1%.

Q2. Refinancing through an NCD issue [L2]

A company repays a ₹100 Cr bank loan (carrying amount ₹98.9 Cr) using proceeds of a ₹100 Cr NCD issue with ₹80 lakh issue costs and a 1% prepayment penalty to the bank. Pass the entries for the repayment and the NCD issue, and state the impact on finance cost.

Q3. Debt-for-equity restructuring [L3]

Under a resolution plan, lenders convert ₹60 Cr of a ₹200 Cr loan into equity shares with fair value ₹35 Cr, extend the remaining ₹140 Cr by three years and reduce interest from 11% to 9%. The original EIR is 11.2% and carrying amount ₹199 Cr. Analyse the accounting for the borrower, including the extinguishment of the converted portion and the 10% test on the residual loan.

Q4. Interview: when is a reset not a modification? [L1]

Explain, in 4–5 sentences, the difference between a contractual repo-rate reset, a bank-initiated spread increase under an existing loan clause, and a negotiated spread cut, and how each is accounted for.
