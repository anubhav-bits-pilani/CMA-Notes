Subtopic note

Initial Measurement and Day-1 Differences on Interest-Free Loans

Scope: Initial measurement: fair value + transaction costs (non-FVTPL); trade receivables at transaction price; day-1 differences on interest-free loans to subsidiaries/employees

Why it matters

Initial measurement sets the starting point of every EIR schedule and every fair value roll-forward. In Indian groups, interest-free or concessional loans to subsidiaries, employee loans, security deposits paid for leased premises and deferred-payment receivables are pervasive, and each produces a day-1 difference between cash paid and fair value that must be classified correctly — as investment in subsidiary, employee benefit, prepaid rent or revenue adjustment. Getting this wrong misstates interest income for years and distorts standalone net worth. Auditors routinely test the discount rate and the debit side of the day-1 entry.

Core notes

The general rule (5.1.1)

At initial recognition, a financial asset or liability is measured at its fair value plus or minus, for items not at FVTPL, transaction costs directly attributable to its acquisition or issue (Ind AS 109.5.1.1). For FVTPL items, transaction costs are expensed immediately.

  • Fair value at initial recognition is normally the transaction price (B5.1.1), i.e., the fair value of consideration given or received. Where part of the consideration is for something other than the financial instrument (for example, a below-market rate loan), the fair value is estimated as the present value of all future cash receipts discounted at the prevailing market rate for a similar instrument (similar currency, term, type of interest rate and credit rating) (B5.1.1).
  • Transaction costs are incremental costs directly attributable to the acquisition, issue or disposal — fees and commissions to agents, advisers, brokers, levies by regulators and exchanges, transfer taxes and duties, stamp duty. They exclude debt premiums or discounts, financing costs, internal administrative or holding costs (B5.4.8).

Assets: Dr Asset at (fair value + costs). Liabilities: Cr Liability at (fair value − costs).

Trade receivables (5.1.3)

Trade receivables that do not contain a significant financing component under Ind AS 115 (or where the practical expedient for credit terms of one year or less is applied, Ind AS 115.63) are measured at initial recognition at their transaction price as defined in Ind AS 115, not at fair value (5.1.3). Receivables with a significant financing component are measured at fair value (present value), with interest income recognised separately from revenue. This avoids discounting every 60–90 day receivable.

Day-1 differences: the principle

If fair value at initial recognition differs from the transaction price, 5.1.1A and B5.1.2A apply:

  • If fair value is evidenced by a Level 1 quoted price or based on a valuation technique using only observable market data, recognise the difference as a gain or loss.
  • Otherwise, defer the difference and recognise it only to the extent it arises from a change in a factor market participants would consider (time, for example).

For below-market loans the difference is not a "gain or loss" in the trading sense; B5.1.1 says that if part of the consideration is for something other than the financial instrument, that part is accounted for under the relevant standard. The debit side therefore depends on why the lender gave the concession.

Lender's relationship with borrower Nature of the concession Debit for day-1 difference
Parent lends interest-free to subsidiary Capital contribution Investment in subsidiary (separate FS)
Company lends to employee at concessional rate Employee benefit (Ind AS 19) Prepaid employee benefit; expensed over service/loan period
Lessee pays interest-free refundable security deposit Additional lease payment ROU asset (Ind AS 116)
Subsidiary lends interest-free to parent Distribution to owner Equity (deemed distribution)
Customer gets extended interest-free credit in a sale Financing component Reduces revenue (Ind AS 115)

Interest-free loan to a subsidiary

Prakash Holdings Ltd lends ₹10 Cr interest-free to its wholly owned subsidiary on 1 April 2025, repayable on 31 March 2030. The subsidiary's market borrowing rate is 10%.

\[ PV = \frac{10}{(1.10)^{5}} = 6.21 \text{ Cr} \]

Day-1 difference = ₹3.79 Cr = capital contribution.

Parent (separate financial statements):

Entry Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Loan to subsidiary (amortised cost) 6.21
Investment in subsidiary — deemed equity contribution 3.79
Bank 10.00
Loan to subsidiary (FY 2025-26 unwind 10% × 6.21) 0.62
Interest income 0.62

Subsidiary:

Entry Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Bank 10.00
Borrowings — loan from parent 6.21
Other equity — deemed capital contribution from parent 3.79
Finance cost 0.62
Borrowings 0.62

On consolidation, both sides eliminate. If the loan is repayable on demand, its fair value equals the face amount (Ind AS 113.47), and no day-1 difference arises. If the loan has no fixed term and repayment is at the subsidiary's discretion, the instrument may be equity of the subsidiary in substance.

Employee loans

Neha Auto Ltd gives a ₹20 lakh housing loan at 2% to an employee, repayable in equal annual instalments over 5 years; market rate 9%. Annual instalment at 2% \( = 20 \div 4.7135 = 4.243 \) lakh. PV at 9% \( = 4.243 \times 3.8897 = 16.50 \) lakh. Day-1 difference ₹3.50 lakh is a prepaid employee benefit, expensed over the loan period (or the service period required if the loan becomes repayable on leaving). Interest income is recognised at 9% on ₹16.50 lakh. For tax, perquisite valuation under Rule 3(7)(i) uses SBI rates — a separate computation.

Security deposits

Refundable security deposits paid for leased premises (common for retail chains) are financial assets measured at the PV of refund; the difference is additional lease payment added to the ROU asset under Ind AS 116. Discount at the lessee's incremental borrowing rate or the market rate for a similar receivable.

Keep face value, day-1 difference and its classification as separate rows in the loan register so that deferred tax can be analysed. For the parent, neither the unwinding income nor the recovery of principal is taxable, so the loan's tax base equals its carrying amount (Ind AS 12.7) and no temporary difference arises on the loan itself. The ₹3.79 Cr added to investment in subsidiary, however, makes its carrying amount exceed its tax cost — an outside-basis difference that is not recognised where the parent controls reversal and reversal is not probable in the foreseeable future (Ind AS 12.39).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Discount rate. Use the borrower's market rate for a loan of similar tenor and credit risk, not the lender's cost of funds; evidence through bank quotes or the subsidiary's actual borrowings.
  • Loans repayable on demand carry no day-1 difference; but if the parent has no intention of demanding repayment for years, classification as current and ECL (lifetime vs 12-month) still need consideration.
  • Concessional loans from government (e.g., state subsidy loans) — the benefit is a government grant under Ind AS 20.10A, recognised as deferred income.
  • Bulk employee loans can be computed by portfolio using average rates and tenors if not material individually.
  • Transaction costs on FVTPL assets go straight to P&L; a common error is to capitalise brokerage on mutual fund purchases.
  • Trade receivables with long credit (e.g., 18-month deferred payment for capital equipment) contain a significant financing component and must be discounted.

Key takeaways

  • Initial measurement = fair value ± transaction costs for non-FVTPL items; costs expensed for FVTPL.
  • Trade receivables without a significant financing component are recognised at Ind AS 115 transaction price.
  • Below-market loans are recognised at PV using a market rate; the day-1 difference is classified by the reason for the concession.
  • Parent-to-subsidiary concessions debit investment; employee concessions debit prepaid employee benefit; lease deposits debit ROU.
  • Loans repayable on demand have no day-1 discount.
  • Record the face value alongside amortised cost to drive deferred tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. Interest-free loan to a step-down subsidiary [L2]

On 1 April 2025 Vihaan Industries Ltd lends ₹25 Cr interest-free to its 100% subsidiary, repayable in one bullet on 31 March 2028. The subsidiary's market borrowing rate is 11%. Pass the entries in both entities for FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27, and state the consolidation elimination at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: The parent's debit for the day-1 difference is an increase in its investment, not an expense.

Q2. Employee loan and Ind AS 19 [L2]

A company gives an employee a ₹12 lakh vehicle loan at 0% repayable in 48 equal monthly instalments; the market rate is 10% p.a. The loan becomes immediately repayable if the employee resigns. Compute the initial fair value, the day-1 difference and its accounting, and prepare the first three months' entries.

Q3. Transaction costs by category [L1]

A company pays brokerage of ₹40,000 and STT of ₹10,000 to buy listed shares for ₹50 lakh, and pays ₹60,000 stamp duty and ₹1 lakh arranger fees on subscribing ₹10 Cr of NCDs held to collect. State the initial carrying amount of each and where the costs go, assuming (a) the shares are FVTPL and (b) the shares are FVOCI-equity.

Q4. Security deposit for a retail lease [L3]

A retail chain pays a ₹1.5 Cr refundable interest-free deposit for a 9-year store lease; the incremental borrowing rate is 9.5%. Compute the PV, the amount added to the ROU asset, the unwind in year 1, and explain the deferred tax implications on both the deposit and the ROU asset.
