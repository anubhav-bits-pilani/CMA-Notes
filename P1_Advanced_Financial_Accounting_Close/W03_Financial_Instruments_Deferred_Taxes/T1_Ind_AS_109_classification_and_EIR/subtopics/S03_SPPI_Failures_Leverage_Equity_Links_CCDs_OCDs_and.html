Subtopic note

SPPI Failures: Leverage, Equity Links, CCDs, OCDs and MF Units

Scope: SPPI failures: leverage, equity-linked or profit-linked returns, conversion features — CCDs, OCDs, mutual fund units (FVTPL)

Why it matters

Indian corporate and start-up funding relies heavily on hybrid instruments — compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), optionally convertible debentures (OCDs), structured NCDs linked to Nifty or to project cash flows, and mutual fund units as the default cash parking vehicle. For the holder, almost all of these fail SPPI and must be measured at FVTPL, which brings Level 3 valuations, P&L volatility and deferred tax at capital gains rates. Misclassifying them at amortised cost is a recurring audit observation in holding companies, family offices and corporate venture arms, and in NBFCs that lend through structured instruments.

Core notes

Why these features fail

SPPI requires cash flows consistent with a basic lending arrangement: compensation for time value, credit risk, other basic lending risks, costs and a margin (B4.1.7A). Contractual terms that introduce exposure to risks or volatility unrelated to a basic lending arrangement — such as changes in equity prices or commodity prices — fail, as do terms linked to the debtor's performance (B4.1.8, B4.1.16). The test looks at the contract as a whole: an embedded feature in a financial asset is not separated (unlike in liabilities), so a non-SPPI feature makes the entire instrument FVTPL (4.3.2).

Leverage

Leverage increases the variability of contractual cash flows so that they no longer have the economic characteristics of interest (B4.1.9). Stand-alone options, forwards and swaps are examples. A loan whose coupon is "2 × repo rate − 4%" (a leveraged floater) fails, as does an inverse floater (B4.1.14 Instrument G), because a 1% change in repo changes the coupon by 2%. A plain floating rate (repo + spread) passes. Interest caps and floors can pass if they limit variability without introducing leverage (B4.1.11(b)).

Equity-linked and profit-linked returns

  • Equity-indexed returns. A market-linked debenture whose redemption amount depends on Nifty 50 performance fails SPPI (B4.1.7A, B4.1.8) — the holder is exposed to equity price risk.
  • Profit-linked returns. Interest that varies with the borrower's revenue, EBITDA or net profit fails, because it compensates for the borrower's performance rather than credit risk (B4.1.8). Revenue-share financing to start-ups and "participating" debentures are examples.
  • Non-recourse look-through. A loan to a project SPV repayable only from the project's cash flows may fail if the holder is effectively exposed to asset performance (B4.1.16–B4.1.17); this requires a look-through assessment of loan-to-value, equity cushion and the SPV's other resources.

Conversion features: CCDs and OCDs

A conversion option into the issuer's equity exposes the holder to equity price risk.

CCDs (compulsorily convertible debentures). CCDs carry a small coupon (often 0.01%–8%) and must convert into equity at a fixed or formula ratio by a specified date. The holder never receives principal in cash; its return is the value of shares received. Cash flows are therefore not payments of principal and interest — CCDs fail SPPI and are FVTPL for the holder. (If the conversion ratio is fixed and the holder's investment is economically equity, some argue the CCD is an equity instrument of the issuer; even so, an equity instrument held cannot be at amortised cost, and the holder can consider the FVOCI election only if the instrument meets the Ind AS 32 equity definition for the issuer in its entirety, which CCDs with coupons usually do not.)

OCDs (optionally convertible debentures). The holder can either redeem for principal plus interest or convert into shares. The conversion option is an embedded equity derivative; because hybrid financial assets are not bifurcated, the whole OCD is FVTPL (4.3.2, B4.1.14 Instrument F).

Issuer side contrast. The issuer of an OCD with a fixed-for-fixed conversion option has a compound instrument under Ind AS 32 (liability plus equity, s04). The asymmetry — issuer splits, holder carries everything at FVTPL — is deliberate.

Mutual fund units

Units of open-ended mutual funds (liquid, debt, equity, hybrid) are puttable at NAV. For the holder:

  • They are not equity instruments under Ind AS 32 (the puttable exception in 32.16A–16D only allows the issuer to classify them as equity), so the 5.7.5 FVOCI election is not available.
  • Their cash flows are not SPPI — NAV reflects the fair value of the underlying portfolio, not principal and interest on a principal amount.
  • Hence they are FVTPL, irrespective of whether the fund invests only in overnight T-bills.

The same reasoning applies to units of AIFs, REITs and InvITs (puttability may differ, but returns depend on underlying asset performance).

Illustration: start-up investment portfolio

Ishaan Ventures Pvt Ltd invests ₹30 Cr in instruments of portfolio companies on 1 April 2025:

Instrument Terms SPPI? Category
₹10 Cr CCDs of A Ltd 0.1% coupon, convert in 3 years at fixed ratio No — return is equity FVTPL
₹8 Cr OCDs of B Ltd 10% coupon, holder may convert at Series B price No — embedded equity option FVTPL
₹7 Cr venture debt to C Ltd 13% fixed + warrants issued separately Debt: Yes; warrants separate derivative Debt: AC (if hold to collect); warrants: FVTPL
₹5 Cr revenue-share notes of D Ltd 4% of monthly revenue until 1.6× repaid No — performance-linked FVTPL

The warrants are a separate instrument (freestanding), so they do not taint the loan; if they were embedded in the loan contract, the whole loan would fail.

At 31 March 2026, fair values come from Level 3 techniques (recent funding rounds adjusted, option pricing models, DCF) — see w3_t6. Gains are taxed as capital gains on transfer, so deferred tax is computed at capital gains rates on the FVTPL gains (w3_t3).

Practical checklist for SPPI failures

  1. Is the instrument's return linked to equity prices, an index, commodities or the borrower's performance?
  2. Does it contain a conversion option or settlement in shares?
  3. Is the interest leveraged (multiplier on a benchmark)?
  4. Is the holder exposed to underlying asset performance through non-recourse terms?
  5. Is the instrument a unit in a fund or trust redeemable at NAV?
  6. Is the feature de minimis or non-genuine (B4.1.18)? If so, it can be ignored.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • ESG-linked or sustainability-linked loans. Margin changes tied to ESG targets require judgement; the IASB amended IFRS 9 in May 2024 to clarify that contingent features not directly related to basic lending risks can still pass if cash flows are not significantly different. Check whether the equivalent Ind AS 109 amendment has been notified by MCA before relying on it.
  • CCDs held by a parent in a subsidiary (separate financial statements) may be treated as part of the investment in subsidiary if they are in substance equity and the entity applies cost under Ind AS 27.
  • Interest-free loans with conversion fail on the conversion feature regardless of the day-1 discounting.
  • Default interest and penalty rates reflecting credit risk pass; a "premium" if a target IRR is not met by the issuer's equity value fails.
  • Liquid funds and overnight funds are still FVTPL, even though fair value changes are small; cash and cash equivalents classification under Ind AS 7 is a separate question.

Key takeaways

  • Any exposure to equity, commodity or borrower-performance risk fails SPPI.
  • Hybrid financial assets are classified in their entirety; embedded derivatives are not separated on the asset side.
  • CCDs and OCDs are FVTPL for the holder; the issuer may split them under Ind AS 32.
  • Mutual fund units are FVTPL — not SPPI, and not eligible for the FVOCI-equity election.
  • Leverage on a benchmark fails; caps and floors usually do not.
  • FVTPL classification drives Level 3 valuation and capital-gains-based deferred tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. SPPI screening of a structured debt book [L2]

An NBFC holds: (a) ₹50 Cr market-linked debentures paying 0% coupon with redemption at 100% plus 60% of Nifty 50 upside; (b) ₹40 Cr NCDs at T-bill + 2.5%; (c) ₹20 Cr NCDs paying 3 × T-bill − 12%, floored at 0; (d) ₹30 Cr project loans to a solar SPV with recourse limited to the project, 70% loan-to-value. Classify each, explaining the SPPI analysis.

💡 Hint: For (d), the answer depends on whether the holder is exposed to asset performance; set out the factors that would decide it.

Q2. CCD investment accounting [L2]

On 1 April 2025 Aarav Holdings Ltd invests ₹15 Cr in 0.01% CCDs of an unlisted company, converting in 2 years into a fixed number of equity shares. Transaction costs are ₹10 lakh. At 31 March 2026 the investment is valued at ₹18.2 Cr using a recent funding round adjusted for liquidation preference. Pass the entries for FY 2025-26, including deferred tax at 14.3% on the unrealised gain.

Q3. Embedded vs freestanding warrants [L2]

A venture debt fund lends ₹10 Cr at 14% to a start-up and receives warrants to subscribe to shares. Explain how the classification of the loan differs if (a) the warrants are issued under a separate agreement and can be transferred independently, versus (b) the conversion right is written into the loan agreement.

Q4. CFO challenge on liquid funds [L3]

The CFO argues that ₹100 Cr parked in overnight mutual funds should be at amortised cost or FVOCI because they "are just like cash". Draft a short note explaining the correct classification, presentation in Schedule III and Ind AS 7, the P&L effect, and the deferred tax consequence.
