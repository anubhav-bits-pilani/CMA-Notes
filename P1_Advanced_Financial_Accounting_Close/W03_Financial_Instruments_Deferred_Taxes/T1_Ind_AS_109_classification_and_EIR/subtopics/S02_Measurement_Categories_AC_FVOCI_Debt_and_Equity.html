Subtopic note

Measurement Categories: AC, FVOCI Debt and Equity, FVTPL, FVO

Scope: Amortised cost, FVOCI-debt (recycling), FVOCI-equity election (no recycling, 5.7.5), FVTPL; fair value option to remove accounting mismatch

Why it matters

The measurement category determines what hits profit, what sits in OCI, and whether gains are ever recycled. For a listed Indian company with large treasury holdings, the difference between FVTPL and FVOCI can swing reported PAT by tens of crores in a volatile bond or equity market. Analysts adjust "other income" for mark-to-market gains; lenders' covenants often exclude OCI; deferred tax on FVOCI items goes to OCI. Getting the subsequent measurement mechanics right — especially the dual-track FVOCI-debt accounting and the no-recycling rule for equity — is a common exam and interview topic and a frequent audit adjustment.

Core notes

Amortised cost

Assets that meet the hold-to-collect and SPPI conditions (4.1.2) are measured at amortised cost: the amount at initial recognition, minus principal repayments, plus or minus cumulative amortisation using the effective interest method of any difference between initial amount and maturity amount, adjusted for any loss allowance (Appendix A definition). Interest revenue is EIR × gross carrying amount (5.4.1), except for purchased or originated credit-impaired assets (credit-adjusted EIR) and assets that become credit-impaired (Stage 3: EIR × amortised cost, i.e., net of allowance). ECL applies. Gains and losses arise only on derecognition, impairment or reclassification (5.7.2). Financial liabilities default to amortised cost (4.2.1).

FVOCI for debt instruments (with recycling)

Assets meeting the hold-to-collect-and-sell and SPPI conditions (4.1.2A) are carried at fair value on the balance sheet, but the P&L is the same as if the asset were at amortised cost (5.7.10–5.7.11):

  • Interest revenue at EIR on the amortised cost, impairment gains/losses (ECL) and foreign exchange gains/losses go to P&L.
  • The remaining fair value change goes to OCI.
  • On derecognition, the cumulative OCI gain or loss is reclassified (recycled) to P&L (5.7.10).

The ECL allowance does not reduce the balance-sheet carrying amount (which is fair value); it is recognised in OCI as an offset to the fair value reserve (5.5.2).

Illustration. On 1 April 2025 Jyoti Textiles Ltd buys a ₹10 Cr 8% bond at par (EIR 8%), classified FVOCI. At 31 March 2026 the fair value is ₹9.70 Cr and 12-month ECL is ₹0.05 Cr.

If the bond is sold on 1 April 2026 for ₹9.70 Cr, the ₹0.25 Cr net OCI debit balance (fair value loss ₹0.30 Cr less the ₹0.05 Cr ECL credit) is recycled to P&L as a loss on sale; together with the ₹0.05 Cr ECL already charged, cumulative P&L loss equals ₹0.30 Cr, the difference between cost and proceeds.

FVOCI election for equity (no recycling)

At initial recognition an entity may make an irrevocable election, instrument by instrument, to present subsequent fair value changes in OCI for an investment in an equity instrument within the scope of Ind AS 109 that is neither held for trading nor contingent consideration recognised by an acquirer in a business combination (4.1.4, 5.7.5). Consequences (5.7.6, B5.7.1):

  • All fair value changes go to OCI; there is no impairment test.
  • Amounts in OCI are never recycled to P&L, even on disposal; the cumulative gain may be transferred within equity (typically to retained earnings).
  • Dividends go to P&L (Ind AS 109.5.7.1A), unless they clearly represent a recovery of part of the cost.

"Equity instrument" means equity as defined in Ind AS 32 from the issuer's perspective. Instruments classified as equity by the issuer only under the puttable exception (32.16A–16D) do not qualify for the holder — so mutual fund units, which are puttable at NAV, cannot be designated FVOCI.

Typical Indian uses: strategic stakes in listed or unlisted companies (e.g., shares in a supplier, a captive power company or a stock exchange), where management does not want market volatility in P&L.

FVTPL

FVTPL is the residual category (4.1.4): debt assets that fail SPPI or are held in an "other" business model, equity without the FVOCI election, derivatives, and held-for-trading items. All fair value changes, interest and dividends go to P&L. Transaction costs are expensed on day one (5.1.1). No ECL. Entities may present interest and dividends separately from fair value changes; the policy must be disclosed.

The fair value option (FVO)

At initial recognition an entity may irrevocably designate a financial asset at FVTPL if doing so eliminates or significantly reduces a measurement or recognition inconsistency (an accounting mismatch) that would otherwise arise (4.1.5). For financial liabilities the FVO is available for accounting mismatch, for a group of financial liabilities (or assets and liabilities) managed and evaluated on a fair value basis under a documented risk management strategy, or for hybrid contracts with embedded derivatives (4.2.2, 4.3.5).

Example of mismatch: an NBFC holds a fixed-rate bond (would be amortised cost) funded by a fixed-rate borrowing economically hedged with an interest-rate swap at FVTPL. Designating the bond at FVTPL means its fair value change offsets the swap's, without applying hedge accounting. The designation must be made at inception and documented; it cannot be revoked.

Presentation and tax

Schedule III Division II requires separate disclosure of non-current and current investments by category, and "Equity instruments through OCI" and "Items that will be reclassified" versus "will not be reclassified" are separated in OCI. Deferred tax on FVOCI gains is charged to OCI (Ind AS 12.61A), using the capital gains rate expected on sale (after the July 2024 changes, LTCG generally 12.5% plus surcharge and cess for companies).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Strategic stake later sold for cash needs. The FVOCI-equity election remains; disposal gains stay out of P&L. Analysts sometimes ask for disclosure of the realised gain transferred to retained earnings (Ind AS 107.11A–11B).
  • Investments in subsidiaries, associates and JVs in separate financial statements are outside Ind AS 109 if carried at cost under Ind AS 27.10; if the entity chooses Ind AS 109 measurement, the FVOCI election is available.
  • Preference shares held are often debt-like for the issuer (redeemable) and hence debt for the holder, so the FVOCI-equity election is not available; SPPI may fail if dividends are discretionary.
  • FVOCI-debt sold at a gain followed by repurchase does not change classification but the frequency of such sales is evidence for the business model.
  • Deferred tax rate for FVOCI-equity reflects the expected manner of recovery (sale versus dividend), per Ind AS 12.51.

Key takeaways

  • Amortised cost and FVOCI-debt produce identical P&L; FVOCI-debt also carries a fair value reserve in OCI that recycles on sale.
  • The FVOCI-equity election is irrevocable, instrument-by-instrument, never recycles and has no impairment.
  • Only Ind AS 32 equity instruments qualify; puttables such as mutual fund units do not.
  • FVTPL is the residual category; transaction costs are expensed immediately.
  • The FVO is available only at inception and only to fix an accounting mismatch (plus liability-specific grounds).
  • Deferred tax follows the gain into OCI or P&L.

Practice Questions

Q1. FVOCI-debt entries over two years [L2]

On 1 April 2025 Sameer Logistics Ltd buys a ₹5 Cr, 3-year, 7% bond at ₹4.87 Cr (EIR 8%), classified FVOCI. Fair values: ₹4.95 Cr at 31 March 2026 and ₹4.90 Cr at 31 March 2027. 12-month ECL is ₹2 lakh at 31 March 2026 and ₹3 lakh at 31 March 2027. The bond is sold on 1 April 2027 for ₹4.92 Cr. Pass all entries and show the cumulative OCI balance and amount recycled.

💡 Hint: Build the amortised-cost schedule first; OCI is fair value minus amortised cost, adjusted for the ECL credit.

Q2. Equity election decision [L2]

Anand Engineering Ltd holds (a) 2% of a listed peer bought for ₹12 Cr as a long-term strategic investment, (b) units of a large-cap equity mutual fund costing ₹6 Cr, (c) shares bought for ₹1 Cr for short-term trading, and (d) unlisted equity of a power-purchase SPV required to buy captive power. For each, state whether the FVOCI election is available and what you would recommend, with reasons.

Q3. Fair value option for an NBFC [L3]

An NBFC funds a ₹200 Cr fixed-rate 8% corporate loan book with floating-rate borrowings and enters into a pay-fixed, receive-floating swap. The loans are held to collect and pass SPPI. Explain the accounting mismatch, whether the FVO can be applied to the loans or the borrowings, the alternative of hedge accounting, and the P&L volatility under each approach when rates fall 100 bps.

Q4. Dividends and disposal under FVOCI-equity [L1]

A company with an FVOCI-equity stake (cost ₹3 Cr, carrying amount ₹4.5 Cr) receives a dividend of ₹0.2 Cr and then sells the stake for ₹4.8 Cr. Pass the entries, including any transfer within equity, and state the P&L impact.
