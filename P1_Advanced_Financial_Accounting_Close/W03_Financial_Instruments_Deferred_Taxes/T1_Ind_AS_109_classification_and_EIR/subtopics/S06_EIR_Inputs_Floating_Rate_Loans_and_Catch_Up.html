Subtopic note

EIR Inputs, Floating-Rate Loans and Catch-Up Adjustments

Scope: EIR inputs: processing fees, upfront fees, stamp duty, legal costs; floating-rate loans (B5.4.5) and catch-up on revised estimates (B5.4.6)

Why it matters

Almost every Indian term loan, working-capital term loan, NCD and ECB carries upfront costs — processing fees of 0.25%–2%, stamp duty on the loan agreement and mortgage, legal and valuation charges, arranger and trustee fees. Whether these enter the EIR determines finance cost every year and the carrying amount of borrowings. Most bank loans are floating-rate (EBLR, MCLR or T-bill linked), and borrowers often prepay or refinance, so the schedule must handle both market-rate resets and revised cash-flow estimates. The distinction between B5.4.5 (prospective) and B5.4.6 (catch-up through P&L) is a frequent source of audit adjustments and a common interview question.

Core notes

Which fees and costs enter the EIR

The EIR includes all fees and points paid or received between parties that are an integral part of the effective interest rate, transaction costs and all other premiums or discounts (Appendix A; B5.4.1–B5.4.3). Practical classification for an Indian borrower:

Cost Treatment Reason
Processing / upfront fee charged by lender Include in EIR Integral to obtaining the loan (B5.4.2(c))
Stamp duty on loan agreement, hypothecation, mortgage Include Transfer tax/duty directly attributable (B5.4.8)
Legal, title search, valuation fees for the loan Include Incremental, directly attributable
Arranger, syndication, trustee acceptance fees (NCDs) Include Incremental issue costs
Credit rating fee for a specific NCD issue Include Incremental to the issue
Annual trustee fees, annual rating surveillance, annual renewal charges Expense as incurred Service fees, not integral (B5.4.3)
Internal treasury staff cost, CFO time Exclude Not incremental
GST on processing fee with full input credit Exclude Recoverable
GST on fee where input credit is blocked or reversed (e.g., exempt-supply entities, 50% ITC option for banks/NBFCs) Include the non-creditable part Becomes a cost
Commitment fee on an undrawn facility likely to be drawn Defer and include in EIR on drawdown (B5.4.2(b)) Integral to the loan
Commitment fee where drawdown is not probable Amortise straight-line over commitment period Service for keeping facility available
Prepayment penalty paid on refinancing Part of extinguishment loss or modification analysis (s07) Relates to old instrument

The fees are amortised over the expected life of the instrument, or a shorter period if they relate to it — for example, to the next repricing date where fees relate to a rate that resets to market at that date (B5.4.4).

Building the EIR with fees

Ekta Motors Ltd draws a ₹10 Cr 5-year bullet loan on 1 April 2025 at 9% fixed, interest payable annually. Costs: processing fee 1.5% (₹15 lakh, GST creditable), stamp duty ₹5 lakh, legal ₹5 lakh. Net proceeds ₹9.75 Cr. The EIR solves

\[ 9.75 = \sum_{t=1}^{5} \frac{0.90}{(1+r)^t} + \frac{10}{(1+r)^5} \]

giving \( r \approx 9.654\% \).

Year to 31 Mar Opening Interest at EIR Coupon paid Closing
2026 9.750 0.941 0.900 9.791
2027 9.791 0.945 0.900 9.836
2028 9.836 0.950 0.900 9.886
2029 9.886 0.954 0.900 9.940
2030 9.940 0.960 0.900 + 10.000 0.000

Total EIR interest ₹4.75 Cr = coupons ₹4.50 Cr + costs ₹0.25 Cr. That equality is the first check on any EIR schedule.

Floating-rate instruments (B5.4.5)

For floating-rate financial assets and liabilities, periodic re-estimation of cash flows to reflect movements in market rates of interest alters the effective interest rate (B5.4.5). When the benchmark resets, you do not recompute the carrying amount or book a gain or loss — you simply apply the new rate going forward. If a floating-rate loan is recognised initially at an amount equal to the principal receivable or payable, re-estimating future interest payments normally has no significant effect on the carrying amount.

Where fees exist on a floating-rate loan, two common practical methods are used:

  1. Spread method. Compute a fee-amortisation spread at inception (the EIR on a constant-benchmark assumption minus the contractual rate) and add it to the reset coupon each period; the fee unwinds smoothly.
  2. Re-solve on reset. At each reset, re-compute the EIR that equates remaining expected cash flows at the new rate with the current carrying amount, and apply it prospectively.

Both keep the fee unwind prospective. The amortisation period for the fee should still be the expected life unless the fee relates only to the period to the next reset.

Example: a ₹50 Cr loan at repo + 3.25% (repo 6.50% at drawdown, so 9.75%) with a ₹50 lakh fee. On a reset of repo to 6.00% the new coupon is 9.25%; the EIR moves from about 10.0% to about 9.5% from the reset date, with no P&L catch-up.

Revised estimates of cash flows (B5.4.6)

If an entity revises its estimates of payments or receipts (excluding modifications and changes in ECL), it adjusts the gross carrying amount of the financial asset or the amortised cost of the liability to reflect the revised estimated contractual cash flows, recalculated as the present value of the revised cash flows discounted at the original EIR (or the revised EIR for floating-rate instruments under B5.4.5). The adjustment is recognised in P&L as income or expense (B5.4.6).

Typical triggers: expected prepayment earlier or later than assumed; expected exercise of a borrower call option; changes in expected cash flows on instruments with contingent interest (e.g., a step-down in margin on achieving a rating) that pass SPPI.

Continuing the Ekta example. At 31 March 2027 (carrying ₹9.836 Cr), management now expects to prepay the full loan at par on 31 March 2028, without penalty.

\[ \text{Revised carrying amount} = \frac{0.90 + 10.00}{1.09654} = 9.940 \text{ Cr} \]

Catch-up = ₹9.940 − ₹9.836 = ₹0.104 Cr: Dr Finance cost 0.104, Cr Borrowings 0.104. The remaining unamortised fee is recognised immediately rather than over three more years.

Presentation and tax link

The unamortised costs sit within Borrowings (no separate "prepaid finance charges" asset). The tax base of the borrowing is the principal; because the fee was deducted for tax when incurred (Sec 36(1)(iii) read with Sec 2(28A)), the difference between ₹10 Cr and the carrying amount is a taxable temporary difference that reverses as the fee unwinds (w3_t3).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Fees paid to arrange a revolving facility (cash credit, overdraft) where drawings fluctuate: amortise straight-line over the facility period rather than through an EIR.
  • Loans with a moratorium need the EIR computed on actual dated cash flows; interest capitalised during moratorium increases principal.
  • Borrowing costs capitalised under Ind AS 23 use EIR interest, so fee amortisation during construction is capitalised into the qualifying asset.
  • Small fees relative to the loan may be expensed on materiality grounds, but the policy must be documented and applied consistently.
  • Upfront fee received by an NBFC lender is deferred and included in interest income through the EIR, not booked as fee income — a common Ind AS transition adjustment for NBFCs.
  • ECL changes are excluded from B5.4.6; they are recognised through the loss allowance.

Key takeaways

  • Integral fees and incremental transaction costs (processing fee, stamp duty, legal, arranger) enter the EIR; recurring service fees do not.
  • Total EIR interest over the life equals coupons plus net costs — use it as a check.
  • Floating-rate resets change the EIR prospectively with no catch-up (B5.4.5).
  • Other revised cash-flow estimates are discounted at the original EIR and the difference goes to P&L immediately (B5.4.6).
  • Unamortised fees reduce Borrowings and create a taxable temporary difference.
  • Non-creditable GST on fees is a transaction cost.

Practice Questions

Q1. EIR with mixed costs [L2]

On 1 April 2025 Lakshya Textiles Ltd draws ₹15 Cr at 9.25% fixed, interest payable quarterly, principal in 8 equal quarterly instalments from 30 June 2027. Costs: processing fee 1% plus 18% GST (input credit available), stamp duty ₹7.5 lakh, legal ₹2.5 lakh, annual rating fee ₹1 lakh. Identify the costs that enter the EIR, compute the EIR using quarterly periods, and prepare the schedule for FY 2025-26.

💡 Hint: Exclude both the GST and the annual rating fee; solve the quarterly IRR and annualise it.

Q2. Floating rate reset [L2]

A ₹40 Cr 4-year bullet loan bears interest at T-bill + 2.5% reset annually (initially 9.0%) with a ₹40 lakh processing fee. After one year the rate resets to 8.25%. Explain, with numbers, how the EIR and carrying amount are updated, and contrast with the result if you wrongly applied B5.4.6.

Q3. Prepayment estimate catch-up [L2]

An NBFC lends ₹5 Cr for 5 years at 12% with a ₹10 lakh upfront fee received. After two years the borrower signals it will prepay at the end of year 3 with a 2% prepayment charge. Compute the catch-up adjustment and pass the entry in the NBFC's books.

Q4. Commitment fee on a term loan facility [L1]

A company pays a ₹20 lakh commitment fee for a ₹50 Cr facility available for 12 months. It is probable that ₹30 Cr will be drawn. Explain the treatment of the fee before and after drawdown, and if the facility lapses undrawn.
