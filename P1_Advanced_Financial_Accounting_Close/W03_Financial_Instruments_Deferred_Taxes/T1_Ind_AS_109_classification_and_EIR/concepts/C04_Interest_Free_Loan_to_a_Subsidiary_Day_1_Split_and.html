Key concept

Interest-Free Loan to a Subsidiary: Day-1 Split and Unwinding

Concept: Interest-free loan to subsidiary: Dr Loan (PV) Dr Investment in subsidiary (difference) Cr Bank; unwinding Dr Loan Cr Interest income

Statement

For a fixed-term interest-free (or below-market) loan from a parent to a subsidiary, in the parent's separate financial statements:

\[ PV_0 = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + k)^t}, \qquad D = \text{Cash lent} - PV_0 \]

where \( CF_t \) are the contractual repayments, \( k \) is the market interest rate for a similar loan to a borrower of the subsidiary's credit standing and tenor, \( PV_0 \) the fair value of the loan at initial recognition and \( D \) the day-1 difference. Entries:

Entry Dr Cr
Initial Loan to subsidiary \( PV_0 \); Investment in subsidiary \( D \) Bank (cash lent)
Each period Loan to subsidiary \( (PV_{\text{open}} \times k) \) Interest income
Repayment Bank Loan to subsidiary

Explanation

Ind AS 109.5.1.1 and B5.1.1 require the loan to be recognised at fair value. The parent gave up interest because it owns the subsidiary, so the forgone interest is in substance an additional capital contribution — an increase in the cost of investment (Ind AS 27 cost basis). The loan then accretes back to face value through notional interest income at \( k \).

Conditions and variations:

  • Repayable on demand: fair value equals face value; no day-1 difference.
  • No fixed repayment and repayment at the subsidiary's discretion: the "loan" may be equity of the subsidiary; the parent adds it to investment entirely.
  • Loan from subsidiary to parent: the difference is a distribution to the owner (debit equity in the subsidiary), not an expense.
  • Loan to a fellow subsidiary: the difference is a distribution by the lender to the parent and a contribution by the parent to the borrower — debit equity in the lender, credit equity in the borrower.
  • ECL applies to the loan at amortised cost (12-month or lifetime per staging).

In the subsidiary's books the mirror entries are Cr Borrowings at \( PV_0 \), Cr Other equity (capital contribution) \( D \), and notional finance cost. All of it eliminates in consolidation.

Illustration

Uday Agro Ltd lends ₹8 Cr interest-free to its subsidiary on 1 April 2025, repayable ₹4 Cr on 31 March 2027 and ₹4 Cr on 31 March 2028. Market rate 9.5%.

\[ PV_0 = \frac{4}{(1.095)^2} + \frac{4}{(1.095)^3} = 3.336 + 3.047 = 6.383 \text{ Cr} \]

Day-1: Dr Loan 6.383, Dr Investment in subsidiary 1.617, Cr Bank 8.000. FY 2025-26: Dr Loan 0.606, Cr Interest income 0.606 (closing 6.989). FY 2026-27: interest 0.664; repayment ₹4 Cr; closing 3.653. FY 2027-28: interest 0.347; repayment ₹4 Cr; closing 0.

Connections

  • s05 sets the general principle of fair value at initial recognition and day-1 differences for subsidiaries and employees.
  • c02: the unwinding schedule is a standard amortised-cost roll-forward.
  • w3_t5 consolidation eliminates the loan, the notional interest and the deemed contribution.
  • w3_t3: tax base of the loan equals its carrying amount because neither the notional interest nor the principal recovery is taxable; the uplift in investment is an outside-basis difference (12.39).
  • Transfer pricing: interest-free loans to foreign subsidiaries may attract a transfer-pricing adjustment under Sec 92; the Ind AS notional income is not the same as the arm's-length interest for tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. Entries in both entities [L2]

On 1 April 2025 Vedant Pharma Ltd lends ₹15 Cr interest-free to its 100% subsidiary, repayable in full on 31 March 2029. The subsidiary's market borrowing rate is 10.5%. Prepare the entries in both entities for FY 2025-26 and the consolidation eliminations at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: The parent's notional interest income and the subsidiary's notional finance cost eliminate against each other.

Q2. Loan to a fellow subsidiary [L2]

Subsidiary A lends ₹5 Cr interest-free for 3 years to fellow subsidiary B, both owned 100% by P. Market rate 9%. Explain the entries in A and B and why neither shows an expense or income for the day-1 difference.

Q3. Demand loan review [L1]

A parent has given ₹20 Cr interest-free to a subsidiary, documented as repayable on demand, but has not demanded repayment for six years and has no intention to. Discuss whether discounting is required and what other accounting questions arise.
