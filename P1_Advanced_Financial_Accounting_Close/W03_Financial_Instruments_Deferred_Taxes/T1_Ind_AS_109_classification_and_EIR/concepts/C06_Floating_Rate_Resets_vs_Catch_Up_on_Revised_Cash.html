Key concept

Floating-Rate Resets vs Catch-Up on Revised Cash Flow Estimates

Concept: Floating-rate resets change EIR prospectively; other cash-flow estimate changes → catch-up at original EIR

Statement

Two different revision mechanisms apply to amortised-cost instruments:

  1. Market-rate resets on floating-rate instruments (B5.4.5): periodic re-estimation of cash flows to reflect movements in market interest rates alters the effective interest rate. The carrying amount is not remeasured; the new rate applies from the reset date.
  2. Other revisions of estimated cash flows (B5.4.6): the carrying amount is adjusted to the present value of revised estimated contractual cash flows discounted at the original EIR (or the current EIR for floating-rate instruments), with the adjustment recognised immediately in P&L:

\[ \Delta = \sum_{t} \frac{CF^{\text{revised}}_t}{(1 + r_0)^{\tau_t}} - AC_{\text{current}} \]

where \( r_0 \) is the original (or current floating) EIR, \( CF^{\text{revised}}_t \) the revised expected cash flows, \( \tau_t \) the time to each flow, and \( AC_{\text{current}} \) the carrying amount before revision. A positive \( \Delta \) on a liability is an expense.

Explanation

The distinction keeps P&L faithful to the instrument's economics. A floating-rate loan is designed to reprice to market: if repo rises, interest rises, and there is no "gain" or "loss" to recognise — only higher future finance cost. Applying B5.4.6 to a rate reset would wrongly recognise the PV of all future rate changes immediately, and then reverse it later.

By contrast, a change in the expected timing or amount of cash flows for other reasons (expected prepayment, expected exercise of a call, revised estimate of a performance-linked margin step-down that passes SPPI) changes the value of the instrument at the original yield. Catching up immediately keeps the remaining schedule at the original EIR, so the fee unwind over the remaining life reflects the new expected life.

Exclusions: contractual modifications (s07 — 10% test, modification gain/loss), and changes in expected credit losses (recognised through the ECL allowance, not B5.4.6). B5.4.6 applies to both assets and liabilities.

Illustration

Reset. Kabir Electricals Ltd has a ₹25 Cr 4-year bullet loan at repo + 3% (9.5% at drawdown) with ₹30 lakh fees. Using the spread method, the fee adds about 0.33% a year, so the EIR is about 9.83%. After 12 months repo falls 50 bps: the coupon becomes 9.0% and the EIR about 9.33% prospectively. No entry other than the period's interest.

Catch-up. A fixed-rate 9% ₹10 Cr bullet loan maturing 31 March 2030 has carrying amount ₹9.836 Cr at 31 March 2027 at an original EIR of 9.654%. Management now expects to prepay at par on 31 March 2028:

\[ \frac{10.90}{1.09654} = 9.940 \text{ Cr} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \Delta = 0.104 \text{ Cr} \]

Dr Finance cost 0.104, Cr Borrowings 0.104 at 31 March 2027.

Connections

  • s06 develops both rules with fee-classification detail; c05 and s07 deal with modifications, which follow a separate path.
  • Pitfall: treating every interest reset as a modification.
  • c02: catch-up resets the opening balance in the roll-forward.
  • FP&A: forecast models for floating debt should project finance cost from a rate curve without catch-ups; prepayment scenarios should show the one-off acceleration of fees.
  • Ind AS 107.40 interest-rate sensitivity uses floating-rate exposure, which is affected by resets but not by catch-ups.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choose the mechanism [L1]

For each event state whether B5.4.5, B5.4.6, modification accounting or ECL applies: (a) MCLR reset from 8.9% to 9.1%; (b) borrower now expects to prepay two years early; (c) lender agrees to cut the spread by 75 bps; (d) borrower's credit deteriorates and the lender expects partial non-recovery.

Q2. Catch-up computation [L2]

A ₹40 Cr 5-year 8.5% bullet NCD with ₹60 lakh issue costs (EIR 8.88%) is callable at par by the issuer after year 3. At the end of year 2, the issuer decides it will call at the end of year 3. Compute the carrying amount before revision, the revised carrying amount and the catch-up entry.

💡 Hint: Discount the revised year-3 cash flow at the original EIR.

Q3. Floating loan with fees across a reset [L2]

A ₹15 Cr 3-year T-bill-linked loan at 8.75% carries ₹22.5 lakh fees. After year 1, the rate resets to 9.5%. Show how the EIR and the remaining fee amortisation are updated using the re-solve method, and compare total finance cost in year 2 with the result under the spread method.
