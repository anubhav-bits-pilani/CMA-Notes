Key concept

Amortised-Cost Roll-Forward: Opening Plus EIR Interest Less Cash

Concept: Amortised cost (closing) = Opening + Interest at EIR − Cash paid (interest + principal)

Statement

For a financial liability at amortised cost, each period:

\[ AC_{\text{close}} = AC_{\text{open}} + AC_{\text{open}} \times \left[(1 + r)^{d/365} - 1\right] - (I_{\text{paid}} + R_{\text{paid}}) \]

where \( AC \) is amortised cost, \( r \) the annual EIR, \( d \) the days in the period, \( I_{\text{paid}} \) contractual interest paid and \( R_{\text{paid}} \) principal repaid. For a financial asset the same equation holds with cash received, and the gross carrying amount is reduced by the loss allowance to give amortised cost (Ind AS 109 Appendix A). With equal periods and a periodic EIR \( r_p \), the interest term simplifies to \( AC_{\text{open}} \times r_p \).

Explanation

The roll-forward is the "engine" of every EIR schedule. Interest at EIR is the P&L finance cost; cash interest is the coupon; the difference — the fee unwind or discount accretion — increases the carrying amount. Principal payments reduce it. Because the EIR was solved so that the PV of all cash flows equals the initial net carrying amount, the closing balance after the last payment must be zero. A non-zero residual means the EIR, a date, or a cash flow is wrong.

Two implementation choices matter:

  • Compounding vs simple day-count. XIRR assumes annual compounding on actual/365. Using simple interest (opening × r × d/365) within the year makes the schedule close with a small residual. Either compute interest with the compounding formula, or solve the EIR with the same convention you use in the schedule (e.g., periodic IRR).
  • Accruals at reporting dates. If payment dates do not coincide with 31 March, split the period at the reporting date: accrue interest to 31 March (part to borrowings, part as interest accrued under other financial liabilities, per Schedule III), then continue.

The schedule also supports the current/non-current split: the current portion is the principal repayable within 12 months (Ind AS 1.69), while the carrying amount adjustment (unamortised fees) is usually allocated to the non-current portion or pro rata.

Connections

  • c01 supplies the EIR; c03 sets the opening balance net of fees.
  • The same engine runs the Ind AS 116 lease liability, security deposits (s05) and Ind AS 37 provision unwinding — the week's first cross-cutting theme.
  • s06 catch-ups reset the opening balance mid-schedule; s07 modification gains reset it to the PV of new flows.
  • In a 3-statement model, closing amortised cost links to the balance-sheet debt line and EIR interest to the P&L.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build and check a schedule [L2]

A ₹6 Cr loan is drawn on 1 April 2025 with ₹9 lakh fees, 8.75% coupon payable half-yearly, and principal of ₹1 Cr repayable every half-year from 30 September 2027. Build the half-yearly amortised-cost schedule to maturity, show the check that the final balance is zero, and pass the entries for FY 2025-26.

💡 Hint: Solve the half-yearly IRR on net proceeds first, then use it consistently in each row.

Q2. Reporting date not on a payment date [L2]

A loan pays interest on 30 June and 31 December each year. Explain how you would structure the schedule so that the 31 March balance sheet shows borrowings at amortised cost and interest accrued separately, and give the Schedule III line items used.

Q3. Diagnosing a residual [L1]

A reviewer finds that an EIR schedule closes at ₹1.8 lakh instead of zero after the last repayment. List four likely causes and how to test each.
