Key concept

EIR Definition and Computing It with IRR and XIRR

Concept: EIR = rate discounting estimated future cash flows to net initial carrying amount; Excel IRR/XIRR on (net proceeds, −interest, −principal)

Statement

The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash payments or receipts through the expected life of the financial instrument to its gross carrying amount (asset) or amortised cost (liability) at initial recognition (Ind AS 109 Appendix A). For a borrowing:

\[ P_0 - C_0 = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{I_t + R_t}{(1 + r)^{\tau_t}} \]

where \( P_0 \) is gross proceeds, \( C_0 \) the directly attributable transaction costs and integral fees, \( I_t \) the contractual interest paid on date \( t \), \( R_t \) the principal repaid on date \( t \), \( \tau_t \) the time from drawdown to date \( t \) in years (actual days ÷ 365), and \( r \) the annual EIR. The calculation uses estimated cash flows considering all contractual terms (prepayment, call and similar options) but not expected credit losses (except for credit-impaired assets).

Explanation

The EIR converts every cost of borrowing — coupon, fees, discounts, premiums — into a single constant yield on the outstanding carrying amount. Because fees reduce net proceeds, the EIR exceeds the coupon. The "expected life" is normally the contractual life; a shorter life is used only when the entity can reliably estimate earlier settlement.

In Excel there are two routes:

  • IRR for equally spaced periods (monthly, quarterly). The result is a periodic rate; annualise as \( (1+r_p)^{m} - 1 \) where \( m \) is periods per year.
  • XIRR for actual dates. It returns an annual rate with actual/365 compounding, ideal for loans with moratoria, broken first periods or irregular repayments.

The cash flow vector from the borrower's view is: net proceeds positive on the drawdown date, then each interest and principal payment negative on its date.

=XIRR(B2:B22, A2:A22)
=(1+IRR(C2:C21))^4-1

If the vector does not change sign exactly once, IRR may have multiple solutions; supply a guess close to the coupon.

Illustration

Ritu Polymers Ltd borrows ₹10 Cr on 1 April 2025 at 9.5%, interest paid annually, repayable ₹5 Cr on 31 March 2029 and ₹5 Cr on 31 March 2030. Fees and stamp duty total ₹18 lakh.

Date Cash flow (₹ Cr)
1 Apr 2025 +9.820
31 Mar 2026 −0.950
31 Mar 2027 −0.950
31 Mar 2028 −0.950
31 Mar 2029 −5.950
31 Mar 2030 −5.475

IRR on this annual vector is about 10.02%; XIRR on the actual dates gives a very similar number (365 or 366 days per year). The 0.52 percentage-point excess over the coupon is the fee spread; it applies to a falling balance, so it is not simply 1.8% ÷ 5.

Practice Questions

Q1. XIRR with a moratorium [L2]

A ₹12 Cr loan drawn on 15 May 2025 carries 10% interest payable quarterly on calendar quarter-ends (first period broken), with principal in 12 equal quarterly instalments from 30 September 2026. Fees total ₹20 lakh. Lay out the dated cash flow vector and the Excel formula to compute the EIR, and explain why IRR on quarterly flows would be inaccurate here.

💡 Hint: The first interest period is short; think about which function handles unequal spacing.

Q2. Annualising a periodic IRR [L1]

The quarterly IRR on a loan's cash flows is 2.41%. Compute the annual EIR and explain why multiplying by 4 understates it.

Q3. Lender's EIR on an NBFC loan [L2]

An NBFC disburses ₹50 lakh at 14% for 3 years with equal monthly instalments, collecting a 2% processing fee and paying a 1% DSA commission. Set up the cash flow vector from the lender's perspective and state whether the lender's EIR is above or below 14%, with reasons.
