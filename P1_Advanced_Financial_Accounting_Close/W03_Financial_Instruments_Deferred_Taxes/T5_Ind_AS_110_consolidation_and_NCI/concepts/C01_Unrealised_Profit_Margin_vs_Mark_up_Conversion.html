Key concept

Unrealised Profit: Margin vs Mark-up Conversion

Concept: Unrealised profit = Intra-group inventory on hand × Gross margin % (or Markup ÷ (1 + Markup))

Statement

\[ \text{URP} = I \times g = I \times \frac{m}{1 + m} \]

where:

  • \(I\) = carrying amount (at the buyer's cost, i.e., intra-group transfer price) of goods bought from another group entity and still held at the reporting date;
  • \(g\) = seller's gross margin as a percentage of the intra-group selling price;
  • \(m\) = seller's mark-up as a percentage of its cost.

The relationship \(g = m \div (1 + m)\) converts a mark-up on cost into a margin on sales. URP is eliminated in full for subsidiaries (110.B86(c)), and to the extent of the investor's share for associates/JVs (Ind AS 28.28).

Explanation

The buyer records inventory at the price it paid — which includes the seller's profit. From the group's perspective that profit is not earned until the goods are sold to an outside party. So the consolidated carrying amount must be brought back to the group's original cost, which means stripping out the seller's margin on the goods still on hand.

Practical points:

  • Margin vs mark-up. "Sold at cost plus 25%" means \(m = 25\%\), \(g = 20\%\). "Sold at a 25% margin" means \(g = 25\%\). Exam and real-life errors almost always come from applying the mark-up percentage to the selling price.
  • Only the portion on hand. If 70% has been sold on, the profit on that 70% is realised.
  • Which margin. Use the actual intra-group margin of the specific transactions (or a reliable weighted average by product), not the seller's overall gross margin which includes third-party sales.
  • Freight and other costs. If the buyer's inventory cost includes freight-in paid to a third party, that freight is a real group cost; only the seller's profit element is eliminated.
  • Opening URP. Last year's URP reverses this year as the goods are sold; the P&L effect is closing URP minus opening URP.
  • Tax. A DTA arises at the buyer's tax rate (c05).

Illustration

Ankur Ltd (parent) sells components to its 80% subsidiary Bija Ltd at cost plus 20%. During FY26, sales were ₹180 Cr. Bija's inventory of these components was ₹24 Cr at 31 March 2025 and ₹42 Cr at 31 March 2026.

\[ g = \frac{0.20}{1.20} = 16.67\% \]

  • Closing URP = \(42 \times 16.67\% = 7.0\) Cr
  • Opening URP = \(24 \times 16.67\% = 4.0\) Cr
  • Net increase in elimination (charged to FY26 COGS) = ₹3.0 Cr; DT credit at 25.168% = ₹0.76 Cr.
  • Downstream (parent is seller) → entirely attributable to owners.

If someone wrongly applied 20% to the closing inventory, the closing URP would be ₹8.4 Cr — an overstatement of the elimination by ₹1.4 Cr.

Connections

Links to s05 (direction and attribution), c02 (journal entries for upstream vs downstream), c05 (DTA on eliminated profit), s08 (investor-share elimination for associates). The Saturday case test requires this computation. In FP&A, intra-group margin and inventory-days assumptions drive the consolidated gross margin forecast; a group that grows intra-group inventory faster than external sales will see a margin drag from rising URP.

Practice Questions

Q1. Convert and compute [L1]

A subsidiary sells to its parent at a 33⅓% mark-up on cost. The parent's closing inventory includes ₹60 Cr of such goods (₹45 Cr at the start of the year). Compute the closing URP, the opening URP and the FY26 P&L effect before tax.

Q2. Mixed product margins [L2]

Intra-group inventory at year-end: Product X ₹20 Cr (mark-up 25%), Product Y ₹35 Cr (margin 15% on sales), Product Z ₹10 Cr (transferred at cost plus freight of ₹0.5 Cr paid to an outside transporter, no profit). Compute total URP.

💡 Hint: Convert each to a margin on transfer price; consider whether the freight is a group cost.

Q3. Review the working [L2]

A consolidation working applies the seller's overall company gross margin of 32% to all intra-group inventory, while the intra-group transfer price policy is cost plus 10%. Explain the error, its direction and how you would correct the working.
