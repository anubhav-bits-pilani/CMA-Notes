Key concept

Stake Changes without Loss of Control through Equity

Concept: Stake change without loss of control: Consideration − Change in NCI carrying amount → equity attributable to owners

Statement

For a change in the parent's ownership interest in a subsidiary that does not result in loss of control (Ind AS 110.23, B96):

\[ \Delta E_{\text{owners}} = \Delta \text{NCI} - \text{FV of consideration paid} \]

for a purchase of NCI (usually a debit to owners' equity when consideration exceeds the NCI carrying amount acquired), and

\[ \Delta E_{\text{owners}} = \text{FV of consideration received} - \Delta \text{NCI} \]

for a sale to NCI (usually a credit). Here \(\Delta \text{NCI}\) is the change in the NCI carrying amount reflecting the change in relative interests (including the proportionate share of accumulated OCI reattributed). No gain or loss in P&L; goodwill is not remeasured.

Explanation

Once control exists, NCI holders are treated as owners of the group. A purchase or sale between the parent and NCI is a transaction between owners — like a buy-back or share issue — so its difference from carrying amount goes to equity. This reflects the "economic entity" view of Ind AS 110.

Mechanics:

  1. Compute the NCI carrying amount immediately before the transaction (including its share of PPA balances and, under the fair value method, goodwill).
  2. Compute \(\Delta \text{NCI}\) = carrying amount × (change in NCI % ÷ NCI % before), or for increases in NCI, the new NCI % × net assets (after any new cash) less old NCI.
  3. Reattribute OCI components (FCTR under Ind AS 21.48C, FVOCI reserves, hedge reserves) between owners and NCI.
  4. Record the difference in owners' equity — many Indian groups use a separate reserve line ("transactions with NCI") within other equity.
  5. Transaction costs and current tax on any gain in the parent's standalone books are recognised in equity in the CFS (Ind AS 12.61A).

Illustration

Purchase. P owns 70% of S; NCI carrying amount ₹240 Cr (including ₹18 Cr NCI share of FCTR). P buys 12% from the minority for ₹130 Cr.

  • \(\Delta \text{NCI} = 240 \times 12/30 = 96\) Cr (of which FCTR 7.2).
  • Net debit to owners' equity = 130 − 96 = ₹34 Cr, of which ₹7.2 Cr of FCTR is reattributed from NCI to owners.

Owners' equity falls by ₹34 Cr net (−41.2 + 7.2); total FCTR in the group is unchanged — only its attribution moves.

Sale. P sells 10% of a 100% subsidiary (net assets in CFS ₹600 Cr) for ₹90 Cr: NCI recognised ₹60 Cr; credit to owners' equity ₹30 Cr. The standalone capital gain of P is P&L in standalone books but equity in the CFS.

Practice Questions

Q1. Buy-out of NCI [L1]

P owns 80% of S; NCI carrying amount ₹150 Cr. P buys the remaining 20% for ₹210 Cr and incurs ₹2 Cr legal costs. Pass the CFS entry and state the effect on goodwill.

Q2. Dilution through a new issue [L2]

S (P owns 100%; CFS net assets ₹400 Cr) issues new shares for ₹150 Cr to a PE investor, giving the investor 25% post-issue. Compute NCI recognised and the effect on owners' equity.

💡 Hint: NCI is 25% of post-issue net assets including the cash raised.

Q3. Tax on sale within control [L2]

P sells 15% of its WOS for ₹300 Cr; P's cost of investment is ₹90 Cr (standalone LTCG at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess, effective about 14.95%). The NCI recognised is ₹180 Cr. Prepare the CFS entries including current tax, citing the relevant Ind AS 12 paragraph.
