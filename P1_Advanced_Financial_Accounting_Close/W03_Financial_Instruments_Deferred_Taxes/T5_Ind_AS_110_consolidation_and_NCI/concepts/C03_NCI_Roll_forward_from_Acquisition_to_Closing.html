Key concept

NCI Roll-forward from Acquisition to Closing

Concept: NCI closing = NCI at acquisition + NCI share of post-acquisition profit and OCI − Dividends to NCI

Statement

\[ \text{NCI}_{\text{close}} = \text{NCI}_{\text{acq}} + n \times \sum (\text{PAT}^{\text{adj}} + \text{OCI}^{\text{adj}}) - \text{Div}_{\text{NCI}} \pm \Delta_{\text{ownership}} \]

where:

  • \(\text{NCI}_{\text{acq}}\) = NCI at acquisition date (fair value or proportionate share of identifiable net assets, 103.19);
  • \(n\) = NCI's present ownership percentage (changing over time if ownership changes);
  • \(\text{PAT}^{\text{adj}}\), \(\text{OCI}^{\text{adj}}\) = subsidiary's post-acquisition profit and OCI as included in the CFS — after PPA amortisation/depreciation and DT unwind, policy alignment, and upstream URP eliminations;
  • \(\text{Div}_{\text{NCI}}\) = dividends declared to NCI;
  • \(\Delta_{\text{ownership}}\) = adjustments from changes in ownership without loss of control (110.23).

Check: under the proportionate method, \(\text{NCI}_{\text{close}} = n \times \text{Subsidiary net assets in CFS (incl. unamortised PPA, net of URP)}\); under the fair value method, add the NCI's share of goodwill (less any impairment allocated to NCI).

Explanation

NCI is a running balance, not a recomputed one — but the two views must agree. The roll-forward explains movements for the statement of changes in equity; the net-assets check proves the balance. Differences usually come from:

  • forgetting PPA adjustments in the NCI's profit share (NCI shares in extra depreciation and amortisation on step-ups);
  • downstream URP wrongly charged to NCI, or upstream URP not charged;
  • OCI items (FCTR, actuarial remeasurements) not attributed;
  • dividends paid to NCI not deducted;
  • losses capped at zero instead of being attributed (B94).

Illustration

P acquired 80% of S on 1 April 2024. Identifiable net assets at FV ₹500 Cr (including a brand step-up of ₹60 Cr, life 10 years, and its DTL); NCI proportionate = ₹100 Cr.

₹ Cr FY25 FY26
S's reported PAT 50.0 70.0
Brand amortisation net of DT: 6 × (1 − 0.25168) (4.5) (4.5)
Upstream URP (closing less opening, net of tax) (1.5) (0.8)
Adjusted PAT 44.0 64.7
S's OCI (actuarial loss) (2.0) 1.0
Dividend declared by S 10.0 20.0

NCI roll-forward:

₹ Cr FY25 FY26
Opening NCI 100.0 106.4
Share of adjusted PAT (20%) 8.8 12.9
Share of OCI (20%) (0.4) 0.2
Dividends to NCI (20%) (2.0) (4.0)
Closing NCI 106.4 115.5

Check at 31 March 2026: S's net assets in CFS = 500 + (44.0 − 2.0 − 10.0) + (64.7 + 1.0 − 20.0) = 577.7; 20% = ₹115.5 Cr. Agrees.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build an NCI roll-forward [L2]

P acquired 60% of S on 1 April 2025 with NCI at fair value ₹250 Cr (identifiable net assets ₹550 Cr; goodwill ₹40 Cr of which ₹10 Cr relates to NCI). FY26: S's PAT ₹80 Cr; PPA depreciation net of DT ₹6 Cr; upstream URP net of tax ₹3 Cr; FCTR gain ₹15 Cr; dividend ₹25 Cr. Prepare the roll-forward and the net-assets check.

💡 Hint: Under the fair value method the check includes NCI's share of goodwill.

Q2. Find the error [L2]

The NCI roll-forward closes at ₹212 Cr, but 25% of the subsidiary's net assets in the CFS is ₹205 Cr (proportionate method). The subsidiary has a PPA brand amortised at ₹20 Cr per year (DT 25.168%), and the team has used reported PAT. Explain and quantify the likely cause, and identify one other possible cause to test.

Q3. Model-build: NCI schedule [L3]

Design an Excel NCI schedule for three subsidiaries (one with a mid-year acquisition, one with a 10% stake increase in Q3, one loss-making). Specify columns, formulas for time-apportioned profit, the treatment of the stake change, and the check row against net assets.
