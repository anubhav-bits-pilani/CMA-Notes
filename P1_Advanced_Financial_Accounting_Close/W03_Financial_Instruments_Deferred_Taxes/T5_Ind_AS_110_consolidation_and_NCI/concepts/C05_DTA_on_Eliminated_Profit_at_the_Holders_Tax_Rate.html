Key concept

DTA on Eliminated Profit at the Holder's Tax Rate

Concept: DTA on eliminated profit = Unrealised profit × Tax rate of the entity holding the inventory

Statement

\[ \text{DTA} = U \times t_{h} \]

where \(U\) is the unrealised intra-group profit eliminated from the carrying amount of inventory (or PPE) and \(t_{h}\) is the tax rate enacted or substantively enacted for the entity holding the asset (the buyer), applicable to the period in which the asset will be recovered (Ind AS 12.47). Recognised subject to the probability of future taxable profit in that entity (12.24, 12.27–31).

Explanation

Tax is levied entity by entity. The buyer's tax base for the inventory is its purchase price (including the seller's profit). On consolidation, the carrying amount is reduced by \(U\) to group cost. So:

\[ \text{Carrying amount (CFS)} < \text{Tax base (buyer)} \Rightarrow \text{deductible temporary difference} = U \]

When the buyer sells the inventory to a third party, it will claim the full tax base as a deduction — its taxable profit will be lower than the group's accounting profit on that sale. That reversal occurs in the buyer's tax return, so the buyer's rate is the correct measure.

Why not the seller's rate? The seller has already paid current tax on its profit. That tax remains a real cost of the group. The DTA represents the future tax saving when the buyer sells the goods — measured by the buyer's rate. Deferring the seller's tax instead (and so effectively using the seller's rate) is the US GAAP approach for intra-entity inventory transfers under ASC 740-10-25-3(e), retained even after ASU 2016-16 removed it for other assets; Ind AS 12 has no such exception.

Points:

  • Same-country groups at the same rate (e.g., all 115BAA at 25.168%): no difference. With some Indian entities in the old regime (34.944%) and others in 115BAA, the rate matters.
  • Cross-border groups: a large differential (e.g., UAE 9% seller, Indian buyer 25.168%) changes consolidated ETR.
  • Recoverability: if the buyer has losses and no convincing evidence of future profits, the DTA may not be recognised.
  • Attribution: for upstream sales, the DTA is attributed between owners and NCI in the same proportion as the URP (c02).
  • PPE transfers: DTA on the unamortised eliminated gain, unwinding as the excess depreciation is reversed.

Illustration

A Singapore subsidiary (17%, 100% owned) sells goods to the Indian parent (25.168%). Transfer price ₹200 Cr, margin 30%; the parent holds ₹50 Cr at year-end.

  • URP = 50 × 30% = ₹15 Cr; Singapore current tax already charged on this profit = 15 × 17% = ₹2.55 Cr.
  • DTA at the holder's (Indian parent's) rate = 15 × 25.168% = ₹3.78 Cr.
  • Consolidated tax effect of the elimination in the current year = DT credit ₹3.78 Cr against a pre-tax URP reduction of ₹15 Cr, so the group's ETR on this item reflects 25.168%, while the Singapore current tax of ₹2.55 Cr remains in current tax expense.
  • ETR reconciliation line: "Effect of tax rate differences on intra-group eliminations" = 15 × (25.168% − 17%) = ₹1.23 Cr.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute DTA across regimes [L1]

An Indian subsidiary under the old regime (34.944%) sells to the Indian parent under 115BAA (25.168%); closing URP is ₹12 Cr. Compute the DTA and explain the rate used.

Q2. Loss-making holder [L2]

A parent with unrecognised tax losses (no convincing evidence of future profits) holds inventory bought from a profitable subsidiary with URP ₹20 Cr. Explain whether the DTA is recognised and the effect on the consolidated ETR reconciliation.

💡 Hint: Recoverability is assessed in the entity that will claim the deduction.

Q3. ETR reconciliation line [L2]

Draft the ETR reconciliation lines arising from a URP elimination of ₹25 Cr where the seller is taxed at 15% and the holder at 25.168%, and the group statutory rate used for the reconciliation is 25.168%.
