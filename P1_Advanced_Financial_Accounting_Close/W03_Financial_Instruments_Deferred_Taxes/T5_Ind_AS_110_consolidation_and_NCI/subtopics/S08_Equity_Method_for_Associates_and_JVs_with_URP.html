Subtopic note

Equity Method for Associates and JVs with URP Elimination

Scope: Equity method for associates/JVs (Ind AS 28/111) with investor-share unrealised profit elimination

Why it matters

Indian groups hold many strategic minority stakes — 26% JV partners in insurance, defence and telecom, 30–49% stakes in listed associates, 50:50 joint ventures with foreign technology partners. These are not consolidated line by line but carried as a single line under the equity method, with a one-line share of profit in the P&L. The equity method is effectively "one-line consolidation": goodwill, PPA adjustments and unrealised profits all exist, but inside the investment balance. The key difference from subsidiaries is that intra-group profits are eliminated only to the extent of the investor's interest. Mistakes here inflate group profit and the investment carrying amount, and the equity method share often forms a material part of reported PAT for holding-company structures.

Core notes

Significant influence and joint control

  • Associate (Ind AS 28.3, 28.5–6) — an entity over which the investor has significant influence: the power to participate in financial and operating policy decisions, but not control or joint control. Presumed at 20% or more of voting power (rebuttable); indicators: board representation, participation in policy-making (including dividends), material transactions, interchange of managerial personnel, provision of essential technical information. Potential voting rights that are currently exercisable or convertible are considered (28.7–8).
  • Joint arrangement (Ind AS 111) — two or more parties have joint control: contractually agreed sharing of control, where decisions about relevant activities require unanimous consent of the parties sharing control (111.7).
  • Joint operation — parties have rights to the assets and obligations for the liabilities (e.g., unincorporated contract-mining arrangement or a structure where outputs are taken by the parties). Account for own share of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses line by line (111.20).
  • Joint venture — parties have rights to the net assets of the arrangement (typically an incorporated JV company). Account using the equity method (111.24).

Equity method mechanics (28.10, 28.32)

  • Initial recognition at cost. On acquisition, the difference between cost and the investor's share of the net fair value of the investee's identifiable assets and liabilities is:
  • goodwill — included in the carrying amount, not amortised, not separately tested (28.32(a));
  • excess of share of net fair value over cost — under Ind AS (a carve-out from IAS 28, consistent with Ind AS 103), recognised directly in equity as capital reserve in the period of acquisition (28.32(b)).
  • Subsequently. Carrying amount is increased/decreased by the investor's share of profit or loss (in P&L) and share of OCI (in OCI), and reduced by dividends received (28.10). The share is computed after notional PPA adjustments (e.g., extra depreciation on FV step-ups, 28.32) and after uniform accounting policies (28.35–36).
  • Reporting date. The most recent financial statements; if dates differ, adjust for significant events; the difference shall be no more than three months — Ind AS 28.34 adds "unless it is impracticable", a known Ind AS modification.
  • Losses (28.38–39) — once the investor's share of losses reduces the interest (including long-term interests that in substance form part of the net investment, e.g., long-term loans) to zero, further losses are recognised only to the extent of legal or constructive obligations or payments made. Long-term interests are first subject to Ind AS 109 (including ECL) before equity-method losses are allocated (28.14A).
  • Impairment (28.40–43) — the entire carrying amount is tested as a single asset under Ind AS 36 when there is objective evidence of impairment; goodwill is not tested separately.
  • Discontinuing (28.22) — on loss of significant influence/joint control: retained interest at FV, gain/loss in P&L, OCI reclassified as if disposed.

Unrealised profit elimination (28.28–29)

Gains and losses from upstream (associate → investor) and downstream (investor → associate) transactions are recognised in the investor's financial statements only to the extent of unrelated investors' interests in the associate. The investor's share of the gains/losses is eliminated.

\[ \text{URP eliminated} = \text{Unrealised profit} \times \text{Investor's \% interest} \]

If a downstream transaction provides evidence of a reduction in NRV or an impairment of the assets transferred, the loss is recognised in full (28.29).

Downstream illustration. Investor I holds 30% of associate A. I sells goods costing ₹40 Cr to A for ₹50 Cr; A holds all the goods at year-end. URP = ₹10 Cr; I's share = 30% × 10 = ₹3 Cr; DTA on the eliminated ₹3 Cr at 25.168% → ₹0.76 Cr (the goods are held by A; where A's tax rate differs, use the rate of the entity that will recover the asset, consistent with s05).

Two presentation approaches are seen in practice for downstream eliminations:

Approach Entry (₹ Cr)
Adjust revenue/COGS Dr Revenue 15 (30% × 50), Cr COGS 12 (30% × 40), Cr Investment in A 3
Adjust share of profit (common in India) Dr Share of profit of associate 3, Cr Investment in A 3

Upstream illustration. A sells goods costing ₹40 Cr to I for ₹50 Cr; I still holds them. I's share of A's profit includes the ₹10 Cr margin at 30% = ₹3 Cr. Eliminate: Dr Share of profit of associate 3, Cr Investment in A (or Cr Inventory) 3. Many groups reduce inventory in upstream cases because the asset sits in the investor's balance sheet.

Equity method roll-forward (₹ Cr)

I acquired 30% of A on 1 April 2025 for ₹300 Cr. A's FV of net assets ₹850 Cr (book ₹800 Cr; step-up of ₹50 Cr on plant with 10-year remaining life; DT at 25.168%). FY26: A's PAT ₹90 Cr, OCI ₹10 Cr, dividend ₹20 Cr; closing URP on downstream sales ₹6 Cr.

Line Amount
Cost 300.00
Share of net FV: 30% × (850 − 50 × 25.168%) = 30% × 837.4 251.22 (notional goodwill 48.78, inside the 300)
Share of PAT: 30% × 90 27.00
Less: notional extra depreciation net of DT: 30% × 5 × (1 − 0.25168) (1.12)
Less: downstream URP: 30% × 6 (1.80)
Share of OCI: 30% × 10 3.00
Less: dividend received: 30% × 20 (6.00)
Closing carrying amount 321.08

(DT on the URP elimination at the investor level adds a further ₹0.45 Cr DTA if recognised.)

Presentation

  • Balance sheet: a single non-current line "Investments accounted for using the equity method". P&L: a separate line "Share of profit/(loss) of associates and joint ventures accounted for using the equity method" (Ind AS 1.82(c)); Indian groups commonly present it immediately before "Profit before tax" (after exceptional items) — choose a position and apply it consistently. The share is post-tax, so it sits outside the tax expense line.
  • Disclosures under Ind AS 112: summarised financial information of material associates/JVs, reconciliation to carrying amount, commitments and contingent liabilities.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • 26% JV with veto rights — joint control requires unanimous consent over relevant activities; a veto only over fundamental changes is protective → associate, not JV.
  • Joint operation structured through a company — still a joint operation if contractual terms or other facts (e.g., parties take all output and fund all liabilities) give rights to assets and obligations for liabilities (111.B29–B32).
  • Investor share of losses beyond zero — keep a memorandum record of unrecognised losses; resume recognition only after share of profits equals unrecognised losses.
  • Dividends in excess of carrying amount — recognised in P&L once carrying amount is nil, absent obligations.
  • Associate's own subsidiaries — use the associate's consolidated statements (owners' share).
  • Transactions contributing a business to an associate — the 2014 IFRS amendment conflict (IFRS 10 vs IAS 28) is deferred; check the Ind AS 28 text on contribution of non-monetary assets (28.30–31A).

Key takeaways

  • Associates: significant influence (≥20% rebuttable presumption); JVs: joint control with rights to net assets; joint operations: line-by-line share of assets and liabilities.
  • Equity method = cost + share of post-acquisition profit/OCI − dividends, after notional PPA and policy alignment.
  • Goodwill sits inside the investment; excess of share of FV over cost → capital reserve (Ind AS carve-out).
  • URP on upstream and downstream transactions eliminated only to the investor's share; downstream impairment losses recognised in full.
  • Losses stop at zero (after long-term interests), except for obligations.
  • Test the whole investment for impairment under Ind AS 36 when indicators exist.

Practice Questions

Q1. Equity method roll-forward [L2]

An investor acquires 40% of an associate on 1 April 2025 for ₹480 Cr. The associate's book net assets are ₹1,000 Cr, with an unrecognised brand of FV ₹100 Cr (life 10 years; DT 25.168%). FY26: PAT ₹120 Cr, OCI loss ₹15 Cr, dividend ₹30 Cr. Upstream URP in the investor's closing inventory ₹8 Cr. Prepare the investment roll-forward and compute the share of profit in the P&L.

💡 Hint: Compute notional goodwill first, then layer each adjustment.

Q2. Joint venture or joint operation? [L2]

Two companies incorporate a 50:50 JV company to operate a captive power plant; each must buy 50% of the output at cost-plus prices, and the JV has no other customers; the parties fund all its debt service. Classify the arrangement under Ind AS 111 and state the accounting in each party's CFS.

Q3. Downstream loss [L1]

An investor sells equipment to its 25% associate for ₹12 Cr; the carrying amount was ₹15 Cr and the fair value is ₹12 Cr. State how much of the loss is recognised and why.

Q4. Losses beyond zero [L2]

An investor holds 35% of a JV with carrying amount ₹20 Cr plus a long-term loan to the JV of ₹30 Cr (ECL allowance ₹5 Cr). The JV's loss for the year is ₹120 Cr. The investor has no further funding obligation. Compute the losses recognised, the order of allocation, and the memorandum amount carried forward.

Q5. Capital reserve on associate acquisition [L1]

An investor buys 30% of a stressed company for ₹50 Cr when its FV of net assets is ₹200 Cr. Explain the Ind AS treatment of the difference and how it differs from IFRS.
