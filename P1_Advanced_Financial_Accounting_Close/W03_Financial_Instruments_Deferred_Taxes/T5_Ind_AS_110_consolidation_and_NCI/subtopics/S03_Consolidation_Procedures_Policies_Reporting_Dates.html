Subtopic note

Consolidation Procedures: Policies, Reporting Dates and Aggregation

Scope: Procedures (B86): uniform policies, reporting-date differences ≤3 months (B93), line-by-line aggregation

Why it matters

Consolidation is a mechanical process with judgement at the edges. Before any elimination can be passed, the group must ensure each entity's numbers are prepared on the same accounting policies, for the same period, in the same chart of accounts, and translated into the presentation currency. In Indian groups this pre-consolidation work is where most effort goes: subsidiaries on different ERPs (Tally at small units, SAP at the parent), foreign subsidiaries with December year-ends, entities following AS (Indian GAAP) or local GAAP that must be converted to Ind AS for group reporting. Listed groups do this quarterly under SEBI LODR Reg 33, often within 45 days.

Core notes

The consolidation procedure (110.B86)

Consolidated financial statements:

  • (a) Combine like items of assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows of the parent with those of its subsidiaries — line by line.
  • (b) Offset (eliminate) the carrying amount of the parent's investment in each subsidiary and the parent's portion of equity of each subsidiary (Ind AS 103 explains goodwill accounting).
  • (c) Eliminate in full intra-group assets and liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows relating to transactions between group entities. Profits or losses resulting from intra-group transactions recognised in assets (inventory, PPE) are eliminated in full. Intra-group losses may indicate an impairment that requires recognition. Ind AS 12 applies to temporary differences from eliminating profits and losses.

Income and expenses of a subsidiary are based on the amounts of assets and liabilities recognised in the CFS at the acquisition date — e.g., depreciation is based on PPA fair values, not the subsidiary's book values (B88).

Uniform accounting policies (110.19, B87)

A parent prepares CFS using uniform accounting policies for like transactions and other events in similar circumstances. If a member uses different policies, appropriate adjustments are made in preparing the CFS.

Under the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, once a company applies Ind AS, its Indian holding, subsidiary, joint venture and associate companies must also apply Ind AS in their own statutory accounts, so Indian group members are normally on Ind AS already. Alignment work therefore concentrates on foreign subsidiaries (local GAAP or IFRS statutory accounts), on entities outside the Rules (LLPs, trusts), and on policy choices within Ind AS that differ between entities. Foreign subsidiaries submit an Ind AS group reporting package rather than changing their local statutory accounts.

Reporting dates (110.B92–B93)

  • The financial statements of the parent and subsidiaries used in preparing CFS shall have the same reporting date (B92).
  • If the reporting dates differ, the subsidiary prepares additional financial information as of the parent's reporting date, unless impracticable.
  • If impracticable, the parent consolidates the subsidiary using its most recent financial statements, adjusted for the effects of significant transactions or events between that date and the CFS date. The difference between the dates shall be no more than three months, and the length of the reporting periods and the difference in dates must be the same from period to period (B93). Ind AS (unlike IFRS 10) qualifies the three-month limit with "unless it is impracticable to do so" — check the notified text and document impracticability if relied upon.

Indian context: foreign subsidiaries commonly have a 31 December year-end (e.g., China, Germany). Indian listed groups usually require a 31 March "reporting package" from each such subsidiary; where that is impracticable, they use the December accounts adjusted for significant January–March events (a large impairment, a dividend, a refinancing). Note that Sec 2(41) of the Companies Act requires Indian companies to have a 31 March financial year (with Tribunal-approved exceptions for subsidiaries/holding companies of foreign companies), so mismatches arise mainly for foreign subsidiaries.

Line-by-line aggregation: the consolidation workbook

A practical workflow:

  1. Collect group reporting packages — trial balance mapped to the group chart of accounts; intra-group schedules by counterparty; fixed asset and DT schedules; FX translation.
  2. Policy alignment adjustments at entity level (column "Adj — policy").
  3. Translation of foreign operations into the presentation currency (₹) under Ind AS 21: assets/liabilities at closing rate, P&L at average rates, equity at historical rates, difference to FCTR.
  4. Aggregate — sum entity columns (parent + subsidiaries).
  5. Eliminations — investment vs equity at acquisition (with PPA and goodwill), intra-group balances and transactions, unrealised profits, dividends.
  6. NCI — compute NCI share of post-acquisition equity movements.
  7. Checks — Dr = Cr; consolidated TB balances; intra-group balances net to zero; NCI roll-forward ties; cash flow reconciles.

Translation pre-step (Ind AS 21.39) — brief

For a foreign subsidiary with a functional currency other than ₹: assets and liabilities translated at the closing rate; income and expenses at transaction-date rates (average rate as an approximation if rates do not fluctuate significantly); resulting differences in OCI as FCTR, attributed between owners and NCI. Goodwill and FV adjustments are treated as assets/liabilities of the foreign operation and translated at the closing rate (21.47).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Impracticability — must be genuine (systems or local statutory constraints), not convenience; document it and keep the gap constant year to year.
  • Significant events in the gap — e.g., a subsidiary's January bond issue or a March impairment; adjust.
  • Subsidiary uses a policy not permitted by Ind AS (e.g., revaluation of investment property) — mandatory alignment.
  • Policy choice differences permitted by Ind AS (cost vs revaluation of PPE classes) — still align at group level for like assets in similar circumstances.
  • Hyperinflationary subsidiaries — Ind AS 29 restatement before translation (rare in Indian groups, but relevant for subsidiaries in Argentina, Türkiye, etc.).
  • Interim CFS — quarterly consolidation may use estimates for small subsidiaries; materiality judgements must be documented for auditors' limited review.

Key takeaways

  • B86: combine line by line, eliminate investment vs equity, eliminate intra-group items in full, apply Ind AS 12 to eliminations.
  • Subsidiary income and expenses are based on acquisition-date CFS amounts (PPA values).
  • Uniform policies are mandatory; adjust at entity level before aggregation.
  • Same reporting date preferred; otherwise ≤3-month gap, adjusted for significant events, consistent period to period.
  • A disciplined workbook (entity columns, adjustment columns, elimination columns, checks) is the backbone of the close.
  • Translate foreign subsidiaries before aggregation; FCTR is split between owners and NCI.

Practice Questions

Q1. Policy alignment with DT [L2]

A subsidiary depreciates plant (cost ₹120 Cr, acquired 1 April 2024) at 15% WDV; the group uses SLM over 10 years with nil residual value. Tax WDV follows the 15% block rate. Compute the FY26 alignment adjustment to depreciation, the cumulative adjustment to opening reserves and the DT effect at 25.168%.

💡 Hint: Compare book carrying amounts under both methods; the tax base does not change.

Q2. December year-end subsidiary [L2]

A German subsidiary has a 31 December year-end and cannot prepare 31 March accounts. Between 1 January and 31 March 2026 it (i) received a EUR 5 million customer prepayment, (ii) impaired a CGU by EUR 8 million, (iii) paid a dividend of EUR 3 million to the Indian parent. Explain how the parent consolidates the subsidiary in its FY26 CFS and which events must be adjusted.

Q3. Build the workbook skeleton [L3]

Design a consolidation workbook for a parent with three subsidiaries (one foreign, one 70% owned, one acquired mid-year). Specify the tabs, column structure, named ranges, elimination journal layout, NCI schedule and at least six check rows, with sample Excel formulas for two checks.

Q4. Interview: why B88 matters [L1]

Explain why consolidated depreciation for a subsidiary acquired with a ₹150 Cr PPE step-up differs from the subsidiary's own depreciation, and how this is reflected in the consolidation workbook every year.
