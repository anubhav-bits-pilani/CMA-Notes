Subtopic note

Loss of Control and Foreign Subsidiary Translation (FCTR)

Scope: Loss of control (110.25): derecognise, remeasure retained interest, reclassify OCI; foreign subsidiaries translation and FCTR (Ind AS 21)

Why it matters

Selling a controlling stake, diluting below control, or having a subsidiary admitted into insolvency ends consolidation. Ind AS 110 treats loss of control as a significant economic event: the whole subsidiary is derecognised, any retained stake is remeasured to fair value, and OCI balances such as FCTR are recycled. The resulting gain can be large and is typically reported as an exceptional item. For Indian groups with overseas subsidiaries, translation and FCTR accounting runs through every quarter's consolidation and crystallises on disposal — forecasting the FCTR recycle is a frequent FP&A and investor-relations question.

Core notes

Loss of control (110.25, B97–B99)

When a parent loses control of a subsidiary, it:

  1. Derecognises the assets (including goodwill) and liabilities of the former subsidiary at their carrying amounts at the date control is lost, and the carrying amount of any NCI (including any OCI components attributable to it).
  2. Recognises: - the fair value of consideration received, if any; - any distribution of shares of the subsidiary to owners, if the transaction involves one; - any investment retained in the former subsidiary at fair value at the date control is lost — that fair value becomes its initial carrying amount as a financial asset (Ind AS 109) or the cost on initial recognition of an associate/JV (Ind AS 28).
  3. Reclassifies to P&L, or transfers directly to retained earnings if required by other Ind AS, the amounts recognised in OCI in relation to the subsidiary, on the same basis as if the parent had directly disposed of the related assets or liabilities (B99): - FCTR → reclassified to P&L (Ind AS 21.48); - FVOCI-debt reserve → reclassified to P&L; - cash flow hedge reserve → reclassified to P&L; - revaluation surplus (Ind AS 16) → transferred to retained earnings, not P&L; - FVOCI-equity reserve and actuarial remeasurements → not reclassified; may be transferred within equity.
  4. Recognises any resulting gain or loss in P&L attributable to the parent.

Multiple arrangements (B97) — two or more disposal transactions may be accounted as one if, e.g., entered into at the same time or in contemplation of each other, form a single transaction designed to achieve an overall commercial effect, or one is dependent on the other.

Gain on loss of control

\[ \text{Gain} = (\text{FV consideration} + \text{FV retained interest} + \text{NCI carrying amount}) - \text{Net assets incl. goodwill} + \text{OCI recycled} \]

where "OCI recycled" is the parent's share of reclassifiable OCI (positive if a credit balance, e.g., an FCTR gain).

Illustration. P holds 80% of S (Singapore). At 30 September 2025: S's net assets in CFS ₹500 Cr, goodwill ₹60 Cr, NCI carrying amount ₹100 Cr, accumulated FCTR attributable to owners ₹24 Cr (credit). P sells 50% for ₹380 Cr; the retained 30% (now an associate) has FV ₹225 Cr.

₹ Cr
Consideration 380
FV of retained 30% 225
NCI derecognised 100
Less: net assets and goodwill derecognised (500 + 60) (560)
Gain before OCI recycling 145
FCTR recycled to P&L 24
Gain on loss of control 169

The ₹225 Cr becomes the cost of the associate under Ind AS 28, with a fresh notional PPA of S at that date.

Common Indian triggers

  • Stake sale below 50% to a strategic partner (e.g., JV formation).
  • Dilution when the subsidiary issues shares to a new investor and the parent does not subscribe.
  • CIRP admission under IBC — control passes to the resolution professional; groups generally deconsolidate at the admission date, recognising the retained interest at fair value (often nil) and the related losses.
  • Contractual change — expiry of a shareholders' agreement giving board control.

Foreign subsidiaries: translation (Ind AS 21)

Functional currency (21.9–14) is the currency of the primary economic environment — determined by sales prices, costs, financing and retention of receipts. A foreign subsidiary that is merely an extension of the parent (e.g., a sales office remitting all cash) may have ₹ as functional currency, in which case no translation (only remeasurement) arises.

Translation to presentation currency (21.39):

Item Rate
Assets and liabilities (including goodwill and FV adjustments, 21.47) Closing rate at reporting date
Income and expenses Exchange rates at transaction dates (average rate for the period acceptable if rates do not fluctuate significantly, 21.40)
Equity components (share capital, pre-acquisition reserves) Historical rates
Resulting exchange differences OCI — foreign currency translation reserve (FCTR)

FCTR attribution — exchange differences attributable to NCI are allocated to NCI (21.41).

Illustration. A US subsidiary (functional currency USD), net assets USD 500 million at 1 April 2025, profit USD 60 million for FY26, no dividends. Rates: opening ₹83.0, average ₹84.5, closing ₹86.0.

₹ Cr (USD million × rate ÷ 10)
Opening net assets 500 × 83.0 4,150
Profit 60 × 84.5 507
Closing net assets 560 × 86.0 4,816
FCTR for the year = 4,816 − 4,150 − 507 159

Components: opening net assets 500 × (86.0 − 83.0) ÷ 10 = 150; profit 60 × (86.0 − 84.5) ÷ 10 = 9. Total ₹159 Cr credit to OCI.

Net investment in a foreign operation (21.15, 21.32–33)

A monetary item receivable from/payable to a foreign operation for which settlement is neither planned nor likely in the foreseeable future is part of the net investment. Exchange differences on it go to P&L in standalone statements but are reclassified to OCI (FCTR) in the CFS.

Disposal and partial disposal of a foreign operation (21.48–48D)

  • Disposal (including loss of control, loss of significant influence/joint control) → cumulative FCTR attributable to owners reclassified to P&L; FCTR attributable to NCI derecognised but not reclassified to P&L (21.48B).
  • Partial disposal without loss of control → proportionate share of FCTR reattributed to NCI (21.48C), not recycled.
  • Partial disposal of an associate/JV keeping significant influence → proportionate share of FCTR reclassified to P&L.
  • Write-down of a foreign operation's carrying amount (losses, impairment) is not a partial disposal (21.48D).

Tax

FCTR is an OCI item; any DT on the translation of a foreign operation (outside-basis difference, usually not recognised under 12.39 if the parent controls reversal) is also in OCI. On disposal, the capital gains tax computed in rupees on the actual shares sold is current tax in P&L.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Retained interest FV — Level 3 valuation often needed; avoid using the sale price per share if the sale block carried a control premium.
  • Deconsolidation with negative net assets — derecognising a deficit subsidiary can produce a gain; check for guarantees or funding commitments the parent must now recognise.
  • Disposal group held for sale — Ind AS 105 classification before loss of control; discontinued operation presentation if a major line of business.
  • Contingent consideration receivable — financial asset at FV, part of consideration.
  • Average rate appropriateness — volatile periods (e.g., sharp INR moves) require monthly averages or transaction rates.
  • Hyperinflationary economies — Ind AS 29 then translate all amounts at the closing rate (21.42).

Key takeaways

  • On loss of control: derecognise everything (assets, goodwill, NCI), recognise consideration and retained interest at FV, recycle OCI as if assets were disposed.
  • The gain is attributable to the parent and recognised in P&L.
  • Translation: closing rate for assets/liabilities, transaction/average rate for P&L, differences to FCTR attributed between owners and NCI.
  • FCTR recycles on disposal or loss of control; partial disposals within control only reattribute.
  • Net investment monetary items: FX in OCI in CFS.
  • Plan FCTR recycles in forecasts — they can dwarf the operating result of the disposed entity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Gain on loss of control [L2]

P owns 75% of S. At the date of sale: S's net assets in CFS ₹640 Cr; goodwill ₹90 Cr; NCI ₹160 Cr; FCTR attributable to owners ₹30 Cr (debit); revaluation surplus attributable to owners ₹20 Cr. P sells 60% for ₹700 Cr, retaining 15% (FVOCI-equity elected, FV ₹170 Cr). Compute the gain on loss of control and state the treatment of each OCI balance.

💡 Hint: Revaluation surplus is transferred within equity, not recycled.

Q2. FCTR computation [L2]

A UK subsidiary (GBP functional) has opening net assets GBP 30 million, FY26 profit GBP 4 million, dividend paid GBP 2 million on 30 September 2025. Rates: opening ₹105, 30 September ₹108, average ₹107, closing ₹110. P owns 80%. Compute the FCTR for FY26 and its split between owners and NCI.

Q3. Partial disposal within control [L1]

P reduces its stake in a foreign subsidiary from 90% to 70%. Accumulated FCTR attributable to owners is ₹45 Cr. Explain the accounting for the FCTR and contrast it with a reduction from 30% to 20% in a foreign associate.

Q4. CIRP admission [L3]

A 100% subsidiary (net assets in CFS ₹(−)80 Cr; parent guarantee of ₹150 Cr of its bank debt) is admitted into CIRP on 15 February 2026. Analyse whether control is lost, the deconsolidation accounting, the effect of the guarantee in the CFS after deconsolidation, and the disclosures.

Q5. Net investment designation [L2]

An Indian parent lends USD 20 million to its US subsidiary with no repayment schedule and no intention of repayment. The rupee moves from ₹83 to ₹86. State the FX accounting in the parent's standalone statements and in the CFS, and what happens if the parent later demands repayment.
