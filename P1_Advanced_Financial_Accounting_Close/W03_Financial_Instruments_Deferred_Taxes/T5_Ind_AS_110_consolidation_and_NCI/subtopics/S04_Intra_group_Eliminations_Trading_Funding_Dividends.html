Subtopic note

Intra-group Eliminations: Trading, Funding, Dividends and Guarantees

Scope: Intra-group eliminations: sales/purchases, receivables/payables, loans and interest, dividends, management fees, guarantees

Why it matters

A group cannot trade with itself, lend to itself or guarantee itself. Every intra-group transaction must disappear from the CFS, otherwise revenue, expenses, assets and liabilities are grossed up, and ratios (asset turnover, interest cover, debt/equity) become meaningless. In Indian groups intra-group flows are large — shared-service charges, brand royalties, treasury loans to subsidiaries, corporate guarantees to banks for subsidiary borrowings — and they rarely reconcile at period-end because of timing, GST, TDS and FX differences. Reconciliation and elimination are core close activities (→ Week 4 intercompany reconciliation) and a regular audit focus.

Core notes

Principle (110.B86(c))

Intra-group assets and liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows relating to transactions between entities of the group are eliminated in full — irrespective of the parent's percentage holding. NCI does not change the amount eliminated; it only affects how unrealised profits on upstream transactions are attributed (→ s05).

Sales and purchases

Dr Revenue (seller), Cr Purchases/Cost of goods sold (buyer), for the full intra-group sale value. If the goods have been sold on to third parties, no profit adjustment is required; if still in inventory, eliminate the unrealised profit (s05).

GST and TDS. Intra-group supplies between distinct GSTINs (even within the same legal entity across states) attract GST; the GST charged is an output tax liability of the seller and an input tax credit of the buyer — these are balances with the government and are not eliminated. Only the transaction value net of GST is eliminated from revenue and purchases. Similarly, TDS deducted by the payer on intra-group fees/interest is a receivable from the tax department for the recipient — it is not intra-group.

Receivables and payables

Dr Trade payables (buyer), Cr Trade receivables (seller). Mismatches arise from:

  • Goods in transit — seller recorded the sale on dispatch; buyer has not received. Adjust in the buyer's column: Dr Inventory (in transit) Cr Payables, then eliminate.
  • Cash in transit — buyer has paid; seller has not received. Dr Cash in transit Cr Receivables (seller), then eliminate.
  • Disputes and debit notes — agree the correct amount; adjust the entity that is wrong.
  • FX differences — balances in a foreign currency remeasured at closing rate by the entity for which it is foreign; the exchange difference does not eliminate (Ind AS 21.45) and stays in consolidated P&L, unless the item forms part of a net investment in a foreign operation (then OCI in CFS, 21.32).

Loans and interest

Dr Borrowings (subsidiary), Cr Loans receivable (parent); Dr Interest income (parent), Cr Finance cost (subsidiary). Points:

  • If the parent lent at below-market rates and recognised a day-1 difference as an additional investment in subsidiary (Ind AS 109 fair value at inception; → w3_t1 c04), the CFS eliminate the loan, the interest unwinding on both sides, and the deemed investment against the subsidiary's equity contribution reserve.
  • Capitalised interest — if the subsidiary capitalised intra-group interest into a qualifying asset under Ind AS 23, the group must reverse it unless the group itself has external borrowings that qualify (the capitalisation test is applied at group level).
  • ECL recognised by the parent on the intra-group loan is reversed on consolidation.

Dividends

A dividend from a subsidiary to the parent:

  • Parent records dividend income (Ind AS 27.12) — eliminated against the subsidiary's dividend debit to retained earnings.
  • The NCI's share of the dividend is not eliminated; it reduces NCI.
  • Dividend declared but unpaid at year-end: eliminate parent's receivable and subsidiary's payable; the NCI portion remains a liability to NCI.

Illustration. S (80% owned) declares and pays ₹50 Cr dividend.

Entry Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Dividend income (parent) 40
Retained earnings — dividend (subsidiary) 40
NCI 10
Retained earnings — dividend (subsidiary) 10

Dividend distribution tax no longer applies (abolished from 1 April 2020); dividends are taxed in shareholders' hands, and an Indian parent receiving a dividend from a subsidiary may claim Sec 80M deduction to the extent it distributes dividends onward. Withholding tax on dividends paid by a foreign subsidiary is a real tax cost for the group — not eliminated.

Management fees, royalties and shared services

Eliminate income and expense in full. Where the fee has been capitalised by the recipient (e.g., project management fees charged by parent to a subsidiary building a plant), the capitalised mark-up is an unrealised profit to be eliminated from PPE, with subsequent depreciation adjusted (s05). Transfer-pricing documentation (Sec 92–92F) and GST valuation (Rule 28 of CGST Rules for related parties) affect standalone accounts, not the elimination principle.

Guarantees

Parent guarantees a subsidiary's bank loan:

  • Standalone (parent) — a financial guarantee contract under Ind AS 109: initial fair value (often the PV of a guarantee fee differential) recognised as a liability with Dr Investment in subsidiary (deemed capital contribution) if no fee is charged; subsequently the higher of ECL allowance and amount initially recognised less cumulative income.
  • Standalone (subsidiary) — may recognise a deemed contribution in equity, and the guarantee lowers the borrowing's market rate.
  • CFS — the guarantee is not a separate obligation; the group's liability is the subsidiary's borrowing itself, already consolidated. Eliminate the guarantee liability, related income amortisation and deemed investment/contribution. The guarantee remains relevant for liquidity disclosures and Sec 186 compliance.

Guarantees given to associates or JVs or third parties are not eliminated.

Elimination journal checklist

Item Debit Credit
Intra-group sales Revenue COGS/purchases
Receivables/payables Payables Receivables
Loans Borrowings Loan receivable
Interest Interest income Finance cost
Dividends Dividend income; NCI (NCI share) Retained earnings
Management fees Other income Other expenses (or PPE if capitalised)
Guarantees Financial guarantee liability; guarantee income Investment in subsidiary; deferred income

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Intra-group transactions before acquisition — not eliminated for pre-acquisition periods; balances outstanding at acquisition become intra-group from that date.
  • Partial-year subsidiaries — eliminate only transactions after the acquisition date.
  • Intra-group losses — eliminating a loss on inventory transfer may reveal that the inventory is impaired (B86(c)); recognise the impairment in the CFS.
  • Cash flow statement — intra-group cash flows (loan disbursements, dividends) are eliminated; dividends to NCI appear as financing outflows.
  • Netting via treasury pooling — notional pooling across group entities is eliminated; physical cash pools need careful classification.
  • Unreconciled differences — never plug to P&L silently; track a reconciling items schedule with ageing and owner.

Key takeaways

  • Intra-group items are eliminated in full regardless of ownership percentage.
  • GST, TDS and withholding taxes are balances with governments — not eliminated.
  • FX differences on intra-group monetary items survive consolidation unless they relate to a net investment in a foreign operation.
  • Dividends: eliminate the parent's share against the subsidiary's equity; NCI's share reduces NCI.
  • Capitalised intra-group fees and interest must be reversed from assets.
  • Parent guarantees of subsidiary debt are eliminated in CFS; the underlying debt remains.

Practice Questions

Q1. Reconcile and eliminate [L2]

At 31 March 2026, Parent shows a receivable from Sub of ₹48 Cr; Sub shows a payable of ₹41 Cr. Differences: goods invoiced by Parent on 30 March worth ₹5 Cr (received by Sub on 2 April), Sub's payment of ₹3 Cr on 31 March credited to Parent's bank on 1 April, and a ₹1 Cr debit note raised by Sub not yet accepted by Parent (Parent accepts it is valid). Pass the adjusting and elimination entries.

💡 Hint: Fix each entity's column first, then the balances should agree.

Q2. Intra-group loan with FX [L2]

An Indian parent (functional currency ₹) lends USD 10 million to its US subsidiary (functional currency USD), repayable in 3 years, at 6%. Rates: ₹83 at drawdown, ₹85 at year-end, average ₹84. Explain what is eliminated, what FX difference survives, and how the answer changes if the loan is designated as part of the net investment (no repayment planned).

Q3. Dividend with NCI [L1]

A 70% subsidiary declares a dividend of ₹30 Cr on 25 March 2026, paid on 15 April 2026. Pass the consolidation entries at 31 March 2026 and state the cash flow statement treatment in FY27.

Q4. Guarantee elimination [L2]

Parent provided a corporate guarantee for a ₹200 Cr term loan of its WOS without charging a fee; it recognised a financial guarantee liability of ₹4 Cr (Dr Investment in WOS) on 1 April 2025, amortising ₹0.8 Cr in FY26. Pass the CFS elimination entries at 31 March 2026.

Q5. Capitalised management fee [L3]

Parent charged a subsidiary ₹12 Cr (cost to parent ₹9 Cr) for project management of a new plant capitalised on 1 October 2025 (life 15 years, SLM). Pass the FY26 consolidation entries including depreciation and DT at 25.168%.
