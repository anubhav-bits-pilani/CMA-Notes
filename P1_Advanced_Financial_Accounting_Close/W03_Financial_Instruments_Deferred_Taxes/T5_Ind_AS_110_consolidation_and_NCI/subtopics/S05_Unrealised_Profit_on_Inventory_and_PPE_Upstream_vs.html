Subtopic note

Unrealised Profit on Inventory and PPE: Upstream vs Downstream

Scope: Unrealised profit on inventory and PPE transfers: downstream vs upstream, NCI share of upstream eliminations, deferred tax

Why it matters

When one group entity sells to another at a margin and the goods (or the asset) remain within the group at period-end, the group has not earned that profit — it has only moved inventory from one warehouse to another. Indian groups with manufacturing subsidiaries selling to a marketing parent, or a parent selling machinery to a subsidiary, eliminate significant amounts every quarter. The elimination must be attributed correctly between owners and NCI and must carry deferred tax, otherwise consolidated equity, NCI and ETR are misstated. These are favourite exam and interview questions, and the Saturday case test for this week includes intra-group inventory.

Core notes

Unrealised profit computation

\[ \text{URP} = \text{Intra-group inventory on hand} \times \text{Gross margin \%} = \text{Inventory on hand} \times \frac{m}{1 + m} \]

where \(m\) is the mark-up on cost. A 25% mark-up on cost equals a 20% margin on selling price.

Direction matters for attribution, not for the amount

Ind AS 110.B86(c) requires profits in inventory/PPE arising from intra-group transactions to be eliminated in full whichever way the goods flow. What differs is who bears the elimination:

Direction Seller Elimination amount Attribution
Downstream Parent sells to subsidiary 100% of URP Entirely to parent (owners' retained earnings) — the profit was recorded in the parent's books
Upstream Subsidiary sells to parent 100% of URP Between owners and NCI in proportion to ownership of the selling subsidiary — the profit was recorded in the subsidiary's books, which NCI shares
Sideways Subsidiary 1 to Subsidiary 2 100% of URP Between owners and NCI of the selling subsidiary

Inventory elimination entries

Downstream — Parent sells goods costing ₹80 Cr to its 75% subsidiary for ₹100 Cr; 40% remains in inventory at year-end. URP = \(40 \times 20/100 = 8\) Cr.

Account Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
Revenue 100
Cost of sales 100
Cost of sales (URP) 8
Inventory 8
Deferred tax asset (8 × 25.168%) 2.01
Deferred tax expense 2.01

The net ₹5.99 Cr reduction in profit is attributable wholly to owners.

Upstream — the same facts, but the 75% subsidiary sells to the parent. Elimination entries are identical in the P&L/balance sheet; additionally, NCI absorbs 25%:

Account Dr (₹ Cr) Cr (₹ Cr)
NCI (25% × 5.99) 1.50
Profit attributable to NCI (P&L attribution) 1.50

i.e., profit attributable to NCI is reduced by ₹1.50 Cr; profit attributable to owners by ₹4.49 Cr.

Opening URP

URP eliminated last year reverses in the current year when the goods are sold to third parties: Dr Opening retained earnings (and NCI for upstream) / Cr Cost of sales. Then eliminate the closing URP. In the workbook:

\[ \text{P\&L effect} = \text{Opening URP} - \text{Closing URP} \]

PPE transfers

Parent sells a machine (carrying amount ₹30 Cr) to a subsidiary for ₹40 Cr on 1 April 2025; remaining life 5 years, SLM.

  • Eliminate the gain ₹10 Cr: Dr Other income (gain) 10, Cr PPE 10.
  • Each year, the subsidiary's depreciation is ₹8 Cr vs group depreciation ₹6 Cr; reverse excess depreciation ₹2 Cr: Dr Accumulated depreciation 2, Cr Depreciation 2.
  • Net unrealised profit at 31 March 2026 = ₹8 Cr; DTA at the buyer's rate on ₹8 Cr.
  • If upstream, attribute each year's net effect between owners and NCI.

Deferred tax on eliminations

Ind AS 12 applies to temporary differences from eliminating unrealised profits (B86(c)). The consolidated carrying amount of the inventory is lower than its tax base (which is the buyer's cost, since tax is computed entity by entity), giving a deductible temporary difference and a DTA:

\[ \text{DTA} = \text{URP} \times t_{\text{buyer}} \]

Use the tax rate of the entity holding the inventory (the buyer) — that is the entity whose future taxable profit will be lower when it sells the goods at a smaller margin than its cost suggests. Ind AS 12 is explicit that DT is measured at rates expected to apply on recovery of the asset, which happens in the buyer's jurisdiction. This matters for cross-border groups: a Singapore subsidiary (17%) selling to an Indian parent (25.168%) gives DTA at 25.168%. Recognise the DTA only if the buyer is expected to have sufficient taxable profit (12.24).

Losses on intra-group transfers

If goods are sold at a loss, eliminate the loss, but test whether the loss signals that the inventory's NRV is below cost to the group (B86(c)); if so, the impairment remains in the CFS.

Worked case — combined

P owns 80% of S. During FY26 S sold goods to P for ₹60 Cr at a mark-up of 25%. P's closing inventory includes ₹15 Cr of these goods (opening inventory had ₹10 Cr from S at the same mark-up). Tax rates: both 25.168%. S's PAT for FY26 is ₹40 Cr.

  • Closing URP = \(15 \times 0.25/1.25 = 3.0\); opening URP = \(10 \times 0.2 = 2.0\).
  • Net P&L charge before tax = ₹1.0 Cr; DT credit ₹0.25 Cr; net ₹0.75 Cr.
  • S's adjusted profit = 40 − 0.75 = ₹39.25 Cr; NCI share = 20% × 39.25 = ₹7.85 Cr.
  • Closing inventory in CFS reduced by ₹3.0 Cr; DTA ₹0.76 Cr.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mark-up vs margin confusion — always convert; the exam trap is applying the mark-up % to selling price.
  • Goods transferred and capitalised (inventory becomes PPE of the buyer) — eliminate profit from PPE, then adjust depreciation.
  • Different tax rates — the buyer's rate; some practitioners wrongly use the seller's rate because the seller paid the tax.
  • Partially owned buyer, downstream — even though NCI holds 25% of the buying subsidiary, Ind AS 110 attribution of downstream URP is to the parent (the selling entity's owners).
  • Equity-accounted investees — only the investor's share is eliminated (→ s08), unlike full elimination for subsidiaries.
  • Mid-year acquisitions — URP on inventory sold before acquisition is not eliminated; only post-acquisition intra-group sales.

Key takeaways

  • URP = intra-group inventory on hand × margin on selling price, or × \(m/(1+m)\).
  • Eliminate URP in full regardless of direction.
  • Downstream: charge wholly to owners; upstream/sideways: share with NCI of the selling subsidiary.
  • Reverse opening URP and eliminate closing URP; the P&L effect is the movement.
  • Recognise DTA on eliminated profit at the buyer's tax rate.
  • PPE transfers require gain elimination plus annual depreciation adjustments until the asset is fully depreciated or sold.

Practice Questions

Q1. Upstream vs downstream [L1]

P owns 60% of S. Intra-group sales of ₹90 Cr at a 50% mark-up on cost; one-third remains in the buyer's closing inventory. Tax rate 25.168%. Compute URP, DTA and the effect on profit attributable to owners and NCI if (a) P sold to S, (b) S sold to P.

Q2. Cross-border tax rate [L2]

A UAE subsidiary (9% tax) sells goods to its Indian parent (25.168%) at ₹120 Cr with a 20% margin on sales; ₹45 Cr remains in the parent's inventory. Compute the DTA and justify the rate used; explain the effect on consolidated ETR.

💡 Hint: Identify which entity recovers the inventory carrying amount.

Q3. PPE transfer over time [L2]

S (70% subsidiary) sold equipment to P on 1 October 2024 for ₹25 Cr; S's carrying amount was ₹18 Cr; remaining life 7 years; SLM. Prepare the consolidation adjustments for FY25 and FY26, including NCI attribution and DT at 25.168%.

Q4. Opening and closing URP [L2]

In the CFS for FY26, opening inventory of P includes ₹24 Cr bought from S (80%) at a 20% mark-up; closing includes ₹36 Cr at a 25% mark-up. Pass the entries for opening and closing URP, and compute the net effect on profit attributable to NCI.

Q5. Exam-style interview question [L3]

A reviewer notices consolidated gross margin rose 120 bps in Q4 while the parent's and subsidiaries' standalone margins were flat. Intra-group sales doubled in Q4 and the subsidiary's closing inventory of parent-supplied goods tripled. Explain what might have gone wrong in the consolidation and the tests you would perform.
