Subtopic note

Companies Act Rules for Subsidiaries, CFS and AOC-1

Scope: Companies Act: Sec 2(87) subsidiary, Sec 129(3) CFS and Form AOC-1, layer restriction rules; Schedule III entity-wise share of net assets and profit

Why it matters

Indian groups live under two parallel perimeters: the Companies Act definitions that drive legal obligations (who must prepare CFS, which entities appear in AOC-1, layer limits, related-party approvals) and Ind AS 110 control that drives what is actually consolidated. They usually coincide but not always. A controller or company secretary must reconcile them, prepare Form AOC-1 and the Schedule III "additional information" table, and confirm compliance with the layer rules before group restructurings. Auditors report on CFS under Sec 143 and CARO 2020 clause 3(xxi) (qualifications in component auditors' CARO reports).

Core notes

Sec 2(87): subsidiary company

A company in relation to another (the holding company) is a subsidiary if the holding company:

  • (i) controls the composition of its Board of Directors; or
  • (ii) exercises or controls more than one-half of the total voting power either at its own or together with one or more of its subsidiaries (the 2017 amendment replaced "total share capital" with "total voting power").

Explanations: a company is a subsidiary even if control is through another subsidiary (layers); "control of composition of board" means the power to appoint or remove all or a majority of directors; "company" includes a body corporate (so foreign companies count).

Layer restriction (proviso to Sec 2(87) and Companies (Restriction on Number of Layers) Rules, 2017): prescribed classes of holding companies shall not have more than two layers of subsidiaries. Key features:

  • A layer consisting of one or more wholly owned subsidiaries is not counted.
  • Exempt: banking companies, NBFCs registered with RBI, insurance companies, government companies.
  • A company may acquire a foreign company having subsidiaries beyond two layers if permitted by that country's laws.
  • Separately, Sec 186(1) restricts making investments through more than two layers of investment companies (subject to exceptions).

Sec 2(87) vs Ind AS 110

Situation Sec 2(87) subsidiary? Ind AS 110 subsidiary?
45% with de facto control (dispersed holders) No (≤ half voting power; unless board control) Yes
51% voting power but a JV agreement requires unanimous consent on all relevant activities Yes No — joint control (JV under Ind AS 111)
Securitisation trust controlled through contracts No (not a company) Yes
Board control via contract with 30% stake Yes (controls board) Usually yes

CFS follow Ind AS 110; the Act's definitions decide legal compliance (AOC-1 listing, Sec 188 related parties, layers). Ind AS 110 is applied to the CFS because Sec 129(3) requires CFS "in the same form and manner" as standalone, prepared under Sec 133 notified standards.

Sec 129(3): consolidated financial statements

Where a company has one or more subsidiaries or associate companies, it shall, in addition to its own financial statements, prepare a consolidated financial statement of the company and of all its subsidiaries and associate companies in the same form and manner as its own, and lay it before the AGM along with standalone statements. Explanation: "subsidiary" includes associate company and joint venture for this purpose.

  • Rule 6 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 — CFS are prepared per applicable accounting standards; an intermediate wholly owned subsidiary (or partly owned whose other members have been informed and do not object) that is unlisted and whose ultimate or intermediate holding company files Ind AS/AS-compliant CFS is exempt. A company having only associates/JVs and no subsidiaries also prepares CFS (equity method).
  • Sec 136 — the CFS and separate audited accounts of each subsidiary are placed on the company's website.
  • Sec 129(4) — provisions on preparation, adoption and audit apply mutatis mutandis to CFS.

Form AOC-1 (Rule 5)

A separate statement containing the salient features of the financial statements of subsidiaries, associates and JVs is attached to the standalone financial statements in Form AOC-1:

  • Part A — Subsidiaries: name; date of acquisition; reporting period if different; reporting currency and exchange rate (for foreign subsidiaries); share capital; reserves and surplus; total assets; total liabilities; investments; turnover; profit before tax; provision for tax; profit after tax; proposed dividend; % shareholding.
  • Part B — Associates and JVs: latest audited balance sheet date; shares held (number, amount, % holding); description of how there is significant influence; reason for not consolidating, if any; net worth attributable to shareholding; profit/loss for the year — considered and not considered in consolidation.
  • Also lists entities yet to commence operations and those liquidated or sold during the year.

Other Companies Act touchpoints

  • Sec 2(6) associate — significant influence (≥20% voting power or control/participation in business decisions under an agreement); includes JV.
  • Sec 2(46) holding company — includes a body corporate.
  • Sec 186 — loans/guarantees to subsidiaries (WOS exemptions in 186(3)/(11)).
  • Sec 134 Board's report — performance and financial position of each subsidiary, associate and JV (Rule 8(1)).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Foreign holding of an Indian sub-group — the Indian intermediate parent may claim Rule 6 exemption only if unlisted and the conditions are met; listed intermediate parents always prepare CFS.
  • Associate under Sec 2(6) but not under Ind AS 28 (e.g., 22% with no board seat and contractual exclusion) — CFS follow Ind AS 28; AOC-1 Part B explains why not equity-accounted.
  • Layer count with step-down JVs — layer rules count subsidiaries, not JVs or associates.
  • Foreign subsidiaries' reporting currency — AOC-1 needs the exchange rate used; keep it consistent with Ind AS 21 translation.
  • Schedule III table for loss-making subsidiaries — negative net assets percentages must still be shown.

Key takeaways

  • Sec 2(87): board composition control or more than half of total voting power (including through subsidiaries).
  • No more than two layers of subsidiaries for prescribed holding companies; WOS layers excluded; banks/NBFCs/insurers/government companies exempt.
  • Sec 129(3) requires CFS covering subsidiaries, associates and JVs; Rule 6 gives a narrow intermediate-parent exemption.
  • AOC-1 summarises each subsidiary (Part A) and associate/JV (Part B) and is attached to standalone FS.
  • Schedule III requires entity-wise share of net assets, profit, OCI and TCI in the CFS.
  • Ind AS 110 control determines what is consolidated; Companies Act definitions determine legal compliance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Perimeter reconciliation [L2]

Holding H has: A (100%), B (51% but all relevant activities require unanimous consent with the 49% partner), C (44%, board control via SHA right to appoint 5 of 9 directors), D (35%, dispersed others, de facto control under Ind AS 110), E (25%, two board seats). Classify each under Sec 2(87)/2(6) and under Ind AS 110/28/111, and state the AOC-1 part in which each appears.

Q2. Layer rule compliance [L2]

An unlisted manufacturing company (not an NBFC) has: WOS W1; W1 holds 70% of X; X holds 60% of Y; Y proposes to acquire 55% of Z. Determine whether the acquisition breaches the layer rules, explaining how wholly owned layers are counted.

💡 Hint: Identify which layers consist only of wholly owned subsidiaries.

Q3. Prepare the Schedule III table [L2]

Given consolidated net assets ₹2,400 Cr and consolidated profit ₹300 Cr; parent net assets ₹1,500 Cr and profit ₹180 Cr; S1 ₹600 Cr / ₹80 Cr; foreign S2 (75%) ₹400 Cr / ₹60 Cr; associate share ₹50 Cr / ₹10 Cr; eliminations −₹50 Cr / −₹10 Cr, prepare the entity-wise table including the NCI line.

Q4. Rule 6 exemption [L1]

An unlisted Indian company is a 90% subsidiary of a Japanese parent that prepares IFRS consolidated statements; the 10% minority is an Indian bank. The Indian company has two subsidiaries. Is it exempt from preparing CFS under Rule 6? State the conditions you would check.
