Subtopic note

NCI Attribution, Deficit Balances and Stake Changes within Control

Scope: NCI: share of profit/OCI, losses attributed even if deficit (B94), ownership changes without loss of control in equity (110.23)

Why it matters

NCI is the minority shareholders' slice of the group's equity and results. It appears in three places — equity in the balance sheet, the profit/TCI attribution at the foot of the P&L, and the statement of changes in equity — and it is a frequent source of review comments because it is computed by the consolidation team rather than by any entity. Indian groups frequently change stakes within control: rights issues where minority partners don't subscribe, ESOP dilution in subsidiaries, secondary purchases from PE investors, and IPOs of subsidiaries (offer for sale reducing the parent's stake from 100% to 75%). All of these go through equity, not P&L — a significant difference from pre-Ind AS practice.

Core notes

Presentation (110.22; Ind AS 1.54(q), 1.81B)

  • NCI is presented within equity, separately from equity attributable to owners of the parent.
  • Profit or loss and each component of OCI are attributed to owners and NCI (B94); the P&L shows "Profit attributable to: Owners of the parent; Non-controlling interests", and similarly for TCI.
  • EPS is computed on profit attributable to owners only.

Measuring NCI's share of profit and OCI

NCI's share is based on present ownership interests, not potential voting rights (B89), except where potential rights currently give access to returns. The share is computed on the subsidiary's results as included in the CFS — i.e., after:

  • PPA adjustments (extra depreciation/amortisation on step-ups, unwind of DTL);
  • uniform-policy alignment adjustments;
  • upstream URP eliminations (NCI shares in these).

\[ \text{NCI share of profit} = \text{NCI\%} \times (\text{Subsidiary PAT} \pm \text{PPA effects} \pm \text{Policy adjustments} - \text{Upstream URP net of tax}) \]

Cumulative preference shares classified as equity and held by NCI: the parent computes its share of profit after adjusting for dividends on such shares, whether or not declared (B95).

Losses and deficit balances (B94)

Total comprehensive income is attributed to owners and NCI even if this results in the NCI having a deficit balance. There is no cap at zero (unlike pre-2008 IFRS and old Indian GAAP AS 21, which charged excess losses to the parent unless the minority had a binding obligation).

Illustration. P owns 70% of S, a start-up. At 1 April 2025 NCI = ₹6 Cr. S's FY26 loss (after PPA) = ₹40 Cr. NCI share = 30% × −40 = −₹12 Cr. Closing NCI = 6 − 12 = −₹6 Cr (debit balance within equity). The parent does not absorb the minority's share even if it is funding the losses. If the parent has a contractual commitment to fund the NCI's share, that is a separate obligation to analyse under Ind AS 37/109.

Changes in ownership without loss of control (110.23, B96)

Changes in a parent's ownership interest that do not result in loss of control are equity transactions — transactions with owners in their capacity as owners. On such a change:

  1. Adjust the carrying amounts of the controlling and non-controlling interests to reflect the change in relative interests.
  2. Recognise directly in equity attributable to owners the difference between the amount by which NCI is adjusted and the fair value of consideration paid or received (B96).
  3. No change to goodwill, no remeasurement of assets, no P&L gain/loss.
  4. Reattribute accumulated OCI components (e.g., FCTR) between owners and NCI in proportion (Ind AS 21.48C for FCTR; similarly FVOCI reserves).

Stake increase (buying NCI). P owns 70% of S; NCI carrying amount (including share of goodwill if fair value method) = ₹150 Cr. P buys a further 10% from the minority for ₹65 Cr.

  • NCI adjustment = 10/30 × 150 = ₹50 Cr.
  • Difference = 65 − 50 = ₹15 Cr debit to equity attributable to owners (e.g., "retained earnings" or a separate "transactions with NCI" reserve).

Stake decrease (selling to NCI, e.g., subsidiary IPO via offer for sale). P owns 100% of S (net assets in CFS ₹800 Cr, goodwill ₹100 Cr — total ₹900 Cr). In an IPO, P sells 25% via OFS for ₹420 Cr, net of OFS expenses.

  • NCI recognised = 25% × net assets attributable, ₹800 Cr (+ goodwill share per policy — commonly 25% × 900 = ₹225 Cr; the group must choose a consistent approach for whether goodwill is included).
  • Gain to equity = 420 − 225 = ₹195 Cr credit to owners' equity.
  • The standalone books of P will show a capital gain in P&L on sale of shares; the CFS show it in equity. Tax on that capital gain in the CFS: current tax charged directly to equity (Ind AS 12.61A), since the transaction itself is in equity.

Subsidiary issues new shares to outsiders (dilution). S issues shares to a new investor for cash; P's interest falls from 80% to 70%. The new NCI share of net assets (including the cash raised) is computed; the difference between the cash raised attributable to NCI and NCI's increase is credited/debited to owners' equity.

− Dividends to NCI
± Changes in ownership without loss of control

Closing NCI

The closing NCI must equal NCI% × subsidiary net assets as included in the CFS (+ NCI goodwill under the fair value method) — a key check row.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Put options written to NCI — financial liability for the redemption amount (Ind AS 32.23); policy choice whether to continue recognising NCI; subsequent changes in the liability in P&L under Ind AS 109.
  • Transaction costs of buying/selling NCI — deducted from equity, not P&L.
  • Goodwill in partial disposals — no reallocation under Ind AS 110; some groups attribute a share of goodwill to new NCI on disposal; disclose the policy.
  • NCI in a sub-group — compute indirect NCI on effective interest (e.g., P 80% of S1, S1 60% of S2 → owners 48%, NCI 52% of S2's results).
  • Deficit NCI and impairment — a debit NCI balance is not an asset and is not tested for recoverability.
  • Schedule III disclosure — NCI share in net assets, P&L, OCI and TCI in the entity-wise table.

Key takeaways

  • NCI is equity; profit, OCI and TCI are attributed between owners and NCI.
  • NCI's share is computed on CFS-basis results after PPA, policy and upstream URP adjustments.
  • Losses are attributed to NCI even if NCI becomes a deficit balance (B94).
  • Stake changes without loss of control are equity transactions; goodwill is unchanged (110.23, B96).
  • Reattribute OCI reserves, such as FCTR, on stake changes.
  • Reconcile NCI with a roll-forward and a check against NCI% × net assets.

Practice Questions

Q1. NCI share with PPA adjustments [L2]

P acquired 75% of S on 1 April 2024; PPA step-up on a brand of ₹80 Cr (life 10 years) with DTL at 25.168%. S's FY26 PAT is ₹50 Cr; upstream closing URP ₹4 Cr (opening ₹2 Cr), DT at 25.168%. S paid a dividend of ₹20 Cr. Compute profit attributable to NCI and the NCI dividend effect for FY26.

Q2. Deficit NCI [L1]

NCI in a 60% subsidiary is ₹10 Cr at 1 April 2025. FY26 total comprehensive loss is ₹45 Cr. Compute closing NCI and explain why the parent does not absorb the excess loss.

Q3. Buying out part of NCI [L2]

P owns 65% of S. NCI carrying amount ₹210 Cr including FCTR of ₹12 Cr attributable to NCI. P buys 15% from the minority for ₹120 Cr. Pass the journal, including the reattribution of FCTR.

💡 Hint: The FCTR reattribution is proportional to the NCI interest acquired.

Q4. Subsidiary IPO [L3]

P owns 100% of S. S does an IPO: fresh issue of ₹300 Cr (new shares equal to 15% post-issue) and OFS by P of 10% for ₹200 Cr. S's net assets in CFS before IPO ₹1,400 Cr; goodwill ₹200 Cr. Compute P's post-IPO interest, NCI recognised, and the equity effects, and state where the capital gains tax on the OFS is presented in the CFS.

Q5. Indirect NCI [L2]

P owns 80% of S1; S1 owns 70% of S2. S2's PAT is ₹60 Cr; S1's own PAT (excluding dividend from S2) is ₹40 Cr. Compute the NCI share of group profit and explain the effective-interest approach.
