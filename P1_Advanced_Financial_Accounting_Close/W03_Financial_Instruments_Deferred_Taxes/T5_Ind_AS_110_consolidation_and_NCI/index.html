Roadmap topic: Consolidated Financial Statements (Ind AS 110): Line-by-line elimination of intra-group balances, unrealized inventory profits, and Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) measurement

Overview

Consolidated financial statements present a parent and its subsidiaries as a single economic entity. Ind AS 110 supplies two things: a single control model that decides which entities are subsidiaries (power, variable returns, and the link between them), and the consolidation procedures — line-by-line aggregation, elimination of the investment against equity, full elimination of intra-group balances and transactions, unrealised profit elimination with deferred tax, and attribution of results and equity to non-controlling interests. Around it sit Ind AS 103 (goodwill and NCI at acquisition), Ind AS 28/111 (equity method for associates and JVs), Ind AS 21 (translation of foreign subsidiaries), Ind AS 12 (deferred tax on consolidation adjustments) and Ind AS 112 (disclosures).

For FP&A, reporting and audit roles in India, consolidation is a quarterly reality. SEBI LODR Reg 33 requires listed entities to publish consolidated results every quarter; Sec 129(3) of the Companies Act requires annual CFS with Form AOC-1 and the Schedule III entity-wise table. Group finance teams spend most of the close on intercompany reconciliation, policy alignment and NCI computations, and most review comments on the CFS concern eliminations, NCI and deferred tax on consolidation adjustments. Building a robust consolidation workbook — entity columns, elimination journals, NCI schedule, check rows — is a core skill this week, and the Saturday case test (parent + 80% subsidiary acquired mid-year with a plant step-up and intra-group inventory) exercises every piece.

Learn it in sequence: first control (s01) and the Companies Act overlay (s02) to define the perimeter; then the procedures (s03) — policies, reporting dates and the workbook; then the eliminations: intra-group balances and transactions (s04) and unrealised profits with their upstream/downstream attribution and deferred tax (s05). Next, NCI attribution and stake changes within control (s06), and the end of the life cycle — loss of control and foreign subsidiaries (s07). Finally, contrast full consolidation with the equity method (s08), where only the investor's share of unrealised profit is eliminated. The concept notes give the formulas and entries you will use in every consolidation.

Related concepts to study

US GAAP ASC 810 consolidation models

US GAAP ASC 810: VIE vs voting interest models

US GAAP uses two consolidation models. The variable interest entity (VIE) model applies first, to entities where equity at risk is insufficient, or equity holders lack decision rights or the obligation to absorb losses/right to receive returns; the primary beneficiary — the party with power over the most significant activities and the obligation to absorb losses or right to receive benefits that could be significant — consolidates. Other entities follow the voting interest model, generally majority voting control. Ind AS 110 instead applies one control model to all entities, with the structured-entity guidance covering most VIE-type situations. Practical differences arise for de facto control (not generally recognised under the US voting model), kick-out rights in limited partnerships, and securitisation vehicles. Indian subsidiaries of US groups must map each entity under both frameworks for the group package.

SEBI LODR consolidated results and material subsidiaries

SEBI LODR Reg 33 consolidated results; Reg 24 material subsidiary (income or net worth >10% of consolidated)

Reg 33 requires listed entities to submit standalone and consolidated financial results quarterly within 45 days of quarter-end (60 days for the annual audited results), with limited review or audit by the statutory auditor; the consolidated figures must be reviewed/audited for at least a prescribed share of consolidated revenue, assets and profits. Reg 16(1)(c) defines a material subsidiary as one whose income or net worth exceeds 10% of consolidated income or net worth in the preceding year. Reg 24 then imposes governance: an independent director of the listed entity on the board of unlisted material subsidiaries (using a 20% threshold for this specific requirement), audit committee review of unlisted subsidiaries' investments, special resolutions for disposals that reduce holding below 50% or cede control of a material subsidiary, and Reg 24A secretarial audit of material unlisted Indian subsidiaries. The consolidation perimeter therefore drives governance obligations.

Links to acquisition accounting and intercompany reconciliation

→ w3_t4 goodwill and NCI at acquisition; → Week 4 (w4_t4) IC reconciliation as pre-consolidation control

The consolidation of a subsidiary begins with its acquisition-date balances from Ind AS 103: identifiable net assets at fair value, PPA step-ups and DTL, goodwill and NCI at fair value or proportionate share (w3_t4). These feed the investment-elimination journal and every subsequent year's PPA unwinding, NCI share and impairment testing. The day-to-day accuracy of eliminations, however, depends on the intercompany reconciliation process covered in Week 4 (w4_t4): matching IC balances and transactions by counterparty before consolidation, resolving in-transit items, disputes and FX differences, and enforcing cut-off. Without that pre-consolidation control, the IC netting check (c06) fails and teams resort to plugs. Treat w3_t4 as the opening balance and w4_t4 as the monthly process that keeps the consolidation clean.

Excel consolidation workbook design

Excel: consolidation workbook — entity columns, elimination journal columns, NCI schedule, check rows

A robust workbook has: an inputs tab per entity (mapped TB to group chart of accounts, IC schedule by counterparty, FX rates); a consolidation tab with columns for each entity, policy adjustments, translation, aggregate, then one column per elimination journal (investment/goodwill, PPA unwind, IC balances, IC transactions, URP, dividends, NCI), and the consolidated column; an NCI schedule computing NCI share from CFS-basis profit with roll-forward; a journals register with numbering, narration and preparer/reviewer; and a checks tab (journals balance, IC lines zero, NCI tie-out, BS balances, cash flow reconciles). Use SUMIFS on a mapping key, avoid hard-coded numbers in the consolidated column, and lock prior-period columns once signed off.

=SUMIFS(TB_Parent[Amount],TB_Parent[GroupCode],A5)+SUMIFS(TB_Sub[Amount],TB_Sub[GroupCode],A5)

Common pitfalls

Eliminating full upstream profit against parent equity only

The mistake: charging the entire URP on a subsidiary-to-parent sale to the parent's retained earnings, leaving NCI untouched. Why it happens: the elimination amount is correctly 100%, and teams conflate "eliminate in full" with "charge in full to owners". Correct treatment: eliminate 100% from inventory, but attribute the reduction in the selling subsidiary's profit between owners and NCI in proportion to ownership (c02). Detection: check that NCI's share of profit is computed on subsidiary profit after upstream URP; the NCI tie-out (NCI% × subsidiary net assets in CFS) fails if this is missed.

Omitting deferred tax on unrealised profit eliminations

The mistake: eliminating URP pre-tax with no DTA, depressing consolidated profit by the gross amount and distorting the ETR. Why it happens: DT is computed entity by entity from tax computations; consolidation adjustments are outside those workings. Correct treatment: Ind AS 12 applies to temporary differences arising from eliminations (110.B86(c)); recognise DTA = URP × holder's tax rate (c05), attributed like the URP. Detection: ETR reconciliation shows an unexplained excess; consolidation journals register has URP entries without matching DT lines; review the consolidated DT roll-forward for a "consolidation adjustments" line.

Equating Ind AS 110 control with more than 50% voting power under Sec 2(87)

The mistake: consolidating every entity where the group holds over half of voting power and none below. Why it happens: the Companies Act definition is familiar and drives AOC-1; old AS 21 used a similar test. Correct treatment: apply the three-element control test: a 45% holder with dispersed others may control (de facto); a 51% holder may lack control where a partner's consent is needed on relevant activities (JV); structured entities may be controlled via contracts. Detection: maintain a perimeter memo reconciling Sec 2(87)/2(6) classifications with Ind AS 110/111/28 conclusions; look for entities with 40–50% holdings, SHAs with vetoes, trusts and SPVs.

Not aligning subsidiaries' accounting policies or reporting dates

The mistake: aggregating subsidiaries' statements as reported — WDV depreciation in one, SLM in another; a 31 December foreign subsidiary consolidated without adjustment. Why it happens: time pressure and reliance on local statutory accounts. Correct treatment: uniform policies for like transactions (110.19, B87) with adjustments and DT; same reporting date or additional financial information, else a ≤3-month gap consistently applied and adjusted for significant events (B92–B93). Detection: group accounting manual and reporting-package questionnaire; compare subsidiaries' policy notes with the group's; check dates of subsidiary statements used.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mid-year acquisition with step-up and intra-group inventory [L3]

On 1 October 2025, Prabal Ltd acquired 80% of Sahaj Ltd for ₹640 Cr cash. At that date Sahaj's book net assets were ₹600 Cr (share capital ₹200 Cr, reserves ₹400 Cr) and its plant had a fair value ₹80 Cr above book (remaining life 8 years). Both companies are 115BAA (25.168%); NCI is measured at proportionate share. For FY26 (year ended 31 March 2026): Prabal's PAT ₹210 Cr; Sahaj's PAT ₹96 Cr accruing evenly. From 1 October 2025, Sahaj sold goods to Prabal for ₹120 Cr at a 25% mark-up; Prabal holds ₹30 Cr of these at 31 March 2026. Prabal owes Sahaj ₹18 Cr at year-end. Sahaj paid no dividend. (a) Compute goodwill. (b) Prepare the elimination journals for FY26. (c) Compute profit attributable to owners and NCI. (d) Compute closing NCI and perform the NCI tie-out check.

💡 Hint: Only post-acquisition profit is consolidated; the URP is upstream.

Q2. Perimeter, policies and eliminations [L3]

A listed group has: S1 (100%, Indian, depreciates plant WDV while the group uses SLM; plant cost ₹200 Cr acquired 1 April 2023, group life 10 years, WDV rate 15%); S2 (45%, dispersed others, AGM turnout 60%); S3 (55%, but a JV agreement requires unanimous consent over budgets and capex); S4 (100%, Singapore, 31 December year-end; dividend of SGD 2 million paid to parent on 15 February 2026). (a) Classify S2 and S3. (b) Compute the FY26 policy-alignment adjustment for S1 with DT. (c) Explain how S4 is consolidated and which post-December event must be adjusted. (d) List the AOC-1 parts and Schedule III table lines for each entity.

Q3. Stake changes and loss of control [L3]

P owns 70% of S (NCI at proportionate share). At 1 April 2025, S's net assets in CFS were ₹900 Cr, goodwill ₹120 Cr, NCI ₹270 Cr; FCTR attributable to owners ₹21 Cr (credit), to NCI ₹9 Cr. On 1 July 2025 P buys 10% from NCI for ₹110 Cr (S's net assets then ₹930 Cr). On 1 January 2026 P sells 45% for ₹600 Cr, retaining 35% (associate; FV ₹460 Cr); S's net assets then ₹980 Cr and FCTR unchanged. (a) Account for the July purchase including FCTR reattribution. (b) Compute the gain on loss of control in January. (c) State the initial carrying amount of the associate and what must be computed for the equity method going forward.

Q4. Associate vs subsidiary URP [L2]

P sells goods to E at a 20% margin; E holds ₹50 Cr of these at year-end. Tax 25.168%. Compute the URP eliminated, DT and the effect on consolidated profit attributable to owners if E is (a) a 100% subsidiary, (b) a 60% subsidiary, (c) a 30% associate, (d) a 50% JV. Then explain why the answers differ.

Q5. Review a consolidation pack [L3]

You are reviewing the FY26 consolidation pack of an NSE-listed group a week before the board meeting. You find: IC receivables exceed IC payables by ₹14 Cr after elimination with the difference posted to "other expenses"; NCI closing balance is ₹8 Cr higher than NCI% × subsidiary net assets; consolidated ETR is 29.5% versus a statutory 25.168% with no permanent differences identified; a 31 December subsidiary's statements were used without adjustment despite a ₹40 Cr impairment in March. For each, explain the likely cause, the correct treatment, and the evidence you would request.
