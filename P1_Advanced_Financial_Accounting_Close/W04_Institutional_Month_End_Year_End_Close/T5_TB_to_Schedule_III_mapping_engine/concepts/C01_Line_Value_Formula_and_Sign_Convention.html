Key concept

Line Value Formula and Sign Convention

Concept: Line value = SUMIFS(TB balance, Map line, Line ID) × sign convention (assets/expenses positive; liabilities/income negative)

Statement

For a Schedule III line \(L\):

\[ V_{L} = \sigma_{L} \times \sum_{a \,:\, \text{Map}(a) = L} B_{a} \]

where \(B_{a}\) is the final TB balance of account \(a\) stored with debits positive and credits negative, \(\text{Map}(a)\) is the Line ID the account is mapped to, and \(\sigma_{L}\) is the display sign of the line: \(+1\) for asset and expense lines, \(-1\) for liability, equity and income lines, so that every line displays as a positive number in its natural sense. In Excel:

=SUMIFS(FinalTB[Bal], FinalTB[Line], [@LineID]) * [@DisplaySign]

Explanation

The TB sign convention (Dr +, Cr −) guarantees that \(\sum_{a} B_{a} = 0\), which is what makes the TB check work. But Schedule III shows trade payables, share capital and revenue as positive numbers. The display sign converts storage convention into presentation convention once, at the line level.

Why the sign sits on the line, not on the account:

  • A line can contain accounts of opposite natural signs (e.g., trade receivables gross Dr and ECL allowance Cr both map to the receivables line); the line value is their net, flipped once. Flipping per account would double-negate contra accounts.
  • Contra balances on the wrong side (a credit-balance receivable account) then show as a reduction of the line, which the abnormal-balance test (w4_t4 c05) catches for reclassification.

Rules that keep it consistent:

  • One place only: either the TB stores natural signs and the engine never flips, or the engine flips via \(\sigma_{L}\); mixing them produces negative liabilities (topic pitfall).
  • Subtotals and totals are computed from the stored (unflipped) sums by parent attribute and then flipped by the parent's sign — e.g., "Profit before tax" = −(Σ income + Σ expenses) under Dr + / Cr −.
  • P&L items in brackets (e.g., "Less: exceptional items") are a presentation choice; keep values positive in the engine and handle brackets with number formatting.
  • BS check is computed on flipped totals: Total assets − (Equity + Liabilities) = 0, equivalently \(\sum\) of all stored BS balances + current-year profit (stored) = 0.

Illustration

Final TB (₹ lakh; Dr +, Cr −):

Account Line Stored balance
Trade receivables – third party BS.CA.FA.TR 1,860.0
Trade receivables – IC BS.CA.FA.TR 240.0
ECL allowance BS.CA.FA.TR (74.0)
Trade payables – MSME BS.CL.FL.TP (310.0)
Trade payables – others BS.CL.FL.TP (1,145.0)
Sale of products PL.REV.OPS (9,420.0)

Line values: Trade receivables \(= +1 \times (1,860 + 240 - 74) = 2,026.0\); Trade payables \(= -1 \times (-310 - 1,145) = 1,455.0\); Revenue from operations \(= -1 \times (-9,420) = 9,420.0\). If someone also stored payables as positive in the mapping step, the face would show (1,455.0) — a negative liability.

Connections

Used throughout s03 (formula engine) and s06 (integrity checks); the normal-sign attribute from s02 drives both this display sign and the abnormal-balance test in w4_t4 c05. In 3-statement modelling, the same convention decides whether historical liabilities pulled into the model need flipping — set it once in the historical mapping tab.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute line values [L1]

Stored balances (₹ crore; Dr +, Cr −): equity share capital (50.0); securities premium (120.0); retained earnings (210.4); current-year profit (stored as net of P&L accounts) (38.6); PPE gross 420.0; accumulated depreciation (130.0); borrowings (95.0); other net assets 224.0. Compute the displayed values of Equity, PPE (net) and Borrowings, and perform the BS check.

💡 Hint: Group by parent line before applying display signs.

Q2. Sign bug hunt [L2]

After a change to the mapping macro, the BS shows "Other current liabilities" as (12.4) crore and the BS check fails by 24.8 crore. Explain the likely cause and how to design the engine so that this type of error is caught automatically.

Q3. Contra accounts [L2]

Explain why applying the sign at account level rather than line level breaks lines that combine gross and contra accounts, using PPE gross block and accumulated depreciation as the example.
