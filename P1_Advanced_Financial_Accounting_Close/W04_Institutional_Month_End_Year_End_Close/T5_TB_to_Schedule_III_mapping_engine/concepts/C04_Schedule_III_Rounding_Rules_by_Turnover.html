Key concept

Schedule III Rounding Rules by Turnover

Concept: Rounding: turnover < ₹100 Cr → hundreds/thousands/lakhs/millions or decimals; ≥ ₹100 Cr → lakhs, millions or crores or decimals

Statement

Schedule III Division II (General Instructions — rounding off): depending upon the total income of the company, the figures appearing in the financial statements may be rounded off as below.

Total income Permitted rounding
Less than ₹100 crore To the nearest hundreds, thousands, lakhs or millions, or decimals thereof
₹100 crore or more To the nearest lakhs, millions or crores, or decimals thereof

Once a unit is selected it shall be used uniformly in the financial statements (faces, notes and comparatives). Rounding is optional — a company may present in full rupees.

In the engine:

\[ V_{L}^{\text{rounded}} = \text{ROUND}\left(\frac{V_{L}}{u}, d\right) \]

where \(V_{L}\) is the unrounded line value in rupees, \(u\) the unit divisor (100; 1,000; 1,00,000; 10,00,000 for millions; 1,00,00,000 for crores) and \(d\) the number of decimals shown.

Explanation

The rule balances readability with precision: small companies can show more granular units; large companies may not show figures in hundreds or thousands (which would make statements unwieldy) and must use lakhs, millions or crores. The test is on total income as per the P&L (revenue from operations plus other income); in practice "turnover" in the concept text refers to this test — confirm the reference year when a company is near the ₹100 crore boundary and apply consistently with comparatives.

Implementation rules:

  • Round at the output layer only. Compute every line and subtotal in rupees, then round each displayed figure. Totals computed from unrounded values may differ by 1–2 units from the sum of rounded components; companies either accept and footnote this or apply a controlled rounding adjustment. Never round account balances before aggregation.
  • Single unit cell. A named cell (Unit) and (Decimals) drive every output; changing unit is a one-cell change.
  • Uniformity check. All output tabs reference the same unit cell; a check flags any output formula with a different divisor.
  • Non-currency figures (shares, EPS in ₹, ratios, percentages) are not rounded to the unit; EPS is presented in rupees and paise.
  • XBRL filings usually carry the unrounded rupee value with a decimals attribute (s08).

Illustration

A company's total income is ₹486.3 crore, so it may use lakhs, millions or crores (with decimals). It chooses ₹ crore with 2 decimals.

Line Unrounded ₹ Displayed (₹ crore)
Revenue from operations 4,79,12,34,560 479.12
Other income 7,16,48,210 7.16
Total income 4,86,28,82,770 486.29

Sum of displayed components = 486.28, displayed total = 486.29: a 0.01 casting difference arising purely from rounding, which the rounding check accepts (≤ 1 unit). Presenting this company's FS in ₹ thousands would breach Division II.

Connections

Part of s07 presentation; implemented in the s03 formula engine; interacts with s06 checks (rounding tolerance applies after rounding only) and s08 XBRL scale. Similar rounding conventions apply to SEBI results formats, which are often presented in ₹ lakh or crore with the same uniformity requirement.

Practice Questions

Q1. Permitted units [L1]

State the permitted rounding units for companies with total income of (a) ₹62 crore, (b) ₹100 crore exactly, (c) ₹2,450 crore, and whether each may present in ₹ thousands.

Q2. Rounding implementation [L2]

Describe how you would implement rounding in the Project 1 workbook so that the unit can be switched between ₹ lakh and ₹ crore in one place, totals reconcile within tolerance, and EPS and ratios are unaffected. Write the formulas using named cells.

💡 Hint: Separate the calculation layer from the display layer.

Q3. Crossing the threshold [L2]

A company reported in ₹ thousands when total income was ₹88 crore; this year total income is ₹131 crore. Explain the change required, the treatment of comparatives, and any disclosure you would make.
