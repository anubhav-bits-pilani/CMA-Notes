Subtopic note

Chart of Accounts Design for a Mapping-Driven Close

Scope: Chart of accounts design: numeric ranges by class, natural account + cost centre/segment/entity dimensions

Why it matters

A mapping engine is only as good as the chart of accounts (COA) it maps. A COA with 4,000 accounts where "Repairs – Plant A", "Repairs – Plant B" and "Repairs – Building" are separate natural accounts, IC and third-party balances share codes, and new accounts are created freely, cannot generate Schedule III notes, segment reporting (Ind AS 108), the cash flow statement or deferred tax schedules without manual analysis. A well-designed COA separates what the balance is (natural account) from where/who it belongs to (dimensions), uses numeric ranges that make classification predictable, and is governed so that additions trigger mapping review. This is also the foundation of 3-statement modelling: historicals extracted from a disciplined COA drop straight into a model template.

Core notes

1. Natural account with numeric ranges by class

The natural account describes the nature of the balance — the level at which Schedule III, Ind AS and tax care. A common Indian corporate structure (6-digit):

Range Class Examples
100000–199999 Non-current assets 110000 Freehold land; 121000 Plant & machinery – gross; 121900 P&M – accumulated depreciation; 140000 ROU assets; 150000 Non-current investments; 170000 Deferred tax assets
200000–299999 Current assets 210000 Raw materials; 230000 Trade receivables – third party; 231000 Trade receivables – IC; 239000 ECL allowance; 250000 Bank – current accounts; 270000 GST input ledgers
300000–399999 Equity 310000 Equity share capital; 330000 Securities premium; 350000 Retained earnings; 360000 OCI reserves
400000–499999 Non-current liabilities 410000 Term loans; 430000 Lease liabilities – non-current; 450000 Gratuity provision
500000–599999 Current liabilities 510000 Cash credit; 520000 Trade payables – MSME; 521000 Trade payables – others; 540000 GST output; 550000 TDS payable; 590000 Suspense/clearing (error range)
600000–699999 Income 610000 Sale of products – domestic; 611000 Sale of products – export; 620000 Sale of services; 650000 Other income
700000–899999 Expenses 710000 Cost of materials consumed; 750000 Employee benefits; 780000 Finance costs; 790000 Depreciation; 800000+ Other expenses
900000–999999 Statistical / memo Headcount, units, quantities (non-financial)

Design rules:

  • Range implies class, so a mapping rule can default by range and unmapped accounts are easy to classify.
  • Gross and contra accounts are paired (gross cost and accumulated depreciation, receivables and ECL allowance) to generate PPE and receivables notes.
  • Separate accounts where Schedule III or Ind AS requires separate disclosure: MSME vs other trade payables; secured vs unsecured borrowings; IC vs third-party; related-party loans; capital advances vs other advances; contract assets vs receivables (Ind AS 115); current vs non-current portions of lease liabilities when not computed.
  • Do not encode location, product or department in the natural account — that belongs in dimensions.
  • Reserve a range for suspense/clearing accounts so the mapping engine can flag them as errors (w4_t4).

2. Dimensions

Dimension Purpose Reporting use
Entity / company code Legal entity Standalone FS, consolidation, IC
Cost centre Responsibility (department, plant, function) Management P&L, cost of production vs selling vs admin, budget ownership
Profit centre / segment Business line or geography Ind AS 108 segment note, segment P&L
Trading partner Counterparty group entity IC matching and eliminations (w4_t4 s01)
Product / project / WBS Product line, project or capex project Gross margin by product, CWIP ageing by project
Location / GSTIN State registration GST reconciliation by GSTIN (w4_t2)
Functional area (SAP) Cost of sales / distribution / admin Function-of-expense analysis

A full GL string might read: Entity 1100 · Natural 750100 (salaries) · Cost centre 2310 (Pune plant maintenance) · Profit centre P02 (Auto components) · Trading partner blank.

3. Why dimensions instead of more accounts

  • Fewer accounts, cleaner mapping: one "Repairs – plant & machinery" natural account mapped once, rather than 40 location-specific accounts mapped 40 times.
  • Multi-view reporting from the same posting: Schedule III (nature of expense), management (by function/cost centre), segment (by profit centre) and budget (by owner).
  • Consistency across entities: a group COA with shared natural accounts and entity-specific dimensions makes consolidation and benchmarking possible.

4. Tally and smaller ERPs

Tally Prime uses ledger groups rather than numeric ranges; emulate the design by (a) structuring groups to mirror Schedule III lines, (b) adding a numeric code in the ledger alias, and (c) using cost centres/cost categories for dimensions. The mapping table then keys on ledger name plus alias.

5. COA governance

  • New accounts requested via a form specifying nature, Schedule III line, note, current/non-current logic, Ind AS 7 and DT category (s02).
  • Approval by the financial reporting owner; mapping table updated before the account is opened for posting.
  • Periodic review for dormant accounts (block for posting), duplicates and accounts with mixed nature.

Illustration

A company has these legacy accounts: "Salary – Mumbai office", "Salary – Pune plant", "Salary – Chennai sales", "Bonus – all locations", "PF contribution – Pune". Redesign:

Natural account Dimension tags
750100 Salaries and wages Cost centre: Mumbai HO / Pune plant / Chennai sales
750200 Bonus and incentives Cost centre as above
750300 Contribution to PF and other funds Cost centre as above

Schedule III note "Employee benefits expense" now draws from three natural accounts; management reporting splits manufacturing vs selling vs admin by cost centre attribute; budget owners see their cost centres.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mixed-nature accounts (e.g., "Advances – others" used for supplier advances, employee advances and security deposits) cannot be mapped correctly; split them or require a sub-classification dimension.
  • Current/non-current usually cannot be encoded in the natural account for loans and leases — maturity changes over time. Keep one account and compute the split in the mapping/adjustment layer (s05).
  • Statistical accounts must be excluded from the TB-nets-to-zero check.
  • ERP migrations often create one-to-many or many-to-one mappings from old to new accounts; maintain a bridge table so comparatives can be regenerated.
  • Group vs local COA: subsidiaries may need local accounts for statutory/tax reasons (e.g., Sec 43B items); map local accounts to group accounts in a second mapping layer.

Key takeaways

  • Separate the natural account (what) from dimensions (where/who/which segment).
  • Numeric ranges by class make default mapping and error detection predictable; reserve a range for suspense/clearing.
  • Create separate natural accounts wherever Schedule III or Ind AS requires separate disclosure.
  • Dimensions support Ind AS 108 segments, cost-centre budgets, IC matching and GSTIN-level reconciliations from one posting.
  • Govern COA changes so mapping is updated before posting begins.

Practice Questions

Q1. Range design [L1]

Propose a 6-digit numeric range structure for an Indian manufacturing company and allocate natural accounts for: MSME trade payables, capital advances, current maturities of term loans, contract liabilities, provision for gratuity, unbilled revenue, and securities premium. Justify each placement.

Q2. Accounts vs dimensions [L2]

A retail company has 2,600 GL accounts, including 180 rent accounts (one per store) and 120 electricity accounts. Redesign the structure using natural accounts and dimensions, and explain how the Schedule III note "Other expenses", the Ind AS 116 lease schedule and store-level FP&A reporting would each be generated after the redesign.

💡 Hint: Think about what each consumer of the data actually needs to aggregate by.

Q3. Mixed-nature account clean-up [L2]

An account "Loans and advances – others" has a balance of ₹14.8 crore comprising security deposits ₹6.2 crore, supplier advances ₹3.9 crore (of which ₹1.4 crore for capital goods), employee loans ₹1.1 crore, loans to a subsidiary ₹3.0 crore repayable on demand, and GST refund receivable ₹0.6 crore. Design the replacement accounts and state the Schedule III line and any 2021-amendment disclosure each component requires.

Q4. COA governance policy [L3]

Draft a COA change-control procedure (request, approval, mapping update, testing, communication) for a group of six entities using SAP S/4HANA, and explain how it prevents unmapped accounts at month-end.
