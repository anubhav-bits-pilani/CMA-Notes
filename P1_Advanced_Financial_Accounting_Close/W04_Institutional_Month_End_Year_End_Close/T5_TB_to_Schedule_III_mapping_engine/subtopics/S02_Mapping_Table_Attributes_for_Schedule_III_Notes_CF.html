Subtopic note

Mapping Table Attributes for Schedule III, Notes, CF and DT

Scope: Mapping attributes: Schedule III line, note, sub-note, current/non-current flag, Ind AS 7 category, DT category, segment

Why it matters

The mapping table is the "brain" of an automated financial statement build. If each GL account carries only a face-line mapping ("Trade payables"), the balance sheet can be produced but the notes, MSME split, cash flow statement, deferred tax reconciliation and segment information all need manual work every close. If each account carries a full attribute set, the same TB produces the face statements, every note and sub-note, the indirect cash flow, the DT schedule and segment data — and an adjusting entry posted at T+6 updates all of them at once. This is what "single source of truth" means in practice, and it is the step that separates a reporting workbook from a stack of linked spreadsheets.

Not every attribute applies to every account, but every account must have a Line ID, statement and normal sign; blanks in mandatory fields are an error.

2. Schedule III line, note, sub-note

  • Line = face-level caption as per Division II (e.g., "Financial liabilities – Borrowings", "Other current liabilities").
  • Note = the note that analyses that line (Division II requires sub-classification in notes — e.g., borrowings into bonds, term loans, deposits, loans from related parties, secured/unsecured).
  • Sub-note = row inside the note or a required disclosure (MSME vs others; ageing buckets come from sub-ledger data rather than the GL map, but the GL map identifies which accounts feed the ageing totals that must tie to the face).

A three-level hierarchical Line ID (e.g., BS.NCA.PPE → N3 → N3.GrossBlock.PlantMachinery) allows SUMIFS at any level.

3. Current / non-current flag

Ind AS 1.66 and 1.69 define current assets and liabilities (operating cycle, held for trading, 12 months after reporting period, no right to defer settlement). Three flag types:

  • C or NC — fixed by nature (inventories C; deferred tax always NC under Ind AS 1.56).
  • Compute — the account holds both portions (term loans, lease liabilities, security deposits, long-term prepaids); the split comes from a maturity schedule in the adjustment layer (s05). The 2020 amendments to Ind AS 1 on classification of liabilities (right to defer settlement at the reporting date; covenants) apply when computing.

4. Ind AS 7 category

Tagging each BS account with its cash flow role allows the indirect method to be generated from balance sheet movements:

BS account Tag CF treatment
Trade receivables OP-WC − increase
Inventories OP-WC − increase
Trade payables OP-WC + increase
PPE gross INV-CAPEX (with non-cash adjustments for disposals, ROU, capitalised interest) − additions
Accumulated depreciation NONCASH-DEP add back via P&L
Term loans FIN-BORROW + proceeds / − repayments (from loan schedule)
Lease liabilities FIN-LEASE (principal) − payments; additions non-cash
Cash and bank CASH Closing cash

P&L accounts carry tags too (depreciation, interest expense, interest income, dividend income, FX unrealised) so non-cash and reclassification adjustments are automated. Gross movements (additions, disposals) come from sub-schedules, not net BS movements alone — this was covered in w1_t6.

5. DT category

Each account that can give rise to a temporary difference carries the category used in the DT working (w3_t3): PPE (book vs tax WDV), ROU assets/lease liabilities (Ind AS 12.15 exemption no longer applies to leases after the 2023 amendment — DT on both sides), provisions allowable on payment (Sec 43B, Sec 40(a)(ia)), ECL allowance, fair-value changes, unabsorbed depreciation/losses (from tax computation). The mapping makes the DT schedule's book bases tie to the TB automatically.

6. Segment attribute

Where the segment can be derived from the profit centre dimension, the mapping holds "use dimension"; where an account is common (corporate costs, treasury, tax), it is tagged "unallocated" so the Ind AS 108 reconciliation to entity totals can be generated.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • One account, two lines over time: a bank account that is overdrawn at year-end needs a reclassification rule (s05), not a mapping change.
  • Accounts that split between notes (e.g., "Other payables" containing capital creditors and employee dues) — split the account or add sub-classification via a sub-ledger attribute; mapping cannot apportion reliably.
  • Ind AS 7 category for interest and dividends differs from IAS 7: Ind AS 7.33 requires entities other than financial institutions to classify interest paid as financing and interest and dividends received as investing, and dividends paid are financing (Ind AS 7.34). Encode these as fixed tags rather than leaving them to preparers, and remember interest capitalised under Ind AS 23 still needs a decision on presentation consistent with the policy.
  • DT categories for accounts with permanent differences (CSR expense, penalties) should be tagged "permanent" so the tax rate reconciliation (Ind AS 12.81(c)) can also be generated.
  • Version control: mapping changes alter comparatives; date-stamp mapping versions and regenerate the prior year with the current mapping when comparatives are regrouped (s07).

Key takeaways

  • Map every account to line, note and sub-note — face-only mapping cannot produce notes (topic pitfall).
  • Use C/NC flags, with "Compute" for accounts whose split depends on maturity schedules.
  • Ind AS 7 and DT tags turn the same TB into the cash flow statement and DT schedule automatically.
  • Segment, related-party, FI category and ratio-role attributes extend the same engine to Ind AS 108, 24, 107 and the ratio note.
  • Mandatory attributes left blank are errors that must fail the integrity check.

Practice Questions

Q1. Complete the mapping [L1]

For the following accounts, fill in Statement, Line ID (your own naming), Note, Sub-note, C/NC, Ind AS 7 tag, DT category and normal sign: capital advances; lease liabilities; provision for leave encashment; FVTPL mutual fund investments; unbilled revenue (Ind AS 115 contract asset); interest expense on lease liabilities; CSR expense.

Q2. Generate the cash flow from tags [L2]

Using the tags in your answer to Q1 and the following movements for FY 2025-26 (₹ crore) — trade receivables up 6.2, inventories down 2.4, trade payables up 3.1, PPE gross additions 18.0 (including 2.0 ROU additions and 0.5 capitalised borrowing cost), term loan proceeds 10.0, repayments 4.5, lease principal paid 1.8 — explain how the engine classifies each item and which items need a sub-schedule rather than a net BS movement.

💡 Hint: Non-cash additions must be stripped out of investing cash flows.

Q3. DT schedule tie-out [L2]

Explain how DT category tags allow the deferred tax working to reconcile to the TB automatically, using gratuity provision (₹3.4 crore, deductible on payment under Sec 43B) and PPE (book WDV ₹120 crore, tax WDV ₹96 crore) as examples at a 25.168% tax rate.

Q4. Design review [L3]

A reporting workbook maps 1,100 accounts to face lines only. Management wants notes, CF and segment information generated automatically from next quarter. Prepare a project plan for enriching the mapping table: attribute definitions, data sources, review and approval, testing against the last audited FS, and controls over future changes.
