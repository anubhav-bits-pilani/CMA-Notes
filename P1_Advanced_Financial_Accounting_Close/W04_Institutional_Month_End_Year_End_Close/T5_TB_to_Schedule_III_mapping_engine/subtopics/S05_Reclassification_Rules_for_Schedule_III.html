Subtopic note

Reclassification Rules for Schedule III Presentation

Scope: Reclassification logic: debit-balance creditors to advances, current maturities of long-term debt, overdrawn banks to borrowings

Why it matters

ERP balances are organised by process, Schedule III by nature and maturity. A vendor control account that nets advances against payables, a term-loan account holding both current and non-current instalments, and a cash credit account that swings between debit and credit cannot be presented as-is without breaching Ind AS 1 offsetting and classification rules. These reclassifications change the current ratio, net working capital, debt-equity, DPO and covenant calculations, so they matter to lenders, analysts and FP&A — and auditors routinely raise them as presentation misstatements. Encoding them as rules in the mapping engine makes them repeatable and reviewable instead of a year-end scramble.

Core notes

1. Principles

  • No offsetting of assets and liabilities, or income and expenses, unless required or permitted by an Ind AS (Ind AS 1.32). Financial assets and liabilities are offset only with a legally enforceable right and intention to settle net or simultaneously (Ind AS 32.42).
  • Classification by nature and maturity at the reporting date (Ind AS 1.60–1.76).
  • Presentation-only: reclassifications do not change profit or equity; they move amounts between lines, usually as reversing entries in the adjustment layer (s04) or by rule in the mapping engine.
  • Rule-based: each reclassification has a trigger (sign, maturity, party type), a source and target Line ID, and a data source (sub-ledger, loan schedule).

2. Debit-balance creditors to advances

Trigger: vendor-level balance in a trade payables account is debit (w4_t4 c05).

Nature of debit balance Target line
Advance against supply of goods/services (revenue nature) Other current assets — advances to suppliers
Capital advance (for PPE) Other non-current assets — capital advances (Schedule III requires separate disclosure)
Refundable overpayment / duplicate payment Other current financial assets
Debit note pending vendor acceptance Other current financial assets (or reduce payable to the vendor if agreed)

Implementation: party-level AP open-item extract with sign test; reclass amount = sum of debit balances by category; reverse next day. The same rule in reverse applies to credit-balance debtors → contract liabilities (advances from customers, Ind AS 115) or other financial liabilities (refunds due).

3. Current maturities of long-term debt

Trigger: instalments of non-current borrowings due within 12 months after the reporting date (Ind AS 1.69(c)) or loans where the entity does not have the right at the reporting date to defer settlement for at least 12 months (Ind AS 1.69(d), as amended).

  • Under Schedule III Division II (2021 amendment), current maturities of long-term borrowings are presented within current borrowings (Note: "Current maturities of long-term borrowings" as a sub-item), not under other financial liabilities as earlier.
  • Data source: loan repayment schedule (principal only). Interest accrued but not due is an other current financial liability, not borrowings.
  • Covenant breaches — an Ind AS carve-out: if a material provision of a long-term loan is breached on or before the reporting date so that the loan becomes payable on demand, IAS 1 would classify the whole loan as current unless a waiver was obtained by the reporting date. Ind AS 1.74 differs: the liability is not classified as current if the lender agrees, after the reporting period but before the FS are approved for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach. If no such agreement exists by approval date, the whole loan is current. Document the waiver date against the board approval date. Disclosure of covenant terms and breaches is still required (Ind AS 107 defaults and breaches; Schedule III notes on defaults in repayment).
  • Lease liabilities follow the same split using the lease schedule (principal portion of payments due within 12 months).

4. Overdrawn banks to borrowings

Trigger: a bank account (current account or cash credit/overdraft facility) with a credit balance at the reporting date.

  • Balance sheet: present the credit balance under current borrowings — "Loans repayable on demand from banks" (secured/unsecured) — not as negative cash. Debit balances on other accounts with the same bank are not netted unless Ind AS 32.42 conditions are met (rare in practice).
  • Cash flow statement: bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form an integral part of the entity's cash management are included as a component of cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.8). The CF closing cash will then equal BS cash and cash equivalents minus such overdrafts; the CF-to-BS check (s06) must include this reconciling line, and Ind AS 7.45 requires the reconciliation to be disclosed.
  • Cash credit facilities used as working capital funding are generally treated as borrowings (financing), not cash equivalents, unless they meet the "integral part of cash management" test — a judgement to document.

5. Illustration (₹ lakh, 31 March)

Item Ledger balance Reclass Presented as
Trade payables control 3,240 Cr Debit vendors 185 (supplier advances 120, capital advances 50, overpayment 15) Trade payables 3,425 Cr; Other current assets 120; Other non-current assets 50; Other current financial assets 15
Term loan (repayable ₹150 per quarter) 2,100 Cr Current maturities 600 Non-current borrowings 1,500; Current borrowings 600
Bank – HDFC current account 310 Dr — Cash and cash equivalents 310
Bank – SBI cash credit 845 Cr Overdraft 845 Current borrowings 845 (CF: borrowings unless integral to cash management)

Effect on current ratio: compared with a ledger-based view, current liabilities rise by 785 (185 grossed-up payables + 600 current maturities) and current assets rise by only 135 (120 supplier advances + 15 overpayment), because the 50 capital advance is non-current. If the preparer had also netted the SBI cash credit against the HDFC balance, cash would have shown a negative 535 — a further presentation error. The ratio can therefore move materially purely from correct presentation.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rolling cash credit with annual renewal is current even though "in substance" long term, because the entity lacks a right to defer settlement beyond 12 months unless the facility gives that right at the reporting date.
  • Refinancing after year-end does not make current maturities non-current (Ind AS 1.72); only a right to roll over existing at the reporting date counts (Ind AS 1.73). Do not confuse this with the Ind AS 1.74 covenant-waiver carve-out, which is specific to breaches.
  • Customer advances vs deposits: refundable security deposits from customers are financial liabilities; non-refundable advances for future supply are contract liabilities.
  • Sweep/pooled accounts — offsetting may be appropriate only with legal right and net-settlement practice; otherwise gross.
  • Comparatives must be reclassified on the same basis (Ind AS 1.41) — see s07.
  • MSME disclosures: reclassification of debit-balance MSME vendors must also update the MSMED Act disclosure figures.

Key takeaways

  • Reclassifications enforce Ind AS 1 no-offsetting and maturity rules; they move amounts between lines without changing profit.
  • Debit-balance creditors go to advances (capital advances separately) or financial assets; credit-balance debtors go to contract or financial liabilities.
  • Current maturities of long-term borrowings sit within current borrowings under Division II; covenant breaches at the reporting date make the whole loan current unless the Ind AS 1.74 carve-out (lender agreement before FS approval) applies.
  • Overdrafts are borrowings on the BS but may be cash equivalents in the CFS (Ind AS 7.8), creating a known reconciling item in the CF check.
  • Encode each reclassification as a rule with trigger, source, target and data source.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and present [L1]

At 31 March: vendor debit balances ₹2.4 crore (₹0.9 crore capital advances for a new line, ₹1.2 crore raw-material advances, ₹0.3 crore duplicate payment); customer credit balances ₹1.1 crore (all advances for future supply); a cash credit account with a credit balance of ₹6.8 crore. State the Schedule III line for each and pass the reclassification entries.

Q2. Current maturities with a covenant breach [L2]

A company has a ₹60 crore term loan repayable in 12 equal half-yearly instalments, the first due 30 June of the coming year. At 31 March it breaches the debt-service coverage covenant; the lender issues a waiver on 20 April for 15 months. Determine the current and non-current classification at 31 March, the disclosure required, and the effect on the current ratio if current assets are ₹85 crore and current liabilities before this loan are ₹62 crore.

💡 Hint: Consider when the waiver was obtained relative to the reporting date and the FS approval date, and whether Ind AS differs from IAS 1 here. Assume the board approves the FS on 22 May.

Q3. Overdraft and the cash flow check [L2]

BS cash and cash equivalents are ₹4.2 crore; an overdraft of ₹1.6 crore with the same bank is used for daily cash management and fluctuates between debit and credit. The CF statement shows closing cash of ₹2.6 crore. Explain whether the CF-to-BS check passes, what disclosure is needed, and how the check formula in the engine should be written.

Q4. Rule engine design [L3]

Write the rule table and the Power Query or Excel steps that would generate reclassification entries automatically each month for: debit-balance creditors (by category), credit-balance debtors, current maturities from a loan schedule with five loans, and overdrawn banks. Explain how the rules reverse and how you would evidence them for audit.
