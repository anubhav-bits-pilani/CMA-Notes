Subtopic note

Reusing the Mapping for MCA and SEBI XBRL Filings

Scope: XBRL reuse: MCA AOC-4 XBRL (Ind AS taxonomy) and SEBI results XBRL

Why it matters

The same financial statements are filed in machine-readable XBRL with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (annual FS in Form AOC-4 XBRL) and, for listed entities, with the stock exchanges (quarterly and annual results under SEBI LODR Reg 33). XBRL tagging is often outsourced and done from the final PDF, which means re-keying, a second reconciliation and a risk that the filed data differ from the audited FS — differences that regulators, data aggregators and analysts can now detect automatically. If the mapping table already carries an XBRL element for each line and note, the filing becomes a by-product of the engine rather than a separate project.

Core notes

1. What XBRL is

eXtensible Business Reporting Language tags each reported value with:

  • an element from a taxonomy (e.g., "TradeReceivablesCurrent", "RevenueFromOperations"),
  • a context (entity, period — instant for balances, duration for flows; and dimensions such as standalone/consolidated, segment, class of share),
  • a unit (INR, shares, pure for ratios) and decimals/scale.

Validation rules (calculation linkbase) check that tagged values add up, which mirrors the integrity checks in s06.

2. MCA — AOC-4 XBRL

  • Applicability (Companies (Filing of Documents and Forms in Extensible Business Reporting Language) Rules 2015, as amended): (a) companies listed in India and their Indian subsidiaries; (b) companies with paid-up capital of ₹5 crore or more; (c) companies with turnover of ₹100 crore or more; (d) companies required to prepare FS under the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules 2015. Banking, insurance, power and NBFC companies are excluded (they follow sector formats). Verify current applicability on the MCA portal before relying on this list.
  • Taxonomy: MCA publishes separate taxonomies for Ind AS-based FS (the "Ind AS taxonomy") and for Accounting Standards-based FS. The Ind AS taxonomy follows the Division II structure and includes elements for notes and the 2021 additional disclosures.
  • Filing: AOC-4 XBRL within 30 days of the AGM (Sec 137), with standalone and consolidated FS (AOC-4 CFS XBRL). Since the move to the MCA V3 portal, XBRL instance documents are prepared with the MCA tool or third-party software, validated and attached to the web form.
  • Content: FS, notes, auditor's report details (including CARO responses in coded form), directors' report items in summary, and general information about the company.

3. SEBI — results XBRL

  • Listed entities file quarterly and annual financial results under Reg 33 in PDF and in XBRL on the stock exchange platforms (NSE NEAPS/Digital Exchange, BSE Listing Centre) within the prescribed timeline — the XBRL filing generally within 24 hours of the PDF submission.
  • The exchanges publish their own taxonomies/Excel utilities for results (standalone and consolidated, segment information, statement of assets and liabilities for half-years, cash flow statement for half-years).
  • SEBI's Integrated Filing (Financial) framework, introduced from 2025, combines Reg 33 results and related financial disclosures (e.g., statement of deviation in use of proceeds, RPT disclosures, outstanding defaults) into a single quarterly XBRL-based filing — check the current SEBI circular and exchange formats for scope and timelines.
  • Corporate governance, shareholding pattern (Reg 31) and other periodic filings are also XBRL-based; consistency across these filings and the results is reviewed by exchanges.

4. Reusing the mapping engine

Add XBRL attributes to the Lines table (and to sub-notes where notes are tagged):

Line ID Caption MCA Ind AS element SEBI results element Period type Sign in taxonomy
BS.CA.FA.TR Trade receivables (current trade receivables element) (results SOAL element) Instant Debit
PL.REV.OPS Revenue from operations (revenue from operations element) (revenue from operations element) Duration Credit
PL.EXP.FIN Finance costs (finance costs element) (finance costs element) Duration Debit

Element names should be copied from the taxonomy version in use (taxonomies are revised; element names and hierarchy can change). The engine then exports a two-column table (element, value) with context and unit to the XBRL tool, or populates the exchange's Excel utility directly.

Benefits:

  • One reconciliation: XBRL values = engine values = audited FS, verified by a check (sum of tagged values vs face totals).
  • Sign handling: XBRL elements carry a balance attribute (debit/credit); values are entered as positive numbers in their natural sense, with negative values only for genuinely reversed amounts. The engine's display sign (c01) usually aligns with this.
  • Rounding/scale: XBRL values are often filed in full rupees with a decimals attribute, while the PDF uses lakhs or crores — keep unrounded values available for tagging.

Illustration

A listed company files Q2 results on 11 November (board meeting date). The engine output for revenue from operations is ₹1,24,56,78,912; the PDF shows ₹12,456.79 lakh; the SEBI XBRL tags the value in rupees with decimals −5 (or as the utility requires). Because both come from the same Line ID, a check PDF value × 1,00,000 − XBRL value within rounding tolerance passes automatically. The same Line IDs are reused in March for the AOC-4 XBRL, with the MCA element names.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Company-specific line items not in the taxonomy require extension or the "other" elements; avoid creating extensions where a standard element fits — data comparability suffers.
  • Restated comparatives must be tagged as restated; mismatches between the previous year's filed XBRL and the current comparative are common queries.
  • Consolidated vs standalone contexts must not be mixed; the engine's reporting-basis tag (s04) should drive the context.
  • Late changes after board approval (e.g., a typo in a note) require consistency across PDF and XBRL; re-filing may be needed.
  • Taxonomy versions change; maintain a version field in the XBRL attribute table and re-map when the MCA or exchanges update.
  • Ratios and non-financial values (shares, percentages, headcount) use different units — tag with the correct unit type.

Key takeaways

  • XBRL tags each value with element, context, unit and decimals; calculation rules mirror the engine's integrity checks.
  • AOC-4 XBRL applies to listed companies and subsidiaries, companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹5 crore or turnover ≥ ₹100 crore, and Ind AS companies (with sector exclusions); it uses the Ind AS taxonomy.
  • Listed entities file Reg 33 results in XBRL soon after the PDF, increasingly through SEBI's integrated filing framework.
  • Adding XBRL element attributes to the Line ID table makes filings a by-product of the same single source of truth.
  • Keep unrounded values and taxonomy versions in the engine to avoid scale and re-mapping errors.

Practice Questions

Q1. Applicability test [L1]

Determine whether AOC-4 XBRL filing applies to each company and why: (a) unlisted private company, paid-up capital ₹3 crore, turnover ₹140 crore, following AS; (b) wholly-owned Indian subsidiary of an NSE-listed company, paid-up capital ₹10 lakh, turnover ₹8 crore; (c) NBFC with turnover ₹600 crore; (d) unlisted public company preparing Ind AS FS voluntarily, paid-up capital ₹2 crore, turnover ₹40 crore.

Q2. Extending the mapping table [L2]

Design the additional columns you would add to the Lines table to support both MCA AOC-4 XBRL and SEBI results XBRL, and describe the export process and the reconciliation check between the PDF results and the XBRL instance.

💡 Hint: Think about period type, context, unit, scale and taxonomy version.

Q3. XBRL query from the exchange [L2]

After filing Q3 results, the exchange flags that the XBRL shows total income of ₹4,812.6 crore while the PDF shows ₹4,821.6 crore. Describe how this could happen, how you would investigate, and what process change prevents it.

Q4. Interview: why XBRL matters to FP&A [L1]

Explain why an FP&A or investor-relations analyst should care about XBRL tagging of the company's and peers' results, giving two practical uses.
