Subtopic note

Flux Threshold Design: Dual Tests and the Right Comparatives

Scope: Threshold design: dual % and absolute ₹ tests; BS vs prior month-end/year-end; P&L MoM, YoY and vs budget

Why it matters

Flux commentary is only as useful as the list of movements it explains. Thresholds that are too tight produce hundreds of trivial explanations nobody reads; thresholds that are too loose let a ₹20 crore receivable build-up pass without comment; the wrong comparative (e.g., comparing a March P&L to February when the business is seasonal) produces flags that are "explained" by the calendar rather than by performance. Good threshold design decides which movements management must understand each month, and the same thresholds underpin SA 520 analytics, audit committee packs and the Project 1 checklist requirement to explain > 10% movements tied to the final TB.

Core notes

1. Dual percentage and absolute ₹ tests

A line is flagged for commentary when both tests are met (c01):

\[ \left\lvert \frac{C - P}{\lvert P \rvert} \right\rvert > X\% \quad \text{and} \quad \lvert C - P \rvert > A \]

where \(C\) is the current amount, \(P\) the comparative, \(X\%\) the percentage threshold (the roadmap standard is 10%) and \(A\) the absolute threshold.

Setting A. Derive \(A\) from materiality so that commentary, JE review and reconciliation escalation use a consistent yardstick (week cross-cutting item):

Base Typical overall materiality Commentary threshold \(A\)
PBT (profit-oriented, stable) 5% of PBT 5–10% of overall materiality, or a round ₹ amount near it
Revenue (low-margin or loss-making) 0.5–1% of revenue as above
Total assets (asset-heavy, financial services) 0.5–1% of total assets as above

Example: PBT ₹160 crore → overall materiality ≈ ₹8 crore → performance materiality ≈ ₹6 crore → \(A\) ≈ ₹50 lakh for monthly line-level commentary, with a lower \(A\) for sensitive lines (related-party, exceptional items, statutory dues).

Override. Any movement above performance materiality is flagged regardless of %, so large balances moving < 10% are not missed.

Granularity. Apply thresholds at the level at which commentary is written — Schedule III line or management P&L line — not at GL account level, where offsetting reclassifications between accounts create noise.

2. Balance sheet comparatives

Balance sheet items are stocks, so the natural comparatives are:

  • Prior month-end — for monthly management review: detects build-ups in receivables, inventory, payables, provisions within the year.
  • Prior year-end (31 March) — for quarterly/annual reporting and for the Schedule III/limited-review perspective (the BS in Reg 33 half-year results is compared with the previous year-end).
  • Same month-end last year — for seasonal businesses (e.g., a fertiliser company's inventory before kharif, a retailer before Diwali).

3. P&L comparatives

P&L items are flows, so commentary uses:

  • MoM (month vs previous month) — operational trend; sensitive to number of days, holidays and seasonality.
  • YoY (month or YTD vs the same period last year) — removes seasonality; matches the quarterly results format (quarter vs corresponding quarter of previous year, and vs preceding quarter).
  • Vs budget/forecast — management accountability; the comparative FP&A uses for performance (budget vs actual vs latest forecast).

A practical monthly pack shows actual, prior month, same month last year, budget and YTD equivalents, with flags computed on each comparison and commentary written for the comparisons that management uses (commonly YoY and vs budget for P&L; vs prior month-end and year-end for BS).

4. Excel implementation

Named cells FluxPct, FluxAbs, FluxOverride; table Flux with columns Line, Curr, PM, PY, Bud:

=LET(c,[@Curr], p,[@PY], d,c-p, pct,IF(p=0,1,ABS(d)/ABS(p)),
     IF(OR(ABS(d)>FluxOverride, AND(pct>FluxPct, ABS(d)>FluxAbs)), "EXPLAIN", ""))

Repeat for PM and Bud columns, or wrap in a LAMBDA (see c01). Conditional formatting highlights EXPLAIN cells; a FILTER builds the commentary list.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Zero or tiny comparatives — percentage is meaningless; rely on the absolute test.
  • Sign changes (profit to loss, asset to liability) — use \(\lvert P \rvert\) in the denominator and describe in words; percentage changes across zero mislead.
  • Day-count effects — February vs March, or 4-4-5 calendars; consider per-day metrics for MoM in volume businesses.
  • Budget phasing — a budget phased evenly across months when the business is seasonal creates false flags; fix the phasing, not the threshold.
  • Qualitative flags — some items need commentary regardless of size: related-party transactions above Reg 23 limits, exceptional items, any movement in provisions for litigation, changes in accounting estimates.
  • Threshold review — recalibrate annually (and when PBT changes significantly) and document the basis; auditors ask how thresholds were set when they rely on management's analytics.

Key takeaways

  • Flag a movement only when it exceeds both the % threshold and an absolute ₹ threshold, with an absolute override for very large moves.
  • Derive the ₹ threshold from materiality so that commentary, JE review and reconciliation escalation are consistent.
  • Compare BS to prior month-end and year-end (and same month last year if seasonal); P&L MoM, YoY and vs budget.
  • Apply thresholds at the level commentary is written, not at GL account level.
  • Handle zero comparatives, sign changes, phasing and qualitative items explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply thresholds to a pack [L1]

Using thresholds of 10% and ₹40 lakh with an override of ₹5 crore, identify which lines need commentary YoY: Revenue ₹86.2 crore vs ₹79.5 crore; Freight ₹3.1 crore vs ₹2.5 crore; Legal fees ₹0.35 crore vs ₹0.12 crore; Inventories ₹64.0 crore vs ₹59.2 crore (vs PY end); Provisions ₹6.9 crore vs ₹6.6 crore.

Q2. Calibrating thresholds from materiality [L2]

A listed FMCG company has revenue ₹4,200 crore and PBT ₹520 crore; the auditor's overall materiality is ₹26 crore and performance materiality ₹19.5 crore. Propose monthly and quarterly thresholds (%, absolute, override) for P&L and BS commentary, explaining your choices and any lines with lower thresholds.

💡 Hint: Monthly figures are roughly one-twelfth of annual flows; consider whether the same ₹ threshold suits both.

Q3. Choosing comparatives [L2]

For a seed company whose sales are 60% in May–July, recommend the comparatives for (a) the monthly P&L review, (b) monthly inventory and receivables review, (c) quarterly board pack. Justify each and explain how budget phasing should be built.

Q4. Formula build [L3]

Write a single LET-based Excel formula (no absolute cell references) that returns "EXPLAIN-YOY", "EXPLAIN-BUD", "EXPLAIN-BOTH" or blank for each line in a flux table with columns Curr, PY and Bud, using named cells for thresholds and an override. Then describe how you would produce a filtered commentary list for the pack.
