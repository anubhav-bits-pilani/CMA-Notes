Subtopic note

Commentary Structure: What, Why, Recurring, Forecast, Action

Scope: Commentary structure: what moved, why (driver/root cause), recurring or not, forecast impact, owner/action

Why it matters

Management reads flux commentary to decide: is anything wrong, will it continue, and what do we do? Commentary that only describes ("Other expenses increased due to higher expenses") answers none of these. A fixed structure forces each explanation to quantify the movement, identify the driver and root cause, say whether it will recur, translate it into the forecast, and name who owns the follow-up. The structure also makes commentary reviewable — a controller can check each element against the numbers — and reusable in MD&A, results press releases and audit committee packs. It is the standard the Week 4 checklist sets: quantified, driver-based commentary for > 10% movements tied to the final TB.

Core notes

1. The five elements

Element Question answered Good example Weak example
What moved Which line, by how much, vs which comparative "Freight outward ₹6.8 crore, up ₹1.4 crore (26%) vs Q3 FY 25" "Freight increased"
Why — driver Which operational quantities explain the change, quantified "₹0.9 crore higher despatch volume (+17% tonnes), ₹0.5 crore diesel-linked rate increase (+8% per tonne-km)" "due to higher sales"
Why — root cause What caused the driver to move "Volume from the new Gujarat dealer network; rate from the quarterly fuel escalation clause in transporter contracts" —
Recurring or not Will it repeat? "Volume effect recurring; ₹0.2 crore of the rate effect is a one-time retrospective escalation for Q2" —
Forecast impact Effect on full-year/forecast "Q4 forecast increased by ₹1.1 crore; FY 26 freight-to-sales ratio 4.1% vs budget 3.8%" —
Owner / action Who does what by when "Logistics head to rebid Gujarat lanes by 15 February; FP&A to update rolling forecast" —

2. Writing rules

  • Lead with the number and the comparative. State the amount, the change in ₹ and %, and the comparative (PY, budget, prior month).
  • Quantify every driver so that the drivers add up to the movement (with an explicit "other" if needed — and "other" should be small).
  • Use operational language — tonnes, headcount, price per unit, days — not accounting language ("higher provisions").
  • Separate one-offs and FX first (s03, s02); then explain the underlying movement.
  • Tie to the final TB. Every number in commentary must agree with the final post-adjustment TB (topic pitfall); include a reference (line ID, version) and re-run commentary if late adjustments change the numbers.
  • Brevity: 2–4 sentences per line; a bridge chart for the headline lines (revenue, EBITDA, PAT, net debt).
  • Consistent tense and units — ₹ crore with one decimal or two for the pack, matching the FS rounding.

3. Root cause vs driver

The driver is the arithmetic ("volume +17%"); the root cause is the business reason ("new dealer network", "competitor plant shutdown", "monsoon delay"). Controllers obtain root causes from business owners — sales, procurement, plant, HR — using a commentary request that pre-populates the numbers and drivers and asks only for the cause, recurrence and action. This is the "five whys" discipline applied to finance.

4. Recurring vs non-recurring

Classify each driver:

  • Recurring/structural — price increases, new contracts, headcount additions, permanent cost changes.
  • Timing — will reverse in a later period (early shipments, delayed invoices, accrual catch-up).
  • One-off — will not recur (settlements, write-offs, exceptional items).

Timing items are the ones most often misread; commentary must state when they reverse.

5. Forecast impact

Translate the classification into the forecast: recurring drivers roll into future months; timing items reverse; one-offs are excluded from run-rate. FP&A uses this to update the rolling forecast and to explain forecast changes ("Q4 EBITDA forecast up ₹3.2 crore: ₹2.4 crore price carried forward, ₹0.8 crore timing reversal of Q3 maintenance deferral").

6. Owner and action

Each material adverse or unexplained item has an owner (by name and function), an action and a date, tracked in the next month's pack. Items with "no action required" should say why.

Illustration — full commentary for one line

Employee benefits expense — ₹48.6 crore, up ₹5.9 crore (13.8%) vs Q3 FY 25. Driver: average headcount +6.1% (2,140 to 2,270) adds ₹2.6 crore; average cost per head +7.3% adds ₹3.3 crore — ₹2.5 crore annual increment (8% effective 1 April), ₹0.8 crore higher variable pay accrual reflecting 112% YTD achievement. Root cause: 130 hires for the Pune line commissioned in July; incentive accrual true-up after Q2 achievement exceeded plan. Recurring: headcount and increment recurring; ₹0.4 crore of the incentive true-up relates to H1 (timing). Forecast: FY 26 employee cost forecast ₹193 crore (budget ₹188 crore). Owner/action: HR head to confirm Q4 hiring freeze for non-plant roles by 31 January; FP&A to reflect in rolling forecast.

Check: 2.6 + 3.3 = 5.9; 2.5 + 0.8 = 3.3.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Offsetting drivers — a small net movement can hide large offsetting drivers (price down ₹5 crore, volume up ₹5 crore); comment when gross drivers exceed the threshold even if the net does not.
  • Unexplained residual — if drivers do not reconcile, say so and investigate; do not distribute the residual into drivers.
  • Sensitive explanations — commentary that will be shared with auditors or the board must be factual; speculative causes should be labelled as such.
  • Multiple comparatives — write the primary commentary on the comparative management uses (often budget), and add a short YoY note where different.
  • Late adjustments — re-run the flux after the final adjustment layer and highlight commentary that changed between drafts.

Key takeaways

  • Every explanation covers what moved, why (driver and root cause), recurrence, forecast impact and owner/action.
  • Quantify drivers so they sum to the movement; keep any residual small and visible.
  • Separate one-offs and FX before explaining underlying performance.
  • Classify drivers as recurring, timing or one-off and translate them into the forecast.
  • Commentary must reconcile to the final post-adjustment TB.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rewrite weak commentary [L1]

Rewrite the following using the five-element structure, inventing reasonable root causes but using only the numbers given: "Power and fuel increased by 18% due to higher production and higher rates." Data: power and fuel ₹14.2 crore vs ₹12.0 crore; production 52,000 tonnes vs 48,000 tonnes; units consumed 2.04 crore kWh vs 1.92 crore kWh.

💡 Hint: Decompose into output, efficiency and rate before writing.

Q2. Commentary request template [L2]

Design a commentary request template that the controller sends to business owners at T+4, pre-populated from the flux engine. Specify fields, which are auto-filled and which the business fills, and the review steps before the pack is issued at T+7.

Q3. Timing vs recurring [L2]

Revenue in Q3 is ₹14 crore above budget: ₹6 crore from a customer pulling forward Q4 orders, ₹5 crore from a 3% price increase effective 1 October, ₹3 crore from a new export customer with an annual contract. Write the commentary and the forecast impact for Q4 and FY 26.

Q4. Review a commentary pack [L3]

You review a 40-line commentary pack in which 12 explanations say "in line with business", drivers on 9 lines do not sum to the movement, and 3 numbers disagree with the final TB after an auditor adjustment. Draft your review notes, the remediation, and the process controls you would introduce for future closes.
