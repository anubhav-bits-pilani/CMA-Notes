Subtopic note

One-Offs in Flux Analysis: Exceptional Items, Errors, Reclasses, Policies

Scope: One-offs: exceptional items (Ind AS 1.97, Schedule III), prior-period corrections, reclassifications, policy changes

Why it matters

A flux analysis that mixes recurring performance with one-off items misleads management in both directions: a one-time ₹18 crore insurance claim makes a weak quarter look strong; a restructuring charge makes an improving business look worse. The same items also drive external reporting choices — what is presented as "exceptional items" on the face of the Schedule III P&L, what is restated under Ind AS 8, what is "regrouped" — and analysts and SEBI look closely at whether companies use "exceptional" to flatter underlying earnings. Commentary must therefore identify one-offs explicitly, quantify them, and separate them from operating drivers before the remaining movement is explained.

Core notes

1. Exceptional items — Ind AS 1.97–98 and Schedule III

  • Ind AS 1.97: when items of income or expense are material, the entity discloses their nature and amount separately.
  • Ind AS 1.98 lists circumstances that give rise to separate disclosure: write-downs of inventories to NRV or PPE to recoverable amount and reversals; restructurings and reversals of restructuring provisions; disposals of PPE; disposals of investments; discontinued operations; litigation settlements; other reversals of provisions.
  • Ind AS 1.87: no item may be presented as an extraordinary item, in the statement of profit and loss or in the notes.
  • Schedule III Division II P&L format includes a line "Exceptional items" between "Profit/(loss) before exceptional items and tax" and "Profit/(loss) before tax". Ind AS does not define "exceptional"; the ICAI Guidance Note on Division II indicates it is for items of such size, nature or incidence that separate disclosure is relevant to explain performance — the Ind AS 1.97 test.
  • Policy discipline: define exceptional items in an accounting policy (size threshold and nature), apply symmetrically (gains as well as losses), and do not present recurring items (e.g., annual restructuring) as exceptional.
  • SEBI results format for listed entities also presents exceptional items as a separate line; quarterly results commentary and analyst presentations should use the same classification.

2. Prior-period corrections — Ind AS 8

  • Errors (omissions and misstatements arising from failure to use reliable information) are corrected retrospectively by restating comparatives (Ind AS 8.42), unless impracticable (8.43–8.45). The correction is not included in current-period profit.
  • Disclosures: nature of the error, amount of the correction for each line item and for basic/diluted EPS, amount at the beginning of the earliest period presented (Ind AS 8.49). A third balance sheet may be required (Ind AS 1.40A).
  • Flux implication: compare current actuals with restated comparatives. If the error was corrected in the current period's books (management accounts) rather than retrospectively, separate it as a one-off in the management flux even if statutory FS restate.
  • Distinguish errors from changes in accounting estimates (Ind AS 8.36–38) — recognised prospectively in current and future profit or loss (e.g., revised useful lives, ECL assumptions, warranty rates). Estimate changes are explained as drivers in the current period, not restated.

3. Reclassifications

  • A change in presentation or classification requires comparatives to be reclassified (Ind AS 1.41) with disclosure of nature, amount and reason.
  • Flux implication: a reclassification between lines (e.g., moving freight-in from other expenses to cost of materials) creates offsetting fluxes on two lines with no economic change. The commentary engine should compare against regrouped comparatives (generated by applying the current mapping to the prior-year TB — w4_t5 s07) and list remaining reclassifications as "presentation, nil impact".
  • Month-end presentation reclasses (debit-balance creditors, current maturities — w4_t5 s05) affect BS flux and ratios; note them so they are not explained as operating movements.

4. Accounting policy changes

  • A voluntary change is permitted only if it results in reliable and more relevant information (Ind AS 8.14); applied retrospectively (8.19–8.22) unless transitional provisions of a new standard specify otherwise.
  • New standards or amendments notified by MCA (e.g., amendments to Ind AS 21 on lack of exchangeability, Ind AS 116 sale-and-leaseback amendments) often have specific transition requirements.
  • Flux implication: where comparatives are restated, compare on the restated basis; where transition is prospective or cumulative catch-up (as with Ind AS 115/116 modified approaches historically), quantify the "policy effect" separately so like-for-like trend is visible.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Recurring "exceptionals": restructuring charges every year are part of the business; persistent use invites regulatory and analyst challenge.
  • Asymmetry: classifying impairment losses as exceptional but reversals or gains within other income distorts the trend — apply the policy to both.
  • Error vs estimate: an ECL model change because new information became available is an estimate change; an ECL computed with a formula error is an error. The distinction determines whether comparatives restate.
  • Materiality of errors: immaterial prior-period errors may be corrected in the current period (Ind AS 8 applies to material errors); disclose in commentary as a one-off.
  • Tax effect: exceptional items have tax consequences (capital gains on land at a different rate); present tax on exceptional items consistently when computing adjusted PAT.
  • Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105) are presented separately below profit from continuing operations — not as exceptional items — and comparatives are re-presented.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 1.97 requires separate disclosure of material items; Schedule III Division II provides an "Exceptional items" line; extraordinary items are prohibited (Ind AS 1.87).
  • Correct prior-period errors retrospectively (Ind AS 8.42) and compare flux against restated comparatives; estimate changes are prospective drivers.
  • Compare against regrouped comparatives so reclassifications do not appear as performance.
  • Quantify policy-change effects separately to preserve like-for-like trends.
  • Maintain a one-off register used consistently across internal flux, results and normalised EBITDA.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the items [L1]

Classify each as exceptional item, Ind AS 8 error, estimate change, reclassification or policy change, and state the effect on comparatives: (a) ₹12 crore inventory write-down after a fire; (b) revision of warranty provision rate from 1.5% to 2.0% of sales; (c) discovery that FY 25 depreciation was understated by ₹4 crore due to a wrong useful life entered in the asset register; (d) moving packing material from other expenses to cost of materials consumed; (e) ₹6 crore litigation settlement received.

Q2. Flux with a restatement [L2]

Q4 FY 26 revenue is ₹420 crore. Q4 FY 25 revenue was reported at ₹392 crore but is restated to ₹385 crore for a cut-off error. Q4 FY 26 also includes ₹9 crore from a one-time licence sale classified as revenue. Compute reported growth, restated growth and underlying growth excluding the one-off, and draft the commentary.

💡 Hint: Decide which comparative and which current figure each growth rate uses before computing.

Q3. Exceptional items policy [L3]

Draft an accounting policy and a board-approved internal guideline for classifying exceptional items in a listed manufacturing company, including a size threshold, nature criteria, symmetry, tax presentation and consistency with results press releases. Critically assess whether the company's three-year history of "restructuring costs" (₹14 crore, ₹11 crore, ₹16 crore) qualifies.

Q4. Policy change and like-for-like trend [L2]

A company changes its cost formula for raw-material inventories from weighted average cost to FIFO (both permitted by Ind AS 2) on the grounds that FIFO better reflects its physical flow. Under the old formula FY 25 closing inventory was ₹84 crore; under FIFO it would have been ₹88.5 crore (opening FY 25: ₹79 crore vs ₹81 crore). Explain the effect on cost of materials consumed, gross margin and EBITDA for FY 25 and FY 26, how comparatives are treated under Ind AS 8, and how the flux commentary should present the change.
