Subtopic note

Driver Decomposition: PVM, Rate × Volume, Headcount, FX

Scope: Driver decomposition: price-volume-mix, rate × volume costs, headcount × cost per head, FX translation vs transactional

Why it matters

"Revenue up 12%" is a restatement; "revenue up ₹14.2 crore — ₹9.6 crore volume, ₹5.8 crore price, offset by ₹1.2 crore adverse mix" is an explanation that management can act on and FP&A can forecast. Driver decomposition breaks a movement into the operational quantities that caused it, so that commentary identifies whether the business sold more, charged more, sold a different mix, paid more per unit, hired more people, or simply translated foreign results at a different exchange rate. It is the analytical core of variance analysis in FP&A and of the "quantified, driver-based" commentary standard (c04).

Core notes

1. Price-volume-mix (PVM) for revenue

Notation for product \(i\): \(Q_{i}\) quantity, \(P_{i}\) price; subscripts 0 = comparative (prior period or budget), 1 = actual.

Two-way (price and volume), c02 convention:

\[ \text{Volume effect} = \sum_{i} (Q_{1,i} - Q_{0,i}) \times P_{0,i}, \qquad \text{Price effect} = \sum_{i} (P_{1,i} - P_{0,i}) \times Q_{1,i} \]

Volume at prior price, price at actual volume; together they reconcile exactly to the total change.

Splitting volume into pure volume and mix: let \(Q_{1}^{T} = \sum_{i} Q_{1,i}\), \(Q_{0}^{T} = \sum_{i} Q_{0,i}\) and \(s_{0,i} = Q_{0,i} / Q_{0}^{T}\) the comparative mix share.

\[ \text{Pure volume} = \sum_{i} (Q_{1}^{T} \times s_{0,i} - Q_{0,i}) \times P_{0,i}, \qquad \text{Mix} = \sum_{i} (Q_{1,i} - Q_{1}^{T} \times s_{0,i}) \times P_{0,i} \]

Pure volume = effect of selling more units at the old mix; mix = effect of the actual mix differing from the old mix, valued at old prices. Pure volume + mix = total volume effect.

Illustration.

Product \(Q_{0}\) (tonnes) \(P_{0}\) ₹/t \(Q_{1}\) \(P_{1}\)
Premium 2,000 60,000 2,100 63,000
Standard 6,000 40,000 7,400 41,000

Revenue: prior ₹36.00 crore; actual ₹43.57 crore; change ₹7.57 crore.

  • Volume effect: Premium 100 × 60,000 = ₹0.60 crore; Standard 1,400 × 40,000 = ₹5.60 crore; total ₹6.20 crore.
  • Price effect: Premium 3,000 × 2,100 = ₹0.63 crore; Standard 1,000 × 7,400 = ₹0.74 crore; total ₹1.37 crore.
  • Check: 6.20 + 1.37 = 7.57.
  • Mix split: \(Q_{1}^{T}\) = 9,500; prior mix 25%:75%. Pure volume = (2,375 − 2,000) × 60,000 + (7,125 − 6,000) × 40,000 = ₹2.25 crore + ₹4.50 crore = ₹6.75 crore. Mix = (2,100 − 2,375) × 60,000 + (7,400 − 7,125) × 40,000 = −₹1.65 crore + ₹1.10 crore = −₹0.55 crore. Check: 6.75 − 0.55 = 6.20.

Commentary: "Revenue up ₹7.57 crore: volume +₹6.75 crore (total tonnage +18.75%), price +₹1.37 crore (Premium +5%, Standard +2.5%), mix −₹0.55 crore (Premium share fell from 25% to 22%)."

2. Rate × volume for costs

The same logic applies to variable costs: \(\text{Cost} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Volume}\) — power (₹/kWh × kWh), freight (₹/tonne-km × tonne-km), raw material (₹/kg × kg consumed), commission (% × eligible sales).

\[ \text{Volume effect} = (V_{1} - V_{0}) \times R_{0}, \qquad \text{Rate effect} = (R_{1} - R_{0}) \times V_{1} \]

Where consumption per unit of output matters (efficiency), add a usage layer: Volume of input = Output × Usage per unit, giving output effect, efficiency effect and rate effect — the standard-costing variances familiar from CMA studies.

3. Headcount × cost per head

Employee costs decompose into:

\[ \text{Cost} = \overline{H} \times \overline{c} \]

with \(\overline{H}\) average headcount (or FTE) and \(\overline{c}\) average cost per head for the period. Headcount effect \(= (\overline{H}_{1} - \overline{H}_{0}) \times \overline{c}_{0}\); cost-per-head effect \(= (\overline{c}_{1} - \overline{c}_{0}) \times \overline{H}_{1}\). Cost per head can be split further into annual increments (timing — April in most Indian companies), promotions, mix of grades/locations, variable pay accrual changes (w4_t3 s04), and one-offs (actuarial changes on gratuity/leave are in OCI or P&L depending on the plan type; retention bonuses).

4. FX: translation vs transactional

  • Transactional FX — exchange differences on foreign-currency monetary items in an entity's own books (Ind AS 21.28), and the effect of exchange rates on foreign-currency revenues/costs of an INR-functional entity (an exporter's USD revenue converted at a higher rate). These affect reported operating results and are partly operational (pricing currency choices, hedging).
  • Translation FX — the effect of translating a foreign operation's results and net assets into the presentation currency (Ind AS 21.39): income and expenses at actual or average rates, assets and liabilities at closing rate, differences to OCI (FCTR). In consolidated results, a stronger USD increases the INR value of a US subsidiary's revenue and costs without any change in underlying performance.
  • Constant-currency analysis — restate current-period foreign-currency results at the comparative period's rates:

\[ \text{Translation effect} = \text{Actual}_{\text{INR}} - \text{Actual}_{\text{FC}} \times \text{Rate}_{0} \]

The remaining change is the constant-currency (operational) movement. Commentary must keep these separate (topic pitfall).

Illustration. A US subsidiary's revenue: prior year USD 20 million at average ₹82.0 = ₹164.0 crore; current year USD 21 million at average ₹84.5 = ₹177.45 crore. Change ₹13.45 crore: constant-currency growth = (21 − 20) × 82.0 = ₹8.20 crore (+5%); translation effect = 21 × (84.5 − 82.0) = ₹5.25 crore.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Order of decomposition matters: price at actual volume vs at prior volume changes the split (the joint price × volume term goes to price under the c02 convention). Choose one convention and keep it.
  • Mix at what level? Mix by product, customer, channel or geography gives different stories; pick the dimension management controls.
  • New and discontinued products have no prior price or volume — treat them as separate "new product" and "discontinued" effects rather than forcing them into price.
  • Discounts and rebates (Ind AS 115 variable consideration) belong in net price; separate list-price and discount effects if commercial teams manage them separately.
  • Hedging: if cash flow hedges are used, the reclassified hedge gain/loss in revenue is part of transactional FX and should be shown separately.
  • Headcount averages — use monthly averages, not opening/closing, when hiring is lumpy.

Key takeaways

  • Volume at prior price + price at actual volume reconciles exactly to the total change; split volume further into pure volume and mix.
  • Apply rate × volume (and usage) decomposition to variable costs, and headcount × cost per head to employee costs.
  • Separate translation FX (consolidating foreign operations) from transactional FX and from constant-currency operating growth.
  • Fix the decomposition convention and dimension, and apply it consistently across months.
  • Decomposition outputs become the numbers quoted in commentary.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-way and three-way PVM [L2]

A cement company sells OPC and PPC. Prior quarter: OPC 1.2 lakh tonnes at ₹5,400/t, PPC 2.8 lakh tonnes at ₹5,100/t. Current quarter: OPC 1.1 lakh tonnes at ₹5,550/t, PPC 3.3 lakh tonnes at ₹5,050/t. Compute the volume, price, pure volume and mix effects and draft a one-sentence quantified commentary.

💡 Hint: Compute the prior mix shares from total prior tonnage first.

Q2. Power cost decomposition [L2]

Power cost was ₹8.4 crore (1.2 crore kWh) last year for output of 60,000 tonnes and ₹10.1 crore (1.32 crore kWh) this year for output of 64,000 tonnes. Decompose the change into output, efficiency (kWh per tonne) and rate effects.

Q3. Employee cost bridge [L2]

Average headcount rose from 1,840 to 1,955 and employee benefits expense from ₹184.0 crore to ₹211.1 crore. The annual increment of 9% was effective 1 April for both years; the current year includes a one-off retention bonus of ₹3.2 crore. Build the bridge into headcount, cost-per-head and one-off effects, and state what further split of cost per head you would request.

Q4. Constant currency [L3]

A consolidated group's European subsidiary reports revenue of EUR 48 million (PY EUR 45 million) and EBITDA EUR 6.0 million (PY EUR 6.3 million). Average EUR/INR was 89.0 (PY 91.5). Compute reported, constant-currency and translation effects on both revenue and EBITDA in ₹ crore, and draft commentary for the board pack that does not blend translation with performance.
