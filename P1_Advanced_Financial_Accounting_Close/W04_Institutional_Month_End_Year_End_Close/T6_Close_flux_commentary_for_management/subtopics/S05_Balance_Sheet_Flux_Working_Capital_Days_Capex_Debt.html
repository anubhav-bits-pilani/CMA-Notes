Subtopic note

Balance Sheet Flux: Working Capital Days, Capex, Debt, Provisions

Scope: BS flux: DSO/DIO/DPO, capex vs CWIP, debt and covenants, provision movements

Why it matters

P&L flux tells management how the business performed; balance sheet flux tells them whether that performance turned into cash and whether the balance sheet is getting riskier. A 12% revenue increase with a 30% receivables increase is a collection problem; a large CWIP balance with little capitalisation is either a delayed project or a depreciation understatement; a rising net debt with a covenant headroom of 0.1× is a refinancing risk; a falling warranty provision while claims rise is an earnings-management red flag. Lenders, rating agencies and auditors read BS movements this way, so management commentary must anticipate them.

Core notes

1. Working capital days — DSO, DIO, DPO

(c03 for formulas.)

\[ \text{DSO} = \frac{\text{Trade receivables}}{\text{Revenue}} \times \text{Days}, \quad \text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times \text{Days}, \quad \text{DPO} = \frac{\text{Trade payables}}{\text{COGS or purchases}} \times \text{Days} \]

\[ \text{Cash conversion cycle} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} \]

Explaining a receivables movement by days separates the activity effect (higher sales → higher receivables at constant DSO) from the days effect (slower collection):

\[ \Delta \text{TR} = \underbrace{\left(\text{Rev}_{1} - \text{Rev}_{0}\right) \times \frac{\text{DSO}_{0}}{\text{Days}}}_{\text{activity}} + \underbrace{\left(\text{DSO}_{1} - \text{DSO}_{0}\right) \times \frac{\text{Rev}_{1}}{\text{Days}}}_{\text{days}} \]

Illustration. Quarterly revenue ₹300 crore (PY quarter ₹270 crore); receivables ₹230 crore (PY ₹180 crore); 90-day quarter.

  • DSO: PY 180 ÷ 270 × 90 = 60.0 days; CY 230 ÷ 300 × 90 = 69.0 days.
  • Activity effect: 30 × 60 ÷ 90 = ₹20 crore. Days effect: 9 × 300 ÷ 90 = ₹30 crore. Total ₹50 crore.
  • Commentary: "Receivables up ₹50 crore: ₹20 crore from higher sales, ₹30 crore from DSO deterioration of 9 days, driven by two OEM customers moving to 75-day terms in October and ₹12 crore overdue from a distributor in Bihar (ECL stage review in progress)."

Consistency rules: use the same revenue basis (gross including GST in receivables vs revenue excluding GST — either gross up revenue or note the bias), annualise or use period days consistently, and use averages or closing consistently.

2. Capex vs CWIP

  • Capex (additions to PPE + CWIP + intangibles) explains investing cash flows; CWIP movement = additions − capitalisation.
  • Commentary links to the approved capex plan: project-wise spend vs budget, capitalisation vs planned commissioning dates.
  • Red flags: CWIP rising while capitalisation lags (delayed commissioning; also risk that assets available for use are not being depreciated — Ind AS 16.55 depreciation begins when the asset is available for use); costs capitalised after the asset is ready (Ind AS 16.20 stops capitalisation); borrowing costs capitalised on suspended projects (Ind AS 23.20 suspends capitalisation during extended periods of inactivity).
  • Schedule III CWIP ageing and completion schedule for overdue/over-budget projects (w4_t5 s07) must be consistent with the commentary.

3. Debt and covenants

  • Net debt bridge: opening net debt + capex + working capital increase + dividends + interest − operating cash flow ± other = closing net debt. This is the BS-flux equivalent of an EBITDA bridge.
  • Covenant monitoring: compute covenants on the lender's definitions (often based on standalone or consolidated audited FS, with specific EBITDA definitions) — net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, interest coverage, TOL/TNW, current ratio. Report headroom (actual vs covenant) and forecast headroom for the next test date.
  • Classification impact: a covenant breach at the reporting date can make long-term debt current unless the lender agrees before FS approval not to demand payment (Ind AS 1.74 carve-out — w4_t5 s05).
  • Rate and mix: finance cost flux decomposed into average debt (volume) and average rate (rate × volume, s02), plus lease interest and unwinding of discount on provisions.

4. Provision movements

Explain provisions with a roll-forward (as required by Ind AS 37.84): opening + additions − utilisation − unused amounts reversed + unwinding of discount ± FX = closing.

Provision Driver of additions What to watch
Warranty Sales volume × claim rate × cost per claim Rate changes are estimate changes (Ind AS 8); compare claims experience
Litigation New cases, reassessment of probability Reversals presented with care (Ind AS 1.98(g)); contingent liability disclosures
Employee benefits (gratuity, leave) Service cost, interest, actuarial (OCI for gratuity remeasurements) Discount rate and salary escalation assumptions
Onerous contracts, restructuring Specific events Recognition criteria (Ind AS 37.66, 37.72)
ECL allowance (not an Ind AS 37 provision) Receivables ageing, forward-looking factors Stage movements; write-offs vs allowance

A provision falling while its driver rises is a red flag for earnings management ("cookie jar" release) and must be explained with evidence.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Year-end window dressing — factoring/bill discounting near year-end lowers receivables and DSO; disclose and analyse with/without factoring (and check derecognition under Ind AS 109).
  • Supplier finance arrangements extend DPO; amendments to Ind AS 7 and Ind AS 107 on supplier finance disclosures (notified in India for periods from 1 April 2025 — confirm) require disclosure of terms and amounts.
  • Advances to suppliers reduce payables if netted — use gross payables (w4_t5 s05) for DPO.
  • GST and TDS in receivables inflate DSO relative to net revenue; be consistent.
  • Capitalised interest and FX on CWIP can make CWIP rise without physical progress.
  • Seasonality — compare working capital with the same month last year, not only the prior month.

Key takeaways

  • Explain receivables, inventory and payables movements as activity effect plus days effect, using DSO/DIO/DPO.
  • Link capex and CWIP to project plans; watch capitalisation lags, depreciation start dates and suspended borrowing-cost capitalisation.
  • Use a net debt bridge and covenant headroom (current and forecast) for debt commentary; remember classification consequences.
  • Explain provisions with an Ind AS 37 roll-forward and challenge releases that contradict driver trends.
  • Adjust for factoring, supplier finance, netting and GST effects before concluding on working capital.

Practice Questions

Q1. Inventory activity vs days [L2]

Quarterly COGS ₹420 crore (PY ₹380 crore); inventory ₹210 crore (PY ₹160 crore); 90-day quarter. Compute DIO for both periods and decompose the inventory movement into activity and days effects. Draft the commentary assuming ₹25 crore of the increase is pre-buying of imported resin ahead of an announced price increase.

💡 Hint: Use COGS as the activity measure and the prior DIO for the activity effect.

Q2. CWIP challenge [L2]

CWIP rose from ₹86 crore to ₹142 crore during the year; capitalisation was ₹12 crore against ₹70 crore planned. The new line has been producing saleable output since December. Identify the accounting issues, quantify what you need to determine, and draft the questions you would put to the project team.

Q3. Covenant headroom commentary [L3]

Covenants: net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5×, DSCR ≥ 1.3×. At 31 December: net debt ₹640 crore; LTM EBITDA ₹270 crore; LTM DSCR 1.42×. Forecast for 31 March: net debt ₹700 crore, LTM EBITDA ₹262 crore, DSCR 1.31×. Prepare the debt and covenant section of the board pack, including headroom, risks, mitigating actions and the accounting implications of a potential breach at year-end.

Q4. Provision roll-forward review [L2]

The warranty provision moved from ₹18.0 crore to ₹14.5 crore. Additions ₹6.0 crore, utilisation ₹7.1 crore, reversals ₹2.4 crore. Sales units rose 12% and claims per 1,000 units rose from 8 to 9. Evaluate the movement and draft the commentary or review challenge you would raise.
