Key concept

Working Capital Days: DSO, DIO and DPO

Concept: DSO = Trade receivables ÷ Revenue × Days; DIO = Inventory ÷ COGS × Days; DPO = Trade payables ÷ COGS (or purchases) × Days

Statement

\[ \text{DSO} = \frac{\text{TR}}{\text{Rev}} \times D, \qquad \text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Inv}}{\text{COGS}} \times D, \qquad \text{DPO} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{COGS or Purchases}} \times D \]

\[ \text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} \]

where \(\text{TR}\) is trade receivables (closing or average, gross or net of ECL — define consistently), \(\text{Rev}\) revenue for the period, \(\text{Inv}\) inventories, \(\text{COGS}\) cost of goods sold (cost of materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + changes in inventories, plus manufacturing costs where included), \(\text{TP}\) trade payables, \(D\) days in the period (365 for a year, 90/91/92 for a quarter, or 30/31 for a month) and \(\text{CCC}\) the cash conversion cycle.

Explanation

Working-capital days translate balances into time, making movements comparable across periods with different activity levels. They are central to BS flux (s05), to the Schedule III ratio note (turnover ratios are the inverse: e.g., receivables turnover = revenue ÷ average receivables, so DSO = D ÷ turnover) and to FP&A cash forecasting (forecast receivables = forecast revenue × DSO ÷ D).

Definition choices that must be fixed and disclosed:

  • Closing vs average balance: closing balances react faster (useful for monthly management); averages smooth volatility (used in Schedule III ratios per the ICAI Guidance Note).
  • Revenue gross or net of GST: receivables include GST while revenue excludes it, inflating DSO by up to the GST rate; either gross up revenue or accept the bias consistently.
  • DPO denominator: purchases is conceptually better (payables arise from purchases, including capex creditors if not separated), COGS is more commonly available; use purchases when inventory is volatile. Exclude capital creditors from trade payables (Schedule III classifies them as other financial liabilities).
  • Period days vs annualised: a quarterly DSO using quarterly revenue × 90 days is consistent; mixing quarterly balances with annual revenue is not.
  • Seasonality: compare to the same period last year.

DPO increases are not automatically good — stretching MSME suppliers beyond 45 days breaches the MSMED Act and makes the expense non-deductible until paid under Sec 43B(h) (w4_t2 related item).

Illustration

FY 2025-26 (₹ crore; 365 days; closing balances):

Item FY 26 FY 25
Revenue 1,460 1,280
COGS 1,022 870
Trade receivables 260 192
Inventories 196 158
Trade payables 154 143
  • DSO: 260 ÷ 1,460 × 365 = 65.0 days (FY 25: 192 ÷ 1,280 × 365 = 54.8).
  • DIO: 196 ÷ 1,022 × 365 = 70.0 days (FY 25: 66.3).
  • DPO: 154 ÷ 1,022 × 365 = 55.0 days (FY 25: 60.0).
  • CCC: 80.0 days vs 61.1 days — a 19-day deterioration; at FY 26 activity, each day of CCC is roughly ₹4 crore of revenue-equivalent cash (1,460 ÷ 365).

Commentary would quantify: "Working capital absorbed about ₹65 crore more than activity growth alone would imply (FY 25 days applied to FY 26 activity): ₹41 crore from 10 additional days of DSO as two government customers delayed payments, ₹14 crore from 5 fewer days of DPO and ₹10 crore from higher inventory days."

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the days [L1]

Quarter revenue ₹540 crore; COGS ₹390 crore; purchases ₹410 crore; closing trade receivables ₹310 crore; inventories ₹220 crore; trade payables ₹180 crore (including ₹25 crore capital creditors); 91-day quarter. Compute DSO, DIO, DPO (on purchases, excluding capital creditors) and CCC.

💡 Hint: Remove capital creditors from trade payables first.

Q2. GST bias [L2]

A company's receivables include 18% GST. Explain how this affects DSO, recompute DSO for receivables of ₹118 crore and revenue (excluding GST) of ₹600 crore for a 365-day year on both bases, and recommend which basis to use in management reporting.

Q3. DPO stretch and MSME [L2]

Procurement proposes increasing DPO from 52 to 70 days across all suppliers to release cash. MSME suppliers account for 30% of purchases. Evaluate the proposal's cash effect (purchases ₹1,200 crore per year), the legal and tax consequences, and how you would present the trade-off in the flux pack.
