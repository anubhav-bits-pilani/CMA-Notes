Key concept

Quantified, Driver-Based Commentary vs Descriptive Restatement

Concept: Quantified, driver-based wording ("₹3.2 Cr up: ₹2.1 Cr volume, ₹1.1 Cr price"), not descriptive restatement

Statement

A flux explanation is driver-based and quantified when it satisfies:

\[ \Delta = \sum_{k=1}^{K} E_{k} + R, \qquad \lvert R \rvert \le \varepsilon \]

where \(\Delta\) is the movement being explained, \(E_{k}\) the quantified effect of driver \(k\) (volume, price, mix, rate, headcount, FX, one-off, timing), \(R\) the unexplained residual and \(\varepsilon\) a small tolerance (e.g., 10% of \(\Delta\) or below the flux threshold). Each \(E_{k}\) is expressed in ₹ and linked to an operational quantity and a root cause. A descriptive restatement merely repeats the movement in words ("revenue increased 12% due to higher sales") and fails the test because \(K = 0\) and \(R = \Delta\).

Explanation

Descriptive commentary is common because it is fast and safe; it is also useless. It tells the reader nothing they cannot see from the numbers, cannot be checked, and cannot be rolled into a forecast. Driver-based wording does three things:

  1. Explains — splits the movement into causes that management controls or can monitor.
  2. Reconciles — the drivers add up to the movement, so the reviewer can test the arithmetic against the final TB and the decomposition model.
  3. Predicts — each driver can be classified as recurring, timing or one-off and projected (s04).

Wording rules:

  • Open with the line, the amount, the change and the comparative: "Revenue ₹148.2 crore, up ₹3.2 crore (2.2%) vs budget".
  • Follow with drivers in descending order of size, each with ₹ and an operational metric: "₹2.1 crore volume (+3.4% tonnes), ₹1.1 crore price (+1.6% realisation)".
  • Give the root cause in one clause per driver.
  • Avoid weasel words: "mainly", "largely", "various", "in line with business", "due to higher expenses".
  • If a residual remains, state it: "₹0.2 crore unexplained, under review".
  • Use the same units and rounding as the pack, and numbers that agree with the final TB.

Illustration

Weak (descriptive) Strong (driver-based, quantified)
"Revenue up 12% due to higher sales." "Revenue ₹298.6 crore, up ₹32.0 crore (12.0%) YoY: volume +₹21.4 crore (+8.0% units, new Tamil Nadu distributors), price +₹13.1 crore (+4.6%, April list-price increase), mix −₹2.5 crore (lower share of premium SKUs after competitor launch)."
"Employee costs increased on account of increments." "Employee benefits ₹41.8 crore, up ₹4.6 crore (12.4%): increments 9% from 1 April +₹3.3 crore; headcount +2.8% (+₹1.0 crore, new R&D centre); gratuity actuarial charge +₹0.3 crore (higher salary escalation assumption)."
"Finance costs higher due to more borrowings." "Finance costs ₹9.6 crore, up ₹2.7 crore: average debt +₹145 crore (capex loan drawn in July) adds ₹2.3 crore at 8.9%; rate +35 bps on the floating loan adds ₹0.4 crore."

Each strong version reconciles: 21.4 + 13.1 − 2.5 = 32.0; 3.3 + 1.0 + 0.3 = 4.6; 2.3 + 0.4 = 2.7.

Connections

Applies the s02 decomposition and c02 volume/price split; the s04 commentary structure; the s06 external narratives (MD&A and press releases reuse the same drivers). It is the Week 4 checklist standard ("quantified, driver-based commentary for > 10% movements tied to the final TB") and addresses the topic pitfall of restating numbers without drivers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rewrite three lines [L1]

Rewrite as quantified, driver-based commentary: (a) "Freight increased due to higher volumes and diesel prices" — freight ₹12.4 crore vs ₹10.6 crore; tonne-km up 9%; (b) "Other income up on treasury gains" — other income ₹6.8 crore vs ₹4.1 crore; average surplus funds ₹420 crore vs ₹350 crore; yield 6.5% vs 4.7% (both annualised; figures are for one quarter); (c) "Rent decreased" — rent ₹2.2 crore vs ₹3.0 crore; two leases renewed for five years and accounted for under Ind AS 116.

💡 Hint: For (c), think about where the cost moved rather than whether it fell.

Q2. Residual tolerance [L2]

Your decomposition of a ₹14.0 crore increase in raw-material cost gives: volume ₹8.2 crore, price ₹4.1 crore, mix ₹0.6 crore. Evaluate whether the commentary meets the driver-based standard, what you would do about the residual, and how you would word it if the residual cannot be resolved before the pack is issued.

Q3. Interview question [L2]

"Give me an example of a flux explanation you rewrote to make it more useful for management." Prepare a structured answer with a before-and-after example, the analysis you performed and the decision it enabled.
