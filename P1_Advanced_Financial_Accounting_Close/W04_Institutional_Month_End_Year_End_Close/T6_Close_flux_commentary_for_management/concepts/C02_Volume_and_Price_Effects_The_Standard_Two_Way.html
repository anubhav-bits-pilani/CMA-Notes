Key concept

Volume and Price Effects: The Standard Two-Way Split

Concept: Volume effect = (Actual volume − Prior volume) × Prior price; Price effect = (Actual price − Prior price) × Actual volume

Statement

For a single product:

\[ \text{Volume effect} = (Q_{1} - Q_{0}) \times P_{0} \]

\[ \text{Price effect} = (P_{1} - P_{0}) \times Q_{1} \]

\[ \text{Volume effect} + \text{Price effect} = Q_{1}P_{1} - Q_{0}P_{0} = \Delta \text{Revenue} \]

where \(Q_{0}, P_{0}\) are prior (or budget) volume and price, \(Q_{1}, P_{1}\) actual volume and price. For multiple products, sum each effect across products; to isolate mix, split the volume effect into pure volume and mix (s02).

Explanation

The decomposition answers "how much of the change came from selling more versus charging more". The convention matters because of the joint term \((Q_{1} - Q_{0})(P_{1} - P_{0})\): valuing volume at prior price and price at actual volume puts the joint term in the price effect. This is the standard used in cost-accounting variance analysis (sales price variance on actual quantity; sales volume variance at standard/budget price) familiar from CMA studies, and it reconciles exactly with no residual.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Unit of volume must be meaningful and homogeneous (tonnes, units, subscribers, room nights). For heterogeneous portfolios, compute per product/SKU group and sum, or volume becomes contaminated by mix.
  • Price is net realisation per unit (after discounts, rebates, Ind AS 115 variable consideration), excluding GST.
  • New products (no \(Q_{0}\)) — show as a separate "new product" effect; discontinued products (no \(Q_{1}\)) — separate "discontinued" effect.
  • Costs use the same logic as rate × volume (s02): volume at prior rate, rate at actual volume.
  • Budget comparisons use budget price and volume as the "0" values — variance language: favourable/adverse.
  • FX: for export revenue, price can be split further into price in foreign currency and the exchange-rate effect.

Illustration

A two-wheeler dealer network (single model for simplicity):

Prior quarter Current quarter
Units 18,000 19,800
Net realisation per unit (₹) 82,000 84,500
Revenue (₹ crore) 147.60 167.31
  • Volume effect = (19,800 − 18,000) × 82,000 = ₹14.76 crore.
  • Price effect = (84,500 − 82,000) × 19,800 = ₹4.95 crore.
  • Total = ₹19.71 crore = 167.31 − 147.60. ✓

Commentary: "Revenue ₹167.3 crore, up ₹19.7 crore (13.4%): volume +10% (+₹14.8 crore) from the festive season and two new dealerships; realisation +3.0% (+₹5.0 crore) from the October price increase."

Connections

Core formula behind s02 driver decomposition and c04 quantified commentary; same structure as standard-costing variances; used in FP&A budget-vs-actual bridges and waterfall charts (topic related items). For BS, the analogous split is activity vs days in s05.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-way split [L1]

A telecom tower company had 24,500 tenancies at an average monthly rental of ₹38,000 last year and 25,900 tenancies at ₹37,200 this year (monthly figures). Compute the monthly volume and price effects and draft the one-sentence commentary.

Q2. Convention sensitivity [L2]

Using Q1's data, recompute the split with volume valued at actual price and price at prior volume. Explain the difference, which term moved, and why a consistent convention matters for month-to-month commentary.

💡 Hint: Identify the joint term explicitly.

Q3. Export realisation [L2]

An exporter sold 5,000 units at USD 120 when the rate was ₹82.5 last year and 5,400 units at USD 118 at ₹84.0 this year. Decompose the revenue change into volume, USD price and exchange-rate effects, stating the convention you use.
