Key concept

Flux Percentage and the Dual-Test Commentary Flag

Concept: Flux % = (Current − Comparative) ÷ |Comparative|; flag if |Flux %| > 10% AND |Δ| > materiality threshold

Statement

\[ \text{Flux}\% = \frac{C - P}{\lvert P \rvert}, \qquad \Delta = C - P \]

\[ \text{Flag} = \left(\lvert \text{Flux}\% \rvert > 10\%\right) \wedge \left(\lvert \Delta \rvert > M_{f}\right) \]

where \(C\) is the current-period amount, \(P\) the comparative (prior month, same period last year, prior year-end or budget), \(\Delta\) the absolute change and \(M_{f}\) the flux materiality threshold in ₹ (derived from performance materiality). The absolute value in the denominator keeps the sign of Flux% meaningful for credit balances and negative comparatives: a positive Flux% always means the amount moved upward algebraically.

Explanation

  • Why \(\lvert P \rvert\): if a loss of ₹(10) crore becomes ₹(4) crore, \((C - P)/P = 6/(-10) = -60\%\), suggesting deterioration; with \(\lvert P \rvert\) the result is +60%, correctly signalling improvement. For liabilities stored as negatives, the same logic applies — but in commentary, describe the direction in words ("payables increased") to avoid confusion.
  • Zero comparatives: Flux% is undefined; treat the % test as met and rely on the absolute test (new lines, first-time items).
  • Sign crossings (profit to loss, net cash to net debt) — the % is technically computable but misleading; flag on the absolute test and describe qualitatively.
  • AND, not OR: both tests must be met — this filters out large-% moves on small balances and small-% moves on large balances (but add an absolute override, so very large moves on big balances are always explained — s01).
  • 10% is a convention, not a rule: it is the roadmap standard and common practice; adjust by line volatility (e.g., 5% for revenue, 20% for small discretionary costs) only with documented reasoning.

Excel LAMBDA for reuse across the pack:

FLUXFLAG = LAMBDA(curr, comp, pct, matl,
  LET(d, curr - comp,
      p, IF(comp = 0, 1, ABS(d) / ABS(comp)),
      AND(p > pct, ABS(d) > matl)))

Called as =FLUXFLAG([@Curr], [@PY], FluxPct, FluxMatl).
