Key concept

Normalised EBITDA and a Consistent Definition

Concept: Normalised EBITDA = Reported EBITDA ± Exceptional/one-off items, with a consistently disclosed definition

Statement

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{reported}} = \text{PBT} + \text{Finance costs} + \text{D\&A} - \text{Other income (per policy)} \pm \text{Exceptional items (per policy)} \]

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{normalised}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{reported}} + \sum_{j} A_{j} \]

where \(\text{D\&A}\) is depreciation and amortisation expense (including ROU depreciation under Ind AS 116), and \(A_{j}\) are adjustments that remove one-off or non-recurring items still included in reported EBITDA — added back if they are charges, deducted if they are gains. Each \(A_{j}\) must be identified in a one-off register (s03), quantified, and the definition of both measures disclosed and applied consistently across periods.

Explanation

EBITDA is not defined by Ind AS. Division II (as explained in the ICAI Guidance Note) permits EBITDA as a separate line on the face of the P&L if measured consistently; most companies present it in results presentations. Normalised (or adjusted) EBITDA goes further, removing items management considers non-recurring, to show run-rate operating performance — used in FP&A forecasting baselines, covenant calculations (lenders' definitions often specify permitted add-backs), valuation (EV/EBITDA multiples apply to normalised EBITDA) and management incentive plans.

Discipline required:

  • Definition first: state what EBITDA includes (e.g., whether other income, FX gains/losses on operating items, and share of JV profit are included) and what normalisation adjusts.
  • Symmetry: remove one-off gains as well as losses; a definition that adds back only charges is biased.
  • Consistency: the same items are adjusted every period; changes in definition are disclosed with restated comparatives.
  • Reconciliation: present a bridge from the nearest Ind AS subtotal (PBT or profit before exceptional items and tax) to reported and normalised EBITDA.
  • Ind AS 116 effect: since leases moved on balance sheet, EBITDA excludes lease rentals; some companies also show "EBITDA after leases" (pre-Ind AS 116 basis) — label clearly.
  • IFRS 18 direction: IFRS 18 (effective internationally for periods beginning 1 January 2027) requires management-defined performance measures used in public communications to be disclosed in the FS with a reconciliation and explanation. An Ind AS equivalent has not been notified by MCA at the time of writing — monitor ICAI/MCA developments — but adopting MPM-style discipline now is best practice.
  • Not every unusual item is one-off: recurring restructuring, annual inventory write-downs, regular litigation costs are part of the business.

Illustration

FY 2025-26 (₹ crore):

Item Amount
Profit before tax 142.0
Add: finance costs 38.5
Add: depreciation and amortisation 64.0
Less: other income (treasury) (11.5)
Add: exceptional items — VRS cost (net loss presented as exceptional) 9.0
EBITDA (company definition: before other income and exceptional items) 242.0
Add: one-off — inventory write-off from flood at Silvassa warehouse (included in COGS) 6.2
Less: one-off — retrospective price escalation received for prior years (in revenue) (4.8)
Add: one-off — acquisition-related advisory fees (in other expenses) 2.1
Normalised EBITDA 245.5

Disclosure: "Normalised EBITDA excludes items that are non-recurring in nature and not reflective of ongoing operations, identified per the company's one-off policy; adjustments are applied to both gains and losses. A reconciliation to profit before tax is presented above." Note that the flood write-off was not presented as an exceptional item in the FS; if material, consider whether Ind AS 1.97 separate disclosure is required, so that statutory and non-GAAP presentations do not conflict.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute normalised EBITDA [L2]

PBT ₹86.0 crore; finance costs ₹22.4 crore; D&A ₹31.0 crore; other income ₹7.6 crore (all treasury income); exceptional gain on sale of land ₹14.0 crore; within other expenses ₹3.5 crore of IPO-related costs not eligible for adjustment against securities premium; within revenue ₹2.2 crore of one-time export incentive arrears for prior years. Compute EBITDA (excluding other income and exceptional items) and normalised EBITDA, and present the reconciliation.

💡 Hint: Remember the exceptional item is a gain, so its sign in the bridge differs from a charge.

Q2. Definition critique [L3]

A company's investor presentation reports "Adjusted EBITDA" that adds back ESOP costs, all restructuring costs (recurring for four years), FX losses (but not FX gains) and new-store pre-opening costs. Critique the definition against consistency, symmetry and MPM principles, and propose a revised definition and disclosure.

Q3. Lender vs management definitions [L2]

Explain why a lender's covenant EBITDA may differ from management's normalised EBITDA, give three typical differences, and describe how you would present both in the board pack without confusing readers.
