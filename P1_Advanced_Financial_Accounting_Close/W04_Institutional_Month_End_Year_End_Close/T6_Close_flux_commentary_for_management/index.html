Roadmap topic: Close Flux Commentary: Writing management-ready explanations for >10% MoM/YoY balance sheet and P&L movements

Overview

Flux commentary is the narrative layer of the close: for every balance sheet and P&L line whose movement breaches the thresholds, finance explains what moved, why, whether it will recur, what it means for the forecast and who owns the follow-up. It is the step that converts a correct set of numbers into management information. Done well, it lets the CFO walk into a review meeting knowing the three things that changed and why; done badly, it is a spreadsheet of "increase due to higher expenses" that nobody reads and that auditors cannot rely on.

For the target roles, flux commentary is a daily skill. A financial reporting or controllership analyst writes it every month-end and quarter-end, and answers the auditor's limited-review queries with it. An FP&A analyst uses the same decomposition — price-volume-mix, rate × volume, headcount × cost per head, FX translation vs transactional — for budget-vs-actual and forecast bridges. In listed companies, the same explanations flow into SEBI LODR Schedule V MD&A, the Schedule III ratio note and results press releases, and they are scrutinised by audit committees under Sec 177 and Reg 18. Interviewers for FP&A roles in India routinely ask candidates to explain a variance in this structure.

The subtopics follow the workflow. First design thresholds and comparatives (s01) using the dual-test flux flag (c01). Then decompose each flagged movement into drivers (s02), with the volume and price split (c02) as the core formula. Identify and separate one-offs — exceptional items, prior-period corrections, reclassifications and policy changes (s03) — and, where needed, compute normalised EBITDA (c05). Write the explanation using the five-element commentary structure (s04) and the quantified, driver-based wording standard (c04). Extend the analysis to the balance sheet — working-capital days (c03), capex vs CWIP, debt and covenants, provisions (s05). Finally, connect the internal commentary to external narratives (s06) and to governance uses — SA 520 analytics, audit committee packs and limited review queries (s07).

Related concepts to study

Accounting drivers from Weeks 1–3

Weeks 1–3 accounting drivers of movements: contract balances, lease remeasurements, ECL, DT rate changes

Many flux items are caused by accounting mechanics rather than operations, and commentary must recognise them. Ind AS 115 contract balances (w1) move with billing milestones: revenue can rise while receivables fall because unbilled revenue (contract assets) increased. Lease remeasurements and modifications (w2) change ROU assets, lease liabilities, depreciation and interest without any cash change. ECL allowances (w3) move with ageing, stage transfers and forward-looking macro inputs — a provision-matrix update can change impairment expense materially. Deferred tax moves with rate changes (e.g., a move to the Sec 115BAA regime remeasures DTA/DTL) and recognition judgements. Commentary should name the mechanism and quantify it, then explain the operational driver separately.

FP&A variance frameworks

FP&A budget vs actual vs forecast variance frameworks in later FP&A weeks

FP&A analyses every result against three comparatives: budget (the annual commitment), latest forecast (the current expectation) and prior year. Close flux commentary feeds all three: drivers identified at month-end become variance explanations against budget, and their classification as recurring, timing or one-off drives the forecast update. Later FP&A weeks formalise this into budget-vs-actual packs, rolling forecasts and driver-based planning models (volume, price, headcount, rate drivers linked to the P&L). Writing close commentary in the s04 structure now means the same explanations can be reused directly in FP&A variance packs without rework, and differences between accounting and FP&A views are minimised.

Excel bridges, flag tables and LET/LAMBDA

Excel: waterfall/bridge charts, variance tables with flags, LET/LAMBDA for reusable flux formulas

Excel's built-in waterfall chart presents a bridge from comparative to current (e.g., PY EBITDA → volume → price → mix → costs → FX → CY EBITDA); set the first and last bars as totals and order drivers by size. Variance tables use structured tables with columns for each comparative, Δ, Flux% and a flag column; conditional formatting highlights flagged lines and a FILTER produces the commentary list. LET makes long flux formulas readable by naming intermediate values; LAMBDA (saved via Name Manager) turns the dual-threshold flag or a PVM split into a reusable function such as FLUXFLAG(curr, comp, pct, matl), ensuring every tab applies identical logic. Avoid absolute references by using named threshold cells.

IFRS 18 MPMs and non-GAAP reconciliation

IFRS 18 management-defined performance measures and non-GAAP reconciliation → w1_t1

IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements (effective internationally for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027) introduces defined P&L subtotals (operating profit, profit before financing and income taxes) and requires disclosure, in a single note, of management-defined performance measures used in public communications — with a reconciliation to the most comparable IFRS subtotal, the tax and NCI effect of each reconciling item, and an explanation of why the measure is useful. India has not yet notified an Ind AS equivalent (check current ICAI/MCA status), but listed companies already publish EBITDA, adjusted PAT and normalised EBITDA (c05). Applying MPM discipline — clear definitions, symmetry, consistency and reconciliation — to flux commentary and press releases now anticipates the standard (w1_t1).

Common pitfalls

Large % on tiny balances, missing large absolute moves

Explaining large % moves on tiny balances while missing large absolute moves

The mistake: commentary covers "bank charges up 180%" while a ₹25 crore (6%) receivables increase goes unexplained. Why it happens: %-only thresholds, or sorting the flux table by % rather than by ₹. Correct treatment: dual thresholds (c01) with an absolute override at or near performance materiality (s01); sort commentary by absolute movement; explain offsetting gross drivers even when the net is small. Detection: compare the list of commented lines with a list of the ten largest absolute movements; any large movement without commentary is a gap.

Restating numbers without drivers

Restating numbers ("revenue up 12%") without drivers or root cause

The mistake: commentary that repeats the movement in words or gives a vague cause ("higher sales", "in line with business"). Why it happens: time pressure, lack of access to operational data, and fear of being wrong. Correct treatment: decompose (s02) and write quantified drivers that sum to the movement with a root cause for each (c04), using the five-element structure (s04); request root causes from business owners using pre-populated templates. Detection: commentary with no ₹ amounts beyond the headline; drivers that do not add up; repeated phrases across lines and months.

Blending translation FX with performance

Blending translation FX with operating performance

The mistake: reporting consolidated revenue growth of 14% as operational when 6 percentage points came from a stronger USD translating the US subsidiary's results. Why it happens: consolidated numbers are reported in INR and the translation effect is not computed. Correct treatment: compute constant-currency results (current foreign-currency amounts at prior-period rates) and present translation effects separately from transactional FX and from operating drivers (s02); remember translation differences on net assets go to OCI (FCTR) under Ind AS 21 while transactional differences hit P&L. Detection: large INR movements in foreign subsidiaries with flat local-currency results; commentary that never mentions FX in a group with significant foreign operations.

Commentary not reconciled to the final TB

Commentary not reconciled to the final post-adjustment TB

The mistake: commentary written on the T+4 draft numbers is issued at T+7 after auditor adjustments, top-sides and reclassifications changed several lines. Why it happens: commentary is prepared in a separate document with typed numbers. Correct treatment: generate flux tables from the final TB via the mapping engine (w4_t5); link commentary numbers to the flux table; re-run flags after the last adjustment and highlight changed lines; include a version reference. Detection: a tie-out check comparing every number in commentary to the final flux table; differences between pack numbers and the FS; adjustments in the adjustment layer posted after the commentary timestamp.

Practice Questions

Q1. Monthly management pack for a consumer company [L3]

Sahyadri Foods Ltd (NSE-listed; FY ending 31 March) reports for Q3 FY 26 vs Q3 FY 25 (₹ crore): revenue 612.0 vs 548.0; volumes 1.84 lakh tonnes vs 1.72 lakh tonnes (single blended product for simplicity); cost of materials 381.0 vs 334.0; employee benefits 52.4 vs 46.1 (average headcount 3,120 vs 2,980); other expenses 88.6 vs 79.2, including a ₹4.2 crore one-time legal settlement; trade receivables 214.0 vs 168.0; inventories 176.0 vs 149.0; trade payables 132.0 vs 128.0; 92-day quarter. Thresholds: 10% and ₹3 crore, override ₹15 crore. Required: (a) identify lines requiring commentary; (b) decompose revenue into volume and price, and employee costs into headcount and cost per head; (c) compute DSO, DIO and DPO (on cost of materials) and decompose the receivables movement into activity and days effects; (d) write five-element commentary for revenue, employee costs and receivables; (e) compute EBITDA impact of the one-off and explain its presentation.

💡 Hint: Work out the realisation per tonne in each period before splitting revenue.

Q2. Group results with FX and one-offs [L3]

A consolidated group (presentation currency INR) has an Indian parent and a UK subsidiary (GBP functional). UK revenue: GBP 30 million (PY GBP 28 million) at average rates ₹108.0 (PY ₹104.0). Indian revenue: ₹1,120 crore (PY ₹1,040 crore), including ₹18 crore export revenue gain from INR depreciation on USD sales. The group also restated PY revenue down by ₹6 crore for a cut-off error and recognised a ₹22 crore exceptional gain on sale of a warehouse. Required: (a) compute reported, restated and constant-currency revenue growth; (b) separate translation and transactional FX; (c) draft the results press release paragraph and the audit committee commentary; (d) state the Ind AS references for each presentation decision.

Q3. External narrative consistency [L2]

For FY 26, the Schedule III ratio note shows debt-equity 0.82 (PY 0.58), net profit ratio 5.1% (PY 7.2%) and trade receivables turnover 5.4 (PY 7.0). The MD&A draft explains only the net profit margin, and the press release attributes margin decline solely to raw material costs while the internal flux pack shows ₹14 crore of the PAT decline came from finance costs on new borrowings. Required: (a) identify which ratios require explanations under Schedule III and Schedule V; (b) draft consistent explanations; (c) describe the review control that would have caught the inconsistency; (d) explain the auditor's responsibility under SA 720.

Q4. Building a reusable flux workbook [L3]

Specify an Excel flux workbook that takes the final TB from the Project 1 mapping engine and produces: a variance table vs prior month, same month last year and budget with dual-threshold flags (named threshold cells, no absolute cell references); a LAMBDA-based flag; a PVM calculator for three product groups; an EBITDA waterfall chart; a commentary register with five-element fields, owner and status; and a tie-out check to the final TB. Describe tabs, key formulas and controls.

Q5. Interview case: the auditor's query list [L2]

Your limited-review auditor sends 28 queries two days before the board meeting, most asking "reasons for variance" on P&L lines. Explain how you would respond this quarter and what you would change so that next quarter's query list is much shorter, referencing SA 520 and SRE 2410.
