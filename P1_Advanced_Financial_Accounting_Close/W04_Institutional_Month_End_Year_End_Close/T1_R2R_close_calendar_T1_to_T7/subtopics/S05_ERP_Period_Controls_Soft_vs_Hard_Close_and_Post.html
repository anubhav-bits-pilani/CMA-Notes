Subtopic note

ERP Period Controls, Soft vs Hard Close and Post-Close Adjustments

Scope: ERP period controls (e.g., SAP OB52), soft vs hard close, post-close adjustment governance

Why it matters

A close calendar is only as strong as the system controls that enforce it. If any user can post to a closed month, the TB used for the reporting pack can change after review without anyone noticing. ERP period controls make the calendar binding; soft- and hard-close conventions define how much estimation is acceptable each month; and post-close adjustment governance determines what happens when, inevitably, something is found after the lock. Auditors assess these as IT general controls (ITGCs) and as part of management override risk under SA 240; Rule 11(g) audit-trail reporting (s08) adds a statutory angle.

Core notes

SAP OB52 — posting period variants

In SAP ECC and S/4HANA, transaction OB52 maintains open and closed posting periods per posting period variant (usually one variant per company code or group of company codes). Key features:

  • Account type granularity: periods are opened or closed separately for account types: + (valid for all), A (assets), D (customers), K (vendors), M (materials), S (GL accounts), and in some configurations V (contract accounts). Ranges of GL accounts can be given separate windows.
  • Two intervals: each row has Interval 1 and Interval 2 (from period/year to period/year). Interval 1 can be restricted to an authorisation group, so only the close team can post to the prior period while everyone else is limited to Interval 2 (the current period).
  • Special periods: periods 13–16 for year-end audit adjustments, so audit entries are visible separately from period 12 operations.
  • Related controls: MMPV / MMRV for the materials-management period (inventory movements), asset fiscal-year change (AJRW) and year-end close (AJAB), and GL balance carry-forward (FAGLGVTR). Locking OB52 for M without closing the MM period still allows goods movements to the old period if MM is open — both must be aligned.

A typical month-end configuration:

Account type Interval 1 (close team only) Interval 2 (all users) Comment
+ Period 12 (March) to 12 Period 1 (April) to 1 Default
D, K Closed after T+1 April only AR/AP locked first
A, M Closed after T+2 April only After depreciation and costing
S March open until T+6 for authorisation group FIN_CLOSE April GL adjustments only
S (special periods 13–16) Open at year-end for audit entries — Restricted

Oracle E-Business Suite / Fusion uses period statuses (Never Opened, Future-Entry, Open, Closed, Permanently Closed) per ledger and per sub-ledger application (Payables, Receivables, Assets, Inventory). Tally Prime lacks granular period locking by module; controls rely on "Allow back-dated entries" restrictions, user-level security and edit log (which must not be disabled — s08).

Soft close vs hard close

Feature Soft close (non-quarter months) Hard close (quarter-end, year-end)
Purpose Management reporting, MIS, covenant monitoring Statutory results (Reg 33), limited review/audit, tax filings
Estimates Run-rate accruals, standard cost without full variance analysis, provisional IC Actual invoices where available, full variance settlement, confirmed IC
Procedures Key reconciliations only (bank, IC, high-risk accounts), analytical review All balance sheet reconciliations, physical counts (year-end), actuarial and impairment work
Tax Effective-rate estimate Full current and deferred tax computation
External involvement None Auditor limited review (Reg 33(3)(c)) or audit
Period lock Sub-ledgers locked; GL may reopen with lower approval GL locked; reopening only via formal governance

The choice matters for consistency: estimates allowed in a soft close must be trued up in the next hard close, and the true-up is a close KPI (c02). Concept c05 covers the definition in more detail.

Post-close adjustment governance

Post-close adjustments are entries posted after the GL lock or after the reporting pack is issued. They arise from audit findings, late invoices, IC disputes, tax revisions and subsequent events. Governance design:

  1. Classification: (a) adjusting entries required for the current period (material, or affecting reported figures), (b) entries that can be posted in the next period as a current-period correction (immaterial), (c) disclosure-only items (non-adjusting subsequent events under Ind AS 10.21).
  2. Thresholds: below a defined threshold (e.g., clearly trivial — 3–5% of overall materiality) book in the next period; above it, reopen the period with CFO approval (concept c03 discusses alignment with audit materiality).
  3. Reopening procedure: written request with reason, impact on reported numbers, approval by financial controller and CFO, temporary opening of OB52 Interval 1 for a named user and a named account range, posting, re-run of checks and mapping, re-lock, and log update.
  4. Documentation: each adjustment carries a reference (e.g., PCA-2026-03-007), source (auditor, internal), preparer, approver, date and supporting evidence; the adjustment layer of the Month-End Closing Workbook (Topic 5) mirrors this.
  5. Monitoring: count and value of post-close entries by source and by root cause, reported monthly. Rising counts indicate a close that is too fast or controls that are failing upstream.

Prior-period errors discovered after financial statements are issued fall under Ind AS 8 (retrospective restatement where material), which is distinct from post-close adjustments before issue.

Illustration — reopening request

On T+8, the statutory auditor identifies that a ₹2.6 crore customer rebate for Q4 was not accrued. Overall materiality is ₹4.0 crore and the clearly-trivial threshold is ₹20 lakh. The item exceeds the threshold and affects revenue (a KPI line), so the period is reopened: Dr Revenue (variable consideration) ₹2.6 crore, Cr Refund liability ₹2.6 crore, posted in special period 13 with reference PCA-Q4-003, approved by the CFO, and the root cause (rebate scheme not in the accrual template) is logged for the next pre-close.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Firefighter access: emergency super-user IDs (e.g., SAP GRC Firefighter) must have logged sessions reviewed by someone independent of the user.
  • Interfaces after lock: automated feeds (billing, payroll, bank) may attempt to post into a closed period; design them to fail and queue rather than silently post into the next period with the old date.
  • Back-dated posting in the new period: entries posted in April but relating to March are not "post-close" if the period is correctly closed — they are current-period corrections; ensure materiality is assessed cumulatively.
  • Year-end special periods: some groups use period 13 for all audit entries so that period 12 equals the management close; mapping and reporting must include period 13–16 balances.
  • Tally/SME ERPs: where period locking is weak, compensating controls include TB snapshots at lock, comparison of later TB to snapshot and edit-log review.

Key takeaways

  • OB52 controls posting periods by account type with two intervals and authorisation groups; align with MM and asset period controls.
  • Soft closes allow documented estimates for management reporting; hard closes require full procedures and external review.
  • Post-close adjustments need classification, thresholds, a formal reopening procedure and referenced documentation.
  • Monitor post-close entries by count, value and root cause as a close-quality KPI.
  • Where ERPs lack locking, use TB snapshots and edit-log review as compensating controls.

Practice Questions

Q1. Configure OB52 [L1]

Prepare an OB52-style table for company code IN01 on T+3 of the September quarter close where AR and AP are locked, inventory and assets are locked, and GL remains open for the close team only. Identify the account types, intervals and authorisation group settings.

Q2. Soft vs hard close policy [L2]

Draft a one-page policy specifying, for each of the following, the treatment in a soft-close month vs a hard-close quarter: (a) utility accruals, (b) standard cost variances, (c) ECL provision, (d) current tax, (e) IC balances, (f) bonus accrual.

Q3. Post-close adjustment decision [L2]

Overall materiality is ₹6.0 crore; performance materiality ₹4.2 crore; clearly trivial ₹30 lakh. After the lock, the following are identified: (i) unrecorded freight accrual ₹18 lakh, (ii) FX revaluation omitted on a EUR payable — difference ₹74 lakh, (iii) litigation settled on T+9 for ₹3.1 crore relating to a dispute existing at period-end, (iv) fire at a warehouse on T+5 destroying stock of ₹5.4 crore. Classify each and state the action.

💡 Hint: Consider Ind AS 10 adjusting vs non-adjusting events alongside the thresholds.

Q4. Interface posting into a closed period [L3]

A payroll interface failed on T+1 and, when rerun on T+4, posted ₹3.8 crore of March salaries into April because March was closed for type S. Explain the reporting impact, the immediate correction and the system and process changes required.

Q5. Interview question — reopening governance [L3]

"How do you stop people from reopening periods every month?" Answer with specific controls, metrics and escalation mechanisms you would implement as a financial controller.
