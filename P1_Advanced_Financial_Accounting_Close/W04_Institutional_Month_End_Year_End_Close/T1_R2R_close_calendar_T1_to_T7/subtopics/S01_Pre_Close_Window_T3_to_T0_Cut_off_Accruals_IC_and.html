Subtopic note

Pre-Close Window (T−3 to T0): Cut-off, Accruals, IC and Master Data

Scope: Pre-close (T−3 to T0): cut-off communications, accrual requests, provisional IC confirmations, master data freeze

Why it matters

A T+5 or T+7 close is decided before month-end, not after it. Most close overruns trace back to work that could have been done in the last three working days of the period: a business unit that did not know the cut-off time for GRNs, a plant head who submitted accrual inputs on T+3, an overseas subsidiary that disputed an inter-company (IC) recharge on T+4, or a new vendor created on T+1 with the wrong reconciliation account. The pre-close window converts these surprises into scheduled tasks. For an auditor-turned-controller, this is where you design preventive controls; for an FP&A analyst, this is where the flash estimate is born, because the accrual requests and IC confirmations collected here are the earliest reliable view of the month.

Core notes

Defining the window

T0 is the last calendar day of the period (31 March for year-end). Working days before it are T−1, T−2, T−3; working days after it are T+1 onwards. The pre-close window runs T−3 to T0 and has four workstreams that run in parallel, each with an owner and a hard deadline:

Cut-off communications

The cut-off memo is a control document, not a courtesy e-mail. It should state: (i) the last time for dispatches to be invoiced in the period (e.g., 31 March, 23:59, with e-way bill and e-invoice IRN generated); (ii) the last time GRNs can be posted for receipts physically received by T0; (iii) the treatment of goods in transit under each Incoterm (see s04); (iv) the cut-off for expense claims, petty cash and corporate cards; (v) the cut-off for payroll inputs (joiners, leavers, variable pay); and (vi) contacts for exceptions. Keep evidence that it was sent and acknowledged — auditors testing cut-off under SA 500/SA 330 will ask how the entity ensured completeness at the boundary, and the memo is part of your entity-level control evidence for internal financial controls (IFC).

Accrual requests

Accruals for goods or services received but not invoiced are the largest source of close-day noise. The pre-close request goes to every budget owner with a pre-filled list: open POs with GRNs pending invoice, recurring contracts (rent, CAM, AMC, security, housekeeping), utilities, professional fees and variable pay. Owners confirm, amend or add items and attach the basis (contract rate, meter reading, timesheet, statement of work). Requesting at T−3 means the controller has the inputs on T−1 and can post on T+1–T+2 rather than chasing on T+3. Week 4 Topic 3 covers the accrual templates and the auto-reversal mechanics; the pre-close discipline is what feeds them.

A useful completeness test at T−1: compare the accrual list against (a) the prior-month accrual list (anything missing?), (b) the vendor master for recurring vendors with no invoice received this month, and (c) the open PO/GRN report. Any gap goes back to the owner with a deadline.

Provisional IC confirmations

IC mismatches are the commonest reason consolidation slips from T+5 to T+8. A provisional confirmation at T−2 agrees the IC balance as at T−2 by counterparty pair and transaction type (trade, loans, interest, service recharges, cost allocations), plus the expected flows to T0. Both sides then raise or accrue the remaining invoices by T0 using the same document references and the same agreed rate (see Week 4 Topic 4). Major mismatches — a disputed management fee, a recharge not yet approved by the receiving entity's board, a transfer-pricing true-up — must be escalated before T0 so a decision exists before the books close. For Indian groups, provisional IC also flags cross-border items needing FEMA documentation and withholding tax (WHT) computation at month-end.

Master data freeze

A master data freeze stops creation or modification of GL accounts, cost centres, profit centres, vendor/customer masters, material masters and price/valuation records from about T−1 to T+2. Reasons: new GL accounts arriving mid-close break the TB-to-Schedule III mapping (they appear as unmapped lines); vendor master edits (bank account, GSTIN, PAN) during payment runs are a classic fraud vector and a SA 240 risk; standard cost or valuation price changes during inventory costing distort cost of goods sold. The freeze needs an emergency path: a documented request, approval by the financial controller, and an immediate mapping review if a GL account is created.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Quarter-end and year-end: extend the window to T−5 for year-end, add physical inventory counts and fixed asset verification, and align with the auditor's attendance plan.
  • Late accrual inputs: if an owner misses the deadline, the controller should estimate using run-rate and flag it as a controller estimate, rather than leave the expense unrecorded. Record who made the estimate and why.
  • Emergency master data changes: a vendor bank-detail change during the freeze needs independent call-back verification; never accept it on e-mail alone.
  • Provisional IC not agreed by T0: both entities should record their own best estimate and tag the difference (timing, disputed, missing entry) for elimination analysis; do not let one side unilaterally push the difference to suspense.
  • Quarter-ends of listed entities: pre-close communications should remind designated persons of the trading window closure from quarter-end (SEBI PIT Regulations), since the close team has unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI).

Key takeaways

  • The pre-close window (T−3 to T0) removes the commonest causes of close slippage before the period ends.
  • Cut-off memos are control evidence: they define timings for dispatch, GRN, claims and payroll and must be acknowledged.
  • Accrual requests go out pre-filled from POs, contracts and prior-month lists, and are checked for completeness at T−1.
  • Provisional IC confirmations by counterparty pair and transaction type stop consolidation disputes from surfacing at T+4.
  • A master data freeze protects mapping integrity, costing stability and fraud controls, with a documented emergency path.

Practice Questions

Q1. Drafting a cut-off memo [L1]

List the minimum contents of a year-end cut-off communication for a manufacturing company with domestic dispatches (e-invoicing applicable), export shipments on FOB and CIF terms, and a payroll processed on the 28th of each month.

Q2. Accrual completeness test [L2]

At T−1 for March, the prior-month accrual list has 46 items totalling ₹2.84 crore. Inputs received so far: 39 items totalling ₹2.51 crore, plus 5 new items of ₹0.42 crore. The open GRN-not-invoiced report shows ₹0.96 crore, of which ₹0.61 crore is already in the inputs received. Describe the steps you would take before T0 and quantify the gaps you would chase.

💡 Hint: Compare three sources — prior-month list, GRN report and owner inputs — and treat each mismatch separately.

Q3. IC dispute before close [L2]

Indian parent A charges its Dubai subsidiary B a quarterly management fee of USD 2,20,000 under a services agreement. At T−2, B's CFO says the fee for the quarter has not been approved by B's board and will not be booked. Explain how you would handle the provisional confirmation, the entries in each entity at T0 and the information you would hand to the consolidation team.

Q4. Master data freeze breach [L3]

On T+1 a GL account "Sales promotion — new channel" was created by the IT team at the request of the marketing head, and ₹64 lakh was posted to it on T+2. The TB-to-Schedule III mapping does not include the account. Evaluate the control failure, its impact on the close and the financial statements, and propose changes to the freeze procedure.

Q5. Interview question — pre-close design [L3]

An interviewer asks: "Our close is T+10 and most delay is in IC and accruals. What would you change in the three days before month-end?" Structure a 2-minute answer with specific tasks, owners and measurable targets.
