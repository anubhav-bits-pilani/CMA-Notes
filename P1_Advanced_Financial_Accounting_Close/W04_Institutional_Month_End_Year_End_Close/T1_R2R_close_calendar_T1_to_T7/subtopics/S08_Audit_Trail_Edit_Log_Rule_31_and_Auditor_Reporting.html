Audit Trail (Edit Log): Rule 3(1) and Auditor Reporting Under Rule 11(g)

Scope: Audit trail (edit log) under Companies (Accounts) Rules Rule 3(1) and auditor reporting under Rule 11(g)

Why it matters

From financial years beginning on or after 1 April 2023, every Indian company keeping books in accounting software must use software that records an audit trail of every transaction, creates an edit log of each change with its date, and cannot have that feature disabled. Statutory auditors report on compliance in the main audit report. For the close, this means every post-close adjustment, back-dated entry, master data change and reversal is traceable — the edit log is both a control and evidence. Many companies found in their first year that database-level changes, interfaces and legacy modules were not covered, leading to modified Rule 11(g) reporting. A controller must understand the requirement well enough to design the close so that nothing escapes the trail.

Core notes

The requirement — Rule 3(1)

Rule 3(1) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 deals with books of account maintained in electronic mode. The proviso inserted by the Companies (Accounts) Amendment Rules, 2021 (applicable from FY commencing 1 April 2023, after two deferrals) requires that a company using accounting software shall use only software which:

  1. records an audit trail of each and every transaction;
  2. creates an edit log of each change made in books of account, along with the date when such changes were made; and
  3. ensures that the audit trail cannot be disabled.

Related provisions:

  • Sec 128(1) and 128(5): books of account, including in electronic form, must be preserved for not less than eight financial years preceding the current year; audit trail records are preserved in line with these retention requirements.
  • Rule 3(5): back-up of books and papers maintained electronically, including at a place outside India, must be kept on servers physically located in India on a daily basis (as amended in 2022).
  • Rule 3(6): details of the service provider (where books are maintained on a cloud or third-party server) are intimated to the Registrar annually at the time of filing financial statements.

What the audit trail must capture

The rules do not list fields; practice (and the ICAI Implementation Guide on Reporting under Rule 11(g), revised edition) expects at least: what changed (record identifier, field, old value, new value), who changed it (user ID), when (date, and ideally timestamp), and the nature of the change (create, modify, delete). Coverage should include:

  • Transactions: journals, invoices, receipts, payments, GRNs, reversals and deletions.
  • Masters that affect books: GL accounts, vendor/customer masters (including bank details), asset masters, valuation prices — to the extent changes affect records of transactions.
  • Period controls and configuration where they affect postings.
  • Changes made through the front end and directly at the database level (e.g., by IT administrators running scripts), since the requirement covers each change in books; database-level logging is the area most frequently found deficient.

Typical ERP mechanisms

System Mechanism Common gap
SAP ECC/S/4HANA Document change logs (CDHDR/CDPOS), table logging (rec/client parameter and table settings), security audit log (SM19/SM20 or RSAU) Table logging disabled for performance; direct DB updates by BASIS without log

Auditor reporting — Rule 11(g)

Rule 11 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014 lists other matters for the auditor's report under Sec 143(3)(j). Clause (g) requires the auditor to state whether the company, for financial years commencing on or after 1 April 2023, has used accounting software for maintaining books of account which has a feature of recording audit trail (edit log) facility, and whether:

  • the feature has operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions recorded in the software;
  • the audit trail feature has not been tampered with; and
  • the audit trail has been preserved by the company as per the statutory requirements for record retention (preservation was reported from the second year onward, i.e., FY 2024-25, since it relates to retention of the previous year's trail).

The auditor's work includes understanding all software used to maintain books (including payroll, billing and consolidation tools where they constitute books), testing whether the feature was enabled throughout the year, checking for gaps or disabling events, and evaluating database-level controls. Exceptions are reported factually (e.g., "audit trail was not enabled at the database level for direct changes" or "the feature was enabled from 15 July 2025"). The matter also informs the auditor's IFC opinion under Sec 143(3)(i) and the reporting on whether proper books have been kept under Sec 143(3)(b).

Designing the close for audit-trail compliance

  • Post all adjustments in the ERP, including consolidation adjustments where the ERP's group reporting module is used; where top-sides sit in Excel, document them with versioning and approval evidence, and consider whether the spreadsheet functions as part of books of account.
  • Prohibit deletion of posted documents; correct through reversal entries so the trail shows original and correction.
  • Restrict and log firefighter/super-user access; review logs monthly as part of manual JE review (s06).
  • Include audit-trail evidence in the close checklist: monthly confirmation from IT that logging was active for all in-scope systems, with any downtime documented.
  • Retain logs for at least eight years, with daily backups to servers in India.

Illustration

During FY 2025-26, a company's billing system (which posts sales to SAP via interface) had edit logging enabled only from 1 September 2025 after an upgrade. IT also updated 312 invoice dates directly in the database in January 2026 to correct a time-zone error. Consequences: (i) the auditor reports under Rule 11(g) that the audit trail feature did not operate throughout the year for the billing software (April–August) and was not enabled at the database level; (ii) cut-off testing for the year requires alternative evidence for the date corrections; (iii) management's IFC assessment should record a deficiency and its remediation.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Which software is "accounting software": payroll, inventory and billing systems that create records of transactions forming part of books are generally in scope; pure reporting tools may not be — document the scoping judgement.
  • Third-party SaaS: the company remains responsible; obtain service-organisation reports (SOC 1 / SA 402 perspective) covering audit-trail controls.
  • Performance trade-offs: IT may switch off table logging to improve performance; this is non-compliant and must be escalated.
  • Legacy data migrations: migration scripts that load opening balances must be logged and reconciled; note any period when logging was absent.
  • Tampering vs configuration change: a documented, approved change to logging parameters is still a period when the trail may not have operated; the auditor will report it.

Key takeaways

  • Rule 3(1) proviso (from FY 2023-24): accounting software must record an audit trail of every transaction, an edit log of each change with dates, and the feature must not be disableable.
  • Coverage extends to all software forming books, including changes at the database level.
  • Rule 11(g) requires auditor reporting on operation throughout the year, non-tampering and preservation.
  • Correct by reversal, not deletion; restrict and review super-user activity.
  • Retain audit trails for eight years with daily backup on Indian servers (Rule 3(5)).

Practice Questions

Q1. Requirement recall [L1]

State the three conditions in the Rule 3(1) proviso and the three matters the auditor reports on under Rule 11(g). Explain why preservation was not reported in the first year of application.

Q2. Scoping software [L2]

A group uses SAP S/4HANA for GL, AP and AR, a separate SaaS payroll, a warehouse management system that sends stock movements to SAP, and Excel for consolidation adjustments. Assess which systems are in scope for the audit-trail requirement and what evidence you would collect for each.

💡 Hint: Ask whether each system creates or alters records that form part of books of account.

Q3. Draft Rule 11(g) reporting [L2]

Facts: SAP edit logs operated throughout the year at application level; database-level logging was enabled only from 1 November; the payroll SaaS provider's SOC 1 report confirms audit-trail controls for the full year; no evidence of tampering was found. Draft the factual Rule 11(g) wording the auditor might include and list the follow-up actions for management.

Q4. Close process redesign [L3]

A company currently corrects posting errors after the lock by asking IT to update fields directly in the database "to keep the books clean". Redesign the post-close correction process to comply with Rule 3(1), covering approvals, reversal entries, logging, monthly review and evidence retention.

Q5. Interview question — audit trail and IFC [L2]

Explain how a Rule 11(g) exception could affect the auditor's opinion on IFC under Sec 143(3)(i) and the reporting under Sec 143(3)(b).
