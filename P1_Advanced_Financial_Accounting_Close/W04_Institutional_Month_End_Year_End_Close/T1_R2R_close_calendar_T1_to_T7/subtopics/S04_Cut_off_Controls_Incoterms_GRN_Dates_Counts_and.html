Subtopic note

Cut-off Controls: Incoterms, GRN Dates, Counts and Unbilled Revenue

Scope: Cut-off controls: control transfer vs dispatch (Incoterms), GRN vs invoice dates, inventory counts and roll-back, unbilled revenue

Why it matters

Cut-off errors move revenue, cost and inventory across periods. They are a favourite area for both fraud (pulling next-quarter sales into the current quarter to meet guidance) and error (GRNs posted on invoice date rather than receipt date). Auditors test cut-off under SA 330 and SA 500 by sampling transactions either side of the period-end, and SA 240 presumes a fraud risk in revenue recognition. As a controller you need preventive controls designed into systems and processes so the close does not rely on auditors finding errors.

Core notes

Control transfer vs dispatch

Under Ind AS 115.31–38, revenue from goods is recognised when (or as) the customer obtains control. Dispatch from the factory is only one fact; the contract terms determine when control passes. Indicators in Ind AS 115.38: the entity has a present right to payment; the customer has legal title; physical possession has transferred; significant risks and rewards have transferred; the customer has accepted the asset. Incoterms 2020 allocate risk and delivery obligations and are a strong (not conclusive) indicator:

Incoterm Point where risk passes to buyer Typical revenue point for seller
EXW (Ex Works) Goods made available at seller's premises When made available and buyer notified
FCA (Free Carrier) Handover to buyer's carrier Handover to carrier
FAS / FOB Alongside / on board vessel at port of shipment Loading at port (FOB) — after shipping-bill date and on-board bill of lading
CFR / CIF / CPT / CIP On board / handover to first carrier, though seller pays freight (and insurance for CIF/CIP) At shipment; freight/insurance may be a separate performance obligation (shipping service) if after control transfer
DAP / DPU / DDP Delivery at named destination (DPU: unloaded) On delivery at destination

Consequences: an Indian exporter shipping CIF on 29 March recognises goods revenue on loading (if on board by 31 March), but if the freight service is a separate performance obligation it is recognised as the voyage is performed — partly deferred at 31 March. A domestic DDP-equivalent sale (delivered to customer's warehouse) dispatched on 30 March and delivered on 2 April is April revenue and remains March-end inventory ("goods in transit — own stock"). Customer acceptance clauses (Ind AS 115.B83–B86) can delay control further unless acceptance is a formality.

Bill-and-hold (Ind AS 115.B79–B82) requires substantive reason, separate identification, readiness for physical transfer and inability to redirect — all four — and is a red-flag area at quarter-end.

GRN vs invoice dates

The receiving entity's liability and asset arise when goods are received (or control transfers under FOB-origin terms), not when the vendor's invoice is dated or entered. Controls:

  • GRN posting date = physical receipt date, enforced by gate-entry register and system timestamp; back-dating requires approval.
  • Invoices dated T0 or earlier with no GRN by T0 are reviewed: goods in transit under buyer-risk terms should be accrued; otherwise they are next period.
  • GRNs dated T0 with invoices arriving in T+1 are accrued through GR/IR — the automatic three-way-match logic handles this if GRNs are timely.
  • For services, the equivalent of a GRN is a service entry sheet or a business owner's confirmation of service rendered by T0.

GST note: ITC eligibility depends on receipt of goods (Sec 16(2)(b) of the CGST Act) and the supplier's reporting in GSTR-2B, so a T0 invoice with goods received on T+2 creates an ITC timing difference that the T+4 GST rec must track.

Inventory counts and roll-back / roll-forward

Physical counts are often taken on a date other than T0 (e.g., 25 March or 5 April). The count must be bridged to the period-end:

\[ \text{Inventory}_{T0} = \text{Count quantity} + \text{Receipts between count and } T0 - \text{Issues between count and } T0 \]

for a count before T0 (roll-forward), and

\[ \text{Inventory}_{T0} = \text{Count quantity} - \text{Receipts between } T0 \text{ and count} + \text{Issues between } T0 \text{ and count} \]

for a count after T0 (roll-back). The reliability of the bridge depends on cut-off of the intervening movements; SA 501 requires auditors to perform procedures on the movements when the count is not at period-end. Controls: freeze movements during count, document last GRN/dispatch numbers before and after, investigate book-to-physical differences above threshold before posting, and post count adjustments before costing.

Illustration: count on 5 April shows 12,400 units of SKU A. Between 1 and 5 April, receipts were 3,100 units and issues 2,250 units. Rolled back, 31 March inventory = 12,400 − 3,100 + 2,250 = 11,550 units. If the book quantity at 31 March is 11,820 units, the shortage of 270 units is investigated (theft, unrecorded issues, cut-off) before adjusting.

Unbilled revenue

When performance is complete (or progresses over time) but invoicing lags — milestone billing, time-and-material services billed monthly in arrears, deliveries invoiced on POD — revenue is recognised and a contract asset or unbilled receivable is recorded. Under Ind AS 115.105–108, an unconditional right to consideration (only passage of time required) is a receivable; a right conditional on something other than time is a contract asset. For the trade receivables ageing schedule introduced by the 2021 Schedule III amendments, the ICAI Guidance Note on Division II expects unbilled dues to be disclosed separately from billed, ageing-bucketed amounts. Controls: unbilled schedule by contract with basis (timesheets, delivery proof, percentage completion), ageing, and subsequent billing tracked — unbilled amounts not invoiced within 60–90 days are reviewed for recoverability and for whether revenue was really earned.

Entry at T0 for services rendered not billed of ₹42 lakh (unconditional right):

Account Dr (₹) Cr (₹)
Unbilled receivables 42,00,000
Revenue from operations 42,00,000

On invoicing in April, the unbilled balance is reversed to billed receivables and output GST is charged on the invoice (GST time of supply under Sec 13 for services also needs review where invoicing is delayed beyond the prescribed time).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • FOB terms with seller-arranged freight in India: "FOB" is used loosely in domestic contracts; read the actual delivery and risk clauses and the customer's acceptance practice.
  • Consignment stock: goods at a dealer on consignment remain the seller's inventory (Ind AS 115.B77–B78); dispatch is not a sale.
  • Quarter-end channel stuffing: unusual volumes in the last week, extended credit terms or side letters with return rights indicate cut-off manipulation; variable consideration and refund liabilities apply.
  • Rolled-back counts with high activity: if movements between count and T0 are large relative to stock, the bridge becomes unreliable; consider a count at T0.
  • Time of supply mismatch: recognising unbilled revenue under Ind AS 115 does not change GST time of supply, but long-delayed invoicing can breach the invoice time limit.

Key takeaways

  • Control transfer under Ind AS 115.38, informed by Incoterms, determines revenue timing; dispatch is not the test.
  • GRN date must equal receipt date; invoices and GRNs straddling T0 are accrued or deferred using GR/IR.
  • Counts not at T0 are bridged by roll-forward or roll-back, with cut-off of intervening movements tested.
  • Unbilled revenue is a receivable or contract asset with evidence and ageing; old unbilled items need challenge.
  • Quarter-end cut-off is a presumed fraud-risk area; design system controls, not just reviews.

Practice Questions

Q1. Incoterm-based revenue timing [L1]

For each shipment, state whether revenue is recognised in March or April: (a) EXW, goods made available on 31 March, collected on 2 April; (b) DAP Hamburg, shipped from Nhava Sheva on 20 March, arrived 12 April; (c) CIF Singapore, loaded on board on 30 March, arrived 6 April; (d) FCA Delhi ICD, handed to buyer's carrier on 1 April.

Q2. CIF freight as a separate obligation [L2]

An exporter sells goods CIF for USD 5,00,000, of which the standalone selling price of freight and insurance is USD 30,000. Goods are loaded on board on 26 March; the voyage takes 20 days. The rate on 26 March is ₹85.00/USD. Explain the revenue recognised at 31 March and the related balance sheet items, stating your assumptions on measure of progress.

💡 Hint: Separate the goods obligation from the shipping service and measure the shipping service over time.

Q3. Count roll-forward [L2]

A count on 26 March showed 8,200 MT of steel coils. Between 27 and 31 March: receipts 1,450 MT (GRNs), issues to production 2,030 MT and a dispatch of 120 MT for which the invoice is dated 31 March but the gate pass is dated 1 April. Book stock at 31 March is 7,420 MT. Compute the rolled-forward quantity under correct cut-off and identify the issues to investigate.

Q4. Unbilled revenue review [L3]

An IT services company reports unbilled revenue of ₹38 crore at 31 March, up from ₹22 crore a year earlier, while revenue grew 9%. ₹9 crore relates to a single customer milestone billed only on customer sign-off. Discuss the accounting classification (receivable vs contract asset), the controls you would test and the Schedule III disclosure.

Q5. GRN back-dating [L2]

A stores clerk posted 14 GRNs worth ₹1.12 crore on 2 April with posting date 31 March, based on vendor invoices dated 30–31 March. Gate entry shows receipt on 1–2 April. Discuss the effect on inventory, payables, GST ITC and the cut-off control design.
