Subtopic note

Close Governance: RACI, Maker-Checker, JE Approvals and IFC

Scope: RACI, maker-checker, JE approval thresholds, manual JE review; IFC (Sec 134(5)(e), auditor reporting Sec 143(3)(i))

Why it matters

Indian company law converts close governance into a statutory responsibility. Directors of listed companies state that they have laid down internal financial controls (IFC) that are adequate and operating effectively; statutory auditors of most companies report on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of IFC with reference to financial statements. The close process — who prepares, who reviews, who approves journals, and how manual entries are scrutinised — is the heart of IFC over financial reporting. Weaknesses here become audit qualifications, CARO comments and, in fraud cases, regulatory action. For a CMA-qualified professional with audit experience, this is where you move from testing controls to designing them.

Core notes

RACI for the close

RACI assigns each task: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome; one person only), Consulted (provides input before), Informed (receives output after).

Task Accounts executive GL accountant Financial controller CFO Business owner Tax team
Accrual inputs I R A — C (provides data) I
Bank reconciliation R C A I — —
GST/TDS reconciliation C R I — — A
FX revaluation — R A I — —
Top-side consolidation entries — C R A — I
Reporting pack — R A A (sign-off on pack) C C

Rules: exactly one A per task; R and A should be different people for any task producing a journal; a task with no R or A is an orphan and will slip.

Maker-checker

Maker-checker (four-eyes principle) requires that the person preparing a transaction is not the person approving it. In an ERP this is enforced through workflow (parked documents released by an approver — SAP FV50/FBV0 park-and-post, or S/4 Fiori "Verify General Journal Entries" workflow) and through role design that prevents a user from holding both create and approve rights for the same object (segregation of duties, SoD). Typical SoD conflicts to block: create vendor + post vendor invoice; post journal + approve journal; maintain bank master + release payments; change period controls + post journals.

JE approval thresholds

Approval levels scale with value and risk:

JE type Up to ₹10 lakh ₹10 lakh – ₹1 crore Above ₹1 crore Always
Recurring/standard (auto-generated from templates) System approval GL lead Financial controller —
Manual — balance sheet only GL lead Financial controller CFO —
Manual — P&L impact Financial controller Financial controller CFO —
Top-side / consolidation — — — CFO
Entries to revenue, related parties, suspense, equity — — — Financial controller + CFO

Thresholds should be derived from materiality (concept c03) rather than round numbers chosen arbitrarily, and should be reviewed annually.

Manual JE review

Manual journals bypass sub-ledger controls and are the main vehicle for management override (SA 240.31–32 requires auditors to test journal entries). A monthly manual JE review by someone independent of the preparers screens the full population using risk criteria:

  • Posted after the lock or in special periods; posted on weekends or holidays.
  • Round-sum amounts (e.g., multiples of ₹1 lakh), or just below approval thresholds.
  • Unusual account combinations (Dr Trade receivables, Cr Revenue via manual JE; Dr Inventory, Cr COGS).
  • Posted by senior management, by users who seldom post, or by IT/firefighter IDs.
  • Entries with blank or vague narrations; entries reversing shortly after period-end.
  • Entries to suspense, IC, equity and related-party accounts.

A simple Excel or Power Query screen over the JE extract (columns: document number, posting date, entry date, user, amount, accounts, narration) flags each criterion; reviewers sample flagged entries and document evidence.

=LET(amt, ABS([@Amount]), wk, WEEKDAY([@EntryDate], 2),
     flags, (MOD(amt,100000)=0) + (wk>=6) + ([@EntryDate]>LockDate) + (amt>=0.95*Threshold)*(amt<Threshold),
     IF(flags>0, "Review", ""))

IFC under the Companies Act 2013

  • Sec 134(5)(e): for listed companies, the directors' responsibility statement in the Board's report states that directors had laid down internal financial controls to be followed by the company and that such controls are adequate and operating effectively. The Explanation to Sec 134(5)(e) defines IFC broadly — policies and procedures for orderly and efficient conduct of business, adherence to policies, safeguarding of assets, prevention and detection of frauds and errors, accuracy and completeness of accounting records, and timely preparation of reliable financial information.
  • Sec 143(3)(i): the auditor's report states whether the company has adequate IFC with reference to financial statements in place and the operating effectiveness of such controls. MCA's June 2017 notification exempts private companies that are OPCs or small companies, or that have turnover below ₹50 crore per the latest audited financial statements and aggregate borrowings from banks, financial institutions or bodies corporate below ₹25 crore at any time during the year.
  • Sec 177(4)(vii): the audit committee evaluates internal financial controls and risk management systems.
  • ICAI Guidance Note on Audit of IFC over Financial Reporting: provides the auditor's approach (based on a recognised framework, commonly COSO 2013), including evaluation of design and operating effectiveness, and the meaning of material weakness and significant deficiency.

The close team produces the evidence the auditor tests: risk-control matrices (RCMs) for the R2R process, sign-offs on reconciliations, JE approval logs, and evidence of manual JE review.

Illustration — a control in an RCM

Risk Control Type Frequency Owner Evidence
Unauthorised or erroneous manual journals misstate the FS All manual JEs above ₹10 lakh are approved in workflow by a person other than the preparer; monthly independent review of flagged JEs Preventive + detective, automated + manual Each JE / monthly Financial controller Workflow log; signed JE review checklist

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Small teams: where one accountant prepares and posts, compensate with detailed CFO review of the JE listing and system-enforced approval for entries above a low threshold.
  • Automated JEs: recurring templates still need control over the template (who can change the amount or accounts) — change management is the control.
  • Threshold splitting: two ₹9 lakh entries to avoid a ₹10 lakh limit; detect by aggregating JEs by preparer, account and day.
  • Group reporting: consolidation top-sides often sit outside the ERP (in Excel or a consolidation tool); they need the same maker-checker and evidence standards.
  • IFC exemption: an exempt private company still needs controls for Sec 134(5)(f) compliance systems and practical risk; exemption covers only auditor reporting under Sec 143(3)(i).

Key takeaways

  • RACI gives every close task exactly one accountable owner and separates preparer from approver.
  • Maker-checker and SoD rules are enforced in ERP workflow and role design, not just policy.
  • JE approval thresholds should be materiality-derived and escalate for P&L, top-side and sensitive accounts.
  • Monthly manual JE review screens the full population with SA 240-style risk criteria.
  • Sec 134(5)(e) (directors), Sec 143(3)(i) (auditor) and the ICAI Guidance Note anchor IFC in India.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a RACI [L1]

Prepare a RACI matrix for these close tasks: inventory costing run, payroll accrual, IC confirmation, ECL provision, deferred tax computation, board pack preparation. Roles: plant accountant, GL accountant, IC accountant, financial controller, CFO, HR, tax manager.

Q2. JE screening build [L3]

You have a JE extract of 18,400 lines for March with fields: DocNo, PostingDate, EntryDate, UserID, Account, Amount, Narration, Source (manual/auto). Specify the Power Query steps and Excel formulas to flag (a) weekend entries, (b) round-sum entries ≥ ₹1 lakh, (c) entries within 5% below the ₹10 lakh approval threshold, (d) entries posted after the lock on 6 April, and (e) users posting fewer than 5 entries in the year. Describe how you would sample and document the review.

Q3. IFC applicability [L2]

Determine whether auditor reporting under Sec 143(3)(i) applies to: (a) a private company with turnover ₹46 crore and maximum bank borrowings of ₹31 crore during the year; (b) a private company with turnover ₹120 crore and no borrowings; (c) a small company; (d) an unlisted public company with turnover ₹12 crore. Justify each.

Q4. SoD conflict analysis [L2]

A user role list shows that the GL lead can (i) create and post manual journals, (ii) maintain OB52, (iii) create GL accounts, (iv) approve journals parked by others. Identify the conflicts, the risks and the remediation, including compensating controls if headcount is limited.

Q5. Design deficiency vs operating deficiency [L3]

During IFC testing, the auditor finds that 6 of 25 sampled manual JEs above ₹1 crore lacked CFO approval in workflow, though the CFO had e-mailed approvals for 4 of them. Evaluate whether this is a design or operating deficiency, its likely severity, and the remediation management should implement before year-end.
