Key concept

Soft Close vs Hard Close

Concept: Soft close (monthly, estimates permitted) vs hard close (quarter/year-end, full procedures, limited review/audit)

Statement

A soft close is a monthly close for management reporting in which documented estimates may replace full procedures for selected balances, subject to true-up in the next hard close. A hard close is a quarter-end or year-end close performed with full procedures — complete reconciliations, actual cut-off, full tax and provision computations — because its output is subject to statutory auditor limited review (quarters, SEBI LODR Reg 33) or audit (year-end, Companies Act 2013). Estimation error permitted in a soft close is bounded:

\[ \left\lvert \text{Soft-close estimate} - \text{Subsequent actual} \right\rvert \le T \]

where \(T\) is a defined tolerance (e.g., the clearly-trivial threshold per account or in aggregate), monitored as a true-up KPI.

Explanation

Soft closes exist because the cost of a full close every month is high and management needs speed: a T+3 flash on run-rate accruals may be more useful than a T+8 precise number. Items commonly estimated in a soft close: utilities and services accruals (run-rate), inventory variances (standard cost without full settlement), ECL (rolled forward with ageing changes), current tax (effective tax rate on PBT), actuarial obligations (annual valuation projected monthly), IC (provisional). Items that should not be estimated even in a soft close: bank reconciliations, cash, revenue cut-off on major contracts, and anything covenant-sensitive.

Hard closes add: full balance sheet reconciliation certification, physical counts and FA verification (year-end), actuarial valuations under Ind AS 19, impairment tests under Ind AS 36, subsequent events review under Ind AS 10, going-concern assessment, full current and deferred tax with tax-audit alignment, and IC confirmations signed by both sides. Interim (quarterly) reporting follows Ind AS 34, which accepts greater use of estimation methods than annual reporting (Ind AS 34.41 and its illustrative examples on the use of estimates) but still requires reliable measurement.

Governance risks: soft-close estimates that are never trued up accumulate; quarter-ends that are run as soft closes produce limited-review findings; and P&L volatility appears when the hard close "catches up" (e.g., bonus accruals — Topic 3 pitfall). Policies should list, account by account, what is permitted in each type of close.

Illustration

Item Soft close (May) Hard close (June, Q1)
Power accrual 3-month average ₹42 lakh Meter reading-based ₹45.6 lakh; ₹3.6 lakh true-up
Current tax 25.17% of PBT ₹30 crore = ₹7.55 crore Full computation ₹7.21 crore
Inventory variance Not settled; standard cost Settled; ₹1.2 crore unfavourable to COGS
IC balance Provisional Confirmed, differences tagged

The ₹3.6 lakh power true-up is within tolerance (CT ₹25 lakh); the ₹1.2 crore variance shows that inventory variance settlement should be at least quarterly and monitored monthly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify items [L1]

For each item, state whether it may be estimated in a soft close and what the hard-close procedure is: bank reconciliation, gratuity expense, ECL, warranty provision, deferred tax, GST ITC reconciliation, revenue from a milestone contract.

Q2. True-up analysis [L2]

In April and May (soft closes), bonus was accrued at ₹50 lakh per month. At the June hard close, the expected annual bonus is revised to ₹8.4 crore. Compute the June charge needed for the quarter to reflect the revised annual estimate on a straight-line basis, and discuss the commentary you would write.

💡 Hint: Compare the cumulative required accrual at three-twelfths of the annual estimate with what has been booked.

Q3. Policy design [L3]

Draft a soft vs hard close policy for a listed company with monthly MIS and quarterly limited review, specifying permitted estimates, tolerances, true-up rules, sign-offs and how the policy would be evidenced for IFC testing.
