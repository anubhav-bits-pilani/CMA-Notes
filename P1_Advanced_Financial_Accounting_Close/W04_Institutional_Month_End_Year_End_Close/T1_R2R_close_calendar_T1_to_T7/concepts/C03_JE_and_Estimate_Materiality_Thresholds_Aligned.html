Key concept

JE and Estimate Materiality Thresholds Aligned With Audit Materiality

Concept: JE and estimate materiality thresholds (e.g., % of PBT/revenue) aligned with audit materiality

Statement

Close thresholds are derived from the same benchmark the auditor uses for overall materiality under SA 320:

\[ M = b \times B \]

where \(B\) is the benchmark (profit before tax from continuing operations, revenue, total assets or equity) and \(b\) the rate (commonly 5% of PBT, 0.5–1% of revenue, 1–2% of total assets). Then:

\[ PM = p \times M, \quad CT = c \times M \]

where \(PM\) is performance materiality with \(p\) typically 50–75%, and \(CT\) is the clearly-trivial threshold (SA 450) with \(c\) typically 3–5%. Internal thresholds for JE approval, estimate review, reconciliation escalation and post-close reopening are set as fractions of \(M\), \(PM\) or \(CT\).

Explanation

Using an aligned framework ensures consistency (week cross-cutting concept on materiality): an item the auditor would accumulate as a misstatement should not be ignored internally, and internal review effort should concentrate where the auditor will also look. A typical mapping:

Internal threshold Basis Example (M = ₹10 crore)
Post-close adjustment: book in current period > CT > ₹50 lakh
JE requiring CFO approval > 10% of M > ₹1 crore
Estimate (provision, accrual) requiring documented methodology and review > 25% of PM > ₹1.75 crore (PM = ₹7 crore)
Reconciliation item escalation > CT or ageing > 60 days > ₹50 lakh
Flux explanation (Topic 6) > 10% movement and > CT > ₹50 lakh

Judgement points: use normalised PBT if profits are volatile or near zero (otherwise thresholds collapse); qualitative materiality overrides numbers for related-party items, covenant-sensitive lines, directors' remuneration and fraud indicators (SA 320 and SA 450 recognise qualitative factors); thresholds should be recomputed annually and when forecasts change significantly. Management does not need to adopt the auditor's exact figure — but significant divergence should be justified.

Illustration

FY 2026-27 budget PBT ₹180 crore; revenue ₹2,400 crore. 5% of PBT gives \(M\) = ₹9 crore; 0.5% of revenue gives ₹12 crore. The company adopts ₹9 crore (PBT is stable and is the primary user focus). With \(p = 65\%\), \(PM\) = ₹5.85 crore; with \(c = 5\%\), \(CT\) = ₹45 lakh. JE CFO approval threshold: 10% of M = ₹90 lakh, rounded to ₹1 crore for practicality. Any post-close finding above ₹45 lakh is booked in the current period.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute a threshold set [L1]

PBT ₹64 crore, revenue ₹1,150 crore, total assets ₹980 crore. Using 5% of PBT, performance materiality at 60% and clearly trivial at 4%, compute M, PM and CT, and propose JE approval, post-close and reconciliation escalation thresholds.

Q2. Volatile profits [L2]

A company's PBT has been ₹40 crore, −₹12 crore and ₹6 crore over three years; revenue is steady at about ₹900 crore. Recommend a benchmark for close thresholds, justify it, and explain the risk of using current-year PBT.

💡 Hint: Consider normalised or average PBT and revenue-based benchmarks.

Q3. Qualitative override [L3]

A ₹22 lakh manual JE (below CT of ₹40 lakh) reclassifies an amount from "Other expenses" to "Loans to related parties — director". Explain why this should be escalated despite being below threshold, citing the relevant auditing and Companies Act considerations.
