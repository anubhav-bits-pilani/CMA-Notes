Key concept

Close Critical Path: The Longest Dependency Chain

Concept: Close critical path = longest dependency chain (sub-ledger close → recs → tax → consolidation → review)

Statement

The close critical path is the sequence of dependent tasks with the greatest total duration from T0 to the issue of the reporting pack. Its length is the minimum achievable close duration:

\[ D_{\text{close}} = \max_{p \in P} \sum_{i \in p} d_i \]

where \(P\) is the set of all dependency paths through the close task network, \(p\) is one path, and \(d_i\) is the duration (in working days or hours) of task \(i\) on that path. Each task's float (slack) is

\[ F_i = LS_i - ES_i \]

where \(ES_i\) is the earliest start (after all predecessors finish) and \(LS_i\) is the latest start that does not delay \(D_{\text{close}}\). Critical-path tasks have \(F_i = 0\).

Explanation

The close is a project network: billing must finish before revenue cut-off review; AP and accruals before reconciliations; reconciled balances before tax; entity tax before consolidation; consolidation before review and the reporting pack. In most groups the critical path runs sub-ledger close → reconciliations → tax → consolidation → review, because each step consumes the final output of the previous one. Tasks off the path (e.g., preparing the fixed-asset note, drafting commentary templates, payroll clearing rec) have float and can slip without delaying the close.

Why it matters: effort spent speeding up non-critical tasks does not shorten the close. To compress, either shorten durations on the critical path (automation, pre-close work), or break dependencies (start tax provision on a T+3 provisional TB with a defined true-up). Critical paths also shift — if IC matching normally takes half a day but a dispute adds two days, IC becomes critical. That is why trackers should compute float daily rather than fix the path once.

The concept comes from the Critical Path Method (CPM) in project management; the close application adds gates (exit criteria) and estimates where dependencies are deliberately broken.

Illustration

Task Duration (days) Predecessor
A Sub-ledger close 1.0 —
B Accruals and FA 1.0 A
C IC matching and FX 1.0 B
D Reconciliations 1.0 C
E Tax and provisions 1.0 D
F Consolidation 0.5 E
G Review and pack 1.0 F, H, I
H Commentary templates 1.5 A
I FA note drafting 0.5 B

Path A-B-C-D-E-F-G = 6.5 days (critical). Path A-H-G = 3.5 days: H finishes at day 2.5 but G cannot start before day 5.5, giving H a float of 3 days. Path A-B-I-G = 3.5 days, giving I the same 3 days of float. If C slips by 1 day, the close becomes 7.5 days; if H slips by 2 days, nothing changes.

Connections

Links to s03 (T+1 to T+7 day plan), s02 (sub-ledger sequencing) and the topic pitfall on calendars without dependencies. In Excel the predecessor-based "Blocked" flag in the close tracker is a simplified CPM; in FP&A, the same logic schedules budget cycles and board pack production. It also informs c02 (days to close KPI).

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify the critical path [L2]

Tasks (duration in days, predecessors): A 1 (—), B 0.5 (A), C 1.5 (A), D 1 (B, C), E 1 (D), F 0.5 (C), G 1 (E, F), H 0.5 (G). Identify all paths, the critical path, total close duration and float for B and F.

💡 Hint: List every path from A to H and sum durations; float is the difference from the longest path at the merge point.

Q2. Breaking a dependency [L3]

The tax team needs the final reconciled TB (T+4) to start the tax provision, which takes 1.5 days. Propose a method to start tax on T+3 using a provisional TB, define the true-up rule and the materiality condition under which a re-run is required, and explain the effect on the critical path.

Q3. Interview question [L1]

Explain in plain terms to a non-finance stakeholder why automating the fixed-asset note will not shorten the close but automating IC matching might.
