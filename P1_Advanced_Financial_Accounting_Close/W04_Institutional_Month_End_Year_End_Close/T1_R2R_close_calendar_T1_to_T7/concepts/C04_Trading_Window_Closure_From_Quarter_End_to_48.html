Key concept

Trading Window Closure From Quarter-End to 48 Hours After Results

Concept: Trading window closed from quarter-end until 48 hours after results are published (SEBI PIT Regulations)

Statement

Under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, Schedule B (minimum standards for a listed company's code of conduct), designated persons and their immediate relatives may not trade in the company's securities while the trading window is closed. For financial results:

\[ \text{Window closed} = \left[\, \text{day after quarter-end},\; t_R + 48 \text{ hours} \,\right] \]

where \(t_R\) is the time the financial results are disseminated to the stock exchanges. The compliance officer may close the window earlier or for other UPSI events (mergers, fund raising, dividends).

Explanation

Financial results are presumptively UPSI (Reg 2(1)(n) lists financial results among UPSI items). The close team has access to them from T+1 onward, well before publication. The window closure is a bright-line control that removes the need to prove possession of UPSI for each trade. Key mechanics:

  • Designated persons are identified by the board in consultation with the compliance officer, typically including finance, accounts and FP&A staff with access to UPSI, plus their immediate relatives.
  • Structured digital database (SDD): under Reg 3(5), the company maintains a database of persons with whom UPSI is shared, with PAN and time-stamping; close-team members and external parties (auditors, consultants) receiving draft results are recorded.
  • Pre-clearance: trades by designated persons above thresholds need pre-clearance when the window is open; trading plans under Reg 5 permit pre-scheduled trades even during closure (subject to conditions, as amended in 2024).
  • Contra trades: designated persons may not enter an opposite transaction within six months of a trade.
  • 48 hours is measured from public dissemination, so a 5:30 p.m. Tuesday announcement means trading can resume from 5:30 p.m. Thursday.

For the close calendar, this means: issue a trading-window reminder in the pre-close communication, restrict draft results distribution to SDD-recorded persons, and record the results dissemination time so the window reopening is clear.

Illustration

Q1 FY 2026-27 ends 30 June 2026. Results are approved and disseminated on Thursday 6 August 2026 at 4:10 p.m. The window is closed from 1 July 2026 until 4:10 p.m. on Saturday 8 August; practically, trading resumes on Monday 10 August. An accounts executive who prepared the T+2 accrual schedule and sold shares on 28 July has traded during closure — a violation regardless of whether the trade used UPSI, requiring reporting to SEBI by the compliance officer and disciplinary action under the code.

Practice Questions

Q1. Window dates [L1]

Q3 ends 31 December 2026. Results are disseminated on Tuesday 9 February 2027 at 6:00 p.m. State when the trading window closes and when it reopens, and list three categories of close-team staff who should be designated persons.

Q2. SDD and external parties [L2]

During the Q2 close, draft results are shared with the statutory auditor, a valuation consultant working on an impairment test, and the group's bankers for a covenant waiver. Explain the SDD obligations for each and the control the close manager should implement.

💡 Hint: Consider who is receiving UPSI for a legitimate purpose and what must be recorded.

Q3. Case — trading plan [L3]

The CFO has a trading plan approved and disclosed under Reg 5 that schedules a sale of 10,000 shares on 15 July. The trading window for Q1 is closed on that date. Discuss whether the sale may proceed and what conditions must have been satisfied.
