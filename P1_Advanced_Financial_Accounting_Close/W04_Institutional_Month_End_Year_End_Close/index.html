Objective: Standardize an R2R (Record-to-Report) month-end close calendar, automated reconciliation workpapers, and audit-ready financial statements.

How this week fits

Week 4 closes Phase 1 (Advanced Financial Accounting & Close Mastery). Weeks 1–3 built the technical schedules: revenue waterfall, Schedule III structure and cash flow tagging (Week 1); leases, provisions, employee benefits and related parties (Week 2); EIR, ECL, deferred tax, consolidation and FX (Week 3). This week turns them into a governed close: a T+1 to T+7 calendar, automated bank/GST/TDS reconciliations, accrual and prepaid schedules, IC and suspense protocols, a TB-to-Schedule III mapping engine and flux commentary.

The deliverable is Project 1: a linked Month-End Closing Workbook in which every Week 1–4 schedule posts into an adjustment layer, the final TB flows through the mapping to the FS and notes, and a master check flag proves integrity. Its mapping logic is reused in Phase 2 to load historicals into 3-statement models. Prerequisites: the Week 1–3 schedules, Schedule III Division II, SA 240/320/520 from audit practice, GST/TDS return mechanics and intermediate Excel.

Refresh before starting

Weeks 1–3 schedules as close inputs

Weeks 1–3 schedules (revenue waterfall, leases, EIR, ECL, DT) as close inputs

Recall each schedule's monthly output and the entry it generates. Revenue waterfall: revenue recognised, contract assets/unbilled receivables and contract liabilities by contract (T+1 input). Lease schedule (Ind AS 116): interest on lease liability, ROU depreciation, payments, current/non-current split of the liability, remeasurements (T+2). EIR schedule (Ind AS 109): interest income/expense at the effective rate and amortised cost balance, including transaction-cost amortisation (T+3). ECL: provision matrix or stage-based ECL with movement in loss allowance (T+5). Deferred tax: temporary differences by category, DTA/DTL movement split between P&L and OCI (T+5). Each schedule must tie to its GL accounts after posting, and each becomes a check line in the Project 1 workbook.

Audit and ICFR concepts

Audit and ICFR concepts: control design, maker-checker, SA 520, SA 240

Control design: a control must address a specific risk (risk-control matrix), have an owner, frequency, evidence and precision; design deficiency means the control would not prevent/detect the misstatement even if operated; operating deficiency means it was not performed as designed. Maker-checker: preparer and approver are different people, enforced by system workflow and segregation of duties. SA 520: analytical procedures — develop an expectation, set a threshold for acceptable difference, investigate and corroborate differences; the same logic drives flux review. SA 240: presumed fraud risks in revenue recognition and management override; journal entry testing, review of estimates for bias, and evaluation of unusual transactions.

GST/TDS data fields for reconciliation

GST/TDS return data fields relevant to reconciliation (GSTIN, invoice keys, TAN/PAN, section codes)

GST: 15-character GSTIN (2-digit state code + 10-character PAN + entity number + "Z" + check digit); invoice number (up to 16 characters, unique per FY per GSTIN), invoice date, document type (invoice/debit note/credit note), place of supply, taxable value, IGST/CGST/SGST/cess, IRN for e-invoices, GSTR-2B ITC availability flags and reasons, IMS action status. TDS: 10-character TAN of the deductor and PAN of the deductee, section/nature-of-payment codes, date of payment/credit, amount paid/credited, TDS deducted and deposited, challan identification number (BSR code + date + serial), quarter, and certificate numbers for lower deduction. Remember that section codes change under the Income-tax Act 2025 from 1 April 2026.

Excel skills for the build

Excel: Power Query, XLOOKUP, SUMIFS, dynamic arrays, structured tables, conditional formatting

Power Query: Folder connector, data types, Merge (inner, left anti, right anti), Group By, unpivot, period parameters. XLOOKUP on composite keys; SUMIFS by account, line ID and period; dynamic arrays (FILTER, UNIQUE, SEQUENCE, LET, LAMBDA) for note tables and amortisation grids; structured tables; formula-based conditional formatting for overdue tasks, ageing breaches and failed checks; data validation lists.

Cross-cutting concepts

Single source of truth

Single source of truth: TB extract → mapping → FS → notes → checks, with Weeks 1–3 schedules posting into the adjustment layer

Every number in the financial statements must be traceable to one place: the ERP trial balance extract for the period, plus referenced adjustments. The flow is linear. (1) The TB extract (account, entity, cost centre, period balances) is loaded unchanged by Power Query. (2) The adjustment layer adds entries not yet in the ERP — schedule postings from Weeks 1–3 (lease, EIR, ECL, DT, revenue), audit and top-side adjustments, reclassifications — each with a reference, preparer, approver and reason. (3) The final TB = extract + adjustments, by account. (4) The mapping table assigns each account to a Schedule III line, note, sub-note, current/non-current flag, cash flow category and DT category. (5) FS and notes are formula outputs (SUMIFS on the mapping), never typed. (6) Checks compare outputs with each other and with the schedules. Hard-coded numbers in FS tabs, or schedules that bypass the adjustment layer, break the chain and make the workbook unauditable.

Control evidence for every balance

Control evidence: every balance has owner, reconciliation, reviewer sign-off and aged reconciling items

An audit-ready close can show, for every balance sheet account, who owns it, how it was reconciled, who reviewed it and when, and what reconciling items remain with their ages. In the workbook this is a reconciliation register tab: account, owner, risk rating, reconciliation type (bank, sub-ledger, schedule, portal), evidence reference, preparer and reviewer dates, gross reconciling items by ageing bucket, and status. This supports the directors' IFC statement under Sec 134(5)(e), the auditor's Sec 143(3)(i) testing and CARO reporting. Evidence must be independent (bank statement, GSTR-2B, Form 26AS, counterparty confirmation), complete and retained; reviewers sign after preparers; items are aged gross from origination; and escalation thresholds trigger decisions, not just e-mails. A balance without an owner or reconciliation should fail the master check.

Project 1 check flags

Project 1 check flags: TB = 0, BS balances, CF closing cash = BS cash, schedules tie to GL, IC nets to zero, suspense nil

The checks tab turns integrity into numbers. TB check: sum of final TB balances = 0.00 (debits = credits) for each entity. BS check: total assets − (equity + liabilities) = 0.00, including current-year profit flowing to retained earnings. CF check: closing cash per cash flow statement − cash and cash equivalents per balance sheet (after reclassifying overdrafts that form part of cash management, per Ind AS 7.8) = 0.00. Schedule ties: each schedule's closing balance (lease liability, ROU, EIR amortised cost, ECL allowance, DTA/DTL, accruals, prepaids, contract balances) − mapped GL balance = 0.00. IC: Σ IC receivables − Σ IC payables per counterparty pair = 0 after tagged differences. Suspense: balance of all suspense/clearing accounts flagged in mapping = 0 or fully itemised. Unmapped accounts = 0. Each check returns TRUE/FALSE with tolerance ±0.5 for rounding; the master flag is TRUE only if all are TRUE.

Consistent materiality

Consistent materiality across flux thresholds, JE review and reconciliation escalation

Use one materiality framework, aligned with how the auditor will set SA 320 materiality (e.g., 5% of normalised PBT), and derive all internal thresholds from it: clearly trivial (3–5% of materiality) for booking post-close adjustments and escalating reconciling items; a fraction of materiality for JE approval tiers; and the absolute leg of flux thresholds (e.g., movement > 10% and > clearly trivial). Consistency matters because inconsistent thresholds create blind spots — a reconciling item below the escalation threshold may still exceed the flux threshold, or a JE may avoid review while the resulting P&L movement is flagged as unexplained. Qualitative overrides apply to related-party, fraud-indicative and covenant-sensitive items regardless of amount. Store the thresholds in a single named-range assumptions block in the workbook and reference them everywhere.

Workbook design and version control

Workbook design: tab map (inputs, schedules, TB, mapping, FS, notes, checks, dashboard), colour conventions, version control

A standard tab order makes the workbook readable by a reviewer in minutes: Cover and tab map → Assumptions (period, rates, thresholds) → Inputs (TB extract, bank, GSTR-2B, 26AS, IC data) → Schedules (Weeks 1–4) → Adjustment layer → Final TB → Mapping → FS (BS, P&L, SOCIE, CF) → Notes → Checks → Dashboard (close tracker KPIs, reconciliation status). Colour conventions: blue font for hard-coded inputs, black for formulas, green for links to other tabs, and a distinct fill for check cells (green TRUE, red FALSE). Avoid merged cells in data areas, use structured tables, and keep one formula per column. Version control: file naming with period and version (e.g., MEC_FY27_M03_v03), a change log tab (date, author, change, reason), locked prior-period versions, and storage on SharePoint with version history so the approved version can be reproduced.

Hands-on exercise brief

Roadmap exercise: Final assembly of Project 1: Integrate all Week 1–4 schedules into a single linked Month-End Closing Workbook with automated check flags.

Scenario and inputs

Entity: Deccan Precision Components Ltd (unlisted public company, FY ending 31 March), with one Indian subsidiary (Deccan Tooling Pvt Ltd) and one Singapore subsidiary (functional currency SGD). Close period: March 2027 (year-end) with February 2027 as the comparative month and 31 March 2026 audited balances as the prior year.

Input Specification
TB extract ~180 GL accounts per entity, columns: Entity, Account, Description, CostCentre, Opening, Debits, Credits, Closing; include 3 new accounts not yet mapped and 1 suspense account with ₹6.4 lakh
Bank 2 INR current accounts, 1 cash credit (overdrawn ₹3.1 crore), 1 EEFC USD account (USD 2,40,000; carrying rate ₹84.20; closing rate ₹86.10)
GST Purchase register 1,200 lines and GSTR-2B download for 2 GSTINs; ITC per books ₹1.86 crore vs 2B ₹1.79 crore
TDS TDS receivable ₹42 lakh vs 26AS ₹38.5 lakh; TDS payable by section for March
Accruals 40 accrual lines (utilities, CAM, audit fee ₹36 lakh annual, security, consultancy, interest, bonus pool ₹2.4 crore at 100% achievement)
Prepaids 15 prepaid items including a 3-year insurance policy ₹27 lakh (1 October 2026–30 September 2029)
IC Parent receivable from Singapore SGD 1,50,000 (parent carries at ₹63.40; Singapore carries payable at ₹63.90 equivalent); management fee recharge to the Indian subsidiary ₹48 lakh disputed by ₹4 lakh
Weeks 1–3 schedules Revenue waterfall, lease schedule (2 leases), EIR schedule (1 term loan ₹25 crore), ECL matrix, deferred tax computation
Thresholds Materiality ₹2.5 crore; clearly trivial ₹12.5 lakh; flux 10% and ₹12.5 lakh

Required tabs and schedules

  1. Cover, tab map, change log and assumptions block (period, rates, thresholds as named ranges).
  2. Close tracker: 45 tasks over T−3 to T+7 with owners, predecessors, WORKDAY-based dates, blocked/overdue flags and a completion dashboard.
  3. Reconciliations: two-layer EEFC rec with revaluation; bank recs with ageing; ITC bridge (GSTIN + normalised key matching via Power Query anti-joins); TDS 26AS bridge with tags; reconciliation register with owner, reviewer and gross ageing.
  4. Accrual and prepaid schedules with template fields, reversal flags, overlap-day amortisation grid and current/non-current split.
  5. IC matching matrix by counterparty pair with tagged differences (timing, FX, disputed, missing) and a suspense ageing schedule.
  6. Adjustment layer: each Weeks 1–4 schedule posting and each reclassification (overdraft to borrowings, debit-balance creditors to advances, current maturities of long-term debt) with a reference.
  7. Final TB, mapping table (Schedule III line, note, current/non-current, Ind AS 7 category, DT category) and Schedule III BS, P&L, SOCIE, CF and at least eight notes generated by formulas.
  8. Flux tab with dual thresholds and commentary for every flagged line.
  9. Checks tab and master check flag.

Required checks

TB nets to 0.00 per entity; BS balances; P&L ties to the retained earnings movement; CF closing cash = BS cash; every schedule ties to its GL; IC nets to zero per pair after tags; suspense nil or itemised; unmapped accounts = 0; accrual and prepaid GL = schedules; reconciliation register shows 100% reviewed for high-risk accounts.

Deliverable format

One .xlsx workbook (Power Query connections retained), named per the version convention, plus a one-page close memo: master check status, open reconciling items above clearly trivial, post-close adjustments and the top five flux explanations.

Q4. Accruals, prepaids and bonus [L2]

Q5. Tax interplay and classification [L2]

Q6. Governance and audit trail case [L3]

Q7. Interview-style explanation [L1]

(6 marks) In under 300 words, explain to a CFO how your Project 1 workbook ensures that the financial statements update automatically when an adjusting entry is posted, and which three check flags you would look at first and why.
