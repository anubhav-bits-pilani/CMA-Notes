Subtopic note

TDS Receivable vs Form 26AS/AIS, Form 16A and Write-off Policy

Scope: TDS receivable vs Form 26AS/AIS, Form 16A collection, unclaimed TDS write-off policy

Why it matters

When customers deduct tax at source on payments to the company — on professional or technical fees, contract payments, rent, commission, interest — the company records a TDS receivable that it will set off against its own income-tax liability. The credit is allowed in assessment only if it appears in the company's Form 26AS (the annual tax credit statement against its PAN) or AIS. For service companies TDS receivable can run to 1–2% of revenue, and differences between books and 26AS are a common reason for refund delays, demands and eventual write-offs. A monthly or quarterly 26AS reconciliation is the control that converts book TDS into tax credit actually available.

Core notes

Sources of truth

  • Form 26AS: the annual tax statement maintained by the tax department against a PAN, showing TDS/TCS deducted by each deductor (TAN, name, section, transaction date, amount paid/credited, tax deducted, tax deposited), plus advance tax, self-assessment tax and refunds. It is populated from deductors' quarterly TDS statements (24Q, 26Q, 27Q, 27EQ) processed by TRACES.
  • Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS): a broader statement of reported financial transactions (including TDS/TCS, interest, dividends, securities transactions, GST turnover). AIS allows the taxpayer to give feedback on incorrect entries.
  • Form 16A: the TDS certificate for non-salary deductions, issued quarterly by the deductor from TRACES within 15 days of the due date of furnishing the TDS statement (Rule 31 of the Income-tax Rules, 1962). Form 16A is evidence of deduction, but credit is given on the basis of the 26AS/departmental data, not the certificate.

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026, replacing "previous year/assessment year" with "tax year"), the corresponding statements and certificate forms continue in substance but section and form numbers have changed under the new Rules; map the references in your reconciliation template before using them for tax year 2026-27 onwards.

Reconciliation design

Key fields: customer TAN, customer PAN/name, invoice number and date, amount credited/paid, section (e.g., 194J professional/technical, 194C contract, 194I rent, 194H commission, 194A interest, 194Q purchase of goods under the 1961 Act numbering), TDS rate, TDS amount, quarter.

Matching levels:

  1. Customer (TAN) × quarter: sum of TDS per books vs 26AS. Fast, catches non-filers.
  2. Customer × section × quarter: catches wrong-section and rate differences.
  3. Transaction: where customers report transaction dates and amounts, match to invoices or receipts.

Form 16A collection

Form 16A is collected quarterly as supporting evidence and for audit: maintain a tracker by customer and quarter (requested date, received date, matches 26AS: Y/N). Missing certificates are a lead indicator of customers who did not deposit. In practice, TDS with 26AS reflection but no 16A is claimable; TDS with 16A but no 26AS reflection needs customer correction or, as a last resort, representations to the assessing officer supported by the 16A and evidence of deduction (the protection under Sec 205 of the 1961 Act that the deductee should not be called upon to pay tax already deducted has been recognised by courts, but relying on it means litigation).

Unclaimed TDS write-off policy

TDS that will never be credited is not an asset. A policy should define:

  • Ageing triggers: TDS receivable not reflected in 26AS by the due date of the income-tax return for the year (first review), and after the time limit for customer correction statements and the assessment of that year (final review).
  • Follow-up evidence: at least two documented follow-ups, escalation to the customer's finance head, and a decision log.
  • Recognition: provide for or write off: Dr Bad debts/TDS written off (other expenses), Cr TDS receivable. Where the customer short-paid because of TDS that it never deposited, the recoverability question is effectively a receivable credit risk.
  • Approvals: controller below a threshold; CFO above it; audit committee for large aggregate write-offs.
  • Recovery: where the customer later corrects, reverse the write-off.

Presentation

TDS receivable is a current tax asset under Ind AS 12. It is offset against current tax liability only when the entity has a legally enforceable right to set off and intends to settle net (Ind AS 12.71) — typically per assessment year with the same authority. Under Schedule III, net amounts appear as current tax assets (net) or income tax assets (net, non-current, for years under assessment or appeal) and current tax liabilities (net).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • TDS on advances: TDS deducted on an advance relates to income of a later year; claim it when the income is offered (Rule 37BA(3)), so carry it forward as a timing item rather than writing it off.
  • Lower-deduction certificate held by the company: if customers ignore the certificate and deduct at the normal rate, 26AS will show higher TDS; reconcile and ensure the customer applies the certificate prospectively.
  • Mergers and PAN changes: after an amalgamation, customers may deduct against the transferor's PAN; credit can be claimed by the successor but needs documentation.
  • Unbilled revenue with TDS: customers deduct on payment or credit in their books; timing differences with revenue recognition are normal and should be tracked.
  • AIS errors: where AIS shows incorrect income (e.g., duplicate reporting), submit feedback to keep the record clean for assessment.

Key takeaways

  • TDS credit is allowed on the basis of 26AS/departmental data; Form 16A alone is not enough.
  • Reconcile by customer TAN × quarter, then by section and transaction, with a bridge to 26AS.
  • Root-cause each difference: non-filing, wrong PAN/TAN, wrong section/quarter, GST-inclusive deduction, gross booking.
  • A write-off policy with ageing triggers, evidence and approvals prevents surprise write-offs at assessment.
  • Present TDS as a current tax asset, offset only where Ind AS 12.71 criteria are met.

Practice Questions

Q1. TDS bridge [L2]

TDS receivable per books for FY 2025-26: ₹2.84 crore. Findings: Customer A (₹0.18 crore) has not filed Q3 and Q4 26Q; Customer B deducted ₹0.06 crore under a PAN with one character wrong; ₹0.04 crore appears in 26AS for FY 2026-27 Q1 though deducted on 30 March 2026 credits; ₹0.03 crore in 26AS relates to receipts booked gross. Prepare the bridge to 26AS and the entries needed in books.

💡 Hint: Some items need customer action, one needs a books entry, one is timing.

Q2. Write-off policy drafting [L3]

Draft a TDS receivable write-off policy for an IT services company with 1,400 customers, including ageing triggers, follow-up steps, thresholds for approval, accounting entries and the review evidence for auditors.

Q3. Presentation [L1]

At 31 March 2026: TDS receivable for FY 2025-26 ₹3.6 crore, advance tax paid ₹12.0 crore, current tax provision ₹14.1 crore; TDS receivable for FY 2022-23 (under appeal) ₹1.1 crore. Explain how these are presented under Schedule III and Ind AS 12.

Q4. Customer non-compliance case [L2]

A large PSU customer deducts TDS at 10% under the professional-fee section on invoice values inclusive of 18% GST, and its 26AS reporting lags by one quarter. Identify the issues, the reconciliation tags you would use and the steps to resolve them.
