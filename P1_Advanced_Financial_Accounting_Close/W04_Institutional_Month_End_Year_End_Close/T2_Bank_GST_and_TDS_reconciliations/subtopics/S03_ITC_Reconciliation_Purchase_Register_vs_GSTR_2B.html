Subtopic note

ITC Reconciliation: Purchase Register vs GSTR-2B and IMS Actions

Scope: ITC rec: purchase register vs GSTR-2B (Sec 16(2)(aa)), Invoice Management System accept/reject/pending actions, vendor follow-up and payment holds

Why it matters

Since 1 January 2022, a recipient can claim input tax credit (ITC) only to the extent the supplier has reported the invoice and it appears in the recipient's GSTR-2B. For a company with thousands of vendors, the monthly ITC reconciliation is therefore a working-capital and compliance control: ITC not in GSTR-2B is cash locked up, ITC claimed in excess triggers DRC-01C intimations (s04) and demands with interest, and ITC on invoices that should be rejected (wrong GSTIN, duplicate, fictitious supplier) is a fraud risk. The Invoice Management System (IMS) has turned this from a passive comparison into an active monthly workflow. In the close calendar, the ITC rec sits on T+4 and must be complete before the GSTR-3B on the 20th.

Core notes

Legal basis

Sec 16(2) of the CGST Act lists cumulative conditions for ITC: (a) possession of a tax invoice or debit note; (aa) the details of the invoice or debit note have been furnished by the supplier in the statement of outward supplies (GSTR-1/IFF) and communicated to the recipient (operationally through GSTR-2B, read with Rule 36(4)); (b) receipt of goods or services; (ba) the details are not restricted in the statement under Sec 38; (c) tax charged has actually been paid to the Government by the supplier; and (d) the recipient has furnished its return under Sec 39. The second proviso to Sec 16(2) requires reversal if the recipient does not pay the supplier within 180 days (Rule 37, s05). Time limits are in Sec 16(4) (concept c06).

Data and keys

Source Key fields
Purchase register (books) Supplier GSTIN, supplier invoice number, invoice date, document type (invoice/DN/CN), place of supply, taxable value, IGST/CGST/SGST/cess, ITC eligibility flag, GL posting period
GSTR-2B (portal, generated on the 14th of the following month) Supplier GSTIN, trade name, invoice number, date, type, taxable value, tax heads, ITC availability (available/not available), reason (e.g., Sec 17(5), place of supply), GSTR-1 filing period
IMS (portal, continuous) Each inward record with status Accept / Reject / Pending / No action

Matching must use supplier GSTIN + normalised invoice number + financial year of invoice date as the primary key (topic pitfall 1). Normalisation removes spaces, slashes, hyphens, leading zeros and common prefixes, and upper-cases text, so that "INV/0045/26-27" and "INV45/2627" can be compared.

Match categories

Category Definition Action
Exact match Key + taxable value + tax within ₹1 Claim
Value mismatch Key matches, tax differs Investigate: wrong rate, partial booking, supplier error; IMS reject if supplier error
Date/period mismatch Key matches, invoice in a different 2B period Timing; claim in the period it appears
In books, not in 2B Supplier not filed / filed late / wrong GSTIN Do not claim; hold in "ITC pending reflection"; vendor follow-up
In 2B, not in books Invoice not received or not booked, or not ours Book if genuine; otherwise IMS reject
Ineligible per books Sec 17(5) blocked items, personal use Do not claim regardless of 2B

Concept c03 gives the bridge between ITC per books and ITC per 2B.

Invoice Management System (IMS)

IMS went live on the GST portal from October 2024. Every invoice, debit note and credit note uploaded by suppliers flows to the recipient's IMS dashboard, where the recipient can:

  • Accept: the record flows into GSTR-2B as eligible ITC.
  • Reject: the record is excluded from GSTR-2B; the supplier sees the rejection and can correct through GSTR-1A or a later GSTR-1. Use for wrong GSTIN, duplicates, invoices not belonging to the recipient, incorrect values.
  • Keep pending: the record is held out of the current GSTR-2B and carried forward, subject to time limits (Sec 16(4)); used when goods are not yet received or the invoice is under dispute.
  • No action: the record is deemed accepted when GSTR-2B is generated.

GSTR-2B is generated on the 14th of the following month based on IMS actions, and can be recomputed if the recipient takes further actions before filing GSTR-3B. Rejecting or keeping a supplier's credit note pending affects the ITC the recipient must reverse. Legal backing for IMS-driven GSTR-2B was strengthened by amendments to Sec 38 (Finance Act 2025), and the GSTN has since issued changes on pending actions for credit notes and declaration of ITC reversal amounts. These procedures are evolving — verify the current GSTN advisories before designing the workflow.

Workflow in the close: T+1 to T+10 of the following month, the AP/GST team downloads IMS records, runs them against the purchase register, accepts matched records, rejects invalid ones and keeps pending the timing items. The audit trail of IMS actions (who decided, why) belongs in the reconciliation evidence.

Vendor follow-up and payment holds

For invoices in books but not in 2B:

  1. Vendor communication: automated e-mails listing missing invoices, with GSTR-1 filing status checked on the portal (search taxpayer → return filing status).
  2. Payment hold: hold the GST component (or the full payment, per contract) until the invoice reflects in 2B. Contract clauses should provide that tax amounts are payable only on reflection and that the supplier indemnifies against ITC loss, interest and penalty.
  3. Vendor rating: track each vendor's filing reliability; chronic non-filers are replaced by compliant alternatives or moved to stricter payment terms (tax component paid only after reflection).
  4. Accounting: keep unreflected ITC in a separate GL account ("GST ITC — pending reflection") rather than in eligible ITC, so the GL can be compared with the electronic credit ledger (s05).

Illustration: March purchase register ITC ₹4.86 crore; GSTR-2B ITC ₹4.71 crore. Bridge: in books not in 2B ₹0.29 crore (14 vendors not filed — hold ₹0.29 crore of payments); in 2B not in books ₹0.11 crore (₹0.08 crore invoices received on 3 April, booked in April; ₹0.03 crore not ours — reject in IMS); value mismatches net ₹0.03 crore. ITC claimed in GSTR-3B: ₹4.57 crore (matched and eligible only).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Imports of goods: IGST on imports flows to GSTR-2B from ICEGATE (bill of entry data) — reconcile against the BoE register, not supplier GSTINs.
  • Reverse charge: RCM ITC is self-declared and does not depend on supplier filing; keep it out of the 2B match but reconcile to RCM liability paid.
  • ISD distribution: ITC distributed by an Input Service Distributor (mandatory ISD for common services from 1 April 2025) appears separately; match to the ISD's GSTR-6.
  • Multi-GSTIN entities: reconcile per GSTIN (state registration); an invoice addressed to the Maharashtra GSTIN cannot be claimed in Karnataka.
  • Supplier cancelled registration: invoices from a cancelled GSTIN with retrospective effect may disappear or be flagged; reverse and recover.

Key takeaways

  • Sec 16(2)(aa) and Rule 36(4) restrict ITC to what appears in GSTR-2B.
  • Match on supplier GSTIN + normalised invoice number + FY, never on invoice number alone.
  • IMS actions (accept, reject, pending, deemed accept) now determine GSTR-2B; procedures are evolving.
  • Hold unreflected ITC in a separate account and hold vendor payments until reflection.
  • Document IMS decisions and vendor follow-up as reconciliation evidence.

Practice Questions

Q1. Match categories [L1]

Classify each into a match category and state the action: (a) invoice booked in March, supplier filed GSTR-1 in April; (b) invoice in 2B with supplier GSTIN of a different company in the same group; (c) invoice for a staff car (Sec 17(5)) in both books and 2B; (d) invoice in 2B for ₹1.18 lakh tax, booked at ₹1.08 lakh.

Q2. Normalised key build [L2]

Write Power Query or Excel steps to create a match key from supplier GSTIN, invoice number and invoice date, handling slashes, hyphens, spaces, leading zeros and FY suffixes such as "/25-26". Explain how you would test that the normalisation does not create false matches.

💡 Hint: Check for duplicate keys after normalisation within each dataset before joining.

Q3. ITC bridge and GSTR-3B claim [L2]

Data for June: ITC per purchase register ₹2.38 crore (of which ₹0.06 crore blocked under Sec 17(5)); ITC per 2B ₹2.31 crore; in books not in 2B ₹0.15 crore; in 2B not in books ₹0.09 crore (₹0.05 crore genuine, booked in July; ₹0.04 crore not ours). Prepare the bridge, the eligible ITC to claim in GSTR-3B and the IMS actions.

Q4. Vendor policy case [L3]

A procurement head objects to holding payments to 40 small vendors who file GSTR-1 late, arguing it damages relationships and that many are MSMEs (Sec 43B(h) applies). Draft a balanced vendor follow-up and payment-hold policy that protects ITC while managing MSME payment timelines.
