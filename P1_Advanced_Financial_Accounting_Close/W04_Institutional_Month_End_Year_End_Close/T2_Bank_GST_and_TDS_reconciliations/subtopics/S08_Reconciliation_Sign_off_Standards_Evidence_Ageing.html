Subtopic note

Reconciliation Sign-off Standards: Evidence, Ageing and Escalation

Scope: Sign-off standards: preparer/reviewer, evidence, reconciling-item ageing, escalation thresholds

Why it matters

A reconciliation that is prepared but not reviewed, or reviewed without evidence, is not a control — it is a spreadsheet. Auditors testing IFC under Sec 143(3)(i) look for a documented standard: which accounts must be reconciled, by whom, by when, with what evidence, how reconciling items are aged and when they must be escalated or written off. Without such a standard, balances drift, old items accumulate in "unreconciled differences", and year-end becomes a clean-up exercise. In the Project 1 workbook, the sign-off standard defines the reconciliation status columns that feed the master check flag and the close dashboard.

Core notes

Scope and risk-rating of accounts

Every balance sheet account is assigned to a reconciliation owner and given a risk rating that sets frequency and review level:

Risk rating Examples Frequency Reviewer
High Bank, IC, GST/TDS, suspense/clearing, revenue-related contract balances, related-party balances Monthly, by T+4 Financial controller
Medium Trade receivables/payables control, accruals, prepaid, payroll liabilities, inventory Monthly GL lead / senior accountant
Low Security deposits, long-term advances, share capital, static provisions Quarterly GL lead

Zero-balance and inactive accounts are still certified (a one-line confirmation) so that unexpected postings are noticed.

Preparer and reviewer responsibilities

Role Responsibilities
Preparer Agree the GL balance to the final TB; obtain independent evidence (bank statement, 26AS, GSTR-2B, sub-ledger, confirmation); list every reconciling item individually with date, amount, description, owner and expected clearance date; propose adjusting entries; sign and date
Reviewer Check GL balance agrees to TB; re-perform key ties (statement balance, sub-ledger totals); challenge reconciling items for validity and age; ensure adjustments are posted or approved; confirm no netting; sign and date — after the preparer and within the deadline
Approver (high-risk) Review aged items above thresholds and approve carry-forward or write-off

Segregation: the preparer cannot review their own work; the reviewer should not have posted the main transactions in the account. Sign-off must be evidenced in a system (BlackLine, Oracle ARCS, SharePoint with version history) or in a locked workbook with timestamps; e-mail approvals are weaker evidence.

Evidence standards

Evidence must be independent (from outside the GL — bank, portal, counterparty), complete (statement for the full period, not a screenshot of the closing balance) and retained (linked to the reconciliation and kept for the eight-year retention period under Sec 128(5)). Examples:

Reconciliation Required evidence
Bank Bank statement for the month (PDF or MT940 import with completeness check), list of unpresented cheques with dates
GST ITC GSTR-2B download, IMS action log, ECL extract
GST output GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filed copies, e-invoice register
TDS receivable Form 26AS/AIS extract, Form 16A tracker
TDS payable Challans (CIN), filed statements, TRACES default summary
IC Counterparty confirmation, IC matching report

Reconciling-item ageing

Each reconciling item is aged from its origination date (the date the transaction occurred on one side but not the other), not the date it was first noticed. Buckets: 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, over 90 days. Ageing is computed on gross items — debits and credits are aged separately even if they net to a small amount (topic pitfall 4). A reconciliation showing "net unreconciled ₹0.4 lakh" may hide ₹62 lakh of debits and ₹61.6 lakh of credits, each of which may be months old.

=LET(age, RecDate - [@OriginDate],
     IFS(age<=30,"0-30", age<=60,"31-60", age<=90,"61-90", TRUE,">90"))

Escalation thresholds

Escalation combines amount and age, aligned with close materiality (Topic 1 c03):

Condition Escalate to Required action
Any single item > clearly trivial (e.g., ₹40 lakh), any age Financial controller Root cause and clearance plan by next close
Items 31–60 days, aggregate > 25% of clearly trivial Financial controller Clearance plan
Items 61–90 days, any amount CFO Decision: clear, adjust or provide
Items > 90 days CFO + audit committee summary Write-off/provision or documented exception
Unknown bank debits, any amount CFO immediately Fraud check with bank

Escalation must result in a decision, recorded in the reconciliation, not just a notification.

Reconciliation status dashboard

A summary tab lists each account with: owner, risk rating, due date, prepared date, reviewed date, status (on time/late/not done), GL balance, unreconciled gross items by bucket, and escalations open. KPIs: % reconciliations prepared and reviewed on time (Topic 1 c02), value of items > 60 days, and number of open escalations. The Project 1 master check flag should fail if any high-risk reconciliation is not reviewed or any item exceeds 90 days without approval.

Illustration: at 31 March, 84 accounts are in scope. 79 are reviewed on time (94%); 3 are reviewed late; 2 are not prepared (a payroll clearing account and an EEFC account). Gross items > 60 days total ₹1.12 crore (₹0.74 crore in GST ITC pending reflection, ₹0.38 crore in unidentified receipts). Status: master check fails until the two missing recs are completed and the > 60-day items are approved by the CFO.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Auto-certified accounts: automated tools may auto-certify accounts meeting criteria (zero balance, no activity, balance matches sub-ledger); criteria must be approved and periodically tested.
  • Recurring timing items: unpresented cheques that clear in the first week each month are normal; the same cheque appearing for three months is not.
  • Reviewer override: a reviewer accepting an aged item must state why it remains valid (e.g., legal dispute) and a revisit date.
  • Late adjustments after review: if the GL balance changes after review (post-close entry), the reconciliation must be re-performed and re-signed.
  • Small entities: where one person prepares all recs, the CFO or an external accountant should review high-risk accounts monthly.

Key takeaways

  • Risk-rate every balance sheet account to set reconciliation frequency and reviewer level.
  • Preparers provide independent, complete, retained evidence; reviewers re-perform and challenge, after preparers and on time.
  • Age reconciling items gross from origination date; never net debits and credits.
  • Escalate on combined amount and age thresholds aligned with materiality; escalation must produce a decision.
  • Reconciliation status feeds the close dashboard and the Project 1 master check flag.

Practice Questions

Q1. Risk-rating exercise [L1]

Assign a risk rating, frequency and reviewer to each: EEFC USD account, GST ITC pending reflection, security deposits for leased premises, payroll clearing, provision for warranty, IC payable to Singapore subsidiary, share capital.

Q2. Netting exposure [L2]

A reconciliation for "GST input — under reconciliation" shows a net balance of ₹1.6 lakh, made up of 214 debit items of ₹48.3 lakh (ages: 0–30 days ₹31.0 lakh; 31–90 days ₹12.1 lakh; > 90 days ₹5.2 lakh) and 96 credit items of ₹46.7 lakh (0–30 days ₹40.2 lakh; > 90 days ₹6.5 lakh). With a clearly-trivial threshold of ₹25 lakh, determine the escalations required and explain why the net figure is misleading.

💡 Hint: Apply the thresholds to gross aged items on each side.

Q3. Draft a sign-off standard [L3]

Draft a two-page reconciliation sign-off standard for a group of four Indian entities, covering scope, risk rating, deadlines in the T+7 calendar, evidence requirements, ageing method, escalation matrix, system of record and the monitoring KPIs reported to the audit committee.

Q4. Review failure case [L2]

During IFC testing, the auditor finds that 9 of 40 sampled reconciliations were signed by the reviewer before the preparer, and that 5 had reconciling items older than 180 days with no comment. Evaluate the deficiencies and propose remediation.
