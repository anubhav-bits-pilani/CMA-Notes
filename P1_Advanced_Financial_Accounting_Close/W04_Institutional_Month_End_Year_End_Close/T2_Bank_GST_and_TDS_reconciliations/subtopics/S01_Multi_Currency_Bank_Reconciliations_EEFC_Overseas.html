Subtopic note

Multi-Currency Bank Reconciliations: EEFC, Overseas Accounts and FX

Scope: Multi-currency bank recs: EEFC and overseas accounts, Ind AS 21 closing-rate revaluation, realised vs unrealised FX, FIRC/BRC matching

Why it matters

Exporters, IT/ITeS companies and groups with overseas subsidiaries hold foreign-currency balances in Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency (EEFC) accounts in India and in accounts abroad. Each such account must be reconciled twice: in the transaction currency (does the USD balance per books equal the USD balance per bank?) and in the functional currency (has the INR carrying amount been revalued at the closing rate, with the difference in profit or loss?). Errors here are common because the bank statement only shows foreign currency, while the GL is in INR with a mix of historical rates. Export realisation evidence — FIRCs and BRCs — also links the bank rec to FEMA compliance, GST refund claims on zero-rated exports, and export incentive schemes.

Core notes

Account types

  • EEFC account: a foreign-currency account with an authorised dealer bank in India into which exporters and other foreign-exchange earners may credit up to 100% of their foreign-exchange earnings (FEMA framework and RBI Master Direction on deposits and accounts). Balances are non-interest-bearing current accounts and may be used for permitted current-account payments abroad.
  • Overseas bank accounts: maintained by Indian entities abroad (for branches, project offices or under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme/ODI rules for permitted purposes) or by foreign subsidiaries. Repatriation and reporting conditions apply under FEMA.
  • Foreign-currency loan and deposit accounts: packing credit in foreign currency (PCFC), foreign-currency term loans, fixed deposits.

Two-layer reconciliation

Layer 1 — foreign-currency reconciliation: identical to a normal bank rec (concept c01) but performed in FC units:

Item USD
Balance per bank statement, 31 March 4,82,350
Add: remittances in transit (credited by bank 2 April) 25,000
Less: outward payments recorded in books, not yet debited (12,400)
Adjusted bank balance 4,94,950
Balance per books (FC), after posting bank charges USD 150 and interest credit nil 4,94,950
Difference 0

Layer 2 — functional-currency (INR) revaluation: a foreign-currency bank balance is a monetary item. Under Ind AS 21.23(a), monetary items are translated at the closing rate at each reporting date, and exchange differences on settlement or retranslation are recognised in profit or loss (Ind AS 21.28), except for qualifying hedges and net investment items.

\[ \text{Revaluation gain/(loss)} = \text{FC balance} \times (\text{Closing rate} - \text{Carrying rate}) \]

where the carrying rate is the weighted rate at which the INR balance currently sits in the GL (concept c02).

Illustration: books carry USD 4,94,950 at ₹4,15,98,000 (weighted carrying rate about ₹84.045/USD). The closing rate (RBI reference rate or the policy rate, e.g., the bank's TT buying rate — choose consistently) is ₹85.50/USD. Revalued amount = 4,94,950 × 85.50 = ₹4,23,18,225. Unrealised gain = ₹4,23,18,225 − ₹4,15,98,000 = ₹7,20,225.

Realised vs unrealised FX

  • Realised exchange differences arise on settlement: e.g., an export receivable booked at ₹84.20/USD and realised into EEFC at ₹85.10/USD gives a realised gain of ₹0.90 per USD on the receivable. Conversion of EEFC USD to INR at a rate different from the account's carrying rate also produces a realised difference.
  • Unrealised differences arise from closing-rate retranslation of outstanding monetary balances at the reporting date.

Both go to profit or loss under Ind AS 21.28; the split matters for (i) management reporting and flux commentary (Topic 6 separates transactional FX from operating performance), (ii) tax — Income Computation and Disclosure Standard (ICDS) VI treats exchange differences on monetary items as income/expense of the year, broadly aligned with book treatment, and (iii) Schedule III, which requires net gain/loss on foreign currency transactions and translation (other than finance cost) to be disclosed in other income or other expenses, and exchange differences regarded as an adjustment to borrowing costs to be shown in finance costs (Ind AS 23.6(e)).

Many ERPs (SAP FAGL_FCV) post unrealised revaluation with automatic reversal on day 1 of the next period, so that realised gains are measured against the original transaction rate; others revalue without reversal and update the carrying rate. The rec must know which method the system uses, or the "carrying rate" will be misstated.

FIRC/BRC matching

When export proceeds are received, the bank issues an e-FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) or credit advice, and export realisation is reflected against the shipping bill in the RBI's Export Data Processing and Monitoring System (EDPMS). The electronic Bank Realisation Certificate (e-BRC), generated on the DGFT platform from bank data, evidences realisation for export incentives and GST refund documentation. Matching keys: shipping bill number and date, invoice number, buyer name and currency. Short-realisation due to bank charges or discounts requires write-off approval within RBI-permitted limits. Under the current FEMA export regulations, export value must be realised and repatriated within nine months from the date of export (fifteen months for goods exported to warehouses abroad); check current RBI directions, as timelines and the realisation framework have been revised from time to time.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Which closing rate: Ind AS 21 refers to the spot exchange rate at the reporting date; entities commonly use the RBI/FBIL reference rate or the bank's TT rate. Document the policy and apply it consistently across bank, receivables and payables.
  • Advances in foreign currency: an advance received from a customer or paid to a vendor is non-monetary (Ind AS 21.16 and Appendix B on advance consideration) and is not revalued — see topic pitfall 2.
  • Overseas account of a foreign subsidiary: translation of the subsidiary's statements into INR for consolidation uses Ind AS 21.39 (assets and liabilities at closing rate, differences to OCI as FCTR) — not the same as transaction revaluation in the entity's own books.
  • EEFC to INR conversion: the bank's conversion rate differs from the GL rate; book the difference as realised FX, not as a bank charge.
  • Unmatched FIRCs: remittances received without identifying the shipping bill sit as "unidentified receipts" (Topic 4 suspense) until mapped; they also block e-BRC generation.

Key takeaways

  • Foreign-currency bank recs need two layers: FC reconciliation and INR closing-rate revaluation.
  • Revaluation applies to monetary items only (Ind AS 21.23(a)); differences go to profit or loss (Ind AS 21.28).
  • Separate realised from unrealised FX for management reporting, while presenting both under Schedule III rules.
  • Know whether the ERP reverses unrealised revaluation; otherwise the carrying rate is wrong.
  • FIRC/BRC and EDPMS matching ties bank receipts to shipping bills and FEMA realisation deadlines.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-layer rec [L2]

EUR account per bank at 31 March: EUR 2,14,600. Books: EUR 2,10,250 carried at ₹1,91,32,750. Reconciling items: an outward payment of EUR 5,000 recorded on 31 March, debited by the bank on 2 April; bank charges of EUR 650 not yet booked. Closing rate ₹92.40/EUR. Prepare the FC reconciliation, the entry for bank charges (at closing rate) and the revaluation entry.

💡 Hint: Post the bank-only item first, then revalue the corrected FC balance.

Q2. Realised vs unrealised [L1]

An export invoice of USD 80,000 was booked on 12 February at ₹83.90. On 20 March, USD 50,000 was received into the EEFC account at ₹84.60. The closing rate on 31 March is ₹85.10. Compute the realised and unrealised FX on the receivable and on the EEFC balance (assuming no other EEFC movements), and state the Schedule III presentation.

Q3. FIRC/BRC exception report [L2]

Design an Excel exception report that matches 1,200 shipping bills with bank remittance data, flags short-realisations above USD 100, shipping bills outstanding beyond 240 days and remittances not mapped to a shipping bill. Specify the matching keys and formulas.

Q4. Policy judgement [L3]

A company revalues all USD balances, including advances from customers and advances to vendors, at the closing rate, and does not reverse revaluation entries. Evaluate the policy against Ind AS 21 and explain the corrections needed and the effect on realised FX reporting.
