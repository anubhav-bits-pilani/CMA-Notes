Subtopic note

Output Tax Reconciliation: Books, E-Invoice, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and DRC-01B/01C

Scope: Output rec: books vs e-invoice/IRN vs GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B; DRC-01B (liability mismatch) and DRC-01C (ITC mismatch) intimations

Why it matters

Output GST flows through four records that must agree: the sales ledger in the books, e-invoices registered on the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) with an Invoice Reference Number (IRN), the GSTR-1 statement of outward supplies, and the GSTR-3B return in which tax is actually paid. Any break in that chain shows up as revenue cut-off issues, under-paid tax with interest under Sec 50, or system-generated intimations (DRC-01B, DRC-01C) that block GSTR-1 filing if ignored. With GSTR-3B liability increasingly auto-populated from GSTR-1 and locked, the output rec has moved from "year-end GSTR-9 clean-up" to a monthly close control. The same rec is also the revenue completeness test auditors and GST officers both perform.

Core notes

The four-way chain

Record Source What it proves Typical breaks
Books (sales register + GST output ledgers) ERP Revenue and output tax recognised Revenue without invoice (unbilled), credit notes not reported, non-GST revenue
E-invoice / IRN IRP via ERP API or GSP Invoice registered with IRN and signed QR code; auto-populates GSTR-1 (B2B, exports, CDNR) IRN cancelled within 24 hours but invoice not reversed in books; invoices raised without IRN (invalid for B2B where e-invoicing applies)
GSTR-1 / IFF / GSTR-1A Portal Outward supplies reported for recipients' GSTR-2B B2C supplies not auto-populated; amendments in later periods; HSN summary errors
GSTR-3B Portal Tax paid (cash and credit ledgers) Liability under-reported vs GSTR-1; timing of amendments

E-invoicing applies to B2B supplies, exports and certain other documents of taxpayers whose aggregate turnover in any FY from 2017-18 exceeds ₹5 crore. Taxpayers with aggregate turnover of ₹10 crore and above must report e-invoices to the IRP within 30 days of the invoice date (from April 2025, per the GSTN advisory); verify the current thresholds before applying.

Reconciliation steps (monthly)

  1. Books → e-invoice: for every B2B and export invoice, check IRN exists and IRN status is active; list books invoices without IRN and IRNs without books invoices (cancelled IRNs not reversed, or invoices created outside the ERP).
  2. E-invoice → GSTR-1: e-invoice data auto-populates GSTR-1 tables 4A, 6A, 9B; compare auto-populated values with the ERP's GSTR-1 working; add B2C (tables 5, 7), nil-rated/exempt (table 8), advances (table 11) and HSN summary (table 12), document summary (table 13).
  3. GSTR-1 → GSTR-3B: GSTR-3B table 3.1 liability is auto-drafted from GSTR-1/1A; from the July 2025 tax period the auto-populated liability in table 3.1 is not editable in GSTR-3B (corrections go through GSTR-1A or later GSTR-1) — confirm the current hard-lock status.
  4. Books → GSTR-3B: output tax ledgers in the GL (CGST/SGST/IGST output) for the month should equal GSTR-3B liability ± timing items (advances, credit notes reported in later periods, amendments).

The bridge table:

Item ₹ lakh
Output tax per books (March) 1,248.6
Less: invoices dated 31 March reported in April GSTR-1 (cut-off error) (12.4)
Add: credit notes in books not yet reported in GSTR-1 0.0
Less: credit notes reported in GSTR-1, booked in April (3.1)
Add: tax on advances received (services) not in revenue 6.2
Output tax per GSTR-1 = GSTR-3B table 3.1 1,239.3

Each reconciling item needs an action: the 31 March invoices must be reported (and tax paid) for March with interest if paid late; credit notes booked in April but reported in March are a books cut-off error.

DRC-01B — liability mismatch (Rule 88C)

Where the output tax liability reported in GSTR-1/IFF exceeds the tax paid in GSTR-3B for a tax period by more than a notified threshold, the system issues an intimation in Form GST DRC-01B Part A (Rule 88C of the CGST Rules, introduced in 2022). The notified thresholds combine an absolute amount (₹25 lakh) and a percentage (20%) of the GSTR-1 liability — check the current construction of the test in the notification. The taxpayer must, within seven days, either pay the differential tax with interest through DRC-03 or explain the difference in Part B (e.g., GSTR-1 error to be amended, tax paid in an earlier period). If neither is done, filing of the next GSTR-1/IFF is blocked under Rule 59(6).

DRC-01C — ITC mismatch (Rule 88D)

Where the ITC claimed in GSTR-3B exceeds the ITC available in GSTR-2B for a tax period beyond a notified threshold (again an absolute and a percentage limit), the system issues Form GST DRC-01C Part A (Rule 88D, effective from the November 2023 period onwards). The taxpayer must, within seven days, reverse the excess ITC with interest through DRC-03 or explain in Part B (e.g., IGST on imports, RCM, ISD, ITC of earlier periods appearing in an earlier 2B). Non-response blocks GSTR-1 filing.

Close integration

  • The output rec runs at T+4 (books vs e-invoice vs GSTR-1 working) and again after GSTR-1 filing on the 11th (portal vs books).
  • GST output ledgers in the GL must be cleared to the GST payable/electronic ledgers each month; residual balances are aged (Topic 4 clearing accounts).
  • Annual: GSTR-9 (annual return) and GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement for turnover above ₹5 crore) reconcile audited turnover and tax with returns; monthly recs make GSTR-9C mechanical.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unbilled revenue: Ind AS 115 revenue recognised before invoicing does not create GST liability until time of supply (Sec 12/13); the book-GST difference is a timing item, but delayed invoicing beyond the prescribed time (Rule 47: 30 days for services) is a compliance issue.
  • Deemed supplies and stock transfers: Schedule I supplies (e.g., between distinct persons — branch transfers across states) attract GST without revenue in books.
  • Non-GST revenue: interest, dividends, sale of land — no output tax; exclude from the bridge but include in exempt/non-GST tables.
  • IRN cancellation window: IRN can be cancelled only within 24 hours; later corrections need a credit note.
  • Responding to DRC-01B/01C: an explanation that the difference relates to an earlier period must be supported; repeated unexplained intimations attract scrutiny under Sec 61.

Key takeaways

  • Output GST must reconcile across books, IRN, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B every month.
  • E-invoice data auto-populates GSTR-1, which drives GSTR-3B; editing of auto-populated liability is now restricted.
  • DRC-01B (Rule 88C) flags GSTR-1 liability above GSTR-3B; DRC-01C (Rule 88D) flags GSTR-3B ITC above GSTR-2B.
  • Both intimations require payment or explanation within seven days or GSTR-1 filing is blocked.
  • Monthly recs make GSTR-9/9C and audit procedures routine.

Practice Questions

Q1. Four-way chain breaks [L1]

For each situation identify which link of the chain breaks and the corrective action: (a) IRN generated, invoice deleted from ERP; (b) export invoice booked in books, missing from GSTR-1 table 6A; (c) GSTR-1 shows ₹18 lakh more tax than GSTR-3B for October; (d) credit note issued in books, not reported in GSTR-1.

Q2. DRC-01B response [L2]

For August, GSTR-1 liability is ₹3.42 crore and GSTR-3B liability is ₹2.81 crore. Investigation shows: ₹0.38 crore relates to invoices wrongly reported twice in GSTR-1; ₹0.23 crore is genuinely unpaid tax due to a missed branch sales register. Draft the Part B response and payment action, compute interest at 18% per annum for 45 days of delay on the unpaid tax, and state the GSTR-1 amendment required.

💡 Hint: Separate the reporting error (to be amended) from the genuine short payment (pay via DRC-03 with interest).

Q3. Build the output rec [L3]

Design an Excel/Power Query output reconciliation for a company with 3 GSTINs, 12,000 B2B invoices and 80,000 B2C invoices per month. Specify data sources (ERP sales register, IRP e-invoice download, GSTR-1 JSON, GSTR-3B), match keys, the bridge layout and the exception reports.

Q4. DRC-01C analysis [L2]

In GSTR-3B for December, ITC claimed is ₹5.10 crore; GSTR-2B shows ₹3.95 crore. Of the difference, ₹0.62 crore is IGST on imports (reflected in 2B in January due to late BoE data), ₹0.18 crore is RCM ITC, and ₹0.35 crore relates to invoices of suppliers who had not filed GSTR-1. Explain the expected intimation, the Part B response and any reversal required.
