Subtopic note

TDS Payable vs 24Q/26Q, Challans, TRACES Defaults and Section Remapping

Scope: TDS payable vs 24Q/26Q, challans, TRACES defaults, lower-deduction certificates; section remapping under Income-tax Act 2025

Why it matters

As a deductor, the company must deduct tax correctly, deposit it on time, file quarterly statements and issue certificates. Each failure has a direct cost: interest, late-filing fees, penalties, disallowance of the underlying expense (Sec 40(a)(i)/(ia) of the 1961 Act), and "assessee in default" demands raised by TRACES. The TDS payable reconciliation — GL vs challans vs quarterly statements vs TRACES — is a statutory dues control that auditors report on under CARO 2020 clause 3(vii). From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, and the section codes used in ERP tax configuration, vendor masters and returns must be remapped without breaking the reconciliation.

Core notes

The four-way TDS chain

Record What it shows Key checks
GL — TDS payable by section Tax deducted on vendor invoices, salaries, interest, rent Rate × base by section; no debit balances
Challans (via e-Pay Tax, CIN = BSR code + date + serial) Tax deposited by section/nature and minor head Deposited by due date; correct TAN, section and assessment/tax year
Quarterly statements — 24Q (salary), 26Q (resident non-salary), 27Q (non-resident), 27EQ (TCS) Deductee-wise details with PAN, section, amount, date, challan mapping All challans consumed; PAN valid; correct section codes
TRACES (processing, defaults, certificates) Short deduction, short payment, late payment/deduction interest, late filing fee, PAN errors Default summary nil; Form 16/16A generated

Due dates and costs (1961 Act framework, carried substantially into the 2025 Act)

  • Deposit: by the 7th of the following month; for tax deducted in March, by 30 April (Rule 30 of the Income-tax Rules, 1962).
  • Statements: Q1 by 31 July, Q2 by 31 October, Q3 by 31 January, Q4 by 31 May (Rule 31A).
  • Certificates: Form 16A quarterly within 15 days of the statement due date; Form 16 annually by 15 June.
  • Interest (Sec 201(1A)): 1% per month or part from date deductible to date of deduction; 1.5% per month or part from date of deduction to date of payment.
  • Late-filing fee (Sec 234E): ₹200 per day, capped at the TDS amount; penalty (Sec 271H) ₹10,000 to ₹1,00,000 for non-filing or incorrect filing (with relief if filed within one year with fee and interest paid).
  • Disallowance: 30% of the expense for resident payments (Sec 40(a)(ia)); 100% for non-resident payments (Sec 40(a)(i)) until TDS is paid.
  • Higher rates: Sec 206AA (no PAN, generally 20%); the higher-rate regime for non-filers under Sec 206AB/206CCA was omitted by the Finance Act 2025.

Monthly reconciliation

Example for tax deducted in February (deposit due 7 March):

Section Deducted per GL (₹) Challan deposited (₹) Challan date Difference Comment
194C 8,42,300 8,42,300 6 March 0 —
194J 21,16,800 20,96,800 6 March 20,000 Invoice booked late on 28 February, deduction missed in challan; pay with interest
194I 3,60,000 3,60,000 9 March 0 Late by 2 days → interest at 1.5% for one month (part month counts as a month)
195 6,20,000 6,20,000 6 March 0 Form 15CA/CB filed before remittance
192 (salary) 48,90,000 48,90,000 5 March 0 —

The quarterly check then ensures: Σ challans by section = Σ deductee rows in the statement; every challan is fully consumed; deductee PANs validated (TRACES PAN verification); lower-deduction certificates applied only within their validity, amount and rate.

TRACES defaults

After processing, TRACES issues an intimation (under Sec 200A) and a justification report listing defaults: short payment, short deduction (e.g., wrong rate, LDC exceeded), interest on late payment/deduction, late-filing fee, and PAN errors. The close treatment: download the default summary quarterly, analyse each line, and either (i) pay the demand, or (ii) file a correction statement (via the consolidated "conso" file) where the default arises from data errors (wrong challan tagging, PAN typo, wrong section). Book unavoidable interest as an expense (not deductible: Sec 40(a)(ii) treatment of interest on TDS is debated; many companies treat it as disallowable), and track open defaults in the statutory dues register used for CARO 3(vii).

Lower-deduction certificates (LDC)

A deductee (vendor) may obtain a certificate for lower or nil deduction (Sec 197 of the 1961 Act for residents; Sec 195(3) for non-residents), issued through TRACES with a specified rate, amount limit and validity period, addressed to a specific deductor's TAN. Controls:

  • Verify the certificate on TRACES before applying (certificate number, TAN, validity, threshold).
  • Configure the rate in the vendor master with an amount ceiling; once cumulative payments exceed the certificate amount, the normal rate applies.
  • Report LDC deductions with the correct remark code in 26Q/27Q; incorrect use creates a short-deduction default.

Section remapping under the Income-tax Act, 2025

The Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026) consolidates TDS/TCS provisions. In the enacted structure, salary TDS sits in Sec 392, most other TDS for residents and non-residents is consolidated into tabular entries in Sec 393, TCS in Sec 394, and lower/nil deduction certificates in a separate section of the same chapter; "tax year" replaces previous year/assessment year. The practical remapping work:

Area Action
ERP tax codes (withholding tax types/codes) Add new-Act section/table identifiers; keep old codes for transactions dated up to 31 March 2026
Vendor masters Map each vendor's nature of payment to the new table entry; re-verify thresholds
Statements and challans Use the forms and nature-of-payment codes notified under the new Rules for tax year 2026-27 onwards; Q4 FY 2025-26 statements filed in May 2026 remain under the 1961 Act
Reconciliation templates Add an "old section → new section/table entry" mapping column so multi-year ageing and defaults remain comparable
Disallowance and deduction rules Map 40(a)(i)/(ia) and 43B equivalents for tax computation

Verify each table serial number and form number against the enacted text, the Income-tax Rules notified under the new Act and CBDT mapping utilities before configuring; do not rely on memory or this note for exact numbers.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • TDS on year-end provisions: where payees are identifiable, deduct TDS on provisions (credit to "any other account" is a credit to the payee) and deposit by 30 April; otherwise accept Sec 40(a)(ia) disallowance risk (Topic 3 s07).
  • Sec 194Q vs 206C(1H): for purchases of goods above ₹50 lakh from a seller, buyer's TDS under 194Q takes precedence; ensure only one applies.
  • Non-residents and DTAA: Sec 195 deduction at treaty rate requires TRC, Form 10F and no-PE declaration; Form 15CA/CB for remittances.
  • Vendor credit notes after deduction: TDS already deducted and deposited is not refunded by reversal in books; adjust against future deductions for the same vendor within the year.
  • Challan errors: wrong assessment year or section on a challan can be corrected by the assessing officer or via the bank within limited windows — act within the quarter.

Key takeaways

  • Reconcile TDS payable GL to challans, quarterly statements and TRACES every month and quarter.
  • Know the deposit and filing due dates and the cost of failures: interest, 234E fees, 271H penalties, disallowances.
  • Analyse TRACES justification reports and file correction statements for data errors.
  • Apply lower-deduction certificates only within verified validity, rate and amount limits.
  • Remap ERP tax codes, vendor masters and templates to the Income-tax Act 2025 structure, keeping old codes for pre-April 2026 transactions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Interest and fees [L2]

TDS of ₹4,80,000 under the professional-fee section was deducted on 18 January and deposited on 12 March. The Q4 statement was filed on 20 July instead of 31 May. Compute interest under Sec 201(1A) and the late-filing fee under Sec 234E, and state whether Sec 271H penalty can be avoided.

💡 Hint: Interest is computed for each month or part of a month from the date of deduction to the date of payment.

Q2. Reconcile GL to statement [L2]

Q2 data: GL TDS payable credits — 194C ₹12.4 lakh, 194J ₹31.8 lakh, 194I ₹6.0 lakh; challans — 194C ₹12.4 lakh, 194J ₹30.9 lakh, 194I ₹6.0 lakh; 26Q statement rows — 194C ₹12.1 lakh, 194J ₹30.9 lakh, 194I ₹6.0 lakh. Identify the differences, likely causes and corrective actions.

Q3. LDC configuration [L1]

A vendor provides a Sec 197 certificate for 2% on payments up to ₹60 lakh, valid from 1 June to 31 March, addressed to your TAN. Payments in the year: ₹14 lakh in April–May, ₹52 lakh from June to December, ₹18 lakh from January to March. Determine the rates to apply to each tranche and the reporting codes required.

Q4. Remapping project [L3]

Your company uses SAP with 26 withholding tax codes under the 1961 Act. Plan the transition to the Income-tax Act 2025: inventory of codes, mapping approach, testing, cut-over for March 2026 vs April 2026 transactions, changes to reconciliation templates and controls for the first quarter under the new Act.
