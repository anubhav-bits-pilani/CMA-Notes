Key concept

TDS Receivable Bridge: Books to Form 26AS

Concept: TDS rec: TDS per books − Not in 26AS (customer default, PAN/TAN errors) + In 26AS not booked = TDS per 26AS

Statement

\[ TDS_{26AS} = TDS_{\text{books}} - N_{\text{default}} - N_{\text{error}} - N_{\text{timing}} + M_{\text{unbooked}} \]

where \(TDS_{\text{books}}\) is TDS receivable recorded for the year; \(N_{\text{default}}\) is TDS deducted per books but not reflected because the customer did not deposit or file; \(N_{\text{error}}\) is TDS not reflected because of the customer's PAN, TAN, section or challan errors; \(N_{\text{timing}}\) is TDS reflected in a different quarter or year; and \(M_{\text{unbooked}}\) is TDS in 26AS that the books have not recorded (receipts booked gross, TDS on income not yet recognised, or entries that are not ours). Only TDS reflected in 26AS and relating to income offered in the year is claimable in the return (Rule 37BA).

Explanation

The bridge serves three purposes. First, it quantifies credit at risk: \(N_{\text{default}} + N_{\text{error}}\) is TDS the company has suffered but cannot yet claim. Second, it drives follow-up: each item has an owner and customer action (file, deposit, correct). Third, it detects book errors: \(M_{\text{unbooked}}\) often reveals receivables still showing the TDS portion as due from the customer, overstating receivables and understating tax assets.

Practicalities:

  • Run the bridge quarterly after TDS statement due dates plus processing time (roughly 45 days after quarter-end) and annually before the tax return.
  • Match first by customer TAN and quarter, then by section; transaction-level matching only for large customers.
  • Tag every item; untagged residuals are not acceptable.
  • Items unresolved by the return due date feed the write-off policy (s06).

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, from tax year 2026-27 the statement names and section references change; the bridge logic is unchanged.

Illustration

Item ₹ lakh
TDS receivable per books, FY 2025-26 920.0
Less: customer default — 9 customers not filed Q4 (42.5)
Less: PAN error by 2 customers (11.8)
Less: reported in FY 2026-27 Q1 by customer (timing) (6.9)
Add: in 26AS, receipts booked gross (reclassify from receivables) 8.3
Add: in 26AS, TDS on advance received (income in FY 2026-27) 2.1
TDS per 26AS 869.2

Books entry for the gross-booked receipts: Dr TDS receivable ₹8.3 lakh, Cr Trade receivables ₹8.3 lakh. The ₹2.1 lakh is recognised as TDS receivable and carried forward for claim in the year the related income is offered (concept c05).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and tag [L1]

TDS per books ₹1,46,00,000; 26AS ₹1,31,40,000. Known: customer non-filing ₹9,80,000; wrong TAN by one customer ₹2,10,000; TDS on receipts booked gross ₹1,30,000; reflected in next FY ₹4,00,000. Prepare the bridge and identify any unexplained residual.

Q2. Process design [L2]

Design the quarterly TDS receivable reconciliation process for a company with 600 customers, including data extraction (26AS text/PDF or AIS JSON), matching levels, tagging taxonomy, customer communication templates and reporting to the CFO.

💡 Hint: Start at TAN × quarter level and drill down only where differences exceed a threshold.

Q3. Judgement — gross-booked receipts [L2]

Explain why receipts booked gross (TDS left in trade receivables) distort DSO, ECL and the tax computation, and how you would prevent it in the cash application process.
