Key concept

ITC Time Limit Under Sec 16(4)

Concept: ITC time limit: earlier of 30 November following FY or annual return filing (Sec 16(4))

Statement

Under Sec 16(4) of the CGST Act (as amended by the Finance Act 2022), a registered person cannot take ITC on an invoice or debit note after the earlier of:

\[ D_{\text{limit}} = \min\left(30 \text{ November following the end of the FY of the invoice},\; \text{Date of filing the annual return (GSTR-9) for that FY}\right) \]

The relevant FY is that of the invoice or debit note date (debit notes are linked to their own date since 2021). ITC must be claimed in a GSTR-3B filed on or before \(D_{\text{limit}}\).

Explanation

The limit forces year-end ITC clean-up. Invoices from vendors who report late (in GSTR-1 after the cut-off) and invoices booked late in the recipient's books both risk permanent ITC loss. Before 2022, the limit was the due date of the September return; the Finance Act 2022 extended it to 30 November.

Exceptions and nuances:

  • Re-availment of reversed ITC: ITC reversed under Rule 37 (non-payment) and re-availed on payment under the third proviso to Sec 16(2) and Rule 37(4) is generally understood not to be constrained by the Sec 16(4) limit, since it is a re-availment of credit originally taken in time; Rule 37A re-claims follow a similar logic. Confirm against current CBIC clarifications before relying on it.
  • Retrospective relief: Sec 16(5) (inserted by the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, effective retrospectively from 1 July 2017) allows ITC for FY 2017-18 to 2020-21 in returns filed up to 30 November 2021; Sec 16(6) gives relief where registration was cancelled and later revoked.
  • Early annual return filing: filing GSTR-9 before 30 November shortens the window — finish ITC clean-up first.
  • IMS pending: keeping a record pending in IMS does not extend the Sec 16(4) deadline.

Close implications: from September, run a "Sec 16(4) watchlist" of prior-FY invoices in books but not claimed, chase vendors, and decide by mid-November which ITC will be lost and must be expensed.

Illustration

Invoice dated 22 March 2026 (FY 2025-26), ITC ₹3.6 lakh. Supplier reports it in GSTR-1 for November 2026, filed on 11 December 2026, so it appears in the November 2026 GSTR-2B generated on 14 December. The FY 2025-26 deadline was 30 November 2026 (assuming GSTR-9 not filed earlier). ITC cannot be claimed; entry: Dr Purchase cost/expense ₹3.6 lakh, Cr GST ITC — pending reflection ₹3.6 lakh, and recover from the supplier under the contract's indemnity clause where possible.

If the supplier had reported in GSTR-1 for October 2026 (filed 11 November), ITC could be claimed in the October 2026 GSTR-3B filed by 20 November 2026.

Practice Questions

Q1. Deadline determination [L1]

For each, state the last date to claim ITC: (a) invoice dated 5 April 2025, GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 filed on 25 October 2026; (b) invoice dated 31 March 2026, GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 not filed until 20 December 2026; (c) debit note dated 10 May 2026 relating to an invoice of February 2026.

Q2. Year-end watchlist [L2]

At 1 October 2026, prior-FY invoices in books but not in 2B total ₹48 lakh ITC across 37 vendors: 22 vendors (₹29 lakh) have filed GSTR-1 late for March 2026 and should appear in October 2B; 15 vendors (₹19 lakh) are non-responsive. Draft the actions and decision timeline up to 30 November and the accounting for amounts lost.

💡 Hint: Work backwards from the October GSTR-3B due date and the 30 November limit.

Q3. Annual return timing judgement [L2]

The tax team wants to file GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 in September 2026 to "finish early". Explain the effect on Sec 16(4) and advise on timing.
