Key concept

Bank Reconciliation Identity

Concept: Bank rec: Balance per bank ± Unpresented cheques / deposits in transit = Balance per books after posting bank-only items (charges, interest, direct credits)

Statement

\[ B_{\text{bank}} - U + D \pm E_{\text{bank}} = B_{\text{books}}^{*} \]

\[ B_{\text{books}}^{*} = B_{\text{books}} - C + I + R \pm E_{\text{books}} \]

where \(B_{\text{bank}}\) is the closing balance per bank statement (credit balance shown as positive cash), \(U\) is unpresented cheques/payments recorded in books but not yet debited by the bank, \(D\) is deposits in transit recorded in books but not yet credited by the bank, \(E_{\text{bank}}\) are bank errors, \(B_{\text{books}}\) is the GL balance before adjustments, \(C\) is bank charges, \(I\) is interest credited, \(R\) is direct credits/receipts not yet in books (and direct debits enter with a negative sign), and \(E_{\text{books}}\) are book errors. \(B_{\text{books}}^{*}\) is the corrected GL balance, which is what appears in the financial statements.

Explanation

The reconciliation has two halves. Bank-only items — charges, interest, direct credits from customers, NACH debits, GST on charges — are items the company has not yet recorded; they must be posted in the books before the close, because they are real transactions of the period. Book-only items — unpresented cheques and deposits in transit — are timing differences that will clear in the bank later; they remain as reconciling items, and they are aged. Only after bank-only items are posted does the identity balance with zero difference.

Two rules make the identity reliable: (i) reconciling items are listed individually, never as a single balancing figure; (ii) bank-only items are posted, not carried. A rec that carries bank charges for three months as reconciling items is hiding an unrecorded expense. Stale cheques (beyond three months' validity) should be reversed to the liability, and unknown bank debits investigated immediately as potential fraud.

Where the account is overdrawn (bank shows a debit balance), the same identity holds with signs reversed; the corrected overdraft is reclassified to borrowings for Schedule III presentation (Week 4 Topic 5 reclassification logic).

Practice Questions

Q1. Prepare a bank rec [L1]

Bank balance ₹62,48,300 (credit); books ₹58,91,600 (debit). Unpresented cheques ₹7,12,000; deposit in transit ₹2,35,000; bank charges ₹4,720; NACH debit for insurance premium ₹1,18,000 not booked; interest credit ₹2,400. Prepare the reconciliation and the entries.

💡 Hint: Post bank-only items first; the difference should then be fully explained by timing items.

Q2. Spotting a hidden error [L2]

A reconciliation shows unpresented cheques of ₹14.6 lakh including a cheque of ₹3.2 lakh dated 18 months ago, deposits in transit of ₹9.8 lakh including a ₹6.0 lakh deposit recorded on 31 March that the bank credited on 9 April, and "other differences" of ₹0.85 lakh carried for five months. Identify the issues and the correct treatment of each.

Q3. Overdraft presentation [L1]

A cash credit account shows a debit balance of ₹4.2 crore per bank at 31 March; books show a credit balance of ₹4.05 crore with cheques of ₹0.15 crore issued but unpresented. Reconcile and explain the Schedule III presentation.
