Key concept

TDS Credit Matched to the Year Income Is Offered (Rule 37BA)

Concept: TDS credit claimed in the year the related income is offered to tax (Rule 37BA)

Statement

Under Sec 199 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 read with Rule 37BA(3) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962, credit for tax deducted at source is given for the assessment year for which the related income is assessable. Where income is assessable over more than one year, credit is allowed across those years in the same proportion as the income:

\[ \text{Credit}_y = TDS \times \frac{\text{Income assessable in year } y}{\text{Total income on which TDS was deducted}} \]

Credit is allowed on the basis of information in the department's records (Form 26AS/TRACES data) under Rule 37BA(2). Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (from tax year 2026-27), the equivalent principle applies with "tax year" terminology and renumbered provisions — verify the corresponding section and rule before citing.

Explanation

Timing differences between when the payer deducts TDS and when the payee offers income are common: customers deduct on advance payments, on provisional bills, or on accrual in their own books in March while the supplier recognises revenue in April; long-term contracts spread revenue over years. Claiming all TDS in the year of deduction mismatches credit with income; the system may allow it at processing, but it is wrong and leads to later adjustments, or, conversely, the credit is lost if not claimed in the later year.

Practical consequences for the close:

  • Maintain a TDS carry-forward register: TDS reflected in 26AS where the related income is deferred (contract liabilities, advances), with the expected year of income.
  • In the tax return, claim TDS for the current year from 26AS and the carry-forward register; schedule TDS2 (or its equivalent) captures TDS brought forward and carried forward.
  • Where income was offered in an earlier year and TDS appears in a later year's 26AS (customer deducted late), the 1961 Act provides relief through amendment of the assessment under Sec 155(20) (inserted by the Finance Act 2023), on an application in the prescribed form (Form 71) made within two years from the end of the financial year in which tax was deducted; check the 2025 Act equivalent and current procedure.
  • Accounting: TDS on deferred income is still a current tax asset; it is not written off merely because it cannot be claimed this year.

Illustration

A software company receives an advance of ₹2.0 crore on 20 March 2026 for implementation services to be delivered April–September 2026; the customer deducts 10% TDS = ₹20 lakh, reflected in the company's 26AS for FY 2025-26. Revenue of ₹2.0 crore is recognised in FY 2026-27. TDS credit of ₹20 lakh is claimed in tax year 2026-27 (the year the income is offered), carried forward in the TDS schedule of the FY 2025-26 return. If instead ₹1.2 crore of services were delivered in March 2026 and ₹0.8 crore in April, credit would be split ₹12 lakh (FY 2025-26) and ₹8 lakh (tax year 2026-27).

Practice Questions

Q1. Allocate TDS credit [L1]

A customer deducts ₹15 lakh TDS on a ₹1.5 crore milestone invoice raised on 25 March. Revenue recognised: ₹0.9 crore in March (current FY) and ₹0.6 crore in the next FY. Allocate the TDS credit to each year and describe the return disclosure.

Q2. Late reflection case [L2]

Income of ₹40 lakh was offered in FY 2024-25 (return filed), but the customer deducted and reported ₹4 lakh TDS only in FY 2025-26 Q2. Explain the options to claim the credit and the time limits that apply.

💡 Hint: Consider the rectification route introduced for TDS deducted in a later year than the income.

Q3. Register design [L2]

Design the columns and controls of a TDS carry-forward register that links 26AS entries to contract liabilities in the revenue waterfall, and explain how it ties to the Project 1 workbook checks.
