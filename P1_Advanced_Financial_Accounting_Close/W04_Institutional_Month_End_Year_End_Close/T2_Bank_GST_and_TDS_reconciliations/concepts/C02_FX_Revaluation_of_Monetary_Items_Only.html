Key concept

FX Revaluation of Monetary Items Only

Concept: FX revaluation (monetary items only) = FC balance × (Closing rate − Carrying rate); advances/prepayments not revalued

Statement

For each foreign-currency monetary item at the reporting date:

\[ \Delta_{\text{FX}} = Q_{\text{FC}} \times (r_{\text{close}} - r_{\text{carry}}) \]

where \(Q_{\text{FC}}\) is the outstanding balance in foreign currency (positive for assets, negative for liabilities), \(r_{\text{close}}\) is the closing (spot) rate at the reporting date and \(r_{\text{carry}}\) is the rate at which the item is currently carried in the functional-currency books (transaction rate or last revaluation rate). \(\Delta_{\text{FX}}\) is recognised in profit or loss (Ind AS 21.28). Non-monetary items measured at historical cost — including advances paid for goods/services and advances received from customers — remain at the transaction-date rate (Ind AS 21.23(b)) and are not revalued.

Explanation

Ind AS 21.8 and 21.16 define monetary items as units of currency held and assets and liabilities to be received or paid in a fixed or determinable number of units of currency: cash, bank, receivables, payables, loans, lease liabilities, accrued interest, deposits. The essential feature of a non-monetary item is the absence of a right to receive (or obligation to deliver) a fixed or determinable number of units of currency — prepayments for goods and services and advances against which goods/services will be delivered fall here. Appendix B to Ind AS 21 (Foreign Currency Transactions and Advance Consideration) confirms that, when advance consideration is paid or received, the date of transaction for the related asset, expense or income is the date of initial recognition of the non-monetary prepayment or deferred income; the later revenue or expense is recognised at that historical rate, without further exchange differences.

Judgement points:

  • Refundable deposits and advances that will be settled in cash (e.g., a security deposit refundable in USD) are monetary.
  • An advance that the counterparty is obliged to refund in cash if goods are not delivered remains non-monetary while the expectation is delivery; reclassify to monetary if it becomes a refundable claim.
  • Carrying rate depends on ERP method: if revaluation is auto-reversed on day 1, the carrying rate is always the transaction rate; if not reversed, it is the last closing rate.

Illustration

At 31 March, closing rate ₹85.40/USD:

Item FC (USD) Carrying rate Monetary? Revaluation (₹)
Trade receivable — US customer 2,50,000 84.10 Yes +3,25,000
Trade payable — German vendor in USD (1,20,000) 84.80 Yes −72,000
ECB loan (10,00,000) 82.50 Yes −29,00,000
Advance paid to Chinese vendor for machinery 3,00,000 83.70 No Nil
Advance received from Dubai customer (80,000) 84.20 No Nil

Net revaluation loss = ₹3,25,000 − ₹72,000 − ₹29,00,000 = −₹26,47,000 (to P&L; part of the ECB difference may be treated as a borrowing-cost adjustment under Ind AS 23.6(e) if conditions are met). Revaluing the two advances would wrongly add a gain of ₹5,10,000 on the vendor advance and a loss of ₹96,000 on the customer advance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Monetary or non-monetary [L1]

Classify and state whether to revalue: (a) USD security deposit refundable on lease end; (b) EUR advance paid for software licences to be delivered; (c) GBP accrued interest on a loan given; (d) USD customer advance for an export order; (e) USD lease liability under Ind AS 116.

Q2. Revaluation computation [L2]

At 31 March, closing rate ₹85.80/USD. Balances: receivables USD 6,40,000 at carrying ₹84.60; payables USD 2,10,000 at carrying ₹85.10; EEFC USD 1,25,000 at carrying ₹85.20; advance to vendor USD 90,000 at ₹84.00. The ERP auto-reverses revaluation on day 1. Compute the revaluation entry and state what the carrying rates will be for the April close.

💡 Hint: With auto-reversal, April revaluation starts again from transaction rates.

Q3. Appendix B application [L2]

An Indian company paid a USD 50,000 advance on 15 January (₹83.00) for consulting services received in March (rate ₹85.00 on the service date; ₹85.40 at 31 March). Explain the expense amount recognised in March and why no exchange difference arises.
