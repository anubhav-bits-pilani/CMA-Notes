Roadmap topic: Advanced Balance Sheet Reconciliations: Multi-currency bank recs, GST Input/Output vs GSTR-2B/3B ledger recs, TDS payable/receivable vs 26AS/AIS recs

Overview

Balance sheet reconciliations prove that every balance in the trial balance is supported by independent external evidence. Three families dominate the T+4 day of an Indian close: bank (including EEFC and overseas accounts in foreign currency), GST (input tax credit against GSTR-2B and the Invoice Management System; output tax across books, e-invoices, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B; reversals and electronic ledgers), and TDS (receivable against Form 26AS/AIS; payable against challans, quarterly statements and TRACES). Each has a well-defined external source of truth, a matching key, a bridge that explains differences, and a set of consequences — interest, demands, blocked filings, lost credits — when differences are not resolved.

For the target roles, these recs are where an audit background becomes a controller skill set. Auditors test them; controllers design, automate and sign them off. FP&A teams depend on them for cash-tax forecasts, working-capital analysis (ITC and TDS locked up) and FX exposure reporting. In interviews, a candidate who can explain why GSTR-2B must be matched on GSTIN plus normalised invoice key, why customer advances are not revalued, and how Rule 37BA affects TDS credit timing stands out from one who describes reconciliations generically.

The learning sequence: start with multi-currency bank recs (s01) and the bank identity (c01) with FX revaluation of monetary items (c02); move to automation (s02), which applies equally to GST and TDS matching. Then GST: ITC rec and IMS (s03, c03), output rec and DRC intimations (s04), reversals and ledgers (s05) and the Sec 16(4) time limit (c06). Then TDS: receivable against 26AS with write-off policy (s06, c04, c05) and payable against statements and TRACES, including the Income-tax Act 2025 remapping (s07). Finish with sign-off standards (s08), which define how all of these become auditable controls.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 21 monetary vs non-monetary and FX sensitivity

Ind AS 21 monetary vs non-monetary items; FX sensitivity disclosures → w3_t6

The bank rec's revaluation layer applies Ind AS 21's distinction: monetary items (cash, receivables, payables, loans) are retranslated at the closing rate, while non-monetary items (advances for goods/services, prepayments, inventory, PPE) stay at historical rates, with Appendix B fixing the transaction date for advance consideration. Week 3 Topic 6 covered Ind AS 107 market-risk disclosures; the same monetary-item inventory that drives revaluation is the base for FX sensitivity analysis (e.g., effect on profit of a 5% INR depreciation against USD). A clean, currency-wise list of revalued balances from the close therefore serves both the P&L entry and the disclosure note.

GSTR-9/9C and Sec 43B(h) MSME tracking

GSTR-9/9C reconciliation with audited FS; Sec 43B(h) MSME payment tracking from AP ageing

GSTR-9 (annual return) and GSTR-9C (self-certified reconciliation statement for turnover above ₹5 crore) reconcile audited turnover, tax liability and ITC with the year's returns. If monthly output and ITC recs are clean, GSTR-9C becomes an aggregation exercise. Sec 43B(h) of the 1961 Act (from AY 2024-25) allows deduction for amounts owed to micro and small enterprises only on payment within the MSMED Act Sec 15 time limit (15 days, or up to 45 days if agreed). The same AP ageing that drives Rule 37's 180-day ITC reversal can flag MSME invoices approaching their 43B(h) date — one dataset, two tax controls; map to the 2025 Act equivalent from tax year 2026-27.

CARO 2020 clauses 3(vii) and 3(ii)(b)

CARO 2020 clause 3(vii) statutory dues; clause 3(ii)(b) quarterly bank statements vs books (working capital limits > ₹5 Cr)

Clause 3(vii)(a) requires the auditor to report whether undisputed statutory dues (GST, income tax including TDS, PF, ESI, customs, cess) have been regularly deposited, and to list arrears outstanding for more than six months at year-end; 3(vii)(b) lists disputed dues not deposited. The TDS payable and GST recs are the evidence base. Clause 3(ii)(b) applies where the company has sanctioned working capital limits exceeding ₹5 crore in aggregate from banks or financial institutions on the security of current assets: the auditor reports whether quarterly returns or statements filed with banks agree with the books, with details of differences. Stock and debtor statements must therefore be generated from reconciled books, not estimates.

Excel/Power Query matching techniques

Excel/Power Query: anti-joins for 2B vs books, XLOOKUP on GSTIN + normalised invoice key, fuzzy matching

Left-anti and right-anti merges in Power Query produce the two unmatched lists (in books not in 2B; in 2B not in books) directly, after an inner join on GSTIN plus normalised invoice key produces matches. In Excel, XLOOKUP on a concatenated key (GSTIN & "|" & normalised invoice number & "|" & FY) gives a quick match flag. Power Query's fuzzy merge (with a similarity threshold and transformation table) helps for residual mismatches like "INV-045" vs "INV45", but fuzzy matches must be reviewed, never auto-accepted, and should always be constrained to the same GSTIN. The same techniques serve bank and 26AS matching.

Suspense ageing link to Topic 4

→ w4_t4: unreconciled items parked in suspense must be aged and cleared

Reconciliations often create temporary holding balances: unidentified bank receipts, ITC pending reflection, TDS under reconciliation, FX differences on IC. Topic 4 sets the zero-tolerance protocol for such clearing and suspense accounts — target nil at month-end, 0–30/31–60/>60-day buckets, mandatory escalation or write-off — and the Project 1 workbook flags any suspense balance as an error in the mapping engine (Topic 5). The sign-off standard in s08 and the suspense protocol in Topic 4 must use the same ageing method and thresholds.

Common pitfalls

Matching 2B on invoice number alone

Matching 2B on invoice number alone without GSTIN and normalised keys

The mistake: VLOOKUP of invoice number from books into GSTR-2B. Invoice numbers are not unique across suppliers ("1", "INV-001") and suppliers format them differently from how AP types them. This produces false matches (claiming ITC on another supplier's invoice) and false mismatches (chasing vendors who have filed). The correct method is to match on supplier GSTIN + normalised invoice number + FY, then check tax values, and to test for duplicate keys after normalisation. Detect it in review by sampling matched pairs where GSTINs differ or trade names differ, and by checking the number of many-to-one matches.

Revaluing foreign-currency advances

Revaluing foreign-currency advances (non-monetary) at closing rate

The mistake: the ERP's FX valuation run includes vendor and customer advance accounts (special GL indicators in SAP, prepayment accounts), so they are retranslated. It happens because advances sit in the same vendor/customer reconciliation accounts and the run is configured by account rather than nature. Under Ind AS 21.16 and Appendix B, advances for goods/services are non-monetary and stay at the payment-date rate. The fix is to exclude advance accounts (or special GL indicators) from the valuation run and to reverse any revaluation previously booked. Detect by reviewing the FX valuation log for advance accounts and by checking that expense/revenue recognised against advances uses the advance date rate.

Carrying TDS receivable without 26AS tracking

Carrying TDS receivable without tracking 26AS reflection, leading to write-offs at assessment

The mistake: TDS receivable grows year after year based on books, without periodic comparison to 26AS. The gap surfaces only at assessment or when the refund is processed, by which time customers' correction windows may have closed. People make it because TDS feels like a "tax team" item and the balance is small relative to revenue. The correct treatment: quarterly bridge (c04), tagging, follow-up and a write-off policy (s06), with Rule 37BA carry-forward items separately tracked. Detect by ageing TDS receivable by financial year and comparing each year's balance to the claim in the return and the processed intimation.

Netting reconciling items

Netting reconciling items so ageing disappears

The mistake: reconciling items are presented as a net figure ("other differences ₹0.6 lakh"), or old debits are matched off against unrelated credits of similar amount, so ageing reports look clean. It happens under pressure to show "no old items". The result is that genuine errors — unrecorded receipts, duplicate payments, missing ITC — persist for years. The correct approach is item-by-item listing with origination dates and gross ageing on each side (s08), with clearing only against genuinely matching items. Detect by asking for the gross composition of any net reconciling item and by checking whether old items were "cleared" by journal rather than by actual matching transactions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Integrated month-end reconciliation pack [L3]

Konkan Exports Pvt Ltd (FY ending 31 March; working capital limit ₹18 crore from a bank on the security of stock and debtors) provides the following for March 2026. Bank — EEFC USD: bank balance USD 3,12,400; books USD 3,05,900 carried at ₹2,57,10,895; inward remittance of USD 7,200 received on 31 March not booked; bank charges USD 700 not booked; closing rate ₹85.60/USD; customer advance of USD 40,000 received in February at ₹84.30 is sitting in a customer account included in the FX valuation run. GST: ITC per books ₹1.42 crore; GSTR-2B ₹1.33 crore; not in 2B ₹0.12 crore (₹0.07 crore timing, ₹0.05 crore vendor default); in 2B not in books ₹0.03 crore (genuine, invoices received 2 April). TDS receivable: books ₹38.6 lakh; 26AS ₹33.9 lakh; one customer (₹3.2 lakh) has not filed Q4; ₹1.5 lakh deducted by another customer on 31 March credits appears in that customer's FY 2026-27 Q1 statement; separately, ₹0.8 lakh appearing in both books and 26AS relates to an advance whose income falls in FY 2026-27. Required: (a) prepare the two-layer EEFC reconciliation and entries; (b) correct the FX treatment of the advance; (c) prepare the ITC bridge and state the ITC claimable in the March GSTR-3B; (d) prepare the TDS bridge with tags and the write-off/carry-forward decision; (e) state the CARO 3(ii)(b) implication if the March stock statement to the bank used pre-close figures.

Q2. Output tax and DRC intimations [L2]

For September, a company's books show output tax ₹6.48 crore; e-invoice register ₹5.92 crore (B2B and exports); B2C output tax ₹0.61 crore; GSTR-1 liability ₹6.53 crore; GSTR-3B liability ₹5.98 crore; GSTR-3B ITC ₹4.40 crore vs GSTR-2B ITC ₹3.61 crore. Required: (a) reconcile books to GSTR-1 and identify likely causes of the ₹0.05 crore difference; (b) assess whether DRC-01B and DRC-01C intimations are likely and prepare Part B responses; (c) compute interest at 18% per annum on any short payment for 40 days; (d) list control changes to prevent recurrence.

💡 Hint: Test both intimations against an absolute and a percentage threshold and separate reporting errors from real short payments.

Q3. TDS payable, LDC and 2025 Act transition [L3]

In Q4 FY 2025-26, TDS payable GL shows ₹1.84 crore; challans deposited ₹1.79 crore (one challan of ₹6 lakh deposited on 3 May for March deductions); the 26Q shows ₹1.77 crore with 14 PAN errors; a vendor's 1% LDC was applied on ₹2.1 crore though its limit was ₹1.5 crore. From 1 April 2026, the company must apply the Income-tax Act 2025. Required: (a) reconcile GL, challans and statement and compute Sec 201(1A) interest on the late challan; (b) compute the short deduction due to the LDC overrun at a normal rate of 10%; (c) outline the TRACES correction steps; (d) design the remapping plan for April 2026 transactions, including reconciliation template changes.

Q4. Automation and sign-off design [L3]

A group with 4 entities, 31 bank accounts (6 in foreign currency), 5 GSTINs and 2,400 vendors wants to complete all bank, GST and TDS reconciliations by T+4 with evidence suitable for IFC testing. Required: design (a) the data sources and import completeness checks; (b) the matching passes for bank, ITC and 26AS; (c) the reconciliation status dashboard and gross ageing; (d) the escalation matrix aligned with a clearly-trivial threshold of ₹30 lakh; (e) controls over matching-rule changes.
