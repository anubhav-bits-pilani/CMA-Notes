Key concept

Advertising and Promotion: Expense on Receipt, Prepaid Only for Advances

Concept: Advertising/promotion expensed when goods/services are received; prepaid only for advance payments (Ind AS 38.69–70)

Statement

Under Ind AS 38.69, expenditure on advertising and promotional activities (including mail-order catalogues) is recognised as an expense when it is incurred. Ind AS 38.69A clarifies timing: for the supply of goods, when the entity has a right to access those goods; for the supply of services, when the entity receives the services. Ind AS 38.70 states that Ind AS 38.69 does not preclude recognising a prepayment as an asset when payment for goods or services has been made in advance of the entity obtaining the right to access the goods or receiving the services.

\[ \text{Prepaid A\&P}_{t} = \text{Payments made} - \text{Value of goods accessed and services received to date} \]

Explanation

The rule prevents companies from deferring brand-building costs on the argument that they generate future benefits. A television campaign that has aired is an expense even if its effect on sales lasts a year; catalogues and brochures are expensed when the entity has the right to access them (typically on delivery by the printer to the entity or distributor), not when they are distributed to customers. Only a genuine advance — payment for media slots that have not yet aired, or for goods not yet delivered — is a prepaid asset.

Applications in Indian practice:

  • Media spends: an annual IPL sponsorship paid upfront is prepaid and expensed as the matches (services) are delivered — often heavily concentrated in April–May, not straight-line.
  • Celebrity endorsement fees: where the contract grants usage rights over a period, the fee is recognised over the period of services/usage rights; the portion paid for future periods is prepaid.
  • Point-of-sale material and samples: expensed when the entity has the right to access the goods, even if stored in a warehouse for later distribution (Ind AS 38.69A and the "right to access" concept).
  • Trade fairs and events: payments for future events are prepaid until the event.
  • Distinguish: consideration payable to customers (e.g., co-operative advertising allowances to distributors, slotting fees) is generally a reduction of revenue under Ind AS 115.70–72, not advertising expense.

Illustration

A consumer company pays ₹12 crore on 15 March 2026 for a television campaign: ₹8 crore for slots airing 1 April–31 May 2026 and ₹4 crore for slots aired 15–31 March 2026. It also received 5 lakh printed catalogues costing ₹1.5 crore on 28 March, to be distributed in April.

At 31 March 2026: expense ₹4 crore (aired) + ₹1.5 crore (catalogues — right to access obtained on delivery) = ₹5.5 crore; prepaid ₹8 crore (slots not yet aired), amortised as slots air in April–May based on the airing schedule.

Practice Questions

Q1. Expense or prepaid [L1]

For each, determine the 31 March treatment: (a) brochures delivered by the printer on 25 March, distribution in April; (b) advance to a digital agency for an April campaign; (c) radio spots aired in March, invoice received in April; (d) annual sponsorship of a cricket league paid in February for matches in April–May.

Q2. Endorsement contract [L2]

A company signs a celebrity for 2 years from 1 October 2025 for ₹6 crore, paying ₹4 crore on signing and ₹2 crore on 1 October 2026. The celebrity will shoot campaigns in October 2025 and October 2026, and the company has usage rights over the 2 years. Explain the recognition pattern and compute the balances at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: Identify what services are received and when, before choosing between straight-line and event-based recognition.

Q3. Revenue vs expense judgement [L2]

A company pays its modern-trade customers ₹3.6 crore for "in-store promotional displays" during the year. Explain how to determine whether this is an advertising expense or a reduction of revenue, citing Ind AS 115.
