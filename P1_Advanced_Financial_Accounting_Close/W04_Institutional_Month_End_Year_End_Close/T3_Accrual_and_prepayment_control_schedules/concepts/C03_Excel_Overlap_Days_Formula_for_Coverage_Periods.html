Key concept

Excel Overlap-Days Formula for Coverage Periods

Concept: Excel overlap days = MAX(0, MIN(Cover_end, Month_end) − MAX(Cover_start, Month_start) + 1)

Statement

For a coverage interval \([s, e]\) and a reporting interval \([a, b]\) (both inclusive dates):

\[ d = \max\left(0,\; \min(e, b) - \max(s, a) + 1\right) \]

where \(s\) is coverage start, \(e\) coverage end, \(a\) month (or period) start, \(b\) month (or period) end, and \(d\) the number of overlapping days. In Excel:

=MAX(0, MIN(Cover_end, Month_end) - MAX(Cover_start, Month_start) + 1)

with Month_start = EOMONTH(Month_end, -1) + 1.

Explanation

The later of the two starts and the earlier of the two ends bound the overlap. Adding 1 makes the count inclusive of both start and end dates. If the intervals do not overlap, the difference is negative or zero, and MAX(0, …) returns zero. The formula works for any interval lengths — a month, a quarter, a financial year or the "next 12 months" window used for the current portion (c06).

Implementation tips:

  • Store dates as true Excel dates (not text); use EOMONTH to generate month-ends.
  • For a full amortisation grid, put month-end dates across a header row and apply the formula across the grid; with dynamic arrays, a single formula can spill over all items and months:
=LET(ME, EOMONTH(StartMonth, SEQUENCE(1, 24, 0)), MS, EOMONTH(ME, -1) + 1,
     days, IF(Tbl[CoverEnd] < ME, Tbl[CoverEnd], ME) - IF(Tbl[CoverStart] > MS, Tbl[CoverStart], MS) + 1,
     amt, Tbl[Amount] * IF(days > 0, days, 0) / (Tbl[CoverEnd] - Tbl[CoverStart] + 1),
     ROUND(amt, 0))

Note that MIN and MAX aggregate whole arrays, so inside array formulas use IF comparisons (as above) instead of MIN/MAX to keep the element-wise behaviour.

  • Rounding: rounding each month separately can leave a few rupees at the end; put the residual in the final month (final month = amount − sum of previous months) so the tracker clears to exactly zero.
  • Check column: sum of the grid row = amount (for fully amortised periods) — a built-in integrity check.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the formula [L1]

Compute overlap days for coverage 10 March 2026–9 March 2027 with each period: (a) March 2026; (b) Q1 FY 2026-27 (1 April–30 June 2026); (c) FY 2025-26; (d) April 2027.

Q2. Build an amortisation grid [L3]

Write the Excel formulas (non-array and dynamic-array versions) to produce a 24-month amortisation grid for a table of prepaids with columns ID, Amount, CoverStart, CoverEnd, including a residual-in-final-month rounding rule and a row check that the grid sums to the amount.

💡 Hint: Generate month-ends with EOMONTH and SEQUENCE and replace MIN/MAX with IF comparisons inside arrays.

Q3. Debug a formula [L2]

A colleague's tracker uses =Amount*(MIN(CoverEnd,MonthEnd)-MAX(CoverStart,MonthStart))/(CoverEnd-CoverStart). Identify the errors, show their effect on a policy covering 1 April–31 March, and correct the formula.
