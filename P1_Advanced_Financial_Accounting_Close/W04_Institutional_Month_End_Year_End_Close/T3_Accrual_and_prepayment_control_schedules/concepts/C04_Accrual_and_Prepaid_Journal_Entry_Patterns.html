Key concept

Accrual and Prepaid Journal Entry Patterns

Concept: Entries: Dr Expense Cr Accrued expenses (auto-reverse day 1); Dr Prepaid Cr Bank, then Dr Expense Cr Prepaid monthly

Statement

Accrual cycle (auto-reversing):

Date Dr Cr
Period-end \(T0\) Expense \(A_p\) Accrued expenses \(A_p\)
Day 1 of next period Accrued expenses \(A_p\) Expense \(A_p\)
Invoice date Expense \(I\); Input GST \(G\) Vendor \(I + G\) (less TDS to TDS payable)

Net next-period expense = \(I - A_p\) (the true-up).

Prepaid cycle:

Date Dr Cr
Payment/invoice Prepaid expense \(P\); Input GST \(G\) Bank / Vendor \(P + G\)
Each month \(m\) Expense \(X_m\) Prepaid expense \(X_m\)

with \(X_m = P \times d_m / D\) (concept c02) and \(\sum X_m = P\).

Explanation

The two cycles are mirror images. An accrual recognises an expense before the invoice; a prepaid defers an expense after the payment. Both aim to put the expense in the period the service is received.

Auto-reversal keeps the accrual ledger clean: each month-end's accrual balance contains only that month's estimates, and the invoice is processed normally by AP without needing to know an accrual existed. The cost is that if an invoice is delayed beyond the next month, the reversal leaves the next period under-stated unless the item is re-accrued (s03). For balances built over time (bonus, audit fees), the non-reversing cumulative true-up pattern is used instead.

For prepaids, the initial debit must go to the prepaid (balance sheet) account, not to expense; AP coding rules route multi-period invoices to the prepaid GL above the threshold (s06). The monthly credit can be generated by the ERP (SAP recurring entries via FBD1 or accrual engine; Oracle recurring journals) or uploaded from the tracker. TDS, where applicable, is deducted at invoice or credit, whichever is earlier — including on prepayments, because payment triggers TDS.

Practical controls: accrual journals carry the reversal date in the header; a T+1 checklist step confirms that the reversal run completed; prepaid amortisation journals are reconciled to the tracker each month; and the prepaid GL equals the tracker's closing balance.

Illustration

AMC accrual of ₹3,00,000 at 31 March; invoice of ₹3,20,000 + GST ₹57,600 on 15 April, TDS 2% under the contracts section:

Date Dr (₹) Cr (₹)
31 Mar Repairs — AMC 3,00,000 Accrued expenses 3,00,000
1 Apr Accrued expenses 3,00,000 Repairs — AMC 3,00,000
15 Apr Repairs — AMC 3,20,000; Input GST 57,600 Vendor 3,71,200; TDS payable 6,400

April net AMC expense for March service = ₹20,000.

Annual insurance of ₹12,00,000 + GST ₹2,16,000 paid 1 April for April–March:

Date Dr (₹) Cr (₹)
1 Apr Prepaid insurance 12,00,000; Input GST 2,16,000 Bank 14,16,000
30 Apr Insurance 98,630 (30/365 × 12,00,000) Prepaid insurance 98,630

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Pass entries [L1]

A consultancy accrual of ₹4,50,000 is made at 31 March. The invoice of ₹4,20,000 + 18% GST arrives on 22 April; TDS at 10% on professional fees. Pass all entries and state the April P&L effect.

Q2. Prepaid with TDS [L2]

On 10 March, a company pays ₹24,00,000 + 18% GST in advance for a 12-month technical support subscription from 1 April, from a resident vendor; TDS at 2% applies. Pass the entries on 10 March, 31 March and 30 April, and explain why TDS is deducted in March although no expense is recognised until April.

💡 Hint: TDS is triggered by payment or credit, whichever is earlier.

Q3. Error diagnosis [L2]

In April the AMC expense line shows ₹6.2 lakh against a normal ₹3.1 lakh. The March accrual of ₹3.0 lakh was posted without a reversal date, and the April invoice for March services of ₹3.1 lakh was posted to expense. Explain the error, the correcting entry and the control that would have caught it.
