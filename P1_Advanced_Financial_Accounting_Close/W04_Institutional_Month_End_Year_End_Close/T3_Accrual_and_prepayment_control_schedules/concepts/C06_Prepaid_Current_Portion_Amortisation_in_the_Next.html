Key concept

Prepaid Current Portion: Amortisation in the Next 12 Months

Concept: Prepaid current portion = Amortisation falling within next 12 months

Statement

At reporting date \(R\), for a prepaid with unamortised balance \(B_R\):

\[ \text{Current}_R = \sum_{m \in (R,\, R + 12 \text{ months}]} X_m = P \times \frac{d_{(R,\,R+12]}}{D}, \qquad \text{Non-current}_R = B_R - \text{Current}_R \]

where \(X_m\) is the monthly amortisation (concept c02), \(d_{(R,\,R+12]}\) is the number of coverage days falling in the twelve months after \(R\) (computed with the overlap formula, concept c03), \(P\) is the prepaid amount and \(D\) total coverage days.

Explanation

Ind AS 1.66 classifies an asset as current when it is expected to be realised (or consumed) in the normal operating cycle, held primarily for trading, expected to be realised within twelve months after the reporting period, or is cash. A prepaid is "realised" by consuming the service, so the portion consumed in the next twelve months is current; the rest is non-current. Schedule III Division II presents prepaid expenses within "Other current assets" and "Other non-current assets" (with capital advances separately in other non-current assets).

Why it matters: multi-year insurance, multi-year SaaS and long-term licence prepayments can be large; classifying the whole balance as current overstates the current ratio (also a Schedule III ratio note item) and working capital. In the 3-statement model, the non-current portion is a long-term asset whose change runs through operating cash flow like other prepaids, but it should not be included in net working capital metrics used for DSO-style analysis.

The operating-cycle criterion rarely matters for prepaids; use the twelve-month test unless the entity's operating cycle is longer and the prepaid is part of it (e.g., a construction company with a three-year cycle — Ind AS 1.68), and disclose the cycle.

Illustration

Three-year cyber-insurance premium ₹3,65,00,000 (excl. GST) for 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2028 (1,096 days); daily rate ₹33,303 (rounded).

At 31 March 2026: days consumed = 274 (1 July 2025–31 March 2026); balance = ₹3,65,00,000 × (1,096 − 274)/1,096 = ₹2,73,75,000. Days in next 12 months (1 April 2026–31 March 2027) = 365; current portion = ₹3,65,00,000 × 365/1,096 = ₹1,21,55,566; non-current = ₹1,52,19,434.

At 31 March 2027: balance = ₹1,52,19,434; days remaining = 457 (1 April 2027–30 June 2028, including 29 February 2028); next 12 months = 366 days (1 April 2027–31 March 2028, a leap year span); current = ₹3,65,00,000 × 366/1,096 = ₹1,21,88,869; non-current = ₹30,30,565.

Connections

Uses c02 and c03; applied in the s05 tracker. Links to Week 1 Topic 1 (current/non-current classification under Ind AS 1 and Schedule III) and Topic 5 mapping attributes (current/non-current flag). In modelling, it affects the balance sheet forecast of other non-current assets and the current ratio.

Practice Questions

Q1. Split a balance [L1]

A two-year AMC paid in advance: ₹14,60,000 for 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2027 (730 days). Compute the balance at 31 March 2026 and its current and non-current portions.

Q2. Portfolio split in Excel [L2]

Write the Excel formula to compute the current portion for each item in a prepaid tracker at a reporting date held in cell RepDate, using the overlap-days logic, and a check that current + non-current equals the closing balance.

💡 Hint: Use RepDate + 1 as the window start and EDATE(RepDate, 12) as the window end.

Q3. Ratio effect [L2]

A company classified its entire ₹18 crore prepaid balance (including ₹11 crore relating to years 2–5 of a five-year insurance programme) as current. Current assets were ₹240 crore and current liabilities ₹200 crore. Compute the current ratio before and after correction and discuss the Schedule III ratio note implications if the change from the prior year exceeds 25%.
