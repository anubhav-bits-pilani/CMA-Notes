Key concept

Prepaid Monthly Charge by Coverage Days

Concept: Prepaid monthly charge = Amount × Days of coverage in month ÷ Total coverage days

Statement

\[ X_m = P \times \frac{d_m}{D} \]

where \(X_m\) is the amortisation charge for month \(m\); \(P\) is the prepaid amount capitalised (excluding recoverable GST, at the payment-date rate for foreign-currency payments); \(d_m\) is the number of days of the coverage period falling within month \(m\) (concept c03); and \(D\) is the total number of days in the coverage period, inclusive of start and end dates. Over the full coverage period, \(\sum_m X_m = P\).

Explanation

Daily pro-rating is the most neutral pattern for time-based services (insurance, subscriptions, AMCs, licences) because the service is consumed evenly through time. It handles coverage periods that start mid-month, leap years and odd-length periods without special cases. Equal monthly instalments (\(P / 12\)) are a common simplification when coverage aligns with calendar months; the difference is small but can matter at a month-end with a large policy, and mixed methods across a tracker complicate tie-outs — choose one and apply it consistently.

The base \(P\) must be correct: recoverable GST is excluded (it is an ITC asset); non-recoverable GST, stamp duty or brokerage directly attributable to the service are included. Amortisation starts at coverage start, not payment date (topic pitfall 3). If the coverage period changes (extension, cancellation), the remaining balance is amortised over the remaining revised period prospectively (change in estimate) unless the original dates were simply recorded wrongly (error correction).

A non-straight-line pattern is used only when the service is clearly not consumed evenly — e.g., a seasonal maintenance contract where most visits occur in the monsoon months — and should be documented.

Illustration

Group health insurance premium ₹1,46,00,000 for 10 February 2026 to 9 February 2027 (365 days). Daily rate = ₹40,000.

Month Coverage days \(d_m\) Charge \(X_m\) (₹)
February 2026 19 (10–28 Feb) 7,60,000
March 2026 31 12,40,000
April 2026 30 12,00,000
February 2027 9 (1–9 Feb) 3,60,000

At 31 March 2026, amortised = 50 days × ₹40,000 = ₹20,00,000; balance = ₹1,26,00,000. Equal-instalment monthly amortisation (₹12,16,667) would over-charge February by ₹4,56,667.

Connections

The days count \(d_m\) is computed by the Excel overlap formula (c03); the entries are in c04; the current/non-current split is c06. Used in s05 (tracker) and s06 (threshold aggregate test). The same pro-rating logic applies to accruals spanning partial months (c01) and to the capitalised contract cost tracker of Week 1.

Practice Questions

Q1. Monthly charges [L1]

A software subscription of ₹9,13,000 covers 20 March 2026 to 19 March 2027 (365 days). Compute the charges for March 2026, April 2026 and March 2027, and the balance at 31 March 2026.

Q2. Coverage extension [L2]

An annual AMC prepaid of ₹6,00,000 for 1 July 2025–30 June 2026 is extended on 1 January 2026 at no extra cost to 30 September 2026. Compute the monthly charge before and after the extension and explain whether the change is an estimate change or an error correction.

💡 Hint: Spread the unamortised balance at the date of change over the remaining revised coverage days.

Q3. Choice of method [L2]

Compare daily pro-rating and equal monthly instalments for a ₹2.4 crore annual D&O policy starting 18 November. Quantify the difference in November and December charges and recommend a policy for a company with 300 prepaid items.
