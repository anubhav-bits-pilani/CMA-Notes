Subtopic note

Accruals vs Provisions (Ind AS 37.11) and Schedule III Placement

Scope: Accruals vs provisions (Ind AS 37.11) and Schedule III placement

Why it matters

The words "provision" and "accrual" are used loosely in Indian practice — many ledgers are named "Provision for expenses" even when they hold routine accruals. The distinction matters for measurement (provisions require best-estimate and, where material, discounting under Ind AS 37; accruals are measured at the amount due), for disclosure (provisions need a movement reconciliation under Ind AS 37.84–85; accruals do not), for Schedule III classification (trade payables, other financial liabilities or provisions), and for ratios (current ratio, debt definitions in covenants). Misclassification is a frequent review comment on draft financial statements and creates noise in the mapping engine (Topic 5).

Core notes

The Ind AS 37 distinction

Ind AS 37.10 defines a provision as a liability of uncertain timing or amount. Ind AS 37.11 distinguishes provisions from other liabilities such as trade payables and accruals because there is uncertainty about the timing or amount of the future expenditure required in settlement:

  • Trade payables are liabilities to pay for goods or services received or supplied that have been invoiced or formally agreed with the supplier.
  • Accruals are liabilities to pay for goods or services received or supplied that have not been paid, invoiced or formally agreed with the supplier, including amounts due to employees (for example, amounts relating to accrued vacation pay). Although it is sometimes necessary to estimate the amount or timing of accruals, the uncertainty is generally much less than for provisions.
  • Ind AS 37.11 also notes that accruals are often reported as part of trade and other payables, whereas provisions are reported separately.

A provision is recognised (Ind AS 37.14) when there is a present obligation (legal or constructive) from a past event, an outflow is probable, and a reliable estimate can be made. Measurement is the best estimate (Ind AS 37.36), with risk and uncertainty considered (37.42) and discounting where the time value is material (37.45).

Decision table

Question Accrual Provision
Has the good/service been received? Yes Not necessarily (obligation can arise from other past events — warranty, litigation, restructuring)
Is the counterparty and amount broadly known? Yes — contract rate, usage, PO No — significant uncertainty in timing/amount

Bonus under Ind AS 19 is not within Ind AS 37 scope (Ind AS 37.5 excludes items covered by other standards); employee benefit obligations are presented according to their nature, as discussed below.

Schedule III placement

Schedule III Division II (Ind AS) requires classification on the face of the balance sheet into financial liabilities (borrowings, lease liabilities, trade payables, other financial liabilities), provisions, and other liabilities, each split into current and non-current.

Item Placement Reason
Accrued expenses for goods/services from suppliers (invoiced or not) Trade payables (with MSME/others split and ageing) ICAI Guidance Note on Division II: amounts due for goods purchased or services received in the normal course are trade payables; GRNI and service accruals fall here
Interest accrued on borrowings Other financial liabilities Contractual obligation to pay cash; Schedule III lists "interest accrued" under other financial liabilities
Unpaid dividends, capital creditors, security deposits received, payables for purchase of PPE Other financial liabilities Contractual cash obligations not arising from purchases in the normal operating course (capital creditors)
Salaries, wages, bonus payable with determinable amounts Commonly "Employee benefits payable" in other financial liabilities, or in current provisions — policy choice applied consistently Ind AS 32.4(b) excludes employers' obligations under employee benefit plans (to which Ind AS 19 applies) from Ind AS 32 scope, so classifying them as "financial liabilities" is a presentation convention rather than a strict Ind AS 32/109 classification; many Indian companies present gratuity and leave as provisions and salaries/bonus payable as other financial liabilities
Gratuity, compensated absences (actuarially measured) Provisions — employee benefits (current/non-current split per actuarial report) Uncertain amount and timing
Warranty, litigation, onerous contracts, restoration Provisions — others Ind AS 37 provisions
Statutory dues payable (GST, TDS, PF) Other current liabilities Non-contractual — arise from statute, so not financial liabilities under Ind AS 32
Advances from customers / contract liabilities Other current liabilities Non-financial — settled by goods/services

The ICAI Guidance Note on Division II also expects the trade payables note to show amounts due to micro and small enterprises separately and the ageing schedule (from the 2021 amendments), which applies to accrued trade payables too — include "not due" and "unbilled" categories as appropriate.

Mapping implications

In the mapping engine (Topic 5), the GL ledger name does not determine classification: a ledger called "Provision for expenses" should usually map to trade payables (accruals), and "Provision for bonus" to employee benefits payable or provisions per policy. Map at the level of sub-ledgers or accrual categories (from the accrual taxonomy, s01) so that Schedule III placement is attribute-driven, not name-driven.

Illustration

GL "Provisions — expenses" at 31 March: ₹14.6 crore, composed of:

Component ₹ crore Classification
GRNI and service accruals (suppliers) 8.9 Trade payables (MSME ₹1.4 crore, others ₹7.5 crore)
Interest accrued on NCDs 1.2 Other financial liabilities (current)
Bonus payable (formula-based) 2.6 Employee benefits payable (other financial liabilities per company policy)
Warranty provision 1.5 Provisions — current (₹0.9 crore) and non-current (₹0.6 crore)
Litigation provision 0.4 Provisions — current

Only ₹1.9 crore is a provision under Ind AS 37 requiring a movement table; reporting the full ₹14.6 crore as "provisions" would overstate provisions, understate trade payables (and DPO in Topic 6), and omit the MSME disclosure for ₹1.4 crore.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disputed supplier claims: the undisputed portion is a trade payable; a probable additional outflow for the disputed part is a provision; a possible outflow is a contingent liability.
  • Volume rebates payable to customers: refund liabilities under Ind AS 115 — generally other financial liabilities (if cash) or as presented by policy, not Ind AS 37 provisions.
  • Accruals in long-term contracts: retention money payable to contractors is a financial liability (capital creditor or trade payable), possibly non-current.
  • Restructuring: a restructuring provision is recognised only when a detailed formal plan has been announced or begun (Ind AS 37.72); accruing redundancy costs earlier is not permitted.
  • Covenant definitions: loan agreements may define debt to include certain "other financial liabilities"; classification affects covenant ratios — check definitions.

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 37.11: accruals have much less uncertainty than provisions and are usually reported in trade and other payables.
  • Provisions require recognition criteria, best-estimate measurement and a movement disclosure; accruals do not.
  • Supplier accruals map to trade payables (with MSME split and ageing); interest accrued and capital creditors to other financial liabilities.
  • Employee benefit obligations are outside Ind AS 32; present salaries/bonus payable and actuarial obligations consistently per policy.
  • Map by accrual category, not by ledger name, so Schedule III placement is driven by attributes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify liabilities [L1]

Classify under Schedule III, with reasons: (a) accrued freight for March, invoice pending; (b) interest accrued but not due on term loans; (c) provision for warranty for 2 years; (d) leave encashment per actuarial valuation; (e) GST payable for March; (f) payable to a contractor for factory building (capital creditor); (g) estimated penalty in a probable regulatory dispute.

Q2. Decompose a "provision" ledger [L2]

A GL "Provision for expenses" of ₹6.8 crore contains: supplier accruals ₹3.9 crore (of which ₹0.7 crore to micro enterprises), interest accrued ₹0.6 crore, bonus payable ₹1.2 crore, onerous lease-service contract ₹0.8 crore (₹0.5 crore payable after 12 months), and a ₹0.3 crore estimate for a customer claim that is possible but not probable. Prepare the Schedule III classification and identify any item that should not be recognised.

💡 Hint: Test each item against Ind AS 37.14 and 37.11 before choosing a line.

Q3. Mapping design [L2]

Explain how you would design mapping attributes in the TB-to-Schedule III engine so that accrual and provision ledgers are classified correctly, including the fields you would add to the accrual schedule.

Q4. Interview question — disclosure impact [L3]

"Why does it matter if we show accruals as provisions? The total liabilities are the same." Answer with reference to Ind AS 37 disclosures, MSME and ageing disclosures, ratio and covenant effects, and audit review.
