Subtopic note

Auto-Reversing Accruals, True-ups and Accrual-to-Actual Tracking

Scope: Auto-reversing accruals, true-ups and accrual-to-actual variance tracking

Why it matters

An accrual is a temporary bridge until the invoice arrives. If the bridge is not removed when the invoice posts, the expense is counted twice; if it is removed in the wrong period, the expense swings. Auto-reversal and true-up mechanics are the two standard ways of managing that bridge, and accrual-to-actual tracking measures whether estimates are any good. Poor estimates are not harmless: systematic over-accrual creates hidden reserves that can be released to smooth profit (an earnings-management red flag under SA 240), while under-accrual creates surprises in the next month's flux. Controllers use these mechanics to keep the monthly P&L stable; FP&A uses accrual accuracy to decide how much to trust soft-close numbers.

Core notes

Method 1 — auto-reversing accruals

At T0 the accrual is posted with a reversal date of day 1 of the next period. When the invoice arrives, it is posted normally to expense. The net effect in the next period is invoice minus reversed accrual — the accrual-to-actual difference.

Date Entry Dr (₹) Cr (₹)
31 March Dr Security expense, Cr Accrued expenses (accrual, reversal date 1 April) 18,80,000 18,80,000
1 April Dr Accrued expenses, Cr Security expense (auto-reversal) 18,80,000 18,80,000
12 April Dr Security expense ₹19,10,000, Dr Input GST ₹3,43,800, Cr Vendor ₹22,53,800 (invoice for March services) 22,53,800 22,53,800

April P&L effect from March services = −18,80,000 + 19,10,000 = ₹30,000 (the true-up, recognised in April). This is the cleanest approach for usage and service accruals where each month's invoice relates to one period. In SAP, FBS1 posts an accrual/deferral document with a reversal date and F.81 (or the S/4 equivalent) mass-reverses on day 1; Oracle uses reversing journal categories; Tally supports reversing journals via scheduled vouchers or manual reversal.

Method 2 — cumulative true-up (non-reversing)

For balances that build up over a performance period (bonus, incentives, audit fees, warranty-type estimates), the accrual is not reversed each month. Instead, each month the required closing balance is recomputed and the GL is adjusted by the difference:

\[ \text{Charge}_m = \text{Required balance}_m - \text{Balance before adjustment}_m \]

where the required balance is the best estimate of the obligation at month-end (e.g., expected annual bonus × months elapsed ÷ 12, less amounts paid). This avoids monthly reversal noise and makes the balance itself reviewable.

Method 3 — cleared on invoice (standing accrual with matching)

Some ERPs match the invoice against the specific accrual (e.g., GR/IR for GRNI; "accrual clearing" against a PO or contract). The invoice clears the accrual and only differences hit expense. GRNI is always handled this way via three-way match.

True-ups

A true-up is the correction of an estimate when better information arrives. Good practice:

  • Post true-ups in the period the information becomes available (change in estimate under Ind AS 8.36 — prospective, not a prior-period error, unless the original estimate ignored information available at the time).
  • Tag true-ups separately (e.g., a "true-up" flag or GL sub-account) so flux commentary can separate run-rate movements from estimate corrections.
  • Quarter-end and year-end true-ups are larger because hard-close procedures use actual data (Topic 1 c05); disclose large true-ups in commentary.

Accrual-to-actual variance tracking

For each accrual ID, compare the accrual with the invoice that eventually arrives for the same period:

\[ \text{A2A variance} = \text{Actual invoiced for period} - \text{Accrual for period}, \qquad \text{A2A \%} = \frac{\text{A2A variance}}{\text{Accrual for period}} \times 100 \]

Accrual ID Period Accrued (₹ lakh) Actual (₹ lakh) Variance % Flag
ACR-UTIL-P2 Feb 35.2 36.0 0.8 2.3% —
ACR-SEC-ALL Feb 18.6 18.9 0.3 1.6% —
ACR-LEGAL-ARB Feb 8.0 14.6 6.6 82.5% Review basis
ACR-FREIGHT Feb 22.4 17.1 (5.3) (23.7%) Over-accrual
Total 84.2 86.6 2.4 2.9%

Interpretation: aggregate accuracy looks fine (2.9%), but two lines are poor — the legal accrual is consistently under-estimated (partner estimates should be updated mid-month), and freight is over-accrued (trips double-counted with a direct-billing vendor). Track by line and by owner over several months; persistent bias in one direction is a control issue.

Excel approach: a separate table of invoices tagged with accrual ID and service period, then

=SUMIFS(Invoices[Amount], Invoices[AccrualID], [@AccrualID], Invoices[ServicePeriod], [@Period]) - [@Accrued]

with conditional formatting where ABS(variance) exceeds both 10% and ₹1 lakh.

Unmatched reversals

If the invoice does not arrive in the month after reversal, the expense for that period is understated (reversal credit with no invoice). The template should carry such items forward by re-accruing them at the next month-end ("re-accrual of open items"), and the A2A tracker should show the invoice as pending rather than a 100% favourable variance.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Invoice arrives before T0 for the same period: the accrual must be reduced by the invoiced amount (c01) or the expense is doubled before reversal even happens.
  • Invoice covering multiple months: a quarterly CAM invoice in April for January–March clears three monthly accruals; reversal method requires all three to have reversed — cumulative true-up may be cleaner.
  • Reversal date in a closed period: if April is not yet open when F.81 runs, reversals fail silently; check the reversal log as part of the T+1 checklist.
  • Year-end: auditors examine April invoices for March services (search for unrecorded liabilities); a strong A2A history reduces audit adjustments.
  • Tax: auto-reversed provisions still raise TDS questions (s07): reversal in books does not undo the TDS obligation if the provision was a credit to the payee's account.

Key takeaways

  • Auto-reversal suits usage and service accruals; cumulative true-up suits balances built over a performance period; GRNI clears via GR/IR.
  • True-ups are changes in estimates, recognised prospectively and tagged for commentary.
  • Track accrual-to-actual variance by line and owner to detect bias, not just aggregate accuracy.
  • Re-accrue items whose invoices have not arrived after reversal.
  • Check reversal logs on T+1 — failed reversals are a common cause of double expense.

Practice Questions

Q1. Journal entries with auto-reversal [L1]

On 31 March, a ₹6,40,000 accrual is posted for March housekeeping. The invoice for ₹6,72,000 plus 18% GST arrives on 9 April. Pass all entries from 31 March to 9 April and state the P&L effect in March and in April.

Q2. Cumulative true-up for audit fees [L2]

The statutory audit fee for FY 2025-26 is ₹60 lakh per the engagement letter. The company accrues on a straight-line basis, and ₹15 lakh was invoiced in December for interim work. In February, the auditor agrees an additional ₹8 lakh for expanded IFC testing. Compute the monthly charges for February and March under cumulative true-up and the closing accrual at 31 March.

💡 Hint: Recompute the required balance each month from the revised total fee and months elapsed, then deduct amounts invoiced.

Q3. A2A analysis [L2]

Six months of A2A data for the logistics accrual show variances of +12%, +9%, +14%, +11%, +10% and +13% (actual above accrual). Explain what the pattern indicates, its effect on monthly results, and how you would correct the methodology.

Q4. Failed reversal case [L3]

On T+1 of the April close, the reversal log shows that 37 accruals totalling ₹2.84 crore from March failed to reverse because April had not been opened for GL postings when F.81 ran. April invoices of ₹1.96 crore for March services have since been posted. Describe the impact on the April and March financial statements, the correcting entries and the control changes.
