Subtopic note

Prepaid Expense Trackers and the Current vs Non-Current Split

Scope: Prepaid trackers: insurance, software subscriptions, AMC, licence fees; current vs non-current split

Why it matters

Annual and multi-year payments in advance — insurance premiums, SaaS subscriptions, AMCs billed upfront, licence fees — are common, and if they are expensed when paid the P&L lurches: a ₹3 crore insurance renewal in October makes October look like a loss month. A prepaid tracker spreads each payment over its coverage period, gives a balance that can be reconciled to the GL every month, and splits that balance between current and non-current assets for Schedule III. It is also an audit favourite: auditors test prepaid balances by recomputing amortisation from coverage dates, and errors in start dates (topic pitfall 3) are easy to find.

Core notes

What qualifies as a prepaid

A prepayment is an asset when the entity has paid for goods or services before receiving them: it has a right to receive future services (an asset under the Conceptual Framework; for services, Ind AS 38.70 explicitly permits recognising a prepayment for advertising/promotional activities paid in advance — see concept c05). The asset is non-monetary (not revalued for FX, Topic 2 c02) and non-financial (not a financial asset under Ind AS 32, because it will be settled by services, not cash). Expense is recognised as the services are received — normally straight-line over the coverage period unless the pattern of service is clearly different.

Common prepaid categories

Category Typical terms Amortisation pattern Notes
Insurance (fire, marine, D&O, group health, key-man) Annual, sometimes 3-year (e.g., long-term fire policies) Straight-line over policy period by days Group health premium is an employee benefit cost; marine cargo premium may be inventoriable (Ind AS 2)
Software subscriptions (SaaS) Annual or multi-year, billed upfront Straight-line over subscription term Check whether arrangement is a service (prepaid) or an intangible asset (perpetual licence) — SaaS without control of software is a service contract
AMC billed in advance Annual Straight-line AMC billed in arrears is an accrual instead (s01)
Licence fees (statutory licences, trade licences, regulatory fees) Annual / multi-year Straight-line over validity Some regulatory licences meeting Ind AS 38 criteria are intangible assets instead
Rent paid in advance for short-term/low-value leases Up to 12 months Straight-line Leases under Ind AS 116 recognition go to ROU asset, not prepaid
Subscriptions, memberships, conference fees Annual Straight-line / on event date Event-based costs expensed when the event occurs

The monthly charge for each prepaid and month uses the overlap-days approach:

=[@Amount] * MAX(0, MIN([@CoverEnd], MonthEnd) - MAX([@CoverStart], MonthStart) + 1) / ([@CoverEnd] - [@CoverStart] + 1)

A dynamic-array version produces the full month-by-month amortisation grid across a row of month-end dates (EOMONTH series), so the tracker shows the schedule for every item and every month at once.

Worked example with current/non-current split

A 3-year fire insurance policy, premium ₹54,00,000 (GST credit available, excluded), coverage 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2028 (1,096 days, including 29 February 2028). Paid 28 September 2025.

Date Entry Dr (₹) Cr (₹)
28 Sep 2025 Dr Prepaid insurance, Dr Input GST, Cr Bank 54,00,000 (+ GST)
Each month Dr Insurance expense, Cr Prepaid insurance (days in month × 54,00,000 ÷ 1,096) e.g., October: 31 × 4,927.01 = 1,52,737

At 31 March 2026: days elapsed = 182 (1 October–31 March), amortised = 182 × ₹4,927.01 = ₹8,96,715; closing balance = ₹45,03,285. Current portion (1 April 2026–31 March 2027, 365 days) = 365 × ₹4,927.01 = ₹17,98,358; non-current = ₹27,04,927. Schedule III: current portion in "Other current assets" (prepaid expenses), non-current portion in "Other non-current assets".

Reconciliation and controls

  • Tracker closing balance = prepaid GL balance each month (check flag in the Project 1 workbook).
  • New payments to prepaid GL must appear in the tracker the same month (completeness): AP posting rules route invoices with service periods spanning months to the prepaid account, or a review of large expense invoices flags multi-period coverage.
  • Cancellations and refunds: if a policy is cancelled, the unamortised balance becomes a receivable (refund) or expense (no refund).
  • Fully amortised items drop to zero and remain in the tracker history for audit.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Coverage starts after payment: amortise from coverage start, not payment date (topic pitfall 3); the balance sits fully prepaid until then.
  • Back-dated coverage: a policy renewed late but covering from an earlier date requires catch-up amortisation in the month of booking for the elapsed days.
  • Multi-element SaaS invoices: implementation fees may be a separate service (expensed as performed) while the subscription is prepaid; configuration/customisation costs in cloud computing arrangements are generally expensed unless they create a separately controlled intangible asset.
  • GST: recoverable GST is not part of the prepaid; non-recoverable GST is. ITC on a long-period prepayment is claimable on invoice, not spread.
  • Foreign-currency prepaids: recorded at the payment-date rate and not revalued (non-monetary).

Key takeaways

  • Prepaids are non-monetary, non-financial assets for services paid in advance; expense them as services are received.
  • The tracker records coverage dates, amount, daily amortisation, closing balance and the current/non-current split.
  • Use the overlap-days formula to compute monthly charges for any coverage period.
  • Current portion = amortisation falling in the next 12 months; the rest is non-current under Schedule III.
  • Reconcile tracker to GL monthly and route multi-period invoices to the prepaid account.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a prepaid row [L1]

A SaaS subscription of ₹18,25,000 (excluding GST) covers 15 January 2026 to 14 January 2027 and was paid on 5 January 2026. Compute the charges for January, February and March 2026 and the closing balance at 31 March 2026, with the current/non-current split.

💡 Hint: Use days of coverage in each month divided by total coverage days.

Q2. Multi-year insurance split [L2]

A 5-year key-man insurance premium of ₹1,20,00,000 covers 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2030 (assume 1,826 days). Compute the closing balance at 31 March 2026 and its current and non-current portions, and pass the entries for the year.

Q3. Tracker build in Excel [L3]

Design an Excel prepaid tracker for 120 items with a month-by-month amortisation grid using dynamic arrays, a tie-out to the prepaid GL, current/non-current split columns and flags for (a) coverage start later than payment date, (b) items with no evidence link, (c) tracker vs GL differences. Specify formulas and layout.

Q4. Classification judgement [L2]

For each, decide whether it is a prepaid, an intangible asset, a ROU asset or an immediate expense: (a) 3-year perpetual ERP licence purchase; (b) 3-year SaaS CRM subscription paid upfront; (c) 11-month warehouse lease rent paid in advance; (d) trade fair participation fee paid in February for an event in May; (e) annual statutory pollution control consent fee.
