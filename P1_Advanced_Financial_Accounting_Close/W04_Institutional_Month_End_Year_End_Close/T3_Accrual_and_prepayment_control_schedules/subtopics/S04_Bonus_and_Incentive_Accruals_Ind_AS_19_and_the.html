Subtopic note

Bonus and Incentive Accruals: Ind AS 19 and the Code on Wages 2019

Scope: Bonus/incentive accruals as Ind AS 19 short-term benefits; statutory bonus now under Code on Wages 2019 (8.33% minimum, 20% maximum)

Why it matters

Bonuses and sales incentives are among the largest discretionary-looking costs in an Indian P&L, but accounting treats most of them as obligations that build up as employees work. Companies that expense the annual bonus only when it is paid (often in June or July) or only at the March close show artificially high profits in Q1–Q3 and a Q4 spike — a classic topic pitfall that distorts quarterly results, flux analysis and FP&A run-rates. Statutory bonus adds a legal floor: since the Labour Codes came into force, statutory bonus is governed by the Code on Wages, 2019, which carries forward the 8.33% minimum and 20% maximum of the repealed Payment of Bonus Act, 1965, with a wider "wages" definition that can change the base.

Core notes

Ind AS 19 — short-term employee benefits

Short-term employee benefits are those expected to be settled wholly before twelve months after the end of the annual reporting period in which employees render the related service (Ind AS 19.8 definitions; 19.9). They include wages, paid annual leave, and profit-sharing and bonuses payable within twelve months.

  • Recognition (Ind AS 19.11): when an employee has rendered service during the period, recognise the undiscounted amount of short-term benefits expected to be paid in exchange for that service as a liability (accrued expense), after deducting amounts already paid, and as an expense (unless another standard permits capitalisation, e.g., Ind AS 16 or Ind AS 2).
  • Profit-sharing and bonus plans (Ind AS 19.19): recognise the expected cost when (a) the entity has a present legal or constructive obligation to make the payments as a result of past events, and (b) a reliable estimate of the obligation can be made. A present obligation exists when the entity has no realistic alternative but to pay.
  • Constructive obligation (Ind AS 19.20–21): an entity with no legal obligation may have a constructive obligation from past practice; the measurement reflects the possibility that some employees leave without receiving the bonus.
  • Reliable estimate (Ind AS 19.22): possible when the formal terms of the plan contain a formula, the entity determines the amounts before the financial statements are authorised, or past practice gives clear evidence of the amount.
  • Nature (Ind AS 19.23): the obligation arises from employee service, not from a transaction with owners — the cost is an expense, not a distribution of profit.
  • Beyond 12 months (Ind AS 19.24): if the bonus is not expected to be settled wholly within twelve months (e.g., deferred retention bonus over three years), it is an "other long-term employee benefit" measured with actuarial assumptions and discounting.

Monthly accrual mechanics

For an annual performance bonus with a 1 April–31 March performance year:

\[ \text{Required balance}_m = \text{Expected annual payout} \times \frac{m}{12} \times (1 - \text{expected forfeiture rate}) - \text{Paid to date} \]

where \(m\) is months of service elapsed. Each month, charge the difference between the required balance and the existing balance (cumulative true-up, s03). The expected payout is driven by the plan formula: target bonus × achievement factor (e.g., EBITDA vs budget) × individual multiplier. FP&A's latest forecast of the achievement factor should be used — not the budget, once actuals deviate.

Illustration: eligible target bonus pool ₹6.0 crore; forfeiture 5%. April–August were accrued at the budget achievement factor of 100%: \(6.0 \times 1.00 \times 0.95 \times 5/12\) = ₹2.375 crore. After Q2, FP&A forecasts 110% achievement, so expected payout = \(6.0 \times 1.10 \times 0.95\) = ₹6.27 crore. Required balance at 30 September (m = 6) = \(6.27 \times 6/12\) = ₹3.135 crore. September charge = ₹3.135 crore − ₹2.375 crore = ₹0.76 crore, of which ₹0.5225 crore is the normal monthly charge at the new estimate (6.27 ÷ 12) and ₹0.2375 crore is the catch-up for April–August (\((6.27 - 5.70) \times 5/12\)), disclosed as a true-up in the flux commentary.

Sales incentives on monthly or quarterly targets are accrued in the month or quarter they are earned; for incentives tied to annual targets with a quarterly payout, accrue on expected annual attainment.

Statutory bonus under the Code on Wages, 2019

The four Labour Codes, including the Code on Wages, 2019, were brought into force from 21 November 2025; the Code subsumes the Payment of Wages Act, the Minimum Wages Act, the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 and the Equal Remuneration Act. Statutory bonus is in the Code's chapter on payment of bonus. Key features (verify against the notified central and state rules):

  • Eligibility: employees drawing wages not exceeding an amount per month notified by the appropriate Government, who have worked for not less than 30 days in the accounting year. Until revised notifications are made, many employers continue to apply the earlier thresholds (₹21,000 per month for eligibility; calculation on the higher of ₹7,000 or the applicable minimum wage) — check the current notification.
  • Minimum bonus: 8.33% of wages earned or ₹100, whichever is higher, payable whether or not the employer has allocable surplus (subject to exemptions, e.g., certain new establishments).
  • Maximum bonus: 20% of wages, where allocable surplus exceeds the minimum.
  • Allocable surplus and set-on/set-off: bonus above the minimum depends on available surplus computed from profits; excess or shortfall is carried forward for set-on/set-off as under the earlier law.
  • Payment timeline: within eight months from the close of the accounting year.
  • Wages definition: the Code's uniform definition of wages excludes specified allowances, but if excluded components exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages. This can raise the bonus base (and PF and gratuity bases) for employees with allowance-heavy structures.

Accounting: statutory bonus is a legal obligation accruing with service. Accrue monthly at the expected rate — at least 8.33% of eligible wages, and at the expected percentage up to 20% if the allocable surplus computation (or established practice of paying above minimum) supports it.

Entry (monthly): Dr Employee benefits expense — bonus, Cr Bonus payable (employee benefits payable). On payment: Dr Bonus payable, Cr Bank.

Tax

Under Sec 43B(c) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, bonus or commission to employees is deductible only in the year of actual payment, with relief if paid on or before the due date of the return (s07). The accrual creates a deferred tax asset if paid after the return due date (Week 3 deferred tax). From tax year 2026-27, map to the Income-tax Act, 2025 equivalent.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Discretionary bonuses: a board-approved "discretionary" bonus paid every year for ten years is a constructive obligation under Ind AS 19.19–21; accrue it.
  • Clawback or service conditions after year-end: if employees must be employed on the payment date, the measurement reflects expected forfeiture; if the payment depends on service beyond 12 months, it may be a long-term benefit.
  • Capitalisation: bonus for employees working on qualifying assets (projects in CWIP) or production is capitalised in inventory or PPE per Ind AS 2/16.
  • Group-wide plans: bonuses based on group performance but paid by each subsidiary must be accrued by each employing entity.
  • Ex gratia vs statutory: ex gratia payments to employees above the eligibility threshold are not statutory bonus but are still short-term benefits.

Key takeaways

  • Bonus and profit-sharing are Ind AS 19 short-term benefits recognised when a legal or constructive obligation and a reliable estimate exist (Ind AS 19.19–22).
  • Accrue through the performance year using the latest expected payout and forfeiture, with cumulative true-ups.
  • Statutory bonus now sits under the Code on Wages, 2019 (in force from 21 November 2025): 8.33% minimum, 20% maximum, eight-month payment window; verify notified ceilings.
  • The Code's 50% wages rule can enlarge the bonus base.
  • Sec 43B links deduction to payment, creating deferred tax where payment is delayed.

Practice Questions

Q1. Monthly bonus accrual [L2]

A company's annual bonus plan pays 15% of fixed pay of eligible employees (₹48 crore annual fixed pay) at 100% achievement, scaled linearly between 80% and 120% achievement. Expected forfeiture is 6%. At 31 December, FP&A forecasts 92% achievement; accruals to 30 November total ₹4.62 crore. Compute the required balance at 31 December and the December charge.

💡 Hint: Compute the expected annual payout first, then apply nine-twelfths and deduct the balance already accrued.

Q2. Statutory bonus computation [L2]

An establishment has 120 eligible employees with average monthly wages for bonus purposes of ₹18,500, but bonus is computed on a ceiling of ₹7,000 per month (assume this is the notified calculation ceiling and exceeds the minimum wage). Allocable surplus supports 12%. Compute the annual statutory bonus and the monthly accrual, and explain how the answer changes if allocable surplus supports only 6%.

Q3. Constructive obligation [L3]

A listed company has paid a "discretionary" year-end bonus of about 10% of fixed pay for seven consecutive years. The CFO wants to accrue nothing until the board approves the bonus in May, arguing it is discretionary. Evaluate under Ind AS 19 and explain the effect on quarterly results and on the audit.

Q4. Wages definition impact [L2]

An employee's monthly remuneration is ₹60,000: basic + DA ₹24,000, HRA ₹15,000, special allowance ₹18,000, conveyance ₹3,000. Explain how the Code on Wages' 50% rule affects the wage base, and why this matters for bonus eligibility and for other benefits (PF, gratuity) accrued in the close.
