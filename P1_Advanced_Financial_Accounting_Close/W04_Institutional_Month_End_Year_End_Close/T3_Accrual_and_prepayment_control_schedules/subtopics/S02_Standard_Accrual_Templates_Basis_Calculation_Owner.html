Subtopic note

Standard Accrual Templates: Basis, Calculation, Owner, Reversal, Evidence

Scope: Standard templates: basis (contract, run-rate, PO, usage), calculation, owner, reversal flag, evidence

Why it matters

Without a standard template, each accountant accrues differently: one uses last month's bill, another the budget, a third waits for an e-mail from the business. The result is inconsistent estimates, missing reversals, and accruals that cannot be explained to auditors three months later. A standard template forces every accrual line to declare how it was calculated, who is responsible, whether it reverses, and what evidence supports it. It converts a judgement-heavy area into a schedule that can be reviewed in minutes, automated in Excel or the ERP, and tied directly to the GL. In the Project 1 workbook, the accrual schedule is one of the supporting schedules that posts into the adjustment layer and must tie to the accrued-expenses GL.

The four bases

Basis When to use Calculation Strength Risk
Contract Fixed fees: CAM, AMC billed in arrears, audit fees, rent escalations, retainer fees, interest Contract rate × period (pro-rated by days) Objective, automatable Contract amendments not captured
Run-rate Recurring variable costs without period-end data: telecom, courier, small utilities Trailing 3- or 6-month average of actual invoices, adjusted for known changes Simple, stable Lags price/volume changes; hides trends
PO Goods/services under POs where receipt is evidenced by GRN or service entry sheet Received quantity × PO price (not open PO value) Linked to procurement data Over-accrual if PO value used instead of receipts
Usage Consumption measurable at T0: power (meter), fuel, freight trips, man-days Measured usage × contracted rate Most accurate Data capture effort; meter reading timing

Choosing the basis is a hierarchy: use usage or contract data where available; PO-based where receipts are captured in the ERP; run-rate only as a fallback, with a note of why better data is unavailable.

Calculation conventions

  • Pro-rating by days (actual/actual) for periods that do not align with months; the overlap-day formula (concept c03) applies equally to accruals and prepaids.
  • Accruals net of amounts already invoiced for the same period (concept c01).
  • GST: accruals are booked at the expense amount excluding recoverable GST (input GST is recognised when the invoice is received and conditions under Sec 16(2) are met); non-recoverable GST (blocked credits, exempt businesses) is included in the accrual.
  • Currency: foreign-currency accruals recorded at the spot rate at T0 (monetary liability).
  • Rounding: round to the nearest ₹100 or ₹1,000 per policy; keep the unrounded figure in the template.

Excel implementation of a usage-basis row with invoiced deduction and reversal flag:

=LET(cost, [@Usage]*[@Rate] + [@FixedCharge],
     acc, MAX(0, cost - [@InvoicedForPeriod]),
     ROUND(acc, -2))

A summary pivot by GL account and cost centre, compared with the accrual GL after posting, provides the tie-out check:

=SUMIFS(Accruals[Amount], Accruals[GL], [@GL], Accruals[Period], CloseMonth) + SUMIFS(TB[Balance], TB[GL], [@GL])

The second formula should return zero when the accrued-expenses GL (a credit balance stored as negative in the TB) equals the schedule.

Owner and evidence

The owner is the business person who confirms that the service was received and the driver data is correct — not the accountant. Owners sign off through a workflow or a monthly confirmation e-mail stored as evidence. The evidence column must point to something an auditor can inspect: contract clause, meter reading, service entry sheet, timesheet, partner estimate e-mail, HR bonus model. "Per discussion" is not evidence.

Review procedures

  • Completeness: compare this month's accrual IDs with last month's and with the vendor list of recurring suppliers with no invoice received.
  • Accuracy: sample high-value lines and re-perform calculations; compare accrual to subsequent invoice (accrual-to-actual variance, s03).
  • Reasonableness: flux check of each accrual vs prior month and vs budget (Topic 6 thresholds).
  • Reversals: confirm that all Y-flagged accruals from last month reversed on day 1 (s03 and topic pitfall 2).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mixed bases: a logistics contract may have a fixed monthly fee (contract) plus per-trip charges (usage); split into two lines.
  • Vendor already invoiced partly: a monthly AMC invoiced for half the month requires only the remaining half; the "invoiced for period" field prevents double-counting.
  • Unknown amount, known obligation: a consultant has worked but not issued a statement; use the SOW rate × best estimate of effort, documented by the owner — still an accrual, not a provision, if the uncertainty is small.
  • Changes in basis: moving from run-rate to usage-based may cause a one-time step change; disclose in commentary to avoid misleading flux analysis.
  • Accruals for capital items: route to CWIP, with capitalisation date tracked in the FA schedule.

Key takeaways

  • Every accrual line declares basis, calculation, owner, reversal flag and evidence.
  • Bases form a hierarchy: usage/contract first, PO (on receipts) next, run-rate as fallback.
  • Calculations net of amounts already invoiced and pro-rated by days.
  • Owners are business people who confirm receipt and inputs; accountants prepare and review.
  • The schedule must tie to the accrual GL and be tested for completeness, accuracy and reversal.

Practice Questions

Q1. Fill the template [L1]

Prepare template rows (all fields) for: (a) quarterly CAM of ₹4.5 lakh billed in arrears for January–March; (b) freight on 214 trips in March at ₹6,800 per trip, ₹3.9 lakh already invoiced; (c) monthly retainer of ₹1.5 lakh for a tax consultant, unbilled for March.

Q2. Choosing a basis [L2]

A company has accrued telecom costs at a 6-month run-rate of ₹9.2 lakh per month. In March, it moved 2,000 employees to a new unlimited plan at ₹380 per user per month, effective 1 March, with the old per-minute plan ending 28 February. Explain which basis you would now use, compute the March accrual and describe how you would explain the change in the flux commentary.

💡 Hint: The contract rate is now known; a run-rate based on the old plan is no longer the best estimate.

Q3. Build the schedule in Excel [L3]

Design an Excel accrual workbook with an input table (structured table named Accruals), a calculation column per basis type, a reversal flag, a GL tie-out check and a summary by GL and cost centre. Specify formulas, data validation lists for basis and reversal flag, and the conditional formatting for missing evidence or owner.

Q4. Review findings [L2]

In reviewing a 180-line accrual schedule, you find: 12 lines with "per discussion" as evidence; 9 lines with basis "PO" whose amounts equal open PO values; 5 Y-flagged lines from February that did not reverse in March. Without quantifying, explain the risk of each finding and the corrective action.
