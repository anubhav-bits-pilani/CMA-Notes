Subtopic note

Accrual Taxonomy: What Gets Accrued at Month-End and How

Scope: Accrual taxonomy: utilities, CAM, AMC, audit fees, GRNI, services not invoiced, consultancy, bonus/incentives, interest

Why it matters

Accruals are the largest group of manual estimates in a monthly close and the main reason P&L lines swing from month to month. A recurring expense that is booked only when the vendor's invoice arrives will show nil in one month and double in the next; an annual bonus expensed when paid creates a Q4 spike. A taxonomy — a standard list of accrual types, each with its own basis, data source and owner — turns accruals from ad hoc judgement into a repeatable control. It is also what auditors test for completeness of liabilities (search for unrecorded liabilities after year-end), and what FP&A uses as a run-rate baseline.

Core notes

Definition

Under Ind AS 37.11, accruals are liabilities to pay for goods or services that have been received or supplied but have not been paid, invoiced or formally agreed with the supplier, including amounts due to employees. The uncertainty in timing or amount is generally much less than for provisions. They arise from the accrual basis required by Ind AS 1.27–28 and Sec 128(1) of the Companies Act 2013 (books on accrual basis and double-entry). The general formula (concept c01) is expected period cost less amount already invoiced for the period.

The taxonomy

Utilities Electricity, water, gas, telecom Meter reading × tariff; or trailing 3-month average adjusted for production days Meter logs, DISCOM tariff, prior bills Plant admin / facilities Auto-reverse

GRNI (goods received not invoiced) Raw materials, consumables, capital goods received GRN quantity × PO price ERP GR/IR Procurement / AP System-managed via GR/IR (not manual)

Consultancy / professional fees Legal, advisory, IT projects Milestones achieved or time spent × rate per statement of work SOW, timesheets, partner estimate Business owner + legal Auto-reverse
Bonus / incentives Annual performance bonus, sales incentives, statutory bonus Expected payout × proportion of performance period elapsed (s04) HR policy, sales performance HR + FP&A Cumulative true-up (not reversed)
Interest Interest on loans, debentures, MSME delayed payment interest Principal × rate × days (EIR from Week 3 where applicable) Loan schedules Treasury Cleared on payment

Basis by category — why they differ

  • Usage-based (utilities, services): amount depends on actual consumption, so the best evidence is a meter reading or activity log at T0. A trailing average is a fallback and should be adjusted for known drivers (production days, tariff changes).
  • Contract-based (CAM, AMC, audit fees, interest): amount is fixed by contract and time; the estimate is mechanical and should be automated as a recurring entry.
  • Receipt-based (GRNI): amount is the value of goods actually received at PO price — never PO value outstanding (topic pitfall 1).
  • Performance-based (bonus, incentives, consultancy milestones): amount depends on performance or completion; requires judgement and periodic true-up.

Item Computation Accrual (₹ lakh)

(Electricity: 4,18,000 × 8.10 = ₹33.86 lakh + ₹3.2 lakh = ₹37.06 lakh, rounded.)

Entry: Dr each expense, Cr Accrued expenses / Interest accrued (other financial liabilities), with auto-reversal on 1 April for the usage and service items (s03).

Presentation

Accrued amounts for goods and services from suppliers are generally presented within trade payables (the ICAI Guidance Note on Division II treats amounts due for goods purchased or services received in the normal course as trade payables); interest accrued is shown in other financial liabilities; employee-related accruals are commonly shown as employee benefits payable within other financial liabilities or within provisions, depending on the entity's policy and the certainty of the amount (see s08). The MSME disclosure (Schedule III and MSMED Act Sec 22) must include accrued amounts due to micro and small enterprises.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Disputed invoices: if a vendor bills ₹12 lakh and the company accepts only ₹8 lakh of service, accrue ₹8 lakh and disclose a contingent liability if the dispute is significant; if outflow of the disputed part is probable, it becomes a provision.
  • Capital vs revenue: accruals for capital work (contractor running bills) go to CWIP, not to expense.
  • Accruals in foreign currency: accrued expenses payable in foreign currency are monetary liabilities and are revalued at the closing rate.
  • Unclaimed accruals: standing accruals not invoiced for more than 6–12 months need challenge — they may be overstated liabilities (and income smoothing).
  • Related parties: accruals to group companies must agree with the counterparty's receivable (Topic 4 IC matching).

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 37.11 accruals are liabilities for goods/services received, with less uncertainty than provisions.
  • A taxonomy assigns each accrual type a basis (usage, contract, receipt, performance), data source, owner and reversal method.
  • GRNI is system-managed through GR/IR at received quantity × PO price.
  • Bonus and incentives are cumulative true-up accruals, not auto-reversing accruals.
  • Presentation varies: trade payables for suppliers, other financial liabilities for interest, policy-based for employee items.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify accrual basis [L1]

For each item, state the basis category (usage, contract, receipt, performance), data source and reversal method: telecom bills, quarterly CAM, inventory received on 30 March without invoice, quarterly sales incentive, debenture interest, legal opinion fee, temporary staffing.

Q2. Compute month-end accruals [L2]

For March, compute accruals: (a) water: meter reading 18,400 kL at ₹42 per kL, February bill already received; (b) AMC for DG sets billed half-yearly in arrears at ₹7.2 lakh per half-year, last billed for April–September; (c) tax audit fee ₹6 lakh, no work done yet; (d) housekeeping 28 staff × 26 days × ₹850 per day; (e) interest on an MSME supplier balance of ₹18 lakh overdue by 35 days at three times the bank rate of 6.5% per annum.

💡 Hint: For (c), consider whether the obligation has arisen if no service has been received; for (e), MSMED Act interest is compounded monthly.

Q3. Taxonomy design [L3]

Design an accrual taxonomy for a 400-store retail chain, covering rent and CAM, utilities, store security, logistics, marketing agencies, incentives and interest. For each category, specify basis, data feed, owner, review threshold and reversal method, and explain how you would test completeness at year-end.

Q4. Standing accrual challenge [L2]

The accrual listing includes 23 items older than 9 months totalling ₹1.46 crore, mainly for legal and consultancy fees "pending invoices". Explain the risks and the review steps, and draft the policy rule you would introduce.
