Roadmap topic: Accrual & Prepayment Control Schedules: Standardizing recurring utility/audit/bonus accruals and prepaid amortization trackers to eliminate P&L volatility

Overview

Accruals and prepayments are the timing machinery of accrual accounting. Accruals bring forward the expense for services received but not yet invoiced; prepayments defer the expense for services paid in advance. Each month-end, a mid-sized Indian company may post hundreds of such entries — utilities, CAM, AMCs, audit fees, security and housekeeping, consultancy, interest, bonuses, insurance, SaaS subscriptions and licences. Done ad hoc, they create P&L volatility unrelated to business performance: months with missing invoices look profitable, months with annual renewals look loss-making, and Q4 absorbs a year's bonus. Done through standard control schedules, they produce a smooth, explainable P&L whose movements reflect real drivers.

For the target roles this is daily work. A controller owns the accrual and prepaid schedules, their tie-out to the GL and their evidence for auditors (completeness of liabilities, cut-off, recomputation of prepaid amortisation). An FP&A analyst depends on them for run-rates, because a clean accrual base is the starting point for forecasts and variance analysis. Interviewers test the mechanics (overlap-day formulas, auto-reversal, true-ups) and the judgement (accrual vs provision, bonus under Ind AS 19, tax interplay with Sec 40(a)(ia) and 43B).

The learning sequence: start with the accrual taxonomy (s01) and the accrual formula (c01), then the standard template that every accrual line must follow (s02). Learn the reversal and true-up mechanics with accrual-to-actual tracking (s03) and the journal entry patterns (c04). Bonus accruals (s04) apply Ind AS 19 and the Code on Wages. Then turn to prepaids: the tracker (s05), the monthly charge and overlap formulas (c02, c03), the current/non-current split (c06), the advertising rule (c05) and materiality thresholds for small items (s06). Close with tax interplay (s07) and the accrual-vs-provision distinction with Schedule III placement (s08), which connects the schedules to the mapping engine of Topic 5.

Related concepts to study

Short-term employee benefits and provisions

Short-term employee benefits → w2_t5; provisions vs accruals → w2_t4

Week 2 Topic 5 covered Ind AS 19 in full — short-term benefits, post-employment plans and other long-term benefits. In this topic, the bonus and incentive schedule (s04) applies the short-term benefit rules: undiscounted accrual as service is rendered, with recognition of profit-sharing and bonus when a legal or constructive obligation and a reliable estimate exist. Week 2 Topic 4 covered Ind AS 37 recognition, measurement and disclosure of provisions. Here the focus is the boundary (s08): routine accruals have much less uncertainty than provisions and are usually presented in trade and other payables, while genuine provisions need best-estimate measurement and a movement table. The accrual schedule should carry a classification attribute so the two are not mixed.

Capitalised contract cost amortisation

Capitalised contract costs (commissions) amortisation tracker → w1_t3

Week 1 Topic 3 covered incremental costs of obtaining a contract under Ind AS 115.91–94 (e.g., sales commissions), capitalised and amortised on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services, with the practical expedient to expense when the amortisation period would be one year or less. Mechanically, that tracker is a prepaid tracker with a different amortisation driver: instead of calendar days, the pattern follows revenue recognition of the related contract (and anticipated renewals). The same template fields, overlap logic, current/non-current split and GL tie-out from s05 and c02–c06 apply, plus an impairment test under Ind AS 115.101.

FP&A run-rates and smoother P&L

FP&A: accrual run-rates as forecast baseline; smoother P&L for variance analysis

FP&A teams build forecasts from the actual run-rate of recurring costs. If actuals are lumpy because accruals are missing or prepaids are expensed upfront, the run-rate is wrong and variance analysis explains timing noise instead of performance. Clean accrual and prepaid schedules provide: (i) a reliable monthly cost base by cost centre; (ii) accrual-to-actual history to calibrate forecast accuracy; (iii) known future amortisation from prepaid trackers (a committed cost for the next 12 months); and (iv) bonus accruals linked to forecast achievement. The FP&A forecast and the accrual model should use the same driver assumptions, especially for bonus and incentives.

ERP recurring and reversing entries

ERP recurring and reversing entries (e.g., SAP FBS1/F.81) and accrual workflow tools

SAP provides FBS1 to post accrual/deferral documents with a planned reversal date and F.81 to mass-reverse them on day 1; recurring entries are created via FBD1 and executed with F.14; S/4HANA's Accrual Engine and "Manage Accruals" apps automate periodic accruals for purchase orders and service contracts. Oracle offers reversing journals and recurring journal formulas; Tally supports reversing journals and scheduled vouchers. Close-automation tools (BlackLine Journals, FloQast) manage accrual workflows with owner sign-offs and evidence. Whatever the tool, controls are the same: template change management, reversal-run verification on T+1, and tie-out of the accrual and prepaid GLs to the schedules.

Common pitfalls

Accruing on PO value instead of receipts

Accruing on PO value rather than goods/services actually received

The mistake: accruing the open PO value at month-end ("PO raised for ₹40 lakh, invoice pending") regardless of how much has been delivered. It happens because the open PO report is easy to pull and business owners confuse commitment with consumption. The result is overstated liabilities and expenses (or understated, where deliveries exceed PO). The correct basis is received quantity × PO price (GRN or service entry sheet) or measured usage. Detect it by comparing accrual lines with basis "PO" against GRN/service entry data, and by A2A analysis showing systematic favourable variances.

Missing reversals

Missing reversals, causing double expense when the invoice posts

The mistake: an accrual is posted without a reversal date, or the reversal run fails because the next period is not open, and the invoice then posts to expense — doubling the cost. It happens under close pressure and when accrual journals are uploaded manually. The correct approach: reversal flag in the template (s02), ERP reversal date in every accrual document, and a T+1 check that all flagged accruals reversed. Detect by reconciling the accrued-expenses GL to the current month's schedule — any residual from prior months indicates a missed reversal — and by flux review of expense lines spiking after month-end.

Amortising from payment date

Amortising from payment date instead of coverage start date

The mistake: a prepaid paid on 20 March for coverage from 1 April is amortised from March, or a policy renewed late is amortised from the booking date. It happens because the tracker is populated from AP data (invoice/payment date) rather than the policy schedule. The correct treatment is to amortise from coverage start to coverage end using the overlap formula (c03), with catch-up for back-dated coverage. Detect by checking tracker start dates against policy documents and by flagging rows where amortisation start equals payment date.

Expensing annual bonus only when paid

Expensing annual bonus only when paid, creating Q4 spikes

The mistake: the annual bonus is expensed in the month of payment (or only at the March close when the pool is approved). It happens because the bonus is perceived as discretionary until approved. Under Ind AS 19.19–22, a legal or constructive obligation (including from past practice) with a reliable estimate requires monthly accrual through the performance year, with true-ups as achievement forecasts change. Detect by comparing bonus expense by quarter (a Q4 spike or a Q1 spike in the payment month) and by checking whether quarterly results include a bonus accrual consistent with the plan formula.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the accrual and prepaid schedules for a quarter-end [L3]

Malabar Foods Ltd (FY ending 31 March) has the following at 31 December 2025: (i) electricity — meter readings show 6,10,000 kWh for December at ₹8.25 plus a fixed charge of ₹4.1 lakh, no invoice received; (ii) CAM for October–December billed quarterly in arrears at ₹3.8 lakh per month; (iii) statutory audit fee ₹42 lakh for the year, ₹10 lakh invoiced in November for the limited reviews, work 70% complete; (iv) annual performance bonus — target pool ₹7.2 crore, forecast achievement 95%, forfeiture 4%, accrued to 30 November ₹4.75 crore; (v) fire insurance ₹27,40,000 for 1 August 2025 to 31 July 2026, paid 28 July 2025, amortised to date from 28 July; (vi) a two-year SaaS subscription of ₹36,50,000 for 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2027 (730 days), currently fully classified as current. Required: (a) prepare the December accruals with template fields; (b) compute the bonus December charge; (c) correct the fire insurance amortisation and compute the 31 December balance; (d) compute the SaaS balance and its current/non-current split at 31 December; (e) pass all entries.

Q2. Tax interplay and Schedule III placement [L2]

At 31 March 2026, the "Provision for expenses" ledger of ₹9.4 crore includes: professional fees to identified firms ₹1.6 crore (no TDS deducted); contract services ₹2.2 crore (TDS deducted and deposited on 28 April); MSME supplier accruals ₹1.1 crore beyond 45 days, paid in May 2026; bonus ₹3.0 crore paid in August 2026; warranty provision ₹1.5 crore. Return due date is 31 October 2026. Required: (a) compute Sec 40(a)(ia) and Sec 43B disallowances; (b) compute the deferred tax asset at 25.168%; (c) prepare the Schedule III classification of the ledger; (d) list the disclosures triggered.

💡 Hint: Treat each component separately for TDS, 43B and classification before aggregating.

Q3. Accrual-to-actual review and control redesign [L3]

A company's monthly accrual schedule has 220 lines. Six months of A2A data show: utilities within ±3%; logistics consistently 12–18% under-accrued; consultancy accruals reversing with no invoice in 30% of cases; and five lines with basis "PO" equal to open PO values. Also, February's reversal run failed for 18 lines. Required: (a) quantify the risks qualitatively and identify which topic pitfalls are present; (b) redesign the template, basis rules and reversal controls; (c) propose KPIs for accrual quality to include in the close dashboard; (d) explain how the redesign would improve FP&A forecasting.

Q4. Prepaid threshold and advertising case [L2]

A consumer company's policy expenses advance payments below ₹2 lakh. At 31 March it has: 180 dealer-display rental advances of ₹1.5 lakh each for April–September; a ₹9 crore payment for IPL media slots airing April–May; ₹1.2 crore of printed catalogues delivered on 27 March for April distribution; and a ₹2.4 crore celebrity endorsement for 1 March 2026–28 February 2027 (shoot done in March). Required: apply Ind AS 38.69–70 and the threshold policy (including an aggregate test with a tolerance of ₹10 lakh), compute expense and prepaid balances at 31 March, and identify any items that may instead be consideration payable to customers under Ind AS 115.
