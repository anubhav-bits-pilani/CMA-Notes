Subtopic note

Clearing and Suspense Account Inventory

Scope: Clearing/suspense inventory: GR/IR, bank clearing, payroll clearing, unidentified receipts, cash-in-transit, IC suspense

Why it matters

Clearing and suspense accounts are where ERP processes hand off to each other — goods receipt to invoice, payment run to bank statement, payroll to disbursement. They are designed to clear to zero; when they do not, they hide unrecorded liabilities, misappropriated cash, duplicate payments or misclassified revenue. Schedule III has no suspense line, so any residual must be classified into a real line item or written off before reporting (topic related item). Auditors treat large or old suspense balances as a fraud-risk indicator under SA 240 and as an ICFR deficiency. The first step in controlling them is an inventory: a register of every clearing/suspense account, its purpose, expected clearing event, owner and normal balance.

Core notes

1. The clearing account register

For each account record: GL code, name, process it supports, what posts in (debit/credit), what clears it out, expected clearing timeline, owner, reviewer, reconciliation frequency, Schedule III classification for any residual, and escalation threshold (s06).

2. Account types

GR/IR (goods received / invoice received) clearing

  • Purpose: three-way match bridge. GRN posts Dr Inventory/Expense, Cr GR/IR; vendor invoice posts Dr GR/IR, Cr Vendor.
  • Credit balance = goods received not invoiced (GRNI) — an accrued liability; debit balance = invoiced not received — an advance/receivable from vendor or a timing issue.
  • Clearing: automatic on matching PO line; residuals from price/quantity variances, partial deliveries, cancelled GRNs. In SAP, F.13/F.13E clears matched items and F.19 (or FAGL_GR_IR) regroups residual GR/IR into "GRNI liability" and "goods-in-transit/advance" for reporting.
  • Reporting: credit residual → other current financial liabilities/trade payables (accrued); debit residual → other current assets after review.

Bank clearing (incoming and outgoing)

  • Outgoing payment clearing: payment run posts Dr Vendor, Cr Bank clearing; bank statement posts Dr Bank clearing, Cr Main bank. Residual = cheques/NEFT issued but not debited (unpresented) or rejected payments.
  • Incoming clearing: collection posted from bank statement before application to customer invoice.
  • Link to bank reconciliation (w4_t2 c01): the clearing account is the reconciling-items list. Old items signal stale cheques (reverse to vendor liability after policy period) or rejected payments.

Payroll clearing

  • Payroll posting: Dr Salary expense, Cr Net pay payable (clearing), Cr PF/ESI/PT/TDS (24Q) payables. Disbursement clears net pay. Statutory payables clear on challan payment.
  • Residuals: unpaid salaries of exits/full-and-final pending, bounced salary transfers, unreconciled statutory payables (the Sec 43B and Sec 36(1)(va) employee-contribution deadlines make these tax-sensitive — employee PF/ESI contributions are deductible only if deposited by the due date under the relevant Act).

Unidentified receipts (unapplied cash)

  • Bank credits with no identifiable customer/invoice: Dr Bank, Cr Unidentified receipts.
  • Risk: customer balances overstated, ECL computed on the wrong receivables, revenue cut-off errors, and possible advances that attract GST (on services) if they are actually advances.
  • Clearing: daily cash application with remittance advice, virtual account numbers per customer, UTR search. Residual at month-end is reported as a liability (other current financial liability or contract liability if an advance), never netted against trade receivables without identification.

Cash-in-transit

  • Store/branch cash deposited with a CIT agency or bank but not yet credited; card settlement receivables from acquirers (T+1/T+2); UPI settlements.
  • Clearing: bank statement credit or settlement report. Persistent residual is a misappropriation red flag; reconcile to pickup slips and acquirer MIS.
  • Reporting: cash and cash equivalents only if it meets the definition; otherwise other current financial assets.

IC suspense

  • Temporary holding for IC postings where the counterparty or stream is unknown, or for IC differences during consolidation.
  • Policy: should be nil at month-end. IC mismatches are resolved in the IC accounts with counterparty agreement (topic pitfall), not parked here.

Others to include in the inventory: GST suspense/ITC-on-hold, TDS clearing, employee advance clearing, card/expense clearing (corporate cards), asset clearing (AuC to asset), inventory price differences, rounding accounts and migration/conversion suspense after ERP changes.

The IC suspense and the unitemised portion of unidentified receipts are the immediate escalations; the > 60-day items in every row require controller sign-off or write-off (s06).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Netting debit and credit items within a clearing account to report a small net balance hides ageing (w4_t2 pitfall). Reconcile and age gross.
  • Stale cheques and unclaimed amounts — reversing them to income too quickly may be inappropriate; amounts due to vendors remain liabilities until legally extinguished. For companies, certain unclaimed amounts (dividends, matured deposits) must go to IEPF under Sec 125 of the Companies Act after seven years.
  • GR/IR debit balances may be genuine advances (invoice before delivery) or invoice errors; classify only after review.
  • Migration suspense after ERP go-live often carries conversion differences for years; set a sunset date and board-approved write-off.
  • Unidentified receipts near year-end may be advances from customers — GST liability on advances for services (time of supply Sec 13) and contract liability under Ind AS 115.
  • Payroll clearing and Sec 43B/36(1)(va): late deposit of employee PF contributions permanently disallows the deduction (Supreme Court, Checkmate Services, 2022).

Key takeaways

  • Maintain a register of every clearing/suspense account with purpose, clearing event, owner, timeline and residual classification.
  • GR/IR credit residual is a GRNI liability; debit residual needs review before reclassification.
  • Bank and payroll clearing balances are reconciling items lists — they must reconcile to the bank rec and statutory challans.
  • Unidentified receipts and cash-in-transit are the highest fraud and misstatement risk; clear daily and itemise at month-end.
  • IC suspense is a nil-balance account; Schedule III reporting requires every residual to be classified or written off.

Practice Questions

Q1. Building a clearing account register [L1]

List eight columns you would include in a clearing/suspense account register and explain why each is needed for audit evidence.

Q2. GR/IR analysis [L2]

At 31 March the GR/IR account of a manufacturer shows a net credit of ₹2,04,00,000, consisting of credit items of ₹2,61,00,000 (GRNs without invoices, of which ₹18,00,000 are older than 90 days) and debit items of ₹57,00,000 (invoices without GRN, of which ₹22,00,000 relate to a vendor whose goods were rejected and returned). Explain how each component should be investigated and presented in the Schedule III balance sheet, and pass any reclassification entries.

💡 Hint: Presenting the net figure is the trap; think gross and by nature.

Q3. Unidentified receipts at year-end [L2]

A B2B services company has ₹74 lakh of unidentified bank credits at 31 March: ₹40 lakh received in the last week (remittance advices awaited), ₹22 lakh from 45–90 days, ₹12 lakh older than 180 days. Discuss the accounting, GST and ECL implications of leaving these unapplied and propose a clearing protocol.

Q4. Cash-in-transit red flags [L3]

A retail chain's cash-in-transit account has grown from ₹18 lakh to ₹96 lakh over six months while sales are flat. Design the investigation steps (data, reconciliations and analytics) you would perform, and the controls you would recommend.

Q5. Payroll clearing and tax [L2]

The payroll clearing analysis shows employee PF contributions of ₹6,40,000 for February deposited on 28 March (due 15 March) and net salaries of ₹3,10,000 for exited employees unpaid for 75 days. Explain the tax consequences and the close-process actions.
