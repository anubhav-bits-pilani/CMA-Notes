Subtopic note

Automated Trial Balance Flux and Journal Entry Exception Checks

Scope: Automated TB flux checks: MoM movements, abnormal-sign balances, unmapped/new accounts, round-sum manual JEs, weekend postings

Why it matters

A month-end TB with 1,500 accounts cannot be reviewed line by line every month. Automated checks narrow attention to the accounts and entries most likely to be wrong: large unexplained movements, balances with the wrong sign, new accounts that the reporting engine does not know about, and manual journals with characteristics associated with error or fraud. These are the same tests auditors run under SA 520 (analytical procedures) and SA 240 (testing journal entries for management override), so running them first lets the close team fix issues before the auditors find them. In the Project 1 workbook these checks feed the "checks" tab and the master check flag.

Core notes

1. Month-on-month (MoM) movement checks

For each account (or mapping line), compare the current balance with the prior month-end (balance sheet) or prior month's activity (P&L), and flag where both a percentage and an absolute threshold are breached (c04):

\[ \text{Flag} = \left(\frac{\lvert B_{t} - B_{t-1} \rvert}{\lvert B_{t-1} \rvert} > X\%\right) \wedge \left(\lvert B_{t} - B_{t-1} \rvert > A\right) \]

where \(B_{t}\) is the current balance, \(B_{t-1}\) the comparative, \(X\%\) the percentage threshold (e.g., 10%) and \(A\) the absolute threshold (e.g., ₹10 lakh, derived from performance materiality). Handle a zero comparative separately (any movement above \(A\) is flagged). Variants: YoY for P&L, vs budget, and vs 3-month trailing average for seasonal accounts. Flagged items require commentary (w4_t6).

2. Abnormal-sign balances

Each natural account carries an expected sign in the mapping (c05):

Account nature Normal sign Abnormal sign means
Trade receivables Dr Customer advance / unapplied cash → reclassify to contract liability
Trade payables Cr Advance to supplier / duplicate payment → reclassify to other current assets
Bank Dr Overdraft → reclassify to borrowings (w4_t5 s05)
Accumulated depreciation Cr Over-reversal on disposal
Revenue Cr Credit notes exceed sales / misposting
Expense Dr Excess reversal of accrual (missed invoice)
Provisions Cr Utilisation without provision

The test runs at account level and, for receivables/payables, at sub-ledger party level — a payables control account can be net credit while hiding ₹2 crore of debit-balance vendors.

3. Unmapped and new accounts

  • Compare the TB account list to the mapping table; any account not found is unmapped (w4_t5 c02). Count must be 0 before the FS are generated.
  • Accounts created since the last close (from the GL master creation date, or not in prior TB) are listed for mapping review even if mapped by default, because a default mapping may be wrong.
  • Accounts with balances that were previously zero/dormant are flagged too.

4. Round-sum manual journal entries

From the JE listing (document type = manual, source = GL/FBS1-type, not system-generated), flag:

  • Amounts that are round (e.g., divisible by ₹1,00,000, or ending in 000 with no paise) above a threshold.
  • Repeated identical amounts.
  • Entries just below approval thresholds (e.g., ₹9.9 lakh where ₹10 lakh needs CFO approval).
  • Entries to unusual account combinations (e.g., Dr Revenue / Cr Reserves; Dr Cash / Cr Revenue).
  • Entries with blank or generic narrations ("adjustment", "as per discussion").

5. Weekend, holiday and after-hours postings

Postings on Saturdays/Sundays, public holidays, outside working hours, or after the period was closed/soft-locked, and entries posted by users who do not normally post JEs (senior management, IT users). Normal close days that fall on weekends should be whitelisted for the close team, otherwise the test floods.

6. Excel / Power Query implementation

TB table TB (Account, Desc, Bal, PriorBal) and mapping table Map (Account, Line, NormalSign); JE table JE (DocNo, PostDate, PostTime, User, Source, Account, Amount):

FluxFlag    = LET(d,[@Bal]-[@PriorBal], p,IF([@PriorBal]=0,1,ABS(d)/ABS([@PriorBal])), IF(AND(p>FluxPct,ABS(d)>FluxAbs),"FLAG",""))
Mapped      = IF(ISNA(XLOOKUP([@Account],Map[Account],Map[Account],NA())),"UNMAPPED","")
SignFlag    = IF(SIGN([@Bal])*XLOOKUP([@Account],Map[Account],Map[NormalSign],0)<0,"ABNORMAL","")
RoundFlag   = IF(AND([@Source]="Manual",MOD(ABS([@Amount]),100000)=0,ABS([@Amount])>=RoundMin),"ROUND","")
WeekendFlag = IF(WEEKDAY([@PostDate],2)>5,"WEEKEND","")
UnmappedCnt = COUNTIFS(TB[Mapped],"UNMAPPED")

(NormalSign = +1 for debit-nature, −1 for credit-nature, with the TB storing debits positive.) A FILTER on the TB table returns the exception list dynamically. In Power Query, a left anti-join of TB to Map returns unmapped accounts directly.

Account Prior ₹ lakh Current ₹ lakh Δ % Flags
Power & fuel 82.0 94.5 12.5 15.2% FLUX
Bank charges 1.2 2.8 1.6 133% None (below ₹10 lakh)
Trade payables – Vendor 2207 (14.0) 6.5 20.5 n.m. ABNORMAL (debit)

Bank charges shows why %-only thresholds flood reviewers (topic pitfall); the JE is the item an SA 240 auditor will select.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Sign convention: decide once whether the TB stores credits as negative; every flag formula depends on it.
  • Seasonality: compare to the same month last year or a trailing average to avoid flagging predictable movements (e.g., Diwali sales, Q4 bonus).
  • Reclassified/renamed accounts break MoM comparisons; maintain a mapping of old to new accounts.
  • Threshold calibration: too tight floods reviewers and creates "tick-box" commentary; too loose misses errors. Calibrate to performance materiality and review hit rates quarterly.
  • Automated JEs (depreciation runs, interface postings) can also be wrong — test interface totals separately rather than excluding them entirely.
  • Audit trail: the edit log mandated by Rule 3(1) Companies (Accounts) Rules (w4_t1 s08) is the data source for user, time and change tests.

Key takeaways

  • Combine percentage and absolute thresholds for MoM/YoY flux to focus on material movements.
  • Abnormal-sign tests must run at party level for control accounts, not only at GL level.
  • Unmapped count must be zero; new and reactivated accounts trigger mapping review.
  • Round-sum, just-below-threshold, unusual-pair, weekend and after-hours manual JEs mirror SA 240 tests.
  • Implement with structured tables, XLOOKUP, LET and FILTER (or Power Query anti-joins) feeding the checks tab.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply dual thresholds [L1]

Using thresholds of 10% and ₹15 lakh, identify which accounts require commentary: Freight outward ₹1.40 crore → ₹1.62 crore; Legal fees ₹6 lakh → ₹19 lakh; Trade receivables ₹48.0 crore → ₹50.1 crore; Rent ₹22 lakh → ₹22 lakh; Interest income nil → ₹16 lakh.

Q2. Party-level abnormal balances [L2]

A trade payables control account shows a credit of ₹31.6 crore. The vendor ageing shows 42 vendors with debit balances totalling ₹2.4 crore, including one vendor with ₹1.1 crore arising from a duplicate payment in February. Explain the reclassification and presentation required at year-end and the follow-up actions.

💡 Hint: Separate advances from recoverable overpayments.

Q3. Build an exceptions tab [L3]

Specify an Excel "checks" tab for a 900-account TB that reports: count of unmapped accounts, list of new accounts, flux exceptions, abnormal-sign accounts and a JE exception list (round-sum, weekend, just-below-threshold). Write the key formulas and describe how each check feeds a single master check flag.

Q4. SA 240 JE testing in the close [L2]

Explain how the close team's automated JE tests relate to the auditor's responsibilities under SA 240 for journal entries and other adjustments, and why management should not rely solely on the auditor's tests.

Q5. Calibrating thresholds [L3]

A company with revenue of ₹800 crore and PBT of ₹64 crore receives 340 flux exceptions per month using a 5%-only rule. Propose a revised threshold design, justify the absolute amount with reference to materiality, and describe how you would test that the new design does not miss significant movements.
