Transfer Pricing Alignment of IC Recharges and Mark-Ups

Scope: Transfer pricing alignment: service recharges, cost-plus mark-ups, Form 3CEB, domestic TP (Sec 92BA)

Why it matters

Inter-company charges are not just accounting entries — they determine where profit is taxed. If the close process books IC recharges at amounts that differ from the transfer pricing (TP) policy, the group creates (a) TP adjustments at assessment with interest and penalty, (b) a year-end "true-up" scramble that distorts Q4 margins, and (c) differences between the books and the accountant's report in Form 3CEB, which is filed by the same finance team. For FP&A, IC mark-ups shift segment and entity margins, so forecasts must apply the same policy. The close controller is the first line of defence: IC recharges should be calculated from the TP policy every month, not reverse-engineered at year-end.

Core notes

1. Legal framework (Income-tax Act)

  • International transactions between associated enterprises (AEs) must be at arm's length price (ALP) — Sec 92 to 92F of the Income-tax Act 1961. AE is defined in Sec 92A (capital, management, control, 26% shareholding, guarantees, dependence tests). From tax year 2026-27 the Income-tax Act 2025 applies and the TP provisions are carried forward with new section numbers; maintain a mapping of old to new references in policy documents and return workpapers.
  • Methods (Sec 92C, Rule 10B): comparable uncontrolled price (CUP), resale price, cost plus (CPM), profit split, transactional net margin method (TNMM), and "other method" (Rule 10AB). Service recharges are typically benchmarked using TNMM with a net cost-plus mark-up as the profit-level indicator.
  • Documentation (Sec 92D, Rule 10D) must be maintained where aggregate international transactions exceed ₹1 crore (and contemporaneously), plus master file (Form 3CEAA) and country-by-country reporting obligations for large groups.
  • Accountant's report — Form 3CEB (Sec 92E): every person entering into an international transaction or specified domestic transaction must obtain and file a report from an accountant, due one month before the due date of the return (the return due date for TP cases is 30 November, so 3CEB is due by 31 October for the preceding financial year — verify the dates under the 2025 Act forms).
  • Safe harbour rules (Rules 10TA–10TG) prescribe minimum margins for certain services (e.g., IT/ITeS, KPO, contract R&D, intra-group loans). Margins and validity years are notified periodically — check the current notification before relying on them.
  • Advance Pricing Agreements (Sec 92CC) can fix the method/margin for up to five years with rollback.

2. Service recharges and cost-plus mark-ups

Typical IC services: management/shared services, IT, HR, treasury, R&D, brand/royalty, guarantees, secondments.

Monthly mechanics for a cost-plus recharge:

  1. Collect the cost pool of the service provider (cost centre or profit centre group), including allocated overheads defined in the TP policy.
  2. Exclude pass-through costs (if policy allows pass-through at cost — e.g., third-party software licences bought on behalf).
  3. Allocate to recipients using the policy key (headcount, revenue, users, time sheets).
  4. Apply mark-up to the value-adding cost base.
  5. Raise the IC invoice with GST (domestic) or as export/import of services (cross-border — see s04) and post to IC accounts with partner codes.

\[ \text{Recharge}_{j} = \left(\text{Cost pool} - \text{Pass-through}\right) \times \frac{\text{Key}_{j}}{\sum \text{Key}} \times (1 + m) + \text{Pass-through}_{j} \]

where \(m\) is the arm's-length mark-up (e.g., 5% for low value-adding services under the OECD simplified approach, or a benchmarked TNMM margin), \(\text{Key}_{j}\) is recipient \(j\)'s allocation driver.

Illustration. Shared Services Centre Ltd (SSC) has March cost of ₹2.40 crore, of which ₹0.40 crore is pass-through cloud licences. Policy: allocate by headcount, mark-up 12% on value-adding cost. Recipients: Opco A 300 heads, Opco B 100 heads.

Opco A Opco B Total
Value-adding cost share (₹ Cr) 1.50 0.50 2.00
Mark-up 12% 0.18 0.06 0.24
Pass-through (allocated on usage 70:30) 0.28 0.12 0.40
Recharge before GST 1.96 0.68 2.64

SSC's operating margin on total cost = 0.24 ÷ 2.40 = 10%; on value-adding cost = 12%. The TP report must state which base the benchmark relates to.

3. Year-end true-ups

Where monthly recharges use budgeted costs, a year-end true-up aligns actual margin to the policy. True-ups should be:

  • Booked before the books are finalised (they change both entities' profits and Form 3CEB values).
  • Invoiced (GST credit/debit note under Sec 34 CGST Act within the time limit — 30 November following year-end for credit notes — and TDS on the incremental amount).
  • Consistent with TP documentation; an upward adjustment booked after the return creates a mismatch with Form 3CEB.

4. Domestic transfer pricing — Sec 92BA

Specified domestic transactions (SDT) are subject to ALP only where the aggregate value exceeds ₹20 crore in the year. After the Finance Act 2017 omitted clause (i) (payments to related persons under Sec 40A(2)(b)), SDTs mainly cover transactions of entities claiming profit-linked deductions or concessional regimes — e.g., transfers of goods/services by units claiming Sec 80-IA/10AA-type benefits (Sec 80-IA(8)/(10)), and transactions of a company opting for Sec 115BAB with close connections that produce more-than-ordinary profits. Most domestic shared-service recharges between ordinary Indian companies are therefore not SDTs — but they remain subject to Sec 40A(2)(b) reasonableness for expense deductibility and to GST valuation rules for related persons.

5. Close-process controls

  • IC recharge calculation workbook owned by the provider, reviewed by the group tax team monthly.
  • Rec of YTD IC revenue/expense by counterparty to the TP policy margin — a margin drift flag (e.g., actual vs policy ± 1 percentage point).
  • At year-end, map IC GL accounts to Form 3CEB transaction categories (purchase of goods, services, royalties, interest, guarantees, reimbursements) and reconcile to the TB before signing.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Reimbursements vs services: pure cost reimbursements without mark-up are accepted only if the payer is the true beneficiary and the provider performs no service; tax officers often re-characterise them as services requiring a mark-up and TDS.
  • Stewardship/shareholder activities of the parent (board governance, investor relations) are not chargeable services; charging them invites disallowance in the recipient.
  • Guarantee commission on parent guarantees of a subsidiary's borrowing is an international transaction where cross-border; domestic guarantees generally are not SDTs.
  • Ind AS vs TP: IC mark-up is eliminated on consolidation (unrealised profit in inventory or capitalised costs must also be eliminated — Ind AS 110 B86(c)), but it remains real for standalone FS and tax.
  • Secondary adjustment (Sec 92CE): where a primary TP adjustment is made, the excess money must be repatriated within the prescribed time or treated as an advance with deemed interest; this has cash and IC balance consequences.

Key takeaways

  • IC recharges must be computed monthly from the TP policy (cost pool, key, mark-up) so that books, TP documentation and Form 3CEB agree.
  • Cost-plus mark-ups depend on the defined cost base; distinguish value-adding cost from pass-through.
  • Year-end true-ups need invoices, GST notes and TDS before finalisation; late adjustments break Form 3CEB reconciliation.
  • Sec 92BA applies only above ₹20 crore and mainly to entities with profit-linked deductions or concessional regimes.
  • Section references are being renumbered under the Income-tax Act 2025 — keep a mapping.

Practice Questions

Q1. Monthly cost-plus recharge [L1]

A group IT centre in Pune incurs ₹1.80 crore in a month, including ₹0.30 crore of third-party licence costs passed through at cost (split 50:30:20 by usage). Value-adding cost is allocated by number of users: Entity X 450, Entity Y 300, Entity Z 250. The mark-up is 8% on value-adding cost. Compute each entity's recharge before GST and the IT centre's margin on total cost.

Q2. Year-end true-up and Form 3CEB [L2]

A subsidiary providing contract software development to its US parent is remunerated at cost + 17%. Monthly invoices during the year were based on budgeted cost of ₹96 crore; actual cost (as per TP policy definition) is ₹104 crore. Explain the true-up required, its accounting, foreign-exchange and documentation implications, and what happens if the true-up is booked after Form 3CEB is filed.

💡 Hint: Think about which values the accountant certifies in 3CEB and the export realisation clock on the additional invoice.

Q3. Is it a specified domestic transaction? [L2]

For each case, state whether Sec 92BA applies and why: (a) two Indian group companies, both ordinary 25% regime taxpayers, with ₹45 crore of management fee recharges; (b) a power-generation undertaking claiming Sec 80-IA selling ₹60 crore of power to its group company's plant; (c) a company under Sec 115BAB buying ₹25 crore of inputs from a group company at below-market prices.

Q4. Margin drift control [L3]

Design a monthly Excel/Power BI control that compares each service provider's YTD actual operating margin on IC services to the policy margin, flags drift beyond ±1 percentage point, and produces the true-up estimate for FP&A forecasts. Specify the data inputs, calculations and outputs.

Q5. Interview: shareholder activities [L1]

An interviewer asks why a parent company cannot simply charge all its head-office costs to Indian subsidiaries at cost plus 5%. Explain the concepts of benefit test, shareholder activities and duplication.
