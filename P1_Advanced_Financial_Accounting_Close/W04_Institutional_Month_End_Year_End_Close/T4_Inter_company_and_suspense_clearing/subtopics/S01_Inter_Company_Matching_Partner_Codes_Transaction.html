Subtopic note

Inter-Company Matching: Partner Codes, Transaction Matching, Cut-Off

Scope: IC matching framework: counterparty/trading-partner codes, transaction-level matching (invoices, loans, recharges), cut-off alignment

Why it matters

Every group that consolidates (Ind AS 110) must eliminate intra-group balances, income and expenses in full (Ind AS 110 B86(c)). Elimination is only mechanical if both sides of every inter-company (IC) balance already agree. When they do not, the consolidation team ends up forcing a plug, the difference lands in an "IC difference" or suspense line, and auditors start asking whether revenue, cost or cash has been misstated in one of the entities. In Indian groups the problem is compounded by related-party disclosure under Ind AS 24 and Schedule III, transfer pricing documentation, GST on cross-charges between distinct persons, and Reg 23 SEBI LODR approvals for related-party transactions — all of which rely on the same IC data. A disciplined matching framework is therefore a close-calendar item (T+3 in the Week 4 day plan), not a consolidation afterthought.

Core notes

1. Counterparty / trading-partner codes

The foundation is that every IC posting carries a machine-readable identifier of the other group entity.

  • Trading-partner field. In SAP this is the Trading Partner (VBUND) field, defaulted from the customer/vendor master when the customer or vendor is a group company. Oracle uses an intercompany segment in the chart-of-accounts string; Tally users typically emulate it with a dedicated ledger group per counterparty ("IC – Sub A Pvt Ltd").
  • Master data rule. Every group entity is set up once as a customer and vendor in every other entity, with the trading-partner code populated and locked. Creating a group company as an ordinary third-party vendor is the most common root cause of unmatched IC.
  • Account design. IC balances sit in dedicated natural accounts (e.g., 1350 IC receivable – trade, 1355 IC loan receivable, 2350 IC payable – trade, 2355 IC loan payable, 4900 IC revenue, 6900 IC recharge expense). Combined with the partner code, this yields a matrix of account × entity × counterparty.
  • Validation. A posting to an IC account without a partner code should be blocked by ERP validation (SAP OB28-style validation) or, at minimum, caught by a close-day exception report.

2. Transaction-level matching

Balance-level comparison ("A shows ₹4.2 Cr receivable from B, B shows ₹4.0 Cr payable to A") tells you that a difference exists, not why. Transaction-level matching pairs individual documents.

IC stream Matching key Typical breaks
Trade invoices (goods) Seller invoice no. + partner code + amount (in transaction currency) Goods in transit, GRN not done, price disputes, partial receipt
Service recharges / cost allocations Recharge reference + period + partner code Recipient has not accrued; allocation key changed; GST added by one side only
IC loans Loan agreement ID; principal and interest legs separately Interest accrued by lender only; different day-count; TDS on interest booked by borrower only
Dividends / capital Board resolution reference Declared vs paid timing, TDS on dividend
Settlements Bank reference / UTR Cash in transit between entities

Rules that make matching work:

  1. Match in transaction currency, not functional currency — FX differences are a separate, explainable bucket (see s02).
  2. Separate principal, interest and withholding tax legs of IC loans so that a TDS difference does not look like a principal dispute.
  3. Tolerances for rounding only (e.g., ₹1,000 or 0.1%), applied per document, not per balance.
  4. Status tagging for every unmatched item: timing, FX, disputed, missing entry (see c01).

A simple Power Query build: load each entity's IC open-items (entity, partner, document reference, currency, amount), negate payables, append both tables, group by partner pair + reference, and filter groups where the sum is not zero. The residue is the exception list.

3. Cut-off alignment

IC differences are disproportionately cut-off differences. The group needs one rule set, applied by both sides:

  • Goods: the seller recognises revenue when control transfers (Ind AS 115.38) — often on dispatch under EXW/FCA terms. The buyer must then record the goods (and the payable) on the same basis, as goods in transit, even if the GRN is posted after month-end. Group policy should state "buyer accrues on seller's dispatch confirmation received by T+1".
  • Services and recharges: the provider issues recharges by a fixed day (e.g., T−1 or T+1), and the recipient accrues even if the invoice arrives late.
  • Loans: both sides accrue interest to the last calendar day of the month using the agreement's day-count convention.
  • IC cut-off calendar: a provisional IC confirmation at T−2 (pre-close, w4_t1 s01), final IC confirmation at T+3, and IC lock after which no IC postings are allowed without the counterpart posting simultaneously.

Illustration

Group: Alpha Ltd (India, parent), Beta Pvt Ltd (India, subsidiary). At 31 March:

Item Alpha (receivable) ₹ lakh Beta (payable) ₹ lakh Difference Tag
Invoice 2201 goods dispatched 30 March, received 2 April 48.0 — 48.0 Timing (cut-off) — Beta must record goods in transit
March management fee recharge 12.0 — 12.0 Missing entry — Beta failed to accrue
Invoice 2188 price 30.0 27.0 3.0 Disputed
Loan interest March 6.0 5.4 0.6 Missing entry — Beta booked interest net of 10% TDS
Total 96.0 32.4 63.6

Only the ₹3.0 lakh is a genuine dispute; ₹63.6 lakh collapses to that once Beta applies cut-off and accrual rules and grosses up the interest (Dr Interest 6.0 / Cr IC payable 5.4 / Cr TDS payable 0.6 — the payable to Alpha is 5.4 but Alpha's receivable of 6.0 is settled by 5.4 cash plus a TDS credit that Alpha reclassifies to TDS receivable). That last point shows why loan legs must be matched separately: the IC identity holds only after Alpha moves ₹0.6 lakh from IC receivable to TDS receivable.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Branches vs subsidiaries. Branch–HO accounts inside one legal entity net to zero within the entity's own TB; they still need matching, because a mismatch inflates both assets and liabilities in the standalone FS. GST registrations in different states are distinct persons (Sec 25(4) CGST Act), so branch cross-charges also carry GST.
  • Joint ventures and associates are not eliminated line-by-line (equity method under Ind AS 28), but transactions with them are related-party transactions and unrealised profits are eliminated to the extent of the investor's interest. Keep them in the matching tool with a different flag.
  • Group entities with different year-ends (Ind AS 110 B92–B93 allow up to three months' difference) — IC balances will never match on the reporting date; adjust for significant transactions in the gap.
  • Third-party invoices paid on behalf of a group company ("pay-on-behalf") create IC balances that one side often books to expense directly; route them through IC accounts.
  • Who is right? Policy should say which side's document prevails pending resolution — commonly the seller/provider for invoices and the lender for loans — so that the consolidation adjustment is deterministic.

Key takeaways

  • Trading-partner codes on every IC posting are the non-negotiable foundation; enforce them through master data and validation rules.
  • Match at transaction level in transaction currency, separating invoices, recharges, loan principal, interest and withholding legs.
  • Most IC breaks are cut-off: align revenue recognition on control transfer with buyer-side goods-in-transit accruals.
  • Tag every unmatched item as timing, FX, disputed or missing entry; only disputes should survive to consolidation.
  • Run provisional confirmations pre-close and final confirmation plus IC lock by T+3.

Practice Questions

Q1. Designing IC master data [L1]

List the master-data and chart-of-accounts elements you would require so that every inter-company posting in a five-entity Indian group can be matched automatically by counterparty and stream (trade, loans, recharges).

Q2. Tagging a mismatch schedule [L2]

At 31 March, Parent P shows IC receivable from S of ₹2,15,00,000; S shows IC payable to P of ₹1,62,40,000. The investigation finds: (a) P dispatched goods worth ₹38,00,000 on 31 March under FCA terms, received by S on 3 April; (b) P raised a March IT support recharge of ₹9,00,000 plus 18% GST, which S has not booked; (c) S has booked P's invoice 5541 at ₹14,60,000 against P's ₹15,00,000 claiming a short-supply; (d) P has not booked a ₹1,20,000 credit note it agreed to issue for (c) earlier. Prepare a tagged reconciliation (timing / FX / disputed / missing entry) and state which entity should post each correcting entry.

💡 Hint: Remember the recharge is invoiced gross of GST, so the receivable includes tax.

Q3. Power Query matching logic [L3]

Describe step by step how you would build a Power Query solution that takes IC open-item extracts from four entities (columns: Entity, Partner, Stream, DocRef, Currency, Amount) and outputs (i) a counterparty-pair matrix of net differences and (ii) a document-level exception list. Explain how you would handle partial matches where one invoice has been part-paid.

Q4. Cut-off policy for a manufacturing group [L2]

A group's Indian plant sells semi-finished goods to its Indian distribution subsidiary under DAP terms; transit takes 4–6 days. Draft the IC cut-off policy wording for month-end (who recognises what, on which document, by which close day) and explain how it interacts with Ind AS 115 control transfer.

Q5. Interview: why balance-level IC confirmation is not enough [L1]

In an interview for a group reporting role, you are asked why a monthly IC balance confirmation e-mail between entities is not an adequate control. Give a structured answer referencing transaction-level matching, root-cause tagging and consolidation eliminations.
