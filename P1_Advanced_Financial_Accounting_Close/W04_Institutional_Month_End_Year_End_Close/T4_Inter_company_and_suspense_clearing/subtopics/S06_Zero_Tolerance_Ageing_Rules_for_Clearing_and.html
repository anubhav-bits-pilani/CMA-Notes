Zero-Tolerance Ageing Rules for Clearing and Suspense Accounts

Scope: Zero-tolerance ageing: target nil at month-end, 0–30/31–60/>60-day buckets, mandatory escalation or write-off

Why it matters

A clearing account that is "usually small" is not a control; a clearing account with a nil target, item-level ageing and automatic escalation is. Zero-tolerance ageing converts a soft expectation into a measurable rule that reviewers, internal auditors and statutory auditors can test. It also prevents the classic failure mode in Indian mid-sized companies: suspense balances that accumulate quietly for years and are finally written off in a single large Q4 hit, distorting EBITDA and raising questions under SA 240 about management override. For the Week 4 Project 1 workbook, "suspense nil" is one of the master check flags.

Core notes

1. Target: nil at month-end

  • Suspense accounts (unidentified receipts, IC suspense, migration suspense, general suspense) — target closing balance nil. If not nil, every rupee must be itemised and aged (c03).
  • Clearing accounts (GR/IR, bank clearing, payroll clearing, cash-in-transit) — a balance is expected at month-end because processes straddle the cut-off, but every item must be itemised, explainable by a known process lag, and inside the expected clearing window (e.g., bank clearing 3 working days, cash-in-transit 2 days, GR/IR 30 days).
  • "Zero tolerance" therefore means zero unexplained and zero over-age items, not necessarily zero balance for clearing accounts.

2. Ageing buckets

Ageing is computed per item from the original posting date (not the last reclassification date — re-posting an item to "refresh" its age is a red flag).

Bucket Status Required action
0–30 days Normal process lag Preparer clears in the normal course; item listed with expected clearing event
31–60 days Exception Escalated to financial controller with root cause and target date; reported in close KPIs
> 60 days Breach Mandatory resolution: clear, reclassify to the correct balance sheet line with evidence, or write off with approval per delegation of authority; reported to CFO (and audit committee if material)

Some groups use tighter windows for high-risk accounts (unidentified receipts: escalate at 15 days) and looser ones for GR/IR in project businesses (justified by long inspection cycles) — the policy should list exceptions explicitly.

3. Mandatory escalation or write-off

Escalation ladder (example):

Trigger Escalate to Evidence
Any suspense balance not nil at T+4 Financial controller Itemised listing, aged
Item 31–60 days or > ₹10 lakh Financial controller Root-cause note
Item > 60 days CFO Resolution plan / write-off proposal
Aggregate > 60-day items > 1% of PBT, or any single item > performance materiality Audit committee pack Summary with remediation

Write-off rules:

  • Write-off is the last resort, after reasonable efforts to identify. It requires approval under the delegation of authority matrix (e.g., ≤ ₹1 lakh controller, ≤ ₹10 lakh CFO, above that CFO + audit committee notification).
  • The write-off must be classified by nature, not to "miscellaneous expense": unidentified customer receipts that are genuinely unclaimable go to other income; unreconciled payment differences to the relevant expense; IC differences resolved bilaterally (topic pitfall).
  • Record root cause and a process fix; a recurring write-off category is a control deficiency, not a close task.
  • Consider tax: write-offs of liabilities can be income (Sec 41(1)) and write-offs of receivables are deductible only if previously offered to tax (Sec 36(1)(vii)/36(2)).

4. Automating the ageing

Excel (structured table Items with columns Account, DocDate, Amount, Status, Owner; named cell CloseDate):

AgeDays    = CloseDate - [@DocDate]
Bucket     = IFS([@AgeDays]<=30,"0-30",[@AgeDays]<=60,"31-60",TRUE,">60")
BreachFlag = IF(OR([@Bucket]=">60",AND([@Bucket]="31-60",ABS([@Amount])>=EscalationAmt)),"ESCALATE","")

Summary by account and bucket with SUMIFS(Items[Amount],Items[Account],A2,Items[Bucket],B1); conditional formatting turns the > 60 column red when non-zero. In Power BI, a calculated column for the bucket and a measure for "> 60-day balance" drive a KPI card with a target of zero; the check feeds the master check flag (suspense nil and no > 60 items).

5. Illustration

Unidentified receipts at 30 June (close date), escalation amount ₹5 lakh:

Item Date ₹ Age Bucket Action
UTR 8812 24 June 2,40,000 6 0–30 Apply on remittance advice
UTR 7710 20 May 6,80,000 41 31–60 Escalate (≥ ₹5 lakh) — controller
UTR 7011 2 May 1,10,000 59 31–60 Exception list
UTR 5520 12 March 85,000 110 > 60 CFO; write-off proposal to other income if unclaimed after customer outreach
Total 11,15,000 Suspense not nil → itemised listing to controller at T+4

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Re-dating by reclassification: moving an item to a new account resets its document date in many ERPs; ageing must carry the original date through a reference field.
  • Partial clearing (part of a receipt applied) — age the residual from the original date.
  • Netting a debit and credit item to "clear" them without a genuine matching relationship is prohibited; it destroys the ageing trail (w4_t2 pitfall).
  • Year-end: a > 60-day item cannot be presented as suspense in Schedule III. If not resolved by the reporting date, classify by best evidence (e.g., unidentified receipts → other current financial liabilities) and disclose if material.
  • Legitimate long-lag items (e.g., GR/IR for imported capital goods awaiting customs clearance) — document as policy exceptions with expected clearing date rather than ignoring the breach.
  • Materiality alignment: escalation amounts should be consistent with flux thresholds and JE review thresholds (week cross-cutting item on consistent materiality).

Key takeaways

  • Suspense accounts target nil; clearing accounts target zero unexplained and zero over-age items.
  • Age items from the original posting date in 0–30, 31–60 and > 60-day buckets.
  • 31–60 days is an exception requiring controller escalation; > 60 days is a breach requiring resolution or write-off.
  • Write-offs need delegated approval, classification by nature, root cause and tax review.
  • Automate ageing and feed a zero-target KPI into the master check flag.

Practice Questions

Q1. Bucket and escalate [L1]

With a close date of 31 March and an escalation amount of ₹3 lakh, classify the following suspense items and state the required action: ₹1,20,000 posted 2 March; ₹4,50,000 posted 18 February; ₹60,000 posted 10 January; ₹2,90,000 posted 25 January.

Q2. Ageing formula build [L2]

Write the Excel formulas (using a structured table and named cells, no absolute cell references) to compute age, bucket and an escalation flag, and a summary table by account and bucket that also flags any account whose suspense balance is not nil. Explain how you would prevent re-dated items from understating age.

Q3. Write-off governance [L2]

The migration suspense account from an ERP upgrade 18 months ago carries a net debit of ₹38 lakh (gross debits ₹1.12 crore, gross credits ₹74 lakh). Management proposes to write off the net ₹38 lakh to miscellaneous expenses in Q4. Critique the proposal and set out the steps, approvals, classification and tax considerations you would require.

💡 Hint: Consider what the gross items represent and Sec 41(1).

Q4. Policy exceptions [L3]

An EPC company argues that a 60-day breach rule is unworkable for its GR/IR account because site inspections take 90 days. Draft a policy exception clause that preserves the control objective, including compensating controls and reporting.

Q5. Audit committee reporting [L2]

Design a one-page audit committee exhibit on clearing and suspense accounts for a listed company, including the metrics, trend and thresholds you would show.
