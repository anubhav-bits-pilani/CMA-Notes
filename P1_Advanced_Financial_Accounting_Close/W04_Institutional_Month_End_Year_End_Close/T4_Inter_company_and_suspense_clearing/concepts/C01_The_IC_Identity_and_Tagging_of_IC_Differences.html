Key concept

The IC Identity and Tagging of IC Differences

Concept: IC identity: Receivable in A = Payable in B at the same rate; differences tagged timing, FX, disputed, missing entry

Statement

For every inter-company pair (A, B), stream and currency, after both entities have applied the group cut-off and rate policy:

\[ R_{A \to B} = P_{B \to A} \]

where \(R_{A \to B}\) is the IC receivable recorded by A from B and \(P_{B \to A}\) is the IC payable recorded by B to A, both expressed in the transaction currency, or in the reporting currency using the same group rate. Any non-zero difference is decomposed:

\[ R_{A \to B} - P_{B \to A} = D_{\text{timing}} + D_{\text{FX}} + D_{\text{disputed}} + D_{\text{missing}} \]

with \(D_{\text{timing}}\) = items recorded in different periods (goods/cash in transit, late accruals), \(D_{\text{FX}}\) = differences from using different rates or dates, \(D_{\text{disputed}}\) = items both sides acknowledge but disagree on, \(D_{\text{missing}}\) = items one side has not recorded at all (including withholding-tax legs not reclassified).

Explanation

The identity is a restatement of double entry at group level: one transaction cannot create an asset in one group company without an equal liability in another. When it fails, something is wrong in at least one set of books — and consolidation (Ind AS 110 B86(c)) cannot eliminate a balance that does not agree.

The four tags matter because each has a different owner and a different fix:

  • Timing — fixed by cut-off rules (buyer accrues goods in transit on seller's dispatch; recipient accrues recharges). Not a dispute; should be resolved by T+3.
  • FX — fixed by a single group rate policy and matching in transaction currency. Genuine exchange differences sit in the entity with foreign-currency exposure and survive elimination in consolidated P&L (Ind AS 21.45); artificial differences from inconsistent rates must be corrected.
  • Disputed — resolved through the dispute ladder; pending resolution the default rule (e.g., seller's invoice prevails) applies.
  • Missing entry — the most serious: one side has an unrecorded liability or revenue. Includes TDS deducted by the payer which the payee has not moved to TDS receivable, and RCM/GST elements booked by one side only.

The identity holds "at the same rate" — matching an INR balance in one entity with a USD balance in another at different rates guarantees a spurious difference.

Illustration

A Ltd (India) and B Ltd (India) at 31 March, ₹ lakh:

₹ lakh Tag
A's receivable from B 210.0
B's payable to A 168.5
Difference 41.5
Goods dispatched 31 March, B records on 2 April 25.0 Timing
A's March recharge not accrued by B 11.8 Missing entry (B)
B deducted 10% TDS on ₹27 lakh royalty; A still shows gross 2.7 Missing entry (A — reclassify to TDS receivable)
Price dispute on invoice 774 2.0 Disputed
Unexplained 0.0

After corrections, A's receivable is 207.3 and B's payable 205.3; the residual 2.0 is the dispute, handled under the default rule before consolidation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Decompose an IC difference [L2]

P Ltd shows a receivable of ₹3,46,00,000 from S Ltd; S Ltd shows a payable of ₹2,98,70,000. Findings: (a) S booked a ₹30,00,000 invoice dated 31 March on 4 April; (b) S deducted 2% TDS under Sec 194C on ₹2,50,00,000 of job-work invoices; P has not reclassified it; (c) S has not booked a ₹9,00,000 debit note for freight that it disputes; (d) a ₹3,30,000 difference remains unexplained. Tag each item and state the correcting entries and which entity posts them.

💡 Hint: Quantify (a)–(c) first and confirm the residual equals (d) before deciding how (d) is escalated.

Q2. "At the same rate" [L1]

Explain, with a short numerical example of your own, why an IC USD balance can show an INR difference even when both entities have recorded every invoice, and how the identity should be tested to avoid this.

Q3. Interview: which tag worries you most? [L3]

An interviewer asks which of the four difference tags is most concerning from an audit and control perspective and why. Prepare a structured answer covering misstatement risk, consolidation impact and process fixes.
