Key concept

Suspense Control: Nil or Fully Itemised, With Escalation

Concept: Suspense control: closing balance nil or fully itemised; items >30/60 days escalated to controller

Statement

At each month-end, for every suspense or clearing account \(k\):

\[ B_{k} = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad B_{k} = \sum_{n=1}^{N} b_{k,n} \text{ with every } b_{k,n} \text{ identified, dated and owned} \]

where \(B_{k}\) is the GL closing balance of the account and \(b_{k,n}\) are the individual open items (each carrying original posting date, source document, description, owner and expected clearing event). The itemised listing must reconcile exactly to the GL (no "unreconciled difference" line). Escalation rule:

\[ \text{Age}_{k,n} = \text{Close date} - \text{Original posting date}_{k,n} \]

Items with \(\text{Age} > 30\) days are escalated to the financial controller; items with \(\text{Age} > 60\) days require controller/CFO-approved resolution (clear, reclassify or write off).

Explanation

The control has three components, each tested separately:

  1. Completeness of itemisation — the open-item listing sums to the GL balance. A suspense account that is "₹14 lakh, mostly old stuff" fails the control even if the amount is immaterial, because no-one can say what it contains.
  2. Quality of items — each item has a source reference (UTR, document number), a description of what it is believed to be, and an owner. An item that cannot be described is escalated immediately regardless of age.
  3. Ageing and escalation — computed from the original posting date so that reposting does not reset the clock. The 30/60-day triggers match the buckets in s06.

Why nil or itemised? Suspense is by definition a temporary classification. Schedule III has no suspense line, so at reporting date every item must be presented according to its nature — which is only possible if it is itemised. Under SA 240, unexplained suspense balances are a classic location for concealment; under ICFR (Sec 134(5)(e), Sec 143(3)(i)), the absence of a reconciliation with ageing and review evidence is a control deficiency regardless of amount.

Evidence of operation: preparer sign-off with date, reviewer sign-off, the itemised listing, ageing summary and escalation e-mails/tickets for > 30-day items.

Illustration

IC suspense at 31 August, GL balance ₹7,45,000 Dr:

Item Posted Age ₹ Description Owner Action
1 21 Aug 10 3,20,000 Payment to unknown group entity, UTR 99812 AP lead Identify partner; clear by T+2
2 25 July 37 2,85,000 IC difference with S3, disputed freight IC lead Escalate to controller
3 18 June 74 1,40,000 Unknown — Breach: > 60 days and unidentified — CFO; resolve before quarter-end
Listing total 7,45,000 Agrees to GL

The account is itemised (listing = GL), but fails "nil", has one > 30-day and one > 60-day item, and item 2 breaks the rule that IC differences are not parked in suspense (topic pitfall).

Practice Questions

Q1. Test the control [L1]

A suspense account has a GL balance of ₹12,60,000. The open-item listing totals ₹11,95,000 with a final line "difference under investigation ₹65,000". Items are aged 5, 22, 41 and 95 days. State every way in which the control fails and the remediation required.

Q2. Escalation pack [L2]

Using the following open items in an unidentified-receipts suspense account at 30 September — ₹4,50,000 (12 Sep), ₹2,10,000 (20 Aug), ₹6,80,000 (14 Jul), ₹95,000 (2 Jun) — prepare the escalation schedule with buckets and owners, and draft the controller escalation note for the items requiring it.

💡 Hint: Compute ages from the original posting dates and apply both the 30- and 60-day triggers.

Q3. Immaterial but failing [L3]

An auditor reports a control deficiency for a ₹2 lakh unitemised suspense balance in a company with ₹40 crore PBT. Management argues the amount is trivial. Evaluate both positions with reference to ICFR, SA 240 and Schedule III presentation.
