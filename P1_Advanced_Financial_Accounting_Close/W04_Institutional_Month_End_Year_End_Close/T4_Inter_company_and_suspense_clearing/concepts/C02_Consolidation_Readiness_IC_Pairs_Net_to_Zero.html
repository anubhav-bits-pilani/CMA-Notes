Key concept

Consolidation Readiness: IC Pairs Net to Zero

Concept: Consolidation readiness: Σ IC receivables − Σ IC payables = 0 per counterparty pair after adjustments

Statement

For every counterparty pair \((i, j)\) in the group, after cut-off, FX and dispute adjustments:

\[ \sum R_{i \to j} - \sum P_{j \to i} = 0 \]

where \(\sum R_{i \to j}\) is the total of all IC receivable-type balances (trade, loans, interest, recharges, other) recorded by entity \(i\) against entity \(j\), and \(\sum P_{j \to i}\) is the total of all IC payable-type balances recorded by \(j\) against \(i\), all in the group reporting currency at the group rate. The same condition applies to IC income and expense streams for the period (IC revenue of \(i\) from \(j\) = IC cost of \(j\) from \(i\)), and to IC dividends and investment/equity.

Group-level corollary:

\[ \sum_{i}\sum_{j \neq i} \left(R_{i \to j} - P_{j \to i}\right) = 0 \]

Explanation

c01 is the identity for a single balance; c02 is the readiness gate before consolidation runs. It is tested per pair, not only in total, because offsetting errors (A–B over by ₹50 lakh, C–D under by ₹50 lakh) net to zero at group level but mean four entities' standalone FS are wrong and the eliminations will be posted against the wrong lines.

Practical points:

  • After adjustments means after entity-level corrections are posted in the entity books (or in a documented consolidation adjustment layer), not after a plug in the consolidation tool.
  • Per stream checks (trade vs loan vs interest) catch classification errors — an IC loan recorded as trade payable in one entity eliminates in total but misstates current/non-current classification in standalone FS.
  • Matrix view: a counterparty-pair matrix (rows = reporting entity, columns = counterparty, cell = net balance) is symmetric in absolute value when the group is ready; any asymmetry is the exception list.
  • Tolerance: only rounding-level differences are acceptable; anything above that is posted to a dedicated "IC elimination difference" account with sign-off, never to suspense, and analysed for FX (Ind AS 21.45 differences belong in P&L, not in the elimination difference).
  • Unrealised profit (Ind AS 110 B86(c)) is a separate elimination — the pair can net to zero while inventory still contains IC margin.

Illustration

Three-entity group, ₹ lakh, after adjustments:

Pair Receivable recorded Payable recorded by counterparty Net
P → S1 420.0 420.0 0.0
P → S2 155.0 149.0 6.0
S1 → S2 88.0 96.0 (8.0)
Group total 663.0 665.0 (2.0)

The group total of (2.0) looks trivial, but the pair view shows two breaks of 6.0 and 8.0 — neither of which is "ready". Investigation finds S2 recorded a ₹6.0 lakh invoice from P under S1's trading-partner code. Correcting the partner code moves S2's payables to P 155.0 and to S1 90.0, so the pair nets become 0.0 and (2.0) — leaving a genuine ₹2.0 lakh difference between S1 and S2 to be tagged.

Connections

Builds on c01 and s01–s02; feeds w3_t5 eliminations and the Project 1 check flag "IC nets to zero". Related to w2_t6 RPT disclosures (pair-level balances are disclosed by related party) and to the counterparty-pair matrix built in Excel/Power Query (topic related item). For FP&A, the same pair test applied to budget IC flows prevents consolidated budget revenue being inflated by unmatched IC sales.

Practice Questions

Q1. Matrix analysis [L2]

Prepare a counterparty-pair matrix from the following balances (₹ lakh) and identify the exceptions: H records receivables from A 300, from B 120; A records payable to H 300, receivable from B 75; B records payables to H 102, to A 75, and a receivable from H of 18 (which H has recorded as a payable of 18). Explain what the group total hides.

💡 Hint: Net each pair across both directions before comparing.

Q2. Readiness gate design [L3]

Design the readiness gate for a 15-entity group's quarter-end consolidation: the tests (balance, stream and P&L), tolerances, sign-off owners, and what happens if a pair fails at T+4 when the board meeting date under Reg 33 is fixed.

Q3. Why not just plug? [L1]

Explain why posting the residual IC difference to a consolidation plug account is not acceptable even when the amount is below group materiality.
