Key concept

Interest Circularity: Average Debt, Iteration and Circuit Breakers

Concept: Circularity: interest on average debt solved via iterative calculation with a circuit-breaker switch, or interest on opening balances

Statement

Interest on average debt creates a loop:

\[ \text{Interest}_t = r \times \frac{D_{t-1} + D_t}{2}, \qquad D_t = f(\text{Cash}_t), \qquad \text{Cash}_t = g(\text{PAT}_t), \qquad \text{PAT}_t = h(\text{Interest}_t) \]

where \(r\) is the interest rate, \(D_{t-1}\) and \(D_t\) are opening and closing debt, and closing debt depends (through a revolver or cash sweep) on cash generated, which depends on interest. Two accepted solutions:

  1. Iterative calculation (File → Options → Formulas → Enable iterative calculation, e.g. maximum 100 iterations, maximum change 0.001) with a circuit breaker:

\[ \text{Interest}_t = \text{CB} \times r \times \frac{D_{t-1} + D_t}{2} + (1 - \text{CB}) \times r \times D_{t-1} \]

where CB = 1 enables average-balance interest and CB = 0 falls back to opening balances.

  1. Interest on opening balances only: \(\text{Interest}_t = r \times D_{t-1}\), which has no loop.

Explanation

Average-balance interest is more accurate when debt changes significantly within a year (large drawdowns for capex, scheduled repayments). Opening-balance interest understates interest when debt rises and overstates it when debt falls. The error is roughly \(r \times \Delta D / 2\), which is small for stable debt and material for a capex-funded expansion.

The danger of iteration is error propagation. If any cell in the loop returns an error (a #DIV/0! from a zero denominator, a #REF!), the error circulates and Excel cannot recover even after the cause is fixed, because every cell in the loop keeps feeding the error back. The circuit breaker cuts the loop: set CB = 0, let the model recalculate cleanly, fix the cause and set CB = 1 again.

Good practice:

  • Put the CB switch on the Inputs sheet (blue input, data validation 0/1) and show its state on the Cover.
  • Build and balance the model with CB = 0 first (s07, block D2-2). Turn on CB = 1 afterwards and confirm both checks remain 0.
  • Confine the circularity to one loop (interest ↔ cash/debt). Interest income on average cash creates a second loop, so include it in the same switch.
  • Document iteration settings on the Cover. Iterative calculation is an application-level setting saved with the workbook, and a recipient's Excel may open with it off, triggering circular reference warnings.
  • Alternatives that avoid iteration: opening-balance interest, a copy-paste macro that pastes the computed interest as values until convergence (Week 15), or an algebraic closed-form solution for a simple revolver.

Many institutional standards (FAST, some bank modelling guides) discourage circularity entirely and prefer opening-balance interest or a macro. In a timed capstone, either approach is acceptable if it is disclosed and the switch works.

Illustration

Term loan opening FY28 ₹600 crore, drawdown ₹300 crore during FY28, repayment ₹50 crore, so closing ₹850 crore. Rate 9.0%.

Method Interest FY28 (₹ crore)
Opening balance: 9% × 600 54.0
Average balance: 9% × (600 + 850) ÷ 2 65.3
Difference (pre-tax) 11.3
After tax at 25.17% 8.4

If the drawdown is funded by a revolver that depends on year-end cash, closing debt depends on interest, so iteration is needed. With a fixed drawdown schedule, average interest is not circular at all, because closing debt does not depend on interest. Circularity only arises when debt or cash balances are themselves outputs of the cash flow.

Practice Questions

Q1. Circuit-breaker formula [L1]

Write the Excel formula for FY28 interest in F40, with opening debt in E35, closing debt in F35, rate in a named cell Int_Rate and the circuit breaker in a named cell CB.

💡 Hint: One formula should produce both behaviours depending on the switch.

Q2. When is it circular? [L2]

For each case, state whether average-balance interest creates a circular reference and why: (a) fixed repayment schedule; (b) cash sweep repaying debt from excess cash; (c) revolver drawn to maintain minimum cash of ₹50 crore; (d) interest income on average cash balances.

Q3. Recovering a broken loop [L2]

After you delete a row on the Debt tab, every cell in the IS, BS and CFS shows #REF! and fixing the deleted reference does not clear it. Explain why, and list the recovery steps.

Q4. Choosing the method [L3]

A reviewer insists on no circularity. Your company draws ₹1,200 crore of debt in FY28 for a new plant (opening debt ₹400 crore) at 9.5%. Quantify the interest understatement under opening-balance interest, and propose a non-circular method that gets close to average-balance accuracy.
