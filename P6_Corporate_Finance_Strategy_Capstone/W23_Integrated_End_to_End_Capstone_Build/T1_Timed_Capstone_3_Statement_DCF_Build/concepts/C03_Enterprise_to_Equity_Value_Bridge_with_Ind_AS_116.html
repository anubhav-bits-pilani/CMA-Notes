Key concept

Enterprise-to-Equity Value Bridge with Ind AS 116 Leases

Concept: Equity value = EV − Net debt (incl. lease liabilities if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116) − NCI − preference + non-operating investments

Statement

\[ \text{Equity value} = \text{EV} - \text{Net debt} - \text{NCI} - \text{Pref} + \text{NOA} \]

\[ \text{Net debt} = \text{Borrowings (current + non-current)} + \text{Lease liabilities}^{*} - \text{Cash and cash equivalents} - \text{Liquid investments} \]

where EV is the present value of FCFF plus PV of terminal value, \(^{*}\)lease liabilities are included when EBITDA and FCFF are on the post-Ind AS 116 basis, NCI is the value of non-controlling interests in consolidated subsidiaries, Pref is redeemable or irredeemable preference capital (at redemption or market value), and NOA is non-operating assets not reflected in FCFF (investments in associates/JVs, surplus land, assets held for sale). Value per share = Equity value ÷ diluted shares.

Explanation

The DCF values the operating business for all capital providers. To reach common shareholders' value, remove every other claim and add every asset whose income was excluded from FCFF. The governing rule is consistency: an item enters the bridge if and only if its cash flows are excluded from FCFF.

  • Leases. On the post-Ind AS 116 basis, rent is not in EBITDA, so lease payments are not in FCFF, and the lease liability is a debt-like claim that must be deducted. On the pre-116 (EBITDAaL) basis, rent is already deducted in FCFF, so deducting lease liabilities again double-counts (topic pitfall 3).
  • Cash and liquid investments (bank balances, FDs, liquid and debt mutual funds) are added (netted in net debt) because their income was excluded from EBIT. Restrict this to surplus cash. Trapped or minimum operating cash is arguably part of NWC.
  • NCI. Consolidated FCFF includes 100% of subsidiaries' cash flows, so the minority share must be removed, ideally at fair value (P/E or EV multiple applied to the subsidiary's earnings), and at book value only if immaterial.
  • Preference shares are senior to equity. Under Ind AS 32 redeemable preference shares are often already classified as financial liabilities (and so may already be in borrowings). Check the note to avoid a double deduction.
  • Non-operating investments. Associates and JVs are equity-accounted, so their profit is below EBIT. Add their carrying value or, if listed, their market value less a holding-company discount.
  • Other debt-like items to consider: unfunded gratuity and leave encashment obligations (net of tax), acceptances or supplier-finance arrangements classified within trade payables, dividends declared but unpaid, and contingent liabilities that are probable (Ind AS 37).

Use the balance sheet closest to the valuation date (the half-year statement of assets and liabilities under LODR Reg 33) and diluted shares (treasury stock method for vested in-the-money ESOPs).

Illustration

Valuation date 30 November 2026, latest BS 30 September 2026 (₹ crore). EV from DCF 9,850 (post-116 basis).

Bridge item ₹ crore

− Borrowings (1,120)

If the model were on the EBITDAaL basis, the ₹340 crore lease liability line would be removed and equity value would be ₹9,235 crore, but EV itself would also be lower because rent would reduce FCFF.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the bridge [L1]

EV ₹6,420 crore (post-116). Borrowings ₹890 crore, lease liabilities ₹210 crore, cash ₹145 crore, FDs ₹300 crore, treasury investments in debt MFs ₹220 crore, NCI at book ₹120 crore, investment in JV ₹95 crore, unfunded gratuity ₹40 crore (tax rate 25.17%). Basic shares 12.0 crore; in-the-money vested options 0.3 crore at an exercise price of ₹150 with a share price of ₹480. Compute equity value and diluted value per share.

💡 Hint: Use the treasury stock method for the options.

Q2. Basis mismatch [L2]

An analyst's FCFF deducts ₹180 crore of annual rent (pre-116 basis), and the bridge deducts lease liabilities of ₹1,050 crore. Explain the effect on equity value and how a reviewer would detect it from the model.

Q3. Redeemable preference shares [L2]

A company has ₹100 crore of 8% redeemable preference shares classified as a financial liability under Ind AS 32 and included in non-current borrowings. Explain how this affects the bridge and what goes wrong if "preference" is deducted again as a separate line.
