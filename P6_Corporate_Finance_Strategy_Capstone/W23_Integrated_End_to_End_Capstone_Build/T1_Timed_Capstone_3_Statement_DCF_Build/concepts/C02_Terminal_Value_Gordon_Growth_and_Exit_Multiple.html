Key concept

Terminal Value: Gordon Growth and Exit Multiple Methods

Concept: Terminal value (Gordon) = FCFF\_(n+1) ÷ (WACC − g); exit-multiple TV = EBITDA\_n × EV/EBITDA multiple

Statement

\[ \text{TV}^{\text{Gordon}}_n = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{n+1}}{\text{WACC} - g}, \qquad \text{FCFF}_{n+1} = \text{NOPAT}_{n} (1+g) \times \left(1 - \frac{g}{\text{RONIC}}\right) \]

\[ \text{TV}^{\text{Exit}}_n = \text{EBITDA}_n \times \left(\frac{\text{EV}}{\text{EBITDA}}\right)_{\text{peer}} \]

where \(n\) is the last explicit forecast year, \(g\) is the perpetual nominal growth rate of FCFF, WACC is the long-run weighted average cost of capital, RONIC is the return on new invested capital, and the multiple is a peer (or through-cycle) enterprise value to EBITDA ratio on the same lease basis as \(\text{EBITDA}_n\). Both give a value as at the end of year \(n\), which must then be discounted to the valuation date.

Explanation

Gordon growth values the business as a growing perpetuity of cash flows. It requires:

  • \(g < \text{WACC}\), otherwise the formula breaks down;
  • \(g\) at or below long-run nominal GDP growth. In India, roughly 4–6% is commonly defended (real growth plus inflation of about 4%, with growth fading as the economy matures). No company can outgrow the economy forever (topic pitfall 2);
  • a steady-state \(\text{FCFF}_{n+1}\): margins normalised, capex consistent with growth. The reinvestment rate \(g/\text{RONIC}\) ensures growth is "paid for". Using \(\text{FCFF}_n \times (1+g)\) when year \(n\) still contains growth capex for a new plant understates TV. Using it when year \(n\) has abnormally low capex overstates TV.

Exit multiple assumes the business could be sold at the end of year \(n\) at the market's typical multiple. It is intuitive and market-anchored, but it imports today's sentiment into a value 10 years out and is circular if the point of the DCF was to test whether the market multiple is justified. Use a through-cycle median, and be consistent: a post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA needs a post-116 multiple.

Cross-checks. The two methods can be reconciled:

\[ g_{\text{implied}} = \frac{\text{TV}^{\text{Exit}}_n \times \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_n}{\text{TV}^{\text{Exit}}_n + \text{FCFF}_n} \]

(using \(\text{FCFF}_{n+1} = \text{FCFF}_n(1+g)\)), and the Gordon-implied multiple is \(\text{TV}^{\text{Gordon}}_n \div \text{EBITDA}_n\). Report TV as % of EV. Above about 75–80% the valuation rests on the perpetuity assumptions, which is a reason to lengthen the explicit period or justify the figure.

Discounting. With the mid-year convention, a Gordon TV is discounted at \(n - 0.5\) (its flows also arrive mid-year), and an exit TV at \(n\) (a sale at year-end) (s06).

Illustration

FY36 (year 10): EBITDA ₹900 crore, NOPAT ₹520 crore. WACC 12.5%, g 5.0%, RONIC 15%, peer EV/EBITDA 12.0×.

\[ \text{FCFF}_{11} = 520 \times 1.05 \times \left(1 - \frac{0.05}{0.15}\right) = 364.0, \qquad \text{TV}^{\text{Gordon}} = \frac{364.0}{0.125 - 0.05} = 4{,}853 \]

\[ \text{TV}^{\text{Exit}} = 900 \times 12.0 = 10{,}800 \]

The Gordon TV implies 5.4× EBITDA, while the exit TV implies perpetual growth well above 5%. That gap says the peer multiple prices in either higher returns (RONIC) or near-term growth that the perpetuity does not capture. The analyst must explain which view is more credible, not average the two blindly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Gordon TV with reinvestment [L2]

Final explicit year NOPAT ₹340 crore, g 5.5%, RONIC 14%, WACC 12.0%. Compute FCFF for year n+1, the Gordon TV and its PV under mid-year convention if n = 10.

💡 Hint: Reinvestment rate equals growth divided by RONIC.

Q2. Implied growth from an exit multiple [L2]

FCFF in year n is ₹410 crore, EBITDA ₹760 crore, WACC 11.8% and the exit multiple 15.0×. Compute the implied perpetual growth rate and comment on whether it is defensible for an Indian building-materials company.

Q3. Year n not in steady state [L3]

Your year 10 includes ₹300 crore of growth capex for a plant commissioning in year 11, which depresses FCFF to ₹80 crore against a normalised ₹380 crore. Explain how using \(\text{FCFF}_n \times (1+g)\) distorts TV and how you would construct a proper \(\text{FCFF}_{n+1}\).
