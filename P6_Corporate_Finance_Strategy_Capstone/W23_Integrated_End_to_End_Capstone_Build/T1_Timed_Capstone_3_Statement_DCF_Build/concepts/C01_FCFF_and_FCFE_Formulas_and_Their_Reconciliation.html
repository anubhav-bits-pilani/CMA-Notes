Key concept

FCFF and FCFE Formulas and Their Reconciliation

Concept: FCFF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − Capex − Δ NWC; FCFE = FCFF − Interest × (1 − t) + Net borrowing

Statement

\[ \text{FCFF}_t = \text{EBIT}_t \times (1 - t) + \text{D\&A}_t - \text{Capex}_t - \Delta \text{NWC}_t \]

\[ \text{FCFE}_t = \text{FCFF}_t - \text{Interest}_t \times (1 - t) + \text{Net borrowing}_t \]

where \(\text{EBIT}_t\) is operating profit before interest and tax (excluding non-operating other income), \(t\) is the marginal tax rate (25.17% under Sec 115BAA), \(\text{D\&A}_t\) is depreciation and amortisation, \(\text{Capex}_t\) is cash spent on PPE and intangibles including CWIP, \(\Delta\text{NWC}_t\) is the increase in operating net working capital, \(\text{Interest}_t\) is finance cost on debt, and \(\text{Net borrowing}_t\) is new borrowings minus repayments.

Explanation

FCFF is the cash generated by the operating business that is available to all capital providers (lenders and shareholders) after the reinvestment needed to sustain and grow it. Taxing EBIT at the full rate gives "unlevered" tax, as though the firm had no debt. The tax saving on interest is captured in the after-tax cost of debt in WACC, so it must not be counted twice. FCFF is therefore discounted at WACC to give enterprise value.

FCFE is what remains for shareholders after lenders are paid: after-tax interest is deducted, and net new borrowing is added because it is cash shareholders can access. FCFE is discounted at Ke to give equity value directly. For a stable capital structure, both routes should give similar equity values. Large differences usually come from inconsistent leverage assumptions.

Conditions and refinements:

  • ΔNWC sign: an increase in receivables or inventory is a use of cash, so it is deducted. A release adds to FCFF.
  • Capex is gross cash capex, not the change in net block (that would net off depreciation and double-count).
  • Leases (post-Ind AS 116 basis): if lease liabilities are in net debt, treat lease interest as financing (EBIT already excludes it) and ROU depreciation as D&A, and deduct new ROU additions as lease capex. In FCFE, lease principal and interest are debt service.
  • Non-cash items other than D&A (share-based payments, provisions) can be added back. Many practitioners treat ESOP cost as a real expense and do not add it back.
  • Tax: use cash tax if losses or MAT credits make it differ materially in specific years. Otherwise the marginal rate keeps the formula clean.
  • FCFE is preferred for financials (s01), where debt is operating.

FY28 forecast for a mid-cap (₹ crore): EBIT 420.0 (excluding treasury income), D&A 110.0, capex 190.0, NWC increase 35.0, interest 42.0, new borrowings 80.0, repayments 50.0, t = 25.17%.

Cross-check from the CFS: CFO − capex + net borrowing ≈ FCFE when the CFS classifies interest paid within financing and other income is small. Differences usually trace to other income, tax timing or working capital definitions.

Connections

FCFF feeds the DCF tab (s06) and the terminal value (c02). Its present value is enterprise value, which is bridged to equity value in c03, and it is discounted at the WACC built in c04. The lease treatment links to the historical restatement in s03 and to the double-counting pitfall in the topic. In FP&A practice, FCFF is the "free cash flow" KPI in board packs (Week 20), and the ΔNWC term connects to working capital release recommendations in the Week 23 t4 deck.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute FCFF and FCFE [L1]

FY29 (₹ crore): EBITDA 680, D&A 150, other income 40 (interest on FDs), interest 55, capex 260, inventory up 30, receivables up 22, payables up 18, borrowings drawn 120, repaid 90, tax rate 25.17%. Compute FCFF and FCFE.

💡 Hint: Remove treasury income from EBIT before taxing it.

Q2. Double-count spotting [L2]

A colleague computes FCFF as PAT + D&A + interest − capex − ΔNWC and then discounts it at WACC. Explain precisely what is wrong with the tax treatment and how the error biases enterprise value.

Q3. Lease-heavy FCFE [L2]

A retail chain has FY28 EBIT (post-116) of ₹210 crore, ROU depreciation of ₹95 crore, other D&A of ₹40 crore, capex of ₹70 crore, new ROU additions of ₹120 crore, NWC change of nil, lease interest of ₹62 crore, lease principal repaid of ₹88 crore, and no other debt. Compute FCFF on the post-116 basis and FCFE, and explain how the lease flows enter each.

Q4. Choosing FCFF or FCFE [L3]

Explain in interview style why you would value a cement mid-cap with FCFF but a housing finance company with FCFE, and what goes wrong if you apply FCFF to the latter.
