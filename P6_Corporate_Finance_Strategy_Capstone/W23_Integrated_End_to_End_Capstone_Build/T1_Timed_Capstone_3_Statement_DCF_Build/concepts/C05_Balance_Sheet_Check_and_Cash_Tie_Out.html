Key concept

Balance Sheet Check and Cash Tie-Out

Concept: Balance check: Total assets − (Total liabilities + Equity) = 0 every period; CFS closing cash = BS cash

Statement

For every period \(t\), historical and forecast:

\[ \text{BS check}_t = \text{Total assets}_t - \left(\text{Total liabilities}_t + \text{Total equity}_t\right) = 0 \]

\[ \text{Cash check}_t = \text{Closing cash}^{\text{CFS}}_t - \text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t = 0 \]

where total equity includes NCI (as presented in a consolidated Schedule III balance sheet), total liabilities include current and non-current liabilities and lease liabilities, and cash means the same definition in both statements. Ind AS 7 cash and cash equivalents may net bank overdrafts repayable on demand, so a reconciliation line is needed if the BS shows "cash and cash equivalents" and "other bank balances" separately.

Explanation

In an integrated model the balance sheet balances because every change in every BS line flows through either the IS (to retained earnings) or the CFS (to cash). If cash is the only BS line taken from the CFS (s04), then the BS check tests whether every other BS movement has been captured in the CFS with the correct sign. A non-zero check means a missing, duplicated or wrongly signed flow.

Implementation rules:

  • Compute the checks on every sheet where they arise and round to avoid floating-point noise: =ROUND(E45-(E78+E92),3). Feed them into the master check (Week 23 t2 c01).
  • Check historical years too. A historical imbalance means a data-entry error or a missed line (often "other equity" components or a reclassification).
  • Never force a balance with a plug (topic pitfall 4). The check must be able to fail.

Diagnosing a non-zero check:

  1. Find the first period where it is non-zero. Errors propagate forward, so fix the earliest.
  2. Is the difference constant or growing? A constant difference from the first forecast year usually means an opening balance mismatch. A growing difference usually means a flow omitted from the CFS (e.g. ΔROU or a provision movement).
  3. Halve or double? If the difference is twice a specific line, that line has the wrong sign in the CFS. If it equals the line exactly, the line is missing.
  4. Compare the BS change line by line against the CFS line by line (a "BS movement versus CFS" reconciliation block on the Checks tab).

Illustration

FY28 forecast BS check shows −14.0 (assets short of liabilities + equity by ₹14.0 crore). FY27 is 0. FY29 shows −29.5.

Diagnostic Finding
First non-zero period FY28, so FY27 opening balances are fine
Pattern Growing (−14.0, −29.5): a recurring flow is missing
BS movement versus CFS Lease liability increased by ₹14.0 crore of new lease additions in FY28 (non-cash, so correctly absent from the CFS), but the ROU asset did not increase by the same amount
Root cause The Leases tab's lease liability roll-forward included new additions, but the ROU asset roll-forward feeding the BS left out the additions row, so assets fell short by the cumulative additions (₹14.0 crore, then ₹14.0 + ₹15.5 crore)

After the additions row is linked into the ROU roll-forward, both checks return 0. A non-cash lease addition must increase the ROU asset and the lease liability equally, with no cash effect.

Connections

The balance and cash checks are the core of the Checks tab (s04) and the first items in the Week 23 t2 integrity checks (s02, c03). The time plan's Checkpoint 2 (s07) is "balance check = 0 all years". Weeks 13–15 built the same checks. In audit terms, the check is analogous to a trial balance agreement, necessary but not sufficient: a model can balance and still be wrong (e.g. capex in the wrong year), which is why the roll-forward and flex tests in t2 also exist.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pattern diagnosis [L2]

Your BS check reads 0 in FY26A, +8.0 in FY27, +8.0 in FY28 and +8.0 in FY29. The cash check is 0 in all years. Explain what this pattern suggests and list the three most likely causes in order.

💡 Hint: A constant difference that starts in the first forecast year points to a single event.

Q2. Sign error [L2]

A model's BS check is −62.0 in FY28. The CFS shows "increase in trade payables" of −31.0 while the BS shows trade payables rising by 31.0. Explain the arithmetic link between the check and the sign error, and write the corrected CFS formula logic.

Q3. Cash definition mismatch [L1]

The BS shows cash and cash equivalents ₹82 crore and other bank balances (FDs over three months) ₹140 crore. The CFS closing cash is ₹82 crore less a ₹15 crore bank overdraft. Design a cash check that handles these definitions correctly.
