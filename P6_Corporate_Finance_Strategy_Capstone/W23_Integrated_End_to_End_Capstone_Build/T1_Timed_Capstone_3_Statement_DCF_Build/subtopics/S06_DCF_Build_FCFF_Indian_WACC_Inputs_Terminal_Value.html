Subtopic note

DCF Build: FCFF, Indian WACC Inputs, Terminal Value, Mid-Year

Scope: DCF: FCFF, WACC (10-year G-sec, Nifty-regressed beta, ERP), terminal value via Gordon growth and exit multiple, mid-year convention

Why it matters

The DCF tab turns the 3-statement model into a value per share that can be compared with the market price. That is the headline of the capstone deck and the number interviewers probe hardest. Each input is a point of judgement: which bond yield, which beta, which equity risk premium, which terminal method, and whether cash flows arrive at year-end or through the year. In Indian practice, the combination of a relatively high risk-free rate (about 6–7%), an ERP of about 6–8% and a 25.17% tax rate gives a WACC for mid-caps typically in the 11–14% range. A model outside that range needs a reason.

Core notes

Step 1 — FCFF from the statements

\[ \text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta \text{NWC} \]

(Full derivation and the FCFE variant in c01.) Practical points:

  • Take EBIT excluding other income (treasury income is valued through the equity bridge, c03).
  • Apply the marginal tax rate, 25.17% under Sec 115BAA (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess), not the effective rate, unless losses or incentives make cash tax materially different in specific years.
  • Capex = maintenance + growth capex from the PPE tab, including CWIP spend in the year (not capitalisations).
  • ΔNWC = increase in operating working capital (excluding cash, borrowings, lease liabilities and current tax). An increase reduces FCFF.
  • Under the post-Ind AS 116 basis, D&A includes ROU depreciation. Add back ROU depreciation only if leases are treated as debt in the bridge, and deduct new ROU additions as "lease capex" for a growing lessee (s03, c03).

Step 2 — WACC inputs for India

Risk-free rate (Rf). Use the yield on the 10-year Government of India benchmark bond on the valuation date (FBIL/CCIL or RBI data). It was broadly in the 6.2–7.2% band during 2024–26. Use the rate on the valuation date, not a textbook figure. Because the cash flows are in nominal rupees, a nominal rupee risk-free rate keeps the currency and inflation consistent.

Beta (β). Regress the stock's returns on Nifty 50 (or Nifty 500 for a broader market) returns:

  • weekly returns over two years or monthly returns over five years. Check R² and the standard error, since mid-caps often show low R² and noisy betas;
  • adjust with the Blume formula \(\beta_{\text{adj}} = 0.67\,\beta_{\text{raw}} + 0.33\);
  • prefer a bottom-up (industry) beta: unlever peer betas, take the median and relever at the target's debt-to-equity:

\[ \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t)\,\frac{D}{E}}, \qquad \beta_L = \beta_U \left[1 + (1 - t)\,\frac{D}{E}\right] \]

The Excel regression slope is =SLOPE(stock_returns, index_returns) (returns as columns of log or simple returns on matching dates).

Equity risk premium (ERP). The premium of Indian equities over the risk-free rate. Common practitioner sources are Damodaran's country ERP (mature-market ERP plus an India country risk premium, recently totalling about 7–8%), historical Sensex/Nifty excess returns, and implied ERP from index earnings yields. Many Indian valuation reports use 6–8%. Whichever you choose, cite it on the Inputs sheet with a source and date.

Cost of debt (Kd). Use the company's marginal pre-tax borrowing rate: a recent NCD yield, the bank MCLR plus spread from the borrowings note, or a synthetic rating spread from interest coverage. Finance cost ÷ average debt is only a cross-check, because it reflects old fixed-rate loans.

Weights. Use target or market-value weights: E at market capitalisation, D at book value of borrowings plus lease liabilities if you are on the post-116 basis.

Illustration: Rf 6.6%, relevered β 1.05, ERP 7.0% gives Ke = 6.6% + 1.05 × 7.0% = 13.95%. Kd 9.0% pre-tax gives 6.73% post-tax. With D/V = 15%, WACC = 0.85 × 13.95% + 0.15 × 6.73% = 12.87%.

Step 3 — terminal value by two methods

Gordon growth: \(\text{TV}_n = \text{FCFF}_{n+1} \div (\text{WACC} - g)\). Use a normalised FCFF for year \(n+1\) in which capex is consistent with growth (reinvestment rate = g ÷ RONIC) and depreciation roughly equals maintenance capex. For India, g is typically 4–6% nominal, at or below long-run nominal GDP growth expectations (topic pitfall).

Exit multiple: \(\text{TV}_n = \text{EBITDA}_n \times \text{EV/EBITDA}\). Take the multiple from peers (Week 8), preferably a through-cycle median rather than the current one, and on the same lease basis as your EBITDA.

Cross-check. Compute the growth rate implied by the exit-multiple TV and the multiple implied by the Gordon TV (c02). If the exit multiple implies 9% perpetual growth, it is embedding today's market optimism. Show both methods and explain which one you rely on. Also report TV as % of EV. Above about 75–80% means the explicit period is too short or the final year is not yet at steady state.

Step 4 — discounting and the mid-year convention

End-of-year discounting assumes all cash arrives on 31 March. In reality FCFF accrues through the year, so the mid-year convention discounts each year's FCFF by \(t - 0.5\) years:

\[ \text{PV}_t = \frac{\text{FCFF}_t}{(1 + \text{WACC})^{t - 0.5}} \]

It raises EV by roughly a factor of \((1 + \text{WACC})^{0.5}\), about 6% at a 12.9% WACC. Terminal value treatment must be consistent:

  • Gordon TV: the perpetuity's flows also arrive mid-year, so discount TV at \(n - 0.5\), or equivalently discount at \(n\) and multiply by \((1+\text{WACC})^{0.5}\).
  • Exit multiple TV: represents a sale price at the end of year \(n\), so discount at \(n\).

Stub period. If the valuation date is 30 November 2026 and FY27 ends 31 March 2027, only 4/12 of FY27 FCFF remains. Use a year fraction \(f = 121/365\), a cash flow of \(f \times \text{FCFF}_{\text{FY27}}\) discounted at \(f/2\), later years at \(f + (t - 1) - 0.5\), and the net debt from the latest balance sheet (September Reg 33 results).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative FCFF in heavy capex years is fine if the terminal year is steady state. Check that value is not concentrated in TV.
  • Beta with R² below 0.1: do not use the raw regression. Use the bottom-up beta.
  • Sec 115BAA versus old regime: a company still using MAT credit and incentives may move to 115BAA later. Model the switch year explicitly, since the option is irreversible once exercised.
  • Size or company-specific premium: some Indian practitioners add 1–3% to Ke for small or illiquid stocks. It is subjective, so disclose it separately rather than hiding it in beta.
  • Gordon g greater than or equal to WACC gives a negative or infinite TV. Add an input check that flags it.

Key takeaways

  • FCFF uses EBIT excluding treasury income, the 25.17% marginal rate, total capex and operating ΔNWC.
  • Rf is the 10-year G-sec yield on the valuation date. Beta should be regressed on the Nifty, Blume-adjusted, and preferably bottom-up relevered. ERP needs a cited source and is typically 6–8%.
  • Show Gordon and exit-multiple TV side by side with implied growth and implied multiple cross-checks, and report TV % of EV.
  • Mid-year discounting is \(t - 0.5\). Gordon TV is consistent at \(n - 0.5\), and exit TV at \(n\). Handle stub periods explicitly.
  • Every WACC input carries a source and date on Inputs.

Practice Questions

Q1. WACC build [L2]

Valuation date 30 November 2026. 10-year G-sec 6.55%; raw two-year weekly beta 1.22 against Nifty 50 (R² 0.31); peer unlevered median beta 0.82; target D/E 0.25; ERP 7.25%; pre-tax Kd 9.4%; tax 25.17%. Compute Ke using (a) the Blume-adjusted raw beta and (b) the relevered bottom-up beta, and WACC for each. Recommend one and justify.

💡 Hint: Convert D/E to D/V and E/V before weighting.

Q2. Mid-year and stub discounting [L2]

FCFF forecasts (₹ crore): FY27 180, FY28 210, FY29 245, FY30 270, FY31 290. WACC 12.5%; Gordon g 5%; valuation date 31 December 2026 (FY27 ends 31 March 2027). Set out the discount periods for each cash flow and the TV under mid-year convention with a stub, and explain each period you chose.

Q3. Reconciling terminal methods [L2]

FY36 EBITDA ₹1,240 crore; FY37 normalised FCFF ₹610 crore; WACC 12.0%; g 5.0%; peer EV/EBITDA 14.0×. Compute both TVs, the growth rate implied by the exit-multiple TV and the EV/EBITDA implied by the Gordon TV, and state which you would rely on.

Q4. Beta judgement [L3]

A mid-cap's five-year monthly beta against Nifty 50 is 0.48 with R² of 0.06, while peers' unlevered betas range 0.75–1.05. The stock is thinly traded and 72% promoter-held. Write a note to your reviewer explaining the beta you will use and why.

Q5. FCFF line consistency [L3]

Your DCF shows FY30 EBIT of ₹520 crore including ₹48 crore of interest on fixed deposits, D&A of ₹140 crore including ₹35 crore ROU depreciation, capex of ₹160 crore and an NWC increase of ₹22 crore, while the equity bridge adds back cash and FDs of ₹640 crore and deducts lease liabilities of ₹290 crore. Identify every inconsistency and restate FCFF correctly.
