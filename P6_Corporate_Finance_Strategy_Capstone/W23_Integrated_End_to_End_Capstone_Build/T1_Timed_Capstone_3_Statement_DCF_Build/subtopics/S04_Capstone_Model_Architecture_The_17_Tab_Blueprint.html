Subtopic note

Capstone Model Architecture: The 17-Tab Blueprint

Scope: Model architecture: Cover, Inputs, Historicals, Revenue build, Cost build, PPE/capex, WC, Leases, Debt, Tax, Equity, IS, BS, CFS, DCF, Sensitivity, Checks

Why it matters

Under time pressure the biggest risk is not a wrong formula but a structure that cannot be checked. A model with a clear flow (inputs → calculations → outputs → checks) can be audited by a reviewer in an hour (Week 23 t2). A model where the BS tab computes interest and the DCF tab contains hard-coded growth rates cannot. A fixed blueprint also saves time: you are filling a known skeleton, not designing one, so all creative effort goes into drivers and judgement. Most valuation case tests and modelling interviews assume this layout, so being fluent in it is interview-relevant.

Core notes

Design principles

  1. One direction of flow: Inputs and Historicals feed schedules, schedules feed statements, statements feed DCF and Sensitivity, and everything feeds Checks. Backward links (a schedule pulling from the DCF) are a red flag.
  2. Identical timeline on every calculation sheet: same columns for the same years (e.g. columns E–I history FY22–FY26, J–S forecast FY27–FY36), driven from one timeline row on Inputs. This makes the one formula per row rule and R1C1 consistency checks possible (t2 s01, c02).
  3. Separation: inputs (blue) only on Inputs and Historicals; calculations (black) on schedules; cross-sheet links (green) clearly marked.
  4. Schedules before statements: the IS, BS and CFS mainly collect results from schedules. They should hold little original computation.

Tab-by-tab specification

8 Leases Ind AS 116 Lease liability roll-forward (opening + additions + interest − payments), ROU asset roll-forward IS, BS, CFS, DCF bridge
9 Debt Borrowings Tranches, drawdowns, repayments, interest (opening or average with circuit breaker, c06), cash sweep optional IS, BS, CFS
10 Tax Income tax PBT, rate (25.17% 115BAA), current vs deferred, MAT credit if old regime, tax paid IS, BS, CFS
11 Equity Share capital and reserves Opening equity, PAT, dividends (payout), buybacks, OCI, NCI roll-forward BS, CFS
12 IS Income statement Revenue to PAT, attribution to owners/NCI, EPS BS, CFS, DCF
13 BS Balance sheet Schedule III Division II order: non-current/current assets, equity, liabilities Checks
14 CFS Cash flow (indirect) CFO from PAT/PBT, CFI, CFF, closing cash = BS cash BS cash, Checks

How the statements are wired

  • IS: Revenue (Revenue build) − costs (Cost build) = EBITDA; − D&A (PPE + Leases ROU depreciation) = EBIT; − finance costs (Debt + Leases interest) + other income (treasury yield on opening cash/investments) = PBT; − tax (Tax) = PAT.
  • BS: PPE/CWIP from PPE; ROU asset and lease liability from Leases; inventories, receivables, payables from WC; borrowings from Debt; equity from Equity; cash from CFS.
  • CFS: PAT + D&A + finance cost (if presented that way) − ΔNWC − tax adjustments = CFO; capex = CFI; borrowings, lease payments, dividends = CFF. The closing cash links to the BS.

Cash should be the only BS line taken from the CFS. Total assets − (liabilities + equity) = 0 then follows mathematically if every other line is right, which is why the balance check works as a test.

Building order within the timebox

Build in dependency order, not tab order: Inputs/timeline → Historicals → Revenue → Cost → PPE → WC → Leases → Tax (initially on PBT before interest) → Debt → Equity → IS → CFS → BS → Checks → DCF → Sensitivity → Cover. Turn on interest-on-average-debt only once the model balances on opening balances (c06).

Naming and layout conventions

  • Row labels in column B, units in column C, a constant or "notes/source" column D, timeline from E.
  • A flag row (=E$5>LastActualYear style, history vs forecast) so a single formula can switch between historical links and forecast logic, or keep history and forecast in separate blocks. Do not mix both approaches.
  • Named ranges for scalars only (Tax_Rate, WACC, Scenario), not for whole time series.
  • Freeze panes at E7 on every calc sheet, and group detail rows (t2 s06).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Fewer tabs. In a two-day build you may merge WC into Cost build or Leases into Debt. That is acceptable if the roll-forwards survive intact. Do not merge schedules into statements.
  • Segments. One Revenue build tab with stacked segment blocks is easier to audit than one tab per segment.
  • Annual versus quarterly. Annual periods suffice for valuation. A quarterly stub is only needed if the valuation date is late in the year and you want a precise net debt figure. Handle it with a partial-period factor in the DCF rather than quarterly columns.
  • NCI and associates. If material, add rows to Equity (NCI share of PAT less NCI dividends) and a non-operating line for associates (equity-method carrying value or market value) in the equity bridge.
  • Explicit period. Use 10 years if the company is still in a growth or capex phase, and 5 years if mature. Either way, the final year must be steady state (s06, c02).

Key takeaways

  • Seventeen tabs, one direction of flow: inputs → schedules → statements → valuation → checks → cover.
  • One identical timeline on every calculation sheet makes consistency checks and one formula per row possible.
  • Statements collect from schedules. Cash is the only BS line sourced from the CFS.
  • Build in dependency order and add circularity last.
  • The Cover and Checks tabs are deliverables, not afterthoughts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Mapping feeds [L1]

For each of the following BS lines, name the source tab and the schedule line that feeds it: net block of PPE, CWIP, ROU asset, lease liability (current and non-current), trade receivables, borrowings, retained earnings, cash and cash equivalents, deferred tax liability.

Q2. Spotting backward links [L2]

A reviewer finds: (a) the Debt tab pulls the cash balance from the BS to compute a cash sweep; (b) the Tax tab contains a hard-coded 25.17% in each forecast column; (c) the DCF tab computes capex as 6% of revenue directly. For each, explain the architectural problem and propose the corrected design.

💡 Hint: Ask for each item whether it breaks flow direction, input separation or the "schedules first" principle.

Q3. Timeline design [L2]

Design the timeline rows for Inputs (FY22–FY26 actuals, FY27–FY36 forecast) including year label, period end date, actual/forecast flag, forecast period number and mid-year discount period. Write the formulas for each row assuming the first year label is in E5 and the last actual year is a named input.

Q4. Merging tabs under time pressure [L3]

At 3 p.m. on Day 1 you have finished Inputs, Historicals, Revenue and Cost builds and are 90 minutes behind plan. Propose which tabs you would merge or simplify to recover time without weakening auditability, and justify each decision against the design principles in these notes.

Q5. Lease tab specification [L2]

Write the row-by-row specification (label, unit, formula logic) for a Leases tab that produces the lease liability roll-forward, ROU asset roll-forward, IS charges and CFS lines, assuming new lease additions are 4% of revenue and the incremental borrowing rate is 9.5%.
