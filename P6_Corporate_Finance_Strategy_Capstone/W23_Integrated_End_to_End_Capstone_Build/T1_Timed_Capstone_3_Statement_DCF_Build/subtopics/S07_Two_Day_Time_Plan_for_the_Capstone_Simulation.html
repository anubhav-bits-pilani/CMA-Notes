Subtopic note

Two-Day Time Plan for the Capstone Simulation

Scope: Time plan for 2 days: e.g. Day 1 data + historicals + schedules; Day 2 statements, DCF, checks, commentary

Why it matters

Modelling tests at Indian equity research houses, investment banks, Big 4 valuation teams and corporate FP&A departments are timed, usually 3 hours to 48 hours. They are failed far more often by running out of time than by technical errors. A candidate who submits a balanced, checked model with a one-page commentary beats one who submits a beautiful revenue build and no DCF. The capstone's two-day window is a rehearsal for this. The plan below is a default allocation for about 16 working hours (two 8-hour days), with checkpoints and decisions on what to cut when behind. The time sheet you keep against it is part of the exercise (week cross-cutting concept on timeboxing).

Core notes

Principles

  1. Front-load the skeleton. Get to a balancing, crude three-statement model early, then refine. A balanced simple model can be improved, but an unbalanced detailed model is a liability at hour 14.
  2. Data entry is capped. Historical entry is the classic time sink (topic pitfall 1). Cap it, use XBRL or copy-paste from PDF with Power Query cleaning, and enter only the lines you will use.
  3. Checks are built as you go, not at the end. Add each roll-forward check when you build its schedule.
  4. Commentary has protected time. Reserve the last 2.5 hours for checks, sensitivity, the Cover and the written output, and do not borrow from them.
  5. Simplify, don't skip. When behind, reduce granularity (fewer segments, one debt tranche, % of revenue for minor costs). Do not drop a schedule or a check.

Checkpoint decisions

At each checkpoint compare actual elapsed time with plan and decide:

  • On time: continue. Optionally add granularity to the highest-value driver.
  • Up to 45 minutes behind: compress the next block by simplifying (single debt tranche, no deferred tax schedule, WC on three main days only).
  • More than 45 minutes behind at Checkpoint 2: stop refining and get the balance sheet to balance with the simplest correct logic. Never use a plug (topic pitfall 4).
  • Behind at Checkpoint 3: drop the bull/bear scenario narrative and keep the base case with WACC × g sensitivity. Commentary time is never cut below 90 minutes.

Keep it on a "Log" tab or in the dossier. It shows interviewers that you manage time deliberately, and it gives you calibration data: by your third simulated case you will know your actual historical entry speed.

Productivity techniques

  • Template: start from your Week 13–16 template with all tabs, timeline, formats and check rows pre-built (cleared of the old company). This saves 1–2 hours and is legitimate if your brief allows it. State it in the README.
  • Keyboard-only navigation (Ctrl+PgUp/PgDn, Ctrl+arrow, F2, F4, Alt shortcuts) and fill-right (Ctrl+R) for row-consistent formulas.
  • Power Query for PDF tables (Data → Get Data → From PDF) when the annual report tables are machine-readable.
  • Commentary skeleton written on Day 1 evening: thesis, three drivers, two risks, valuation range placeholders. Day 2 fills in numbers.

What "done" looks like at the end of Day 2

  1. Model balances in every year with circularity on and off. Cash ties out and the master check shows OK.
  2. DCF value per share under the base case, with Gordon and exit cross-check, TV % of EV, and implied multiples.
  3. Sensitivity tables live and the scenario switch tested and reset to base.
  4. Cover: purpose, outputs, scenario, check status, version, sources.
  5. One-to-two page commentary: recommendation, valuation range, drivers, risks, FP&A observations.
  6. Time sheet completed.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Company turns out messy at hour 2 (a demerger you missed). Switching costs 2 hours, and staying costs perhaps 3 in adjustments. Switch only before historical entry is half done. Otherwise restrict history to the post-event years and disclose.
  • Interrupted days. If you work in fragments, keep the time sheet honest. The aim is calibration, not a perfect record.
  • Over-engineered schedules. A 25-tranche debt schedule for a company with two term loans is time not spent on commentary. Granularity should be proportional to value impact.
  • Perfectionism on formatting. Apply styles with a template or cell styles in one pass in block D2-5. Do not format as you go.

Key takeaways

  • Day 1 covers data, historicals, inputs and all schedules. Day 2 covers statements, DCF, sensitivity, QA and commentary.
  • Three hard checkpoints: schedules complete (end of Day 1), model balances (D2 + 1.5 h), model frozen (D2 + 5.5 h).
  • Cap data entry. When behind, simplify granularity but never drop checks or use plugs.
  • Protect at least 90 minutes for commentary and the Cover.
  • Log planned versus actual time per module and use the variances to calibrate future cases.

Practice Questions

Q1. Re-planning after a slip [L2]

At the end of Day 1 you have completed Historicals, Inputs, Revenue, Cost and PPE builds, but WC, Leases, Tax, Debt and Equity are not started. Actual Day 1 time was 8 hours. Prepare a revised Day 2 plan (8 hours) that still delivers every "done" item, stating which simplifications you make and why.

💡 Hint: List the deliverables first and work backwards from the protected commentary block.

Q3. Plug temptation [L3]

At Checkpoint 2 your balance sheet is out by ₹3.7 crore in FY29 onwards only, and you are 50 minutes behind. Describe a systematic 20-minute debugging routine, and explain why entering the difference into "other current liabilities" is unacceptable even with a disclosure.

Q4. Template ethics [L1]

State when using your own pre-built template is acceptable in a timed modelling test or capstone, and what you should disclose in the README or submission note.

Q5. Commentary skeleton [L2]
