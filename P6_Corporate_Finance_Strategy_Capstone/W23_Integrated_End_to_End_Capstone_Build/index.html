Objective: Integrate accounting, historical analysis, 3-statement modelling, valuation, and FP&A commentary into a cohesive showcase portfolio.

How this week fits

Week 23 is the penultimate week of Phase 6 (Corporate Finance Strategy, Capstone & Career Launch) and the point where the curriculum's separate strands become one deliverable. You have already practised Ind AS technical accounting and Schedule III presentation, ratio and forensic analysis (Weeks 6–7), peer comps (Week 8), Excel and Power Query technique (Weeks 9–10), Power BI (Weeks 11–12), three-statement modelling (Weeks 13–15), WACC and DCF (Week 16), and budgeting, forecasting, variance and MIS commentary (Phase 5). This week you prove you can combine them under time pressure and package the results for employers.

The four topics run in sequence. t1 is the timed two-day capstone: a 3-statement + DCF model of an unfamiliar NSE mid-cap built from raw filings. t2 is model audit and QA, which brings the capstone (and every portfolio workbook) to an institutionally reviewable standard. t3 packages six projects into a portfolio dossier with briefs, evidence and hosting. t4 turns the capstone into a 10–12 slide board-style deck. Saturday's assessment audits the whole portfolio against a four-part rubric.

Prerequisites: a working 3-statement template from Weeks 13–15 with all schedules and check rows, the Week 16 DCF tab, a Week 8 peer comps sheet you can repopulate, and your Phase 5 commentary style guide. Week 24 then uses the dossier and deck for CV bullets, LinkedIn Featured links and valuation case interviews.

Refresh before starting

3-statement and DCF build sequence (Weeks 13–16)

Full 3-statement and DCF build sequence from Weeks 13–16

Recall the dependency order, not the tab order: timeline and Inputs → Historicals (reported → adjustments → normalised) → Revenue build → Cost build → PPE/CWIP corkscrew → working capital days → Leases (Ind AS 116 liability and ROU roll-forwards) → Tax (25.17% under Sec 115BAA) → Debt (opening-balance interest first) → Equity (PAT, dividends, NCI) → IS → CFS (indirect) → BS with cash linked from the CFS → Checks → DCF (FCFF, WACC, Gordon and exit TV, mid-year discounting, EV to equity bridge) → Sensitivity → Cover. Key identities to have at your fingertips: FCFF = EBIT(1 − t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC; Ke = Rf + β × ERP; WACC weights at market values; TV % of EV below about 75–80%; and balance and cash checks at zero every period. Re-run your template once end to end before Day 1 so that every formula row is clean.

Excel shortcuts, formats and data tables

Excel shortcuts, formatting conventions and data table mechanics

Navigation and editing: Ctrl+arrows, Ctrl+PgUp/PgDn, F2, F4 (absolute/relative cycling), Ctrl+R (fill right), Ctrl+D, Alt+= (AutoSum), Ctrl+1 (format cells), Ctrl+` (show formulas), Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+] (precedents and dependents), Shift+Alt+Right (group), and F9 inside the formula bar to evaluate part of a formula. Formatting conventions: blue inputs, black formulas, green cross-sheet links; units in column C; negatives in brackets and zero as a dash; freeze panes below the timeline. Data tables: What-If Analysis → Data Table, with the output formula in the top-left cell, axis values as hard-coded inputs and row and column input cells on the same sheet as the table. The centre value must reproduce the base output. Under "Automatic except for data tables", press F9 before reading.

Schedule III Division II presentation

Ind AS presentation in Schedule III Division II statements

Division II applies to companies preparing Ind AS financial statements other than NBFCs (Division III). Balance sheet order: non-current assets (PPE, CWIP with ageing, ROU assets, goodwill, intangibles, financial assets, deferred tax assets, other non-current assets), current assets (inventories, financial assets including trade receivables with ageing, cash and cash equivalents, other bank balances), equity (equity share capital, other equity, and NCI in consolidated statements), and non-current and current liabilities (borrowings, lease liabilities, trade payables split MSME/others with ageing, other financial liabilities, provisions). Statement of P&L: revenue from operations, other income, expenses by nature, profit before exceptional items and tax, exceptional items, PBT, tax, profit from continuing operations, discontinued operations (Ind AS 105), OCI and its two categories, and EPS. The 2021 amendments added ageing schedules, the shareholding of promoters and additional regulatory information including ratios. Your model's BS and IS should mirror this order so that the historical tie-out is line by line.

Variance and commentary writing (Phase 5 MIS)

Variance and commentary writing from Phase 5 MIS work

Good MIS commentary follows a pattern: headline first ("EBITDA ₹42 crore (9%) below budget, driven by realisation"), then quantified drivers in order of size (price, volume, mix, cost per unit, fixed costs), then cause, then action and owner. Use variance decomposition from Weeks 18–19 (price/volume/mix, rate/efficiency) so every rupee is explained. Keep units and periods explicit (₹ crore, Q2FY27 versus Q2FY26), separate one-offs from run-rate, and end with forward-looking implications. In the capstone this style shapes the one-page investment and FP&A note and the deck's action titles (t4). The capstone forecast also supplies the baseline for "plan versus history" commentary.

Cross-cutting concepts

Traceability of every output number

Traceability: every output number traceable to an input or source document

Traceability means a reviewer can start from any number on the Cover, in the dossier or in the deck and follow an unbroken chain back to either a labelled input or a dated source document. In the model, this requires a source log (S1–Sn with document, filing date and page) and source references on every hard-coded historical cell (a notes column or cell comments). It also requires assumptions on Inputs with their rationale and source, cross-sheet links that point to where a number is calculated (not to an intermediate copy), and no external links or pasted values in calculation areas. Outside the model, every number in the dossier or deck should cite its model cell or sheet ("Model v1.0, DCF!H62") and the source footnote. The test is the "click-back" audit: pick five output numbers at random and trace each to its source within two minutes. Failure modes are a growth rate typed into a formula, a peer multiple with no date, a deck number typed rather than pasted, and a figure from Screener without the annual report page. Traceability is also what makes an analyst defensible in interviews: "that 7.0% ERP is from source S9, dated 1 November 2026" ends the argument.

Consistency across model, dossier and deck

Consistency across model, dossier and deck (same units, dates, scenarios)

The three deliverables describe the same company, so they must show the same numbers. Fix three anchors before writing anything. Units: ₹ crore with one decimal, and per-share values in ₹ with no decimals. Dates: one valuation date and one CMP date, with FY labels in the FY27E format and the period end 31 March. Scenario: the base case is the headline everywhere, and bull and bear are labelled as such. Then enforce the anchors mechanically. Paste deck charts as linked objects or refresh them from a single "Outputs" sheet (t4). Quote dossier figures from the same Outputs sheet. Record the model version on every deliverable's footer. When the model changes after the deck is drafted, a consistency sweep is mandatory: compare a list of 15–20 headline numbers (EV, value per share, upside %, WACC, g, FY28 revenue and EBITDA, net debt, TV %, implied EV/EBITDA) across all three. Common failures are lakh versus crore on one chart, the deck quoting FY26 CMP while the model uses the November CMP, and the dossier still showing the v0.9 value.

Exercise: timeboxing and the time sheet

Exercise: strict timeboxing for the 2-day simulation with a logged time sheet per module

The two-day simulation is run against a fixed plan of about 16 hours (t1 s07), with three hard checkpoints: all schedules built by the end of Day 1, the model balancing by 1.5 hours into Day 2, and the model frozen by 5.5 hours into Day 2. A time sheet logs, for each module, the planned minutes, actual minutes, variance, reason and corrective action. Strict timeboxing means that when a block overruns you simplify the next block (fewer segments, one debt tranche, % of revenue for minor costs) rather than borrowing from QA or commentary. Commentary keeps at least 90 minutes. The time sheet has two purposes. During the exercise it forces decisions at checkpoints. Afterwards it gives calibration data (your real historical-entry speed, your debugging time) and portfolio evidence that you manage time deliberately. Record it honestly, since the value is in the variances.

Exercise: QA checklist and cover status before freezing

Exercise: run the full QA checklist before freezing the model and record check status on the cover

Freezing the model means saving a release version (v1.0) that will not change and from which all PDFs, the dossier and the deck are produced. Before freezing, run the full t2 QA checklist: standards (consistent columns, one formula per row), integrity checks (balance, cash, RE, PPE, CWIP, debt and lease roll-forwards, signs), error detection (errors, external links, hard-codes, IFERROR inventory, circularity with the circuit breaker at 0 and 1), sensitivity integrity (centre checks, F9, scenarios tested end to end), flex tests, presentation, print setup and Cover. Record the result on the Cover: master check status (live), counts by check group, and a typed freeze note stating the date and time, scenario, circuit breaker state, calculation mode and "F9 pressed". The version log entry for v1.0 should list QA fixes made since v0.9. A model frozen with a failing check, or with a typed "OK", fails the Saturday Accuracy criterion.

Exercise: the six-project portfolio repository

Exercise: final portfolio repository with six briefs, files, README and walkthrough links

The portfolio repository is the employer-facing home of the curriculum's six projects: the Ind AS close pack, the ratio/forensic peer benchmark, the Power BI dashboard, the 3-statement DCF capstone, the budget/forecast/variance MIS, and capital budgeting/M&A (t3). Structure it as one top-level folder or GitHub repository with a README (objective, project index, how to navigate, data sources and dates, limitations, contact) and one sub-folder per project named consistently (P1_IndAS_ClosePack, … P6_CapBudget_MA). Each sub-folder holds a one-page brief (PDF), working files (protected xlsx, pbix with a PDF export), annotated screenshots and a link to a 3–5 minute walkthrough video for the capstone and the dashboard. All data must be public, with no employer or client material. Test every link from a logged-out browser and confirm that files open without macros or special permissions before publishing.

Hands-on exercise brief

Roadmap exercise: Execute the 2-day timed Capstone Modelling Simulation and finalize your 6-project Portfolio Repository.

Part A — Capstone simulation (two consecutive days, 8 hours each)

Company: an unfamiliar NSE-listed mid-cap (AMFI ranks 101–250 on the latest list), non-financial, with stable segments and disclosed volume or capacity KPIs. Record the selection scorecard (t1 s01).

Fixed inputs and assumptions (override only with a documented source):

Item Value
Valuation date 30 November 2026 (latest BS: 30 September 2026 Reg 33 results)
Historical window FY22A–FY26A consolidated
Forecast FY27E–FY36E (10 years), annual
Units ₹ crore, one decimal; per share in ₹
Tax 25.17% (Sec 115BAA) unless the tax note shows another regime
Risk-free rate 10-year G-sec yield on the valuation date (cite FBIL/RBI)
ERP 7.0% base; sensitivity 6.0–8.0%
Beta Bottom-up from at least 4 NSE peers, 2-year weekly against Nifty 50, relevered
Terminal growth 5.0% base; table range 4.0–6.0%
Exit multiple Peer median EV/EBITDA on the same lease basis; table range ±2×
Lease basis Post-Ind AS 116 (lease liabilities in net debt)
Interest Average balance with circuit breaker; must balance with CB = 0 and 1
Scenarios Base, bull, bear with at least six differing drivers

Required tabs: Cover, Inputs, Historicals, Revenue build, Cost build, PPE/capex, WC, Leases, Debt, Tax, Equity, IS, BS, CFS, DCF, Sensitivity, Checks, and Log (time sheet).

Required checks (all feeding the master check): balance; cash and opening cash; RE, PPE, CWIP, debt and lease roll-forwards; historical tie-outs (±0.05); sign counts for capex, dividends and repayments; error count per calc sheet; sensitivity centre = base; WACC > g flag; scenario switch in range.

Required outputs: value per share (Gordon and exit), TV % of EV, implied EV/EBITDA and P/E, upside/downside versus CMP, a WACC × g table, a WACC × exit multiple table, a scenario output table, and a one-to-two page commentary (recommendation, valuation range, three drivers, two risks, two FP&A recommendations with quantified impact).

Time sheet: planned versus actual minutes per module (blocks D1-1 to D2-7), with variance reasons.

Part B — QA and freeze (Day 2, last 2.5 hours, plus up to 2 hours the next day)

Run the full t2 QA checklist, perform at least six flex tests (zero revenue, capex at 100% of revenue, 0% tax, repayment above balance, WACC = g + 0.1%, all scenarios), fix the findings, record the check status on the Cover and save as P4_Capstone_<SYMBOL>_v1.0.xlsx with a PDF output pack.

Part C — Portfolio repository (Day 3 onwards in the week)

Six project sub-folders, each with a one-page brief (problem, data sources, approach, tools, key outputs, insights, time taken), working files, screenshots, plus a top-level README, a skills matrix and walkthrough video links for P3 (Power BI) and P4 (capstone). The capstone deck (t4) sits in P4.

Deliverables: frozen model v1.0, PDF output pack, time sheet, QA log with flex test results, commentary note, deck, and the repository link. No solutions are provided. Your Saturday audit grades the result.

Saturday assessment paper

Roadmap assessment: Saturday 3-Hour Portfolio Audit: Peer/self-grade every workbook against institutional modelling rubric (Accuracy, Dynamic Structure, Visual Clarity, Business Insight).

Instructions. Time allowed: 3 hours. Total: 100 marks. Work on your own portfolio files and, where a peer is available, swap one workbook for peer grading. Score each criterion on a 0–5 scale using the rubric descriptors you write in Q1, and support every score with evidence (cell reference, screenshot or check output). Do not fix anything during the audit. Record the findings and fix them afterwards.

Q1. Rubric design (10 marks)

Write descriptors for scores 1, 3 and 5 for each of the four criteria (Accuracy, Dynamic Structure, Visual Clarity, Business Insight), with weights of 35%, 25%, 15% and 25%. Each descriptor must name an observable test (for example, "master check live and OK; zero constants in forecast calc ranges").

Q2. Accuracy audit of the capstone (25 marks)

Within 60 minutes, audit your capstone model v1.0: (a) error and circularity scan with CB = 0; (b) hard-code search on all calc sheets; (c) row consistency on IS, BS, CFS and DCF; (d) three flex tests of your choice; (e) a click-back trace of five Cover numbers to source. Report findings as critical/major/minor with the estimated value impact per share.

💡 Hint: Fix the order of tests before you start and time each block.

Q3. Dynamic structure test (15 marks)

Change the following on a copy of the capstone and record whether every output updates correctly and all checks pass: the scenario switch to bear and back; terminal g from 5.0% to 4.0%; a six-month commissioning delay for the next capacity addition; the tax rate from 25.17% to 34.94% for FY27–FY28 only. Score Dynamic Structure and justify the score.

Q4. Visual clarity and print review (10 marks)

For the capstone and the MIS workbook, check colour coding, units, freeze panes, grouping, print areas, title rows, footers (file, sheet, page) and PDF readability (scale of at least 60%). Score Visual Clarity for each and list the three highest-impact fixes.

Q5. Business insight review (20 marks)

Read the capstone commentary and deck executive summary. Assess: (a) whether the recommendation follows from the valuation range versus CMP; (b) whether the three drivers are the largest value sensitivities shown in your tornado or sensitivity tables; (c) whether each FP&A recommendation is quantified (₹ crore impact on EBITDA or cash); (d) whether TV % of EV and implied multiples are discussed. Score Business Insight and rewrite one weak paragraph.

Q6. Cross-deliverable consistency (10 marks)

Build a 15-line consistency table comparing EV, value per share, upside %, WACC, g, FY28E revenue and EBITDA, net debt, TV %, implied EV/EBITDA, valuation date, CMP, scenario, units and model version across model, dossier and deck. Count mismatches and state the cause of each.

Q7. Portfolio repository check (10 marks)

From a logged-out browser on another device, open the repository README and every project folder. Record broken links, files that need macros or permissions, missing briefs, any non-public data, and file names that do not follow the naming convention. Score the portfolio's readiness to share.

Practice-ready checklist

  • I can build a balanced, checked 3-statement + DCF model for an unfamiliar NSE company within two days Self-test: complete the Part A simulation on a second, different mid-cap within 16 logged hours, with the master check OK, CB = 0 and 1 both balancing, and the time sheet showing no QA or commentary block cut below plan.
  • I can audit a model for hard-codes, inconsistent formulas and broken checks in under an hour Self-test: ask a peer to plant five errors (an embedded constant, a row override, a masked IFERROR, a mis-wired data table, a wrong-signed capex link) in a copy of your model, then find all five within 60 minutes using the s03 audit routine.
  • I can present a 10–12 slide valuation and FP&A story in 10 minutes with action titles Self-test: record a timed run-through of your deck. It should finish within 10 minutes, and a listener who reads only the slide titles should be able to state your recommendation, valuation range, three drivers and two risks.
  • I can share a portfolio link where each project has a one-page brief and working files Self-test: send the repository link to someone outside the course. They should be able to open all six briefs and working files without help, macros or access requests, and summarise each project's result in one sentence.
