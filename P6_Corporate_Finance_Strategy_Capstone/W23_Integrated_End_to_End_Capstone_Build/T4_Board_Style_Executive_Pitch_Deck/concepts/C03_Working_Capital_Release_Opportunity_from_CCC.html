Key concept

Working-Capital Release Opportunity from CCC Reduction

Concept: WC release opportunity = Δ CCC days × (Revenue or COGS ÷ 365)

Statement

The one-time cash released by reducing a working-capital component is the reduction in days multiplied by the daily base that the component is measured on:

\[ \text{WC release} = \Delta\text{Days} \times \frac{\text{Base}}{365} \]

Applied by component of the cash conversion cycle \( \text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO} \):

\[ \text{Release} = \Delta\text{DSO} \times \frac{\text{Revenue}}{365} + \Delta\text{DIO} \times \frac{\text{COGS}}{365} + \Delta\text{DPO} \times \frac{\text{COGS}}{365} \]

where \( \Delta\text{DSO} \) and \( \Delta\text{DIO} \) are reductions (positive when days fall) and \( \Delta\text{DPO} \) is an increase (positive when payable days rise). DSO = trade receivables ÷ revenue × 365; DIO = inventory ÷ COGS × 365; DPO = trade payables ÷ COGS (or purchases) × 365.

Explanation

The formula only works if each component is measured on its own base. Multiplying a total CCC change by revenue overstates the release when inventory and payables are measured on COGS. Use the same base as the definition of days — revenue for receivables (gross of GST if receivables include GST, which they usually do; be consistent), COGS or purchases for inventory and payables.

Key conditions and nuances:

  • One-time versus recurring. The release is a one-time cash inflow (in the year days fall). On a growing base, the benefit grows each year because lower days apply to higher revenue — model it by changing the days assumption in the WC schedule, not by adding a lump sum.
  • Value impact. In a DCF, the release raises FCFF in the year of change and slightly in subsequent years through growth; the PV is less than the headline release if it occurs in the future.
  • Recurring benefit. Interest saved on the released cash at the pre-tax cost of debt (or earned on surplus cash), post-tax.
  • Costs. Early-payment discounts, lost sales from tighter credit, higher prices demanded by suppliers for longer terms, stock-outs from lower inventory.
  • Constraints. MSME suppliers must be paid within the MSMED Act limit (45 days maximum where agreed in writing; 15 days otherwise), and Sec 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act disallows the deduction for late payments until actually paid — so DPO increases on MSME payables are not legitimate levers.
  • Year-end window dressing. Days measured at 31 March can be distorted by quarter-end collections; use average balances where possible.

Illustration

FY25: revenue ₹4,100 crore; COGS ₹2,665 crore. Current DSO 78, DIO 60, DPO 55. Target (peer median): DSO 65, DIO 52, DPO 60 (non-MSME suppliers are 70% of payables, so only 70% of the DPO lever is applied).

Component Δ days Daily base (₹ crore) Release (₹ crore)
Receivables 13 4,100 ÷ 365 = 11.23 146
Inventory 8 2,665 ÷ 365 = 7.30 58
Payables (5 days × 70%) 3.5 7.30 26
Total 230

CCC falls from 83 to 57 days (on headline days). Post-tax interest benefit at 9% pre-tax cost of debt: 230 × 9% × (1 − 0.2517) ≈ ₹15 crore p.a.

Practice Questions

Q1. Component-level release [L1]

Revenue ₹2,800 crore; COGS ₹1,960 crore. DSO falls from 66 to 55, DIO from 48 to 42 and DPO rises from 40 to 46. Compute the release by component and in total, and the new CCC.

Q2. Base error [L2]

An analyst computes WC release as (CCC reduction of 23 days) × revenue ₹2,800 crore ÷ 365. Using the data in Q1, compute the analyst's figure, explain the error, and quantify the overstatement.

💡 Hint: Two of the three components are measured on COGS.

Q3. Modelling the lever [L3]

Explain how you would implement a WC-release scenario in a days-driven WC schedule with a 3-year phase-in (one-third of the days reduction each year from FY27), how it flows to FCFF and the DCF, and why the DCF value uplift differs from the headline release amount. Include the scenario switch formula you would use.
