Key concept

Football Field Chart and Implied Value per Share

Concept: Football field: range bars for each method; implied value per share = (EV − Net debt) ÷ diluted shares

Statement

A football field is a horizontal floating-bar chart with one bar per valuation method, each bar spanning the low-to-high value range from that method, plotted on a common value-per-share axis, with a reference line at the current market price (CMP).

For every EV-based method, the implied value per share is:

\[ \text{Value per share} = \frac{\text{EV} - \text{Net debt}}{\text{Diluted shares}} \]

where:

  • \( \text{EV} \) = enterprise value from the method (DCF, EV/EBITDA comps, precedents);
  • \( \text{Net debt} \) = borrowings + lease liabilities (if EBITDA/FCFF is post-Ind AS 116) + preference capital + NCI − cash and cash equivalents − liquid investments − non-operating assets (the full "EV-to-equity bridge" in compressed form);
  • \( \text{Diluted shares} \) = basic shares outstanding + dilutive potential shares (ESOPs, convertibles) computed by the treasury stock method or if-converted method.

Equity-based methods (P/E, P/B) give equity value directly: value per share = multiple × per-share metric, with no net debt adjustment.

Explanation

The football field shows at a glance whether methods agree, where the CMP sits, and how wide the uncertainty is. Its credibility depends on consistent bridging: every EV-based bar must use the same net debt and the same diluted share count, at the same date. A common error is using the latest balance-sheet net debt for the DCF but a different figure for comps.

Diluted shares should be computed at the implied price: ESOPs with exercise price below the implied value are dilutive. Under the treasury stock method:

\[ \text{Incremental shares} = N_{\text{options}} \times \left(1 - \frac{K}{P}\right) \]

where \( N_{\text{options}} \) is in-the-money options outstanding, \( K \) the exercise price and \( P \) the implied (or market) price. This creates a mild circularity, usually resolved by using CMP or iterating once.

Chart construction in Excel: stacked bar chart with series 1 = low value (no fill) and series 2 = high − low (filled); CMP as a vertical line (scatter series with two points, or an error bar); data labels at both ends; methods ordered with intrinsic first. A shaded vertical band shows the concluded range.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the football field data [L2]

Diluted shares 6.4 crore; borrowings ₹1,120 crore; lease liabilities ₹240 crore (EBITDA post-Ind AS 116); cash ₹380 crore; NCI ₹90 crore; non-operating investment ₹160 crore. EV ranges: DCF ₹10,900–₹12,300 crore; comps ₹9,600–₹11,200 crore; precedents ₹11,500–₹13,000 crore. 52-week range ₹1,380–₹1,760; CMP ₹1,610. Compute net debt as defined above, the value per share range for each method, and describe the Excel series you would build for the chart.

💡 Hint: Non-operating investments and cash reduce net debt; NCI and leases increase it.

Q2. Dilution at implied price [L2]

The company has 6.2 crore basic shares and 0.35 crore ESOPs with an average exercise price of ₹900. Compute diluted shares under the treasury stock method at implied prices of ₹1,600 and ₹2,000, and explain why the diluted share count should differ across football field bars.

Q3. Review a football field [L3]

A peer's football field shows the DCF bar using net debt of ₹600 crore (excluding leases), comps bar using ₹840 crore (including leases), and both using basic shares; comps EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116. Identify each inconsistency, explain the direction of bias for each bar, and specify the corrections.
