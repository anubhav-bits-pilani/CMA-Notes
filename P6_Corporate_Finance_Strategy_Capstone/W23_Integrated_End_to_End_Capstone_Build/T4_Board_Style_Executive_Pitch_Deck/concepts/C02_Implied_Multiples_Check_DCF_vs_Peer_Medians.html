Key concept

Implied Multiples Check: DCF vs Peer Medians

Concept: Implied multiples check: DCF-implied EV/EBITDA and P/E vs peer medians

Statement

The implied multiples check converts the DCF output into market multiples and compares them with peer medians:

\[ \text{Implied EV/EBITDA} = \frac{\text{EV}_{\text{DCF}}}{\text{EBITDA}_{t}}, \qquad \text{Implied P/E} = \frac{\text{Value per share}_{\text{DCF}}}{\text{EPS}_{t}} \]

where \( t \) is the same period used for peers (typically one-year forward, FY26E, or trailing twelve months), and EBITDA, EPS and EV are defined on the same basis as the peer multiples (consolidated, normalised, same Ind AS 116 treatment).

A second check applies to the terminal value: the implied exit multiple of the Gordon-growth TV,

\[ \text{Implied terminal EV/EBITDA} = \frac{\text{TV}_{n}}{\text{EBITDA}_{n}} \]

is compared with current and long-run sector multiples. Conversely, an exit-multiple TV implies a perpetual growth rate found by solving \( \text{TV} = \text{FCFF}_{n+1} \div (\text{WACC} - g) \) for \( g \), i.e. \( g = \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_{n+1} \div \text{TV} \).

Explanation

A DCF can produce any number; the implied multiple tells you what that number means in the market's language. If your DCF implies 25× EV/EBITDA when peers trade at 15–18×, you are claiming the company deserves a large premium — which requires a reason: superior growth, margins, return on capital or lower risk. Without such a reason, the DCF assumptions (usually terminal growth, margin path or WACC) are probably too optimistic.

Interpretation guide:

  • Implied multiple within peer interquartile range: DCF is consistent with market pricing of similar businesses.
  • Premium: justify by growth differential (e.g. 15% EBITDA CAGR versus 9% for peers), ROCE above peers, or a structural change (new capacity not in consensus). Quantify: a PEG-style comparison (P/E ÷ EPS growth) helps.
  • Discount: may reflect higher risk, governance concerns, or conservative assumptions — check whether intentional.

Simplified algebra for the implied growth: from \( \text{TV} = \text{FCFF}_{n} (1+g) \div (\text{WACC} - g) \), rearranging gives

\[ g = \frac{\text{TV} \times \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF}_{n}}{\text{TV} + \text{FCFF}_{n}} \]

Use it to check that an exit-multiple TV does not imply a growth rate above long-run nominal GDP.

Illustration

DCF EV ₹19,300 crore; value per share ₹1,967; FY26E EBITDA ₹1,050 crore; FY26E EPS ₹62. Peer medians: EV/EBITDA 16.0× (IQR 14.5–17.5×); P/E 28× (IQR 25–31×).

Implied EV/EBITDA = 19,300 ÷ 1,050 = 18.4×; implied P/E = 1,967 ÷ 62 = 31.7×. Both sit above the upper quartile. The target's FY26–FY29 EBITDA CAGR is 16% versus a peer median of 10%, driven by capacity additions; FY28E implied EV/EBITDA falls to 13.1×, below peers. Conclusion: the premium on near-term multiples is explained by growth already funded — but state this explicitly on the slide.

Terminal check: TV ₹13,100 crore in present value terms corresponds (at a WACC of 11.8% over ten years, discount factor about 0.328) to an undiscounted TV of about ₹40,000 crore at FY35 on EBITDA of ₹3,100 crore — implied exit multiple about 12.9×, below the current sector median, which is reasonable for a maturing business.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and interpret [L2]

DCF EV ₹12,400 crore; net debt ₹900 crore; diluted shares 5.5 crore; FY26E EBITDA ₹780 crore; FY26E PAT ₹410 crore. Peer median EV/EBITDA 13.5× (IQR 12–15×), P/E 22× (IQR 19–25×). Compute implied EV/EBITDA and implied P/E, compare with peers, and list the assumptions you would re-examine first.

Q2. Implied terminal growth [L2]

A model uses an exit multiple of 14× on terminal-year EBITDA of ₹2,200 crore. Terminal-year FCFF is ₹1,150 crore and WACC is 11.5%. Compute the implied perpetual growth rate using the rearranged formula and assess it against an Indian long-run nominal GDP growth assumption of 9–10% and a sector growth expectation of 6%.

💡 Hint: Compute TV first, then substitute into the rearranged expression for g.

Q3. Justifying a premium in an interview [L3]

An interviewer says: "Your DCF implies 21× EV/EBITDA; peers trade at 15×. Why should I believe you?" Prepare a structured answer covering the quantitative evidence you would present, the forward-year multiple comparison, and the conditions under which you would revise the DCF down.
