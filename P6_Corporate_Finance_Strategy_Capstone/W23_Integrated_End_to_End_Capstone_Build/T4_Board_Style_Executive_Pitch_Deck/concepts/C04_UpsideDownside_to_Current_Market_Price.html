Key concept

Upside/Downside to Current Market Price

Concept: Upside/downside % = (Intrinsic value per share − CMP) ÷ CMP

Statement

\[ \text{Upside (downside)} = \frac{V - \text{CMP}}{\text{CMP}} \times 100\% \]

where \( V \) is the intrinsic value per share (base case, or each end of the concluded range) and \( \text{CMP} \) is the current market price on a stated date (usually the NSE closing price). A positive result is upside; a negative result is downside.

An equivalent "margin of safety" measure divides by intrinsic value instead:

\[ \text{Margin of safety} = \frac{V - \text{CMP}}{V} \]

These are different numbers; state which you use.

Explanation

Upside converts a valuation into the decision-relevant metric: how far the market price is from your estimate of value. It must be interpreted against the uncertainty of the estimate. A 10% upside on a valuation whose sensitivity range is ±20% is not a strong signal; a 25% upside where even the bear case exceeds CMP is.

Practitioner conventions (illustrative, varying by firm):

  • Upside above roughly 15% with the full range above CMP → positive view.
  • Upside between about −10% and +15% or range straddling CMP → neutral.
  • Downside beyond about −10% → negative view.

Adjustments and pitfalls:

  • Time horizon. A DCF value is a present value today; a 12-month target price is typically the value rolled forward at the cost of equity less expected dividends. Upside to a 12-month target and upside to today's intrinsic value are not the same.
  • Dates. CMP and the valuation date should match; if CMP is updated, note the date; do not mix a September valuation with a November price without comment.
  • Dividends. Total shareholder return over a holding period adds dividend yield to price upside.
  • Diluted basis. \( V \) must be on diluted shares (c01).
  • Range presentation. Show upside at low, base and high values, not only base.
  • Regulatory framing. In a public portfolio, present as analytical output, not as a research recommendation (Week 23 t3 s02).

Illustration

CMP ₹1,842 as at 14-Nov-2026. Concluded range ₹1,950–₹2,150; base ₹2,060.

Point Value (₹) Upside
Low 1,950 (1,950 − 1,842) ÷ 1,842 = 5.9%
Base 2,060 11.8%
High 2,150 16.7%

Range is entirely above CMP but base upside is under 15%, and bear-case value (₹1,780) is below CMP. A reasonable conclusion: "modest upside, conditional on plant ramp-up" — a positive-leaning but not high-conviction view. Margin of safety at base = (2,060 − 1,842) ÷ 2,060 = 10.6%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Upside across the range [L1]

CMP ₹575; DCF range ₹610–₹690 with base ₹648; bear case ₹520. Compute upside at low, base and high, the margin of safety at base, and state which recommendation category the illustrative conventions above would suggest.

Q2. Target price versus intrinsic value [L2]

Intrinsic value today is ₹1,967; cost of equity 13.2%; expected dividend per share over the next 12 months ₹18; CMP ₹1,842. Estimate a 12-month target price, compute upside to the target and to today's value, and expected total shareholder return over 12 months if the target is reached.

💡 Hint: Roll the value forward at the cost of equity, then deduct the dividend paid out during the year.

Q3. Stale prices [L2]

Your deck was finalised with CMP ₹1,842 (upside 11.8%). Before presenting two weeks later, CMP is ₹2,010 after strong quarterly results that you have not yet incorporated. Explain what you would update, what you would say on the executive summary slide, and how you would avoid inconsistency between deck and model.
