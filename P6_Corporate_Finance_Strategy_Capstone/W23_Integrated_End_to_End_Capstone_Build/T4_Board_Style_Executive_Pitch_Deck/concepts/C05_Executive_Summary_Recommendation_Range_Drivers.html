Key concept

Executive Summary: Recommendation, Range, Drivers, Risks

Concept: Executive summary: recommendation, valuation range, 3 key drivers, 2–3 key risks

Statement

The executive summary slide contains, in this order:

  1. Recommendation — one sentence stating the view or decision sought (e.g. positive view; approve capex phasing; pursue WC programme), with the condition on which it depends.
  2. Valuation range — concluded value per share range and base case, with upside/downside to CMP and the valuation date; optionally EV range.
  3. Three key drivers — the three assumptions or factors that most explain the value, each quantified.
  4. Two to three key risks — the factors that would most change the conclusion, each with a quantified value impact.

Often a fifth line: the top FP&A action with its quantified impact.

Explanation

The executive summary is the apex of the pyramid (s01) — the single slide a board member must be able to act on. It should be writable before the rest of the deck is built; if you cannot write it, the analysis is incomplete.

Writing rules:

  • Recommendation first, in plain words. Avoid "we believe there may be some value". Conditional recommendations are fine and often more credible: "Positive view, conditional on 80% utilisation of the new line by FY29."
  • Range, not a point. Boards know valuations are uncertain; a range with a base case signals judgement. Tie the range to the football field and sensitivities.
  • Drivers are causes, not outputs. "Revenue CAGR 13%" is an output; "Capacity rises 28% from FY28 and utilisation recovers to 85%" is a driver. Pick the three that move value most — the sensitivity analysis tells you which.
  • Risks are specific and quantified. "Macroeconomic risk" is filler; "Chinese price competition: 5% lower realisation cuts value by ₹190/share" is useful. Mention mitigants briefly if they exist.
  • Consistency. Every number must tie to the model and to later slides — the summary is where inconsistent numbers are most damaging.
  • Length. Three to five bullets plus a small visual (value bar against CMP or mini football field). Text at presentation size.

Illustration

Action title: ABC Chemicals is worth ₹1,950–₹2,150 per share, 6–17% above CMP, if the new plant ramps up on schedule.

  • Recommendation: Positive view, conditional on utilisation of the 40,000-tonne line reaching 80% by FY29; management should prioritise receivable collection to fund capex without new debt.
  • Valuation: Base ₹2,060 per share (11.8% upside to CMP ₹1,842 as at 14-Nov-2026); DCF supported by comps (₹1,650–₹1,960) and precedents (₹1,960–₹2,280).
  • Drivers: (1) capacity +28% from FY28 with utilisation 78% → 85%; (2) EBITDA margin recovery from 15% to 19% as raw-material prices normalise; (3) WACC of 11.8% on beta 1.05.
  • Risks: (1) ramp-up delay — each 5 pp lower utilisation cuts ₹140/share; (2) realisation pressure from Chinese supply — 5% lower prices cut ₹190/share; (3) receivable days rising further from 78 increases funding need.
  • FP&A action: reducing DSO to 65 days releases ₹146 crore, covering 22% of remaining capex.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write an executive summary [L2]

Data: NSE-listed cement company; CMP ₹1,460; concluded range ₹1,520–₹1,780, base ₹1,650; sensitivity shows each ₹100 per tonne change in EBITDA/tonne moves value by ₹85/share and each 50 bps WACC change by ₹95/share; capacity from 18 to 24 MTPA by FY28; WC opportunity ₹180 crore from inventory reduction. Write the action title and the five summary lines.

Q2. Critique a summary [L2]

Critique this summary and rewrite it: "The company has strong fundamentals and good growth prospects. Our DCF valuation indicates fair value of ₹1,612. Key risks include macroeconomic conditions, competition and regulatory changes. We recommend investors consider the stock."

💡 Hint: Check each line against the four required elements and the quantification rule.

Q3. Choosing the three drivers [L3]

Your tornado analysis shows value-per-share swings for ±10% changes: utilisation ₹210, realisation ₹260, raw-material cost ₹180, WACC (±50 bps) ₹120, terminal growth (±50 bps) ₹90, capex ₹40, tax rate ₹15. Choose the three drivers and two or three risks for the summary, explaining why some high-impact items may be better framed as risks than drivers.
