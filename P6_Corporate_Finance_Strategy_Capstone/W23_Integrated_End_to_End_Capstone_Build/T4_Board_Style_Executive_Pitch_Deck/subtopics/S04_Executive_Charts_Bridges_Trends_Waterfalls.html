Subtopic note

Executive Charts: Bridges, Trends, Waterfalls, Football Fields and Heat Maps

Scope: Charts: revenue/EBITDA bridges, margin trends, EV bridge waterfall, football field, sensitivity heat maps

Why it matters

Five chart types carry most of the analytical weight in a valuation and FP&A deck. Each answers a specific question: bridges answer "what caused the change?", margin trends answer "is profitability improving?", the EV bridge answers "how do we get from enterprise value to value per share?", the football field answers "how does our value compare across methods and with the market?", and heat maps answer "how sensitive is the answer?". Mastering their construction in Excel, and their design for an executive audience, is the difference between a deck that informs and one that merely decorates.

Core notes

Revenue and EBITDA bridges

A bridge (walk) decomposes the change between two values into additive drivers, displayed as floating bars between a starting and an ending total.

Revenue bridge (price–volume–mix): for a product set with base-period price \( P_0 \), volume \( Q_0 \), and current \( P_1 \), \( Q_1 \):

\[ \text{Volume effect} = (Q_1 - Q_0) \times P_0, \qquad \text{Price effect} = (P_1 - P_0) \times Q_1 \]

With several products, split the volume effect into a pure volume effect at base mix and a mix effect; the sum of effects must equal total change exactly. Add FX and acquisitions/divestments as separate bars if material.

EBITDA bridge: start from base-year EBITDA, then bars for volume contribution, price/realisation, raw-material cost, power and fuel, employee cost, other expenses, and end at current or forecast EBITDA. Bars are typically coloured green for positive and red for negative, totals in a neutral colour.

Illustration (₹ crore): FY25 EBITDA 615 → FY28E 1,010. Bars: volume +310, realisation +180, raw material −70, employee cost −25. Check: 615 + 310 + 180 − 70 − 25 = 1,010. Keep the arithmetic exact — a bridge that does not close by even ₹1 crore invites the question of what else is wrong.

Excel build: Excel 2016+ has a native Waterfall chart (Insert > Charts > Waterfall); set start and end bars as totals (right-click the data point > Set as total). For older versions or finer control, use a stacked column with an invisible base series computed as the running cumulative minimum. Always include a check cell: start + sum of drivers − end = 0.

Margin trends

Line charts of EBITDA margin, gross margin and PAT margin over historical and forecast years, separated by a vertical dashed line or a shaded forecast region. Show peer median as a grey line for context. Label the last actual and the final forecast data point directly; avoid legends where direct labels fit. Use a consistent y-axis across slides comparing margins. Do not start a margin axis at an arbitrary value that exaggerates small changes without stating it; for margins, a non-zero baseline is acceptable if clearly marked, because the chart encodes position rather than bar length.

EV bridge waterfall

Shows the path from EV to equity value and value per share:

Step ₹ crore
PV of explicit FCFF 6,200
PV of terminal value 13,100
Enterprise value 19,300
Less: borrowings (1,600)
Less: lease liabilities (EBITDA post-Ind AS 116) (300)
Add: cash and liquid investments 450
Less: NCI (150)
Add: non-operating investments 200
Equity value 17,900
Diluted shares (crore) 9.1
Value per share (₹) 1,967

A waterfall with EV as the first total and equity value as the last total makes the bridge items visible; TV share of EV (13,100 ÷ 19,300 ≈ 68%) can be annotated.

Football field

Horizontal floating bars, one per valuation method, plotted on a value-per-share (or EV) axis: DCF (range from sensitivity), trading comps (multiple range × metric, bridged to equity), precedent transactions, 52-week high–low, analyst target range. Add a vertical line at CMP and a shaded band for your concluded range. Build in Excel as a stacked bar: series 1 = low value (formatted with no fill), series 2 = high − low (filled); add CMP as a scatter series with error bars or a separate line. Label the low and high ends of each bar. Order bars logically (intrinsic methods first, then market methods). The football field is covered in depth in c01.

Sensitivity heat maps

A two-way data table (Data > What-If Analysis > Data Table) of value per share across WACC (rows) and terminal growth (columns), formatted with a three-colour scale (Home > Conditional Formatting > Color Scales) — or better, a rule-based diverging scale centred on CMP so cells above CMP are one hue and below another. Highlight the base case cell with a border. Keep steps meaningful (WACC ±50 bps, g ±50 bps), show 5 × 5 grids, round to whole rupees. For operating sensitivities (utilisation × realisation), the same technique applies. Remember calculation mode: if "Automatic except for data tables", press F9 before copying to the deck.

Design principles across all charts

  • One message per chart, aligned to the action title.
  • One accent colour for the bar or line that carries the insight; others grey.
  • Units in the chart label (₹ crore, %, ₹/share), consistent across the deck.
  • Direct data labels instead of gridlines and legends where possible.
  • Source and notes in a footnote.
  • Built on a "Deck_Outputs" sheet that links to the model, not on calculation sheets.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mix effects that look large. Mix can absorb rounding or definitional noise; if mix exceeds volume or price, revisit product granularity.
  • Negative bridge totals. If EBITDA turns negative mid-bridge, native waterfall charts handle it; invisible-base stacked charts need positive and negative base series.
  • Net cash companies. In the EV bridge, net cash adds to EV; label clearly so the waterfall does not look like an error.
  • Football field with wide DCF range. If the DCF bar spans ±40%, the sensitivity steps are probably too wide or the terminal value share too high; narrow to a defensible range and show the full grid in the appendix.
  • Heat map colour scales. Default red–yellow–green implies good/bad; centred on CMP it conveys "above or below market", which is the real question. Ensure colour-blind-safe palettes (blue–orange).
  • Pre- vs post-Ind AS 116 bridges. Keep the EBITDA definition consistent between historical bridges and the EV bridge.

Key takeaways

  • Bridges decompose change into exact additive drivers; always include a reconciliation check.
  • Margin trends separate actuals from forecasts and show peer context.
  • The EV bridge waterfall makes net debt, leases, NCI and non-operating assets explicit on the way to value per share.
  • Football fields use stacked bars with invisible bases and a CMP line; heat maps use data tables with diverging colour centred on CMP.
  • Every chart has one message, one accent colour, clear units and a source.

Practice Questions

Q1. Price–volume bridge [L2]

A company sold 2.40 lakh tonnes at ₹62,000 per tonne in FY24 and 2.85 lakh tonnes at ₹58,500 per tonne in FY25. Compute revenue in each year, decompose the change into volume and price effects using the formulas in these notes, verify that they sum to the total change, and describe the waterfall chart with bar colours and labels.

Q2. EV bridge construction [L2]

From these data build the EV-to-value-per-share bridge table and describe the waterfall: PV of explicit FCFF ₹3,850 crore; PV of TV ₹7,420 crore; borrowings ₹1,120 crore; lease liabilities ₹240 crore (EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116); cash and current investments ₹380 crore; investment in an unrelated listed company at market value ₹160 crore; NCI ₹90 crore; diluted shares 6.4 crore. Also compute TV as a share of EV and state whether it needs comment.

💡 Hint: Classify each item as debt-like, cash-like or a non-operating asset before signing it.

Q3. Heat map design [L2]

Specify a WACC × terminal growth heat map for a base case of WACC 11.5% and g 5.0%: the grid values, the data table set-up (row input and column input cells), the conditional formatting rule centred on CMP ₹1,842, the base-case highlight, and the calculation step before pasting to PowerPoint.

Q4. Chart choice critique [L3]

A deck uses a 3D pie chart for segment revenue across five years, a clustered column chart for the EV-to-equity bridge, and a line chart with 12 peer lines for margins. Critique each choice and propose a replacement, explaining the insight each replacement makes visible.
