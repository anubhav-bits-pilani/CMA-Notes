Subtopic note

Deck Formatting: Units, Sources, Footnotes and Bullet Discipline

Scope: Formatting: consistent units (₹ crore), sources and footnotes, 3–5 bullets per slide, appendix for detail

Why it matters

Formatting is how a board judges care. A deck mixing ₹ crore and ₹ million, unsourced figures, seven-bullet slides in 9 pt font and detail that belongs in an appendix signals that the analysis behind it may be equally careless. Conversely, consistent units, sources on every slide, tight bullets and a well-organised appendix let the audience trust the numbers and focus on the message. In Indian board packs and investor presentations, ₹ crore is the standard unit for corporate figures; using it consistently also prevents conversion errors with lakh and million.

Core notes

Consistent units (₹ crore)

  • Default unit: ₹ crore for all financial amounts (1 crore = 100 lakh = 10 million). Per-share values in ₹; per-unit metrics in ₹ per tonne, ₹ per kWh, ₹ per store.
  • State units once per chart or table, in the chart label or column header: "₹ crore", "%", "₹/share", "×".
  • Precision: whole ₹ crore for amounts above ₹100 crore; one decimal for multiples (16.4×) and percentages (14.8%); whole rupees for per-share values; basis points for changes in rates and margins (+180 bps).
  • Number format in Excel for deck outputs: #,##0;(#,##0);"–" for crore amounts with negatives in brackets and zero as a dash; Indian digit grouping needs a conditional custom format such as [>=10000000]##\,##\,##\,##0;[>=100000]##\,##\,##0;##,##0 (positives only) or an Indian regional setting — be consistent — many decks use international grouping for crore figures since they rarely exceed six digits.
  • Fiscal year labels: FY25 = year ending 31 March 2025; suffix A for actual and E for estimate (FY25A, FY26E). State this in a footnote on the first numerical slide.
  • Currency conversions: if global peers are used, convert at a stated rate and date, or show multiples only.
  • Same basis throughout: consolidated versus standalone, pre- or post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA, normalised versus reported — say which.

Sources and footnotes

Every slide with data carries a source line at the bottom in small type (8–9 pt):

  • "Source: Company annual reports FY21–FY25; Q2 FY27 results (Reg 33); analyst model."
  • "Market data as at 14-Nov-2026 (NSE close); 10-year G-sec yield from RBI/CCIL."
  • "Peer multiples: consensus estimates from [provider] as at 14-Nov-2026; peers listed in Appendix C."

Footnotes explain definitions and adjustments: "EBITDA excludes other income and exceptional items; post-Ind AS 116." "Net debt includes lease liabilities of ₹300 crore." "Diluted shares include 0.12 crore ESOPs by treasury stock method." Use superscript numbers or symbols consistently and keep footnotes to two or three lines.

3–5 bullets per slide

Text-led slides (executive summary, recommendations, risks) use three to five bullets:

  • Each bullet one idea, one to two lines, starting with the conclusion and ending with the evidence: "Capacity-led growth: 28% added capacity drives 13% revenue CAGR to FY31".
  • Parallel grammatical structure across bullets.
  • No sub-bullets beyond one level; if you need them, the slide holds two messages.
  • Font size at least 14 pt for body text in a presented deck (12 pt minimum in a dense pre-read), titles 20–24 pt.
  • Bold the key number or phrase in each bullet sparingly.

The rule exists because working memory handles roughly four chunks at once; more bullets dilute every bullet.

Appendix for detail

Detail that supports but is not needed to follow the story goes to the appendix: full financial statements, WACC derivation and beta regression output, comps and precedent tables, restatement adjustments, additional sensitivities, methodology notes, glossary. Number appendix slides (A1, A2…) and reference them from main slides ("See A3 for peer multiples"). The appendix is also your defence kit in Q&A.

Template discipline

  • One slide master: title position, font (e.g. Calibri/Arial), colour palette (one primary, one accent, greys), logo/footer area.
  • Footer on every slide: deck title, version (matching the model version, e.g. v2.0), page number, "Not investment advice".
  • Align charts and text boxes to a grid (Arrange > Align); consistent chart sizes for similar charts.
  • Same colour for the same entity across all charts (company always navy, peer median always grey).
  • Spell-check and number-check: a final pass comparing every number with the model's Deck_Outputs sheet.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Small companies. For companies with revenue below ₹100 crore, ₹ crore with one decimal or ₹ lakh may be clearer; choose one and apply consistently.
  • Mixed audiences. For global audiences, show ₹ crore with a USD equivalent in brackets on the title or summary slide only, at a stated rate.
  • Dense pre-reads. A board pre-read can carry more text than a presented deck, but still keep the action title and bullets; put dense narrative in a separate memo.
  • Rounding differences. Totals computed from rounded components may not add; add a footnote "Totals may not add due to rounding" or round the total from unrounded data and accept the visible difference.
  • Accessibility. Ensure sufficient colour contrast and avoid conveying meaning by colour alone.

Key takeaways

  • ₹ crore throughout, units stated once per chart, precision rules applied consistently, FY and A/E conventions footnoted.
  • Every data slide has a source line with dates; footnotes define EBITDA, net debt and share counts.
  • Three to five bullets per text slide, one idea each, conclusion first, minimum readable font.
  • Detail lives in a numbered appendix referenced from the main slides.
  • One master template with version-matched footer and consistent entity colours.

Practice Questions

Q1. Unit and precision clean-up [L1]

Rewrite each to the formatting rules: (a) "Revenue Rs. 41,234.56 mn"; (b) "EBITDA margin improved from 0.148 to 0.192"; (c) "EV/EBITDA 16.4321x"; (d) "Value per share INR 2,060.37"; (e) "Net debt 1,35,00,00,000".

Q2. Write the source and footnote block [L2]

For a DCF/WACC slide using a 10-year G-sec yield of 6.45%, a 2-year weekly beta regressed against Nifty 50 of 1.05, ERP of 7.0%, post-tax cost of debt at 25.17% tax, EBITDA post-Ind AS 116, and net debt including ₹300 crore lease liabilities, draft the source line and footnotes.

💡 Hint: Every input that someone could challenge needs a date or basis.

Q3. Bullet discipline [L2]

Condense the following risks into four bullets that follow the rules in these notes: plant commissioning could slip; utilisation might be lower; Chinese competitors may cut prices; raw-material prices are volatile; INR appreciation hurts exports; key customer concentration (top 5 = 38% of revenue); related-party purchases rose to 6% of COGS; WACC could rise if G-sec yields rise; terminal growth assumption uncertain. Quantify each bullet with an illustrative value impact.

Q4. Template specification [L1]

Write a one-page template specification for your capstone deck covering slide master elements, fonts and sizes, colour palette and entity colours, footer contents, number formats and appendix numbering.
