Subtopic note

Valuation Triangulation: DCF, Comps, Precedents and 52-Week Range

Scope: Valuation triangulation: DCF vs trading comps vs precedent transactions vs 52-week range

Why it matters

No single valuation method is reliable on its own. A DCF is only as good as its terminal assumptions; trading comps embed whatever mood the market is in; precedent transactions include control premiums and deal-specific synergies; the 52-week range just shows where the stock has traded. Triangulation compares methods to test whether the intrinsic view is defensible and to explain gaps. Boards, fairness-opinion reviewers and interviewers all expect it: a DCF value presented without a market cross-check invites the question "why should we believe you over the market?". In Indian practice, registered valuers and merchant bankers routinely present multiple approaches (income, market, and where relevant cost) and explain the weightage applied.

Core notes

Method 1 — DCF (intrinsic)

FCFF discounted at WACC with terminal value by Gordon growth and/or exit multiple (Week 16; Week 23 t1). Range from the sensitivity table — typically WACC ±50 bps and terminal growth ±50 bps — or from bear/base/bull scenarios. Strength: captures company-specific drivers (capacity expansion, margin recovery). Weakness: TV often 60–80% of EV; sensitive to WACC and g.

Method 2 — Trading comparables (relative, minority, current)

Value derived from multiples at which listed peers trade: EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/E, P/B, or sector metrics (EV/tonne for cement, EV/store, EV/subscriber). Steps:

  1. Select 5–10 peers by business mix, size, growth, margins and geography (NSE/BSE listed; global peers for niche sectors with an adjustment).
  2. Compute EV consistently (market cap + debt + lease liabilities if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 + NCI + preference − cash and investments) and use forward metrics (FY26E, FY27E) from consensus or your estimates.
  3. Take median and interquartile range; exclude outliers with explanation.
  4. Apply to the target's metric; bridge EV to equity; divide by diluted shares.

Trading comps reflect minority, liquid stakes at current market sentiment — no control premium.

Method 3 — Precedent transactions (relative, control)

Multiples paid in acquisitions of comparable companies: EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, EV per unit of capacity. Sources: stock exchange disclosures, open offer documents under SEBI SAST Regulations, CCI combination filings, press releases, deal databases. Precedents include a control premium (and sometimes synergies), so they usually exceed trading comps. Adjust for timing (deals from different interest-rate or cycle conditions), size and deal structure. Relevant when the question is a sale, acquisition or open offer; less relevant for a minority investment view, but still informative as an upper bound.

Method 4 — 52-week range (market reference)

The stock's high and low over the past 52 weeks. Not a valuation method in itself; it shows where the market has priced the stock and provides context for the CMP. For listed takeover situations, SEBI SAST and delisting regulations use volume-weighted average prices over specified periods as reference prices, which is why market-price references feature in Indian deal valuations. Analyst target-price ranges (consensus high–low) can be added as a fifth reference.

Reconciling the methods

Observation Possible explanation Action
DCF far above comps Aggressive growth/margin or low WACC; or market undervaluing a turnaround Check implied multiples (c02); test assumptions vs history
DCF below comps Conservative assumptions; or sector trading rich Check comps for growth differential; consider a P/E-to-growth view
Precedents above comps Control premium (often 20–40%), synergies Expected; do not use precedents for a minority view
CMP outside 52-week range Recent re-rating Note the event (results, policy)
All methods overlap Robust range Conclude in the overlap zone

The concluded range is a judgement, not an average. Common approaches: centre on DCF and use comps as a sanity check; or weight methods explicitly (e.g. DCF 60%, comps 40%) with justification. State the approach on the slide.

Illustration

Target: FY26E EBITDA ₹720 crore; net debt (incl. leases) ₹850 crore; diluted shares 9.1 crore; CMP ₹1,842.

Method EV range (₹ crore) Equity range Value per share (₹)
DCF (sensitivity) 18,600–20,400 17,750–19,550 1,951–2,148
Trading comps 22–26× EV/EBITDA 15,840–18,720 14,990–17,870 1,647–1,964
Precedents 26–30× 18,720–21,600 17,870–20,750 1,964–2,280
52-week range — — 1,520–2,010

Value per share = (EV − net debt) ÷ diluted shares; e.g. DCF low = (18,600 − 850) ÷ 9.1 = ₹1,951. DCF overlaps the upper half of comps and the lower half of precedents — consistent with a view that the market is not yet pricing the capacity expansion. A concluded range of ₹1,950–₹2,150 is supportable, with the comps median as a downside reference.

Presentation

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Thin peer set. Indian niche mid-caps may have only two or three listed peers; widen to global peers with explicit adjustment for growth, cost of capital and liquidity, or rely more heavily on the DCF and say so.
  • Accounting comparability. Peers may report pre- or post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA, consolidated versus standalone, or with different treatment of other income; standardise before computing multiples.
  • Cyclical companies. Use mid-cycle EBITDA or EV/capacity rather than peak or trough multiples.
  • Stale precedents. A 2018 deal at 25× may not reflect today's rates; show date and consider excluding.
  • Loss-making companies. EV/Sales or sector KPIs replace EBITDA multiples; DCF requires careful long-horizon assumptions.
  • Holding structures. Listed subsidiaries owned by the target should be valued at market (less a holding discount) as non-operating assets, not double-counted in EBITDA.

Key takeaways

  • Triangulate DCF (intrinsic) with trading comps (minority, current), precedents (control) and the 52-week range (market reference).
  • Compute EV and multiples on a consistent basis — same EBITDA definition, lease treatment and period for target and peers.
  • Precedents usually exceed comps because of control premiums; use them as an upper reference for minority views.
  • The concluded range is a reasoned judgement; state the weighting or approach.
  • Explain gaps between methods rather than hiding them.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute a triangulation [L2]

Target data: FY26E EBITDA ₹1,080 crore, FY26E PAT ₹540 crore, net debt including leases ₹1,350 crore, diluted shares 11.0 crore, CMP ₹1,460, 52-week range ₹1,210–₹1,620. DCF EV range ₹17,600–₹22,400 crore. Peer EV/EBITDA interquartile range 15.0–18.0×; peer P/E interquartile range 24–30×. Precedent EV/EBITDA 17.5–20.0×. Compute the value per share range for each method (including P/E-based comps), and recommend a concluded range with justification.

Q2. Peer standardisation [L2]

Three peers report FY25 EBITDA of ₹800 crore (post-Ind AS 116, includes ₹40 crore other income), ₹620 crore (post-Ind AS 116, excludes other income), and ₹505 crore (pre-Ind AS 116 basis presented in investor presentation; lease rent ₹35 crore). Your target's EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 excluding other income. Explain the adjustments to each peer's EBITDA and EV needed for a consistent EV/EBITDA comparison.

💡 Hint: Changing the EBITDA definition requires a matching change in what goes into EV.

Q3. Explaining a gap [L3]

Your DCF gives ₹2,450 per share, trading comps give ₹1,520–₹1,780, precedents ₹1,900–₹2,150, and the 52-week range is ₹1,410–₹1,830. Write the board narrative explaining the gap, the diagnostic tests you ran, and what concluded range and recommendation you would present.

Q4. Choosing methods for the purpose [L2]

Explain how the weight you place on each of the four references would differ for (a) a minority equity investment view; (b) a strategic buyer considering acquiring control; (c) a promoter considering a buy-back. Give one reason for each weighting choice.
