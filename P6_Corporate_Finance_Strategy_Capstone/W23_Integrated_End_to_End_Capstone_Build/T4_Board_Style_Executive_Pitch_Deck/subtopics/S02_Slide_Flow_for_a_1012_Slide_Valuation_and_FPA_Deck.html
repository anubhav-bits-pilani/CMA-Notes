Subtopic note

Slide Flow for a 10–12 Slide Valuation and FP&A Deck

Scope: Slide flow: title, executive summary, company snapshot, industry, historical performance, drivers, projections, DCF/WACC, football field, sensitivities, FP&A recommendations, risks, appendix

Why it matters

A standard slide flow is a checklist that ensures every question a board or investment committee will ask is answered in a predictable place. Readers of sell-side initiations, fairness opinions and board papers expect a familiar sequence: context, performance, outlook, value, actions, risks. Following it lets the audience focus on your content rather than navigating your structure. Within a 10–12 slide limit, the flow also forces prioritisation: every slide must earn its place.

Core notes

The flow below has twelve main slides plus an appendix. With tight limits, merge snapshot and industry, or DCF/WACC and sensitivities, to reach ten.

1. Title

Company name and NSE symbol, deck purpose ("Valuation and FP&A review"), valuation date and CMP date, your name, version number and a disclaimer line ("For educational purposes; not investment advice"). Optional: one headline number (value range).

2. Executive summary

The apex of the pyramid (s01, c05): recommendation, valuation range and upside/downside versus CMP, three key drivers, two to three key risks, and the top FP&A recommendation with its quantified impact. Usually text-led with a small football field or value bar. If the audience reads only one slide, it is this one.

3. Company snapshot

Business description in two lines, segments with revenue share, geography mix, key facts (capacity, store count, customers), shareholding pattern (promoter, FII, DII, public from the latest shareholding filing under LODR Reg 31), market capitalisation and EV. A segment pie or bar plus a compact fact box works well.

4. Industry

Market size and growth, structure (consolidation, top players' share), demand drivers, cost drivers, regulation, and where the company sits (share, cost position). Cite sources (industry reports, company presentations, rating agency notes). Keep to what affects the forecast — this slide justifies the growth and margin assumptions.

5. Historical performance

Five-year revenue, EBITDA and margin trend on a restated basis (exceptional items and other income separated, Ind AS 116 effects flagged), plus return metrics (ROCE, ROE) and cash conversion (CFO/EBITDA). The complication of the SCR usually appears here — e.g. the margin compression or slowing growth. A revenue/EBITDA bridge from FY21 to FY25 or a margin trend line is typical.

6. Drivers

The model's key drivers and their assumed paths: volume × realisation, capacity × utilisation, store additions × revenue per store, or segment growth; cost drivers (raw material per unit, employee cost as % of revenue); capex and WC days. Show history versus forecast for each so the audience can judge reasonableness. This slide answers "why should I believe the projections?".

7. Projections

Summary projected IS lines (revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA margin, PAT, EPS) and FCFF for the explicit forecast period, ideally as a chart plus a small table of selected years (e.g. FY25A, FY26E, FY28E, FY30E, FY35E). Note scenario shown (base) and key milestones (plant commissioning).

8. DCF / WACC

WACC build in a compact table (Rf from 10-year G-sec yield with date, beta with source and regression basis, ERP, Ke, pre-tax Kd, tax 25.17%, target D/V, WACC), FCFF summary, terminal value method and growth/multiple, PV of explicit period versus PV of terminal value (with TV share of EV), and the EV-to-equity bridge (EV − net debt including lease liabilities − NCI − preference + non-operating investments = equity; ÷ diluted shares). The EV bridge is often best shown as a waterfall.

9. Football field

Range bars for each valuation method — DCF (from sensitivity range), trading comps (peer multiple interquartile range), precedent transactions, 52-week trading range, optionally analyst target range — with a vertical line at CMP and your selected range shaded (c01, s05).

10. Sensitivities

WACC × terminal growth heat map of value per share; and one operating sensitivity (e.g. utilisation × realisation, or EBITDA margin × revenue growth). Optionally scenario outputs (bear/base/bull) with probability weights. Highlight base case and CMP-equivalent cells.

11. FP&A recommendations

Three to five management actions with quantified impact on EBITDA, cash, and value per share: cost levers, working-capital release, capex phasing, pricing (s06). Each recommendation with owner, timeline and the KPI to track.

12. Risks

Two to five key risks with direction and magnitude of impact (e.g. "5 pp lower utilisation → −₹140/share"), likelihood and mitigants; include accounting/governance risks if identified in forensic work (related-party transactions, auditor remarks).

Appendix

Full IS, BS, CFS summaries; detailed WACC and beta regression; comps table with peer list and multiples; precedent transaction table; historical restatement adjustments; additional sensitivities; sources and methodology notes. The appendix answers follow-up questions without cluttering the main flow.

Illustration — timing a 10-minute presentation

Slides Minutes
Title + executive summary 1.5
Snapshot + industry 1.0
Historical + drivers 2.0
Projections + DCF/WACC 2.0
Football field + sensitivities 1.5
FP&A recommendations + risks 2.0
Total 10.0

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ten-slide cap. Merge snapshot with industry and DCF/WACC with sensitivities; never cut the executive summary, football field or recommendations.
  • FP&A-led audience. For a CFO audience, the FP&A recommendations may move to slide 3, right after the executive summary, with valuation as support.
  • Conglomerates. A sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuation needs a slide showing each segment's value and holding-company discount; consider moving detail to the appendix.
  • Ind AS 116 presentation. If EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116, show it consistently on historical, projection and comps slides, and state that lease liabilities are in net debt; alternatively present pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA throughout and exclude leases from debt.
  • Interview panels. Some ask for a 5-slide version; prepare a cut-down using slides 2, 6, 8, 9 and 11.

Key takeaways

  • Twelve-slide flow: title, executive summary, snapshot, industry, historical, drivers, projections, DCF/WACC, football field, sensitivities, FP&A recommendations, risks, plus appendix.
  • The executive summary is the apex; the drivers slide justifies the forecast; the football field shows triangulation.
  • The DCF slide must include WACC inputs with dates, TV share of EV and the EV-to-equity bridge.
  • Recommendations and risks are quantified in value-per-share and cash terms.
  • Merge slides to reach ten, but never drop the summary, football field or recommendations.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draft slide contents [L2]

For an NSE-listed specialty chemicals company (FY25 revenue ₹4,100 crore, EBITDA margin 15%, capacity expansion of ₹650 crore, CMP ₹1,842, DCF range ₹1,950–₹2,150), list the specific content — numbers, chart type and source — for slides 3 to 8 of the flow.

Q2. Cut to ten slides [L1]

Your deck has the 12 main slides above. A panel limits you to 10. State which slides you would merge, how the merged slides would be laid out, and what moves to the appendix.

Q3. Appendix design [L2]

List eight appendix slides you would prepare for a capstone deck and, for each, the board or interview question it is designed to answer.

💡 Hint: Work backwards from the likely challenge to each main-slide number.

Q4. Re-sequencing for a CFO audience [L3]

The same capstone is to be presented to the company's CFO rather than an investment committee. Re-sequence the slide flow, justify each change, and rewrite the executive summary bullets so that FP&A recommendations lead and valuation supports them.
