Subtopic note

Action Titles: One-Sentence "So What" Headlines

Scope: Action titles: each slide headline states the "so what" in one sentence

Why it matters

An action title turns a slide from a picture the audience must interpret into a claim the slide proves. Executives reading a pre-read often skim titles only; if the titles carry the argument, the deck works even at that depth. Action titles also discipline the author: if you cannot write the "so what" of a slide in one sentence, the slide has no point and should be cut or reworked. Consulting firms, investment banks and good FP&A teams enforce action titles on every slide; interviewers notice immediately whether a candidate uses them.

Core notes

Definition

An action title (also called headline or assertion title) is a complete sentence at the top of the slide stating the conclusion the slide supports. It contains a subject, a verb and usually a quantified claim with an implication.

Descriptive title (avoid) Action title (use)
Revenue 2020–2030 Capacity-led volume growth lifts revenue to ₹6,900 crore by FY31, a 12% CAGR
EBITDA margin Margins recover from 15% to 19% by FY28 as raw-material costs normalise
WACC calculation WACC of 11.8% reflects a 1.05 beta and 15% target debt
Sensitivity analysis Value stays above CMP unless WACC exceeds 12.8% or terminal growth falls below 4%
Working capital Cutting receivable days to the peer median releases ₹96 crore of cash
Risks Plant ramp-up delay is the largest risk: each 5 pp lower utilisation cuts ₹140/share

Writing rules

  1. One sentence, ideally one or two lines (about 10–20 words). If it needs two sentences, the slide holds two messages — split it.
  2. State the insight, not the topic. Use a verb that conveys direction or judgement: drives, recovers, compresses, releases, exceeds, supports.
  3. Quantify where possible, using the same units and precision as the slide body.
  4. Make the claim provable by the slide. Everything in the body should support the title; anything that does not belongs elsewhere.
  5. Consistent tense and voice. Present tense for findings ("drives"), future tense only for projections where needed.
  6. Avoid hedging stacks ("may potentially possibly") but do not overclaim; "suggests" is acceptable where evidence is partial.
  7. Keep the chart's own label small and descriptive (e.g. "Revenue, ₹ crore, FY21A–FY31E") — the descriptive information moves to the chart label, the insight to the title.

The title test (horizontal read)

Read the titles in sequence — PowerPoint's Outline view (View > Outline View) or a contents list — and check:

  • Do they tell the whole story from situation to recommendation without the bodies?
  • Does each follow from the one before?
  • Does the last main title (risks or recommendations) connect back to the executive summary?

If the titles alone are persuasive, the deck has a storyline; if they read like a table of contents, it does not.

The vertical test (title–body fit)

For each slide, cover the title and ask what one conclusion the body supports. If your answer differs from the title, either the chart or the title is wrong. Common fixes: change the chart type so the insight is visible (e.g. a bridge rather than two bars), highlight the relevant data in an accent colour, or narrow the title to what the chart actually proves.

Building titles from the model

Action titles should be driven by model outputs. One practical technique: in the model's Deck_Outputs sheet, build a text string with TEXT so the title updates with the model during iteration, then paste the final text into PowerPoint before freezing.

="Cutting receivable days to the peer median releases ₹"&TEXT(WC_Release_Rec,"#,##0")&" crore of cash"

Use this only during drafting; final titles should be checked and edited by hand.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Title slide, appendix and section dividers do not need action titles; appendix slides can carry descriptive titles ("Appendix A: Comparable company multiples") because they are reference material.
  • Negative findings. State them plainly: "Return on capital has fallen below WACC since FY23". Softening the title undermines the credibility of positive ones.
  • Uncertain findings. Use a conditional: "Upside of 12% depends on utilisation reaching 80% by FY29".
  • Long company names. Use the short name or NSE symbol consistently to save title space.
  • Font and length. Keep a consistent title font size (often 20–24 pt); if a title runs to three lines, shorten it rather than shrinking the font.
  • Regulatory caution. Avoid "buy" or "target price" language in a publicly shared deck (see Week 23 t3 s02); "intrinsic value range" and "upside to CMP" convey the same analysis.

Key takeaways

  • Every main slide has a one-sentence action title stating the "so what", with a verb and a quantified claim.
  • Descriptive information belongs in the chart label; the insight belongs in the title.
  • Run the horizontal test (titles alone tell the story) and the vertical test (body proves the title).
  • Drive draft titles from model outputs, then freeze and hand-check them.
  • State negative or conditional findings plainly; appendix slides may use descriptive titles.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rewrite descriptive titles [L1]

Rewrite each as an action title using the data given: (a) "Historical revenue" — revenue grew from ₹2,900 crore in FY21 to ₹4,100 crore in FY25 while EBITDA margin fell from 22% to 15%; (b) "Peer comparison" — company trades at 14.2× FY26E EV/EBITDA versus peer median 17.5×; (c) "Capex" — ₹650 crore capex in FY26–FY27 adds 40,000 tonnes, raising capacity by 28%; (d) "Football field" — all methods except the 52-week range lie above CMP of ₹1,842.

Q2. Title–body mismatch [L2]

A slide titled "Margin expansion drives EBITDA growth to FY30" shows a stacked bar chart of revenue by segment for FY25–FY30, with no margin data. Explain the mismatch and propose two alternative fixes — one changing the title, one changing the chart — with the data each would require.

💡 Hint: Decide first which message the slide is meant to carry in the storyline.

Q3. Horizontal title test [L2]

Write a full set of 12 action titles for a capstone on an NSE-listed quick-service restaurant chain (520 stores growing to 800 by FY30; same-store sales growth 3%; pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA margin 12% rising to 15%; DCF value ₹610–₹690 versus CMP ₹575), then check that they pass the horizontal test and explain any changes you made.

Q4. Dynamic title formula [L2]

Write an Excel formula for a draft action title that reads "Value of ₹X per share implies Y% upside to CMP" and switches the word "upside" to "downside" when the value is below CMP, using named cells Value_PS and CMP, with the percentage shown to zero decimals and without a minus sign.
