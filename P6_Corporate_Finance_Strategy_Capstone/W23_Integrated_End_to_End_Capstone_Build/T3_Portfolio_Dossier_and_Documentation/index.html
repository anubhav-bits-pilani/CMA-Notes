Roadmap topic: Portfolio Packaging & Documentation: Creating a structured portfolio dossier summarizing all 6 completed projects with executive summaries and screenshots

Overview

A portfolio dossier is the evidence file that converts twenty-two weeks of private study into something a hiring manager, FP&A head or Big 4 technical accounting partner can assess in five minutes. A CMA Final certificate signals that you passed examinations; it does not show that you can build a lease schedule under Ind AS 116 that reconciles to the cash flow statement, or a Power BI model whose DAX measures survive a slicer change. The dossier closes that gap. It packages six projects — Ind AS close pack, ratio/forensic peer benchmark, Power BI dashboard, 3-statement DCF, budget/forecast/variance MIS, and capital budgeting/M&A — into a consistent, navigable, verifiable set of documents, files and short videos.

The audience matters. A recruiter screens for keywords and outcomes in under a minute; a hiring manager opens one or two files to check that they actually work; an interviewer uses the dossier as the agenda for a technical conversation ("walk me through how your WC schedule drives the CFS"). The dossier therefore has to work at three depths: a one-page brief per project (skim), annotated screenshots and PDF exports (inspect without Excel), and the working files themselves with README and video walkthrough (deep verification). Every layer must be consistent — the same project numbers, file names, units, dates and headline results appear in the brief, the README, the CV bullet and the LinkedIn Featured tile.

The learning sequence for this topic is deliberate. Start with the six-project inventory (s01) so you know what you have and what is missing. Write the one-page brief for each project (s02) using a STAR-style summary (c01) with quantified results. Capture evidence — screenshots, sample outputs, check-sheet status (s03). Decide on hosting (s04) and file formats (s05), applying consistent naming (c02) and a standard README (c03). Record video walkthroughs for the two flagship items (s06). Throughout, apply the confidentiality filter (s07) — public data only. Finish with a skills matrix (c04) that maps each project to the requirements of the roles you are targeting, which is the bridge into Week 24's CV and interview work.

Treat the dossier like a published financial report: it has a basis of preparation (data sources and dates), a structure (numbering and navigation), controls (check status, link testing) and a responsible preparer (you, bound by the ICMAI Code of Ethics). A sloppy dossier signals a sloppy analyst far more loudly than a missing project does.

Related concepts to study

Week 24 t1: CV bullets and LinkedIn Featured links

→ Week 24 t1: CV bullets and LinkedIn Featured links built from dossier summaries

The dossier is the source document; the CV is the extract. Each CV bullet in Week 24 should be a compressed STAR result line lifted from the matching project brief — same project number, same headline figure, same tools. The LinkedIn Featured section then links directly to the brief PDF or the repository folder for that project. Building the dossier first means your CV claims are pre-verified: if a bullet says "built a 3-statement + DCF model of a ₹4,000 crore NSE mid-cap with zero balance-check errors across 10 forecast years", the interviewer can click through and see the check row. Keep a single master sheet of result lines so that edits flow in one direction — dossier to CV to LinkedIn — never the reverse.

Week 23 t4: the executive deck as flagship item

→ Week 23 t4: the executive deck becomes the flagship portfolio item

Of the six projects, the capstone valuation and its 10–12 slide board-style deck is the item most likely to be opened. It demonstrates the full stack — accounting judgement in historical restatement, modelling, valuation, and FP&A recommendation — in a format executives actually consume. In the dossier it should be Project 4 (or pinned first), with the deck PDF, the model, a video walkthrough and a brief that quotes the recommendation and valuation range. The deck's action titles double as the "Result" lines of the STAR summary. Any number that appears in both the deck and the brief must tie exactly; treat the deck as the master for headline valuation figures.

Week 12: Power BI service sharing and export options

→ Week 12: Power BI service sharing, workspace and export options

Week 12 covered how Power BI content is distributed: My Workspace versus shared workspaces, apps, sharing to named users (which needs Pro or Premium Per User licensing, or Premium/Fabric capacity), Export to PDF/PowerPoint, and Publish to web. For the portfolio these choices determine whether a reviewer can see your dashboard at all. Most reviewers will not have access to your tenant, so the realistic options are a PDF export, a pbix download (requires Power BI Desktop, free on Windows only), annotated screenshots, and a video. Publish to web creates a public, unauthenticated URL — acceptable only for fully public data and only where your tenant admin allows it. Understanding these options prevents the common failure where the dashboard link opens a sign-in screen.

Data citation and source dating

Data citation and source dating for public company information

Public company data changes: annual reports are restated, quarterly results under LODR Reg 33 are revised in the following year's comparatives, share prices move daily, and aggregator sites (Screener, Tijori, Trendlyne) update and occasionally mis-tag figures. Every project therefore needs a source table: document name, issuer, period covered, URL or filing reference (e.g. NSE corporate announcement), and the date you accessed it. Market inputs — CMP, 10-year G-sec yield, beta — must carry an "as at" date because a valuation is only meaningful at a point in time. Cite the primary source (the audited annual report or the stock exchange filing) and treat aggregators as cross-checks, not authorities. This is the same discipline as an audit working paper reference.

Common pitfalls

Huge unstructured uploads with no navigation

Uploading huge unstructured files without a summary or navigation guide

The mistake is dumping a 40 MB workbook, a folder of raw annual-report PDFs and a pbix into a Drive folder and sharing the link. People do it because the work feels self-evident to the author and documentation feels like overhead at the end of a long build. A reviewer, however, will not spend ten minutes figuring out which file to open. The correct treatment is a top-level README or index, one folder per project, a one-page brief as the first file, raw source documents moved to a sources subfolder or replaced with links, and a model map in each README. Detect it in review by asking a friend to find a specific output (e.g. "the implied EV/EBITDA") in under two minutes using only your navigation.

Employer or client data in a public portfolio

Using employer/client data or confidential templates in a public portfolio

The temptation is strong: your best Ind AS close work happened on a real audit client, and the employer's MIS template is better than anything you built. Publishing either breaches the confidentiality principle in the ICMAI Code of Ethics, likely breaches your employment or engagement terms, and may expose unpublished price-sensitive information. Correct treatment: rebuild every project on public data (annual reports of listed companies) or clearly labelled synthetic data, and design your own templates. Detect it by scanning files for client names, GSTINs, PANs, internal codes, employer logos, document properties (author, company fields) and hidden sheets; strip metadata with Document Inspector before publishing.

Broken links and files that need macros or permissions

Broken links or files requiring macros/permissions to open

Links break because Drive folders are shared as "restricted", OneDrive links expire, files are moved after the link is created, or a workbook still holds external links to a local path such as C:\Users\...\Data.xlsx. Macro-enabled files (.xlsm) trigger security blocks — Microsoft now blocks macros in files downloaded from the internet by default — and many reviewers will not enable them. Correct treatment: publish macro-free .xlsx, break or remove external links, set sharing to "anyone with the link can view", and test every link from a logged-out or incognito browser and a mobile phone. Detect it with a link-test log recording each URL, test date and result.

Tools without outcomes

Listing tools used without outcomes or insights

Briefs that read "Tools: Excel, Power Query, Power BI, DAX" with no result tell the reader you have touched software, not that you created value. People default to tool lists because they are easy to write and mirror job-ad keywords. The correct treatment is a STAR summary where every Result line is quantified and business-relevant: "Identified ₹38 crore working-capital release from a 12-day reduction in receivable days" or "Reduced monthly variance pack preparation from 2 days to 3 hours via Power Query refresh". Tools still appear, but as the means to an outcome. Detect it by highlighting every sentence in the brief that contains a number tied to a business consequence — if fewer than two exist, rewrite.

Practice Questions

Q1. Dossier audit of a peer's portfolio [L3]

A peer shares a Google Drive link to their portfolio. You find: a single folder containing Model final final.xlsm (42 MB, three external links to D:\Office\Client_TB.xlsx), dashboard.pbix, CV.pdf, and a 2-page Word note listing "Excel, Power BI, DAX, Power Query, SQL" for each project. One dashboard visual shows a company logo you recognise as their current audit client. The Drive link opens only after requesting access. Write a structured review memo identifying every issue against s02–s07, ranking them by severity (confidentiality, accessibility, credibility, presentation), and specifying the remediation and the evidence the peer should show you to prove each fix.

Q2. Building the skills matrix and closing a gap [L2]

You are targeting three roles: (a) Ind AS technical accounting analyst at a Big 4 firm (requirements: Ind AS 115/116/109, consolidation, disclosure checklists), (b) FP&A analyst at an NSE-listed FMCG company (budgeting, variance analysis, Power BI, driver-based forecasting), (c) valuation associate at a mid-size advisory firm (3-statement modelling, DCF, comps, transaction multiples). Your six projects are those in s01, except that your capital budgeting/M&A project contains NPV/IRR analysis only, with no purchase price allocation or accretion/dilution. Construct the skills matrix (projects × requirements, rated 0–3), compute coverage per role, identify the weakest coverage, and propose a scoped enhancement to one project that can be completed in 6 hours, including how its brief, README and file version number would change.

💡 Hint: Coverage can be scored as the sum of ratings divided by the maximum possible for that role's requirement list.

Q3. End-to-end packaging of the capstone [L3]

Your capstone is a 3-statement + DCF model of an NSE-listed specialty chemicals mid-cap (FY21–FY25 historicals, FY26–FY35 forecast), with a 12-slide deck, a 6-minute raw screen recording and a cover sheet showing master check "OK". Market data was taken on 14 November 2026 (CMP ₹1,842, 10-year G-sec 6.45%). Produce: (a) the folder structure and file names using the c02 convention; (b) a complete README per c03; (c) a STAR summary with at least three quantified result lines; (d) a list of the five screenshots you would include with the annotation for each; (e) a plan to cut the video to under 5 minutes, with timestamps per segment.

Q4. Power BI hosting decision [L2]

Your Power BI budget-vs-actual dashboard uses a public company's segment data from its FY25 annual report, plus a synthetic monthly phasing you generated. Your organisation's tenant disables Publish to web; you have a free personal Microsoft account only. Evaluate the options — Publish to web via a personal tenant, sharing the pbix, PDF export, PowerPoint export, screenshots, and video — for a reviewer who uses a Mac and has no Power BI licence. Recommend a combination, state the confidentiality and licensing caveats, and draft the two-line "how to view" note for the README.

Q5. Interview defence of the dossier [L3]

In a panel interview for an FP&A role, the interviewer opens your Project 5 brief, which claims "Forecast accuracy improved from 82% to 94% MAPE-adjusted after driver rebuild". She asks: (i) how was accuracy measured and over what periods, (ii) what was the data source and is it real or synthetic, (iii) where in the workbook can she verify it, and (iv) why the result line uses "improved" if the data is a back-test on public history. Prepare a structured answer to each, identify what in your dossier should be rewritten to make the claim defensible, and rewrite the result line.
