Key concept

Skills Matrix: Mapping Projects to Role Requirements

Concept: Skills matrix: map each project to role requirements (Ind AS, consolidation, modelling, Power BI/DAX, FP&A)

Statement

A skills matrix is a grid with portfolio projects as columns and role requirements as rows. Each cell holds a rating of how strongly the project evidences the skill:

  • 0 = not demonstrated
  • 1 = touched (used, not central)
  • 2 = demonstrated (central, documented)
  • 3 = showcase (central, documented, with an evidence item and a quantified result)

Coverage for a target role with requirements \( r = 1, \dots, R \) is:

\[ \text{Coverage}_{\text{role}} = \frac{\sum_{r=1}^{R} \max_{p} \, s_{r,p}}{3R} \]

where \( s_{r,p} \) is the rating of project \( p \) for requirement \( r \), and \( \max_{p} \) takes the strongest project for each requirement. Requirements with a maximum rating below 2 are gaps.

Explanation

The matrix serves three purposes. First, targeting: for each application you can point to the two or three projects that evidence the job description's core requirements. Second, gap analysis: it exposes skills claimed on the CV but not evidenced (consolidation is a common gap — few self-built projects include Ind AS 110 eliminations or NCI). Third, interview preparation: each high-rated cell is a story you should be ready to tell in STAR form.

The five core requirement families for the target roles:

Requirement family Typical granular requirements
Ind AS Ind AS 115, 116, 109 (ECL, classification), 12, 37, Schedule III presentation, disclosures
Consolidation Ind AS 110 control, intra-group eliminations, NCI, Ind AS 103 PPA, Ind AS 28 equity method
Modelling 3-statement integration, schedules, DCF, WACC, sensitivities, checks
Power BI/DAX Power Query, star schema, DAX measures, time intelligence, RLS, publishing
FP&A Budgeting, rolling forecasts, variance analysis, driver-based planning, commentary, KPIs

Build the requirement rows from actual job descriptions: collect five to ten postings for each target role (from Naukri, LinkedIn, company career pages), extract recurring requirements, and weight them by frequency if you want more precision. Rate honestly — overrating defeats the purpose.

For an Ind AS technical accounting role with requirements (Ind AS 115/116/109, consolidation/Ind AS 103), coverage = (3 + 1) ÷ 6 ≈ 67%; consolidation is a gap. A remedy: extend P6 with a full Ind AS 103 PPA and day-one consolidation (eliminations, NCI at proportionate share or fair value), raising that row to 3 and coverage to 100%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a matrix from job descriptions [L2]

From three job descriptions you collect (one each for Ind AS reporting analyst, FP&A analyst, valuation associate), extract eight granular requirements, build the 8 × 6 matrix for your own portfolio, compute coverage for each role using the formula above, and list every gap.

Q2. Prioritising a gap fix [L3]

Your matrix shows coverage of 92% for valuation roles, 75% for FP&A roles (gap: driver-based rolling forecast rated 1) and 58% for Ind AS roles (gaps: consolidation rated 1, Ind AS 109 classification rated 0). You have 10 hours. Recommend which gap(s) to close, through which project changes, and justify the choice based on your target-role priority and the effort required.

💡 Hint: Weigh coverage improvement per hour against which role you are applying to first.

Q3. Matrix in an interview [L2]

An interviewer asks, "Which of your projects best shows consolidation skills?" and your honest rating is 1. Draft a 60-second answer that is truthful, references the relevant project, and explains how you would approach a consolidation task.
