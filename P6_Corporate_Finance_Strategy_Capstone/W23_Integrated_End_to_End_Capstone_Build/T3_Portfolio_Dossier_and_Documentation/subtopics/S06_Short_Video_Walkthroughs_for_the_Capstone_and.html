Short Video Walkthroughs for the Capstone and Dashboard

Scope: Short video walkthroughs (3–5 min) for the capstone and dashboard

Why it matters

A workbook or pbix proves you built something; a video proves you understand it. Interactivity in Power BI and the flow of a 3-statement model are hard to convey in static images, and many reviewers will watch a 4-minute video when they would never open a 17-tab model. A video also previews your communication style — the skill FP&A and advisory roles test in presentations to CFOs and boards. Keep videos to the two flagship items (P4 capstone and P3 dashboard); six videos dilute attention and multiply maintenance.

Core notes

Length and structure

Target 3–5 minutes. Beyond five minutes, completion rates drop sharply and the reviewer starts skipping. Use an answer-first structure:

Segment Capstone (P4) Dashboard (P3) Time
Hook Recommendation and value range Key business question and headline insight 0:00–0:20
Context Company, data sources, valuation date Data sources, refresh and model design 0:20–0:50
Architecture Tab map: inputs → schedules → statements → DCF → checks Star schema and key DAX measures 0:50–1:40
Key mechanics One schedule in depth (e.g. capacity-driven revenue or debt), statement integration Slicer, drill-through, tooltip, a time-intelligence visual 1:40–3:00
Output and sensitivity DCF, EV bridge, sensitivity table, football field Variance page and insight 3:00–4:00
Integrity and close Checks sheet status; flex test in action; repeat recommendation Reconciliation to source; repeat insight 4:00–4:30

Script

Write a script or detailed bullet outline; read-through speed of about 130–150 words per minute means a 4-minute video is roughly 550 words. Scripted does not mean robotic — rehearse until you can speak from bullets. Every segment should answer "so what": "Revenue is built bottom-up from capacity and utilisation because this company's growth is constrained by plant capacity — the new 40,000-tonne line adds 28% capacity from FY28."

Demonstrating integrity live

The most persuasive 20 seconds in a capstone video: change a key input on camera (e.g. utilisation from 82% to 60% via the scenario switch), show the statements and DCF update, show the balance check still at zero, then reset to base. For the dashboard: select a slicer and show that a card total equals the annual report figure.

Recording set-up

  • Software: Microsoft Teams or Zoom meeting recordings, OBS Studio (free), Loom, Windows Clipchamp or macOS QuickTime/Screenshot (Cmd+Shift+5).
  • Screen: 1920×1080 resolution, Excel zoom at 110–130% so numbers are legible on a phone, close notifications (Focus mode), hide the taskbar and bookmarks bar, clear desktop.
  • Audio: a headset or USB microphone matters more than the camera; record in a quiet room; avoid fans and echo.
  • Camera: optional; a small face-cam bubble in a corner helps engagement but is not essential.
  • Navigation: use named ranges or hyperlinks on the cover sheet to jump between tabs smoothly; use Ctrl+Page Up/Down; pre-set each sheet's scroll position.
  • Pointer: enable pointer highlighting or use a cursor highlight tool so viewers can follow.

Editing and publishing

Trim pauses and false starts, add a title card (project number, title, your name, valuation date) and an end card (link to repository). Add captions — auto-generated captions in YouTube or Clipchamp need correcting for terms like "Ind AS", "EBITDA", "₹ crore". Publish on YouTube as Unlisted (viewable only via link, not searchable) or on Drive/OneDrive with viewer permission; embed or link from Notion and the project README. Name the file consistently: P4_Capstone_Walkthrough_v1.0.mp4.

Illustration — word budget for a 4-minute capstone video

At 140 words per minute, 4 minutes ≈ 560 words: hook 45, context 70, architecture 115, mechanics 190, outputs 95, integrity and close 45. If your draft script is 900 words, it will run about 6.5 minutes — cut the mechanics section, not the hook or the checks.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Model changes after recording. If you change numbers after recording, the video no longer matches. Record after final freeze; note the model version on the title card; re-record if headline numbers change.
  • Nervous delivery. It is acceptable to record segment by segment and join them. Do not over-edit into jump cuts every few seconds.
  • Language. English is standard for portfolio reach; a Hindi or regional-language version is optional for domestic roles but doubles maintenance.
  • Showing confidential windows. Screen recording captures everything — email pop-ups, browser tabs, file paths with employer names. Use a clean user profile or dedicated desktop.
  • Excessive detail. Do not walk through every tab. Choose one schedule to show in depth; the reviewer infers the rest.
  • Accessibility. Captions help non-native listeners and viewers watching without sound.

Key takeaways

  • Record only for the capstone and dashboard; 3–5 minutes each.
  • Structure answer-first: hook, context, architecture, one mechanic in depth, outputs, integrity, close.
  • Budget about 140 words per minute and script accordingly.
  • Show a live flex test and check status — it is the most persuasive segment.
  • Record after final freeze, at legible zoom, with clean desktop and good audio; publish unlisted with captions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Script outline for the dashboard video [L2]

Your P3 dashboard has four pages (overview, segment performance, working capital, variance) built on a star schema with a date table and 18 DAX measures, using five years of data for an NSE-listed consumer durables company. Write a timed outline for a 4-minute video, with the word budget for each segment and the specific interaction you will demonstrate in each.

Q2. Cutting an over-long recording [L2]

Your first capstone recording runs 8 minutes 40 seconds: 1:30 introduction of yourself and the course, 2:10 walking through every historical tab, 2:00 on schedules, 1:20 on DCF, 0:40 on sensitivities, 0:30 on checks, 0:30 closing. Re-plan it to 4:30, stating what you cut, compress or move earlier, and why.

💡 Hint: Ask which segments a hiring manager would skip forward through.

Q3. Live flex test choreography [L2]

Describe the exact on-screen steps for a 25-second live flex test in the capstone video: which input you change, from what to what, which three outputs you show changing, how you show the balance and cash checks, and how you reset — including keyboard navigation or hyperlinks used.

Q4. Recording hygiene review [L1]

List eight items on a pre-recording checklist covering screen, audio, privacy and model state, with a one-line reason for each.
