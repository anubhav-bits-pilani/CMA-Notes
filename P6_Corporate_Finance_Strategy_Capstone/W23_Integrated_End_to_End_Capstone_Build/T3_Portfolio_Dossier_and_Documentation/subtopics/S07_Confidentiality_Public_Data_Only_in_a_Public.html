Confidentiality: Public Data Only in a Public Portfolio

Scope: Confidentiality: public data only, no employer or client data (ICMAI Code of Ethics confidentiality)

Why it matters

Your portfolio is public, permanent and searchable. A single client trial balance, a screenshot of an employer's MIS template or an "anonymised" sales ledger can breach your professional obligations, your employment or engagement contract, data protection law and, for listed companies, insider trading rules. The damage is asymmetric: a confidentiality lapse can end a candidacy or attract disciplinary action, while the benefit of using real client data over public data is negligible. For a CMA with audit experience, demonstrating that you understand and apply confidentiality is itself a signal of professional maturity.

Core notes

The professional obligation — ICMAI Code of Ethics

The ICMAI Code of Ethics for cost accountants is aligned with the IFAC/IESBA framework and sets out fundamental principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, and professional behaviour. The confidentiality principle requires a member to respect the confidentiality of information acquired as a result of professional and business relationships, and not to disclose it to third parties without proper and specific authority unless there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose, nor to use it for personal advantage or the advantage of third parties.

Key implications:

  • The duty applies to members in practice (audit, cost audit, consulting) and members in employment (industry roles).
  • It continues after the relationship ends — leaving a firm does not release you.
  • "Use for personal advantage" covers using client data to build a portfolio that advances your career, even if no one is harmed.
  • It extends to information obtained in a social environment and to prospective clients or employers.
  • Under the Cost and Works Accountants Act, 1959, disclosing information acquired in the course of professional engagement without client consent is professional misconduct for members in practice, subject to disciplinary proceedings.

Other legal and contractual constraints

Source What it restricts
Employment contract / NDA Use or disclosure of employer information, templates, processes; usually survives termination
Engagement letter (audit/consulting) Client information and working papers; working papers belong to the firm (SA 230)
SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 Communicating unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI) about listed companies
Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 Processing and disclosure of personal data (employee names, customer details, PAN, phone numbers)
Copyright Redistribution of proprietary templates, paid training material, paid database exports

What is acceptable

  • Published financial statements of listed companies: annual reports, LODR Reg 33 quarterly results, investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, XBRL filings, credit rating rationales, stock exchange disclosures.
  • Public market data: NSE/BSE prices, RBI yields, index data.
  • Synthetic data you generate yourself, clearly labelled, even if calibrated to a public company's reported totals.
  • Public datasets with licences permitting reuse (check the licence terms).
  • Your own templates, designed from scratch using general professional knowledge and public guidance (ICAI/ICMAI publications, Ind AS illustrative examples).

What is not acceptable

  • Any client or employer trial balance, ledger, MIS, budget, board pack or audit working paper — even with names removed.
  • Employer-designed templates, chart of accounts, policy manuals or checklists.
  • Screenshots that show internal systems (ERP screens, Tally company names, SharePoint paths).
  • Data aggregated from paid databases (Capitaline, Prowess, CMIE) where the licence prohibits redistribution.
  • Anything that could be UPSI — e.g. a listed client's unpublished quarterly numbers.

Why "anonymisation" usually fails

Removing names does not remove identifiability. A combination of industry, revenue scale, location, product mix, and period can identify a company, especially in Indian mid-caps where few firms share a niche. Ratios and structures can also be distinctive. Moreover, the confidentiality duty attaches to the information itself, not just the name; anonymisation does not create authority to disclose. The safe rule: if the data came from a professional or employment relationship, it does not go in the portfolio.

Rebuilding work on public data

Your best real-world experience can still be showcased by rebuilding the technique on public data:

  1. Identify the skill (e.g. Ind AS 116 lease modification accounting in a close).
  2. Find a listed company with a comparable fact pattern (e.g. a retailer with a large lease portfolio disclosed in its notes).
  3. Construct a synthetic but realistic scenario using public disclosures (lease liability opening balance, incremental borrowing rate disclosed or assumed).
  4. Build the schedule and entries, labelled "illustrative, based on public disclosures of X Ltd".
  5. In interviews, describe the real experience verbally at a generic level ("I handled lease modifications for a retail audit client") without naming the client or numbers.

Pre-publication confidentiality scan

Before publishing each file: search for client and employer names, GSTIN patterns (15 characters starting with a 2-digit state code), PAN patterns (5 letters, 4 digits, 1 letter), email domains, internal codes; run Document Inspector; check hidden and very hidden sheets, named ranges and comments; check pbix data sources and imported tables; check image backgrounds and file paths; check the "author", "company" and "manager" document properties.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Employer consent. Written authority from a former employer to publish a sanitised case study removes the confidentiality bar for that employer's information, but still check client information within it and data protection. In practice, obtaining consent is rare and not worth the delay.
  • Public information you learnt privately. If information later becomes public (e.g. published in the annual report), you may use the public version — cite the public source, not your working papers.
  • Generic know-how. General skills and knowledge acquired at work (how to build a provision matrix) are yours to use; specific information (that client's loss rates) is not.
  • Training course material. Case data from paid courses is usually copyrighted; build your own instead of uploading course files.
  • Peer company names in synthetic data. Avoid using real company names for synthetic data that could be mistaken for real results.

Key takeaways

  • The ICMAI Code of Ethics confidentiality principle bars disclosure and personal use of information from professional or employment relationships, and survives after the relationship ends.
  • Contracts, SA 230 working-paper ownership, SEBI PIT regulations, the DPDP Act 2023 and copyright add further constraints.
  • Use only published company data, public market data, licensed public datasets and clearly labelled synthetic data.
  • Anonymisation does not create authority to disclose and often fails to prevent identification.
  • Rebuild techniques on public data; run a confidentiality scan before publishing every file.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the data [L1]

Classify each as acceptable or not acceptable for a public portfolio, with a one-line reason: (a) a listed retailer's FY25 annual report lease note; (b) your former audit client's TB with names replaced by codes; (c) Q2 FY27 results filed on NSE; (d) your current employer's monthly MIS template with all numbers deleted; (e) synthetic monthly sales generated from a listed FMCG company's annual revenue; (f) peer data exported from a paid database to which your employer subscribes; (g) a credit rating rationale published by a rating agency.

Q2. Rebuilding a real engagement [L2]

At your audit firm you reviewed an ECL provision matrix for a private manufacturing client with ₹180 crore of trade receivables. Design a portfolio project that demonstrates the same skill using only public data, specifying the listed company you would look for (characteristics), what disclosures you would use, what you would synthesise, and how you would label it.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 107 credit risk disclosures in listed companies' notes often give ageing buckets.

Q3. Ethical case — peer request [L3]

A colleague who has left your firm asks you to send her the consolidation template your firm uses so she can "clean it up and put it on GitHub as a portfolio piece". Analyse the request under the ICMAI Code of Ethics principles, the firm's ownership of working papers and templates, and contractual obligations, and draft your response to her.

Q4. Confidentiality scan design [L2]

Write a pre-publication confidentiality scan procedure for an Excel workbook and a pbix, including at least three text patterns to search for (with the structure of each pattern), the Excel and Power BI locations where confidential data can hide, and how you would document that the scan was completed.
