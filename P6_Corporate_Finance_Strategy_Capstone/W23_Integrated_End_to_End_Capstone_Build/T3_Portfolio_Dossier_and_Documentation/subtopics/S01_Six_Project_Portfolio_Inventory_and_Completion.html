Subtopic note

Six-Project Portfolio Inventory and Completion Standards

Scope: Six-project inventory: Ind AS close pack, ratio/forensic peer benchmark, Power BI dashboard, 3-statement DCF, budget/forecast/variance MIS, capital budgeting/M&A

Why it matters

Target roles in India for a CMA moving into finance — Ind AS technical accounting, financial reporting analysis, FP&A, valuation and modelling — test overlapping but different skills. A Big 4 accounting advisory interviewer wants to see Ind AS judgement and disclosure; an FMCG FP&A head wants budgeting, variance commentary and dashboards; a valuation boutique wants a clean 3-statement model and DCF. Six projects, each mapped to a distinct skill cluster, give you coverage across all three without the portfolio becoming a scattered collection of exercises. The inventory is also a control: it forces you to state, for each project, what "done" means before you start polishing.

Core notes

The inventory as a register

Maintain an inventory register (a simple Excel table or Notion database) with one row per project and these columns: project number, short name, company/data set, period covered, skill cluster, source weeks, status (draft / checked / packaged / published), check status, file list, brief written (Y/N), video (Y/N), last updated. The register is the backbone for naming (c02), the skills matrix (c04) and the README index.

# Project Skill cluster demonstrated Source weeks (typical)
P1 Ind AS close pack Technical accounting, Schedule III presentation, disclosures Weeks 1–5
P2 Ratio/forensic peer benchmark Financial analysis, red-flag detection Weeks 6–8
P3 Power BI dashboard Data modelling, DAX, visualisation Weeks 9–12
P4 3-statement DCF (capstone) Integrated modelling, valuation Weeks 13–16, 23
P5 Budget/forecast/variance MIS FP&A process, driver-based planning, commentary Weeks 17–20
P6 Capital budgeting/M&A Investment appraisal, deal analysis Weeks 21–22

P1 — Ind AS close pack

A month-end or year-end close pack built on a synthetic trial balance or a public company's reported figures, showing the path from TB to Schedule III Division II financial statements. Minimum content: TB mapping to Schedule III line items; adjusting entries with Dr/Cr tables and Ind AS references (e.g. Ind AS 115 revenue cut-off, Ind AS 116 lease remeasurement, Ind AS 109 expected credit loss on trade receivables using a provision matrix, Ind AS 12 deferred tax); a statement of profit and loss, balance sheet, statement of changes in equity and cash flow statement (Ind AS 7); and two or three note disclosures. Completion standard: TB balances to zero, statements tie to the mapped TB, every adjustment cross-referenced.

P2 — Ratio and forensic peer benchmark

A benchmark of three to five NSE-listed peers in one industry over five years. Minimum content: standardised financials (reclassifying exceptional items and other income), DuPont decomposition, liquidity, leverage, efficiency and cash conversion ratios, and forensic tests — Beneish M-score, accrual ratio (net income minus CFO over average assets), CFO/EBITDA conversion, receivable days trend versus revenue growth, related-party transaction intensity, auditor changes and qualifications (CARO 2020 reporting). Completion standard: every ratio formula documented, peers standardised on the same basis, at least three written insights.

P3 — Power BI dashboard

A dashboard with a proper star schema (fact table plus date, entity, account dimensions), Power Query transformations, and DAX measures such as time intelligence (TOTALYTD, SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR), variance and variance %. Completion standard: date table marked as date table, no implicit measures in visuals, slicers tested, report pages titled with insights.

P4 — 3-statement DCF capstone

The Week 23 t1 deliverable: five years of restated historicals, schedules (revenue, cost, PPE, WC, leases, debt, tax, equity), integrated IS/BS/CFS, FCFF DCF with WACC build, sensitivity tables and a checks sheet, plus the t4 executive deck. Completion standard: master check "OK" in all scenarios, flex test passed, deck numbers tie to the model.

P5 — Budget, forecast and variance MIS

An annual budget with monthly phasing, a rolling forecast (e.g. 9+3), and a variance pack with price/volume/mix analysis and board-style commentary. Completion standard: budget to actual reconciliation, variance bridges that sum to the total, commentary that explains causes and actions rather than restating numbers.

P6 — Capital budgeting and M&A

A project appraisal (NPV, IRR, payback, discounted payback, sensitivity and scenario analysis, with depreciation tax shield under Income-tax Act block-of-assets rules) and/or an M&A case: purchase price, funding mix, accretion/dilution on EPS, and an outline Ind AS 103 purchase price allocation (identifiable intangibles, deferred tax on fair value uplifts, goodwill). Completion standard: cash flows on a consistent nominal/real basis, discount rate matched to the risk of the cash flows, all assumptions sourced.

Scope control and minimum viable versus polished

Each project should have a defined "minimum viable" state (works, checked, briefly documented) and a "polished" state (brief, README, screenshots, video for P3/P4). Prioritise getting all six to minimum viable before polishing any one; an incomplete project six is a bigger gap than an unpolished project two. A practical time budget for packaging is 2–4 hours per project, with more for P4 and P3.

Illustration — status-driven prioritisation

Suppose on Monday of Week 23 your register shows: P1 checked, P2 draft (forensic tests missing), P3 packaged, P4 in build, P5 checked, P6 draft (no sensitivity). With roughly 20 hours available outside the capstone, a sensible allocation is P4 capstone first (it is the flagship), then P2 forensic tests (4 h), P6 sensitivity (3 h), then briefs and READMEs for P1, P2, P5, P6 (2 h each, 8 h), leaving 5 hours for screenshots and links.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Overlapping data sets. Using the same company for P2, P4 and P6 saves data-entry time and tells a coherent story, but reduces the breadth of industries shown. A common compromise: P4 and P6 on the same company (an acquisition case following the valuation), P2 on its peer group.
  • Synthetic versus public data. P1 and P5 often need synthetic monthly data because listed companies do not publish monthly TBs. That is acceptable if labelled clearly ("synthetic data calibrated to FY25 annual report of X Ltd") — never present synthetic results as real.
  • Bank/NBFC projects. If you have strong banking audit experience, a bank-specific ratio project (NIM, GNPA, PCR, CASA) can replace P2, but note that it does not demonstrate corporate modelling.
  • Group work. If a project was done with others, state your contribution explicitly in the brief.
  • Version drift. Projects built in Week 8 may predate skills you learnt later. Decide whether to upgrade (and bump the version) or leave as-is with a "built in Week 8" note.

Key takeaways

  • Six projects map to six skill clusters; together they cover Ind AS reporting, analysis, BI, modelling, FP&A and investment decisions.
  • Keep an inventory register with status, check status and packaging flags — it drives naming, README index and skills matrix.
  • Define a completion standard for each project before polishing.
  • Get all six to minimum viable before polishing any one; the capstone is the flagship.
  • Label synthetic data clearly and state your own contribution for any joint work.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draft the inventory register [L1]

Create the column headers and the six rows of an inventory register for your own portfolio. For each project, fill in the skill cluster, data set, period covered and current status, and define in one sentence the completion standard that must be met before the project is marked "checked".

Q2. Prioritising with limited time [L2]

You have 14 hours of packaging time this week. Status: P1 packaged; P2 checked but no brief; P3 draft with two broken DAX measures; P4 checked, deck incomplete (6 of 12 slides); P5 draft, variance bridge does not sum to total variance by ₹2.3 lakh; P6 packaged. Estimate the hours needed for each remaining task and produce a prioritised plan, justifying your order with reference to reviewer impact and risk.

💡 Hint: Unresolved errors in a published file damage credibility more than a missing brief does.

Q3. Choosing companies across projects [L2]

You are choosing data sets for P2, P4 and P6. Option A: one cement company throughout (valuation in P4, acquisition of a regional peer in P6, peer benchmark in P2). Option B: three different industries (cement, quick-service restaurants, IT services). Evaluate both options on data-entry effort, narrative coherence, breadth signalled to recruiters and Ind AS 116 complexity, and recommend one for an FP&A-focused candidate.

Q4. Synthetic data disclosure [L2]

Your P5 MIS uses monthly phasing you generated by applying seasonality factors to an FMCG company's FY25 annual revenue of ₹6,240 crore. Draft the data-basis paragraph for the brief, explaining what is real, what is synthetic, how the synthetic data was generated and what conclusions a reader can and cannot draw.

Q5. Upgrade or freeze an older project [L3]

Your P2 benchmark, built in Week 7, uses unstandardised figures — other income is included in EBITDA and exceptional items are not separated — and has no forensic tests. An interviewer for a forensic accounting role will likely open it. Decide whether to rebuild it, upgrade it partially or leave it with a caveat, and specify the version number change, the change log entry and the new result lines you would expect to be able to write.
