Roadmap topic: Model Audit & Quality Assurance Checklist: Verifying zero formula errors, dynamic sensitivity tables, clear executive cover sheets, and print-ready page setups

Overview

A model is not finished when it produces a value per share. It is finished when someone other than the author can trust that value. This topic turns the capstone model from Week 23 t1, and every other workbook in your portfolio, into something that passes an institutional review: zero formula errors, integrity checks that pass and can fail, sensitivity tables that are live, a Cover that tells the reader what they are looking at, and printouts and PDFs that are readable and traceable. The Saturday assessment is a three-hour portfolio audit against a rubric of Accuracy, Dynamic Structure, Visual Clarity and Business Insight. Most of the marks are earned or lost on the material in this topic.

For the target roles, model QA is a daily skill rather than a one-off. FP&A analysts inherit budget and forecast models, and deal and valuation teams review client and counterparty models. Lenders' and investors' models are often reviewed against FAST or house standards, and listed companies' spreadsheet-based processes fall within internal financial controls reporting under the Companies Act. Your audit background is directly relevant here. Model QA is substantive testing plus controls testing, applied to a workbook instead of a ledger.

The subtopics follow the logic of an audit. s01 sets the criteria (FAST, ICAEW Code, consistent columns, one formula per row). s02 specifies the integrity checks: balance, cash, retained earnings, debt and PPE roll-forwards and sign conventions. s03 catalogues the error classes, and s04 the Excel tools that find them. s05 tests the sensitivity and scenario machinery. s06 and s07 cover presentation and print, and s08 the executive Cover that reports the results. The key concepts are the controls you implement: the master check (c01), the row consistency test (c02), the cash and RE checks (c03), flex testing (c04) and the calculation mode for data tables (c05).

Related concepts to study

Weeks 9–10: Excel practice, names and dynamic arrays

→ Week 9–10: Excel best practices, named ranges and dynamic arrays

Weeks 9–10 built the Excel habits this topic audits. Named ranges for scalar inputs (WACC, Tax_Rate, Scenario) make formulas readable and are easy to check in Name Manager. Names for time series, or names with errors and external references, are audit findings. Dynamic arrays (FILTER, SORT, UNIQUE, XLOOKUP, LET) reduce helper columns but create spill ranges that a reviewer must recognise. A #SPILL! error is an error like any other and must appear in the error count. LET can make long formulas auditable by naming intermediate steps, which helps FAST's "short formulas" aim. Revisit the shortcut set (Ctrl+[, F2, F9 in the formula bar) since s04 relies on it.

Week 15: circularity and copy-paste macros

→ Week 15: circularity handling and copy-paste macro alternatives

Week 15 covered the two ways to compute interest on average balances: iterative calculation with a circuit breaker, or a copy-paste macro that calculates interest, pastes it as values into an input row, and repeats until the change falls below a tolerance. The macro approach keeps the workbook free of circular references, which FAST and many lenders prefer. The trade-off is that the model is only correct after the macro runs, so a "macro last run" flag and a check comparing pasted and calculated interest are needed. For QA, test both paths: circularity off (CB = 0) and on, or macro run and not run, and confirm the checks pass. Remember that a macro-enabled file (.xlsm) may be blocked when downloaded from the internet, which is relevant to portfolio sharing (t3).

Spreadsheet controls under ICFR and Sec 143(3)(i)

Model risk management and spreadsheet controls in ICFR (Sec 143(3)(i) IFC reporting)

Sec 143(3)(i) of the Companies Act 2013 requires the statutory auditor to report whether the company has adequate internal financial controls with reference to financial statements and whether they operate effectively. Sec 134(5)(e) requires directors of listed companies to state that IFCs are adequate. ICAI's Guidance Note on Audit of Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting sets out how auditors test them. MCA's 2017 exemption for certain private companies (by turnover or borrowings thresholds) is date-sensitive, so check the current notification. Spreadsheets used in financial reporting (provision calculations, ECL models, impairment and fair value models, consolidation workbooks) are end-user computing (EUC) tools within the scope of IFC. Expected controls are an inventory of key spreadsheets, risk rating, access and change control, version control, input reconciliation, built-in checks and independent review. These are the same controls as this topic, applied with documentation. Model risk management in banks (RBI guidance on model risk in credit and market risk models) extends the idea to validation and back-testing.

Version control and change logs

Version control: file naming, change logs, and SharePoint/OneDrive version history

Version control answers "which file produced this number?". Use a file naming convention with an explicit version (P4_Capstone_KBPL_v1.0.xlsx; never "final_FINAL2"), increment minor versions for working changes (v0.6) and major versions for frozen releases (v1.0), and record each version in the Cover's change log with the date, author, description, reviewer and master check status (s08). SharePoint and OneDrive keep automatic version history (File → Info → Version History), which allows restoring and comparing earlier saves. That is useful, but it is no substitute for named releases, because autosave creates many unlabelled versions. Use Inquire's Compare Files (Spreadsheet Compare) to document what changed between releases. For GitHub portfolio hosting (t3), commit only release versions with a meaningful message.

Common pitfalls

IFERROR used to hide genuine errors

Using IFERROR to hide genuine errors instead of fixing their cause

The mistake: wrapping formulas, or even the master check, in IFERROR(…,0) so the model shows no errors. Why it happens: errors look unprofessional, and a blanket wrapper is quicker than diagnosing a lookup failure or a division by zero. Correct treatment: fix the cause. Handle expected conditions explicitly (IF(denominator=0,…)), use IFNA or XLOOKUP's not-found argument for lookups, and let all other errors surface into the error-count check. Detection: Find all IFERROR instances, justify each in a list, and temporarily disable them (e.g. by text substitution on a copy) to see which cells would error (s03).

Inconsistent sign conventions

Inconsistent signs (capex positive in one schedule, negative in another) leading to silent errors

The mistake: capex entered as positive on the PPE schedule and pulled into the CFS with a minus sign, but pulled into the DCF as a positive number and added. Or dividends negative in Equity but subtracted again in the CFS. Why it happens: schedules built at different times, or templates combined from different sources. Correct treatment: choose one convention (natural signs or cash-flow signs), write it on the Cover, and apply it across schedules, statements and DCF. Add sign-count checks on fixed-sign rows (s02). Detection: sign checks; a flex test doubling capex (FCFF must fall, c04); and Trace Precedents on the DCF capex line to confirm its source and sign.

Data tables pointing to other-sheet inputs

Data tables pointing to inputs on another sheet, returning wrong or static values

The mistake: because Excel requires data table input cells to be on the table's sheet, the modeller creates a local copy of WACC and g on the Sensitivity sheet and points the table at them, but the model uses the originals on Inputs. The table then shows the same value in every cell, or values that do not respond to the axes. Why it happens: the "Input cell reference is not valid" error and time pressure. Correct treatment: host the table on the sheet holding the live inputs, or make the live input a formula reading an override cell that the table drives (s05). Detection: a sensitivity centre check (centre = live base output), and visual inspection (a table with identical values in every cell, or no change along an axis).

Scenario switch not reset to base

Forgetting to reset scenario switches to base case before sending

The mistake: sending or freezing the model on the bear (or a flex) scenario, so the Cover, PDFs and the deck's pasted numbers show the wrong case. Why it happens: testing scenarios and flex cases is the last step before sending, and nothing forces a reset. Correct treatment: a pre-send routine (reset switch, circuit breaker on, F9, master check OK, save). The Cover shows the live scenario name in large type, with a non-base warning formula and amber formatting (s08). Optionally add a check row that flags a non-base scenario in "Final" status. Detection: the Cover warning, and comparing headline values in the deck with the base case in the scenario output table.

Practice Questions

Q1. One-hour audit of a peer model [L3]

A peer's capstone (17 sheets) has the following symptoms: the master check shows "OK" but the BS check row has IFERROR wrappers; the DCF capex row is positive and is added in FCFF; the WACC × g table shows ₹540 in every cell; the Cover shows "Bear case"; there are 23 constants in IS forecast columns; one external link to Peers_old.xlsx; and iterative calculation is on with no circuit breaker. Using the tools and checks in this topic, prepare a prioritised findings report (critical/major/minor), the test that confirmed each finding, the fix and the rubric category affected.

💡 Hint: Rank first by impact on the valuation and second by how far the issue undermines trust in the other checks.

Q2. Build the QA checklist [L2]

Design a 25-item QA checklist covering standards, integrity checks, error detection, sensitivity integrity, presentation, print and Cover, with a test procedure and pass criterion for each, suitable for freezing the capstone at v1.0 and recording check status on the Cover.

Q3. Controls mapping for a listed company [L3]

A listed manufacturer's ECL provisioning for trade receivables and its goodwill impairment test are both run in Excel. Map the controls from this topic (standards, checks, error detection, version control, review) to an IFC control matrix under Sec 143(3)(i), naming for each control its objective, its frequency, the evidence retained and how the statutory auditor would test it.

Q4. Sensitivity and scenario redesign [L2]

Your model holds WACC and g on Inputs, the DCF on its own sheet, and three data tables on Sensitivity built on local copies of the inputs. Calculation is Automatic and recalculation takes 40 seconds. Redesign the set-up (input wiring, calculation mode, checks, Cover notes) and write the pre-send routine.

Q5. Presentation and print overhaul [L2]

Specify, step by step, how you would bring a black-font, unformatted 12-sheet FP&A budget model to print-ready standard in 45 minutes, covering colour styles, units, freeze panes, grouping, print areas, title rows, headers, footers and the Cover.
