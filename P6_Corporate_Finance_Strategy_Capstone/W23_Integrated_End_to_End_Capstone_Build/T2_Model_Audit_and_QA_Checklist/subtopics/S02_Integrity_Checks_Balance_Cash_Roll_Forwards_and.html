Subtopic note

Integrity Checks: Balance, Cash, Roll-Forwards and Signs

Scope: Integrity checks: balance check, cash tie-out, retained-earnings roll-forward, debt and PPE roll-forwards, sign conventions

Why it matters

Integrity checks are the model's internal controls. In audit terms, they are the analytical and reconciliation procedures that must pass before anyone relies on the output. A model that balances can still be wrong: a capex cash outflow taken through the CFS and also added to PPE twice, a dividend charged to retained earnings but not paid in cash. Roll-forward checks catch errors the balance check misses, and sign checks catch the silent errors that make a model balance for the wrong reasons. In the portfolio audit's Accuracy category, a visible, complete and passing check suite is the most important single piece of evidence.

Core notes

The check suite

Check Formula logic Location Tolerance
Balance check Total assets − (total liabilities + total equity) BS, linked to Checks ±0.001 after ROUND
Cash tie-out CFS closing cash − BS cash CFS, linked to Checks ±0.001
CFS opening cash CFS opening cash − prior-period BS cash CFS ±0.001
RE roll-forward Opening RE + PAT attributable to owners − dividends ± other movements − closing RE Equity ±0.001
Debt roll-forward Opening debt + drawdowns − repayments − closing debt (per tranche) Debt ±0.001
PPE roll-forward Opening net block + capitalisations − depreciation − disposals (NBV) − closing net block PPE ±0.001
CWIP roll-forward Opening CWIP + capex − capitalisations − closing CWIP PPE ±0.001
Lease roll-forward Opening liability + additions + interest − payments − closing liability Leases ±0.001
Historical tie-out Model historical totals − reported totals (per source) Historicals ±0.05 (rounding in reports)
Sign checks Counts of values with the wrong sign in rows with a fixed sign Checks 0

Each check returns a number (0 when passing), not a text "OK", so that the master check (c01) can sum absolute values.

Balance check and cash tie-out

These prove integration. The cash tie-out is checked separately because a model that sets BS cash from the CFS will have a zero cash check by construction. In that design, the real test is the balance check. If instead BS cash were computed as a balancing figure, the cash check would expose it. Both are needed (Week 23 t1 c05, t2 c03).

Retained-earnings roll-forward

Under Schedule III Division II, "Other equity" includes retained earnings, securities premium, general reserve, other comprehensive income reserves and others (as per Ind AS). The RE roll-forward:

\[ \text{RE}_{\text{close}} = \text{RE}_{\text{open}} + \text{PAT}_{\text{owners}} - \text{Dividends} + \text{Transfers} \pm \text{OCI reclassified to RE} \]

It catches PAT attributable to NCI flowing into owners' RE, dividends deducted in RE but not in CFS cash (the balance check also fails then), and buybacks charged inconsistently. Under the Companies Act, dividends are paid out of current or accumulated profits (Sec 123). A final dividend proposed after the year-end is not a liability at that year-end (Ind AS 10), so in the model the dividend charged in FY28 equals the final dividend for FY27 plus the interim dividend for FY28.

Debt and PPE roll-forwards

Corkscrew structure: each schedule has opening, flows and closing rows, and the opening of period \(t\) links to the closing of period \(t-1\). The check confirms that the closing balance used on the BS equals the corkscrew closing, and that the flows used on the CFS (drawdowns, repayments, capex) are the same cells. Typical failures:

  • BS borrowings linked to a "total debt" cell that excludes a new tranche.
  • CFS capex linked to capitalisations instead of cash capex (CWIP ignored).
  • Disposals at gross block removed from PPE but proceeds not in CFI.

Illustration (₹ crore, FY28): PPE opening net block 1,180; capitalisations 310; depreciation 104; disposals at NBV 6; closing net block per BS 1,386. Check: \(1{,}180 + 310 - 104 - 6 - 1{,}386 = -6\). The BS uses a closing figure that ignores disposals, which is the fix.

Sign conventions

Pick one convention and state it on the Cover:

  • Convention A (natural signs): all amounts positive, with signs applied in formulas (Revenue − COGS). Common in Indian MIS and in Schedule III presentation.
  • Convention B (cash-flow signs): inflows positive, outflows negative throughout (capex negative on the CFS and in the DCF). Common in banking models.

Whichever you choose, apply it to schedules, statements and DCF consistently (topic pitfall). Build sign checks:

=SUMPRODUCT(--(J60:S60>0))
=COUNTIFS(J60:S60,">0")

Both formulas count positive values in a capex row (row 60) where capex must be negative. A non-zero count flags a sign error. Add sign checks for capex, dividends, depreciation, repayments and tax paid.

Checks tab layout

One row per check, one column per period, all linked from the source sheets, a row total of absolute values, and the master check at the top that the Cover displays. Group by type (integration, roll-forwards, historical tie-outs, signs, input validations such as WACC greater than g and the scenario switch in range).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Historical BS that does not tie exactly. Reported totals in ₹ crore with one decimal can differ by 0.1–0.2 because of rounding in the annual report. Use a wider tolerance for historical tie-outs, document it and keep forecast tolerances tight.
  • OCI items. Remeasurement of defined benefit plans and FVOCI gains move other equity without passing through PAT. Include an OCI row in the equity roll-forward, or the RE check will fail for history.
  • Checks that are always zero by construction. A check comparing a cell with itself proves nothing. Each check must compare two independently computed numbers.
  • Tolerance creep. Raising tolerances to make checks pass hides errors. Investigate anything above ₹0.01 crore in forecasts.
  • Direct versus indirect CFS. The capstone uses indirect. If you add a direct-method view for FP&A, check that both give the same CFO.

Key takeaways

  • Balance and cash checks prove integration. Roll-forward checks (RE, debt, PPE, CWIP, leases) prove each schedule is complete.
  • Every check returns a number that is zero when passing, so the master check can aggregate it.
  • Corkscrews must use the same cells for BS closing balances and CFS flows.
  • Choose and document one sign convention, then add sign-count checks on fixed-sign rows.
  • Each check must compare independently computed figures. Keep tolerances tight in forecasts and documented for history.

Practice Questions

Q1. RE roll-forward with OCI and NCI [L2]

FY28 (₹ crore): opening retained earnings 1,840; consolidated PAT 295 of which NCI share 18; final dividend for FY27 paid in FY28 ₹6 per share on 15.8 crore shares; interim dividend FY28 ₹4 per share; actuarial loss on gratuity (net of tax) through OCI 3.2, transferred to retained earnings; closing RE in the model 1,950.4. Run the roll-forward check and identify any error.

💡 Hint: Separate the owners' and NCI shares of PAT before applying dividends.

Q2. Debt corkscrew diagnosis [L2]

Debt schedule FY29 (₹ crore): Tranche A opening 400, repaid 80; Tranche B opening 0, drawn 250. The BS shows borrowings 320 and the CFS shows net borrowing +170. The balance check is −250. Explain the failure and the fix, and write the debt roll-forward check that would have caught it immediately.

Q3. Sign-check design [L1]

Write Excel formulas that count sign violations in forecast columns J:S for capex (must be ≤ 0), depreciation (must be ≥ 0 on the PPE schedule) and dividends paid (must be ≤ 0 on the CFS), and combine them into one sign-check row on the Checks tab.

Q4. Balance without integrity [L3]

Describe two different errors that leave the balance check at zero while overstating FCFF, and specify the roll-forward or tie-out check that would detect each.
