Key concept

Cash Check and Retained-Earnings Roll-Forward Check

Concept: Cash check: CFS closing cash − BS cash = 0; RE check: Opening RE + PAT − Dividends − closing RE = 0

Statement

\[ \text{Cash check}_t = \text{Closing cash}^{\text{CFS}}_t - \text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t = 0 \]

\[ \text{RE check}_t = \text{RE}_{t-1} + \text{PAT}_t - \text{Div}_t - \text{RE}_t = 0 \]

where \(\text{Closing cash}^{\text{CFS}}_t = \text{Opening cash}_t + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}_t\); \(\text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t\) is cash and cash equivalents on the balance sheet (same definition); \(\text{RE}_{t-1}\) and \(\text{RE}_t\) are opening and closing retained earnings; \(\text{PAT}_t\) is profit after tax attributable to owners of the parent; \(\text{Div}_t\) is equity dividends declared and recognised in the period. In a full model the RE check adds other movements: OCI items reclassified to RE, transfers to or from general reserve, buyback-related transfers (capital redemption reserve) and Ind AS 8 adjustments.

Explanation

Cash check. It confirms that the CFS, built from IS and BS movements, reconciles to the BS cash figure. Its value depends on the model design:

  • If BS cash links to CFS closing cash (the recommended design, Week 23 t1 s04), the cash check is zero by construction for the forecast. Its real job is then on historical years (reported CFS versus reported BS) and in catching a broken link (BS cash typed or pointing to a wrong row). Its partner, the CFS opening cash = prior BS cash check, is not trivial and should always be included.
  • If BS cash is a separately computed figure (e.g. in a cash sweep model), the cash check is a genuine integration test.

Definitions must match. The Ind AS 7 cash and cash equivalents in the CFS may net bank overdrafts repayable on demand, while the Schedule III balance sheet shows "Cash and cash equivalents" and "Bank balances other than above" separately, with overdrafts in borrowings. Build the reconciliation explicitly.

RE check. It confirms that every profit and every distribution flows through equity exactly once. It catches:

  • PAT including NCI share flowing into owners' RE (consolidated models).
  • Dividends deducted in RE but not in the CFS, or vice versa. The balance check then also fails, but the RE check locates the problem.
  • Dividend timing errors. Under Ind AS 10, a final dividend proposed after the reporting date is not a liability, so FY27's final dividend is recognised in FY28 RE and paid in FY28 cash.
  • Dividend distribution tax remnants in old templates. DDT was abolished from 1 April 2020, and dividends are now taxed in shareholders' hands with TDS under Sec 194.

Both checks return numbers that feed the master check (c01).

Illustration

FY28 (₹ crore). Opening cash per BS FY27 is 210.0. CFO 365.0, CFI −240.0, CFF −98.0. BS cash FY28 is 237.0.

\[ \text{Closing cash}^{\text{CFS}} = 210.0 + 365.0 - 240.0 - 98.0 = 237.0, \qquad \text{Cash check} = 237.0 - 237.0 = 0 \]

RE: opening 1,620.0; PAT attributable to owners 248.0 (consolidated PAT 262.0, NCI 14.0); dividends recognised in FY28 = FY27 final ₹5/share + FY28 interim ₹3/share on 15.8 crore shares = 126.4; model closing RE 1,755.6.

\[ \text{RE check} = 1{,}620.0 + 248.0 - 126.4 - 1{,}755.6 = -14.0 \]

The difference equals the NCI share of PAT. The Equity schedule rolled RE forward on consolidated PAT (1,620.0 + 262.0 − 126.4 = 1,755.6) instead of PAT attributable to owners (which gives 1,741.6). The balance check may still pass if the NCI line was not credited with its 14.0, so the RE check is what locates the error. The fix is to roll RE forward on owners' PAT and route the NCI share to the NCI line in equity.

Connections

These checks are part of the integrity suite in s02 and the Week 23 t1 balance and cash concept (c05). They feed the master check (c01) and the Cover status (s08). Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both checks [L1]

FY29 (₹ crore): BS cash FY28 237.0, CFO 402.0, CFI −310.0, CFF −55.0, BS cash FY29 274.0. Opening RE 1,741.6, PAT to owners 281.0, dividends 142.2, transfer from general reserve to RE 10.0, closing RE in the model 1,880.4. Compute both checks and identify which item, if any, is missing.

💡 Hint: Write the RE roll-forward with every movement before you compare.

Q2. Trivial check trap [L2]

Explain why a cash check comparing BS cash with CFS closing cash is always zero in a model where BS cash is defined as =CFS!J88, and which two checks you would add so that cash integration is genuinely tested.

Q3. Dividend timing [L2]

A company's board recommends a final dividend of ₹8 per share for FY28 on 12 May 2028, approved at the AGM on 10 August 2028, and pays an interim of ₹4 per share in November 2028. With 20 crore shares, set out which dividends hit RE and cash in FY28 and FY29, citing the Ind AS principle.
