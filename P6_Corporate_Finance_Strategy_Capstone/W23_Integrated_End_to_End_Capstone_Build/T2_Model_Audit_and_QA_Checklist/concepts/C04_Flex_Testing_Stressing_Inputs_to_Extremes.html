Key concept

Flex Testing: Stressing Inputs to Extremes

Concept: Flex test: change key inputs to extreme values (zero revenue, 100% capex) and confirm the model stays balanced

Statement

A flex test applies a set of extreme input values \(x_i^{\text{ext}}\), one at a time and then in combination, and requires that for each test

\[ \text{Master check}\left(x_i^{\text{ext}}\right) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Outputs move in the expected direction} \]

where the master check is the aggregate of all integrity checks (c01) and the expected direction is defined before the test (e.g. higher capex leads to lower FCFF in the year, higher PPE and higher later depreciation). Typical extremes are zero revenue, revenue growth of −50% or +100%, capex at 100% of revenue, a tax rate of 0% or 50%, a debt repayment greater than the outstanding balance, a WACC equal to g + 0.1%, and a commissioning delay to beyond the forecast horizon.

Explanation

Normal inputs exercise only the "comfortable" paths through the logic. Extreme inputs force the model through the edges: negative EBIT (does tax go negative, or is there a loss carry-forward?), negative cash (does the revolver draw, or does cash just go negative?), division by zero in margins and days, and MIN/MAX caps that never bind in the base case. A model that stays balanced and behaves sensibly at the extremes is structurally sound. A model that breaks reveals a hidden assumption.

What each classic test reveals:

Test What should happen What breaks in weak models
Zero revenue EBITDA = −fixed costs; tax nil (or DTA), cash falls, revolver draws; checks pass Days calculations #DIV/0!; tax goes negative as a cash refund; masked IFERROR hides failure
Capex = 100% of revenue FCFF deeply negative; PPE and depreciation rise; funding gap shows CFS uses capitalisations not capex; cash goes negative without funding; balance breaks
Revenue growth +100% WC investment spikes; capacity cap binds if modelled Utilisation above 100% not capped
Repayment > balance Debt floors at zero (MIN logic) Negative debt, i.e. the company "lends" to itself
Tax rate 0% NOPAT = EBIT Hard-coded 25.17% somewhere ignores the input
WACC → g TV explodes; input validation flag trips No guard: negative or infinite TV
All scenarios Checks pass in each Scenario-specific drivers not wired (s05)

Process:

  1. Save a copy, or use a "flex" scenario, so base inputs are not overwritten.
  2. Record expected outcomes before each test.
  3. Change one input, press F9, and record the master check and key outputs.
  4. Restore the input and confirm the outputs return exactly to base (a reversibility test).
  5. Log the results in a flex test table on the Checks sheet or in the QA log, and fix any failures.

Flex tests are a model-risk control in their own right. They are part of the QA checklist (week cross-cutting exercise) and an expected spreadsheet control under ICFR (topic related item).

Illustration

Flex test log extract (base value per share ₹612):

# Input change Expected Master check Observed Result
F1 FY28 revenue growth −100% (zero revenue) Loss, cash falls, revolver draws ERROR (BS −38.6 FY28) Tax row shows −₹38.6 crore (refund) booked as a reduction in current tax payable but not in the CFS Fail → fixed by MAX(0, …) with a loss carry-forward row
F2 Capex 100% of revenue FY28 FCFF negative, PPE up OK Cash −₹1,950 crore (no revolver) Fail (logic) → add minimum cash and revolver
F3 Tax rate 0% NOPAT = EBIT OK NOPAT < EBIT on DCF Fail → DCF had hard-coded 0.2517
F4 Restore all inputs Value per share ₹612 OK ₹612 Pass

Each failure exposed a different weakness: an unguarded tax formula, a missing funding mechanism and a hard-code.

Connections

Flex tests use the master check (c01) and the integrity checks (s02, c03) as the pass criteria, and they find the error classes of s03 (hard-codes, masked IFERROR). They extend the scenario testing of s05 to the extremes. In the Week 23 t1 capstone (s07, block D2-5) they are part of the QA pass before freezing. The revolver and minimum cash logic they often reveal as missing is covered in Week 15.

Practice Questions

Q1. Design a flex test set [L2]

For a capstone with a revenue build (volume × realisation), cost build, PPE, WC, leases, debt (two term loans, no revolver), tax and DCF, design ten flex tests with the expected result for each and the check that should detect failure.

💡 Hint: Aim each test at a specific formula edge (division, floor, cap, sign).

Q2. Interpreting a failure [L2]

When you set FY29 volume to zero, the balance check stays at 0, but receivable days on the WC sheet show #DIV/0! and the Cover still says "OK". Explain what this reveals about the model's check design and how to fix both problems.

Q3. Reversibility [L1]

Explain why restoring inputs after a flex test might not return the model exactly to base values, giving two possible causes, and how you would detect them.
