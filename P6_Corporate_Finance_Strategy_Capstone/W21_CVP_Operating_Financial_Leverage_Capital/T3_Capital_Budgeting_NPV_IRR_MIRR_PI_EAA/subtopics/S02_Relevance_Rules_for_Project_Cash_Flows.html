Subtopic note

Relevance Rules for Project Cash Flows

Scope: Relevance rules: exclude sunk costs and allocated overheads; include opportunity costs, cannibalisation and after-tax salvage

Why it matters

Capex proposals reaching an Indian board often come with cash flows taken straight from the business plan: they include feasibility study fees already spent, a share of head-office overheads, the land "free" because the company already owns it, and no recognition that the new product will eat into existing sales. Each of these errors pushes NPV in a known direction. The relevance rules are the filter an FP&A reviewer applies before any discounting. They are also standard CMA, CFA and interview material, often tested through case questions with deliberately planted irrelevant data.

Core notes

The test

A cash flow is relevant if and only if it is future, incremental (it differs between accept and reject) and cash (not an accounting allocation). Apply the test to each line.

Exclude: sunk costs

Costs already incurred, or committed irrevocably, cannot be changed by the decision. Examples: a ₹2 crore market survey done last year, R&D spent on a prototype, a non-refundable advance to an equipment supplier (if the advance is lost whether or not you proceed). Including them understates NPV and can kill good projects. "We have spent so much, we must continue" (the sunk-cost fallacy) can wrongly keep bad ones alive.

A cost is not sunk if it can still be recovered. A refundable deposit, or an asset already bought that could be sold, is relevant through its opportunity cost.

Exclude: allocated overheads

Head-office costs (corporate finance, legal, CEO office, group IT) allocated to the project by a revenue or headcount key are not incremental unless they actually increase because of the project. Include only the incremental overhead: a new plant HR team, extra IT licences, additional insurance. The same logic applies to allocated depreciation of shared assets.

Exclude: financing flows

Interest, loan drawdowns and repayments, and dividends are excluded from FCFF because the discount rate captures the cost of capital. (Use FCFE with Ke if you want an equity view, and then include them consistently.)

Include: opportunity costs

The value of the best alternative use of a resource the project uses:

  • Land already owned: its market value (or lease rental foregone), net of tax that would have arisen on its sale. The book value is irrelevant.
  • Spare capacity: zero opportunity cost if it truly has no alternative use; otherwise the contribution forgone.
  • Managerial time: generally excluded unless the project needs extra hires or displaces other profitable work.
  • Existing equipment transferred: its realisable value (after tax) or the cost of replacing it for the other user.

Include: cannibalisation and side effects

If the new product takes sales from existing products, the lost contribution of the existing products is an incremental cost. Erosion should be measured against the realistic counterfactual. If competitors would take those sales anyway, the true erosion is smaller. Positive side effects (spillover sales of consumables, stronger dealer network) are incremental benefits, but they need evidence.

Include: after-tax salvage and working capital

Terminal salvage after tax (c05) and working-capital release are relevant inflows. Also include the after-tax proceeds from selling any asset the project replaces, in Year 0.

Include: taxes

All flows are after tax. Use the project's incremental tax, including the tax shield on tax depreciation (s03) and the effect of the company's regime (s04).

Illustration

A Bengaluru FMCG company evaluates a new ready-to-cook line. The proposal lists the following.

Item Amount (₹ crore) Treatment Reason
Consumer research done last year 1.5 Exclude Sunk
Plant and machinery 40.0 Include, Year 0 Incremental capex
Company-owned land: book value ₹2 crore, market value ₹12 crore 2.0 Replace with ₹12 crore less tax on sale foregone Opportunity cost at market value
HO overhead allocation, 3% of revenue 3% Exclude Not incremental
New category team salaries 2.4 a year Include Incremental fixed cost
Lost contribution on existing instant-noodle range 3.0 a year Include Cannibalisation
Interest on ₹30 crore term loan 2.7 a year Exclude Financing; in WACC
Old machine sold at start (tax WDV ₹1.0 crore, sale ₹1.6 crore) 1.6 Include; block reduced (c05) After-tax proceeds
Salvage at Year 8 5.0 Include, after tax Terminal flow

On the land, if it would otherwise be sold for ₹12 crore, the relevant cost is ₹12 crore less the capital gains tax that would have arisen on that sale. Land is not a depreciable asset, so the gain is computed under Sec 45/48 with the indexation or rate rules applicable at the time. Under the post-July 2024 regime, long-term gains on land held over 24 months are generally taxed at 12.5% without indexation (with transitional options for individuals and HUFs on land acquired before 23 July 2024, not companies); confirm the current rule.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Committed but not yet paid costs (a binding contract with no exit) are sunk in substance. Cancellable orders with a penalty are relevant only to the extent of avoidable spend net of the penalty.
  • Excess capacity today, scarce later: if the project uses spare capacity now that would be needed for planned growth in Year 4, include the cost of advancing future capex.
  • Cannibalisation when markets are contested: if not launching means a competitor launches, erosion of your existing range happens anyway. Model the counterfactual explicitly.
  • Allocated costs that do become incremental: when many small projects each claim "no incremental overhead", the cumulative effect is new hires. Use a step-cost view at portfolio level.
  • Tax on opportunity cost: always value opportunity costs after tax, consistently with project flows.

Key takeaways

  • Relevant flows are future, incremental and cash; apply this test line by line.
  • Exclude sunk costs, allocated (non-incremental) overheads and financing flows.
  • Include opportunity costs at market value after tax, cannibalisation against a realistic counterfactual, and side effects with evidence.
  • Include after-tax salvage, proceeds from replaced assets, WC release and the project's tax effects.
  • A clean relevance review changes NPV more often than refinements of the discount rate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the items [L1]

For a new ₹80 crore warehouse automation project, classify each item as relevant or irrelevant and state why: (a) ₹60 lakh consultancy fee paid last quarter; (b) ₹2.5 crore annual saving in contract labour; (c) ₹1.2 crore of corporate IT cost allocated annually; (d) ₹40 lakh a year for a new automation maintenance contract; (e) the existing conveyor system, sold for ₹3 crore at Year 0; (f) interest on the ₹50 crore loan to fund the project.

Q2. Land opportunity cost [L2]

A company plans to build a plant on land it bought in 2012 for ₹3 crore. The land's current market value is ₹25 crore, and a developer has offered ₹24 crore. Explain how you would include the land in the project cash flows, the tax considerations, and why using ₹3 crore is wrong.

💡 Hint: Use the after-tax proceeds foregone, not the historical cost.

Q3. Cannibalisation [L2]

A two-wheeler maker's new 125cc model is forecast to sell 1.2 lakh units a year at a CM of ₹11,000. Market research suggests 40% of buyers would otherwise have bought the company's 110cc model (CM ₹8,500), but half of those would have switched to a competitor's 125cc model anyway if the company had not launched. Compute the annual incremental contribution.

Q4. Board review [L3]

You are reviewing a capex note that shows an IRR of 21% against a hurdle of 13%. The cash flows include allocated HO costs of 2% of revenue, exclude the market value of a surplus building to be used, deduct interest, and omit WC release. Explain the direction of each error's effect on NPV and IRR, and how you would present a corrected analysis.
