Subtopic note

Incremental Project Cash Flows: Capex to Terminal Value

Scope: Incremental cash flows: initial capex, WC investment, operating cash flows, terminal value and WC release

Why it matters

Every capital budgeting decision, whether a greenfield cement plant, a new hospital wing, a capacity debottleneck or an IT platform, is evaluated on incremental cash flows: the difference between the company's cash flows with the project and without it. NPV, IRR and every other metric in this topic are only as good as the cash-flow schedule beneath them. Lenders' TEV studies, board capex approvals and the Week 21 exercise all follow the same structure: initial investment (capex plus pre-operative costs), working-capital investment, operating cash flows after tax, and terminal flows (salvage or terminal value plus working-capital release).

Core notes

The incremental principle

\[ \text{Incremental CF}_t = \text{CF}_t^{\text{with project}} - \text{CF}_t^{\text{without project}} \]

Only cash flows that change because of the decision are relevant. The relevance rules (s02) decide what is in and out. The cash flows are unlevered (free cash flow to the firm, FCFF): no interest, no loan drawdowns or repayments, because financing is reflected in the discount rate (WACC). Including interest and discounting at WACC double-counts (topic pitfall 1).

Component 1: initial capital expenditure

  • Land, buildings, plant and machinery, installation, freight, import duties (net of any GST input credit, which is recoverable and so not a cost, but a timing outflow if credit is delayed).
  • Pre-operative expenses, trial-run costs and engineering fees capitalised under Ind AS 16. For the cash-flow model, what matters is the cash outflow and its timing, not the capitalisation.
  • Construction spread: for a 24-month build, phase capex by quarter or year (for example 30% / 50% / 20%). In discounting, use mid-period timing or XNPV with dates.
  • Contingency (typically 5–10% of hard costs) and expected overruns.
  • Capital subsidies and incentives (state industrial policy, PLI) are separate inflows in the period received (topic related item).

Component 2: working-capital investment

Net working capital (receivables + inventory − payables) built up as revenue ramps:

\[ \text{NWC}_t = w \times \text{Revenue}_t, \qquad \Delta\text{NWC}_t = \text{NWC}_t - \text{NWC}_{t-1} \]

where \(w\) is NWC as a percentage of revenue (or computed from days: receivable days, inventory days, payable days). An increase in NWC is a cash outflow; a decrease is an inflow. Initial inventory build (raw material, spares) often occurs before commercial operations start.

Component 3: operating cash flows

\[ \text{OCF}_t = (\text{Revenue}_t - \text{Cash costs}_t)(1 - t) + t \times \text{Tax depreciation}_t \]

or equivalently EBIT\((1 - t)\) + depreciation, provided EBIT uses tax depreciation (c06). Tax depreciation follows the Income-tax Act WDV block method, not Schedule II book depreciation (s03; topic pitfall 2). Add maintenance capex as an outflow in each operating year.

Component 4: terminal flows

  • Salvage value of assets at the end of the project, after tax (c05): under the block method, the sale reduces the block, and tax arises only if the block becomes negative or ceases (Sec 50).
  • Working-capital release: the cumulative NWC is recovered at the end of the project, an inflow (topic pitfall 3). Some analysts recover it with a haircut for obsolete stock and bad debts.
  • Terminal value for long-lived assets or going-concern projects: a perpetuity on the final-year FCFF, \(\text{TV}_n = \text{FCFF}_{n+1} \div (r - g)\), or an exit multiple. Use it only when the asset genuinely continues beyond the explicit horizon.
  • Decommissioning and site-restoration costs (mines, power plants) are terminal outflows.

Timing conventions

  • Year-end convention: all flows at year-end. Simple, slightly conservative.
  • Mid-year convention: operating flows at mid-year (\(t - 0.5\)), closer to reality for flows spread through the year.
  • Dated flows: XNPV / XIRR with actual dates for construction-phase flows.

Excel layout

Build in rows: (1) timeline flags (construction, operation, terminal), (2) capex, (3) revenue and costs, (4) tax depreciation from the block schedule, (5) tax, (6) ΔNWC, (7) salvage and WC release, (8) FCFF, (9) discount factors, (10) PV. Use flags rather than hard-coded year references:

=IF(Year=Final_Year, NWC_prior, 0)                 ' WC release
=-(NWC_t - NWC_prior)                               ' ΔNWC cash flow
=EBITDA*(1-Tax) + Tax*Tax_Dep - Capex - dNWC        ' FCFF

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Project vs company tax: if the company has other taxable profits, early-year project losses reduce group tax immediately and the tax shield is realised. A standalone SPV must carry losses forward (Sec 72, eight years; unabsorbed depreciation indefinitely, Sec 32(2)). Model accordingly.
  • GST input credit timing: GST on capex is recoverable but may be stuck as accumulated credit until output liabilities arise. That is a WC-like outflow and later inflow.
  • Inflation: keep revenue, costs and salvage consistently nominal or real (s08).
  • Terminal value dominance: if TV exceeds 60–70% of NPV, the decision rests on the perpetuity assumption, so test it.
  • Replacement projects: incremental flows include savings in cash costs, the sale of the old asset (after tax) and avoided maintenance capex.

Key takeaways

  • Evaluate only incremental, unlevered (FCFF) cash flows; financing belongs in the discount rate.
  • Separate capex, ΔNWC, operating cash flow, tax shield and terminal flows on a dedicated schedule.
  • NWC builds with revenue and must be released at the end of the project.
  • Use tax WDV depreciation for the tax shield and after-tax salvage under the block method.
  • Choose and document a timing convention; use XNPV for irregular construction flows.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build an FCFF schedule [L2]

A Pune company considers a ₹60 crore packaging line (Year 0). Revenue: ₹50 crore in Year 1, ₹70 crore in Years 2–5. Cash costs: 65% of revenue. NWC: 15% of revenue, built at the start of each year's revenue change, released at the end of Year 5. Tax depreciation (assume ₹9 crore, ₹7.65 crore, ₹6.50 crore, ₹5.53 crore, ₹4.70 crore). Tax rate 25.168%. Salvage ₹12 crore at the end of Year 5 (assume fully taxable for simplicity). Prepare the FCFF schedule year by year.

💡 Hint: Keep capex, OCF, ΔNWC and terminal flows on separate rows before summing.

Q2. Identify the flows [L1]

For a new 100-bed hospital wing, list every category of incremental cash flow you would model from Year 0 to the end of Year 10, and state where each comes from (capex budget, operating plan, WC assumptions, tax computation).

Q3. Terminal value or salvage? [L2]

A port terminal has a 30-year concession, but your model runs for 15 years. Explain whether you would use a salvage value or a terminal value at Year 15, how you would compute it, and the risks of each approach.

Q4. Construction-phase timing [L2]

A ₹1,200 crore plant has capex of ₹300 crore on 1 July 2026, ₹600 crore on 1 January 2027 and ₹300 crore on 1 September 2027. Commercial operations start on 1 April 2028. Explain how you would model these flows in Excel to get an accurate NPV, and write the formula you would use.

Q5. Interview question [L1]

"Why don't we deduct interest in project cash flows?" Answer in three sentences.
