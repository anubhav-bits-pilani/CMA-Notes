Subtopic note

Indian Tax Depreciation Shield: WDV Blocks, Half-Rate, Additional

Scope: Indian tax depreciation shield: WDV block-of-assets (plant & machinery 15%), half-rate if used under 180 days, additional depreciation

Why it matters

The depreciation tax shield is often the second-largest value driver in an Indian project model after operating margin. It must follow the Income-tax Act, not Ind AS or Schedule II book depreciation (topic pitfall 2). The Act uses a written-down-value (WDV) method on blocks of assets, a half-rate rule for assets put to use for less than 180 days in the year of acquisition, and (outside the concessional regimes) additional depreciation for new plant and machinery in manufacturing. These rules change both the size and the timing of the shield, and so the NPV.

Core notes

Legal framework (Income-tax Act 1961, as applicable up to tax year 2025-26)

  • Sec 32(1)(ii): depreciation on tangible assets (buildings, machinery, plant, furniture) and specified intangibles (know-how, patents, copyrights, trademarks, licences, franchises acquired after 1 April 1998) at prescribed rates on the WDV of the block of assets. Goodwill is excluded from depreciation from AY 2021-22.
  • Sec 2(11): a block of assets is a group of assets in the same class with the same rate of depreciation.
  • Sec 43(6): WDV of the block \(=\) opening WDV \(+\) actual cost of additions \(-\) moneys payable on assets sold, discarded, demolished or destroyed (but not below zero).
  • Rates (Rule 5 / Appendix I, as amended): plant and machinery (general) 15%; buildings (non-residential) 10%; furniture 10%; computers and software 40%; intangibles 25%; certain energy-saving and renewable devices 40% (after the 2017 cap). Rates are capped at 40% from AY 2018-19.
  • Second proviso to Sec 32(1): if an asset is acquired and put to use for less than 180 days in the previous year, depreciation for that year is at half the normal rate. The balance is not lost; it stays in the WDV and is depreciated in later years.
  • Sec 32(1)(iia): additional depreciation of 20% of the actual cost of new plant or machinery (not second-hand, office equipment, vehicles, or machinery installed in offices or residential accommodation) acquired and installed by an assessee engaged in manufacture or production, or in generation, transmission or distribution of power. A higher 35% rate applied to new P&M installed in notified backward areas of Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Telangana and West Bengal only for acquisitions between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2020, so it is not available to a new project today. If the asset is put to use for less than 180 days, half is allowed in the year and the balance in the next year (proviso inserted from AY 2016-17).
  • Additional depreciation is not available to companies opting for Sec 115BAA or 115BAB (s04).
  • The Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 (tax year 2026-27), with renumbered sections. The depreciation concepts carry over; confirm the new section numbers and any rate changes before citing them.

Computation mechanics

For a single new block of plant and machinery at 15%:

\[ D_1 = C \times 15\% \times h, \qquad \text{WDV}_1 = C - D_1, \qquad D_t = \text{WDV}_{t-1} \times 15\% \quad (t \geq 2) \]

where \(C\) is actual cost and \(h = 0.5\) if the asset was put to use for less than 180 days in Year 1, else 1. Additional depreciation (if eligible) is \(A_1 = C \times 20\% \times h\) in Year 1, plus \(C \times 20\% \times (1 - h)\) in Year 2. It also reduces WDV, because it is part of depreciation allowed.

The tax shield each year is \(t \times D_t\).

Illustration

Plant and machinery costing ₹100 crore is put to use on 15 November 2026 (less than 180 days before 31 March 2027).

Case A front-loads a larger shield at a higher rate, but the company pays a higher rate on all its profits. The regime choice is a whole-company decision (s04).

Compared with straight-line book depreciation (say ₹100 crore over 15 years under Schedule II, about ₹6.3 crore a year), the tax WDV schedule gives faster early deductions. Using book depreciation understates early shields and NPV.

Practical WDV issues in project models

  • Block, not asset: once in the block, the asset loses its individual identity. Sale proceeds reduce the block; there is no gain or loss on individual assets unless the block becomes negative or ceases (Sec 50; c05).
  • Infinite tail: WDV never reaches zero while the block exists. At the end of the project's explicit horizon, either (a) assume the block continues in the company and include the PV of the remaining tax shields, or (b) model the sale at salvage and apply Sec 50 if the block ceases. Ignoring the tail understates NPV.
  • Modelling with a single new block: if the company has an existing P&M block, the project's additions merge with it. For incremental analysis, compute the shield on the incremental cost only; the block mechanics give the same incremental depreciation each year.
  • Capitalised interest during construction (Sec 36(1)(iii) proviso and Sec 43(1) Explanation 8) forms part of actual cost for tax depreciation, even though interest is excluded from FCFF.
  • Capital subsidies: subsidies reduce actual cost for tax purposes (Explanation 10 to Sec 43(1)), reducing the shield. Model the subsidy and the reduced shield together.

Excel implementation

=IF(Year=1, Cost*Rate*IF(DaysUsed<180,0.5,1), Opening_WDV*Rate)           ' normal dep
=IF(Eligible_AddDep, IF(Year=1, Cost*0.2*IF(DaysUsed<180,0.5,1),
     IF(Year=2, Cost*0.2*IF(DaysUsed<180,0.5,0), 0)), 0)                    ' additional dep
=Opening_WDV - Normal_Dep - Add_Dep                                         ' closing WDV
=Tax_Rate * (Normal_Dep + Add_Dep)                                          ' tax shield

Set Eligible_AddDep to FALSE automatically when the regime switch is set to 115BAA.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Put to use vs acquired: depreciation starts when the asset is put to use, not when it is bought. Trial runs may or may not count as use; document the basis.
  • Rate disputes: classification (for example, moulds, pollution-control equipment, solar plant) affects the rate. Use the rate supportable before the tax authority.
  • Losses: in a loss-making SPV, the shield is deferred via unabsorbed depreciation, which can be carried forward indefinitely (Sec 32(2)). Its PV is lower.
  • Deferred tax: Ind AS 12 recognises the difference between book and tax WDV. This is an accounting effect only; the cash-flow model uses the tax computation.
  • Income-tax Act 2025: confirm section mapping and any change to rates or additional depreciation before relying on them.

Key takeaways

  • Tax depreciation follows Sec 32 WDV on blocks of assets; P&M is generally 15%.
  • Assets put to use for less than 180 days in the year of acquisition get half the rate in that year.
  • Additional depreciation of 20% on new P&M in manufacturing or power (the 35% backward-area rate has lapsed) is unavailable under 115BAA/115BAB.
  • The shield \(t \times D_t\) must use tax depreciation, not Schedule II book depreciation.
  • Model the WDV tail or the Sec 50 position at the end of the horizon, and adjust cost for capital subsidies and capitalised interest.

Practice Questions

Q1. WDV schedule with half-rate [L1]

Plant costing ₹50 crore is put to use on 10 December of the tax year. The company is under Sec 115BAA. Prepare the tax depreciation schedule and tax shield for four years at 15%.

Q2. Additional depreciation [L2]

Under the old regime (34.944%), a manufacturer buys new machinery for ₹80 crore and puts it to use on 1 June. Compute normal and additional depreciation for the first two years, the closing WDV after Year 2, and the PV of the shield for Years 1–2 at 12%.

💡 Hint: Use the 180-day test to decide whether half-rates apply in Year 1.

Q3. Book vs tax depreciation [L2]

A project model uses Schedule II straight-line depreciation of ₹8 crore a year for 15 years on ₹120 crore of plant, and computes the tax shield at 25.168%. Compute the PV of the shield at 11% for the first five years under the model and under the correct WDV method at 15% (full rate in Year 1), and comment on the difference.

Q4. Capital subsidy and cost [L2]

A state grants a 20% capital subsidy on ₹150 crore of plant and machinery, received in Year 2. Explain how the subsidy affects tax depreciation under Explanation 10 to Sec 43(1), and how you would model the cash flow and the change in the shield.

Q5. Infinite tail [L3]

Your project model runs 10 years and ends with plant having a tax WDV of ₹23 crore. The company will continue to own other plant in the same block. Explain two ways to treat the remaining WDV in the project NPV, and compute the PV of a perpetual 15% WDV shield on ₹23 crore at 25.168% tax and 12% discount rate as at the end of Year 10.
