Tax Regime Choice: Sec 115BAA vs Old Regime in Project Models

Scope: Tax regime choice: Sec 115BAA (22% + surcharge + cess = 25.168%, no additional depreciation) vs old regime; Income-tax Act 2025 renumbering

Why it matters

The tax rate drives three things in a project model: the after-tax operating cash flow, the value of the depreciation tax shield, and the after-tax salvage. In India, a domestic company chooses between the concessional Sec 115BAA regime and the old regime with its exemptions and incentives. The choice is made at company level, not project by project, but a large project can change which regime is optimal (because of the additional depreciation it would generate under the old regime). The regime also determines whether MAT applies. A model that hard-codes 30% or 25% without checking the company's actual position will mis-state NPV. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, so section references in models and board notes need updating.

Core notes

Sec 115BAA (introduced by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act 2019, from AY 2020-21)

  • Rate: 22% base, a flat 10% surcharge regardless of income, and 4% health and education cess:

\[ t = 22\% \times 1.10 \times 1.04 = 25.168\% \]

  • MAT does not apply (Sec 115JB is excluded), and any MAT credit brought forward is lost on switching.
  • Deductions forgone, including: Sec 10AA (SEZ units), Sec 32(1)(iia) additional depreciation, Sec 32AD (backward-area investment allowance), Sec 33AB and 33ABA, scientific research deductions under Sec 35 (various sub-sections, including 35(2AB)), Sec 35AD, 35CCC, 35CCD, and Chapter VI-A deductions other than Sec 80JJAA (new employment) and Sec 80M (inter-corporate dividends).
  • Losses: brought-forward losses and unabsorbed depreciation attributable to the forgone deductions cannot be set off.
  • Irrevocable: once exercised (Form 10-IC filed by the return due date), the option applies to all subsequent years and cannot be withdrawn.
  • Sec 115BAB (15% base, 17.16% effective, for new manufacturing companies) required manufacturing to start by 31 March 2024 and has not been extended, so it is not available for new companies today.

Old regime

  • Base rate: 30%, or 25% if total turnover or gross receipts in the specified earlier year did not exceed ₹400 crore.
  • Surcharge: 7% where total income exceeds ₹1 crore up to ₹10 crore; 12% above ₹10 crore.
  • Cess: 4%.
  • Effective rate for a large company: \(30\% \times 1.12 \times 1.04 = 34.944\%\). For a company at the 25% base with income above ₹10 crore: 29.12%.
  • MAT (Sec 115JB): 15% of book profit plus surcharge and cess (17.472% at 12% surcharge). MAT paid is creditable against normal tax for 15 years.
  • All incentives and deductions remain available, including additional depreciation.

When does the old regime win?

Ignoring MAT, compare tax on taxable income \(Y\) under 115BAA (no special deductions) with tax under the old regime on \(Y - D\), where \(D\) is the deductions that would be forgone:

\[ 0.34944 \times (Y - D) < 0.25168 \times Y \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad D > 0.2798 \times Y \]

So the old regime is better only if the forgone deductions exceed about 28% of taxable income each year, for a large company. That happens with large SEZ profits (Sec 10AA), heavy R&D deductions, or a very large new manufacturing capex relative to profit (additional depreciation of 20% of cost). MAT then sets a floor: under the old regime, tax is at least 17.472% of book profit, which limits the benefit of large deductions. Companies with large MAT credit balances often stay in the old regime until the credit is used, then switch.

Illustration

A Gujarat chemicals company has taxable income (after normal depreciation) of ₹150 crore a year. It plans ₹200 crore of new plant, put to use early in Year 1 (more than 180 days), eligible for additional depreciation of ₹40 crore under the old regime.

₹ crore Year 1, old regime Year 1, 115BAA Year 2 onwards, old regime Year 2 onwards, 115BAA
Taxable income before additional depreciation 150.0 150.0 150.0 150.0
Additional depreciation 40.0 – – –
Taxable income 110.0 150.0 150.0 150.0
Tax rate 34.944% 25.168% 34.944% 25.168%
Tax 38.4 37.8 52.4 37.8

(Ignoring MAT, which would not bind here.) Even in Year 1, with the large additional depreciation, 115BAA gives lower tax, and from Year 2 the gap is ₹14.7 crore a year. The test: ₹40 crore is 26.7% of ₹150 crore, below the 28% threshold. The additional depreciation also reduces WDV and so future normal depreciation, a further small effect under the old regime.

Project model implications

  1. Rate input with a regime switch: =IF(Regime="115BAA", 25.168%, 34.944%), with a separate MAT check if the old regime applies.
  2. Additional depreciation flag tied to the regime switch (s03).
  3. Incremental vs company view: the project's tax effect is the change in company tax. For a profitable company, use the marginal rate. For an SPV with losses, model the carry-forward.
  4. MAT credit: under the old regime, a project that creates large deductions can push the company into MAT; model the MAT credit utilisation in later years.
  5. Sensitivity: show NPV under both regimes when the project is large enough to influence the company's choice.

Income-tax Act 2025 renumbering

The Income-tax Act 2025 received assent in 2025 and applies from 1 April 2026. It replaces "previous year" and "assessment year" with a single "tax year", reorganises and renumbers sections, and is broadly intended to be policy-neutral in its first version. The concessional company regime (current Sec 115BAA), depreciation (current Sec 32), capital gains on depreciable assets (current Sec 50) and MAT provisions all continue under new section numbers. In models and memos dated after 1 April 2026, cite the new section with the old section in brackets, and verify the mapping against the official correlation table and the latest Finance Act. Do not assume rates are unchanged without checking.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • SEZ units with remaining Sec 10AA holiday years usually defer switching. Model the switch year.
  • Group restructuring: an SPV set up for a project has its own regime choice. A new SPV can opt for 115BAA from the start, while the parent stays in the old regime, or vice versa.
  • Turnover-based 25% rate: medium companies in the old regime may be at 25% plus surcharge and cess, so the 115BAA advantage is smaller.
  • Surcharge thresholds: at income below ₹1 crore, there is no surcharge under the old regime, but 115BAA's surcharge is a flat 10% regardless of income.
  • Deferred tax: switching triggers remeasurement of deferred tax balances under Ind AS 12 at the new rate. That is an accounting effect, not a cash flow.

Key takeaways

  • Sec 115BAA: 25.168% effective, no MAT, no additional depreciation or specified deductions, irrevocable once chosen.
  • Old regime for large companies: 34.944%, with MAT at 17.472% of book profit and all incentives.
  • The old regime wins only when forgone deductions exceed about 28% of taxable income (large company), subject to MAT.
  • Tie the tax rate, additional depreciation and MAT logic in the model to one regime switch.
  • From 1 April 2026, cite the Income-tax Act 2025 section numbers alongside the 1961 Act references and verify both.

Practice Questions

Q1. Effective rates [L1]

Compute the effective tax rate for (a) a company under Sec 115BAA with income of ₹5 crore, (b) an old-regime company at the 30% base with income of ₹50 crore, (c) an old-regime company at the 25% base with income of ₹8 crore, and (d) the MAT rate for an old-regime company with book profit of ₹50 crore.

Q2. Regime choice with additional depreciation [L2]

A company has taxable income of ₹80 crore a year before a new capex of ₹150 crore of plant (put to use in Year 1 for more than 180 days). Compare total tax for Years 1 and 2 under the old regime (34.944%, ignoring MAT) and Sec 115BAA, taking into account additional depreciation and the change in normal depreciation at 15% in Year 2.

💡 Hint: Additional depreciation also lowers the WDV on which Year 2 normal depreciation is computed.

Q3. MAT floor [L2]

An old-regime company has book profit of ₹100 crore and, after SEZ and additional depreciation deductions, taxable income of ₹30 crore. Compute normal tax and MAT, the tax payable, and the MAT credit created. Explain how this affects the decision to switch to 115BAA.

Q4. Model design [L2]

Describe how you would build a regime switch in a project model so that the tax rate, additional depreciation, MAT and loss set-off rules change together, and list the outputs you would show to compare the two regimes.

Q5. Board memo with renumbering [L3]

Draft the tax assumptions section of a board memo dated June 2026 for a ₹500 crore greenfield project, citing the relevant provisions under the Income-tax Act 2025 and the corresponding 1961 Act sections, stating the regime assumed, and listing what should be confirmed with tax advisers.
