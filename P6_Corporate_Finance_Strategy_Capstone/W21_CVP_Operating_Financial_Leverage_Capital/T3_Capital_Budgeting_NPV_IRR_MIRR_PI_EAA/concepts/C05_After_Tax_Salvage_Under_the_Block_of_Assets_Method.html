Key concept

After-Tax Salvage Under the Block-of-Assets Method

Concept: After-tax salvage = Salvage − t × (Salvage − Tax WDV); under block-of-assets, the sale reduces the block and capital gain arises only if the block becomes nil/ceases

Statement

Standalone asset (or block ceases):

\[ \text{After-tax salvage} = S - t \times (S - \text{WDV}_{\text{tax}}) \]

where \(S\) is the sale proceeds at the end of the project, \(\text{WDV}_{\text{tax}}\) the tax written-down value of the asset (or of the block when it ceases), and \(t\) the tax rate applicable to the gain. If \(S < \text{WDV}\), the formula gives a tax saving on the loss.

Indian block-of-assets rule (Sec 43(6), Sec 50 of the Income-tax Act 1961):

  • If other assets remain in the block and the block WDV stays positive after deducting \(S\): no immediate tax. The block is reduced by \(S\), which lowers future depreciation.
  • If \(S\) exceeds the block's WDV (plus additions in the year): the excess is a deemed short-term capital gain (Sec 50(1)), and the block becomes nil.
  • If the block ceases (all assets in it are transferred) and \(S\) is less than the WDV: the shortfall is a short-term capital loss (Sec 50(2)).

Explanation

Textbook capital budgeting treats each asset separately, so the gain over book (tax) value is taxed at the end of the project. Indian tax law pools assets of the same rate in a block. The individual asset loses its identity, so a sale only reduces the pool. The "tax on salvage" is then not a lump sum at sale but a stream of foregone future depreciation shields. Its present value, for a WDV rate \(d\) and a perpetual block, is:

\[ \text{PV of lost shields} = \frac{t \times d \times B}{r + d} \]

where \(B\) is the amount by which the block is reduced relative to the no-sale case, and \(r\) the discount rate.

For the incremental project analysis, the project added its cost to the block and depreciated it. At the end, its remaining contribution to the block is \(\text{WDV}_{\text{project}} - S\). If positive, the company continues to claim depreciation on it (a terminal tax-shield tail); if negative, it reduces future shields on the company's other assets. In formula:

\[ \text{Terminal value of salvage (continuing block)} = S + \frac{t \times d \times (\text{WDV}_{\text{project}} - S)}{r + d} \]

Points of care:

  • Character of gain: Sec 50 gains are short-term regardless of holding period and are taxed at the normal rate (25.168% under 115BAA for a domestic company). A short-term capital loss can be set off only against capital gains, not against business income, so the tax saving on a loss may be deferred or lost.
  • Land is not depreciable; its sale is a separate capital gain (long-term after 24 months).
  • Income-tax Act 2025: the block and deemed-STCG concepts continue under renumbered sections from 1 April 2026; verify references.

Illustration

Plant cost ₹100 crore, tax depreciation 15% WDV (full rate in Year 1 for simplicity), project life 8 years, salvage ₹40 crore, tax rate 25.168%, discount rate 12%.

Tax WDV of the project's plant at the end of Year 8 \(= 100 \times 0.85^8 = \text{₹}27.25\) crore.

Case Computation After-tax value of salvage (₹ crore)
Block ceases (standalone formula) \(40 - 0.25168 \times (40 - 27.25)\) 36.79
Block continues (other P&M remains) \(40 + 0.25168 \times 0.15 \times (27.25 - 40) \div (0.12 + 0.15)\) 38.22

The block method defers the tax on the gain into reduced future depreciation, worth about ₹1.4 crore more in PV.

Practice Questions

Q1. Standalone after-tax salvage [L1]

A machine with a tax WDV of ₹6 crore is sold for ₹9 crore at the end of the project, and the block ceases. The tax rate is 25.168%. Compute the tax and the after-tax salvage. Repeat for a sale price of ₹4 crore and explain how the loss is treated.

Q2. Block continues [L2]

A company's P&M block has an opening WDV of ₹220 crore. At the end of a project, it sells project machinery for ₹35 crore; the project's share of the block WDV is ₹28 crore. Tax rate 25.168%, WDV rate 15%, discount rate 11%. Compute the PV effect of the sale on future tax shields and the effective after-tax salvage.

💡 Hint: The company keeps claiming depreciation on the remaining block; value the change in that perpetual stream.

Q3. Block becomes negative [L2]

A company's entire P&M block has a WDV of ₹12 crore. During the year it buys new P&M for ₹5 crore and sells old P&M for ₹22 crore. Compute the Sec 50 capital gain, the closing block and depreciation for the year.
