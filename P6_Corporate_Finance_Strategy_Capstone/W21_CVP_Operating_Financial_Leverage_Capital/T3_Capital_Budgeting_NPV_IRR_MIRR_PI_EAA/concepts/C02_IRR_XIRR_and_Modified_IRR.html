Key concept

IRR, XIRR and Modified IRR

Concept: IRR: rate where NPV = 0 (=IRR, =XIRR); MIRR = (FV of inflows at reinvestment rate ÷ PV of outflows at finance rate)^(1/n) − 1; =MIRR(values, finance_rate, reinvest_rate)

Statement

IRR is the rate \(k\) that solves:

\[ \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + k)^t} = 0 \]

MIRR (modified internal rate of return):

\[ \text{MIRR} = \left( \frac{\sum_{t} \text{CF}_t^{+} (1 + r_{\text{re}})^{n - t}}{\sum_{t} \dfrac{\lvert \text{CF}_t^{-} \rvert}{(1 + r_{\text{fin}})^{t}}} \right)^{1/n} - 1 \]

where \(\text{CF}_t^{+}\) are positive cash flows, \(\text{CF}_t^{-}\) negative cash flows (absolute values in the denominator), \(r_{\text{re}}\) the reinvestment rate, \(r_{\text{fin}}\) the finance rate, and \(n\) the number of periods. Accept an independent project if IRR (or MIRR) exceeds the hurdle rate.

Excel: =IRR(values, [guess]) for periodic flows; =XIRR(values, dates, [guess]) for dated flows (annual effective rate on a 365-day basis); =MIRR(values, finance_rate, reinvest_rate).

Explanation

IRR is the break-even discount rate: the project's NPV profile crosses zero there. It is intuitive and scale-free, so it is popular with promoters and lenders. Its weaknesses (s05):

  • It implicitly assumes interim cash flows are reinvested at the IRR itself, which is unrealistic for high-IRR projects.
  • It can rank mutually exclusive projects differently from NPV (scale and timing).
  • Non-conventional flows can produce multiple IRRs or none, and Excel returns only one root near the guess.

MIRR fixes the first and third problems. Inflows are compounded forward to the end of the project at an explicit reinvestment rate (usually WACC). Outflows are discounted to the present at the finance rate (usually WACC or the cost of debt). One positive root then always exists. MIRR is lower than IRR when IRR exceeds the reinvestment rate. It still does not fix scale conflicts, so NPV remains the decision rule for mutually exclusive projects.

Excel notes: MIRR treats the first value as time 0 and compounds/discounts by period; it needs at least one positive and one negative value. XIRR requires the first date to be the earliest. IRR may return #NUM! if it fails to converge; supply a guess or check that a root exists.

Illustration

A Bhubaneswar EPC company's project (₹ crore); WACC 12%:

Year 0 1 2 3 4
Cash flow −100 30 40 50 40
  • IRR \(\approx 20.5\%\) (Excel =IRR(B2:F2)).
  • NPV at 12% \(\approx \text{₹}19.7\) crore.
  • MIRR at 12% finance and reinvestment rates: FV of inflows at Year 4 \(= 30 \times 1.12^3 + 40 \times 1.12^2 + 50 \times 1.12 + 40 = 42.15 + 50.18 + 56.00 + 40.00 = \text{₹}188.32\) crore. PV of outflows \(= \text{₹}100\) crore. MIRR \(= (188.32 \div 100)^{1/4} - 1 \approx 17.1\%\).

MIRR (17.1%) is below IRR (20.5%) because interim inflows are assumed to earn 12%, not 20.5%. Both exceed the 12% hurdle, consistent with the positive NPV.

Connections

IRR is the NPV root (c01) and is the subject of the NPV–IRR conflicts in s05 (scale, timing, Fisher rate, multiple IRRs). MIRR is required on the Week 21 exercise output block. In project finance, lenders look at project IRR (FCFF) and sponsors at equity IRR (FCFE), which must be compared with Ke, not WACC (consistency principle in the week overview). In private equity and real estate, XIRR on dated flows is the standard reporting metric.

Practice Questions

Q1. IRR and MIRR [L1]

A project has flows (₹ crore) of −250, 60, 80, 100, 110. Using Excel, compute IRR and MIRR with a finance rate of 10% and a reinvestment rate of 12%. Explain why MIRR is lower.

Q2. MIRR by hand with an intermediate outflow [L2]

A plant has flows (₹ crore) of −150 in Year 0, +70 in Year 1, −40 in Year 2 (major overhaul), and +90, +100 in Years 3 and 4. Finance rate 9%, reinvestment rate 12%. Compute MIRR by hand and compare with Excel's MIRR.

💡 Hint: Discount all outflows, including the Year 2 overhaul, to Year 0 at the finance rate.

Q3. XIRR for a PE-style investment [L2]

An investor puts ₹50 crore into a project on 15 May 2026 and ₹20 crore on 1 February 2027, and receives ₹12 crore on 31 March 2028, ₹15 crore on 31 March 2029 and ₹95 crore on 30 September 2030. Write the XIRR formula and explain why IRR on annual buckets would mislead.
