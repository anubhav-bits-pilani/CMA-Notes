Key concept

Equivalent Annual Annuity for Unequal Lives

Concept: EAA = NPV ÷ annuity factor = NPV × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)^−n]; choose the higher EAA

Statement

\[ \text{EAA} = \frac{\text{NPV}}{\text{AF}_{r,n}} = \text{NPV} \times \frac{r}{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}} \]

where NPV is the single-cycle NPV of the project, \(r\) the discount rate, \(n\) the project's life in years, and \(\text{AF}_{r,n} = [1 - (1 + r)^{-n}] \div r\) the present value of ₹1 a year for \(n\) years. For mutually exclusive, repeatable projects with unequal lives, choose the project with the higher EAA. For cost-only alternatives, the equivalent annual cost \(\text{EAC} = \text{PV of costs} \div \text{AF}_{r,n}\); choose the lower EAC.

Explanation

EAA asks: what level annual amount, received every year over the project's life, has the same present value as the project's NPV? Converting to an annual figure puts projects of different lengths on a per-year basis. If each project is replaced indefinitely by an identical one, the PV of the infinite chain is EAA \(\div r\). Since \(r\) is common, ranking by EAA is equivalent to ranking infinite chains, and also equivalent to ranking replacement chains over the lowest common multiple of lives (s07).

Conditions:

  • Projects are mutually exclusive and repeatable on similar terms. If not repeatable, compare plain NPVs.
  • The same discount rate \(r\) is used for NPV and for the annuity factor, appropriate to each project's risk.
  • Real vs nominal: in a nominal model with inflation, identical repetition is unrealistic. Compute EAA in real terms with a real rate, or build explicit chains with inflated replacement costs.
  • EAA does not solve scale differences in capacity or output. If the alternatives deliver different output, compare cost per unit or include the output value.

Excel: =-PMT(r, n, NPV) returns EAA (PMT gives a negative payment for a positive PV, hence the minus sign).

Illustration

Revenue-generating projects. A Coimbatore textile mill compares two mutually exclusive spinning upgrades at 11%:

Upgrade P Upgrade Q
NPV (₹ crore) 14.0 20.0
Life (years) 5 9
Annuity factor at 11% 3.6959 5.5370
EAA (₹ crore) 3.79 3.61
Infinite-chain value, EAA ÷ 11% (₹ crore) 34.4 32.8

Q has the higher NPV, but P has the higher EAA, so P is preferred if the upgrade will be repeated.

Cost-only alternatives. Two compressors at 10%. A costs ₹12 crore with operating costs of ₹3 crore a year for 4 years; B costs ₹20 crore with operating costs of ₹2.4 crore a year for 7 years.

PV of costs: A \(= 12 + 3 \times 3.1699 = \text{₹}21.51\) crore; B \(= 20 + 2.4 \times 4.8684 = \text{₹}31.68\) crore. EAC: A \(= 21.51 \div 3.1699 = \text{₹}6.79\) crore; B \(= 31.68 \div 4.8684 = \text{₹}6.51\) crore. Choose B, despite its higher total PV of costs.

Connections

EAA is the method of s07 (unequal-life mutually exclusive projects) and is required in the week checklist ("reconcile NPV to EAA"). It reuses the annuity factor from the time-value refresher (Excel PMT and PV). It links to optimal replacement cycles for fleets and IT assets, and to lease-vs-buy comparisons where lease terms and asset lives differ. A listed pitfall of this topic is ranking such projects by IRR instead of NPV or EAA.

Practice Questions

Q1. EAA ranking [L1]

Project J has an NPV of ₹8 crore over 4 years and Project K an NPV of ₹13 crore over 8 years. At 12%, compute each EAA and state which you would choose if they are repeatable and mutually exclusive.

Q2. Reconcile NPV to EAA [L2]

Using the Coimbatore illustration in these notes, build a replacement chain over 45 years for both upgrades and show that the chain NPVs rank the projects in the same order as EAA. Explain why the chain NPV ratio equals the ratio of the infinite-chain values only approximately.

💡 Hint: A chain over 45 years is a finite version of the perpetuity EAA ÷ r.

Q3. EAC with salvage [L2]

A delivery van costs ₹14 lakh, with operating costs of ₹3 lakh a year. It can be sold for ₹7 lakh after 3 years or ₹3 lakh after 5 years (operating costs are ₹3.5 lakh in Years 4 and 5). At 10% and ignoring tax, compute the EAC for a 3-year and a 5-year replacement cycle and recommend one.
