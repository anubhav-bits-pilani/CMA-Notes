Key concept

Operating Cash Flow With Tax Shield and the Fisher Relation

Concept: Operating CF = (Revenue − Cash costs) × (1 − t) + t × Tax depreciation; Fisher: (1 + nominal) = (1 + real) × (1 + inflation)

Statement

Operating cash flow (tax-shield form):

\[ \text{OCF}_t = (R_t - C_t)(1 - t) + t \times D_t^{\text{tax}} \]

where \(R_t\) is revenue, \(C_t\) cash operating costs (excluding depreciation and interest), \(t\) the tax rate and \(D_t^{\text{tax}}\) tax depreciation under the Income-tax Act. Equivalent forms: \(\text{OCF} = \text{EBIT}(1 - t) + D^{\text{tax}}\), with EBIT computed using tax depreciation; or \(\text{OCF} = \text{EBITDA} - \text{tax on EBIT}\). FCFF \(=\) OCF \(-\) capex \(-\) ΔNWC.

Fisher relation:

\[ (1 + r_{\text{nom}}) = (1 + r_{\text{real}})(1 + i) \quad \Rightarrow \quad r_{\text{nom}} = r_{\text{real}} + i + r_{\text{real}} \times i \]

where \(r_{\text{nom}}\) is the nominal rate, \(r_{\text{real}}\) the real rate and \(i\) expected inflation.

Explanation

Tax-shield form. Depreciation is not a cash cost, but it reduces taxable income and therefore tax paid. Writing OCF as after-tax cash profit plus the tax saving on depreciation separates the operating economics from the value of the shield. The shield, \(t \times D^{\text{tax}}\), depends on tax depreciation (Sec 32 WDV), not on book depreciation (Schedule II), so the model needs a separate tax depreciation schedule (s03; topic pitfall 2). Interest is excluded because financing is captured in WACC. Including interest in OCF and discounting at WACC double-counts the tax benefit of debt, which is already in the after-tax Kd within WACC (topic pitfall 1).

If the company has losses and cannot use the shield immediately, the shield is deferred (unabsorbed depreciation) and the formula must use tax actually saved in each year.

Fisher relation. It converts between nominal and real rates so that cash flows and discount rates are on the same basis (s08). The approximation \(r_{\text{nom}} \approx r_{\text{real}} + i\) understates the nominal rate by \(r_{\text{real}} \times i\), which matters at Indian inflation rates over long horizons. In practice, Indian WACC is built from nominal government bond yields and nominal ERP, so the natural pairing is nominal cash flows. Tax depreciation shields are fixed in nominal rupees and must not be treated as real.

Illustration

A Hosur electronics plant, Year 3 (₹ crore): revenue 240, cash operating costs 180, tax depreciation 22 (WDV), book depreciation 15 (Schedule II), tax rate 25.168%.

Approach Computation OCF
Tax-shield form \((240 - 180) \times 0.74832 + 0.25168 \times 22\) 44.90 + 5.54 = 50.44
EBIT form (tax dep) \((240 - 180 - 22) \times 0.74832 + 22\) 28.44 + 22 = 50.44
Wrong: book depreciation \((240 - 180 - 15) \times 0.74832 + 15\) 33.67 + 15 = 48.67

Using book depreciation understates this year's OCF by ₹1.76 crore (the shield on the ₹7 crore difference). The difference reverses in later years, but the PV effect is real.

Fisher: real WACC 6.5%, inflation 4.5%. Nominal WACC \(= 1.065 \times 1.045 - 1 = 11.29\%\), against the approximation of 11.0%.

Connections

The OCF formula is the core of s01's operating cash flow line and uses s03 (tax depreciation) and s04 (tax rate under 115BAA or the old regime). The Fisher relation underpins s08 (inflation consistency) and the week's cross-cutting consistency principle (nominal with nominal, FCFF with WACC, FCFE with Ke). In Weeks 13–15, the same OCF logic appears in the 3-statement model's cash flow statement, but there book depreciation drives the P&L and the tax line comes from a separate tax computation.

Practice Questions

Q1. OCF two ways [L1]

Revenue is ₹150 crore, cash costs are ₹105 crore, tax depreciation is ₹12 crore and the tax rate is 25.168%. Compute OCF using the tax-shield form and the EBIT form, and show that they agree.

Q2. Fisher in a long project [L2]

A 15-year project model uses a nominal WACC of 12% but projects cash flows at constant FY26 prices. Inflation is expected at 5%. Compute the real rate implied by Fisher, and explain the direction and approximate size of the error in NPV for a project with level real cash flows of ₹50 crore a year after an outlay of ₹300 crore.

💡 Hint: Compare NPV of the real flows at the real rate with NPV of the same flows at the nominal rate.

Q3. Loss-making SPV [L2]

A new SPV has negative EBITDA in Year 1 and tax depreciation of ₹30 crore, with no other income. Explain how the tax shield should be modelled in Years 1–3 if the SPV turns profitable in Year 3, and how this differs if the project is housed inside a profitable parent.
