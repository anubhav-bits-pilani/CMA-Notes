Key concept

Discounted Payback Period

Concept: Discounted payback = years until cumulative discounted CF turns positive (ignores post-payback flows)

Statement

Discounted payback is the smallest \(T\) such that:

\[ \sum_{t=0}^{T} \frac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + r)^t} \geq 0 \]

With interpolation within the year in which the cumulative discounted cash flow turns positive:

\[ \text{DPB} = (T - 1) + \frac{\left(-\text{Cumulative discounted CF}_{T-1}\right)}{\text{Discounted CF}_T} \]

where \(\text{CF}_t\) is the project cash flow in year \(t\) (with \(\text{CF}_0\) the negative outlay), \(r\) the discount rate, and \(T\) the first year in which the cumulative discounted CF is non-negative. A project is accepted under this rule if DPB is no longer than a target period.

Explanation

Simple payback counts how long it takes to recover the outlay in undiscounted rupees. Discounted payback recovers the outlay plus the cost of capital on it, so it is always longer than simple payback (for positive rates and conventional flows). If a project reaches discounted payback within its life, its NPV is positive. The reverse also holds for conventional projects: positive NPV implies discounted payback is reached before the end of the project.

Why practitioners use it:

  • Liquidity and risk: Indian promoters and lenders care how quickly capital comes back, especially in sectors with policy risk (tariff revisions, export incentives, anti-dumping duties) or technology obsolescence.
  • Communication: "the plant pays back its capital, including a 12% return, in 4.4 years" is easy for a board to grasp.
  • Screening: a maximum DPB acts as a quick first filter.

Limitations:

  • Ignores cash flows after payback: a project with large later flows (long-life infrastructure, brand building) is penalised. Two projects with the same DPB can have very different NPVs.
  • Arbitrary cut-off: the target period has no link to value creation.
  • Non-conventional flows: with later negative flows (overhauls, closure costs), cumulative discounted CF can turn negative again after first turning positive. Report the final crossing and flag it.
  • Not a ranking tool for mutually exclusive projects.

In a model, report DPB next to NPV, IRR, MIRR and PI on the output block (week cross-cutting item), and compute it from the same discounted FCFF row as NPV.

Simple payback \(= 3 + 20 \div 60 = 3.33\) years. Discounted payback \(= 4 + 10.06 \div 28.37 = 4.35\) years. NPV \(= \text{₹}38.58\) crore, of which ₹20.27 crore (Year 6) is ignored by the payback measure.

Excel (Microsoft 365), with discounted flows in C2:I2 and years in C1:I1:

=SCAN(0, C2:I2, LAMBDA(a, x, a + x))                         ' cumulative discounted CF
=XMATCH(TRUE, Cum >= 0) - 1                                  ' year in which cumulative turns non-negative (T)
=LET(k, XMATCH(TRUE, Cum>=0), (k-2) + -INDEX(Cum, k-1)/INDEX(Disc, k))   ' interpolated DPB

In older Excel, use a helper row with =IF(AND(prev<0, curr>=0), year-1 - prev/disc_curr, "") and take the MIN.

Connections

Discounted payback uses the same discounting as NPV (c01) and complements it as a liquidity measure. It sits on the Week 21 exercise output block alongside NPV, IRR, MIRR, PI and peak funding requirement. Peak funding (the most negative point of the cumulative undiscounted CF, including construction-phase outflows and WC build-up) is a related liquidity measure that drives debt sizing (t5). In t4, payback-style thinking explains why some Indian groups prefer quick-return projects even when longer projects have higher NPV.

Practice Questions

Q1. Simple and discounted payback [L1]

A project has flows (₹ crore) of −80, 20, 25, 30, 30, 25 at a WACC of 11%. Compute simple payback, discounted payback and NPV.

Q2. Same payback, different value [L2]

Projects G and H both cost ₹100 crore and have discounted payback of 4.0 years at 12%. G's flows end in Year 5; H's flows continue at ₹30 crore a year for Years 5–12. Explain why ranking by payback would mislead, and estimate how much more NPV H generates after Year 4.

💡 Hint: Value H's post-payback flows as an annuity discounted back from Year 4.

Q3. Non-conventional flows [L2]

A mining project's cumulative discounted CF turns positive in Year 5, but a ₹60 crore closure cost in Year 10 (discounted value ₹19 crore) pushes it negative again from Year 10, against a cumulative discounted CF of ₹12 crore at the end of Year 9. Explain how you would report discounted payback and what the result says about NPV.
