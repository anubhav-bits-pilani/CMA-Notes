Key concept

Profitability Index: PV of Inflows per Rupee Invested

Concept: PI = PV of future cash inflows ÷ PV of initial outlay; accept if PI > 1

Statement

\[ \text{PI} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} \dfrac{\text{CF}_t}{(1 + r)^t}}{I_0} = 1 + \frac{\text{NPV}}{I_0} \]

where \(\text{CF}_t\) are the future (post-investment) cash flows, \(r\) the discount rate, and \(I_0\) the PV of the initial outlay (for multi-period construction, the PV of all investment outflows). Accept an independent project if PI \(> 1\), equivalent to NPV \(> 0\). Some texts define a "net PI" \(=\) NPV \(\div I_0\), with an acceptance threshold of 0.

Explanation

PI expresses value created per rupee of capital committed. For independent projects with no capital constraint, it always agrees with NPV on accept/reject, because PI \(> 1 \Leftrightarrow\) NPV \(> 0\). Its real use is under capital rationing (s06): when capital is the scarce resource, ranking divisible projects by PI maximises total NPV within the budget. It is the capital-budgeting analogue of contribution per unit of the limiting factor (t1 s06).

Limitations:

  • Scale: PI is a ratio, so for mutually exclusive projects without rationing it can favour a small, high-PI project over a larger one with more NPV.
  • Indivisible projects: PI ranking does not guarantee the best combination; search combinations or use integer programming.
  • Definition of outlay: whether later-year capex (staged investment, maintenance capex) goes in the numerator (netted from inflows) or the denominator changes PI, though not NPV. Define it consistently, and under rationing, the denominator should be the capital that is actually constrained.
  • Multi-period constraints: a single PI cannot rank projects when capital is constrained in more than one year.

Illustration

A Rajkot engineering group compares projects at a WACC of 12% (₹ crore):

Project PV of outlay PV of inflows NPV PI
Robotic welding 18 25.2 7.2 1.40
New casting shop 60 76.8 16.8 1.28
ERP upgrade 8 9.0 1.0 1.13
Showroom expansion 12 11.4 −0.6 0.95

All except the showroom are acceptable. If they are mutually exclusive and capital is unlimited, the casting shop (highest NPV) is best despite its lower PI. If capital is limited to ₹30 crore and projects are divisible, rank by PI: robotic welding (₹18 crore), then ₹12 crore of the casting shop (20%), giving NPV \(= 7.2 + 0.2 \times 16.8 = \text{₹}10.56\) crore.

Connections

PI is NPV normalised by investment (c01) and is the ranking tool of s06 (capital rationing). The week's output block requires PI alongside NPV, IRR, MIRR, discounted payback and peak funding. In t4, capital allocation across capex, buybacks and debt prepayment can be framed as value per rupee, using PI-like metrics with a common risk-adjusted basis. In public-sector project appraisal (benefit-cost ratio), the same ratio appears.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute PI [L1]

A project costs ₹45 crore and generates ₹12 crore a year for 6 years. At 11%, compute PV of inflows, NPV and PI, and state the decision.

Q2. Staged investment and PI definition [L2]

A project needs ₹30 crore in Year 0 and ₹20 crore in Year 2, and generates ₹15 crore a year in Years 3–8. At 12%, compute PI with (a) the Year 2 capex in the denominator and (b) the Year 2 capex netted against inflows. Explain which definition is appropriate if only Year 0 capital is rationed.

💡 Hint: NPV is identical in both cases; only the ratio changes.

Q3. PI vs NPV for mutually exclusive projects [L2]

Project M costs ₹10 crore with PV of inflows ₹15 crore; Project N costs ₹50 crore with PV of inflows ₹65 crore. They are mutually exclusive and capital is not rationed. Which should be chosen, and why does PI mislead here?
