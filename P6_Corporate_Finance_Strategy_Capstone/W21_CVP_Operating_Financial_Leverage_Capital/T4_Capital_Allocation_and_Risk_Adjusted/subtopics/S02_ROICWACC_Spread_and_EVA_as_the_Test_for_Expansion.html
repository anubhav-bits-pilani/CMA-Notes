Subtopic note

ROIC–WACC Spread and EVA as the Test for Expansion Capex

Scope: ROIC vs WACC spread and EVA as the value-creation test for expansion capex

Why it matters

NPV is the theoretically correct test for a single project. Boards, investors and incentive plans, though, track value creation year by year through returns on capital. The ROIC–WACC spread and Economic Value Added (EVA) connect the two views. They tell you whether the capital already sunk into a business, and the capital about to be committed to an expansion, earns more than its opportunity cost. In FP&A you will compute divisional ROIC in the monthly MIS, set EVA-based targets for business heads, and show the board how a ₹2,000 crore capacity addition will move group ROIC over the ramp-up years. In valuation, the spread drives the value of growth. Growth adds value only when incremental ROIC is above WACC.

Core notes

Definitions

\[ \text{ROIC} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Invested capital}}, \qquad \text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) \]

\[ \text{EVA} = \text{NOPAT} - \text{WACC} \times \text{Invested capital} = (\text{ROIC} - \text{WACC}) \times \text{Invested capital} \]

Invested capital is the capital tied up in operations. It can be measured from either side:

  • Operating side: Net working capital (operating current assets − non-interest-bearing current liabilities) + net PPE + right-of-use assets + capitalised intangibles + goodwill (if acquisitions are being judged). Exclude CWIP if you want to measure the return on operating assets. Include it if you want to judge the whole capex programme.
  • Financing side: Equity + borrowings + lease liabilities + debt-like items − surplus cash and non-operating investments.

The two must reconcile. A common audit-style check in the model is to compute both and show a zero difference.

NOPAT is operating profit after notional tax on that profit. It excludes interest, because the cost of all capital is charged through WACC. Use a normalised tax rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA for most Indian companies), not the reported ETR, which is distorted by deferred tax and one-offs. Under Ind AS 116, EBIT already excludes the interest on lease liabilities. Lease liabilities must therefore sit in invested capital and in the WACC weights, or ROIC will be overstated.

The spread as the value-creation test

Value is created only if ROIC > WACC. Market value added (MVA) is the PV of future EVA:

\[ \text{MVA} = \text{Enterprise value} - \text{Invested capital} = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \frac{\text{EVA}_t}{(1+\text{WACC})^t} \]

The value-driver formula (McKinsey) states the same idea for a growing firm:

\[ \text{Value} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_1 \times \left(1 - \frac{g}{\text{RONIC}}\right)}{\text{WACC} - g} \]

where \(g\) is the growth rate of NOPAT and RONIC is the return on new invested capital. If RONIC = WACC, growth neither adds nor destroys value. If RONIC < WACC, growth destroys value even though revenue and EBITDA rise.

Linking EVA to NPV

For a project, the PV of its EVA stream equals its NPV, provided capital charges are based on the opening invested capital of each year and depreciation is the same in NOPAT and in the capital roll-forward:

\[ \text{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{NOPAT}_t - \text{WACC} \times \text{IC}_{t-1}}{(1+\text{WACC})^t} \]

So EVA is not a different decision rule. It spreads NPV across years. This matters because a positive-NPV expansion usually has negative EVA in its early years. Capital is fully charged from day one, while volumes ramp up slowly.

Worked illustration: expansion capex

A specialty chemicals company considers a ₹600 crore new plant. The data are WACC 11.5%, tax 25.168%, straight-line depreciation over 10 years, WC of ₹60 crore, and EBIT of ₹40 crore, ₹95 crore and ₹130 crore in years 1–3, then ₹130 crore thereafter.

₹ crore Year 1 Year 2 Year 3
Opening IC (net PPE + WC) 660 600 540
EBIT 40 95 130
NOPAT at 25.168% 29.9 71.1 97.3
Capital charge at 11.5% of opening IC 75.9 69.0 62.1
EVA (46.0) 2.1 35.2
ROIC on opening IC 4.5% 11.8% 18.0%

Year-1 EVA is negative even though the project may have a clearly positive NPV. If business-head bonuses depend on annual EVA, managers will resist exactly the capex that creates value. Remedies used in practice include:

  • Strategic investment buckets. New capex is excluded from the capital base until commercial operation, or for the first N years.
  • Annuity (sinking-fund) depreciation. This flattens the capital charge plus depreciation over the asset's life.
  • Multi-year EVA targets tied to the approved project ramp-up.

EVA adjustments (Stern Stewart style)

Accounting NOPAT and capital are adjusted to approximate economic values:

Adjustment Rationale
Capitalise R&D and brand-building spend; amortise over useful life These are investments, not period costs
Add back goodwill amortised or impaired (cumulative) to capital Shareholders still paid for it
Remove non-recurring items from NOPAT Measures the sustainable spread
Use operating leases as debt (automatic under Ind AS 116) Consistent capital base
Remove CWIP from capital for operating-unit evaluation No return is expected before commissioning
Replace book tax with cash operating taxes Cash view of the tax burden

Implementation in Excel / Power BI

  • In the model, put NOPAT, opening IC and the capital charge in adjacent rows. Then compute =NOPAT - WACC*IC_open per period, and check =NPV(WACC, EVA_row) against the project's DCF NPV.
  • In Power BI, a divisional ROIC measure such as DIVIDE([NOPAT], [Avg Invested Capital]) with a WACC parameter table lets the board see EVA by segment under alternative hurdle rates.

Decision rules for expansion capex

  1. Approve if project NPV > 0 at the project-specific hurdle (s07, c06). This is equivalent to PV(EVA) > 0.
  2. Check that the incremental ROIC at steady state exceeds WACC by a margin that survives the downside scenario.
  3. Check the dilution of group ROIC during ramp-up, and whether covenants or rating metrics (t5) can absorb it.
  4. Reject "ROIC-accretive" arguments that compare the project ROIC with the current group average ROIC rather than with WACC. A 14% ROIC project in a 20% ROIC group dilutes the average but still creates value if WACC is 11.5%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Average vs incremental ROIC. A firm with a 25% historical ROIC on depreciated plant may earn only 10% on new greenfield capacity at today's replacement cost. Judge capex on incremental ROIC (RONIC), not on the legacy book ROIC.
  • Goodwill in or out. Use ROIC including goodwill to judge whether acquisitions earned their price. Exclude it to judge operating performance.
  • Negative working capital businesses (FMCG, quick commerce, airlines with advance sales) can show very high or even negative invested capital, so ROIC loses meaning. Look at absolute EVA or cash returns instead.
  • Cyclicals. Steel ROIC swings from 5% to 25% across the cycle. Compare through-cycle average ROIC with WACC, not one peak year.
  • Inflation. Historical-cost IC understates capital in old plants, so ROIC is inflated. Compare with peers at a similar asset vintage, or use gross cash invested.

Key takeaways

  • ROIC = NOPAT ÷ Invested capital. EVA = (ROIC − WACC) × IC. Value is created only when the spread is positive.
  • Invested capital must be consistent with NOPAT: include lease liabilities and ROU assets after Ind AS 116.
  • The PV of project EVA equals NPV, so EVA spreads NPV over time and is often negative during ramp-up.
  • Growth creates value only if RONIC > WACC. Compare new capex with WACC, not with the group's average ROIC.
  • Design incentives so that early negative EVA does not block positive-NPV capex.

Practice Questions

Q1. ROIC and EVA from a P&L and balance sheet [L1]

A listed FMCG company reports EBIT of ₹1,250 crore, a tax rate of 25.168%, net PPE of ₹2,100 crore, ROU assets of ₹300 crore, goodwill of ₹400 crore, and net operating working capital of ₹(150) crore. It also holds ₹1,800 crore of surplus cash. WACC is 11%. Compute ROIC and EVA with goodwill included and with goodwill excluded. Comment on the effect of negative working capital.

Q2. EVA–NPV reconciliation [L2]

A project costs ₹400 crore at t = 0 and is depreciated straight-line over 4 years to nil. There is no working capital. EBIT is ₹50 crore, ₹80 crore, ₹100 crore and ₹100 crore in years 1–4, tax is 25%, and WACC is 12%. Compute annual EVA using opening invested capital, discount it at WACC, and separately compute the DCF NPV of free cash flows. Show that the two are equal.

💡 Hint: FCF = NOPAT + depreciation − capex. The capital charge must use opening IC each year.

Q3. Ramp-up dilution and incentive design [L3]

A cement business unit currently earns ROIC of 16% on ₹5,000 crore of IC, against a WACC of 11%. The proposed 4 MTPA expansion costs ₹3,200 crore, commissions at the end of year 2 (CWIP until then), and reaches steady-state NOPAT of ₹480 crore in year 5. NOPAT is ₹120 crore in year 3 and ₹300 crore in year 4. Show the unit's blended ROIC for years 1–5 with CWIP included in IC. Propose an EVA bonus design that does not penalise the BU head for approving the project.

Q4. Value of growth [L2]

A company has NOPAT next year of ₹500 crore, a WACC of 11% and long-run growth of 6%. Compute its value using the value-driver formula for RONIC of 8%, 11% and 18%. Explain to a non-finance director why faster growth at a RONIC of 8% lowers value.

Q5. Interview: ROIC-accretive pitch [L1]

A business head argues: "Our segment ROIC is 22%. This new line earns 15%, so it is dilutive and we should reject it." The WACC is 12%. Respond as the FP&A partner.
