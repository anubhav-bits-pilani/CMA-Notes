Divisional Hurdle Rates: Pure-Play Betas, Unlevering and Relevering

Scope: Divisional/project hurdle rates using pure-play betas, unlevering and relevering to project gearing

Why it matters

A diversified Indian group, say one with cement, renewable power and a chemicals division, has a single corporate WACC. Its divisions, however, have very different systematic risk and debt capacity. Applying the group WACC to all of them over-invests in the risky divisions, whose projects look attractive at a too-low rate, and under-invests in the safe ones, which are rejected at a too-high rate. Over time this drags capital toward the riskiest businesses and destroys value. Boards, valuation committees and lenders' TEV consultants therefore expect a divisional or project hurdle rate built from comparable listed companies' betas. FP&A teams maintain a hurdle rate matrix by business, and update it when market data or target gearing changes.

Core notes

The logic in three steps

  1. Find pure-play comparables. These are listed companies whose operations closely match the division or project. Their observed (levered) equity betas embed both their business risk and their own financial leverage.
  2. Unlever each comparable's beta to remove its financial leverage and isolate business (asset) risk. Average or take the median.
  3. Relever that asset beta at the project's target gearing to get the project equity beta. Then compute Ke with CAPM and the project WACC.

Formulas (Hamada, debt beta = 0)

\[ \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t)\dfrac{D}{E}}, \qquad \beta_L^{\text{project}} = \beta_U \left[1 + (1 - t)\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)_{\text{target}}\right] \]

\[ K_e = R_f + \beta_L^{\text{project}} \times \text{ERP} \; (+ \text{specific premia, see c06}), \qquad \text{WACC} = \frac{E}{V}K_e + \frac{D}{V}K_d(1-t) \]

Here \(\beta_L\) is the observed levered equity beta, \(\beta_U\) the unlevered (asset) beta, \(D/E\) the market-value debt-to-equity ratio, and \(t\) the marginal tax rate. Variants include:

  • Harris–Pringle (\(\beta_U = \beta_L \times E/V\)), used when debt is rebalanced continuously to a target ratio.
  • Fernández and versions with non-zero debt beta: \(\beta_U = (E\beta_L + D(1-t)\beta_D)/(E + D(1-t))\). These are relevant for highly levered infrastructure SPVs, where debt carries some systematic risk.

Worked illustration: renewable power division of a cement group

Market data are Rf = 7.0% (10-year G-sec, assumed), ERP = 7.0% and t = 25.168%. Comparables are listed renewable IPPs and utilities:

Comparable Levered β (2-yr weekly vs Nifty) Market D/E Unlevered β
A (solar IPP) 1.05 1.80 1.05 ÷ (1 + 0.7483 × 1.80) = 0.448
B (wind + solar) 0.95 1.50 0.95 ÷ 2.1225 = 0.448
C (integrated utility) 0.80 1.10 0.80 ÷ 1.8232 = 0.439
Median βU ≈ 0.45

The project's target gearing is 70:30 debt-to-equity (D/E = 2.33), which is typical for non-recourse renewable project finance. Kd is 9.0%.

\[ \beta_L = 0.45 \times [1 + 0.7483 \times 2.33] = 0.45 \times 2.744 = 1.235 \]

\[ K_e = 7.0\% + 1.235 \times 7.0\% = 15.6\%; \quad \text{WACC} = 0.30 \times 15.6\% + 0.70 \times 9.0\% \times 0.7483 = 4.69\% + 4.71\% \approx 9.4\% \]

The cement group itself has βU of about 0.80 and targets D/E of 0.3, which gives a group WACC of roughly 12% (with Kd at 8.5–9.5%). Using about 12% for the renewable project would reject value-creating investments. Using 9.4% for a new cement greenfield would accept value-destroying ones.

Practical steps in building comparables

  • Selection. Use the same industry and similar revenue model, regulation (regulated tariff vs merchant) and stage (operational vs construction). Indian listed pure-plays are often few, so supplement with emerging-market peers and convert currency and country risk consistently.
  • Beta estimation. Use 2-year weekly or 5-year monthly returns against Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 (=SLOPE on returns), with the Blume adjustment (0.67 × raw + 0.33). Check liquidity: illiquid stocks have downward-biased betas. Consider Dimson or sum betas for thinly traded stocks.
  • Leverage. Use market-value D/E averaged over the beta estimation window, not the year-end book D/E. Include lease liabilities in D if the beta period is post-Ind AS 116.
  • Cash adjustment. For cash-rich comparables, compute the beta of operating assets by adjusting for cash (which has a beta near zero): \(\beta_{\text{ops}} = \beta_U / (1 - \text{Cash}/V)\).
  • Tax rate. Use the marginal rate: 25.168% for Sec 115BAA companies, 34.944% for those on the old regime with income above ₹10 crore. Use the comparable's own rate when unlevering and the project's rate when relevering.

Project gearing: which D/E to relever at?

  • Use the target or sustainable gearing for the project. This is the debt the project can support at rating-consistent coverage (t5 c06), not the debt the group happens to raise.
  • If the project is financed at group level, with debt raised by the parent, the relevant D/E is the debt capacity the project contributes. The parent's lenders are effectively lending against the project's cash flows.
  • For non-recourse SPVs, use the SPV's financing structure, but recognise that gearing changes over the life as debt amortises. The constant-WACC assumption then breaks down. APV or a year-by-year relevered Ke is more accurate.

Implementation in a model

Keep a hurdle-rate tab with the comparables table, a median-βU cell, project gearing, Rf, ERP, Kd and t inputs, and the computed project WACC. Link the project NPV to this cell, never to a hard-coded corporate WACC. A data table of NPV against βU (±0.1) and target D/E shows how sensitive the decision is to the comparable set.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • No good pure-play exists. Use accounting betas (regress divisional ROA on market ROA), bottom-up industry betas from a global database adjusted for Indian ERP, or full-information betas that regress conglomerate betas on segment weights.
  • Negative or near-zero observed betas for illiquid or regulated stocks are not credible. Floor the estimate or use industry data.
  • Construction vs operational phase. Construction risk is not captured in betas of operating comparables. Handle it through cash-flow scenarios, or a phase-specific discount rate, without double-counting (c06).
  • Divisions with different debt capacity. Allocating group debt in proportion to assets overstates the leverage of risky divisions. Allocate by rating-consistent capacity.
  • Overseas projects. Use a USD or local-currency CAPM with a country risk premium, and convert discount rates with the relative-inflation formula from Week 16.

Key takeaways

  • Use a project-specific hurdle, not the corporate WACC, whenever the project's systematic risk or debt capacity differs.
  • Unlever comparables' betas with their own D/E and tax rate, take the median βU, and relever at the project's target D/E.
  • Use market-value gearing averaged over the beta window, Blume-adjusted betas, and cash-adjusted βU for cash-rich peers.
  • Relever at the debt capacity the project can sustain, not at the group's actual financing of it.
  • Construction or execution risk is not in operating comparables' betas. Handle it once, either in the cash flows or in the rate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Unlever and relever [L1]

Three listed specialty-chemicals companies have levered betas of 1.20, 1.05 and 1.35, market D/E ratios of 0.20, 0.10 and 0.45, and tax rates of 25.168%, 25.168% and 34.944% respectively. Compute each unlevered beta, take the median, and relever it at a target D/E of 0.50 with t = 25.168%.

Q2. Divisional WACC matrix [L2]

A group has three divisions: (a) cement, with βU 0.80 and target D/E 0.40; (b) a hotels business, with βU 0.95 and target D/E 0.60; and (c) data centres, with βU 0.65 and target D/E 1.20. Rf is 6.8%, ERP 7.0%, Kd 9.0%, 9.8% and 8.8% respectively, and t = 25.168%. Build the WACC for each division and compare it with the corporate WACC of 11.6%. Identify which divisions would be misallocated capital under a single corporate rate.

💡 Hint: Compute D/V from D/E as D/E ÷ (1 + D/E) before weighting.

Q3. Cash-adjusted beta [L2]

An IT services comparable has a levered beta of 0.85, no debt, and cash of ₹45,000 crore against a market capitalisation of ₹3,00,000 crore. Compute the beta of its operating assets and explain why using the raw beta would understate a software project's hurdle rate.

Q4. Build a hurdle-rate tab [L3]

Specify the layout, inputs, formulas and checks of an Excel hurdle-rate tab for a five-division group. It should pull comparables' betas, compute βU and relevered βL, Ke and WACC, and feed project NPVs. Include a sensitivity table and a data-validation scheme for selecting a division.

Q5. Interview: one WACC for all? [L1]

"Our group uses one WACC for every project because it is simpler and treats all divisions fairly." Explain the flaw, with an example of the capital misallocation it causes.
