Subtopic note

Dividend Policy: LODR Reg 43A, Sec 123, Signalling and Lintner

Scope: Dividend policy: SEBI LODR Reg 43A dividend distribution policy, Companies Act Sec 123 (dividend out of profits), signalling and Lintner smoothing

Why it matters

Dividend decisions sit where law, accounting and market signalling meet. The company secretary checks the Companies Act conditions. The CFO's team proves there are distributable profits under Sec 123 and Schedule II depreciation. The listed entity must publish a dividend distribution policy under LODR. The board then sets a number that the market will read as a signal about future earnings. In FP&A you will forecast the dividend line in the long-range plan, model payout against the policy, and test it against FCFE and covenant restrictions. Getting it wrong has consequences. An unsustainable dividend has to be cut later, at a heavy price-reaction cost. A dividend declared out of unrealised Ind AS gains is a legal breach.

Core notes

SEBI LODR Regulation 43A: dividend distribution policy

  • Applicability. The top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalisation, calculated as at 31 March of every financial year, must formulate a dividend distribution policy. Other listed entities may adopt one voluntarily.
  • Disclosure. The policy is disclosed on the company's website, with a web link in the annual report.
  • Minimum content: 1. the circumstances under which shareholders may or may not expect a dividend; 2. the financial parameters considered (profitability, free cash flow, capex needs, leverage); 3. internal and external factors (business cycle, regulation, tax changes); 4. the policy on utilising retained earnings; 5. the parameters adopted for various classes of shares.
  • Changes to the policy and the rationale for them must also be disclosed. The policy is not a promise to pay. It is a framework against which deviations must be explained.

Typical Indian policies state a payout range, for example "30–50% of standalone PAT", or link payout to FCF generation, as IT services companies do with "up to 85% of FCF over a five-year period, including buybacks".

Companies Act Sec 123: dividend only out of profits

Sec 123(1) permits a dividend only out of:

  1. profits of the current financial year, arrived at after providing for depreciation in accordance with Schedule II;
  2. profits of any previous financial year(s), arrived at after providing for depreciation and remaining undistributed;
  3. both of these; or
  4. money provided by the Central or State Government for paying dividends under a guarantee.

Further rules:

  • Transfer to reserves is optional. The company may transfer whatever percentage it considers appropriate.
  • Exclusions. A company cannot declare dividends from any reserves other than free reserves. In computing profits, unrealised gains, notional gains, revaluation of assets, and changes in carrying amount on fair value measurement must be excluded (Explanation to Sec 123(1), inserted in 2015). For an Ind AS company this means removing fair-value gains on investments through P&L and on derivatives, as well as other remeasurement gains, from distributable profit.
  • Inadequate profits. If profits are inadequate or absent and the dividend is paid out of accumulated profits, Rule 3 of the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014 applies:
  • the rate must not exceed the average rate of the three preceding years (not applicable if no dividend was paid in those years);
  • the amount drawn must not exceed one-tenth of paid-up capital plus free reserves per the latest audited statements;
  • the amount drawn must first be used to set off losses of the current year before any equity dividend is declared;
  • the balance of reserves after the withdrawal must not fall below 15% of paid-up share capital.
  • Carried-forward losses and depreciation. Losses and depreciation not provided in earlier years must be set off against current profits before declaring a dividend (proviso to Sec 123(1)).
  • Interim dividend (Sec 123(3)). The board may declare an interim dividend out of the surplus in P&L and out of profits of the year in which it is declared. If the company has incurred a loss in the current year up to the end of the quarter preceding the declaration, the interim rate cannot exceed the average of the three preceding years.
  • Mechanics. Deposit the dividend in a separate bank account within 5 days of declaration (Sec 123(4)). Pay it within 30 days (Sec 127; failure is an offence). Transfer any unpaid amount to the Unpaid Dividend Account within 7 days after the 30 days expire (Sec 124). After 7 years, transfer it to the IEPF (Sec 124(5), 125).
  • Sec 123(6). A company that has not complied with Sec 73 and 74 (acceptance and repayment of deposits) cannot declare dividends while the default continues.

Accounting points

  • A final dividend is recognised as a liability only when approved by shareholders at the AGM. A dividend proposed or declared after the reporting date is a non-adjusting event, disclosed in the notes (Ind AS 10.12–13, Ind AS 1.137).
  • An interim dividend is recognised when declared by the board.
  • Dividends are debited to retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity. Ind AS 7.34 requires dividends paid to be classified as financing cash flows. This is an Indian modification that removes the IAS 7 option to show them as operating.

Signalling theory

Managers know more than investors about future cash flows. Raising a dividend is costly to fake, because an unsustainable payout must later be cut. A dividend increase is therefore read as a credible signal of confidence in permanent earnings, and a cut as a signal of distress. Empirically, dividend initiations and increases earn positive abnormal returns and cuts earn sharply negative ones. Indian evidence is consistent with this, though weaker where promoter holdings are concentrated. Implications for policy design:

  • Set the regular dividend at a level sustainable through a downturn.
  • Use special dividends or buybacks for windfalls, so that a non-recurrence is not read as a cut.

Lintner smoothing model

Lintner (1956) found that managers (i) have a long-run target payout ratio, (ii) care more about the change in dividends than the level, and (iii) adjust only partially towards the target each year:

\[ D_t - D_{t-1} = a + c\,(r \times \text{EPS}_t - D_{t-1}) + \varepsilon_t \]

Here \(D_t\) is the dividend per share in year \(t\), \(r\) is the target payout ratio, \(r \times \text{EPS}_t\) is the target dividend, \(c\) is the speed of adjustment (0 < c ≤ 1), and \(a\) is a constant, often small and positive, reflecting reluctance to cut.

Illustration. Target payout r = 40%, c = 0.3, a = 0, last year's DPS ₹8. EPS rises from ₹20 to ₹30, so the target DPS is ₹12. The model DPS is 8 + 0.3 × (12 − 8) = ₹9.20. The implied payout is 30.7%, below target, because the company is not yet sure the higher EPS is permanent.

Estimating c across a company's history in Excel (regress \(\Delta D\) on \(\text{EPS}_t\) and \(D_{t-1}\) using LINEST) gives an empirical smoothing parameter for forecasting dividends in the LRP.

Other theories (brief)

  • MM irrelevance (1961). In perfect markets dividend policy does not affect value. Taxes, signalling, agency costs and transaction costs break this.
  • Walter and Gordon models (CMA syllabus): value depends on payout when the return on retained earnings differs from Ke.
  • Clientele effect. Investors self-select by tax position. The October 2024 change (s05) shifts clienteles.
  • Agency (Jensen). Dividends discipline managers by removing free cash flow (see the topic note).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ind AS retained earnings with OCI items must be stripped of unrealised remeasurement gains before distributable profit is computed. Keep a "distributable reserves" schedule separate from book equity.
  • Standalone vs consolidated. Legal distributability is tested on standalone profits. A holding company with strong consolidated earnings but no upstream dividends from subsidiaries may have no distributable profit.
  • Lender covenants often restrict dividends (for example, permitted only if DSCR > 1.3x and no default), overriding the policy.
  • Dividends in a loss year are legal under Rule 3 limits but may contradict signalling logic. Explain them in the Reg 43A framework.
  • PSU guidelines (DIPAM) can force higher payouts than the board's own policy would suggest.

Key takeaways

  • Reg 43A requires the top 1,000 listed entities to publish a dividend distribution policy with prescribed content.
  • Sec 123 restricts dividends to current or past profits after Schedule II depreciation, excluding unrealised and fair-value gains.
  • Rule 3 limits dividends from accumulated reserves: average-rate cap, 10% of capital + free reserves, and a 15% residual reserve floor.
  • Pay within 30 days, move unpaid amounts to the unpaid dividend account, and transfer to IEPF after 7 years.
  • Dividends signal management's view of permanent earnings, and Lintner shows that companies smooth towards a target payout.

Practice Questions

Q1. Distributable profit under Ind AS [L2]

A listed company's FY25 standalone profit after tax is ₹480 crore. It includes a ₹70 crore fair-value gain on equity investments held at FVTPL (unrealised), a ₹25 crore mark-to-market gain on an undesignated forward contract, and a ₹40 crore realised profit on the sale of land. Opening retained earnings are ₹1,200 crore, with no carried-forward losses. Compute the maximum dividend out of current-year profits under Sec 123 and explain each adjustment.

Q2. Dividend out of reserves in a loss year [L2]

A company incurs a loss of ₹35 crore in FY25. Paid-up capital is ₹100 crore, free reserves are ₹260 crore, and dividend rates in the three preceding years were 15%, 20% and 25%. It wishes to declare a dividend of 20%. Test each condition of Rule 3 and conclude whether the proposed dividend is permissible.

💡 Hint: Check the average-rate cap, the one-tenth drawdown limit, the set-off of the current-year loss and the 15% residual reserve test in that order.

Q3. Lintner forecast [L2]

A company's target payout is 35% and its estimated speed of adjustment is 0.4, with a = 0. DPS in FY25 was ₹6.0. Forecast EPS is ₹22, ₹26, ₹18 and ₹28 for FY26–FY29. Forecast DPS for each year using the Lintner model, and comment on the dividend in FY28 relative to EPS.

Q4. Drafting a Reg 43A policy [L3]

Draft the key clauses of a dividend distribution policy for an NSE-listed cement company with a ₹6,000 crore capex programme over three years, a covenant of Net Debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0x, and volatile earnings. Address all five mandatory elements and include a framework for special dividends.

Q5. Interview: dividend cut decision [L1]

EPS has fallen 40% and the current dividend uses 90% of FCFE. Explain whether you would recommend a cut, using signalling theory and the Lintner framework.
