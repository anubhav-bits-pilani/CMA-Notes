Key concept

Sec 68 Buyback Limits: 25%, 10% and the 2:1 Debt-Equity Test

Concept: Buyback limits (Sec 68): ≤ 25% of paid-up capital + free reserves (equity buyback ≤ 25% of paid-up equity in a year); post-buyback D/E ≤ 2:1; board approval up to 10%

Statement

For a buyback under Sec 68 of the Companies Act, 2013:

\[ \text{Buyback amount} \le 25\% \times (\text{Paid-up capital} + \text{Free reserves}) \]

\[ \text{Equity shares bought back in the FY} \le 25\% \times \text{Paid-up equity capital (number of shares)} \]

\[ \frac{\text{Secured + unsecured debt (post-buyback)}}{\text{Paid-up capital + Free reserves (post-buyback)}} \le 2 \]

The approval required is a board resolution if the buyback is ≤ 10% of paid-up equity capital + free reserves, and a special resolution above that, up to 25%. Free reserves are defined in Sec 2(43) and exclude unrealised and fair-value gains. For the purposes of the limit, the base includes the securities premium account. For listed companies, SEBI applies the D/E test on both standalone and consolidated figures.

Explanation

The limits protect creditors, because a buyback returns capital ahead of them, and protect minority shareholders.

  • The 25% overall limit caps the capital that can leave the company in one offer.
  • The equity-specific 25% cap on the number of shares is a separate constraint. It binds when the share price is low relative to the reserves per share. For example, at a price near book value, 25% of reserves may buy more than 25% of the shares.
  • The 2:1 debt-equity condition is tested after the buyback: debt stays the same while equity falls by the buyback amount. The Central Government can prescribe a higher ratio for a class of companies. Debt means aggregate secured and unsecured debts. Lease liabilities are generally included in practice under Ind AS, but confirm with the auditor's methodology.
  • The 10% board-route threshold allows quick action without a general meeting. It is attractive when timing matters.

Related conditions:

  • one year between buyback offers;
  • completion within one year of the resolution;
  • fully paid shares only;
  • a declaration of solvency;
  • extinguishment of the shares within 7 days;
  • no fresh issue of the same kind within 6 months;
  • a Sec 69 CRR transfer;
  • the Sec 70 prohibitions.

Illustration

Standalone figures at 31 March 2025 (₹ crore): paid-up equity 200 (face value ₹2, so 100 crore shares), securities premium 600, general reserve 1,000, retained earnings 2,200, FVOCI reserve 150, and debt 6,800. The proposed buyback is at ₹50 per share.

Test Computation Result
Base 200 + 600 + 1,000 + 2,200 (FVOCI excluded) 4,000
25% limit 0.25 × 4,000 ₹1,000 crore
10% board limit 0.10 × 4,000 ₹400 crore
Equity share cap 25% × 100 crore = 25 crore shares × ₹50 ₹1,250 crore, not binding
D/E at ₹1,000 crore 6,800 ÷ (4,000 − 1,000) 2.27 : 1, fails
Maximum buyback passing D/E 6,800 ÷ (4,000 − X) = 2, so X = 600 ₹600 crore

The leverage test, not the 25% limit, is binding. A ₹600 crore buyback needs a special resolution, because it exceeds ₹400 crore.

Practice Questions

Q1. Finding the binding constraint [L2]

A company has paid-up equity of ₹50 crore (face value ₹10), securities premium of ₹150 crore, free reserves of ₹550 crore and debt of ₹1,300 crore. It plans a buyback at ₹110 per share. Compute the maximum buyback under each of the three quantitative limits, identify the binding one, and state the approval required.

💡 Hint: Convert the equity-share cap into rupees at the buyback price before comparing it with the other limits.

Q2. Consolidated test [L2]

A listed holding company passes all Sec 68 tests on a standalone basis with D/E of 0.4:1. Its consolidated debt is ₹18,000 crore against consolidated paid-up capital + free reserves of ₹10,000 crore. It proposes a ₹1,500 crore buyback. Assess compliance with the SEBI requirement and suggest options if it fails.

Q3. Board-route timing [L1]

Explain why a company might deliberately size a buyback at just under 10% of paid-up capital + free reserves, and list the conditions that still apply even when only board approval is needed.
