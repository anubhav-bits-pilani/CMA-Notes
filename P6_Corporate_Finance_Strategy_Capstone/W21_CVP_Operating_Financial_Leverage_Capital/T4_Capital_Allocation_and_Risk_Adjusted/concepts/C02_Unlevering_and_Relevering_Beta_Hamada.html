Key concept

Unlevering and Relevering Beta (Hamada)

Concept: Unlevered beta βu = βL ÷ [1 + (1 − t) × D/E]; relevered βL = βu × [1 + (1 − t) × D/E target]

Statement

\[ \beta_u = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t)\dfrac{D}{E}}, \qquad \beta_L^{\text{new}} = \beta_u \times \left[1 + (1 - t)\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)_{\text{target}}\right] \]

where:

  • \(\beta_L\) = observed levered equity beta of a comparable company (regression of its stock returns on the market index);
  • \(\beta_u\) = unlevered (asset) beta, which reflects business risk only;
  • \(D/E\) = market-value debt-to-equity ratio of the comparable, averaged over the beta estimation window;
  • \((D/E)_{\text{target}}\) = the target or sustainable gearing of the project, division or company being valued;
  • \(t\) = marginal corporate tax rate (the comparable's when unlevering, the target's when relevering).

Explanation

Equity holders bear both business risk and the extra variability that fixed interest charges create. This is the market-based counterpart of DFL from topic t2. The Hamada relation follows from MM with corporate taxes, under three assumptions:

  1. debt is risk-free (debt beta = 0);
  2. the amount of debt is fixed, so the tax shield has the risk of the debt;
  3. perpetual cash flows.

The \((1 - t)\) factor appears because the tax shield reduces the government's share of value and the net leverage borne by shareholders.

When the assumptions fail, use variants:

  • Harris–Pringle. For a constant D/V that is rebalanced continuously, \(\beta_L = \beta_u (1 + D/E)\), with no tax term.
  • Non-zero debt beta. \(\beta_L = \beta_u + (\beta_u - \beta_d)(1 - t)D/E\). Relevant for leveraged SPVs or sub-investment-grade companies, where debt beta might be 0.1–0.3.
  • Cash-rich comparables. Unlever further to strip out cash, which has a beta of about 0: \(\beta_{\text{ops}} = \beta_u / (1 - \text{Cash}/V)\).

Practical points: use the median of several comparables' βu rather than the mean, Blume-adjust raw betas, check statistical quality (R², standard error), and keep the definition of debt consistent (include lease liabilities throughout or exclude them throughout).

Illustration

A data-centre project, financed at a target D/E of 1.0. Comparables:

Comparable βL Market D/E Tax βu
P 1.10 0.80 25.168% 1.10 ÷ (1 + 0.7483 × 0.80) = 0.689
Q 0.92 0.40 25.168% 0.92 ÷ 1.2993 = 0.708
R 1.30 1.20 34.944% 1.30 ÷ (1 + 0.6506 × 1.20) = 0.731

The median βu is 0.708. Relevered at D/E = 1.0 and t = 25.168%:

\[ \beta_L = 0.708 \times (1 + 0.7483 \times 1.0) = 1.238 \]

With Rf = 7.0% and ERP = 7.0%, Ke = 7.0% + 1.238 × 7.0% = 15.7%. Using comparable Q's raw beta of 0.92 directly would give 13.4%, understating the hurdle by about 230 bps, because Q is far less levered than the project.

Practice Questions

Q1. Relevering for a new structure [L1]

A comparable has βL of 1.15, market D/E of 0.35 and a tax rate of 25.168%. Compute βu and relever it at D/E of 0.80 for a project in a company on the old tax regime (34.944%).

Q2. Choosing the right formula [L2]

An infrastructure InvIT holds toll roads with D/E of 1.6 and BBB-rated debt with an estimated debt beta of 0.20. Its observed equity beta is 0.95 and the tax rate is 25.168%. Compute βu using (a) Hamada with zero debt beta and (b) the debt-beta variant. Explain which is more appropriate and why.

💡 Hint: The debt-beta variant rearranges to βu = (βL + βd(1 − t)D/E) ÷ (1 + (1 − t)D/E).

Q3. Interview: why unlever at all? [L1]

Explain to a non-finance manager why you cannot simply use a peer's published beta as the project's beta, using two peers with the same business but different gearing as your example.
