Key concept

ROIC, EVA and the ROIC > WACC Value-Creation Condition

Concept: ROIC = NOPAT ÷ Invested capital; EVA = NOPAT − WACC × Invested capital; value created only if ROIC > WACC

Statement

\[ \text{ROIC} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{IC}}, \qquad \text{EVA} = \text{NOPAT} - \text{WACC} \times \text{IC} = (\text{ROIC} - \text{WACC}) \times \text{IC} \]

where:

  • NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − t), with t the normalised marginal tax rate;
  • IC = invested capital, meaning operating net working capital + net fixed assets (including ROU assets) + operating intangibles and goodwill. Equivalently, IC = equity + debt + lease liabilities + debt-like items − surplus cash and non-operating assets;
  • WACC = the weighted average cost of capital appropriate to the risk of the capital measured.

Value is created in a period only if EVA > 0, which is the same as ROIC > WACC. Over the life of an investment, value is created if the PV of EVA, which equals NPV, is positive.

Explanation

Accounting profit charges only the cost of debt (interest). EVA charges the cost of all capital, including the equity holders' required return. A company can therefore report rising PAT while destroying value, if the capital used to produce that PAT earns less than its cost.

Conditions for a meaningful calculation:

  • Consistency of numerator and denominator. If IC includes lease liabilities (Ind AS 116), NOPAT must be EBIT before lease interest. Reported EBIT already satisfies this. If goodwill is in IC, NOPAT includes the acquired businesses' profits.
  • Timing. Use opening or average IC. Opening IC links EVA exactly to NPV.
  • Tax rate. Use the statutory marginal rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA), not the ETR distorted by deferred tax and prior-year items.
  • Exclusions. Remove surplus cash, non-operating investments and CWIP (when measuring operating performance) from IC, and their income from NOPAT.

Exceptions and limits:

  • In negative-working-capital businesses, IC can be tiny, so ROIC becomes meaningless.
  • In early-stage capex, annual EVA is negative even for positive-NPV projects.
  • Historical-cost IC inflates the ROIC of old asset bases.

Illustration

Segment data for FY25 from a listed chemicals company (₹ crore):

Item Amount
EBIT 820
Tax at 25.168% (206)
NOPAT 614
Opening IC (PPE 3,600 + ROU 200 + NWC 900 + goodwill 300) 5,000
WACC 11.0%
Capital charge 550
EVA 64
ROIC 12.3%

The spread is only 1.3 percentage points. Suppose a proposed ₹1,500 crore expansion is expected to earn a 10% steady-state ROIC. It would raise EBIT and PAT but reduce total EVA, since its own EVA is (10% − 11%) × 1,500 = −₹15 crore per year at steady state. That holds unless the project's risk-specific WACC is lower than 11%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute ROIC and EVA [L1]

A company reports EBIT of ₹640 crore and tax at 25.168%. Opening invested capital is ₹4,200 crore, including ₹350 crore of CWIP for a plant that is still under construction. WACC is 11.5%. Compute ROIC and EVA (a) including and (b) excluding CWIP, and explain which you would present to the operating management team.

Q2. Financing-side reconciliation [L2]

From the following, compute invested capital from both the operating and the financing side, and reconcile the two (₹ crore): equity 3,000; borrowings 1,400; lease liabilities 250; cash and liquid investments 900 (of which 150 is operating cash); net PPE 3,200; ROU assets 230; goodwill 0; inventories 700; receivables 600; trade payables 820; other operating liabilities 160.

💡 Hint: Treat operating cash as part of working capital and the rest as surplus. The difference between the two sides should be zero.

Q3. Growth that destroys value [L2]

Explain, with a two-year numerical example of your own design, how a company can grow PAT by 20% a year while EVA falls. State clearly the ROIC on new capital you assume and the WACC.
