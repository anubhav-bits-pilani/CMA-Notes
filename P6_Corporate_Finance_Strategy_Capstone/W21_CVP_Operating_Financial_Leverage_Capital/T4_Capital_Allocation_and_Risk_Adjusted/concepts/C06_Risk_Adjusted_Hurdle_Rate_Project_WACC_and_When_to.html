Key concept

Risk-Adjusted Hurdle Rate: Project WACC and When to Add Premia

Concept: Risk-adjusted hurdle = project-specific WACC (+ explicit country/execution premium only when cash flows are not already risk-adjusted)

Statement

\[ \text{Hurdle}_{\text{project}} = \frac{E}{V}\left[R_f + \beta_L^{\text{project}} \times \text{ERP} + \text{CRP}\right] + \frac{D}{V} K_d^{\text{project}} (1 - t) \; \left(+ \, \pi_{\text{exec}} \text{ only if cash flows are unadjusted}\right) \]

where:

  • \(\beta_L^{\text{project}}\) = the pure-play βu relevered at the project's target D/E (c02);
  • \(E/V\), \(D/V\) = the project's target market-value weights;
  • \(K_d^{\text{project}}\) = the cost of debt consistent with the project's rating-implied credit risk;
  • CRP = the country risk premium, used only when the project's country risk differs from the market whose ERP is used (for example, an Indian company investing in Africa, using an India-based ERP);
  • \(\pi_{\text{exec}}\) = an execution or specific-risk premium. Add it only when the cash flows are a management "most likely" or "success case" forecast that has not been haircut for that risk.

Explanation

The discount rate should price systematic risk, the risk diversified investors require compensation for. Beta captures that. Project-specific, diversifiable risks such as construction delay, technology ramp-up and offtake failure belong in the expected cash flows, through probability-weighted scenarios, contingencies and delayed ramp-up. Mixing the two approaches creates double-counting:

Approach Cash flows Discount rate
Preferred (expected values) Probability-weighted, already haircut for execution risk Project WACC
Acceptable (shortcut) Management base case, not haircut Project WACC + explicit premium, calibrated and disclosed
Wrong Haircut for execution risk Project WACC + premium (risk counted twice)
Wrong Base case, not haircut Corporate WACC (risk ignored or misstated)

Why premia are dangerous: a constant premium added to the rate compounds over time. It penalises distant cash flows progressively more, which is appropriate only if risk truly grows with time. Construction risk, by contrast, is concentrated in the first 2–3 years. A scenario-weighted cash flow or a phase-specific rate is more accurate.

Country risk premia are a judgement call. For an Indian company using an Indian ERP (which already embeds India's country risk), add a CRP only for projects in countries riskier than India. For an overseas investor valuing an Indian project from a mature-market ERP base, the India CRP (sovereign default spread × relative equity volatility) is added, consistently with Week 16.

Illustration

A ₹1,200 crore greenfield specialty-chemicals plant has a project WACC of 12.5% (relevered pure-play beta). Management's base case gives NPV of ₹180 crore at 12.5%. The engineering team estimates a 30% probability of a 12-month delay, which pushes all operating flows back by one year and adds ₹90 crore of overruns.

  • Correct (expected cash flows). Compute the probability-weighted NPV at 12.5%. Suppose the delay case NPV is ₹20 crore. Expected NPV = 0.7 × 180 + 0.3 × 20 = ₹132 crore. Accept.
  • Shortcut. Discount the base case at 12.5% + 1.5% premium = 14%. Suppose that gives NPV of ₹95 crore. The premium is arbitrary, and it under- or over-states risk depending on its calibration.
  • Double-counted. Discount the expected cash flows at 14%. NPV might fall to ₹40 crore or turn negative, rejecting a project that is in fact value-creating.

Practice Questions

Q1. Diagnose the method [L1]

For each of the following, state whether risk is counted correctly, ignored or double-counted: (a) probability-weighted cash flows at the project WACC; (b) the management success case at the corporate WACC for a riskier new-segment project; (c) cash flows reduced 20% for a "risk haircut" and discounted at the project WACC + 3%.

Q2. Overseas project hurdle [L2]

An Indian pharma company plans a plant in Kenya. The pure-play βu is 0.70, target D/E is 0.5, and t is 30% (Kenya). The ERP is 7.0% (India base) and Rf is 7.0%. The Kenya sovereign spread over India is 3.0%, and the relative equity volatility is 1.4. Kd is 12% in local terms. Build the hurdle rate in INR terms, and explain whether an additional execution premium is warranted if the cash flows are already scenario-weighted.

💡 Hint: Add the CRP to Ke only. Do not add it to Kd if Kd already reflects local credit spreads.

Q3. Time-pattern of risk [L2]

Explain, with a 10-year cash-flow profile of your own design, why adding a constant 2% premium for construction risk distorts the NPV of a project whose risk is concentrated in years 0–2. Propose a better approach.
