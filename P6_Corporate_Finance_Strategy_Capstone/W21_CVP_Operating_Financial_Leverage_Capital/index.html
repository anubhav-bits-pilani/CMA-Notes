How this week fits

This is the first corporate-finance strategy week of Phase 6 (Corporate Finance Strategy, Capstone & Career Launch). Until now you have built the tools: Ind AS reporting and analysis (Weeks 1–8), 3-statement modelling with capex, WC and debt schedules (Weeks 13–15), WACC and CAPM (Week 16), and budgeting, flexible budgets and variances (Weeks 18/19). This week uses them to make decisions. Should we cut price? How much volume do we need? Which expansion should we fund, and with how much debt? Can the balance sheet carry it without losing the rating?

The topics run in a chain. t1 (multi-product break-even and contribution margin) gives the volume economics and break-even utilisation of a plant. t2 (DOL, DFL, DCL) turns cost and financing structure into earnings volatility. t3 (capital budgeting) evaluates projects with Indian tax depreciation and the full set of metrics. t4 (capital allocation and hurdle rates) compares expansion capex with debt prepayment, buybacks and dividends against risk-adjusted rates. t5 (capital structure and credit ratings) sets the debt capacity and covenant limits within which all of this must fit. The exercise ties t1–t3 together in one project model, and the Saturday case adds the t2 leverage comparison and the t4/t5 funding judgement.

Next week (Week 22) moves to M&A: acquisition debt capacity, accretion/dilution and post-deal leverage reuse this week's debt-capacity and EPS-leverage tools directly.

Refresh before starting

Time value of money and Excel functions

Time value of money: PV, FV, annuity and growing-annuity factors; Excel PV, FV, PMT, RATE

  • \(\text{PV} = \text{FV} \div (1 + r)^n\); \(\text{FV} = \text{PV} \times (1 + r)^n\).
  • Annuity factor: \(\text{AF}_{r,n} = [1 - (1 + r)^{-n}] \div r\); annuity due multiplies by \((1 + r)\).
  • Growing annuity (first payment \(C\) at end of Year 1, growth \(g\)): \(\text{PV} = C \times [1 - ((1 + g) \div (1 + r))^n] \div (r - g)\); growing perpetuity \(C \div (r - g)\) for \(r > g\).
  • Excel sign convention: cash paid out is negative. =PV(rate, nper, pmt, [fv], [type]), =FV(...), =PMT(rate, nper, pv) (gives EAA when pv is an NPV), =RATE(nper, pmt, pv, [fv]) (gives the IRR of a level annuity).
  • Convert annual to monthly or quarterly rates by compounding, not division, when the rate is effective.

Cost behaviour and marginal costing

Cost behaviour (fixed/variable/semi-variable) and marginal costing from CMA Intermediate/Final

  • Variable costs change in total with activity, fixed costs do not (within the relevant range), and semi-variable costs have both elements. Separate them with high-low (activity extremes) or regression (SLOPE, INTERCEPT, RSQ).
  • Step-fixed costs jump at capacity thresholds.
  • Marginal costing treats fixed production overhead as a period cost; contribution \(=\) sales \(-\) variable costs.
  • Key formulas: BEP units \(= F \div (P - V)\); P/V ratio \(=\) contribution \(\div\) sales; MOS \(=\) actual \(-\) BEP.
  • Limiting factor: rank by contribution per unit of the scarce resource.

Income-tax depreciation and Sec 115BAA

Income-tax depreciation rules (WDV block, Sec 32, half-rate rule) and Sec 115BAA rates

  • Sec 32 WDV on blocks of assets (Sec 2(11), Sec 43(6)); P&M generally 15%, non-residential buildings 10%, computers 40%, intangibles 25%; goodwill not depreciable from AY 2021-22.
  • Half rate if put to use for less than 180 days in the year of acquisition.
  • Additional depreciation of 20% on new P&M for manufacturers (Sec 32(1)(iia)); half deferred to the next year if used under 180 days; not available under Sec 115BAA.
  • Sec 50: deemed short-term capital gain if sale proceeds exceed the block WDV or the block ceases.
  • Sec 115BAA: 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess \(= 25.168\%\); no MAT; specified deductions forgone; irrevocable.
  • Old regime (large company): 34.944%, MAT at 17.472% of book profit. The Income-tax Act 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections; verify the mapping.

WACC, CAPM and 3-statement linkages

WACC/CAPM mechanics from Week 16 and 3-statement linkages from Weeks 13–15

  • \(K_e = R_f + \beta \times \text{ERP}\) (Indian 10-year G-sec for \(R_f\)); \(\text{WACC} = E/V \times K_e + D/V \times K_d(1 - t)\) at market or target weights.
  • Hamada: \(\beta_L = \beta_U[1 + (1 - t)D/E]\).
  • 3-statement links: PAT to retained earnings; depreciation and non-cash items added back in CFO; capex to PPE and CFI; borrowings to the debt schedule and CFF; interest from average debt (circularity handled with a switch or copy-paste macro); cash as the balancing item checked against the balance sheet.
  • Project models reuse the capex, depreciation and WC schedules, but with a separate tax-depreciation schedule and without financing flows in FCFF.

Cross-cutting concepts

Cost structure links break-even, DOL, risk and debt capacity

Fixed vs variable cost structure drives break-even, DOL, project risk and debt capacity together

A business that swaps variable cost for fixed cost (automation, an owned fleet, a captive power plant, a greenfield plant rather than contract manufacturing) raises its contribution margin and its fixed-cost base at the same time. Four things follow together. Break-even volume rises, because more fixed cost must be covered. DOL at any given volume rises, because DOL \(= 1 \div \text{MOS \%}\) and the margin of safety shrinks. Project risk rises: NPV becomes more sensitive to utilisation and price, so the break-even utilisation for NPV \(= 0\) moves closer to the base case. Debt capacity per rupee of expected EBITDA falls, because lenders size debt to downside cash flows, and downside EBITDA falls faster for a high-DOL business. So the same decision appears in t1 (cost indifference point), t2 (DOL and DCL), t3 (sensitivity of NPV) and t5 (rating-consistent leverage). The Saturday case is built around exactly this trade-off: a proposal with lower capex and higher variable cost against one with higher capex and lower variable cost. Analyse it as one decision, not four separate calculations.

Consistency principle for cash flows and discount rates

Consistency principle: nominal flows with nominal rates, FCFF with WACC, FCFE with Ke

Every discounted cash-flow model must pair the cash-flow definition with the matching discount rate. Inflation: nominal cash flows (including expected price and cost escalation) are discounted at a nominal rate. Real cash flows (constant prices) are discounted at a real rate, linked by Fisher: \((1 + r_{\text{nom}}) = (1 + r_{\text{real}})(1 + i)\). Indian WACCs built from G-sec yields are nominal, so build nominal models. Tax depreciation shields are nominal by construction. Claim holders: free cash flow to the firm (before interest and debt flows) is discounted at WACC, which already reflects the after-tax cost of debt; free cash flow to equity (after interest and net borrowing) is discounted at the cost of equity. Mixing them, for example deducting interest and then discounting at WACC, double counts the tax benefit of debt and the financing charge. Currency: INR flows with an INR rate; USD flows with a USD rate. Risk: either risk-adjusted rates or certainty-equivalent flows at the risk-free rate, never both. State the basis of every input in the assumptions block.

Exercise: dedicated cash-flow schedule

Exercise: separate capex, WC ramp-up (WC as % of incremental revenue), tax depreciation and terminal flows on a dedicated schedule

The project model should have a dedicated cash-flow schedule with each component on its own clearly labelled row block. Capex: by asset class (land, buildings, P&M) and by period, with put-to-use dates driving tax depreciation. Working capital: NWC \(=\) WC % \(\times\) incremental revenue each year, with ΔNWC as the cash flow; the WC % is a single input so that sensitivity is easy. Tax depreciation: a separate WDV schedule per block (P&M 15%, buildings 10%), with the half-rate flag and an additional-depreciation flag linked to the regime switch. Book depreciation lives elsewhere and feeds only the projected P&L. Terminal flows: salvage by asset class, tax treatment (block continues or ceases), land at market value, and WC release in the final year. FCFF is then the sum of the blocks. Separate rows make review possible: a reviewer can check that the sum of ΔNWC over the life is zero, that tax WDV never goes negative, and that interest appears nowhere in FCFF.

Exercise: break-even utilisation for EBIT and NPV

Exercise: break-even utilisation for EBIT = 0 and for NPV = 0 (Goal Seek on utilisation input)

Two different break-evens answer two different questions. Accounting break-even utilisation is where the plant's EBIT is zero in a steady-state year: \(\text{BE util} = F \div [(P - V) \times \text{Capacity}]\), computed directly or with Goal Seek on the utilisation input with EBIT as the target. It tells you whether the plant covers its operating costs including depreciation. NPV break-even utilisation is the steady-state utilisation at which the project's NPV is zero, so that it just earns its cost of capital. Use Goal Seek: set the NPV cell to 0 by changing the steady-state utilisation input (keeping the ramp-up profile as a proportion of it). The NPV break-even is always higher than the accounting break-even, because it must also recover the capital invested and a return on it. The gap between base-case utilisation and NPV break-even utilisation is the project's real margin of safety. Report both, and add a cash break-even (excluding depreciation) and a debt-service break-even for lenders.

Exercise: data tables and scenario switch

Exercise: two-way data tables (utilisation × realisation price, capex overrun × WACC) and a scenario switch with CHOOSE

Two two-way data tables are required. The first has steady-state utilisation (for example 60% to 95% in 5-point steps) against realisation price (−10% to +10% in 2.5% steps), with NPV as the output. The second has capex overrun (0% to +30%) against WACC (10% to 14%), with NPV or IRR as the output. Data tables must reference input cells on the same sheet as the table (or link the inputs to it), and the output cell must be in the corner of the table. Set calculation to "Automatic except for data tables" while building, and press F9 to refresh. Use conditional formatting to shade cells where NPV \(< 0\). The scenario switch is a single input cell (1 = Bear, 2 = Base, 3 = Bull) with every scenario driver computed as =CHOOSE(Scenario, Bear, Base, Bull). Keep scenario values in a visible table, never inside the formulas. A live-case label (=CHOOSE(Scenario, "Bear", "Base", "Bull")) on every output page prevents misreporting.

Exercise: single output block

Exercise: report NPV, IRR, MIRR, PI, discounted payback and peak funding requirement on one output block

The output block summarises the project on one screen: NPV (=NPV(WACC, CF1:CFn) + CF0, or XNPV for dated construction flows); IRR (=IRR), with a flag if the cash flows have more than one sign change; MIRR (=MIRR(values, finance_rate, reinvest_rate), with both rates as visible inputs); PI (PV of inflows ÷ PV of investment outflows); discounted payback (first year the cumulative discounted FCFF turns non-negative, with interpolation); and peak funding requirement (the most negative cumulative undiscounted FCFF, which includes construction capex and the WC build-up and sizes the funding need). Add EAA if the project is compared with an alternative of a different life, the accounting and NPV break-even utilisations, and the live scenario name. Every figure should link to the calculation sheets; no hard-coded numbers. Include a check cell confirming that NPV computed with the function equals the sum of the discounted cash-flow row.

Hands-on exercise brief

Exercise: Build a dynamic Capital Budgeting & Expansion Feasibility Model (new plant Capex, working capital ramp, tax depreciation shield, NPV/IRR/MIRR, and break-even utilization sensitivity).

Case: Narmada Specialty Polymers Ltd (NSE-listed, FY ends 31 March)

The company plans a greenfield 30,000 tonnes-per-annum (TPA) engineering-plastics plant at Dahej, Gujarat.

Input Value
Land (owned; market value at FY27) ₹30 crore, growing 5% a year; book value ₹6 crore
Buildings ₹60 crore: 50% in FY27, 50% in FY28
Plant and machinery ₹360 crore: 40% in FY27, 60% in FY28
Pre-operative and contingency 5% of buildings and P&M, capitalised to P&M
Put-to-use date 1 November 2027 (FY28, under 180 days)
Commercial operations From 1 November 2027; FY28 is a 5-month partial year
Utilisation ramp FY28 30% (annualised), FY29 60%, FY30 75%, FY31 onwards 85%
Realisation ₹1,95,000 per tonne in FY28, escalating 3% a year
Variable cost ₹1,30,000 per tonne in FY28, escalating 4% a year
Fixed cash costs ₹38 crore a year (FY28 pro-rated), escalating 6% a year
Maintenance capex 1.5% of cumulative P&M cost a year from FY30, added to the P&M block
NWC 20% of incremental revenue
Tax Sec 115BAA (25.168%); old-regime alternative 34.944% with 20% additional depreciation (switch)
Tax depreciation P&M 15% WDV, buildings 10% WDV, half-rate rule
Project horizon Operations to FY42 (15 years from COD)
Terminal values P&M and buildings salvage at 12% of gross cost; P&M block continues (c05 tail formula); land at market value less tax at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess on the gain over cost
State incentive Capital subsidy 10% of P&M cost, received in FY30 (reduces tax cost of P&M)
WACC 12.0% nominal; MIRR finance rate 10%, reinvestment rate 12%
Scenarios Bear: utilisation −10 points, realisation −7%, capex +15%, 6-month delay; Bull: +5 points, +4%, −5%, no delay

Required tabs

  1. Cover and contents: purpose, live scenario, version, checks summary.
  2. Inputs: every assumption above, colour-coded, with units and basis (nominal/real), plus the scenario table.
  3. Timeline: financial years, flags for construction, partial year, operations and terminal year.
  4. Capex and tax depreciation: capex by class and period; separate WDV schedules for P&M and buildings with half-rate and additional-depreciation flags; subsidy adjustment to actual cost.
  5. Operations: capacity, utilisation, volume, realisation, revenue, variable and fixed costs, EBITDA, book EBIT.
  6. Cash-flow schedule: tax on operating profit (using tax depreciation), ΔNWC, capex, subsidy, terminal flows, FCFF.
  7. Valuation: discount factors, PV, NPV (function and sum-of-PV check), IRR, MIRR, PI, discounted payback, peak funding, EAA.
  8. Break-even and sensitivity: accounting, cash and NPV break-even utilisation (Goal Seek, with the result pasted and dated); two two-way data tables; a tornado table for six inputs.
  9. Outputs: one-page output block and two charts (FCFF profile; cumulative undiscounted and discounted FCFF).

Required checks

  • Sum of ΔNWC over the life equals zero (WC fully released).
  • Tax WDV is never negative; closing WDV rolls to the next opening WDV.
  • NPV by function equals the sum of the PV row.
  • No interest or loan cash flows in FCFF.
  • Regime switch changes the tax rate, additional depreciation and MAT flag together.
  • Scenario switch changes all scenario drivers; the output block shows the live scenario name.
  • Break-even utilisation for NPV \(= 0\) is higher than accounting break-even utilisation.

Deliverable

An Excel workbook (.xlsx, no macros required) and a one-page memo to the CFO stating the base-case results, the range across scenarios, both break-even utilisations, the effect of the regime choice and of the state subsidy, and a recommendation with the three key risks.

Practice-ready checklist

  • I can compute a multi-product break-even using both unit-mix and revenue-mix methods and explain the difference Self-test: take a three-product case, compute BEP by the basket method and by weighted P/V, reconcile them, then show the different answer obtained when the same percentages are read as a revenue mix.
  • I can decompose an EPS change into DOL and DFL effects and find an EBIT–EPS indifference point Self-test: from two years of P&L data, build an EPS bridge separating volume (through DOL × DFL), price, cost, tax and share-count effects, then solve the indifference EBIT for a debt vs equity plan and verify EPS equality.
  • I can build a project model with Indian tax depreciation, WC release and after-tax salvage, and reconcile NPV to EAA Self-test: build an 8-year project with the half-rate rule and regime switch, confirm the ΔNWC sum is zero, apply the block rule to salvage, and show that EAA × annuity factor returns the NPV.
  • I can argue capex vs prepayment vs buyback using ROIC–WACC, after-tax Kd and earnings yield Self-test: given ROIC, WACC, the loan rate, the tax rate and the P/E, write a five-sentence recommendation that compares the returns of each use of ₹500 crore on a risk-adjusted basis.
  • I can estimate the rating-consistent debt capacity of a company from its EBITDA and covenant thresholds Self-test: from a company's EBITDA (with Ind AS 116 adjustments), existing net debt and a target Net Debt/EBITDA of 2.5x, compute the debt capacity, then check it against an interest-cover covenant at a downside EBITDA.
