Target-Profit Volume (Pre- and Post-Tax) and Cash Break-Even

Scope: Target-profit volume (pre-tax and post-tax) and cash break-even excluding non-cash fixed costs

Why it matters

Break-even is a floor. Budgets are set to hit a profit target, a return on capital or a debt-service requirement. FP&A teams are routinely asked "what volume do we need to deliver ₹50 crore PAT?" or "at what utilisation does the plant cover its cash costs and interest?" Lenders and TEV reports compute both accounting and cash break-even. The cash version is the number that tells you whether a plant in its ramp-up year can pay its bills without equity support. Getting the tax gross-up and the treatment of interest right is where most errors happen.

Core notes

Target pre-tax profit (EBIT)

\[ Q_{\text{target}} = \frac{F + \text{Target EBIT}}{P - V}, \qquad S_{\text{target}} = \frac{F + \text{Target EBIT}}{\text{P/V}} \]

where \(F\) is operating fixed costs. Target EBIT is simply treated as an extra fixed cost to be covered.

Target profit after tax (PAT)

PAT is after interest and tax. To get back to the contribution required, gross up PAT for tax and add interest:

\[ Q_{\text{target}} = \frac{F + I + \dfrac{\text{Target PAT}}{1 - t}}{P - V} \]

where \(I\) is interest (finance cost) and \(t\) the effective tax rate. If the target is "PAT after preference dividend", the equity earnings target is also grossed up: preference dividend is paid out of PAT, so add \(D_p \div (1 - t)\) as well.

Using the Indian rate: for a domestic company under Sec 115BAA, \(t = 25.168\%\) (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess). Under the old regime for a large company, \(t\) is typically 34.944% (30% + 12% surcharge + 4% cess). Smaller companies under the old regime may qualify for 25% plus surcharge and cess. MAT does not apply under 115BAA. Tax rates are Finance-Act-sensitive and should be confirmed each year.

Target return on sales or capital

  • Target margin: if the target is EBIT \(= m \times S\), then \(S = F \div (\text{P/V} - m)\). A solution exists only if \(m < \text{P/V}\).
  • Target ROCE: target EBIT \(= \text{ROCE} \times \text{Capital employed}\), then apply the EBIT formula.

Cash break-even

Fixed costs include non-cash items, mainly depreciation and amortisation (including right-of-use asset depreciation under Ind AS 116), and sometimes provisions such as gratuity accrual or ESOP expense. Cash break-even excludes them:

\[ Q_{\text{cash BEP}} = \frac{F - \text{Non-cash fixed costs}}{P - V} \]

Lenders often use an extended cash break-even that adds committed cash outflows:

\[ Q_{\text{cash BEP, lender}} = \frac{F - D + I + \text{Principal repayment} + \text{Lease payments not in } F}{P - V} \]

where \(D\) is depreciation. Under Ind AS 116 the lease cash outflow appears as interest plus principal on the lease liability. Tax is usually ignored at break-even (there is no profit), but advance tax and MAT history may matter in loss-making periods under the old regime.

Illustration

A Rajasthan solar-glass plant (one product) has the following annual data.

Item ₹ crore
Price per tonne (₹) 52,000
Variable cost per tonne (₹) 34,000
Fixed cash costs 45
Depreciation 27
Interest 18
Principal repayment 30
Tax rate (115BAA) 25.168%

CM per tonne \(= \text{₹}18{,}000\).

Break-even type Fixed amount to cover (₹ crore) Tonnes
Accounting BEP (EBIT = 0) 45 + 27 = 72 40,000
Cash BEP (operating) 45 25,000
Lender cash BEP (with debt service) 45 + 18 + 30 = 93 51,667
PBT = 0 72 + 18 = 90 50,000
Target PAT ₹30 crore 72 + 18 + 30 ÷ 0.74832 = 130.09 72,272

The ranking is informative. The plant covers operating cash at 25,000 tonnes but needs 51,667 tonnes to service debt from internal cash. Note that the lender cash BEP here exceeds the PBT break-even because principal (₹30 crore) is larger than depreciation (₹27 crore).

Excel implementation

Keep each break-even definition as a separate labelled row driven by the same inputs:

=(Fixed_Cash + Depreciation) / CM_unit                                   ' accounting BEP
=Fixed_Cash / CM_unit                                                    ' cash BEP
=(Fixed_Cash + Depreciation + Interest + Target_PAT/(1-Tax_Rate)) / CM_unit   ' target PAT volume
=Target_Volume / Installed_Capacity                                      ' required utilisation

Then convert each to a utilisation percentage against installed capacity. This links directly to the Week 21 exercise.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Losses brought forward: if unabsorbed depreciation or business losses are available for set-off (Sec 72, Sec 32(2)), the effective tax on the first rupees of profit is nil. A target-PAT volume computed with the full rate is then conservative. Under Sec 115BAA, brought-forward losses and unabsorbed depreciation attributable to additional depreciation and the specified deductions are deemed to have been given full effect and cannot be set off, so check which part of the carry-forward survives the switch.
  • Interest capitalised under Ind AS 23 during construction is not in the P&L, but it is still a cash outflow. Include it in lender cash break-even.
  • Non-cash items besides depreciation: ESOP cost (Ind AS 102) and provisions are non-cash in the year, but some (gratuity) become cash later. Disclose what you excluded.
  • Working capital: cash break-even on P&L items ignores WC build-up. A growing plant at "cash break-even" can still burn cash through receivables and inventory.
  • Target defined as EPS: convert to PAT (EPS \(\times\) shares), add preference dividend if any, gross up, then add interest.

Key takeaways

  • Target EBIT is treated as an extra fixed cost. Target PAT must be grossed up by \(1 - t\) and then interest added.
  • Use the regime-correct tax rate (25.168% under 115BAA for most domestic companies) and confirm it against the latest Finance Act.
  • Cash BEP excludes depreciation and other non-cash fixed costs. A lender's cash BEP adds interest and principal.
  • Present the full ladder (cash, accounting, PBT, debt-service, target) in utilisation terms.
  • Cash break-even on the P&L ignores working-capital investment.

Practice Questions

Q1. Target PAT volume [L1]

A company sells a product at ₹2,400 per unit with variable cost of ₹1,560. Fixed operating costs including depreciation are ₹21 crore, interest is ₹4.2 crore and the tax rate is 25.168%. Compute the units required to earn PAT of ₹12 crore.

Q2. Break-even ladder for a hospital [L2]

A 200-bed hospital in Lucknow earns average revenue of ₹38,000 per occupied bed-day with variable cost of ₹14,000 per bed-day. Annual fixed cash costs are ₹62 crore, depreciation (including right-of-use assets) is ₹18 crore, interest (including lease interest) is ₹9 crore and principal plus lease principal repayments are ₹14 crore. Compute the accounting, cash and lender-cash break-even occupancy percentages (365 days).

💡 Hint: Convert each break-even bed-day figure into occupancy by dividing by 200 × 365.

Q3. Target margin feasibility [L2]

A company with a P/V ratio of 28% and fixed costs of ₹84 crore wants an EBIT margin of 15%. Compute the required sales. Then explain what happens to the answer if the P/V ratio falls to 16%.

Q4. Regime choice and target volume [L3]

A company targets PAT of ₹40 crore. Under the old regime, its effective rate would be 34.944% but it has ₹25 crore of unabsorbed additional depreciation available for set-off this year; under 115BAA it would pay 25.168%. CM per unit is ₹6,000, fixed costs are ₹120 crore and interest is ₹20 crore. Explain how you would compute the target volume under each regime and what judgement points arise.

Q5. Cash break-even and working capital [L3]

A new plant reaches cash break-even utilisation of 45% in month 9, but the treasury team reports that the plant is still drawing on the cash-credit line. Explain the possible reasons and how you would redesign the break-even report to capture them.
