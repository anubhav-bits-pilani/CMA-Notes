Key concept

Cost Indifference Point Between Two Cost Structures

Concept: Indifference point between two cost structures = Δ Fixed costs ÷ Δ CM per unit

Statement

For two alternatives A and B producing the same output at the same price:

\[ Q^{*} = \frac{F_A - F_B}{\text{CM}_A - \text{CM}_B} = \frac{F_A - F_B}{V_B - V_A} \]

where \(F_A, F_B\) are the fixed costs of each alternative, \(\text{CM}_A, \text{CM}_B\) their contribution per unit, and \(V_A, V_B\) their variable costs per unit. At \(Q^{*}\), both alternatives give the same EBIT. Above \(Q^{*}\), the alternative with the higher fixed cost and higher CM (lower variable cost) is better. Below it, the lower-fixed-cost alternative is better. In rupees of sales: \(S^{*} = \Delta F \div \Delta \text{PV}\).

Explanation

Setting EBIT equal, \(\text{CM}_A Q - F_A = \text{CM}_B Q - F_B\), gives the formula. When prices are equal, the difference in CM is the difference in variable cost. This is the operating twin of the EBIT–EPS financial indifference point (t2 c05). The operating version trades fixed cost against variable cost; the financial one trades interest against the number of shares.

Typical decisions:

  • Automation vs manual: a robotic line (high \(F\), low \(V\)) vs manual assembly.
  • Own fleet vs third-party logistics: depreciation, drivers and insurance vs a per-tonne-km freight rate.
  • Captive power vs grid: a captive or group-captive solar plant with fixed O&M and depreciation vs a variable DISCOM tariff.
  • In-house vs contract manufacturing: asset-heavy vs asset-light (t2 s05).
  • Fixed salary vs commission for a sales force.

Conditions and cautions:

  • Price and quality are assumed equal. If the automated line improves yield or allows a price premium, include that in its CM.
  • The comparison is valid only within both alternatives' relevant ranges. A step cost in either changes \(Q^{*}\).
  • The choice is not only about expected volume. The higher-fixed-cost option has higher DOL and therefore more risk. If volume is uncertain and could fall below \(Q^{*}\), the lower-fixed-cost option carries an option value of flexibility.
  • For multi-year choices with capex, use NPV (t3) rather than a single-year indifference point. The one-year calculation is a screening tool.

Illustration

A Pune auto-component company compares two ways to machine a part, at a price of ₹1,200 per unit.

Manual cell (B) Robotic cell (A)
Fixed costs per year (₹ crore) 1.8 4.6
Variable cost per unit (₹) 820 610
CM per unit (₹) 380 590

\[ Q^{*} = \frac{4.6 - 1.8 \text{ (₹ crore)}}{590 - 380} = \frac{2{,}80{,}00{,}000}{210} = 1{,}33{,}333 \text{ units} \]

At 1,00,000 units: EBIT (B) \(= \text{₹}2.0\) crore; EBIT (A) \(= \text{₹}1.3\) crore. At 2,00,000 units: EBIT (B) \(= \text{₹}5.8\) crore; EBIT (A) \(= \text{₹}7.2\) crore. The DOL at 2,00,000 units is 1.31x for B and 1.64x for A. The robotic cell wins at high volume and is riskier.

Break-evens: B at 47,368 units, A at 77,966 units. A volume forecast of 1,50,000 units with a 30% downside risk (1,05,000 units) straddles \(Q^{*}\), and that is when the risk dimension decides.

Practice Questions

Q1. Own fleet vs 3PL [L1]

A Nagpur distributor can run its own fleet (fixed cost ₹3.2 crore a year, variable cost ₹18 per tonne-km) or use a 3PL at ₹42 per tonne-km with no fixed cost. Compute the indifference volume in tonne-km and state which option is better at 10 lakh tonne-km and at 20 lakh tonne-km.

Q2. Captive solar vs grid [L2]

A textile mill uses 2.4 crore kWh a year. A group-captive solar arrangement costs ₹2.6 crore a year fixed plus ₹1.10 per kWh variable, covering up to 2 crore kWh, with the balance bought from the DISCOM at ₹8.20 per kWh. The alternative is buying everything from the DISCOM at ₹8.20 per kWh. Compute the indifference consumption for the solar-covered portion and the annual saving at current consumption.

💡 Hint: Compare the two options only on the first 2 crore kWh; the remainder costs the same in both.

Q3. Risk vs expected volume [L3]

Using the robotic vs manual illustration in these notes, expected volume is 1,50,000 units, with a 30% chance of 1,00,000 units and a 70% chance of 1,71,429 units. Compute expected EBIT for each option and discuss whether expected EBIT alone should drive the choice.
