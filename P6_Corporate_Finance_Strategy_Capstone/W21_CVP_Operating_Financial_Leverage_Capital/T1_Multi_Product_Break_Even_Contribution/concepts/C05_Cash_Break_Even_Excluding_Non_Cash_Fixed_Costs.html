Key concept

Cash Break-Even Excluding Non-Cash Fixed Costs

Concept: Cash BEP = (Fixed costs − non-cash fixed costs such as depreciation) ÷ CM per unit

Statement

\[ Q_{\text{cash}} = \frac{F - N}{P - V}, \qquad S_{\text{cash}} = \frac{F - N}{\text{PV}} \]

where \(F\) is total fixed costs in the P&L, \(N\) is the non-cash part of fixed costs (depreciation and amortisation, including right-of-use asset depreciation; non-cash provisions and share-based payment expense where justified), \(P - V\) is contribution per unit, and PV is the P/V ratio. The result is the volume at which operating cash inflow from contribution just covers fixed cash costs.

Explanation

Accounting break-even (EBIT = 0) recovers all fixed costs, including depreciation, which is the systematic allocation of capex already spent. In the short run, a plant that covers its cash costs can keep operating even if it reports losses. That is the economics of keeping a plant running in a down-cycle. Cash BEP is therefore lower than accounting BEP by \(N \div (P - V)\).

Variants you will meet:

  • Operating cash BEP (the formula above): ignores interest, principal and tax.
  • Lender / debt-service cash BEP: adds interest and scheduled principal repayment (and lease payments) to the numerator, because those are committed cash outflows. TEV reports often show this as "cash break-even utilisation".
  • Shut-down point: in the short run, compare contribution with avoidable fixed cash costs only. Unavoidable costs (e.g. minimum demand charges, security, statutory maintenance) are incurred even if the plant is idle.

Cautions:

  • Cash BEP is a P&L-based approximation. It ignores working-capital movements, capex (maintenance capex is a real cash cost), and timing differences such as credit periods.
  • Under Ind AS 116, rent has been replaced by ROU depreciation and lease interest. Excluding ROU depreciation as "non-cash" understates cash break-even unless the lease payment is added back in the lender version.
  • Excluding gratuity or leave-encashment provisions as non-cash is defensible for one year, but these become cash liabilities later.

Illustration

A Vizag ferro-alloys plant: price ₹1,10,000 per tonne, variable cost ₹86,000 per tonne (CM ₹24,000). Annual fixed costs of ₹96 crore include depreciation of ₹30 crore and ROU depreciation of ₹4 crore on a leased land parcel (annual lease payment ₹5 crore, of which ₹1 crore is interest). Interest on term loans is ₹14 crore and principal due is ₹20 crore. Installed capacity is 60,000 tonnes.

Break-even Numerator (₹ crore) Tonnes Utilisation
Accounting (EBIT = 0) 96 40,000 66.7%
Operating cash 96 − 30 − 4 = 62 25,833 43.1%
Debt-service cash 62 + 14 + 20 + 5 = 101 42,083 70.1%

The debt-service cash BEP exceeds the accounting BEP because loan principal (₹20 crore) plus lease cash (₹5 crore) plus interest exceed the depreciation add-back. That is common in the early years of a project with a short loan tenor, and it is the reason lenders size repayments to the cash-flow profile and ask for a DSRA (t5).

Connections

This concept underpins s05 and the cash break-even rows in the Week 21 exercise. It links to t3 (operating cash flow in project appraisal includes the tax shield on depreciation, which is a separate effect from excluding depreciation from cash BEP), t2 s06 (Ind AS 116 reclassifies rent), and t5 (DSCR and debt sizing). In practice, plant heads use cash BEP to argue for keeping a unit running during a down-cycle, and FP&A should challenge whether maintenance capex and WC have been ignored.

Practice Questions

Q1. Accounting vs cash BEP [L1]

A unit sells at ₹900 per unit with variable cost of ₹540. Fixed costs are ₹10.8 crore including depreciation of ₹3.6 crore. Compute accounting BEP, cash BEP and the difference in units.

Q2. Lender cash BEP and Ind AS 116 [L2]

A retail chain's fixed costs of ₹220 crore include ROU depreciation of ₹48 crore and other depreciation of ₹22 crore. Lease payments are ₹62 crore (₹14 crore interest included in finance costs, not in fixed costs). Term loan interest is ₹10 crore and principal ₹15 crore. The P/V ratio is 38%. Compute operating cash BEP and debt-service cash BEP in rupees, explaining how you treated the lease.

💡 Hint: Remove both depreciation lines, then add back the full lease cash outflow along with loan debt service.

Q3. Shut-down decision [L3]

During a demand slump, a steel mini-mill can sell only 35% of capacity. Its cash BEP is at 42% utilisation, but ₹18 crore of its ₹60 crore fixed cash costs would continue even if it shut down for six months. Explain how you would decide whether to keep running, what data you need, and which non-financial factors (restart costs, workforce, customer retention) you would include.
