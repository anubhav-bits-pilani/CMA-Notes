Key concept

Weighted-Average CM per Unit and Composite-Unit BEP

Concept: Weighted-average CM per unit = Σ (CM per unit_i × unit mix %_i); BEP (composite units) = Fixed costs ÷ weighted-average CM per unit

Statement

\[ \overline{\text{CM}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(P_i - V_i\right) \times w_i, \qquad w_i = \frac{Q_i}{\sum_j Q_j} \]

\[ \text{BEP (composite units)} = \frac{F}{\overline{\text{CM}}}, \qquad \text{BEP}_i = \text{BEP (composite units)} \times w_i \]

where \(P_i\) and \(V_i\) are price and variable cost per unit of product \(i\), \(w_i\) is its share of total units (unit mix, summing to 1), \(F\) is total fixed costs relevant to the product set, and \(\overline{\text{CM}}\) is the weighted-average contribution per composite unit.

Explanation

The weighted-average CM per unit is the contribution earned by an "average" unit sold when products are sold in the stated physical proportions. Dividing fixed costs by it gives the total number of units, across all products, needed to break even. That total is then split back to products by the same unit mix. It is the basket method of s03 scaled so that one basket equals one unit.

Conditions for the formula to hold:

  • The mix is in units. Weights must be shares of physical quantity. Using revenue shares here mixes a rupee weight with a per-unit margin and gives a wrong answer (topic pitfall 1).
  • The mix is constant across the volume range, so every incremental unit is sold in the same proportions.
  • Prices, variable costs per unit and fixed costs are constant within the relevant range (no step costs, s08).
  • Units are commensurable. Adding tonnes of one product to numbers of another gives a "total units" figure with no physical meaning. Report product-level BEPs in that case.

The weighted CM is a mix-dependent number. Whenever the mix shifts towards products with higher CM per unit, the weighted CM rises and the composite BEP falls, even if no product's economics changed. This is the CVP counterpart of the sales-mix variance in Weeks 18/19.

Illustration

A Ludhiana bicycle manufacturer sells three models in a unit mix of 50% : 30% : 20%. Fixed costs are ₹18 crore.

Model Price (₹) Variable cost (₹) CM (₹) Unit mix Weighted CM (₹)
Kids 4,500 3,600 900 50% 450
Standard 7,000 5,250 1,750 30% 525
Geared 16,000 11,200 4,800 20% 960
Total 100% 1,935

BEP \(= 18{,}00{,}00{,}000 \div 1{,}935 = 93{,}023\) bicycles: 46,512 Kids, 27,907 Standard and 18,605 Geared.

If the mix moves to 40% : 30% : 30%, weighted CM becomes \(360 + 525 + 1{,}440 = \text{₹}2{,}325\) and BEP falls to 77,419 bicycles, a 17% reduction from a mix shift alone.

Connections

This concept is the unit-based half of the multi-product break-even (s02, s03). Its rupee-based counterpart is c02, and the two reconcile when the revenue mix is the one implied by this unit mix. It feeds MOS (s04, c03) and mix- shift scenarios (s07). In modelling, the same weighted-CM construction drives break-even utilisation for a multi-product plant in the Week 21 exercise. In FP&A variance work (Weeks 18/19), the difference between weighted CM at actual and budgeted mix, times actual total volume, is the sales-mix contribution variance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Weighted CM and composite BEP [L1]

A footwear brand sells sandals (CM ₹180), sports shoes (CM ₹650) and formal shoes (CM ₹900) in a unit mix of 5:3:2. Fixed costs are ₹26.6 crore. Compute the weighted-average CM per pair, composite BEP in pairs and BEP for each product.

Q2. Mix sensitivity [L2]

Using the bicycle illustration in these notes, the company expects the Kids share to rise to 60%, with Standard at 25% and Geared at 15%. Compute the new BEP, the percentage change from the base BEP, and explain the change to the CFO in two sentences.

💡 Hint: Recompute the weighted CM first; fixed costs are unchanged.

Q3. Incommensurable units [L2]

A fertiliser company sells urea (tonnes), a micronutrient spray (litres) and soil-testing services (number of tests). Explain why a composite BEP "in units" is misleading here and set out how you would present break-even instead.
