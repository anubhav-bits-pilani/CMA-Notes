Key concept

Rupee Break-Even Using the Weighted-Average P/V Ratio

Concept: BEP (₹) = Fixed costs ÷ weighted-average P/V ratio, where weighted P/V = Σ (P/V_i × revenue mix %_i) = Total CM ÷ Total sales

Statement

\[ \text{BEP (₹)} = \frac{F}{\overline{\text{PV}}}, \qquad \overline{\text{PV}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{PV}_i \times s_i = \frac{\sum_i (P_i - V_i) Q_i}{\sum_i P_i Q_i} \]

where \(F\) is total fixed costs, \(\text{PV}_i = (P_i - V_i) \div P_i\) is the P/V ratio of product \(i\), \(s_i = P_i Q_i \div \sum_j P_j Q_j\) is its share of total revenue, and \(\overline{\text{PV}}\) is the weighted-average P/V ratio of the portfolio.

Explanation

The weighted P/V ratio is the contribution earned per rupee of sales at the stated revenue mix. It is a revenue-weighted average of individual P/V ratios, which simplifies algebraically to total contribution divided by total sales:

\[ \sum_i \frac{(P_i - V_i)}{P_i} \times \frac{P_i Q_i}{\sum_j P_j Q_j} = \frac{\sum_i (P_i - V_i) Q_i}{\sum_j P_j Q_j} \]

That identity is why the shortcut "total CM \(\div\) total sales" works. It is also why you can apply this method to published financial statements: revenue and an estimate of variable costs are enough, without unit data.

Conditions and cautions:

  • Weights must be revenue shares. Weighting P/V ratios by unit shares is the mirror of the error in c01 and overweights low-priced products.
  • The revenue mix is assumed constant as sales scale towards break-even.
  • If prices change (a price-cut scenario), the revenue mix changes even with the same physical mix. Recompute \(\overline{\text{PV}}\) from total CM and total sales after the change.
  • Segment-level fixed costs that are avoidable belong to segment break-evens. Common fixed costs belong to the company-level BEP. Allocated corporate overheads should not be treated as avoidable at product level (topic pitfall 2).

The result is in rupees. To get product-level break-even revenue, multiply BEP (₹) by each \(s_i\). To get units, divide by each price.

Illustration

An NSE-listed QSR chain reports three revenue streams and fixed costs of ₹210 crore.

Stream Revenue (₹ crore) Revenue mix Variable cost ratio P/V Weighted P/V
Dine-in 420 60% 58% 42% 25.2%
Delivery (aggregator) 210 30% 74% 26% 7.8%
Catering 70 10% 65% 35% 3.5%
Total 700 100% 36.5%

Check: total CM \(= 176.4 + 54.6 + 24.5 = \text{₹}255.5\) crore; \(255.5 \div 700 = 36.5\%\). ✓

BEP \(= 210 \div 0.365 = \text{₹}575.3\) crore. MOS \(= (700 - 575.3) \div 700 = 17.8\%\).

If delivery rises to 45% of revenue (dine-in 45%, catering 10%), weighted P/V becomes \(18.9\% + 11.7\% + 3.5\% = 34.1\%\) and BEP rises to ₹615.8 crore. The shift to aggregator channels, with their commissions, raises break-even by about ₹40 crore.

Connections

This is the rupee-based counterpart of c01 and the second method in s03. It underpins MOS in rupees (s04, c03), target-sales calculations (s05), and the mix-shift scenarios of s07. It is also the method you apply when working from Ind AS 108 segment notes (Week 7), where volumes are not disclosed. In FP&A, the weighted P/V is the "margin mix" KPI tracked in monthly reviews, and its movement explains why revenue growth does not always translate into EBIT growth.

Practice Questions

Q1. Weighted P/V from segment data [L1]

A company has three products with revenue of ₹120 crore, ₹80 crore and ₹50 crore and P/V ratios of 25%, 40% and 55%. Fixed costs are ₹78 crore. Compute the weighted P/V ratio, BEP in rupees and break-even revenue for each product at the current mix.

Q2. Channel-mix shift [L2]

Using the QSR illustration in these notes, management targets a 20% MOS next year with total revenue of ₹800 crore and fixed costs of ₹230 crore. What minimum weighted P/V is needed, and what does that imply for the maximum delivery share if dine-in and catering P/V ratios are unchanged and catering stays at 10%?

💡 Hint: MOS of 20% fixes BEP at 80% of sales; then solve for the delivery weight.

Q3. Wrong weights [L2]

An analyst computes weighted P/V by weighting each product's P/V by its unit share. Products: A sells 8 lakh units at ₹250 (P/V 20%) and B sells 2 lakh units at ₹2,000 (P/V 45%). Fixed costs are ₹30 crore. Compute BEP using the analyst's method and the correct method, and quantify the error.
