Key concept

Target Volume for a Post-Tax Profit Goal

Concept: Target volume (post-tax) = [Fixed costs + Target PAT ÷ (1 − t)] ÷ CM per unit (add interest if target is PAT, not EBIT)

Statement

\[ Q_{\text{target}} = \frac{F + I + \dfrac{\text{PAT}^{*}}{1 - t}}{P - V} \]

where \(F\) is operating fixed costs (including depreciation), \(I\) is interest (finance costs), \(\text{PAT}^{*}\) is target profit after tax, \(t\) is the effective tax rate, and \(P - V\) is contribution per unit. For a target in rupees of sales, replace the denominator with the P/V ratio.

If the target is stated as EBIT, drop both \(I\) and the gross-up: \(Q = (F + \text{EBIT}^{*}) \div (P - V)\). If the target is equity earnings after preference dividend \(D_p\), the numerator becomes \(F + I + (\text{PAT}^{*} + D_p) \div (1 - t)\).

Explanation

Contribution must cover, in order: operating fixed costs, then interest, then tax, and leave the target PAT. Interest is deductible, so it is added before the gross-up and is not grossed up itself. PAT is after tax, so the pre-tax profit needed is PAT \(\div (1 - t)\).

The brief's wording "add interest if target is PAT, not EBIT" captures the most frequent error. People gross up PAT and divide by CM, but forget that interest sits between EBIT and PBT. The result understates the required volume, and the understatement grows with leverage.

Rate choice in India:

  • Sec 115BAA regime: 22% base + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%, with no MAT and no additional depreciation or specified deductions.
  • Old regime, large company: 30% + 12% surcharge + 4% cess = 34.944% (MAT at 15% plus surcharge and cess may bind).
  • Sec 115BAB (new manufacturing companies) was a 15% base rate regime, available only for companies that began manufacturing by 31 March 2024; confirm applicability before using it.

Use the effective rate that will actually apply in the target year, after considering loss set-offs and the regime choice. Rates and section numbers are Finance-Act- and Income-tax Act 2025-sensitive; the Income-tax Act 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections, so confirm the mapping.

Illustration

An Indore agro-processing company: price ₹3,200 per quintal, variable cost ₹2,400, CM ₹800. Operating fixed costs are ₹24 crore (including depreciation of ₹6 crore), interest is ₹5 crore, and the company is under 115BAA. Target PAT is ₹15 crore.

Step ₹ crore
Target PAT 15.00
Gross up: 15 ÷ (1 − 0.25168) 20.04
Add interest 5.00
Add operating fixed costs 24.00
Required contribution 49.04
Required volume: 49.04 crore ÷ ₹800 6.13 lakh quintals

Omitting interest would give \(44.04 \div 800 = 5.51\) lakh quintals, understating the target by about 0.63 lakh quintals (10%).

Connections

This is the formula behind s05 (target-profit volume). It links to t2's financial leverage: the interest term is what DFL magnifies. It also links to t3, where the tax rate under 115BAA vs the old regime affects both the target volume and the project's tax shield (t3 s04). In FP&A, target-volume calculations back up the top-down budget negotiation: "to deliver the PAT guidance, the plant must run at X% utilisation".

Practice Questions

Q1. Target PAT with interest [L1]

A company sells at ₹650 per unit with variable cost of ₹455. Fixed costs are ₹11.7 crore and interest is ₹1.95 crore. At a tax rate of 25.168%, compute the units required for PAT of ₹7.5 crore.

Q2. EPS target with preference shares [L2]

A company has 2 crore equity shares and ₹40 crore of 8% non-cumulative preference shares classified as equity under Ind AS 32. It targets EPS of ₹6. CM per unit is ₹1,250, fixed costs are ₹55 crore, interest is ₹9 crore and the tax rate is 25.168%. Compute the target volume.

💡 Hint: Convert the EPS target into PAT for equity holders, then add the preference dividend before grossing up.

Q3. Regime comparison [L2]

Using the Indore illustration in these notes, recompute the required volume under the old regime at 34.944% (ignore MAT and assume no loss set-offs). Explain in two sentences why the regime choice matters more for high-PAT targets.
