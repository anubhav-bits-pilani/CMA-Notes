Key concept

Margin of Safety, the DOL Reciprocal and Profit Identity

Concept: Margin of safety % = (Actual sales − BEP sales) ÷ Actual sales = 1 ÷ DOL; Profit = MOS (₹) × P/V ratio

Statement

\[ \text{MOS \%} = \frac{S - S_{\text{BEP}}}{S} = \frac{1}{\text{DOL}}, \qquad \text{EBIT} = \text{MOS (₹)} \times \text{PV} \]

where \(S\) is actual (or budgeted) sales, \(S_{\text{BEP}}\) is break-even sales, \(\text{MOS (₹)} = S - S_{\text{BEP}}\), PV is the P/V ratio, and DOL \(=\) contribution \(\div\) EBIT at sales level \(S\).

Explanation

Profit identity. EBIT \(= S \times \text{PV} - F\). At break-even, \(F = S_{\text{BEP}} \times \text{PV}\). Substituting gives EBIT \(= (S - S_{\text{BEP}}) \times \text{PV}\). Only sales above break-even generate profit, and each such rupee generates PV rupees of EBIT. Sales up to break-even merely recover fixed costs.

DOL reciprocal. DOL \(= \dfrac{S \times \text{PV}}{(S - S_{\text{BEP}}) \times \text{PV}} = \dfrac{S}{S - S_{\text{BEP}}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{MOS \%}}\). The PV cancels, so the identity holds regardless of the margin level.

Conditions:

  • Linear CVP within the relevant range: constant price, variable cost per unit and fixed costs. With step costs (s08), the BEP must be the one for the band in which actual sales fall.
  • For a multi-product firm, \(S_{\text{BEP}}\) must be computed at the same mix as actual sales. Otherwise both identities break.
  • DOL here is operating leverage only. Fixed costs exclude interest (t2 pitfall 1).
  • Below break-even, MOS is negative and the reciprocal gives a negative DOL, which is mathematically valid but not a useful sensitivity multiplier.

The practical power of the identity is that one number answers two questions. A 20% MOS means sales can fall 20% before EBIT is zero, and every 1% change in sales moves EBIT by 5%.

Illustration

A Noida mobile-accessories maker has sales of ₹150 crore, a P/V ratio of 32% and fixed costs of ₹36 crore.

Measure Calculation Result
BEP sales 36 ÷ 0.32 ₹112.5 crore
MOS (₹) 150 − 112.5 ₹37.5 crore
MOS % 37.5 ÷ 150 25%
EBIT via identity 37.5 × 0.32 ₹12.0 crore
EBIT via P&L 150 × 0.32 − 36 ₹12.0 crore ✓
DOL 48 ÷ 12, or 1 ÷ 0.25 4.0x

A 5% sales increase should therefore lift EBIT by 20%: new EBIT \(= 157.5 \times 0.32 - 36 = \text{₹}14.4\) crore, which is +20%. ✓

Connections

This concept ties s04 (MOS) to t2 (DOL as a point elasticity, t2 c06). It underpins the volume-shock tables of s07, where the MOS tells you the shock that eliminates EBIT. In credit analysis, a thin MOS signals high business risk, which constrains debt capacity (t5). In the Week 21 exercise, the gap between planned utilisation and break-even utilisation is the MOS in capacity terms.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identity check [L1]

A company has sales of ₹90 crore and EBIT of ₹9 crore, with a P/V ratio of 30%. Compute MOS (₹), MOS %, BEP sales and DOL, and verify that DOL equals contribution divided by EBIT.

Q2. Reverse-engineering a peer [L2]

A peer reports that a 10% fall in revenue last year cut EBIT by 35%, with no change in prices or cost structure. Revenue before the fall was ₹480 crore. Estimate the peer's MOS % before the fall, its break-even revenue and explain what additional data would let you estimate fixed costs.

💡 Hint: The observed EBIT sensitivity gives DOL at the starting level.

Q3. Mix inconsistency [L2]

A company reports a BEP of ₹200 crore computed at the budgeted mix (weighted P/V 40%). Actual sales were ₹260 crore at a weighted P/V of 34%, with fixed costs as budgeted. Show that EBIT \(\neq\) MOS (₹) \(\times\) P/V if the budgeted BEP is used, and correct it.
