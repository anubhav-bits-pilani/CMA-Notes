DCL and Decomposing an EPS Swing Into Its Drivers

Scope: DCL and EPS volatility: decomposing an EPS swing into operating and financial drivers

Why it matters

When a company reports "revenue up 8%, EPS up 31%", analysts, investor-relations teams and boards want to know why. Part of the answer is operating leverage (fixed operating costs magnify sales changes into EBIT changes). Part is financial leverage (fixed financing charges magnify EBIT changes into EPS changes). The rest comes from things leverage does not explain: price and cost changes, tax-rate changes, share-count changes, exceptional items and other income. The degree of combined leverage (DCL) gives the expected EPS response to a volume-driven sales change. Decomposing an actual EPS swing into these drivers is a standard FP&A bridge and a common interview task.

Core notes

Definition

\[ \text{DCL} = \text{DOL} \times \text{DFL} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{EBIT} - I - \dfrac{D_p}{1 - t}} = \frac{\%\Delta\text{EPS}}{\%\Delta\text{Sales}} \]

where \(I\) is interest, \(D_p\) equity-classified preference dividend and \(t\) the tax rate. DCL is a point measure at the base level, like DOL (s01). The denominator is the pre-tax profit available to equity shareholders after preference dividend gross-up. When it approaches zero, DCL and EPS volatility explode.

Two-stage chain

\[ \%\Delta\text{EPS} = \text{DFL} \times \%\Delta\text{EBIT} = \text{DFL} \times \text{DOL} \times \%\Delta\text{Sales} \]

This chain holds exactly for volume-driven changes when price, variable cost per unit, fixed costs, interest, tax rate and share count are all unchanged. In real results, several of those move, so an actual EPS bridge has more steps.

Decomposing an actual EPS swing

A practical bridge from EPS\(_0\) to EPS\(_1\):

  1. Volume effect on EBIT: \(\Delta Q \times \text{CM}_0\) per unit (or \(\Delta S_{\text{vol}} \times \text{PV}_0\)).
  2. Price effect: \(\Delta P \times Q_1\), which flows fully to EBIT.
  3. Variable-cost effect: \(-\Delta V \times Q_1\).
  4. Mix effect (multi-product): change in weighted CM at actual volume.
  5. Fixed-cost effect: \(-\Delta F\).
  6. Finance-cost effect: \(-\Delta I\) (including Ind AS 116 lease interest changes).
  7. Tax-rate effect: PBT\(_1 \times (t_0 - t_1)\).
  8. Share-count effect: PAT\(_1 \div N_1 - \text{PAT}_1 \div N_0\) (buybacks, issues, ESOP exercises).
  9. Other: other income, exceptional items, share of associates.

Convert each EBIT effect into EPS by multiplying by \((1 - t) \div N\). The sum must reconcile to the actual change. The leverage-driven part is steps 1 and 4 amplified through the fixed operating and financing charges. The rest is not leverage.

Illustration

A listed auto-ancillary (all figures ₹ crore, shares in crore):

Item FY25 FY26
Volume (lakh units) 100 110
Price per unit (₹) 1,000 1,000
Variable cost per unit (₹) 700 700
Sales 1,000 1,100
Contribution 300 330
Fixed costs 180 180
EBIT 120 150
Interest 40 40
PBT 80 110
Tax (25.168%) 20.13 27.68
PAT 59.87 82.32
Shares 10 10
EPS (₹) 5.99 8.23

At FY25: DOL \(= 300 \div 120 = 2.5\); DFL \(= 120 \div 80 = 1.5\); DCL \(= 300 \div 80 = 3.75\).

Sales +10% gives predicted EPS change \(= 3.75 \times 10\% = 37.5\%\). Actual: \(8.23 \div 5.99 - 1 = 37.5\%\). ✓ The decomposition: of the 37.5% EPS rise, 10% is the sales change itself, operating leverage turns it into 25% EBIT growth, and financial leverage turns 25% into 37.5%.

Now suppose instead that in FY26 the company also took a 2% price increase (price ₹1,020) and bought back 0.5 crore shares. EBIT becomes \(150 + 110 \times 20 \div 100 = \text{₹}172\) crore (the price increase adds ₹22 crore). PBT ₹132 crore, PAT ₹98.78 crore, EPS \(= 98.78 \div 9.5 = \text{₹}10.40\), up 73.7%. The bridge:

Driver EPS effect (₹)
FY25 EPS 5.99
Volume, through DOL and DFL +2.25
Price increase: 22 × 0.74832 ÷ 10 +1.65
Share-count reduction: 98.78 ÷ 9.5 − 98.78 ÷ 10 +0.52
FY26 EPS (difference of ₹0.01 is rounding) 10.40

Only the first ₹2.25 is the "leverage" story. Sales grew 12.2% (to ₹1,122 crore), so quoting a "DCL" of 6.0 (73.7% EPS growth ÷ 12.2% sales growth) as the company's combined leverage would be wrong, because price and share-count effects are mixed in. The true DCL at the FY25 base is still 3.75.

Excel implementation

Build the bridge as a waterfall with each step computed from inputs, and a check cell that the sum equals the actual EPS change. In Power BI, use a waterfall visual with a "Driver" dimension table and DAX measures for each effect. Keep the order of steps fixed and documented, because the size of interaction effects (price × volume) depends on the order.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Order dependence: price measured on new volume vs old volume moves the interaction term between steps. State the convention (volume at old price, then price at new volume is common).
  • Diluted vs basic EPS: use the same basis in both years. Convertibles can change from anti-dilutive to dilutive as EPS rises.
  • Tax-rate changes: a switch to Sec 115BAA (from 34.944% to 25.168%) lifts EPS by about 15% at the same PBT. This is not leverage; show it separately.
  • Exceptional items and other income: strip them out to compute "core" EPS before applying leverage.
  • Negative base EPS: percentage changes are meaningless. Show rupee bridges only.

Key takeaways

  • DCL \(=\) DOL \(\times\) DFL \(=\) contribution \(\div\) \([\text{EBIT} - I - D_p \div (1-t)]\) \(=\) %ΔEPS \(\div\) %Δsales.
  • The chain %ΔEPS \(=\) DFL \(\times\) DOL \(\times\) %Δsales holds exactly only for volume changes with everything else constant.
  • A real EPS bridge separates volume, price, cost, mix, fixed cost, finance cost, tax rate and share count.
  • Do not label an observed %ΔEPS \(\div\) %Δsales as DCL when price, tax or share count changed.
  • Build bridges with a reconciliation check and a documented step order.

Practice Questions

Q1. DCL and EPS prediction [L1]

A company has contribution of ₹450 crore, fixed costs of ₹270 crore, interest of ₹60 crore and no preference shares. Compute DOL, DFL and DCL, and the expected percentage change in EPS for a 6% fall in sales volume.

Q2. EPS bridge [L2]

A consumer company's FY26 EPS rose from ₹12.00 to ₹16.20. Data: volume +8% at a constant CM per unit, a price increase that added ₹18 crore to EBIT, fixed costs up ₹10 crore, interest down ₹6 crore, tax rate constant at 25.168%, shares constant at 15 crore. FY25 contribution was ₹300 crore. Build the EPS bridge and identify any unexplained residual.

💡 Hint: Convert each EBIT-level effect to EPS using (1 − t) ÷ shares, then compare the sum with the actual change.

Q3. Tax regime switch [L2]

A company moves from the old regime (34.944%) to Sec 115BAA (25.168%) in a year when PBT is unchanged at ₹200 crore and shares are 25 crore. Compute the EPS change and explain why this should be excluded from any leverage analysis.

Q4. Board explanation [L3]

A capital-goods company reports sales +5% and EPS −18%. Interest rose after a debt-funded capex, steel costs rose, and depreciation on the new plant started. Draft the structure of an EPS bridge for the board and the narrative explaining how leverage magnified the cost pressures.
