Subtopic note

Ind AS 116 Effect on EBITDA, Fixed Charges and DFL

Scope: Ind AS 116 effect: rent becomes depreciation + interest, raising fixed charges and DFL, and lifting EBITDA

Why it matters

From 1 April 2019, Ind AS 116 brought almost all leases onto the lessee's balance sheet. For retailers, QSR chains, hospitals, airlines, telecom operators (tower leases), logistics companies and IT services firms with large office leases, reported EBITDA rose sharply, finance costs rose and net debt (if leases are included) rose. Nothing about the cash economics changed. Leverage ratios, interest cover, DOL and DFL computed from reported numbers therefore shift, and comparisons across periods, across peers, and with covenants defined on pre-116 bases (frozen GAAP) can be badly distorted. This is a recurring theme in credit analysis, valuation multiples and FP&A reporting.

Core notes

Accounting mechanics (lessee)

At commencement, the lessee recognises:

  • a lease liability at the present value of lease payments not yet paid, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease or, if not readily determinable, the lessee's incremental borrowing rate; and
  • a right-of-use (ROU) asset at the lease liability plus initial direct costs, prepaid payments and restoration costs, less incentives.

Subsequently, the ROU asset is depreciated (usually straight-line over the shorter of lease term and useful life), and the liability accrues interest (effective interest method) and is reduced by payments.

Journal Dr Cr
Commencement ROU asset Lease liability
Monthly / annual interest Finance cost Lease liability
Depreciation Depreciation expense Accumulated depreciation (ROU)
Payment Lease liability Bank

Exemptions: short-term leases (12 months or less) and leases of low-value assets may be expensed on a straight-line basis (practical expedient). Variable lease payments not based on an index or rate (for example, revenue-share rents in malls) stay in operating expenses.

Effect on the P&L lines

Line Pre-Ind AS 116 (operating lease) Post-Ind AS 116
Rent expense (in other expenses) Full rent Removed (except short-term, low-value, variable)
EBITDA After rent Higher, by the fixed rent
Depreciation Own assets + ROU depreciation
EBIT After rent Slightly higher (rent − ROU depreciation ≈ the interest element)
Finance cost Debt interest + lease interest
PBT After rent Lower in early years, higher in later years (front-loaded interest)
Cash flow statement Rent in operating Principal in financing, interest in financing or operating per policy (Ind AS 7)

Over the lease term, total expense is the same. In the early years, interest plus depreciation exceeds rent.

Effect on leverage measures

DOL: the fixed rent was already a fixed operating cost. Post-116 it is split into ROU depreciation (still fixed operating) and interest (now a financing charge). Fixed operating costs fall by the interest element, so EBIT rises and DOL falls slightly.

DFL: interest now includes lease interest, so the denominator of DFL (EBIT − I) falls relative to EBIT, and DFL rises.

DCL: contribution \(\div\) (EBIT − I) is broadly unchanged, because the total fixed charge is the same in total (apart from front-loading). Combined leverage, the economically meaningful number, barely moves.

EBITDA-based metrics: EBITDA rises by the full rent. Net Debt/EBITDA falls if leases are excluded from debt (inconsistent; t5 pitfall), and may rise or fall if leases are included (the liability, at roughly 5–8 times annual rent for long leases, is added to the numerator, and rent is added to the denominator). EBITDA ÷ interest generally falls.

Illustration

A Bengaluru QSR chain with 300 stores, annual fixed store rent of ₹90 crore, lease liability at year start of ₹480 crore (IBR 9%), ROU depreciation of ₹62 crore. Other data: revenue ₹1,500 crore, contribution ₹600 crore, other fixed costs ₹380 crore (including own-asset depreciation of ₹50 crore), bank interest ₹10 crore.

In this year, PBT is lower post-116 because interest plus depreciation (₹105.2 crore) exceeds rent (₹90 crore), which is typical early in a lease portfolio, so DCL is slightly higher. The headline shifts (EBITDA +50%, DFL from 1.08 to 1.51, EBITDA interest cover from 18x to 5.1x) are mostly presentation.

Analyst adjustments

  • Like-for-like comparison: either restate everyone to post-116 (add lease liabilities to net debt, use post-116 EBITDA) or to pre-116 (deduct lease payments from EBITDA, use "EBITDA after rent", exclude lease liabilities). Do not mix.
  • EBITDAR (EBITDA before rent) has been used historically for airlines and hotels. Post-116, EBITDA is close to EBITDAR for capitalised leases.
  • Covenants: many loan agreements define EBITDA and debt on "frozen GAAP" or explicitly exclude lease liabilities. Read the definition before testing headroom.
  • Valuation: if you use post-116 EBITDA in EV/EBITDA, include lease liabilities in EV, and in DCF either treat lease payments as operating (pre-116 FCFF) or treat leases as debt consistently.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Variable rents (revenue share in malls) stay in operating costs, so two retailers with different rent structures report very different EBITDA uplifts.
  • Lease term judgement (extension and termination options reasonably certain to be exercised) changes the liability, depreciation and interest.
  • Sale-and-leaseback and lease modifications can create one-off gains or remeasurements. Strip these from leverage analysis.
  • Tax: for income tax, the rent paid is generally the deductible amount, not depreciation plus interest, so deferred tax arises. The tax shield in models should follow tax law, not the Ind AS split.
  • Lessors are largely unaffected (classification into finance and operating leases continues).

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 116 replaces rent with ROU depreciation plus lease interest; total expense over the term is unchanged.
  • EBITDA rises by the rent; EBIT rises a little; finance cost rises; PBT falls early in the lease.
  • DOL falls slightly, DFL rises, and DCL is broadly unchanged: the reclassification is mostly presentation.
  • Compare peers and periods on a consistent basis: either all post-116 with leases in net debt, or all pre-116.
  • Check covenant definitions (frozen GAAP) before testing leverage headroom.

Practice Questions

Q1. Restating a retailer [L1]

A retailer reports post-116 EBITDA of ₹420 crore, ROU depreciation of ₹110 crore, lease interest of ₹70 crore and lease payments of ₹160 crore. Compute pre-116-equivalent EBITDA (EBITDA after rent) and explain why it differs from EBITDA minus ROU depreciation minus lease interest.

Q2. Leverage measures pre and post [L2]

A hospital chain has contribution of ₹900 crore, fixed costs excluding rent of ₹520 crore, rent of ₹120 crore and bank interest of ₹40 crore. Under Ind AS 116, ROU depreciation would be ₹85 crore and lease interest ₹50 crore. Compute DOL, DFL and DCL under both presentations, and explain which number you would use when comparing with an asset-owning peer.

💡 Hint: Total fixed charges differ only by the front-loading difference between rent and depreciation plus interest.

Q3. Peer comparison [L2]

Peer A owns its stores; Peer B leases all its stores. Both report Net Debt/EBITDA, but Peer B excludes lease liabilities from net debt while reporting post-116 EBITDA. Explain the bias and design a consistent comparison table with the adjustments.

Q4. Covenant definition [L3]

A loan agreement signed in 2017 defines EBITDA "in accordance with the accounting principles applied in the borrower's audited financial statements for FY17" and caps Total Debt/EBITDA at 3.0x. The borrower, a QSR chain, now reports post-116 numbers. Explain how you would compute the covenant, what information you would request from the company, and how you would present headroom to the lender.
