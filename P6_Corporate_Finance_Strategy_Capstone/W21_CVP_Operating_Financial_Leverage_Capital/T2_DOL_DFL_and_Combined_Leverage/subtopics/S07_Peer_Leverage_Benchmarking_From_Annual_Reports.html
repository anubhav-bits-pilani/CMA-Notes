Peer Leverage Benchmarking From Annual Reports

Scope: Peer leverage benchmarking from annual reports (cost structure notes, finance cost notes)

Why it matters

Internal MIS gives you your own company's fixed/variable split. For peers, competitors and acquisition targets you must estimate leverage from published annual reports. Equity research, credit rating rationales, FP&A competitive benchmarking and M&A screening all rely on this outside-in estimate. The main sources in India are the Schedule III statement of profit and loss (expenses by nature), the notes on cost of materials consumed, employee benefits, finance costs, depreciation and "other expenses", the segment note (Ind AS 108), the lease note (Ind AS 116) and the borrowings note. The skill is turning these disclosures into a defensible estimate of DOL, DFL and DCL and making peers comparable.

Core notes

Step 1: assemble the cost base by nature

Schedule III Division II requires expenses by nature: cost of materials consumed, purchases of stock-in-trade, changes in inventories, employee benefits expense, finance costs, depreciation and amortisation, and other expenses. The "other expenses" note breaks out power and fuel, stores and spares, repairs, rent (short-term or variable leases post-116), freight, advertising, CSR expenditure (Sec 135), legal and professional fees, and others above a materiality threshold (Schedule III requires separate disclosure of any item exceeding 1% of revenue or ₹10 lakh, whichever is higher).

Step 2: classify each line as fixed, variable or semi-variable

A typical classification for an Indian manufacturer:

Line (Schedule III / notes) Usual behaviour Comment
Cost of materials consumed, purchases, change in inventories Variable Adjust for inventory change to match production to sales
Power and fuel Mostly variable Fixed demand charges are a small fixed part
Freight and forwarding Variable
Packing materials Variable
Commission, discounts not netted in revenue Variable
Employee benefits Largely fixed (permanent staff); variable for incentives and contract labour Contract labour may sit in "other expenses"
Stores, spares, repairs Semi-variable Often split 50:50 absent better information
Advertising Discretionary fixed
Depreciation and amortisation Fixed Includes ROU depreciation post-116
Rent (residual, post-116) Fixed or variable (revenue share) Read the lease note
CSR expense Fixed (based on 2% of past average profits)
Insurance, rates and taxes, legal, IT Fixed

Refine using management commentary (MD&A), investor presentations (often show cost per tonne breakdowns), rating rationales and, where available, quarterly results. Quarterly data across a cycle allow a regression of total cost on revenue (t1 s01) as a cross-check of the variable cost ratio.

Step 3: compute operating leverage

Contribution \(=\) revenue \(-\) estimated variable costs; EBIT \(=\) contribution \(-\) fixed costs (including depreciation). Reconcile EBIT to reported "profit before exceptional items and tax" plus finance costs minus other income. Then DOL \(=\) contribution \(\div\) EBIT. Exclude other income (treasury income) from EBIT for DOL: it is not part of operations.

Step 4: financial leverage from the finance cost note

The finance cost note typically shows interest on borrowings, interest on lease liabilities, other borrowing costs (processing fees amortised through EIR), unwinding of discounts on provisions, and exchange differences regarded as an adjustment to borrowing costs (Ind AS 23). For DFL:

  • Include interest on borrowings, lease interest (if analysing on a post-116 basis) and EIR-amortised fees.
  • Consider excluding unwinding of discount on provisions (decommissioning, mine closure), which is not a financing charge in the DFL sense. State your choice.
  • Add back interest capitalised (the borrowings note or the CWIP/PPE note discloses it under Ind AS 23) if you want the economic burden. Note that reported DFL omits it.
  • Identify equity-classified preference dividends from the statement of changes in equity and gross them up (s02).

Step 5: normalise and compare

  • Use mid-cycle or average figures for cyclicals.
  • Put all peers on the same Ind AS 116 basis (s06).
  • Strip exceptional items, impairment and one-off gains.
  • Check year ends and accounting policies (e.g. capitalisation of borrowing costs, componentisation).
  • Express results in a comparison table with sources noted for each figure.

Illustration: two cement peers (₹ crore)

Item Peer A Peer B
Revenue from operations 18,000 6,500
Variable costs (materials, power and fuel, freight, packing) 11,700 4,420
Contribution (P/V) 6,300 (35.0%) 2,080 (32.0%)
Fixed costs: employees, other fixed, depreciation 3,600 1,430
EBIT (excluding other income) 2,700 650

PBT before exceptional items, excluding other income 2,400 360
DOL 2.33 3.20
DFL 1.13 1.81

Peer B, the smaller regional player that has expanded with debt, has more than twice the EPS sensitivity. A 10% volume decline would cut Peer B's PBT by about 58% against 26% for Peer A.

Building it in Excel / Power BI

Set up one sheet per peer with source references (annual report page and note number) beside every input, a classification table with a fixed share (0–100%) per line, and a summary sheet that computes the leverage metrics. In Power BI, a table of peers × metrics with conditional formatting gives a quick visual benchmark. Use Power Query to import standardised financials from a data provider (where licensed) and apply the classification table as a merge.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Inventory changes: when production differs from sales, materials consumed differ from the cost of materials in goods sold. Use the "changes in inventories" line to adjust, or the variable cost ratio is distorted.
  • Captive power shifts costs from power purchase (variable) to depreciation and employee costs (fixed), raising DOL. Read the MD&A before comparing.
  • Conglomerates: use segment data to separate businesses with different profiles.
  • Related-party transactions (Ind AS 24): royalty to the parent or purchases from group companies may not be at arm's length and can distort cost behaviour.
  • Restatements and reclassifications across years: use the latest comparative figures.

Key takeaways

  • Start from Schedule III expenses by nature and the supporting notes, and classify each line as fixed, variable or semi-variable.
  • Exclude other income from EBIT for DOL; use the finance cost note carefully for DFL (lease interest, EIR fees, unwinding of provisions, capitalised interest).
  • Normalise for cycle, exceptional items and Ind AS 116 basis before comparing peers.
  • Document sources and classification choices so the estimate can be reviewed.
  • Cross-check variable cost ratios with quarterly regressions and investor-presentation cost breakdowns.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classification exercise [L1]

A tyre company's statement of profit and loss shows revenue of ₹12,000 crore, cost of materials consumed of ₹7,400 crore, employee benefits of ₹900 crore, power and fuel of ₹480 crore, freight of ₹360 crore, advertising of ₹240 crore, stores and repairs of ₹300 crore, other fixed expenses of ₹520 crore, depreciation of ₹600 crore and finance costs of ₹180 crore. Classify each line, estimate contribution and EBIT, and compute DOL, DFL and DCL. State your assumptions.

Q2. Finance cost note adjustments [L2]

A finance cost note shows: interest on term loans ₹210 crore, interest on lease liabilities ₹45 crore, unwinding of discount on mine-closure provision ₹18 crore, EIR amortisation of processing fees ₹7 crore, and exchange difference treated as borrowing cost ₹12 crore. The PPE note discloses borrowing costs capitalised of ₹60 crore. EBIT is ₹1,100 crore. Compute DFL on (a) a reported basis and (b) an economic basis, explaining each adjustment.

💡 Hint: Decide which lines are true financing charges before adding back capitalised interest.

Q3. Inventory distortion [L2]

A company's revenue is ₹2,000 crore and cost of materials consumed is ₹1,300 crore, but inventories of finished goods increased by ₹150 crore during the year (shown as a negative "change in inventories"). Explain how the inventory build affects your estimate of the variable cost ratio and how you would correct it.

Q4. Benchmark deck [L3]

You are asked to prepare a one-page leverage benchmark of five listed cement companies for the strategy team. Describe the data sources, the normalisation steps, the table layout, two charts you would include and the caveats you would disclose.
