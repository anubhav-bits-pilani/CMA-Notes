EBIT–EPS Analysis and the Financial Indifference Point

Scope: EBIT–EPS analysis and the financial indifference point between debt-heavy and equity-heavy plans

Why it matters

When a company funds an expansion, the board chooses between more debt (term loans, NCDs), more equity (QIP, rights issue, preferential allotment) or preference capital. EBIT–EPS analysis shows, for each plan, what EPS will be at each level of EBIT. It identifies the EBIT at which the plans give identical EPS (the indifference point) and the EBIT at which each plan's EPS becomes zero (the financial break-even). It is a staple of CMA and CFA exams and of board papers on financing. Its limits are as important as its mechanics: EPS maximisation is not value maximisation, because more debt also raises risk, the cost of equity and the chance of covenant breaches.

Core notes

EPS as a function of EBIT

\[ \text{EPS}_j = \frac{(\text{EBIT} - I_j)(1 - t) - D_{p,j}}{N_j} \]

for financing plan \(j\), with interest \(I_j\), equity-classified preference dividend \(D_{p,j}\) and \(N_j\) equity shares. Plotted against EBIT, each plan is a straight line with slope \((1 - t) \div N_j\) and horizontal intercept (EPS = 0) at the financial break-even \(I_j + D_{p,j} \div (1 - t)\) (c04).

  • The debt-heavy plan has fewer shares (steeper line) and a higher financial break-even (intercept further right).
  • The equity-heavy plan has more shares (flatter line) and a lower financial break-even.

The lines cross at the indifference EBIT.

Indifference point

Equate EPS for plans 1 and 2:

\[ \frac{(\text{EBIT}^{*} - I_1)(1 - t) - D_{p,1}}{N_1} = \frac{(\text{EBIT}^{*} - I_2)(1 - t) - D_{p,2}}{N_2} \]

With no preference shares, this simplifies to:

\[ \text{EBIT}^{*} = \frac{N_1 I_2 - N_2 I_1}{N_1 - N_2} \]

Above EBIT\(^{*}\) the plan with more debt (fewer shares) gives higher EPS. Below it, the equity plan gives higher EPS.

Illustration

A Hyderabad pharma company has 5 crore shares, existing debt with interest of ₹20 crore, and needs ₹300 crore for a new formulation plant. Tax rate 25.168%. Options:

Plan A: equity Plan B: debt Plan C: 50:50
New shares at ₹500 60 lakh – 30 lakh
New debt at 9% – ₹300 crore ₹150 crore
Total interest (₹ crore) 20 47 33.5
Total shares (crore) 5.6 5.0 5.3
Financial BEP (EBIT, ₹ crore) 20 47 33.5

Indifference A vs B:

\[ \text{EBIT}^{*} = \frac{5.6 \times 47 - 5.0 \times 20}{5.6 - 5.0} = \frac{263.2 - 100}{0.6} = \text{₹}272 \text{ crore} \]

At EBIT of ₹272 crore, EPS under both plans \(= (272 - 20) \times 0.74832 \div 5.6 = \text{₹}33.67\). If expected EBIT after expansion is ₹350 crore, Plan B gives EPS of \((350 - 47) \times 0.74832 \div 5 = \text{₹}45.35\) against ₹44.10 for Plan A.

A versus C and B versus C intersect at the same ₹272 crore. When the plans differ only in the split of the same amount between debt and equity issued at the same price, all three lines pass through one point.

Reading the chart beyond the crossover

  1. Expected EBIT vs indifference point: how far above EBIT\(^{*}\) is expected EBIT, and how likely is EBIT to fall below it? Use the operating distribution (DOL and volume scenarios from t1 s07).
  2. Financial break-even: the debt plan's EPS turns negative at ₹47 crore. A severe downturn hurts more.
  3. Coverage and covenants: interest cover and Net Debt/EBITDA under each plan at downside EBIT (t5).
  4. Cost of equity: higher leverage raises equity beta (Hamada; Week 16). P/E multiples typically compress, so higher EPS need not mean a higher share price.
  5. Control and dilution: promoters may prefer debt to avoid dilution below thresholds (for example, 50% for control or 26% for special-resolution blocking).
  6. Flexibility: unused debt capacity has option value.

Excel implementation

=((EBIT - Interest_A)*(1 - Tax) - PrefDiv_A)/Shares_A          ' EPS line for plan A
=(Shares_A*Interest_B - Shares_B*Interest_A)/(Shares_A - Shares_B)   ' indifference EBIT (no pref)

Build a column of EBIT from ₹0 to ₹500 crore in ₹25 crore steps with EPS columns per plan, and chart them (scatter with straight lines). Add the expected EBIT and downside EBIT as vertical lines. Use Goal Seek on the difference of EPS between two plans to find the indifference point when preference dividends or different tax positions make the closed form awkward.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Different issue prices: if the equity plan requires a QIP at a discount, or a rights issue at a deep discount, \(N\) rises and the indifference point moves up.
  • Preference plan: a preference-share plan has the same slope as the equity-only plan (same \(N\)), so its line is parallel to the equity line. There is no indifference point between them; one dominates at every EBIT.
  • Different interest rates: incremental debt may carry a higher rate if leverage pushes the rating down (t5). Use the plan-specific rate.
  • Tax shield usability: if the company has tax losses, \(t\) is effectively zero in the short run and the debt advantage shrinks.
  • Upfront costs: issue expenses on equity (charged to securities premium under Sec 52 where permitted) and upfront fees on debt (amortised via EIR under Ind AS 109) change effective cost slightly.
  • EPS is not value: the plan with higher EPS at expected EBIT may still have the lower share price if the market applies a lower P/E to the riskier capital structure.

Key takeaways

  • EPS is linear in EBIT: slope \((1 - t) \div N\), intercept at the financial break-even.
  • Indifference EBIT (no preference shares) \(= (N_1 I_2 - N_2 I_1) \div (N_1 - N_2)\).
  • Above the indifference point the debt-heavy plan gives higher EPS; below it the equity plan does.
  • Equity-only and preference plans have parallel EPS lines, so there is no indifference point between them.
  • Decide with the probability of EBIT falling below the indifference point, coverage, ratings and cost of equity, not EPS alone.

Practice Questions

Q1. Indifference EBIT [L1]

A company with 4 crore shares and no debt needs ₹200 crore. Plan X: issue 40 lakh shares at ₹500. Plan Y: borrow ₹200 crore at 10%. Tax rate 25.168%. Compute the indifference EBIT and the EPS at that point.

Q2. Plan with preference shares [L2]

Add Plan Z to Q1: ₹100 crore of 8% equity-classified preference shares plus 20 lakh equity shares at ₹500. Compute the indifference EBIT between Plan Z and each of X and Y, and the financial break-even for each plan.

💡 Hint: Equate EPS expressions including the preference dividend; no simplified formula applies directly.

Q3. Probability-weighted choice [L2]

Using the Hyderabad pharma illustration in these notes, EBIT after expansion is ₹200 crore (25%), ₹320 crore (50%) or ₹420 crore (25%). Compute expected EPS and the range of EPS under Plans A and B. Discuss which plan you would recommend and what further information you need.

Q4. Excel chart build [L2]

Specify the worksheet layout, formulas and chart setup for an EBIT–EPS chart with three plans, including dynamic labels for the indifference points and a shaded band for the downside EBIT range.

Q5. Board judgement [L3]

A promoter-controlled company holds 52% and wants to fund a ₹1,200 crore capex entirely with debt, arguing that EPS will be highest. Expected EBIT is ₹600 crore, the indifference EBIT against a QIP plan is ₹410 crore, and post- issue Net Debt/EBITDA would be 3.4x against a lender covenant of 3.5x. Write a board note on the financing choice, covering EPS, risk, rating, control and flexibility.
