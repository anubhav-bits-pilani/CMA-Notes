Key concept

EPS Indifference Point Between Two Financing Plans

Concept: EPS indifference: (EBIT − I1)(1 − t) ÷ N1 = (EBIT − I2)(1 − t) ÷ N2; solve for EBIT

Statement

\[ \frac{(\text{EBIT}^{*} - I_1)(1 - t)}{N_1} = \frac{(\text{EBIT}^{*} - I_2)(1 - t)}{N_2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{EBIT}^{*} = \frac{N_1 I_2 - N_2 I_1}{N_1 - N_2} \]

where \(I_1, I_2\) are total interest under plans 1 and 2, \(N_1, N_2\) the total equity shares outstanding under each plan, \(t\) the tax rate, and EBIT\(^{*}\) the EBIT at which both plans give the same EPS. With preference dividends, subtract \(D_{p,j}\) from each numerator before dividing by \(N_j\) and solve the linear equation directly.

Explanation

Each plan's EPS is a straight line in EBIT. The equity-heavy plan has more shares (flatter slope) but less interest (starts earning EPS sooner). The debt-heavy plan has fewer shares (steeper slope) but more interest. They cross once, at EBIT\(^{*}\). Because \((1 - t)\) appears on both sides, the tax rate cancels when there are no preference dividends, and the indifference point does not depend on \(t\).

Decision rule: if expected EBIT is comfortably above EBIT\(^{*}\), the debt plan gives higher EPS. But it also has higher DFL, a higher financial break-even, and a higher cost of equity. The full decision weighs the probability of EBIT falling below EBIT\(^{*}\), coverage and rating effects, and control.

Special cases:

  • Equity vs preference plan (same \(N\)): the lines are parallel and there is no indifference point. Equity dominates when \(D_p \div (1 - t)\) exceeds the interest saved, which is usually the case because preference dividend is not tax-deductible.
  • Different issue prices or rates: plug in the plan-specific \(N\) and \(I\). A rights issue at a deep discount raises \(N\) for the equity plan and moves EBIT\(^{*}\) up.
  • Existing debt: include it in both \(I_1\) and \(I_2\). It does not cancel out in the closed form.

In Excel, use the closed form when there are no preference dividends. Otherwise set up a cell with the EPS difference and use Goal Seek (set the difference to 0 by changing EBIT).

Illustration

A Gujarat solar-module maker has 10 crore shares and existing interest of ₹15 crore, and needs ₹400 crore.

  • Plan 1: issue 1 crore shares at ₹400. \(N_1 = 11\), \(I_1 = 15\).
  • Plan 2: borrow ₹400 crore at 9.5%. \(N_2 = 10\), \(I_2 = 15 + 38 = 53\).

\[ \text{EBIT}^{*} = \frac{11 \times 53 - 10 \times 15}{11 - 10} = \frac{583 - 150}{1} = \text{₹}433 \text{ crore} \]

EPS at ₹433 crore: Plan 1 \(= (433 - 15) \times 0.74832 \div 11 = \text{₹}28.44\); Plan 2 \(= (433 - 53) \times 0.74832 \div 10 = \text{₹}28.44\). ✓

If expected EBIT is ₹380 crore, below EBIT\(^{*}\), the equity plan gives the higher EPS (₹24.83 against ₹24.47), and the debt plan also carries more risk. Equity is the better choice on both counts.

Connections

This concept is the algebraic core of s04 (EBIT–EPS analysis). It is the financial counterpart of the cost-structure indifference point in t1 c06 (operating fixed vs variable costs). The financial BEP of each plan (c04) is where its EPS line meets the axis. In t4 and t5, the debt plan's feasibility is checked against rating-consistent debt capacity and covenant headroom. In Week 16, the higher equity beta under the debt plan raises Ke.

Practice Questions

Q1. Indifference EBIT [L1]

A company with 6 crore shares and no debt needs ₹240 crore. Plan A: issue shares at ₹300. Plan B: borrow at 10%. Tax rate 25.168%. Compute the indifference EBIT and the EPS at that point.

Q2. Plans with existing debt and preference shares [L2]

A company has 4 crore shares and existing interest of ₹20 crore. It needs ₹150 crore. Plan X: issue 50 lakh shares at ₹300. Plan Y: issue ₹150 crore of 9% equity-classified preference shares. Plan Z: borrow ₹150 crore at 10%. Tax rate 25.168%. Find the indifference EBIT between X and Z, and explain why there is none between X and Y.

💡 Hint: Compare the slopes of the EPS lines for X and Y before attempting to solve.

Q3. Sensitivity to issue price [L2]

In the Gujarat illustration in these notes, the QIP can be priced only at ₹320 per share (1.25 crore new shares). Recompute EBIT\(^{*}\) and explain the direction of the change.
