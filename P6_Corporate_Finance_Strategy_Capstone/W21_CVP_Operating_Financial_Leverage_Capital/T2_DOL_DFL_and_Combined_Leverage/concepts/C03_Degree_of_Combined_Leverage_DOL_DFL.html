Key concept

Degree of Combined Leverage: DOL × DFL

Concept: DCL = DOL × DFL = Contribution ÷ [EBIT − Interest − Pref div ÷ (1 − t)] = % change in EPS ÷ % change in sales

Statement

\[ \text{DCL} = \text{DOL} \times \text{DFL} = \frac{C}{\text{EBIT}} \times \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{EBIT} - I - \dfrac{D_p}{1 - t}} = \frac{C}{\text{EBIT} - I - \dfrac{D_p}{1 - t}} = \frac{\%\Delta\text{EPS}}{\%\Delta\text{Sales}} \]

where \(C\) is contribution, \(I\) interest, \(D_p\) equity-classified preference dividend, and \(t\) the tax rate. EBIT cancels in the product, so DCL needs only contribution and pre-tax earnings available to equity.

Explanation

DCL captures total leverage, operating and financial, between sales and EPS. Its denominator is the pre-tax profit left for equity after all fixed charges: fixed operating costs, interest and grossed-up preference dividend. So DCL is contribution divided by "contribution minus all fixed charges". It is the same structure as DOL with a larger fixed-cost base.

Uses:

  • EPS sensitivity: %ΔEPS \(\approx\) DCL \(\times\) %Δsales for volume-driven changes.
  • Trade-off between operating and financial risk: a firm with high DOL (capital-intensive) can keep DCL manageable only by keeping DFL low, and vice versa. Boards sometimes set an implicit DCL ceiling when deciding how much debt to add to a capital-intensive project.
  • EPS bridge: the leverage-driven part of an EPS change is DCL times the volume-driven sales change (s03).

Conditions: as for DOL and DFL, it is a point measure valid for volume changes with constant price, unit variable cost, fixed costs, interest, tax rate and share count. When the denominator approaches zero, DCL approaches infinity and EPS percentage changes become meaningless.

A common error is to compute DOL using fixed costs including interest (which is already DCL) and then multiply by DFL, double-counting financial leverage (topic pitfall 1).

Illustration

A Nashik auto-parts maker:

Item ₹ crore
Sales 800
Contribution 280
Fixed operating costs 160
EBIT 120
Interest 36
Equity-classified preference dividend 6
Grossed-up preference dividend: 6 ÷ 0.74832 8.02
Pre-tax earnings for equity 75.98

DOL \(= 280 \div 120 = 2.333\); DFL \(= 120 \div 75.98 = 1.579\); DCL \(= 280 \div 75.98 = 3.685\).

A 6% fall in volume would reduce EPS by about \(3.685 \times 6\% = 22.1\%\). Verification: contribution falls to ₹263.2 crore, EBIT to ₹103.2 crore, pre-tax equity earnings to ₹59.18 crore, a fall of 22.1%. ✓

If the company planned to add debt with ₹20 crore of extra interest for a new line, DCL at the same contribution would become \(280 \div 55.98 = 5.0\), and the same 6% volume fall would cut EPS by 30%.

Connections

DCL links t1 (contribution and break-even), c01 (DOL), c02 (DFL) and s03 (EPS decomposition). It is central to the capital-intensive vs asset-light comparison (s05) and to the Week 21 assessment, where two expansion proposals with different operating leverage are compared. In t5, the DCL logic reappears as the link between business risk and debt capacity: a company with high DOL should carry less debt for the same rating.

Practice Questions

Q1. DCL and EPS change [L1]

A company has contribution of ₹500 crore, fixed operating costs of ₹300 crore, interest of ₹80 crore and no preference shares. Compute DOL, DFL and DCL, and the expected percentage change in EPS for an 8% rise in volume.

Q2. DCL ceiling [L2]

A board wants DCL to stay at or below 4.0. The company has contribution of ₹600 crore, fixed operating costs of ₹360 crore and existing interest of ₹40 crore. Tax rate is 25.168%. What is the maximum additional annual interest it can take on? At 9%, how much additional debt does that imply?

💡 Hint: Set contribution divided by pre-tax equity earnings equal to 4.0 and solve for total interest.

Q3. Spot the error [L2]

An analyst computes DOL as contribution ÷ (contribution − fixed operating costs − interest) = 4.2 and DFL as EBIT ÷ (EBIT − interest) = 1.5, and reports DCL = 6.3. Explain the error and state the correct DCL.
