Key concept

Degree of Financial Leverage With Grossed-Up Preference Dividend

Concept: DFL = EBIT ÷ [EBIT − Interest − Preference dividend ÷ (1 − t)] = % change in EPS ÷ % change in EBIT

Statement

\[ \text{DFL} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{EBIT} - I - \dfrac{D_p}{1 - t}} = \frac{\%\Delta\text{EPS}}{\%\Delta\text{EBIT}} \]

where EBIT is earnings before interest and tax, \(I\) is interest (finance costs on liabilities, including liability-classified preference "dividends" and, on a post-Ind AS 116 basis, lease interest), \(D_p\) is the dividend on equity-classified preference shares, and \(t\) is the marginal tax rate. The denominator is the pre-tax earnings available to equity shareholders.

Explanation

Fixed financing charges must be paid before equity shareholders earn anything. A given change in EBIT therefore produces a larger proportional change in what is left for equity. EPS \(= (1 - t)[\text{EBIT} - I - D_p \div (1 - t)] \div N\), so EPS is proportional to the denominator of DFL. Its percentage change equals the change in EBIT divided by that denominator.

Conditions and points of care:

  • Gross-up: equity-classified preference dividend is paid from after-tax profit, so it is converted to a pre-tax equivalent by dividing by \(1 - t\). Interest is already pre-tax (topic pitfall 2).
  • Ind AS 32: mandatorily redeemable preference shares are usually liabilities. Their "dividend" is in finance cost and is not grossed up (s02).
  • Tax shield: if a part of finance cost is not tax-deductible (for example, preference dividend presented as interest, or interest disallowed under Sec 94B), treat that part like a grossed-up charge for precision.
  • Point measure: DFL is measured at a base EBIT. It rises sharply as EBIT approaches the financial break-even (c04).
  • No debt, no preference: DFL \(= 1\).
  • Other income: if EBIT used for DFL includes treasury income, be consistent between the numerator and the forecast. Many analysts use EBIT excluding other income.

Illustration

A Pune engineering company: EBIT ₹150 crore, interest ₹30 crore, equity-classified preference dividend ₹9 crore, tax rate 25.168%, 8 crore equity shares.

\[ \frac{D_p}{1 - t} = \frac{9}{0.74832} = \text{₹}12.03 \text{ crore}; \quad \text{DFL} = \frac{150}{150 - 30 - 12.03} = \frac{150}{107.97} = 1.389 \]

EPS at EBIT ₹150 crore \(= [(150 - 30) \times 0.74832 - 9] \div 8 = \text{₹}10.10\). At EBIT ₹165 crore (+10%): EPS \(= [(165 - 30) \times 0.74832 - 9] \div 8 = \text{₹}11.50\), a rise of 13.89%. ✓

Without the gross-up, DFL \(= 150 \div 111 = 1.351\), understating EPS sensitivity.

Connections

DFL is the financial half of DCL (c03). Its denominator at zero defines the financial break-even (c04). It underlies EBIT–EPS analysis (s04, c05) and rises with Ind AS 116 lease interest (s06). Practice Questions

Q1. DFL and EPS check [L1]

A company has EBIT of ₹60 crore, interest of ₹15 crore, equity-classified preference dividend of ₹3 crore and a tax rate of 25.168%, with 2.5 crore shares. Compute DFL and verify it by computing EPS at EBIT of ₹54 crore.

Q2. Liability vs equity preference shares [L2]

A company has EBIT of ₹200 crore, bank interest of ₹40 crore and ₹100 crore of 10% preference shares. Compute DFL assuming the preference shares are (a) equity-classified and (b) liability-classified under Ind AS 32 with the dividend non-deductible for tax. Explain why (b) needs care in the denominator.

💡 Hint: In (b) the dividend is in finance cost but still carries no tax shield.

Q3. DFL near financial break-even [L2]

A company's interest is ₹50 crore with no preference shares. Compute DFL at EBIT of ₹55 crore, ₹75 crore, ₹100 crore and ₹200 crore, and describe the pattern in one sentence.
