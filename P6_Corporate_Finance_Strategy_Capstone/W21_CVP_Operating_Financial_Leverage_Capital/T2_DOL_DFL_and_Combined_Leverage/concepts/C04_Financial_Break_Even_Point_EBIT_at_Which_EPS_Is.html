Key concept

Financial Break-Even Point: EBIT at Which EPS Is Zero

Concept: Financial BEP (EBIT at which EPS = 0) = Interest + Preference dividend ÷ (1 − t)

Statement

\[ \text{EBIT}_{\text{FBEP}} = I + \frac{D_p}{1 - t} \]

where \(I\) is interest (all finance costs on liabilities), \(D_p\) is the dividend on equity-classified preference shares and \(t\) is the tax rate. At this EBIT, PBT just covers the grossed-up preference dividend, PAT just pays it, and EPS \(= 0\).

Explanation

EPS \(= [(\text{EBIT} - I)(1 - t) - D_p] \div N\). Setting EPS to zero and solving gives the formula. It is the horizontal-axis intercept of the EPS line in an EBIT–EPS chart (s04). It is also the EBIT at which DFL becomes infinite (the denominator of DFL is zero).

Interpretation:

  • It is the minimum EBIT the company must earn to avoid a loss for equity shareholders. Compare it with the EBIT distribution from operating scenarios. If downside EBIT can fall below it, equity holders face losses.
  • It differs from the operating break-even (EBIT \(= 0\)), which is about covering fixed operating costs. The overall break-even sales level, where EPS \(= 0\), covers both:

\[ S_{\text{overall BEP}} = \frac{F + I + D_p \div (1 - t)}{\text{PV}} \]

  • A higher financial BEP shifts the EPS line to the right. Debt-heavy financing plans have higher financial break-evens.
  • With no preference shares, the financial BEP equals interest. With no debt or preference capital, it is zero.
  • Tax losses: if \(t\) is effectively zero, the gross-up factor is 1. Where only part of the finance cost is deductible, gross up the non-deductible part as well.

Illustration

A Chennai logistics company has interest of ₹42 crore (including Ind AS 116 lease interest of ₹12 crore) and equity-classified 8% preference shares of ₹75 crore. Tax rate 25.168%. Fixed operating costs ₹110 crore, P/V ratio 24%.

\[ \text{EBIT}_{\text{FBEP}} = 42 + \frac{6}{0.74832} = 42 + 8.02 = \text{₹}50.02 \text{ crore} \]

Operating BEP sales \(= 110 \div 0.24 = \text{₹}458.3\) crore. Overall BEP sales \(= (110 + 50.02) \div 0.24 = \text{₹}666.8\) crore. If budgeted sales are ₹720 crore, EPS is positive, but the margin of safety over the overall break-even is only 7.4% of sales. That is a thin cushion, and DCL at budget is \(720 \times 0.24 \div (172.8 - 160.02) = 13.5\).

Connections

The financial BEP is the intercept in EBIT–EPS analysis (s04, c05) and the point where DFL explodes (c02). It combines with t1's operating break-even to give the overall break-even. In credit work, the analogous lender measures are interest cover of 1.0x (EBIT \(=\) interest) and DSCR of 1.0x (t5). In project models, the "debt-service break-even utilisation" plays the same role (t1 s05).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute financial BEP [L1]

A company has interest of ₹28 crore and equity-classified preference dividend of ₹4.5 crore, with a tax rate of 25.168%. Compute the financial BEP and verify that EPS is zero at that EBIT.

Q2. Overall break-even sales [L2]

A company has fixed operating costs of ₹240 crore, a P/V ratio of 30%, interest of ₹60 crore and no preference shares. Compute operating BEP sales, financial BEP EBIT, and overall BEP sales. If planned debt adds ₹25 crore of interest, by how much does overall BEP sales rise?

💡 Hint: Each rupee of extra interest needs 1 ÷ P/V rupees of extra sales.

Q3. Financing plans and downside [L3]

Plan A (equity) has a financial BEP of ₹20 crore and Plan B (debt) of ₹65 crore. The operating model gives downside EBIT of ₹55 crore with 15% probability. Discuss what this means for the choice between the plans beyond EPS at the expected EBIT.
