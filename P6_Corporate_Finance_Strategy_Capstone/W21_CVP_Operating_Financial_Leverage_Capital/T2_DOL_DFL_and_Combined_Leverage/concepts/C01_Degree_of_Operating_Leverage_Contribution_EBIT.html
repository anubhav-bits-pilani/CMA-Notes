Key concept

Degree of Operating Leverage: Contribution ÷ EBIT

Concept: DOL = Contribution ÷ EBIT = % change in EBIT ÷ % change in sales

Statement

\[ \text{DOL} = \frac{C}{\text{EBIT}} = \frac{C}{C - F} = \frac{\%\Delta\text{EBIT}}{\%\Delta\text{Sales}} \]

where \(C\) is total contribution (sales minus variable costs), \(F\) is fixed operating costs (including depreciation, excluding interest), and EBIT \(= C - F\). The elasticity form holds for volume-driven sales changes with constant price, variable cost per unit and fixed costs.

Explanation

DOL measures how strongly fixed operating costs amplify sales changes into EBIT changes. If all costs were variable (\(F = 0\)), EBIT would move exactly in proportion with sales and DOL would be 1. The larger \(F\) is relative to contribution, the higher DOL.

Key properties:

  • It is a point measure at the base level (s01). The same company has a different DOL at different volumes.
  • It is linked to margin of safety: DOL \(= 1 \div \text{MOS \%}\) (c06; t1 c03).
  • It uses operating fixed costs only. Interest belongs in DFL. Including it gives DCL by another name and leads to double counting when multiplied by DFL (topic pitfall 1).
  • The elasticity form applies to volume changes. A price increase with no volume change raises EBIT by the full price change, a much larger proportional effect than DOL suggests. Separate price and volume when interpreting observed sensitivities.
  • In a multi-product firm, DOL depends on mix: \(C\) is the total contribution at the given mix.

Illustration

A Jaipur ceramics company: sales ₹320 crore, variable costs ₹208 crore, fixed operating costs ₹72 crore (including depreciation of ₹20 crore), interest ₹12 crore.

\[ C = 320 - 208 = \text{₹}112 \text{ crore}; \quad \text{EBIT} = 112 - 72 = \text{₹}40 \text{ crore}; \quad \text{DOL} = \frac{112}{40} = 2.8 \]

Check with a 5% volume increase: sales ₹336 crore, variable costs ₹218.4 crore, contribution ₹117.6 crore, EBIT ₹45.6 crore. %ΔEBIT \(= 14\%\), and \(14\% \div 5\% = 2.8\). ✓

The wrong calculation that includes interest in fixed costs would give \(112 \div 28 = 4.0\). That is the DCL, not DOL.

Connections

DOL is the operating half of combined leverage (c03) and is the reciprocal of MOS % (c06). It links to t1 (break-even and MOS), s05 (capital-intensive vs asset-light profiles), s06 (Ind AS 116 shifts part of rent from operating fixed costs to interest, lowering reported DOL) and t5 (business risk drives debt capacity). In valuation and FP&A, DOL is the quick translator from a volume forecast to an EBIT forecast, valid for small changes around the base.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and verify DOL [L1]

A company has sales of ₹75 crore, a P/V ratio of 36% and fixed operating costs of ₹18 crore. Compute DOL and verify it by computing EBIT after an 8% volume increase.

Q2. Price vs volume [L2]

Using the Jaipur illustration in these notes, compare the percentage change in EBIT from (a) a 5% volume increase and (b) a 5% price increase with no volume change. Explain why DOL does not describe case (b).

💡 Hint: A price increase adds the full extra revenue to contribution because variable cost per unit is unchanged.

Q3. Interview question [L1]

"Our DOL is 3. If sales fall 40%, EBIT falls 120%." Explain in three sentences what is right and wrong with this statement.
