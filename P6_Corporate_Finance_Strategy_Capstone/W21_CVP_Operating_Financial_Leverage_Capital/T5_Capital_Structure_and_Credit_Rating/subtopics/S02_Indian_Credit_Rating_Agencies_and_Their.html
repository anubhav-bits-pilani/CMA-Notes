Subtopic note

Indian Credit Rating Agencies and Their Methodologies

Scope: Rating agency methodologies: CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings, Acuité; investment grade = BBB− and above

Why it matters

A company's credit rating decides its cost of debt, its access to the bond market, and often its eligibility for certain lenders and investors. Many mutual fund and insurance mandates, and many bank capital weights, are rating dependent. When an FP&A team models a debt-funded expansion, the key question is often not "is NPV positive?" but "will we stay at AA−, or fall to A+ and pay 60 bps more on ₹5,000 crore?" Reading a rating rationale and reverse-engineering the agency's thresholds lets you forecast the rating consequences of your plan, prepare for the annual surveillance meeting, and negotiate covenant definitions that match the agency's own view.

Core notes

The agencies and their regulation

Agency Affiliation / ownership Notes
CRISIL Ratings S&P Global majority-owned Largest by number of large-corporate ratings; also bank-loan ratings
ICRA Moody's majority-owned Strong in corporate and financial-sector ratings
CARE Ratings (CareEdge) Widely held, listed Large bank-loan rating book
India Ratings & Research Fitch Group Strong in structured finance and infrastructure
Acuité Ratings & Research Independent Large SME and bank-loan rating book
Infomerics Independent Mainly SME and mid-market

All CRAs are registered with and regulated by SEBI under the SEBI (Credit Rating Agencies) Regulations, 1999, as amended. SEBI has standardised many disclosure and process requirements:

  • the rating scale and symbols;
  • probability-of-default (PD) benchmarks and disclosure of default studies;
  • "issuer not cooperating" (INC) disclosure;
  • the timeline for recognising default;
  • disclosure of the factors in a rating rationale.

For bank capital, the RBI separately accredits CRAs as External Credit Assessment Institutions (ECAIs) under Basel III. The risk weight on a bank's corporate exposure depends on the external rating, which gives borrowers a direct incentive to be rated. Brickwork Ratings' registration was cancelled by SEBI in 2022, so ratings from it are no longer current.

Rating scale

Long-term scale (prefix by agency, e.g. CRISIL AA, [ICRA]AA, CARE AA, IND AA, ACUITE AA):

Grade Category Meaning
Investment grade AAA Highest safety
AA (+/−) High safety
A (+/−) Adequate safety
BBB (+/−) Moderate safety. BBB− is the lowest investment-grade notch
Speculative (sub-investment) grade BB Moderate risk of default
B High risk
C Very high risk
D Default

The short-term scale (for CP and short-term bank lines) runs A1+, A1, A2+, … A4, D. Outlook (Stable, Positive, Negative, Developing) indicates the likely direction over 12–24 months. Rating Watch (Credit Watch) with positive, negative or developing implications flags an event-driven review, such as a merger, a large debt-funded acquisition or a regulatory change.

Many institutional mandates require investment grade, and some require AA or above. The investment-grade line therefore marks a step-change in the investor base and in pricing.

Methodology structure (common across agencies)

  1. Business risk profile. - Industry risk: cyclicality, demand outlook, competitive intensity, regulation. - Market position: share, pricing power, brand. - Operating efficiency: cost position, capacity utilisation. - Diversification: products, customers, geography.
  2. Financial risk profile. - Capital structure: gearing, TOL/TNW. - Debt protection: interest cover, NCA/TD, Net debt/EBITDA, DSCR. - Profitability and cash flow adequacy. - Financial flexibility: access to capital markets, unencumbered assets, undrawn lines.
  3. Liquidity. Cash and liquid investments plus undrawn limits against debt maturities over the next 12 months. Agencies label liquidity as Superior, Strong, Adequate, Stretched or Poor.
  4. Management and governance. Track record, risk appetite, related-party transactions, promoter pledges, audit qualifications.
  5. Group or parent linkage. Standalone credit profile, notched up for expected parent or government support, or notched down for drag from a weak group. Agencies apply "consolidated" or "moderate/strong linkage" approaches.
  6. Project or implementation risk. For capex-heavy firms, this covers the funding tie-up, the cost-overrun cushion and the commissioning track record.

Agencies publish sector-specific methodologies (cement, steel, IT services, NBFCs, infrastructure SPVs, real estate) that set out the weight and indicative thresholds for each parameter. They are not mechanical scorecards. Committee judgement on business risk usually anchors the rating, and financial metrics move it within a band.

Indicative financial thresholds

These vary by sector and agency and are for orientation only:

Rating band Net debt/EBITDA EBITDA interest cover Gearing (D/TNW)
AAA < 1.0x > 8x < 0.5x
AA 1.0–2.0x 5–8x 0.5–1.0x
A 2.0–3.0x 3–5x 1.0–1.5x
BBB 3.0–4.0x 2–3x 1.5–2.0x
BB and below > 4.0x < 2x > 2.0x

A rating rationale typically states rating sensitivity factors. Examples are "downward: Net debt/EBITDA above 2.5x on a sustained basis" and "upward: sustained ROCE > 18% with Net debt/EBITDA < 1.0x". These are the thresholds to build into your plan's credit-metric checks.

Default recognition and its consequences

Indian agencies follow a strict "one day, one rupee" default convention. Any delay in servicing rated debt, even by a day, leads to a downgrade to D. SEBI requires CRAs to obtain no-default statements and to monitor bank loans, using banker confirmations for loan ratings. A move to D triggers:

  • cross-default clauses;
  • mutual fund side-pocketing (segregated portfolios);
  • loss of eligibility for many investors.

Using ratings in the model

  1. Map the forecast credit metrics each year to an implied rating band using the agency's sector thresholds.
  2. Map the rating band to a spread over the G-sec or MCLR curve, for example AAA +60–80 bps, AA +100–140 bps, A +180–250 bps for 5-year paper. These levels are illustrative and cycle-dependent.
  3. Feed the spread back into Kd. This creates the rating-dependent WACC curve used in s08.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Split ratings. Two agencies may differ by a notch. Investors and lenders often go by the lower one, and some mandates require two ratings.
  • Instrument vs issuer ratings. Structured obligations (CE, credit enhanced) can be rated above the issuer's standalone rating because of a guarantee or escrow. The suffix (CE) signals this.
  • Rating shopping and INC. SEBI rules require disclosure of ratings that were not accepted. "Issuer not cooperating" ratings usually signal a weak credit.
  • Sovereign ceiling. Domestic ratings are relative to Indian credits, so AAA domestically corresponds to roughly BBB− on a global scale. Do not mix scales when pricing ECBs.
  • Post-Ind AS 116 adjustments. Agencies differ on whether they treat lease liabilities as debt for all sectors. Read the methodology note.

Key takeaways

  • India's main SEBI-registered CRAs are CRISIL (S&P), ICRA (Moody's), CARE, India Ratings (Fitch) and Acuité.
  • BBB− is the lowest investment-grade notch. Crossing below it shrinks the investor base and raises spreads sharply.
  • Methodologies combine business risk, financial risk, liquidity, management and group support, with sector-specific indicative thresholds.
  • Rating sensitivity factors in each rationale give you the exact metrics and levels to monitor.
  • Link forecast metrics to an implied rating and spread in the model, so that leverage decisions reflect a rising Kd.

Practice Questions

Q1. Reading a rating rationale [L1]

A rating rationale for a cement company (AA, Stable) lists as downward sensitivity factors: "Net debt/EBITDA exceeding 2.25x on a sustained basis; large debt-funded capex or acquisitions; dilution in market position". FY25 EBITDA is ₹2,800 crore and net debt is ₹4,200 crore. The company plans ₹3,000 crore of capex in FY26, funded 70% by debt, with EBITDA growing to ₹3,100 crore. Assess the rating risk and suggest mitigants.

Q2. Implied rating and spread [L2]

Using the indicative thresholds table, map the following FY27 forecast to a rating band and estimate Kd for 5-year paper, with the 5-year G-sec at 6.7% and spreads of AA 120 bps, A 220 bps and BBB 350 bps. The forecast is EBITDA ₹900 crore, net debt ₹2,350 crore, interest ₹215 crore and gearing 1.2x. Discuss which metric drives the result.

💡 Hint: Where metrics point to different bands, explain how a committee might weigh them rather than mechanically averaging.

Q3. Group support notching [L2]

A subsidiary's standalone credit profile is BBB+. Its parent is rated AA, owns 74%, shares the brand, and has guaranteed past borrowings. Explain how agencies might notch the subsidiary, and what happens to the subsidiary's rating if the parent sells down to 26%.

Q4. Investment-grade cliff [L3]

A company rated BBB− (Negative) is evaluating a debt-funded acquisition that would push Net debt/EBITDA from 3.6x to 4.4x for two years. Analyse the consequences of a downgrade to BB+ for funding cost, investor eligibility, bank capital weights and covenants. Propose an alternative financing structure that protects the rating.
