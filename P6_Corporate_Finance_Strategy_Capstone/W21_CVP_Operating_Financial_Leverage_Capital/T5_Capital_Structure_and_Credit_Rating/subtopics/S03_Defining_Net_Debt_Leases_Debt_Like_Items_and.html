Subtopic note

Defining Net Debt: Leases, Debt-Like Items and Liquid Cash

Scope: Net debt definition: borrowings + lease liabilities (Ind AS 116) + debt-like items − cash and liquid investments

Why it matters

Net debt appears in three places that must agree with each other: the leverage covenant, the rating agency's metric, and the EV-to-equity bridge in a valuation. Different definitions can move Net Debt/EBITDA by a full turn. That is the difference between passing and breaching a covenant, or between an AA and an A rating. Analysts reading Indian annual reports must build net debt from several notes:

  • the borrowings notes, which in the 2021 Schedule III format show current maturities within short-term borrowings;
  • the lease liabilities note under Ind AS 116;
  • other financial liabilities, which hold acceptances, capital creditors and deferred consideration;
  • the Ind AS 7 reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities;
  • the cash, other bank balances and current investments notes.

Core notes

The building blocks

\[ \text{Net debt} = \text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities} + \text{Debt-like items} - (\text{Cash and cash equivalents} + \text{Liquid investments}) \]

1. Borrowings. These are non-current and current borrowings:

  • term loans and NCDs;
  • commercial paper;
  • working-capital demand loans, cash credit and overdrafts;
  • ECBs and FCCBs (liability component);
  • inter-corporate deposits taken;
  • current maturities of long-term debt, which Schedule III since 2021 shows within current borrowings.

Use the carrying amount (amortised cost, net of unamortised fees) or the principal outstanding, and be consistent. Lenders usually use principal. Include accrued interest if the definition requires it. Many covenants exclude it, because it sits in other financial liabilities.

2. Lease liabilities (Ind AS 116). Include both current and non-current lease liabilities if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116, which is the reported EBITDA. If you use pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA (EBITDA less rent), exclude them. Never mix the two (see the topic pitfalls).

3. Debt-like items. These are obligations with the economic character of financing. Include them if material and consistently across peers:

Item Where found Typical treatment
Redeemable or mandatorily convertible preference shares classified as financial liabilities (Ind AS 32) Borrowings Include
Acceptances and buyer's credit for capex or raw material beyond normal trade terms; supplier-finance arrangements Trade payables / other financial liabilities Include (rating agencies often do)
Deferred or contingent acquisition consideration Other financial liabilities Include at carrying amount
Unfunded employee benefit obligations (gratuity, pension), net of tax Provisions Include in valuation bridges. Covenants usually exclude
Dividends declared but unpaid; unpaid capital creditors overdue Other liabilities Case by case
Factoring or bill discounting with recourse Borrowings, if not derecognised under Ind AS 109 Include
Corporate guarantees for group or JV debt Contingent liabilities Exclude from net debt, but assess separately
Customer advances Other liabilities Exclude (operating), except where they are financing-like

4. Cash and liquid investments (deducted).

  • Cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.6–7.7).
  • Other bank balances: fixed deposits with original maturity over 3 months, if they are free of lien.
  • Current investments in liquid or overnight mutual funds and T-bills.
  • Quoted debt instruments that can be liquidated quickly.

Exclude restricted cash:

  • lien-marked FDs and margin money for LCs or BGs;
  • DSRA balances, if lenders define them as restricted;
  • unpaid dividend accounts;
  • escrow balances;
  • trapped cash in subsidiaries subject to exchange controls.

Do not deduct strategic or long-term equity investments. Treat them separately in valuation.

Worked illustration

Extracts from a listed consumer-durables company at 31 March 2025 (₹ crore):

Component Amount Include?
Non-current borrowings (NCDs, term loans) 820 +
Current borrowings (incl. current maturities 140, CP 200) 610 +
Lease liabilities (current 45, non-current 230) 275 +
Supplier-finance arrangement payables (180-day, bank-paid) 160 + (debt-like)
Deferred acquisition consideration 90 +
Cash and cash equivalents (310) −
FDs > 3 months, of which 60 lien-marked for BGs (210) − 150 only
Liquid mutual funds (240) −
Unpaid dividend account balance (4) Excluded
Net debt 1,255

The figure is 820 + 610 + 275 + 160 + 90 − 310 − 150 − 240 = 1,255. On a "borrowings minus cash" definition, net debt would be 820 + 610 − 310 − 210 − 240 = 670. With EBITDA of ₹600 crore, that is the difference between 2.1x and 1.1x leverage.

Ind AS 7 net-debt reconciliation

Ind AS 7.44A–44E require disclosure of changes in liabilities arising from financing activities. These are cash flows, the effects of changes in control, FX movements, fair-value changes and other non-cash changes, such as new leases. Many Indian companies present this as a net-debt reconciliation. In the model, roll net debt forward as: opening net debt + new leases + (−FCF) + distributions + acquisitions ± FX = closing net debt. Reconcile this to the balance sheet.

Consistency rules

  1. Net debt and EBITDA must have the same perimeter. If leases are in net debt, rent is out of EBITDA. If an acquired company's debt is in net debt, include its EBITDA (on a pro-forma TTM basis).
  2. The same definition across peers for benchmarking, and across periods for trends.
  3. The covenant definition overrides your preferred definition for compliance testing. Keep a separate "analyst net debt" for valuation and rating analysis.
  4. Point-in-time vs average. Year-end net debt can be window-dressed, for example through quarter-end CP repayments or customer collections. Rating agencies look at average or peak debt for working-capital-intensive businesses.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Supplier finance. IFRS added supplier-finance disclosure requirements in 2023. Check the current notified status of the corresponding Ind AS amendments. Regardless of the disclosure rules, agencies increasingly treat extended-term, bank-intermediated payables as debt.
  • Cash in a regulated or ring-fenced subsidiary (insurance, banking or SPV with DSRA) is not available to repay group debt. Exclude it from consolidated net cash.
  • Compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs) may be equity-classified under Ind AS 32 if they convert into a fixed number of shares. Lenders may still treat them as debt if coupon-bearing.
  • Negative net debt (net cash) companies should still have their lease liabilities analysed. A retailer may be "net cash" on borrowings but carry large lease obligations.
  • Hybrid perpetual bonds classified as equity are often given 50% equity credit by agencies. Follow the methodology.

Key takeaways

  • Net debt = borrowings + lease liabilities + debt-like items − unrestricted cash and liquid investments.
  • Include lease liabilities whenever EBITDA is reported post-Ind AS 116. Exclude them only if EBITDA is pre-Ind AS 116.
  • Deduct only unrestricted, readily realisable cash. Exclude lien-marked FDs, DSRA, unpaid dividend accounts and trapped cash.
  • Debt-like items (supplier finance, deferred consideration, redeemable preference shares, recourse factoring) can materially change leverage.
  • Keep separate covenant and analyst definitions, and reconcile net debt to the Ind AS 7.44A disclosure.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build net debt from notes [L1]

From these extracts (₹ crore), compute net debt on (a) a covenant definition of borrowings + leases − cash and cash equivalents, and (b) a full analyst definition:

  • term loans 1,400; CP 300; cash credit 220;
  • lease liabilities 340;
  • redeemable preference shares classified as a liability 150;
  • buyer's credit for capex 180; deferred consideration 60;
  • cash 410; FDs over 3 months 300 (of which 120 lien-marked); liquid MFs 250;
  • DSRA 75.

Q2. Perimeter consistency [L2]

A company acquired a subsidiary on 1 January 2025. The subsidiary has debt of ₹500 crore and annual EBITDA of ₹160 crore. The parent's reported FY25 EBITDA includes only one quarter of the subsidiary's EBITDA. Consolidated net debt at 31 March 2025 is ₹2,800 crore and reported EBITDA is ₹1,000 crore. Compute reported and pro-forma Net debt/EBITDA and explain which a lender would test.

💡 Hint: Pro-forma TTM EBITDA adds back the pre-acquisition quarters of the subsidiary's EBITDA.

Q3. Net-debt roll-forward [L2]

Opening net debt is ₹1,800 crore. During the year: FCF ₹420 crore; dividends ₹150 crore; new leases ₹90 crore; acquisition ₹300 crore (including assumed debt of ₹80 crore); FX loss on ECB ₹25 crore; equity raised ₹200 crore. Build the roll-forward to closing net debt and identify which items appear in the Ind AS 7.44A disclosure.

Q4. Judgement on debt-like items [L3]

A quick-service restaurant chain reports net cash of ₹150 crore, lease liabilities of ₹1,900 crore, and EBITDA (post Ind AS 116) of ₹620 crore. Its rent is ₹410 crore. A peer that reports on a pre-Ind AS 116 basis for covenant purposes shows EBITDA of ₹300 crore and net debt of ₹200 crore. Construct comparable leverage metrics for both on each basis and comment.
