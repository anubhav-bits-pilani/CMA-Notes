Optimal D/E Search: WACC Curve with Rating-Implied Spreads

Scope: Optimal D/E search: WACC curve vs leverage using rating-implied spreads at each debt level

Why it matters

"What is our optimal capital structure?" is one of the most common board and interview questions in corporate finance. The practical answer is a WACC curve: recompute the cost of equity and the cost of debt at each candidate debt level, using rating-implied spreads, and find the level that minimises WACC. Equivalently, it maximises firm value, subject to a rating floor the board is willing to accept. The analysis ties the topic together. It needs:

  • credit metrics (s01);
  • rating thresholds (s02);
  • net debt (s03);
  • trade-off theory (s04, s05);
  • Sec 94B (s07);
  • relevered beta (t4 c02).

It is also a core module in a corporate finance model and in the Saturday assessment.

Core notes

The method (Damodaran-style synthetic rating approach)

For each debt ratio D/V from 0% to, say, 70% in 10% steps:

  1. Compute the debt amount. \(D = (D/V) \times V\), using the current enterprise value. Iterating on V is a refinement.
  2. Compute interest. \(\text{Interest} = D \times K_d\). Start with the previous row's Kd or the current Kd, then iterate, because Kd depends on coverage and coverage depends on interest.
  3. Compute the coverage ratio. \(\text{ICR} = \text{EBIT}/\text{Interest}\), and optionally Net debt/EBITDA.
  4. Map coverage to a synthetic rating using a grid.
  5. Map the rating to a default spread. Then \(K_d = R_f + \text{spread}\).
  6. Apply the tax shield. \(K_d(1 - t)\), but if interest exceeds EBIT the effective t is reduced proportionally, and the Sec 94B cap applies where relevant.
  7. Relever beta. \(\beta_L = \beta_u[1 + (1 - t)D/E]\), so \(K_e = R_f + \beta_L \times \text{ERP}\).
  8. Compute WACC \(= (E/V)K_e + (D/V)K_d(1 - t)\).
  9. Compute firm value. For a stable firm, \(V = \text{FCFF}_1/(\text{WACC} - g)\). Choose the D/V that minimises WACC or maximises V, subject to constraints.

Worked illustration

Inputs: EBIT ₹1,000 crore, t 25.168%, βu 0.80, Rf 7.0%, ERP 7.0%, firm value V ₹10,000 crore (held constant for the weights), and FCFF growth 5%.

D/V D (₹ cr) Interest (₹ cr) ICR Rating Kd Kd(1 − t) D/E βL Ke WACC
0% 0 0 — AAA 7.70% 5.76% 0.00 0.800 12.60% 12.60%
10% 1,000 77 13.0x AAA 7.70% 5.76% 0.11 0.867 13.07% 12.34%
20% 2,000 162 6.2x AA 8.10% 6.06% 0.25 0.950 13.65% 12.13%
30% 3,000 267 3.7x A 8.90% 6.66% 0.43 1.057 14.40% 12.08%
40% 4,000 480 2.1x BB 12.00% 8.98% 0.67 1.199 15.39% 12.83%
50% 5,000 700 1.4x B 14.00% 10.48% 1.00 1.399 16.79% 13.63%

In this example WACC is minimised at around 30% D/V, an A rating. Note:

  • From 0% to 30% the cheaper after-tax debt outweighs the rise in Ke.
  • Beyond 30% the jump in Kd as the rating falls, plus the steeper rise in Ke, pushes WACC up.
  • At 50% D/V, interest of ₹700 crore is still below EBIT, so the full tax shield is available. If interest exceeded EBIT, the effective tax rate on interest would fall, making high leverage even costlier.

The 40% row shows why iteration matters. At the BBB spread (10.2%), interest would be ₹408 crore and ICR 2.45x, which is already in the BB band. Re-running at the BB spread (12.0%) gives ICR 2.08x, which confirms BB. This is the rating cliff: one notch can add 180 bps to Kd on the whole debt stock. Firm value at the minimum is roughly FCFF ÷ (12.08% − 5%), against FCFF ÷ (12.60% − 5%) at zero debt, an increase of about 7%.

Constraints that override the mathematical minimum

  • Rating floor. Many boards target "no lower than AA−" or "investment grade with a buffer". The cost of losing financial flexibility in a downturn is not captured in a static WACC.
  • Covenants. Existing loan covenants (Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0x) may bind before the WACC minimum.
  • Cash-flow volatility. Use downside-case EBIT in the coverage step, not the base case, for cyclical businesses.
  • Sector norms and peer leverage. Rating agencies benchmark against peers, so an outlier faces scrutiny.
  • Promoter and control considerations. Equity issuance dilutes promoters, which pushes them towards debt (pecking order).

Building it in Excel

  • Put the inputs on one block and the D/V steps in rows, as above.
  • Resolve the Kd–interest circularity with iterative calculation (File → Options → Formulas → Enable iterative calculation), a copy-paste macro, or a two-pass approach: compute interest with the previous row's Kd, then check the rating is unchanged.
  • Map rating and spread with XLOOKUP using approximate match on a sorted coverage grid, for example =XLOOKUP(ICR, Grid_ICR_Lower, Grid_Spread, , -1).
  • Chart WACC against D/V, with Ke, Kd(1 − t) and the rating band as secondary series, to show the U-shape.
  • Add data tables: the WACC minimum against βu (±0.1) and against an EBIT downside (−20%).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Holding V constant is a simplification. Strictly, V changes as WACC changes, so iterate until D/V converges or use the APV approach.
  • Hamada assumes debt beta = 0. At high leverage this overstates βL. Using a debt beta flattens the Ke curve.
  • Multiple metrics. Rating agencies use Net debt/EBITDA and NCA/TD as well as ICR. A firm may pass on ICR but fail on leverage. Use the binding metric for the synthetic rating.
  • Tax-loss companies and Sec 94B. Cap the shield. If interest exceeds EBIT, the effective rate is \(t_{\text{eff}} = t \times \text{EBIT}/\text{Interest}\).
  • Financial firms and regulated utilities. Leverage is set by regulation (capital adequacy) or tariff norms (normative 70:30 debt-equity for CERC tariffs), not by a WACC minimum.

Key takeaways

  • At each D/V, recompute interest, coverage, synthetic rating, spread, Kd, relevered βL, Ke and WACC.
  • WACC is typically U-shaped. Cheap after-tax debt dominates first, then rising spreads and Ke dominate.
  • In India the minimum usually lies in the A to AA band for industrial companies, depending on EBIT stability.
  • The Kd–coverage circularity needs iteration or a two-pass approach. Use approximate-match lookups on a sorted grid.
  • Override the mathematical optimum with rating floors, covenants and downside-case coverage.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a WACC curve [L2]

A company has EBIT of ₹600 crore, t 25.168%, βu 0.90, Rf 6.9%, ERP 7.0%, and V ₹6,000 crore. Using the illustrative rating grid in this note, compute WACC at D/V of 0%, 15%, 30%, 45% and 60%. Identify the minimum and the corresponding rating.

💡 Hint: Iterate once on Kd at each step. Compute interest with a starting Kd, find the rating, and recompute interest if the rating changes.

Q2. Downside-case optimum [L2]

Repeat Q1 using a downside EBIT of ₹420 crore for the coverage and rating steps only, keeping V unchanged. Explain how and why the optimal D/V shifts.

Q3. Model specification [L3]

Specify an Excel module that finds the optimal D/V for a listed Indian company. List the input cells, the rating grid, the circularity handling, the lookup formulas, the checks (for example, that interest does not exceed EBIT), the outputs (WACC curve chart, value at each D/V, implied rating) and two data tables. State how you would add a board-specified "AA− floor" constraint.

Q4. Interview: recommend a capital structure [L3]

A debt-free mid-cap FMCG company with stable EBIT of ₹800 crore asks whether it should borrow ₹2,000 crore to fund a buyback. Outline the analysis you would perform, the constraints you would apply, and how you would present the recommendation.
