Subtopic note

Covenants, Cash Sweeps, DSRA and Headroom Analysis

Scope: Covenants: maintenance vs incurrence covenants, cash sweeps, DSRA, and headroom analysis

Why it matters

Covenants turn capital structure theory into enforceable contract terms. A term-loan sanction letter, an NCD debenture trust deed or an ECB facility agreement will specify financial covenants, how and when they are tested, and what happens on breach. For the FP&A team, covenants are among the most important outputs of the model:

  • quarterly compliance certificates must be signed;
  • budget and capex plans must show headroom in a downside case;
  • a forecast breach must be escalated early enough to negotiate a waiver.

For auditors, a covenant breach on or before the balance sheet date can force reclassification of long-term debt as current under Ind AS 1. That affects going-concern assessment and working-capital ratios.

Core notes

Maintenance vs incurrence covenants

Feature Maintenance covenant Incurrence covenant
Test timing Periodically (quarterly or half-yearly, on TTM figures) whether or not anything happens Only when the borrower takes a specified action (raises debt, pays a dividend, makes an acquisition)
Typical metrics Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0x; DSCR ≥ 1.20x; ISCR ≥ 2.5x; TOL/TNW ≤ 3.0x; FACR ≥ 1.25x Pro-forma Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.5x after the new debt; Restricted Payments only if leverage is below a threshold
Common in Indian bank term loans, project finance, private credit High-yield bonds, some NCDs and ECB bonds, investment-grade bonds (lighter)
Effect of EBITDA decline Can trigger a breach even with no action taken No breach. The borrower is simply blocked from the action
Borrower flexibility Low High

Indian bank loans and most domestic NCDs rely mainly on maintenance covenants, plus negative covenants such as restrictions on changes in shareholding or management, further charge on assets, dividends during default, investments in group companies, and mergers.

Cash sweeps

A cash sweep requires a percentage of excess cash flow (ECF) to be applied to mandatory prepayment:

\[ \text{ECF} = \text{CFADS} - \text{Scheduled debt service} - \text{Permitted capex} - \text{DSRA top-up}, \qquad \text{Sweep} = s \times \max(0, \text{ECF}) \]

Here CFADS is cash flow available for debt service, which is EBITDA less tax, less ΔWC, less maintenance capex. The sweep percentage \(s\) often steps down as leverage falls, for example 75% above 3.0x, 50% between 2.0x and 3.0x, and 0% below 2.0x. A sweep accelerates deleveraging, but it restricts dividends and growth capex. In a model, the sweep creates circularity (interest depends on debt, which depends on the sweep, which depends on cash after interest). Handle it with a circuit-breaker toggle, or by computing interest on opening balances (Week 15).

Debt service reserve account (DSRA)

A DSRA holds cash, or a bank guarantee or LC in lieu of cash, equal to the next N months of debt service, where N is typically 1–2 quarters (3–6 months) of principal plus interest. Mechanics:

  • It is funded at financial close or from the first cash flows.
  • It is drawn when CFADS falls short, and must then be replenished before any distribution.
  • It is released at final repayment.
  • In net debt (s03), DSRA cash is restricted and is usually not deducted.

DSRA requirement in the model (with \(m\) the number of months to be covered):

\[ \text{DSRA}_t = \frac{m}{12} \times \text{Debt service over the next 12 months} \]

This is a simplification. Many facilities define it as the actual debt service due over the next \(m\) months.

Headroom analysis

Headroom is how far the underlying driver can move before a covenant breaches. For Net debt/EBITDA with covenant \(C\):

\[ \text{EBITDA headroom \%} = 1 - \frac{\text{Net debt}}{C \times \text{EBITDA}}, \qquad \text{Debt headroom} = C \times \text{EBITDA} - \text{Net debt} \]

For a DSCR covenant \(C_D\):

\[ \text{CFADS headroom \%} = 1 - \frac{C_D \times \text{Debt service}}{\text{CFADS}} \]

Illustration. TTM EBITDA is ₹800 crore, net debt ₹2,000 crore, and the covenant 3.0x. Leverage is 2.5x. EBITDA headroom is 1 − 2,000 ÷ 2,400 = 16.7%. EBITDA can fall to ₹667 crore before breach. Debt headroom is ₹400 crore. If the budget's downside scenario shows a 20% EBITDA fall, the company breaches unless it reduces net debt by about ₹80 crore beforehand (2,000 − 3.0 × 640 = 80).

Show headroom quarterly in a covenant dashboard with conditional formatting:

  • green above 20%;
  • amber 10–20%;
  • red below 10%.

Run it on base, downside and stress cases. In Excel:

=1 - NetDebt_TTM / (Covenant_NDE * EBITDA_TTM)
=Covenant_NDE * EBITDA_TTM - NetDebt_TTM

Breach consequences and remedies

  • Event of default. This triggers acceleration rights, cross-default to other facilities, and penal charges under the RBI 2023 penal-charges framework (charges, not compounded interest).
  • Waiver or amendment. Lenders agree not to act, usually for a fee, a margin step-up or tighter terms.
  • Equity cure. A sponsor injects equity that is counted as EBITDA, or as a debt reduction, for the test period. Cures are usually limited in number, for example no more than 2 over the life and not in consecutive quarters.
  • Covenant reset to looser levels when business conditions change. This is often combined with a rating action.

Accounting: Ind AS 1 classification after a breach

If a breach on or before the reporting date makes the loan payable on demand, the liability is classified as current, unless the conditions in Ind AS 1 are met. The Indian standard contains a carve-out from IFRS (Ind AS 1.74): the liability is not classified as current if the lender agreed, after the reporting period but before approval of the financial statements for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach. Under IFRS the waiver must be obtained by the reporting date. Check the current notified text, since the Ind AS 1 covenant-related amendments track IASB changes with a lag. Disclose breaches and their consequences under Ind AS 107 liquidity risk disclosures.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Definitions drive outcomes. Whether EBITDA is "adjusted" (excluding one-offs, ESOP costs or Ind AS 116 effects) and whether net debt includes leases can move the ratio by 0.5x or more. Negotiate these definitions upfront.
  • Pro-forma acquisitions. Covenants often allow the acquired entity's TTM EBITDA (and sometimes synergies) to be counted. Check the caps.
  • Testing frequency vs seasonality. A sugar or FMCG company with seasonal working capital may breach at a particular quarter-end. Negotiate average-based tests, or time the quarter-end debt level.
  • Cross-default thresholds. A minor breach on a small facility can trigger default on large ones. Map the cross- default web.
  • DSRA via bank guarantee frees cash, but it uses non-fund-based limits and incurs BG commission.

Key takeaways

  • Maintenance covenants are tested periodically and can breach on EBITDA decline alone. Incurrence covenants bite only when the borrower acts.
  • Cash sweeps apply a stepped share of excess cash flow to prepayment, which creates model circularity to manage.
  • A DSRA holds 1–2 quarters of debt service. It is restricted cash, excluded from net debt.
  • Headroom = 1 − Net debt ÷ (Covenant × EBITDA). Monitor it quarterly in base and downside cases.
  • Ind AS 1.74 lets a post-reporting-date waiver avoid current classification. This is an Indian carve-out from IFRS.

Practice Questions

Q1. Headroom and breach point [L1]

A company has TTM EBITDA of ₹1,250 crore and net debt of ₹3,400 crore. Its covenants are Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.25x and ISCR ≥ 3.0x, with interest of ₹290 crore. Compute headroom on both covenants as a percentage of EBITDA, and state the EBITDA level at which each breaches.

Q2. Cash sweep schedule [L2]

A project's CFADS is ₹180 crore, ₹200 crore, ₹230 crore and ₹260 crore in years 1–4. Scheduled debt service is ₹140 crore a year, of which interest is ₹70 crore, ₹64 crore, ₹57 crore and ₹49 crore. Permitted capex is ₹10 crore a year, and there is no DSRA top-up after year 1, when it is ₹15 crore. Opening debt is ₹700 crore. The sweep is 75% while Net debt/EBITDA > 3.0x and 50% otherwise, with EBITDA ₹230 crore, ₹255 crore, ₹285 crore and ₹320 crore. Build the four-year sweep and debt roll-forward, ignoring the interest effect of the sweep within the year.

💡 Hint: Test the leverage band using opening debt each year to avoid circularity.

Q3. Ind AS 1 classification [L2]

At 31 March 2025, a company is in breach of its DSCR covenant on a ₹600 crore term loan due in 2030, and the lender can demand repayment. The lender issues a waiver letter on 20 April 2025, and the financial statements are approved on 15 May 2025. Explain the classification under Ind AS and under IFRS, and the disclosures required.

Q4. Covenant negotiation [L3]

You are negotiating a ₹2,000 crore term loan for a capex programme that will depress EBITDA during a two-year ramp-up. Draft a term-sheet position on: the covenant metrics and levels by year (step-downs), EBITDA and net-debt definitions, the testing frequency, equity-cure rights, the cash sweep and the DSRA. Justify each position against the bank's likely counter-position.
