Subtopic note

Credit Metrics Used by Indian Banks and Rating Agencies

Scope: Credit metrics: interest coverage, DSCR, Net Debt/EBITDA, Debt/Equity, FFO/Debt, TOL/TNW used by Indian banks and rating agencies

Why it matters

Capital structure decisions are made against the metrics lenders and rating agencies actually use. A ₹3,000 crore expansion that is fine for equity holders can still be blocked if it pushes DSCR below the bank's 1.3x floor or Net Debt/EBITDA above the rating agency's downgrade trigger. In practice you will meet these metrics in:

  • bank credit appraisal notes (CAM) and term-loan sanction letters;
  • consortium stock and receivables statements for working-capital limits;
  • rating rationales published by CRISIL, ICRA, CARE and India Ratings;
  • covenant compliance certificates signed by the CFO each quarter.

An FP&A analyst must compute each metric exactly as the lender defines it, forecast it in the 3-statement model, and flag headroom early.

Core notes

Interest coverage ratio (ICR / ISCR)

\[ \text{ICR} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Interest}} \]

Indian banks often compute ISCR as (PBT + Interest + Depreciation) ÷ Interest, which is effectively EBITDA-based. Rating agencies quote "PBILDT interest coverage" (CARE, India Ratings), where PBILDT is profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation and tax, or operating profit ÷ interest. Interest usually means gross finance costs, including interest on lease liabilities under Ind AS 116 and the unwinding of discount on deferred consideration. Some definitions exclude non-cash Ind AS 109 EIR amortisation. Typical comfort levels are > 3x for mid-investment grade (A category) and > 5–6x for AA or AAA, varying by sector.

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest on term debt}}{\text{Interest on term debt} + \text{Principal repayment of term debt}} \]

This is the classic Indian bank and project-finance definition. Points to watch:

  • It is computed annually in the project's repayment schedule.
  • Banks look at both the minimum DSCR (often ≥ 1.20x) and the average DSCR over the loan life (often 1.30–1.50x for infrastructure and manufacturing projects).
  • Rating agencies also use a cash version: (EBITDA − tax − maintenance capex ± ΔWC) ÷ (interest + principal).
  • Lender definitions vary. Check whether non-cash items, lease payments, interest on working-capital limits and dividends are included.

Net Debt/EBITDA

\[ \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{EBITDA}} = \frac{\text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities} + \text{Debt-like items} - \text{Cash and liquid investments}}{\text{EBITDA}} \]

This is the headline leverage metric for rating agencies, bond covenants and M&A financing. EBITDA is usually trailing-twelve-month (TTM). Full definitions are in s03 and c02.

Debt/Equity (gearing) and TOL/TNW

\[ \text{D/E} = \frac{\text{Total debt}}{\text{Tangible net worth}}, \qquad \text{TOL/TNW} = \frac{\text{Total outside liabilities}}{\text{Tangible net worth}} \]

Tangible net worth (TNW) is share capital + reserves − intangibles (goodwill, brand) − deferred tax assets (often) − miscellaneous expenditure not written off − revaluation reserve. Banks may add quasi-equity such as promoter loans subordinated to bank debt.

TOL (total outside liabilities) includes all liabilities other than equity: borrowings, trade payables, provisions, customer advances and other liabilities. It captures reliance on creditors generally, not just lenders. Indian banks typically cap TOL/TNW at around 3x for working-capital borrowers and 4x for some trading entities, following internal credit policies influenced by the Tandon and Chore committee legacy. Gearing (D/E) of < 1x is usually comfortable for manufacturing. Rating agencies use "overall gearing" = total debt ÷ TNW, including lease liabilities and sometimes acceptances.

FFO/Debt

\[ \frac{\text{FFO}}{\text{Debt}} = \frac{\text{EBITDA} - \text{Net interest paid} - \text{Cash tax paid}}{\text{Total debt}} \]

This is the S&P-style metric, which CRISIL uses as an affiliate. Indian agencies more commonly use NCA/TD (net cash accruals ÷ total debt), where NCA = PAT + depreciation + other non-cash charges − dividends (in some definitions). An NCA/TD of 0.25–0.40 or above is typical for comfortable investment grade. The metric shows how quickly debt could be repaid from internal cash.

Other metrics you will see

  • Current ratio ≥ 1.33x (the second method of lending, MPBF, from the Tandon committee), still used in working-capital appraisal.
  • FACR (fixed asset coverage ratio) = net fixed assets ÷ term debt, often ≥ 1.25–1.33x.
  • Debt/EBITDA by segment for conglomerates, and interest/sales for working-capital-heavy sectors.
  • Promoter pledge levels and group exposure: qualitative inputs that rating agencies weigh heavily.

Worked illustration

FY25 data for an NSE-listed auto-ancillary (₹ crore):

Item Amount
EBITDA 640
Depreciation 180
EBIT 460
Finance cost (incl. lease interest 12) 110
PAT 262
Term-loan principal due in FY26 220
Borrowings 1,050 + lease liabilities 120 1,170
Cash and liquid investments 230
Net worth 1,900 (intangibles 150, DTA 30) TNW 1,720
Total outside liabilities 2,450

Metrics:

Metric Value
EBIT interest cover 460 ÷ 110 = 4.2x
EBITDA interest cover 5.8x
DSCR (262 + 180 + 110) ÷ (110 + 220) = 1.67x (all finance cost treated as term-debt interest, for simplicity)
Net debt/EBITDA (1,170 − 230) ÷ 640 = 1.47x
Gearing 1,170 ÷ 1,720 = 0.68x
TOL/TNW 2,450 ÷ 1,720 = 1.42x
NCA/TD (no dividends) (262 + 180) ÷ 1,170 = 0.38

These metrics are consistent with a solid A to A+ profile in many agencies' manufacturing grids. Business risk factors still dominate the final rating.

Model implementation

Build a "credit metrics" block below the debt schedule. Each metric should use named ranges with lender-specific definitions, have conditional formatting against covenant thresholds, and show a "headroom" column (s06). Compute the metrics on TTM figures for each quarter end (see the topic pitfalls).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capitalised interest. Interest capitalised under Ind AS 23 is not in the P&L finance cost. Lenders usually include it in the ICR and DSCR denominator, because it must still be paid.
  • Lease liabilities. After Ind AS 116, EBITDA rises because rent is removed and interest rises. Some bank definitions use pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA (EBITDAR minus rent) to keep old covenants comparable.
  • Holding companies. Standalone metrics can look weak while consolidated metrics look strong, or the reverse. Agencies assess the consolidated metrics and the cash-flow fungibility between group entities.
  • Exceptional items. Agree in the facility agreement whether one-off gains or losses are excluded from EBITDA (an "adjusted EBITDA" definition). This is a negotiable point.
  • Negative TNW (after accumulated losses) makes TOL/TNW meaningless, and is usually itself a covenant breach.

Key takeaways

  • ICR measures the ability to pay interest. DSCR adds principal repayments, which is the binding test for amortising term loans.
  • Net debt/EBITDA is the headline rating and covenant leverage metric. Include leases if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116.
  • TOL/TNW captures reliance on all creditors and is a staple of Indian bank working-capital appraisal.
  • FFO/Debt and NCA/TD show debt payback capacity from internal accruals.
  • Always use the lender's exact definition. It varies on capitalised interest, leases, exceptional items and dividends.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the full metric set [L1]

From the following FY25 data (₹ crore), compute EBIT and EBITDA interest cover, DSCR, net debt/EBITDA, gearing, TOL/TNW and NCA/TD:

  • EBITDA 1,150; depreciation 320; P&L finance cost 240; further interest of 30 capitalised under Ind AS 23 (treat it as interest for the coverage ratios); PAT 430; dividends 90;
  • principal repayments 380;
  • borrowings 2,600; lease liabilities 200; cash 350;
  • net worth 3,400, including goodwill 250 and revaluation reserve 120;
  • total outside liabilities 5,100.

Q2. Lender-definition sensitivity [L2]

Using the Q1 data, recompute DSCR under the following definitions: (a) bank definition, including capitalised interest in the denominator; (b) a cash DSCR = (EBITDA − cash tax 140 − maintenance capex 200) ÷ (interest + principal); (c) a definition that deducts dividends from the numerator. Explain which a project lender would rely on and why.

Q3. Working-capital appraisal [L2]

A trading company has TNW of ₹80 crore, bank borrowings of ₹150 crore, trade payables of ₹90 crore, and other liabilities of ₹20 crore. Its bank's policy caps TOL/TNW at 3.0x. The company wants an additional ₹50 crore of cash-credit limits. Compute the post-enhancement TOL/TNW and the promoter infusion, as equity or as a subordinated loan accepted as quasi-equity, needed to stay within policy.

💡 Hint: Quasi-equity raises TNW and is excluded from TOL, so it works on both the numerator and the denominator.

Q4. Credit memo summary [L3]

Draft the "financial risk profile" paragraph of a rating-style credit memo for the company in the worked illustration. Comment on each metric, its trend relative to an assumed prior year (state your assumptions), and the triggers that could lead to a downgrade.
