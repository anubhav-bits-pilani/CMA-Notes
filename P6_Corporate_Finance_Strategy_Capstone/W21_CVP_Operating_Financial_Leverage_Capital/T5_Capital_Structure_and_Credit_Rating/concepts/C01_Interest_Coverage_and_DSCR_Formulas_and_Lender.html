Key concept

Interest Coverage and DSCR: Formulas and Lender Variants

Concept: Interest coverage = EBIT ÷ Interest (or EBITDA ÷ Interest); DSCR = (PAT + Depreciation + Interest) ÷ (Interest + Principal repayment), lender definitions vary

Statement

\[ \text{ICR} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Interest}} \]

\[ \text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Principal repayment}} \]

where:

  • EBIT = operating profit before finance costs and tax;
  • EBITDA = EBIT + depreciation and amortisation;
  • Interest = finance cost on the debt being tested, including interest capitalised during construction where the lender requires it;
  • PAT = profit after tax;
  • Principal repayment = scheduled repayment of term debt falling due in the period. Bullet maturities that are expected to be refinanced are often excluded.

Explanation

ICR asks whether operating profit can cover interest. DSCR asks whether cash accruals can cover both interest and the principal falling due. That makes DSCR the binding test for amortising term loans and project finance.

The numerator PAT + Depreciation + Interest is a cash-accrual proxy. Adding back depreciation turns PAT into cash from operations before working capital. Adding back interest restores the amount available to service it. Lenders vary the definition:

  • Cash DSCR (CFADS-based): (EBITDA − cash tax − ΔWC − maintenance capex) ÷ debt service. Used in project finance and by rating agencies.
  • Whether lease payments (Ind AS 116 principal and interest) are in debt service.
  • Whether non-cash items (provisions, ESOP charges, Ind AS 109 EIR unwinding, fair-value gains) are adjusted.
  • Whether dividends are deducted from the numerator.
  • Whether interest and principal on working-capital limits are included. They are usually excluded, since the limits revolve.

Benchmarks:

  • ICR above 3x suggests an A rating or better for most industrials. Above 5–6x suggests AA or better.
  • Minimum DSCR is typically ≥ 1.20x and average DSCR 1.30–1.50x for Indian term lending. RBI does not prescribe one number. Each bank's credit policy and project type sets it.

Illustration

A project, year 3 of operations (₹ crore):

Item Amount
EBITDA 210
Depreciation 60
Interest on term loan 55
PBT 95
Tax at 25.168% 24
PAT 71
Principal repayment 80

The ratios are:

  • EBIT ICR = 150 ÷ 55 = 2.7x.
  • EBITDA ICR = 210 ÷ 55 = 3.8x.
  • DSCR = (71 + 60 + 55) ÷ (55 + 80) = 186 ÷ 135 = 1.38x.

If the bank's cash definition deducts maintenance capex of ₹20 crore and a ΔWC of ₹10 crore, the CFADS-based DSCR is (210 − 24 − 10 − 20) ÷ 135 = 156 ÷ 135 = 1.16x. That is below a 1.20x covenant. The definition decides compliance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute ICR and DSCR [L1]

EBITDA is ₹480 crore, depreciation ₹130 crore, finance cost ₹95 crore (with a further ₹15 crore capitalised under Ind AS 23), and tax 25.168% on PBT. Principal due is ₹160 crore. Compute EBIT ICR, EBITDA ICR (with and without the capitalised interest) and DSCR.

Q2. Debt sizing to a DSCR target [L2]

A solar SPV has stable CFADS of ₹90 crore a year for 15 years. The lender requires a minimum DSCR of 1.30x, and the interest rate is 9.0%. Compute the maximum annual debt service, and the maximum debt that could be raised on a level-annuity repayment basis.

💡 Hint: Maximum debt service = CFADS ÷ 1.30. Maximum debt is its PV at 9% over 15 years.

Q3. Definition dispute [L2]

A borrower reports DSCR of 1.28x on the PAT + D + I definition. The bank's sanction letter defines DSCR as (EBITDA − tax − dividends) ÷ (interest + principal + lease payments). With EBITDA ₹300 crore, tax ₹35 crore, dividends ₹40 crore, interest ₹60 crore, principal ₹90 crore and lease payments ₹25 crore, recompute the ratio and assess compliance with a 1.25x covenant.
