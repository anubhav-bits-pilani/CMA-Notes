Key concept

WACC with Leverage-Dependent Ke and Kd

Concept: WACC = E/V × Ke + D/V × Kd × (1 − t); Ke rises with leverage via relevered beta

Statement

\[ \text{WACC} = \frac{E}{V} K_e + \frac{D}{V} K_d (1 - t) \]

\[ K_e = R_f + \beta_L \times \text{ERP}, \qquad \beta_L = \beta_u \left[1 + (1 - t)\frac{D}{E}\right] \]

where:

  • \(E\), \(D\) = the market values (or target values) of equity and debt, with \(V = E + D\). Debt includes lease liabilities when EBITDA and FCFF are post-Ind AS 116;
  • \(K_e\) = the cost of equity from CAPM, using the relevered beta at the same D/E as the weights;
  • \(K_d\) = the pre-tax marginal cost of debt at the rating implied by that leverage;
  • \(t\) = the marginal tax rate at which interest is actually deducted;
  • \(R_f\) = the risk-free rate (10-year G-sec);
  • ERP = the equity risk premium;
  • \(\beta_u\) = the unlevered (asset) beta.

Explanation

WACC is the blended required return of all capital providers, used to discount FCFF. Three internal consistencies must hold:

  1. Weights, beta and Kd must all reflect the same capital structure. If you use a target D/V of 30%, relever beta at the corresponding D/E (0.43) and use the Kd consistent with the rating at 30% D/V.
  2. Market or target weights, not book weights. Book equity in India is often far below market value (high-ROE companies), or above it (distressed companies). Book weights therefore misstate WACC (see the topic pitfalls).
  3. Cash-flow consistency. WACC goes with FCFF (unlevered, pre-interest cash flows). Do not deduct interest in the cash flows and also use WACC.

As leverage rises, two effects pull in opposite directions:

  • Mix effect: more weight on cheaper after-tax debt lowers WACC.
  • Risk effect: βL rises, so Ke rises. Beyond moderate leverage, Kd also rises with the falling rating.

Under MM with taxes the net effect is always a decline. In reality the WACC curve is U-shaped (s08).

Illustration

Rf 7.0%, ERP 7.0%, βu 0.85, t 25.168%.

D/V D/E βL Ke Rating Kd WACC
0% 0.00 0.850 12.95% — — 12.95%
20% 0.25 1.009 14.06% AA 8.1% 0.8 × 14.06 + 0.2 × 6.06 = 12.46%
40% 0.67 1.274 15.92% BBB− 10.5% 0.6 × 15.92 + 0.4 × 7.86 = 12.69%

Note that a company using the 20% D/V weights with a βL computed at its current zero leverage (Ke 12.95%) would compute WACC as 11.57%, understating it by about 90 bps. That error alone can turn a negative-NPV project positive.

Practice Questions

Q1. Consistent WACC [L1]

Rf is 6.9%, ERP 7.0%, βu 0.75 and t 25.168%. The target D/V is 35%, and Kd at that leverage is 9.1%. Compute βL, Ke and WACC. Then compute the WACC an analyst would get by mistakenly using the observed βL of 0.80, measured when the company had D/E of 0.10, and state the error.

Q2. Book vs market weights [L2]

A listed FMCG company has book equity of ₹4,000 crore, market capitalisation of ₹60,000 crore, and debt (including leases) of ₹1,500 crore at a Kd of 8.0%. Its Ke is 12.5% and t is 25.168%. Compute WACC using book weights and using market weights. Explain which is correct and the direction of the error.

💡 Hint: Compute D/V under each basis. The direction of the error depends on which weight is overstated.

Q3. Kd at the margin [L2]

A company's existing debt carries a 7.4% coupon from 2021 NCDs. The current market yield for its rating is 8.6%. Explain which Kd belongs in WACC and why, and how you would treat the difference in valuing the existing debt.
