Key concept

Net Debt/EBITDA and Investment-Grade Covenant Thresholds

Concept: Net Debt/EBITDA = (Total debt + lease liabilities − cash & equivalents) ÷ EBITDA; covenant thresholds typically 2.5–3.5x for investment grade

Statement

\[ \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{EBITDA}} = \frac{\text{Total debt} + \text{Lease liabilities} - \text{Cash and cash equivalents (incl. liquid investments)}}{\text{EBITDA}_{\text{TTM}}} \]

where:

  • total debt = all borrowings (long-term, short-term and current maturities), and debt-like items where the definition includes them;
  • lease liabilities = Ind AS 116 current and non-current lease liabilities;
  • cash and cash equivalents = unrestricted cash, bank balances and liquid investments;
  • EBITDA = trailing-twelve-month EBITDA, post-Ind AS 116 (rent excluded) when leases are in the numerator.

Typical maintenance-covenant thresholds for investment-grade Indian corporates fall in a band of 2.5x–3.5x. They are tighter (≤ 2.0–2.5x) for cyclical sectors and higher-rated issuers, and looser (4–5x+) for contracted infrastructure with stable cash flows.

Explanation

The ratio says how many years of current EBITDA it would take to repay net debt, ignoring tax, capex and working capital. It is the headline leverage metric because:

  • it uses EBITDA, a proxy for debt-service capacity that is not affected by depreciation policy;
  • it nets off cash, recognising liquidity;
  • it is simple to test quarterly.

Conditions for a valid ratio:

  1. Perimeter consistency. Leases are in the numerator only if rent is out of EBITDA. Acquired debt is included only with the acquired EBITDA (pro forma).
  2. TTM EBITDA, not the annual figure from the last year-end, when testing at quarter ends.
  3. Unrestricted cash only. Exclude DSRA, lien-marked deposits and trapped cash.
  4. The covenant definition overrides. Some facilities use gross debt, average debt, or "adjusted EBITDA" with permitted add-backs.

Limitations: the ratio ignores interest rates (a 3x ratio at 7% and at 12% are very different), capex needs, tax and the debt maturity profile. Use it alongside ICR, DSCR and FFO/Debt.

Illustration

Quarter ended 30 September 2025 (₹ crore):

Item Amount
Borrowings 3,600
Lease liabilities 420
Cash and liquid funds (of which 80 lien-marked) 680
EBITDA: Q3 FY25 260, Q4 FY25 300, Q1 FY26 240, Q2 FY26 250 TTM 1,050
  • Net debt = 3,600 + 420 − (680 − 80) = 3,420.
  • Ratio = 3,420 ÷ 1,050 = 3.26x.
  • Against a 3.25x covenant, this is a narrow breach, caused entirely by the restricted cash exclusion.
  • Using the FY25 annual EBITDA (say ₹1,120 crore) would give 3.05x and mask the breach. That is the classic testing-basis error.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and test [L1]

Borrowings are ₹2,200 crore, leases ₹160 crore, unrestricted cash ₹390 crore and DSRA ₹60 crore. The last four quarters' EBITDA were ₹180 crore, ₹205 crore, ₹170 crore and ₹195 crore. Compute Net debt/EBITDA and the headroom against a 3.0x covenant.

Q2. Pre- vs post-Ind AS 116 [L2]

A retailer reports post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA of ₹900 crore, rent of ₹380 crore, borrowings of ₹600 crore, lease liabilities of ₹2,100 crore and cash of ₹250 crore. Compute the ratio on both a post-Ind AS 116 and a pre-Ind AS 116 basis. Explain which a peer comparison should use, and why the two are not interchangeable.

💡 Hint: On the pre-Ind AS 116 basis, remove rent from EBITDA and exclude lease liabilities from net debt together.

Q3. Threshold judgement [L2]

Explain why a toll-road InvIT might comfortably carry Net debt/EBITDA of 5x at AAA, while a steel company at 3x might be rated A−. Use the concepts of cash-flow volatility and asset life.
