Key concept

Debt Capacity from a Target Net Debt/EBITDA Multiple

Concept: Debt capacity = Target Net Debt/EBITDA × EBITDA − existing net debt

Statement

\[ \text{Incremental debt capacity} = M^{*} \times \text{EBITDA} - \text{Net debt}_{\text{existing}} \]

where:

  • \(M^{*}\) = the target Net debt/EBITDA multiple. It is the lower of: the covenant threshold less a safety buffer, the rating-agency downgrade trigger for the desired rating, and the board's internal policy;
  • EBITDA = the EBITDA on which capacity is sized. Use TTM for covenant testing, and forward or downside EBITDA for planning, adding pro-forma EBITDA of any asset being acquired;
  • \(\text{Net debt}_{\text{existing}}\) = current net debt on the same definition as the target multiple, including leases if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116.

A complete sizing also checks the coverage-based capacity, and takes the minimum:

\[ \text{Capacity}_{\text{ICR}} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{ICR}^{*} \times K_d} - D_{\text{existing}}, \qquad \text{Capacity}_{\text{DSCR}}: \text{max debt s.t. } \frac{\text{CFADS}}{\text{Debt service}} \ge \text{DSCR}^{*} \]

Explanation

Debt capacity is the financing envelope for capex, M&A or buybacks (topic t4). The leverage multiple is used because rating agencies and lenders anchor on it. Several judgement points matter:

  • Which EBITDA? A capex-funded expansion produces EBITDA only after commissioning. Sizing on post-commissioning EBITDA while the debt is drawn during construction overstates capacity. Agencies look at peak leverage during construction.
  • Downside EBITDA. For cyclicals (steel, chemicals), size on mid-cycle or downside EBITDA, so that a 20% EBITDA fall does not breach the covenant.
  • Headroom buffer. If the covenant is 3.0x, a prudent target might be 2.5x, leaving about 17% EBITDA headroom.
  • Ratings vs covenants. To protect an AA− rating with a downgrade trigger of 2.25x, the rating constraint binds before the 3.0x covenant.
  • Rate sensitivity. At higher interest rates, ICR may bind before the leverage multiple.

Illustration

An NSE-listed chemicals company has FY26 forward EBITDA of ₹1,400 crore, a downside EBITDA of ₹1,150 crore, current net debt (including leases) of ₹1,900 crore, and EBIT of ₹1,050 crore. The covenant is 3.0x, the AA rating trigger is 2.25x, the internal ICR floor is 4.0x, and Kd at AA is 8.3%. Existing gross debt is ₹2,300 crore.

Constraint Computation Capacity (₹ cr)
Covenant with buffer (2.5x on forward EBITDA) 2.5 × 1,400 − 1,900 1,600
Rating trigger (2.25x on forward EBITDA) 2.25 × 1,400 − 1,900 1,250
Covenant on downside EBITDA (3.0x) 3.0 × 1,150 − 1,900 1,550
ICR ≥ 4.0x 1,050 ÷ (4.0 × 8.3%) − 2,300 ≈ 863
Binding capacity Minimum ≈ 860

Coverage, not leverage, binds here. If the company plans a ₹1,500 crore expansion, roughly ₹640 crore must come from internal accruals, equity or hybrids. Alternatively, the board must accept a lower rating.

Practice Questions

Q1. Capacity on three constraints [L1]

EBITDA is ₹600 crore, EBIT ₹470 crore, net debt ₹950 crore and gross debt ₹1,100 crore. The target multiple is 2.5x, the ICR floor 3.5x, and Kd 9.0%. Compute capacity under the leverage and coverage constraints and identify the binding one.

Q2. Construction-phase capacity [L2]

A company with TTM EBITDA of ₹800 crore and net debt of ₹1,200 crore plans a ₹2,400 crore plant. It will be built over FY26–FY27 and add ₹450 crore of EBITDA from FY28. The covenant is 3.0x, tested annually on TTM figures. Determine the maximum debt funding for the plant if the covenant must hold at every year-end, and the equity or internal accruals needed. Assume FCF before capex of ₹350 crore per year.

💡 Hint: Test FY26 and FY27 year-end leverage with the new EBITDA excluded. That is usually where capacity binds.

Q3. Rating-consistent capacity for an acquisition [L3]

A company rated AA− (downgrade trigger: Net debt/EBITDA > 2.0x sustained) with EBITDA of ₹2,500 crore and net debt of ₹2,800 crore wants to acquire a target with EBITDA of ₹400 crore, for ₹4,000 crore. Compute the debt capacity on a pro-forma basis, the equity or hybrid funding required to protect the rating, and how much deleveraging over two years would allow a temporary breach that the agency might tolerate.
