Objective: Convert your CMA credential and 6-project portfolio into interview offers across Financial Reporting, Financial Modelling, and FP&A roles.

How this week fits

This is the closing week of Phase 6 (Corporate Finance Strategy, Capstone and Career Launch). By now you have built six portfolio projects across the curriculum: an Ind AS close pack and technical memos (Weeks 1–4), Excel/Power Query automation (Weeks 9–11), a linked 3-statement model (Weeks 13–15), a WACC/DCF valuation (Week 16), budgeting and rolling-forecast work (Weeks 17–18), PVM variance analysis and an MIS/Power BI reporting pack (Weeks 19–20), capital budgeting cases (Week 21), and the capstone dossier assembled in Week 23. Week 24 does not teach new accounting or finance. It converts what you already know into evidence that a recruiter, a Big 4 manager, a GCC controller or an FP&A head can test in 30–60 minutes.

The week has five topics. Topic 1 repositions your CV and LinkedIn so that a CMA with audit history reads as a reporting and FP&A candidate. Topic 2 drills timed Excel modelling tests, because most modelling and FP&A shortlists in India now include a take-home or live build. Topic 3 rehearses Ind AS and 3-statement linkage questions. Topic 4 rehearses valuation and FP&A business-partnering cases. Topic 5 maps target roles and runs the outreach pipeline. The prerequisite is that your portfolio files open, balance and are explainable line by line. What comes next is the job search itself: the habits built here (anchored answers, timed builds, tracked outreach) are the operating rhythm for the next 8–12 weeks.

Refresh before starting

Ind AS essentials and 3-statement linkage logic

Ind AS 115, 116, 109, 12, 103 essentials and 3-statement linkage logic (→ Weeks 1–4, 13–15)

  • Ind AS 115: five steps (contract, performance obligations, transaction price, allocation, recognition); over-time criteria in Ind AS 115.35; variable consideration constrained to the amount highly probable not to reverse; principal vs agent turns on control before transfer.
  • Ind AS 116: lessee recognises ROU asset and lease liability at PV of lease payments (incremental borrowing rate if implicit rate not determinable); exemptions for short-term (12 months or less) and low-value leases; EBITDA rises, expense is front-loaded, principal goes to financing cash flow.
  • Ind AS 109: classification by business model and SPPI; ECL three-stage general approach versus simplified lifetime ECL for trade receivables without a significant financing component (provision matrix).
  • Ind AS 12: balance-sheet approach; deferred tax = temporary difference × enacted or substantively enacted rate; DTA only if probable taxable profit; remeasurement when moving to Sec 115BAA (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%).
  • Ind AS 103: acquisition method, PPA to identifiable assets/liabilities at fair value, goodwill or bargain purchase (bargain purchase gain to OCI/capital reserve under the Ind AS carve-out).
  • Linkage: every P&L line hits retained earnings via PAT; non-cash items reverse in CFO; balance-sheet movements explain CFO/CFI/CFF; cash on the CFS equals cash on the BS.

WACC/DCF assumptions and PVM variance mechanics

WACC/DCF assumptions and sensitivities (→ Week 16) and PVM variance mechanics (→ Week 19)

  • \(K_e = R_f + \beta \times \text{ERP}\) with \(R_f\) from the 10-year G-sec yield, beta re-levered to target D/E, ERP sourced (e.g. Damodaran India, or a stated survey).
  • \(K_d\) after tax \(= (R_f + \text{spread}) \times (1 - t)\); weights at target market-value capital structure.
  • Terminal value by Gordon growth \(\text{TV} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{n+1}}{\text{WACC} - g}\) with \(g\) at or below long-run nominal GDP; check TV as a % of EV (typically 60–80% for a mature business); mid-year convention shifts discount periods by 0.5.
  • Sensitivity tables: WACC ±1% against \(g\) ±0.5%, built with Excel data tables.
  • PVM: price variance at actual quantity; volume variance at budget weighted-average price; mix variance at budget prices on the actual-versus-budget mix; the three must sum to the total revenue variance.

Own portfolio models

Own portfolio models: be able to explain every assumption and check

For each of your six projects, write a one-page "defence card": purpose, three key assumptions with sources, the checks built in (balance check, cash tie-out, circularity switch, sum-of-variance check), one limitation, and one thing you would do differently. An interviewer who opens your DCF and asks "why 5% terminal growth?" or "what does cell H42 do?" must get a sourced, one-sentence answer. If you cannot explain a formula, rebuild it until you can.

Excel speed and a blank-sheet routine

Excel speed shortcuts and a blank-sheet build routine

Recall the core set: F2 (edit), F4 (toggle absolute reference), Ctrl+[ (trace precedents), Ctrl+Shift+arrows (select to edge), Ctrl+D/Ctrl+R (fill down/right), Alt+E+S+V or Ctrl+Alt+V then V (paste values), Alt+H+O+I (autofit column), Ctrl+1 (format cells), Alt+W+F+F (freeze panes). The blank-sheet routine: inputs block at the top with blue font, timeline row, IS, BS roll-forwards, CFS, balance check, outputs. Practise it until a skeleton takes under 10 minutes.

Cross-cutting concepts

Anchor every answer

Every answer anchored with a number, a standard reference or a portfolio example

Interviewers in FAAS, FP&A and valuation roles are screening for whether you can be trusted with a number in front of a client or a CFO. An unanchored answer ("leases now go on the balance sheet") shows awareness. An anchored answer ("under Ind AS 116 a five-year lease at ₹1 crore a year in arrears, discounted at a 9% incremental borrowing rate, creates a lease liability of about ₹3.89 crore; in my close pack, moving rent out of opex added 40 bps to EBITDA margin") shows competence. Each answer should carry at least one of three anchors: a number (worked on the spot, even approximately), a standard or legal reference (Ind AS 115.35, Sec 115BAA, CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)), or a portfolio example ("in my PVM project the mix effect was −₹42 lakh because..."). Build a personal anchor bank: for each of the 30 Interview Bank questions, write down which anchor you will use. When you do not know something, anchor the reasoning instead: state the principle, the number you would need, and how you would verify it. This habit also shortens answers, because the anchor replaces vague qualification.

One consistent narrative

Consistent personal narrative across CV, LinkedIn, outreach and interviews

Your story must be identical across every touchpoint: "CMA with X years of statutory, internal and cost audit, moving into financial reporting and FP&A, with a portfolio of six projects covering Ind AS close, 3-statement/DCF and Power BI reporting." The CV summary, LinkedIn headline and About section, Naukri resume headline, cold outreach message and the "tell me about yourself" answer should all express this in different lengths (one line, three lines, 30 seconds, 2 minutes). Inconsistency is a credibility leak: a recruiter who sees "Audit Executive" on Naukri, "Senior Associate" on LinkedIn and "Assistant Manager" on the CV assumes padding, and background verification in Indian hiring will surface the true designation and dates. The narrative should also explain the transition: why reporting/FP&A, why now, and what evidence (portfolio, Ind AS depth from audit) makes the move low-risk for the employer. Write the four lengths once, store them in one document, and copy from there.

Mock 1, 2 and 3 with recorded feedback

Exercise: mock 1 (Ind AS technical), mock 2 (live 3-statement/DCF test), mock 3 (FP&A business-partnering case) with recorded feedback

Three mocks map to the three role families. Mock 1 is a 45-minute Ind AS technical interview: 8–10 questions across Ind AS 115, 116, 109, 12 and 103 plus two linkage questions, delivered by a peer, mentor or a recorded self-interview against the question list. Mock 2 is a 90-minute live build: a prompt with three years of history and assumptions, requiring a balancing 3-statement model and a simple DCF, done on screen-share or recorded with OBS/Teams so you can review keystrokes and hesitation. Mock 3 is a 30-minute business-partnering case: a margin decline or capex request with a data sheet, where you must deliver a headline, three drivers with numbers and a recommendation. Record all three (with consent if a peer is involved). Recording matters because you cannot judge your own filler words, pace or screen navigation in real time. After each mock, write feedback within the hour: three things that worked, three things that failed, and the exact question or step where time was lost.

Interview Bank review with a gap log

Exercise: review all 30 Interview Bank questions with a 2-minute structured answer each and a gap log

The Interview Bank is the curriculum's 30 core questions (10 technical accounting, 10 modelling/valuation, 10 FP&A/behavioural). For each, draft and say aloud a 2-minute answer using the structure: headline (one sentence that answers the question), three supporting points with at least one number or reference, and a close (risk, implication or next step). Time each answer with a phone stopwatch; anything over 2:15 gets cut. Maintain a gap log in Excel with columns: question ID, topic, confidence (1–5), gap description, source to fix (week/note/standard), fix date, re-test score. A gap is anything where you hesitated over 5 seconds, needed notes, or gave a wrong number. Re-test every question rated 3 or below after fixing. The gap log is itself a deliverable: it shows you which week's notes to revisit before a specific interview (e.g. Ind AS 12 gaps before a FAAS interview, PVM gaps before an FP&A interview).

Score the mocks and redo the weakest area

Exercise: score each mock on technical accuracy, structure, time management and communication; redo the weakest area

Use a 4 × 5 rubric: technical accuracy (correct numbers, correct standard, correct treatment), structure (headline first, logical sequence, clear close), time management (finished within the slot, balanced effort across parts), and communication (clarity, pace, absence of filler, confidence under challenge). Score each 1–5 with written evidence, e.g. "Technical 3: correct ROU measurement but forgot DTA on lease liability." Total out of 20 per mock; a pass mark of 15 with no dimension below 3 is a reasonable bar. Then pick the weakest dimension across all three mocks, not the weakest mock, and redo the specific component: if time management on mock 2 scored 2, redo the build with a fresh prompt and the 10/55/15/10 time split; if structure on mock 3 scored 2, redo the case using headline–drivers–recommendation. Re-score and record the delta. Improvement on the redo, not the first score, is the evidence of readiness.

Hands-on exercise brief

Complete 3 full mock interviews (1 Technical Accounting/Ind AS, 1 Live 3-Statement/DCF Test, 1 FP&A Business Partnering Case) and review all 30 Interview Bank questions.

Inputs and assumptions

Mock 1 — Ind AS technical (45 min). Prepare a question set covering: a software licence plus 2-year support contract of ₹60 lakh (allocation by stand-alone selling price); a 5-year office lease at ₹24 lakh a year payable in arrears, IBR 10%, with ₹2 lakh initial direct costs; trade receivables of ₹8 crore with a provision matrix (0–30 days 1%, 31–90 days 4%, 91–180 days 15%, over 180 days 50%); a company switching to Sec 115BAA with opening net DTL of ₹3 crore measured at 34.944%; an acquisition at ₹120 crore for 100% with net identifiable assets of ₹95 crore after PPA; and two linkage questions (depreciation +₹10 lakh at 25.168% tax; inventory write-down of ₹50 lakh).

Mock 2 — live 3-statement/DCF (90 min). Prompt: FY23–FY25 actuals for a mid-cap manufacturer. FY25 revenue ₹500 crore; revenue growth 12%, 11%, 10%, 9%, 8% for FY26–FY30; gross margin 38%; opex 20% of revenue; depreciation 8% of opening net PPE; capex 6% of revenue; DSO 60, DIO 75, DPO 50 days; opening debt ₹120 crore at 9% with ₹20 crore annual repayment; tax rate 25.168%; dividend payout 25%; minimum cash ₹15 crore with a revolver at 10%; WACC 11.5%; terminal growth 5%; mid-year convention; 12 crore shares outstanding.

Mock 3 — FP&A business-partnering (30 min). Data sheet: a consumer durables business with budget EBITDA ₹80 crore and actual ₹64 crore for FY25; three product lines with budget and actual volumes and prices; commodity (copper/steel) cost inflation of 7% versus 3% budgeted; USD/INR at 84.5 versus 82 budgeted on 30% imported content; opex over budget by ₹4 crore. Ask: explain the gap and recommend actions for H1 FY26.

Required tabs and schedules

  • Mock 2 workbook: Inputs, IS, BS, CFS, WC, PPE, Debt, DCF, Checks, Notes. Inputs in blue font; no hard-codes in calculation rows.
  • Mock 3 workbook: Data, PVM (price, volume, mix per product line), EBITDA bridge, Recommendation (one page).
  • Mock 1: an answer sheet with each question, the journal entry or computation, and the paragraph reference.
  • Interview Bank tracker: 30 rows with answer outline, timed duration, confidence score, gap log columns.
  • Mock scorecard: three mocks × four rubric dimensions, evidence column, redo plan and redo score.

Required checks

  • Balance check (assets − liabilities − equity = 0) for every year, with a single master check cell.
  • Cash on CFS equals cash on BS; revolver never negative; minimum cash respected.
  • Sum of PVM components equals total revenue variance; EBITDA bridge reconciles budget to actual to the rupee.
  • TV as % of EV displayed; implied exit EV/EBITDA multiple displayed as a sanity check.
  • Every Interview Bank answer timed at or under 2:15.

Deliverable format

One folder containing: the three recordings (or written transcripts), the two workbooks, the Mock 1 answer sheet, the scorecard with a redo of the weakest area, and the Interview Bank tracker with gap log. Add a one-page summary listing your top three gaps and the dated plan to close them.

Saturday assessment paper

Saturday Final Capstone Defense: Deliver a 20-minute portfolio presentation covering your 3-statement valuation model and FP&A reporting pack.

Instructions

  • Total time: 60 minutes (20-minute presentation, 30-minute panel Q&A, 10-minute live modification).
  • Total marks: 100. Panel of two (one acting as a valuation/FAAS manager, one as an FP&A head) or a recorded self-defence scored against the rubric.
  • Materials: your 3-statement/DCF model, your FP&A reporting pack (Power BI or Excel), a 10–12 slide deck. No notes during Q&A beyond the model itself.
  • Every answer must carry a number, a reference or a model cell.

Q1. Portfolio presentation [L3] (30 marks)

Deliver a 20-minute presentation: (a) 2 minutes on your narrative and the six projects; (b) 9 minutes on the 3-statement valuation model — business, key drivers, statement linkages, checks, WACC build, DCF output, sensitivity table and valuation range; (c) 7 minutes on the FP&A pack — KPI selection, PVM bridge, commentary and one recommendation; (d) 2 minutes on limitations and next steps. Marks: content 12, structure 8, time 5, delivery 5.

Q2. Live linkage under challenge [L2] (10 marks)

The panel says: "In FY26 of your model, depreciation increases by ₹5 crore because useful life of plant is revised from 10 to 8 years under Ind AS 16. Walk me through all three statements and the DCF." Use your model's tax rate.

💡 Hint: Remember that a change in estimate is prospective under Ind AS 8, and that the DCF effect comes only through the tax shield.

Q3. WACC defence [L2] (15 marks)

The panel challenges: (a) your risk-free rate — why that tenor and date; (b) your beta — source, number of peers, levered/unlevered, Blume adjustment; (c) your ERP — source and whether it includes a country risk premium; (d) "If the 10-year G-sec moves from 6.8% to 7.3%, what happens to your equity value?" — answer using your sensitivity table.

Q4. Live modification [L3] (15 marks)

In 10 minutes, modify your model so that DSO rises from 60 to 75 days from FY27 onwards. Show the change in FY27 CFO, closing revolver or cash, and enterprise value. Confirm the balance check holds.

💡 Hint: Change only the input cell and trace the effect; do not overwrite any calculation row.

Q5. FP&A pack challenge [L2] (15 marks)

Your FP&A pack shows revenue of ₹21.2 crore against budget ₹22 crore. Budget: Product A 40,000 units at ₹3,000; Product B 20,000 units at ₹5,000. Actual: Product A 46,000 units at ₹2,900; Product B 15,000 units at ₹5,240. (a) Compute price, volume and mix variances. (b) Deliver a 90-second narrative with one recommendation.

Q6. Model integrity [L2] (10 marks)

The panel asks: "Show me every check in your model and how you would find an imbalance of ₹1.2 crore that appears only from FY28 and grows each year." Explain your debugging sequence using your model.

Q7. Career positioning [L1] (5 marks)

"Why should we hire a CMA with an audit background into this role rather than a CA with two years in FP&A?" Answer in under 90 seconds with three evidence points from your portfolio.

Practice-ready checklist

  • I can walk through any single-line change across all three statements, including tax, in under a minute
  • Self-test: have a peer pick five changes at random (depreciation, inventory build, capex, debt raise, dividend, write-off, prepaid expense); time each walk-through and confirm the balance sheet balances aloud.
  • I can build and balance a basic 3-statement model from a prompt in 60–90 minutes
  • Self-test: take an unseen prompt, start from a blank sheet with a timer, and record the minute at which the balance check first shows zero.
  • I can defend my WACC, terminal growth and WC assumptions with sources
  • Self-test: for each assumption, state the number, the source, the date and an alternative you rejected; if any source is "I assumed", fix it.
  • I can deliver a PVM variance narrative and recommendation in under two minutes
  • Self-test: compute PVM on a fresh two-product dataset, then record a narrative; check it opens with the headline number and ends with an action, and that it is under 2:00.
  • I have role-specific CV variants, an updated LinkedIn and a tracked outreach list of target employers
  • Self-test: confirm four CV variants exist (FAAS, FP&A, valuation, controllership), dates and titles match LinkedIn and Naukri, and the tracker has at least 50 employers with stage and next follow-up date.
