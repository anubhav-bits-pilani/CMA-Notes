Key concept

Depreciation +₹10 at 25% Tax: The Canonical Linkage

Concept: Depreciation +₹10 at 25% tax: PAT −7.5; CFO +2.5 (−7.5 + 10); BS cash +2.5, PPE −10, equity −7.5; balances

Statement

For an increase in depreciation of \(D\) that is fully deductible for current tax at rate \(t\):

\[ \Delta \text{PAT} = -D(1-t), \qquad \Delta \text{CFO} = -D(1-t) + D = D \times t \]

\[ \Delta \text{Cash} = D \times t, \quad \Delta \text{PPE} = -D, \quad \Delta \text{Equity} = -D(1-t) \]

where \(D\) is the change in depreciation expense, \(t\) the current tax rate, PAT profit after tax and CFO cash flow from operating activities (indirect method). With \(D = 10\) and \(t = 25\%\): PAT −7.5, CFO +2.5, cash +2.5, PPE −10, equity −7.5. Assets change by \(+2.5 - 10 = -7.5\); liabilities and equity change by −7.5. The balance sheet balances.

Explanation

Depreciation is a non-cash expense, so its only cash consequence is the tax shield: lower taxable profit means lower tax paid. That is why cash rises even though profit falls. The indirect cash flow method starts from PAT (which already reflects the lower tax) and adds back the full non-cash depreciation.

The canonical answer rests on three assumptions worth stating aloud:

  1. Depreciation is tax-deductible in the same period. In India, tax depreciation follows the Income-tax Act block-of-assets WDV rates (Sec 32 of the 1961 Act, corresponding provisions of the 2025 Act), not book depreciation under Schedule II of the Companies Act. A change in book depreciation alone (e.g., a useful-life revision under Ind AS 16.51) does not change current tax; instead the tax effect is deferred: PAT −7.5 (deferred tax credit 2.5), CFO 0, cash 0, PPE −10, DTL −2.5, equity −7.5.
  2. Tax is paid in cash in the period. If tax is payable later, cash is unchanged and tax payable falls by 2.5.
  3. No capitalisation into inventory. In a manufacturer, production depreciation is absorbed into inventory under Ind AS 2; if the goods are unsold at year-end, part of the depreciation sits in closing inventory and P&L impact is smaller.

Interviewers often follow up with "what about EBITDA?" — unchanged, because depreciation is below EBITDA. And "what if it is amortisation of a PPA intangible?" — same mechanics except there is no current tax deduction in a share deal; the DTL created at acquisition unwinds.

Illustration

A company with PBT ₹100, tax 25% (paid in cash) revises the useful life of a machine, increasing depreciation by ₹10, and the tax department allows the same increase (assume tax follows books for the drill).

Line Before After Change
Depreciation 40 50 +10
PBT 100 90 −10
Tax @ 25% 25 22.5 −2.5
PAT 75 67.5 −7.5
CFO (PAT + depreciation) 115 117.5 +2.5
Closing cash 300 302.5 +2.5
Net PPE 400 390 −10
Retained earnings 500 492.5 −7.5

Assets fall by 7.5; equity falls by 7.5.

Connections

This is the template for every linkage drill in the three-statement linkage subtopic (inventory, capex, debt, dividend, write-offs) and for Ind AS 12 deferred tax when book and tax depreciation differ. It underpins the tax shield in WACC/DCF work (FCFF adds back depreciation; the tax shield is embedded in NOPAT) from Week 16, the depreciation schedule in the Week 13–15 three-statement model, and the "debugging the imbalance" routine in live modelling tests (Week 24 t2): a constant imbalance equal to \(D \times t\) usually means the tax line or cash line was not linked.

Practice Questions

Q1. Change the assumptions [L1]

Repeat the depreciation +₹10 walk-through at a 25.168% tax rate under three cases: (a) tax-deductible and paid in cash; (b) tax-deductible but payable next year; (c) book-only change with no tax deduction. State each statement's change.

Q2. Depreciation absorbed in inventory [L2]

A manufacturer's FY26 factory depreciation rises by ₹10 crore. Of the year's production, 30% remains in closing inventory. Tax rate 25%, tax follows books, tax paid in cash. Walk through IS, CFS and BS effects and explain why the PAT impact is not −₹7.5 crore.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 2 includes production overheads in inventory cost, so only the expensed portion hits COGS.

Q3. Interview twist: amortisation of acquired intangibles [L2]

In consolidated statements after a share acquisition, amortisation of a customer-relationship intangible increases by ₹10 crore; a DTL was recognised at acquisition at 25%. Explain the three-statement effect and how it differs from the canonical answer, including the effect on EBITDA and adjusted PAT commonly reported by Indian listed companies.
