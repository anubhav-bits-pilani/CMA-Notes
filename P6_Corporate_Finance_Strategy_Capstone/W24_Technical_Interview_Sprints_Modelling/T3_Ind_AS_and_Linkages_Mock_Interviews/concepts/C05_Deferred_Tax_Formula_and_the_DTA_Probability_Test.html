Key concept

Deferred Tax Formula and the DTA Probability Test

Concept: Deferred tax = (Carrying amount − Tax base) × enacted/substantively enacted rate; DTA only if probable future taxable profit

Statement

For each asset or liability (Ind AS 12.5, 12.15, 12.24, 12.47):

\[ DT = (CA - TB) \times t_{\text{rev}} \]

where \(CA\) is the carrying amount in the Ind AS balance sheet, \(TB\) the tax base (amount attributable for tax purposes), and \(t_{\text{rev}}\) the tax rate expected to apply when the difference reverses, based on rates enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date. For an asset, \(CA > TB\) gives a deferred tax liability; \(CA < TB\) a deferred tax asset. For a liability, \(CA > TB\) gives a DTA.

Recognition: DTLs are recognised for all taxable temporary differences (subject to the initial recognition and outside-basis exceptions). DTAs are recognised only to the extent it is probable that taxable profit will be available against which the deductible difference, tax loss or credit can be used (12.24, 12.34), with convincing evidence required where there is a history of recent losses (12.35). Deferred tax is not discounted (12.53).

Explanation

The balance sheet approach asks: "if I recovered this asset at its carrying amount, how much tax would I pay or save?" That framing captures items the old income-statement (timing difference) approach missed — revaluations, fair value uplifts in business combinations, and OCI items.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Rate: use the rate for the period of reversal — scheduling matters when rates change (e.g., moving to 25.168% under Sec 115BAA) or when a tax holiday ends. In India, a Finance Bill is generally treated as substantively enacted when passed by both Houses.
  • Manner of recovery (12.51): land held for sale uses the capital gains rate; investment property measured at fair value under IFRS has a presumption of sale, but Ind AS 40 permits only the cost model so this rarely arises.
  • Probability for DTA: first look at reversing taxable differences of the same entity and authority, then future taxable profit forecasts, then tax-planning opportunities (12.28–12.30). Business loss expiry (8 years) constrains recognition; unabsorbed depreciation has no expiry.
  • Reassessment each period: previously unrecognised DTAs are recognised when recovery becomes probable (12.37); recognised DTAs are reduced if no longer probable (12.56).
  • Where the effect goes: P&L, OCI or equity following the underlying item (12.58, 12.61A); in a business combination, goodwill (12.66).

Illustration

A company (rate 25.168%) at 31 March 2026, ₹ crore:

Item CA TB CA − TB Type DT at 25.168%
PPE 250 190 60 Taxable (asset) DTL 15.10
Trade receivables (net of ECL 8) 92 100 (8) Deductible (asset) DTA 2.01
Leave encashment provision 12 0 12 Deductible (liability) DTA 3.02
Unused business loss — — 40 Deductible DTA 10.07 (if probable)

If forecasts support only ₹20 crore of the losses being used before expiry, DTA on losses is limited to ₹5.03 crore. Net DTL = 15.10 − 2.01 − 3.02 − 5.03 = ₹5.04 crore (offset permitted — same entity and authority).

Connections

This concept is the engine of the Ind AS 12 subtopic (rate changes, MAT credit as a DTA, 115BAA remeasurement), connects to the depreciation linkage concept (book vs tax depreciation), the lease concepts (DTA/DTL on lease liability and ROU), PPA in the other-frequent-areas subtopic (DTL on intangible uplifts raises goodwill), and to valuation in Week 16, where DTAs on losses are valued as tax shields in FCFF rather than added at book value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and compute [L1]

At a 25.168% rate, compute deferred tax for: (a) an FVTPL equity investment with CA ₹30 crore and cost ₹18 crore, taxable as long-term capital gain at 12.5% on sale; (b) a warranty provision of ₹6 crore deductible when paid; (c) a capitalised development cost of ₹10 crore fully deducted for tax in the year incurred.

💡 Hint: For (a) consider the manner of recovery and the applicable rate rather than the corporate rate.

Q2. DTA probability judgement [L2]

A company has deductible temporary differences of ₹50 crore, losses of ₹80 crore expiring in FY28, and taxable temporary differences of ₹30 crore reversing in FY27–FY29. Forecast taxable profits (before using losses) are ₹10 crore in FY27 and ₹25 crore in FY28. Determine the DTA you would recognise, with reasoning, at 25.168%.

Q3. Interview: why a balance sheet approach? [L1]

Answer in under one minute: why Ind AS 12 uses the balance sheet approach rather than timing differences, giving one Indian example where the two approaches give different answers.
