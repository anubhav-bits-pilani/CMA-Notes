Key concept

Ind AS 116 Lease Effect on EBITDA, P&L and Cash Flows

Concept: Lease effect: EBITDA rises by lease rentals; P&L shows depreciation + interest (front-loaded expense); principal in financing cash flow

Statement

For a lease capitalised under Ind AS 116 in place of an operating lease rent \(L\) per period:

\[ \Delta \text{EBITDA}_t = +L, \qquad \text{Expense}_t = Dep_t + Int_t, \qquad Int_t = r \times LL_{t-1} \]

\[ \sum_{t=1}^{n} (Dep_t + Int_t) = \text{ROU}_0 + \sum_{t=1}^{n} Int_t \approx \sum_{t=1}^{n} L_t \]

where \(L\) is the lease rental, \(Dep_t\) straight-line ROU depreciation, \(Int_t\) interest at discount rate \(r\) on the opening lease liability \(LL_{t-1}\), and \(n\) the lease term. The total equals total rent exactly when ROU equals the liability (no IDC, incentives or restoration) — hence "broadly the same". Cash flow: principal repayment \(L - Int_t\) is a financing outflow (Ind AS 116.50(a)); interest follows Ind AS 7 — for Indian non-financial entities interest paid is also financing (Ind AS 7.33).

Explanation

Why EBITDA rises by the full rent: rent was an operating expense above EBITDA; under Ind AS 116 it is replaced by depreciation and interest, both below EBITDA. EBIT rises by \(L - Dep_t\) (small). PBT falls in early years and rises in later years because interest is computed on a declining liability — the front-loading effect. For a portfolio of leases at different stages, front-loading partly evens out, which is why mature retailers see a smaller PBT impact than fast-expanding ones.

What does not change: total cash paid, the economic substance, and total expense over the life (subject to IDC/incentives). Saying Ind AS 116 increases total lease cost is a classic interview error.

What else changes: net debt (if lease liabilities included), Net debt/EBITDA (generally up), interest cover (EBITDA/interest may fall or rise depending on rent size vs interest), ROCE (capital employed rises by the ROU asset), and CFO (higher, since rent moves to CFF). Exempt leases (short-term, low-value) and variable revenue-linked rent stay in operating expenses and CFO.

Analyst adjustments: Indian listed retailers and QSR chains often report "pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA" by deducting rent. Valuers must be consistent: if lease liabilities are treated as debt in the EV-to-equity bridge, FCFF must exclude lease payments (EBITDA includes the rent add-back) and EV/EBITDA multiples must be on the same basis for comparables.

EBITDA rises by ₹12 lakh each year; Year-1 PBT falls by ₹1.54 lakh; cumulative expense is identical.

Connections

This concept follows directly from the ROU measurement concept and the Ind AS 116 subtopic, feeds the "Ind AS 116 increases total expense" pitfall in the topic overview, and links to WACC/DCF practice in Week 16 (EV bridge consistency), FP&A KPI design in Weeks 19–20 (choosing pre- or post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA for management reporting), and covenant analysis for lenders. US GAAP comparison: under ASC 842, operating leases keep a single straight-line lease cost within operating expense, so EBITDA does not rise — a key point for GCC interviews.

Practice Questions

Q1. Quantify the KPI effects [L2]

A retailer has revenue ₹2,000 crore, EBITDA ₹180 crore pre-Ind AS 116, rent ₹140 crore, borrowings ₹300 crore, cash ₹50 crore. On adoption, lease liabilities are ₹850 crore, ROU assets ₹800 crore, annual ROU depreciation ₹110 crore and lease interest ₹75 crore. Compute EBITDA margin, Net debt/EBITDA, interest cover (EBITDA/interest, existing interest ₹27 crore) and PBT before and after, and explain the results to a board member.

💡 Hint: Add lease interest to existing finance costs and lease liabilities to borrowings for the post-adoption ratios.

Q2. Front-loading at portfolio level [L2]

A QSR chain opens 20 stores a year, each on an identical 9-year lease of ₹30 lakh p.a. at 10% IBR. Explain qualitatively and with a simple Excel layout how the portfolio's annual P&L difference vs rent evolves over years 1–12 compared with a single lease.

Q3. ASC 842 contrast [L1]

For the lease in the illustration, state what EBITDA, operating expense and CFO would show under ASC 842 if classified as an operating lease and as a finance lease, and why this matters when a GCC reports to a US parent.
