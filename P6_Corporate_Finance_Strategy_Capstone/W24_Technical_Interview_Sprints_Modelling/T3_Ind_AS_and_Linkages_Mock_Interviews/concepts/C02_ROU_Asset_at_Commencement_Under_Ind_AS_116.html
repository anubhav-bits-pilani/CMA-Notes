Key concept

ROU Asset at Commencement Under Ind AS 116

Concept: ROU asset at commencement = Lease liability (PV of lease payments) + initial direct costs + prepaid payments − incentives + restoration estimate

Statement

At the commencement date a lessee measures the right-of-use asset at cost (Ind AS 116.23–24):

\[ \text{ROU}_0 = LL_0 + IDC + P_0 - I + R \]

where:

  • \(LL_0\) = initial lease liability, the present value of lease payments not yet paid at commencement, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate (116.26);
  • \(IDC\) = initial direct costs — incremental costs of obtaining the lease that would not have been incurred otherwise (brokerage, stamp duty and registration on the lease deed) (116.24(c));
  • \(P_0\) = lease payments made at or before commencement (advance rent, first instalment in an in-advance lease);
  • \(I\) = lease incentives received (fit-out contributions, rent-free cash reimbursements);
  • \(R\) = estimate of costs to dismantle, remove or restore the asset or site, recognised as an Ind AS 37 provision (116.24(d)).

Explanation

The liability captures the future obligation; the asset captures everything the lessee has paid or committed to obtain the right of use. Payments made upfront do not sit in the liability (they are already paid) but are still part of the cost of the asset. Incentives reduce cost because they are a reduction in the price of the right.

Points interviewers probe:

  • Security deposits (common in Indian leave-and-licence agreements) are not lease payments; they are financial assets under Ind AS 109 measured at fair value. The difference between the nominal interest-free deposit and its fair value is prepaid rent, which is added to the ROU asset.
  • Internal costs (legal team time, negotiation salaries) are not IDC.
  • GST on rent is excluded where input tax credit is available; stamp duty is IDC.
  • Restoration provision is discounted at a pre-tax rate reflecting the liability's risks; its unwinding is a finance cost separate from lease interest. Later changes in the estimate adjust the ROU asset, applying by analogy Appendix A to Ind AS 16 on changes in decommissioning and restoration liabilities.
  • Rent-free periods within the term are not incentives received in cash; they simply reduce the payments discounted in \(LL_0\).
  • Under the 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment, deferred tax is recognised on both the ROU asset and the lease liability.

Illustration

A retail store lease from 1 April 2025: 5 years, ₹20 lakh annual rent payable in advance, IBR 10%; brokerage ₹1.2 lakh; landlord fit-out contribution ₹3 lakh received; interest-free deposit ₹10 lakh refundable at the end (fair value at 10% = ₹6.21 lakh); restoration estimate ₹2 lakh at the end, PV at 8% = ₹1.36 lakh.

\[ LL_0 = 20 \times \frac{1 - 1.10^{-4}}{0.10} = 20 \times 3.1699 = 63.40 \text{ lakh} \]

Component ₹ lakh
Lease liability (4 remaining payments) 63.40

Initial direct costs (brokerage) 1.20

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the ROU asset [L1]

A warehouse lease: 6 years, ₹15 lakh payable annually in arrears, IBR 9.5%, stamp duty and registration ₹0.9 lakh, legal team time allocated ₹0.4 lakh, landlord reimbursement for fit-out ₹2 lakh, restoration obligation ₹3 lakh at end of year 6 (provision discount rate 8%). Compute the lease liability and ROU asset and pass the journal entry.

💡 Hint: Check which of the listed costs qualify as incremental initial direct costs.

Q2. Deposit and rent-free period [L2]

An office lease has a 3-year term with the first 3 months rent-free, then ₹4 lakh per month payable in advance, and an interest-free deposit of ₹24 lakh refundable at the end. IBR and market rate for the deposit are 10% p.a. Explain how the rent-free period and the deposit enter the ROU measurement and set up the Excel formulas you would use to compute the lease liability on a monthly basis.

Q3. Interview: why not just capitalise rent? [L2]

An interviewer asks why the ROU asset at commencement is usually different from the lease liability, and whether that difference creates any P&L effect on day one. Give a structured answer with a numerical example of your own.
