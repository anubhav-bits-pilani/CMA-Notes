Key concept

Over Time vs Point in Time: The Ind AS 115.35 Criteria

Concept: Ind AS 115 revenue recognised when control transfers: over time if any of the three Ind AS 115.35 criteria are met, else at a point in time

Statement

An entity recognises revenue when (or as) it satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service (Ind AS 115.31). Control is the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all the remaining benefits from, the asset (115.33). A performance obligation is satisfied over time if any one of the following is met (115.35):

  1. (a) the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits as the entity performs (routine services: facility management, payroll processing, telecom);
  2. (b) the entity's performance creates or enhances an asset that the customer controls as it is created (construction on customer's land; customisation of customer-owned software);
  3. (c) the performance does not create an asset with an alternative use to the entity and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date (including a reasonable margin).

If none is met, revenue is recognised at the point in time control transfers, judged using indicators (115.38): present right to payment, legal title, physical possession, significant risks and rewards, and customer acceptance.

Explanation

The order of testing matters in interviews: (a) is the quickest test for services; (b) for work on customer-controlled assets; (c) is the residual test for bespoke goods. Criterion (c) has two limbs and both must be met:

  • No alternative use (115.36, B6–B8): contractual restrictions (the unit is specifically allotted to a buyer) or practical limitations (significant rework cost to redirect the asset). Assessed at inception; not revisited unless a contract modification.
  • Enforceable right to payment (115.37, B9–B13): if the customer terminates for reasons other than the entity's failure, the entity is entitled to at least cost plus a reasonable margin for work done. Assess the contract terms and the legal framework — in India, RERA-registered agreements, case law on forfeiture of booking amounts and allotment letters drive the conclusion.

Once over-time is established, measure progress using an output method (milestones, units delivered, surveys of performance) or input method (costs incurred, labour hours), excluding inputs that do not depict performance, such as uninstalled materials or wasted costs (115.39–45, B14–B19). If progress cannot be reasonably measured but costs are expected to be recovered, recognise revenue to the extent of costs incurred (115.45).

Illustration

A Pune developer sells apartments under a RERA agreement:

Fact pattern (a) (b) (c) no alternative use (c) right to payment Conclusion
Specific unit allotted; buyer can cancel and the developer only retains 10% booking amount No No (developer controls the building until possession) Yes No — retention does not cover cost plus margin Point in time (on possession/conveyance)
Same, but the agreement and enforceability support recovery of the full price on cancellation, and the developer must complete No No Yes Yes Over time — measure progress by cost incurred

Numbers: project cost ₹80 crore, contract price for the sold units ₹120 crore, costs incurred to date ₹32 crore (excluding land acquired but not yet developed). Under over-time recognition, progress = 32 ÷ 80 = 40%; revenue to date = ₹48 crore; margin ₹16 crore. Under point-in-time, revenue is nil until handover and costs sit in inventory (work-in-progress).

Connections

This concept completes step 5 of the five-step model in the Ind AS 115 subtopic, ties to contract assets and liabilities (over-time recognition ahead of billing creates contract assets), and to the related item on Ind AS vs IFRS real estate revenue — the pre-Ind AS 115 ICAI Guidance Note on Real Estate Transactions permitted percentage-of-completion more widely, whereas Ind AS 115 requires the 115.35 test. It also links to FP&A revenue phasing and three-statement models (Weeks 13–15): over-time revenue drives unbilled revenue and working capital, while point-in-time revenue produces lumpy quarters and inventory build-up.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the obligations [L1]

Apply the 115.35 criteria to: (a) a 12-month IT managed-services contract; (b) a turbine manufactured to a customer's specification in the manufacturer's factory, with a termination clause entitling the manufacturer to costs plus 8% margin; (c) standard steel coils sold under a 6-month supply agreement; (d) an EPC contract to build a substation on the customer's land.

💡 Hint: Test criterion (a) first for services and (b) for work on customer-controlled sites.

Q2. Measuring progress [L2]

A contractor under an over-time contract with price ₹50 crore and estimated cost ₹40 crore has incurred ₹18 crore, including ₹6 crore of specialised equipment delivered to site but not yet installed (the contractor is not involved in its design or manufacture) and ₹1 crore of abnormal wastage. Compute revenue and margin to date and explain each adjustment.

Q3. Interview case: SaaS vs on-premise licence [L2]

Explain to an interviewer why revenue from a 3-year SaaS subscription is recognised over time while a perpetual on-premise software licence with no ongoing updates is recognised at a point in time, referencing the relevant criteria and the licence guidance on rights to access vs rights to use.
