Key concept

ECL = PD × LGD × EAD with 12-Month vs Lifetime Horizons

Concept: ECL = PD × LGD × EAD (discounted); 12-month ECL for Stage 1, lifetime ECL for Stages 2–3

Statement

For a single exposure, expected credit loss is the probability-weighted present value of cash shortfalls (Ind AS 109.5.5.17, Appendix A). In the common PD–LGD–EAD decomposition:

\[ \text{ECL} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{PD_t \times LGD_t \times EAD_t}{(1 + EIR)^{t}} \]

where:

  • \(PD_t\) = marginal probability of default in period \(t\) (conditional on survival to \(t\));
  • \(LGD_t\) = loss given default — the share of exposure not recovered, after collateral and costs;
  • \(EAD_t\) = exposure at default — outstanding balance plus expected drawdowns of undrawn commitments;
  • \(EIR\) = the original effective interest rate used to discount (B5.5.44);
  • \(T\) = 12 months for Stage 1 (losses from default events possible within 12 months, 5.5.5), and the remaining contractual life for Stages 2 and 3 (5.5.3).

For Stage 3, PD is effectively 100%, and the allowance equals the PV of expected shortfalls.

Explanation

The formula is an implementation of the standard's principle — Ind AS 109 does not prescribe PD/LGD/EAD, but banks and NBFCs use it because it maps to their credit data. Corporates usually apply the simplified approach via a provision matrix, which is the same concept applied to aged buckets.

Key conditions and nuances:

  • 12-month ECL is not "losses over the next 12 months on all cash flows" — it is the lifetime cash shortfall weighted by the probability of default occurring in the next 12 months.
  • Forward-looking and probability-weighted: multiple macro scenarios (base, upside, downside) weighted, not a single best estimate (5.5.17–18). Indian NBFCs commonly link PD to GDP growth, repo rate or sector indices.
  • Point-in-time PD, not through-the-cycle regulatory PD — adjust rating agency or IRACP data.
  • LGD reflects collateral (property for LAP, vehicle for auto loans) net of realisation costs and time to recover (SARFAESI timelines).
  • EAD for credit cards and overdrafts includes credit conversion factors on undrawn limits.
  • Staging is driven by significant increase in credit risk since origination (30 dpd rebuttable presumption) and credit impairment (90 dpd presumption). The jump from 12-month to lifetime ECL at Stage 2 creates the "cliff effect" interviewers ask about.
  • Simplified approach: trade receivables and contract assets without a significant financing component use lifetime ECL from day one (5.5.15).

Illustration

An NBFC has three loans (₹ lakh, EIR 12%, single-year horizon simplification for Stage 1, two-year horizon for lifetime):

Loan Stage EAD PD year 1 PD year 2 (marginal) LGD ECL
A 1 100 2% — 40% 100 × 2% × 40% ÷ 1.12 = 0.71
B 2 100 (yr 1), 60 (yr 2) 8% 10% 40% 3.20 ÷ 1.12 + 2.40 ÷ 1.2544 = 2.86 + 1.91 = 4.77
C 3 80 100% — 55% 44.0 (PV of shortfalls; recovery timing ignored)

Loan B's allowance is almost seven times loan A's for the same exposure, driven by the lifetime horizon and higher PD. Stage 3 interest revenue for loan C is computed on the net carrying amount of ₹36 lakh.

Connections

This concept underpins the Ind AS 109 subtopic (classification, staging, simplified approach), the pitfall on confusing simplified and general approaches, financial reporting tests in Week 24 t2 (ECL provision matrix), and credit-risk modelling in Ind AS close packs (Weeks 1–4). In FP&A, ECL provisions are a forecastable line driven by ageing and collection assumptions linked to DSO (Week 24 t4). For lenders, RBI prudential provisioning (IRACP) runs in parallel; NBFCs disclose the comparison.

Practice Questions

Q1. Stage 1 vs Stage 2 allowance [L2]

A ₹200 lakh term loan, EIR 11%, remaining life 3 years, amortising ₹66.67 lakh per year. 12-month PD 3%; marginal PDs for years 2 and 3 are 4% and 5%; LGD 35%. Compute the allowance if the loan is Stage 1 and if it moves to Stage 2, and the P&L charge on transfer.

💡 Hint: Use the opening balance of each year as EAD and discount each year's expected loss at the EIR.

Q2. Scenario weighting [L2]

An NBFC's base, upside and downside macro scenarios produce portfolio ECL of ₹420 crore, ₹350 crore and ₹640 crore with weights 60%, 15% and 25%. Compute the reported ECL, explain why it differs from the base-case number, and describe how an auditor would test the weights.

Q3. Interview: explain the cliff effect [L1]

Explain to an FP&A manager, in under a minute and with numbers, why a loan crossing 30 days past due can increase the provision several-fold even though its PD has only doubled.
