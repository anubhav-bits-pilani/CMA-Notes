Subtopic note

Ind AS 116 Interview Drill: Lessee Model, Modifications, KPIs

Scope: Ind AS 116: lessee measurement, exemptions, lease modifications, impact on EBITDA, net debt and cash flow classification

Why it matters

Ind AS 116 changed how every Indian retailer, airline, telecom tower user, QSR chain and IT company with leased offices reports EBITDA, leverage and cash flow. Analysts at KPOs restate EBITDA "pre-Ind AS 116" to compare with older periods; lenders define covenants with or without lease liabilities; FP&A teams must budget depreciation and interest instead of rent. Interviewers probe whether you can build the lease schedule from scratch, explain why EBITDA jumps, and handle a renegotiated lease without confusing a modification with a reassessment.

Core notes

Identifying a lease

A contract contains a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period in exchange for consideration (Ind AS 116.9): the customer obtains substantially all economic benefits and directs how and for what purpose the asset is used (116.B9). Substantive supplier substitution rights defeat "identified asset" (116.B14). Data-centre capacity, dedicated warehouse space and captive power plants are common Indian judgement areas.

Lessee measurement at commencement

Lease liability (116.26–28) = present value of lease payments not yet paid, discounted at the interest rate implicit in the lease or, if not readily determinable (almost always for lessees), the lessee's incremental borrowing rate (IBR). Lease payments include fixed payments (less incentives receivable), variable payments that depend on an index or rate (e.g., CPI-linked escalation) measured at the commencement index, residual value guarantees expected to be payable, purchase option price if reasonably certain, and termination penalties if the term reflects termination.

Excluded: variable payments linked to sales or usage (revenue-share rent in malls), which hit P&L as incurred; non-lease components unless the practical expedient to combine is elected by class (116.15). GST on rent is generally excluded where input tax credit is available.

ROU asset (116.24) = lease liability + payments at or before commencement − incentives received + initial direct costs + estimated dismantling/restoration costs (Ind AS 37 provision).

Lease term (116.18–21) = non-cancellable period + extension options reasonably certain to be exercised + periods after termination options reasonably certain not to be exercised. Indian lock-in clauses matter: a nine-year leave-and-licence agreement with a three-year lock-in and a lessee-only exit right needs the "reasonably certain" assessment, considering leasehold improvements and relocation costs (116.B37).

Subsequent measurement: liability at amortised cost — interest at the discount rate, reduced by payments (116.36); ROU depreciated straight-line over the shorter of lease term and useful life (useful life if ownership transfers) (116.31–32), tested under Ind AS 36.

Recognition exemptions

  • Short-term leases (116.5(a), B34): term of 12 months or less at commencement with no purchase option. Election by class of underlying asset. Note that a "12-month" agreement routinely renewed may have a longer lease term if renewal is reasonably certain.
  • Low-value assets (116.5(b), B3–B8): assessed on the value of the asset when new, regardless of materiality to the entity; lease-by-lease election. Not available if the lessee subleases or the asset is highly dependent on other assets. Typical: laptops, phones, small office furniture. A car is never low-value.

Exempt lease payments are expensed straight-line, remain in EBITDA as rent and are disclosed (116.53).

Lease modifications vs reassessments

A modification is a change in scope or consideration not part of the original terms (116.44–46):

  1. Separate lease if it adds the right to use one or more additional underlying assets and the price rises by an amount commensurate with the stand-alone price — account for the new floor as a new lease.
  2. Otherwise, at the effective date, remeasure the liability using a revised discount rate: - Decrease in scope (fewer floors, shorter term): reduce the ROU asset proportionately and recognise the gain or loss in P&L; then adjust the ROU for the remaining remeasurement. - All other modifications (rent change, term extension): adjust the ROU asset by the remeasurement, no P&L.

A reassessment (116.39–43) arises from events within the original contract — change in assessment of an extension option, change in an index — and uses the original rate (except where the change is in term or purchase option assessment, which uses a revised rate, or a floating-rate change). Interviewers check this distinction.

COVID-era rent concessions were covered by the practical expedient in 116.46A–46B (extended in India to concessions affecting payments due on or before 30 June 2022). The 2024 amendment on lease liability in a sale and leaseback (seller-lessee must not recognise a gain relating to the right of use retained), notified by MCA for periods beginning 1 April 2024, is a current-affairs question.

Impact on EBITDA, net debt and cash flows

Metric Pre-Ind AS 116 (operating lease) Ind AS 116
EBITDA After rent Before rent → rises by the lease rentals capitalised
EBIT After rent After ROU depreciation → small increase (rent − depreciation)
PBT Rent Depreciation + interest → lower in early years
Net debt Excludes leases Includes lease liabilities if the user's definition does (most analysts and Ind AS 7 net debt reconciliations do)
Net debt/EBITDA — Usually rises because the debt increase dominates
CFO Rent outflow Rent no longer in CFO → CFO rises
CFF — Principal repayment

Cash flow classification under Ind AS 7: principal is financing (116.50(a)); interest follows the entity's Ind AS 7 treatment — for non-financial entities, Ind AS 7.33 classifies interest paid as financing, so in India both principal and interest typically sit in CFF; short-term, low-value and variable lease payments stay in operating activities (116.50(c)). Total cash is unchanged — the reclassification is presentation only.

Disclosure of the changes in liabilities arising from financing activities (Ind AS 7.44A–44E) must include lease liabilities, and Schedule III requires lease liabilities as separate line items under financial liabilities.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • IBR derivation: Rf for the term + entity credit spread + security adjustment; group treasury rates are acceptable only if adjusted for the lessee's credit. A one-point change in IBR moves the liability and the front-loading.
  • Leasehold land paid upfront (MIDC 95-year leases): no liability, a prepaid ROU asset amortised over the term.
  • Indexation: liability is remeasured when cash flows change because of the index, not in anticipation.
  • Deferred tax: since the 2023 Ind AS 12 amendment, the initial recognition exemption does not apply to leases giving rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences — recognise both DTA and DTL.
  • Covenants: Indian loan agreements drafted pre-2019 may define debt "as per GAAP", unintentionally pulling leases into leverage tests.

Key takeaways

  • Liability = PV of unpaid fixed and index-linked payments at IBR; ROU = liability + IDC + prepayments − incentives + restoration.
  • Short-term (≤ 12 months, no purchase option) and low-value (asset value when new) are the only recognition exemptions.
  • Modification: separate lease if added asset at stand-alone price; otherwise remeasure at a revised rate, with a P&L gain/loss only for scope decreases.
  • EBITDA and CFO rise; finance cost and net debt rise; total expense over the term is broadly unchanged.
  • In India, lease interest paid by non-financial entities is a financing outflow under Ind AS 7.33.
  • Always quote both the mechanics and the KPI effect — that is what interviewers score.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the lease schedule [L2]

A QSR chain signs a 4-year store lease from 1 April 2025: rent ₹24 lakh p.a. payable annually in advance, ₹2 lakh broker fee, landlord fit-out incentive of ₹3 lakh received on signing, estimated restoration cost ₹1.5 lakh (pre-tax discount rate for provisions 8%), IBR 10%. Build the ROU and lease liability schedules for four years and show the FY26 P&L, balance sheet and cash flow presentation.

💡 Hint: With payments in advance, the first payment is at commencement and is not part of the liability.

Q2. Exemption screening [L1]

For each, state whether the exemption applies and why: (a) 11-month leave-and-licence of a guest house renewed every year for six years; (b) 40 laptops at ₹65,000 each on a 3-year lease; (c) a 9-month crane hire with a purchase option at ₹1; (d) a car lease for a director for 6 months.

Q3. Modification: surrender one floor [L2]

A lessee holds a 6-year lease of three floors at ₹30 lakh p.a. in arrears (₹10 lakh per floor), IBR 9% at commencement. At the end of year 2 (carrying ROU ₹89.72 lakh, liability ₹97.19 lakh) it surrenders one floor and the rent for the remaining two floors is reduced to ₹19 lakh p.a.; the new IBR is 10%. Compute the gain or loss and the post-modification balances, and state the journal entries.

💡 Hint: First reduce the ROU and liability proportionately for the scope decrease, then remeasure the remaining liability at the revised rate.

Q4. Covenant conversation [L3]

A listed retailer's lender covenant requires Net debt/EBITDA below 2.5×. Reported FY26 figures: EBITDA ₹420 crore including ₹150 crore of rent reversed under Ind AS 116; borrowings ₹500 crore; lease liabilities ₹780 crore; cash ₹120 crore. Compute the ratio on both bases, explain to the CFO which definition the loan agreement likely uses and what you would negotiate at renewal.

Q5. Interview rapid-fire on cash flow [L1]

Answer in one line each: where do lease principal, lease interest, variable revenue-linked rent, and a short-term lease payment appear in an Indian non-financial company's cash flow statement, and why does total cash not change?
