Subtopic note

Other Frequent Areas: Ind AS 103, 110, 7, 21, Schedule III, CARO

Scope: Other frequent areas: Ind AS 103 PPA, Ind AS 110 control, Ind AS 7 classification, Ind AS 21 FX, Schedule III, CARO 2020

Why it matters

After revenue, leases, financial instruments and tax, interviewers rotate through a predictable second tier: business combinations (every FAAS team does PPA), consolidation control (structured entities, potential voting rights), cash flow classification (Ind AS has India-specific choices), foreign currency, Schedule III presentation and CARO 2020 reporting. Your audit background is an asset here — CARO and Schedule III are areas where a CMA with audit experience should outperform pure modellers. The goal is a crisp two-minute answer with a number for each.

Core notes

Ind AS 103 — purchase price allocation (PPA)

Acquisition method (Ind AS 103.4–5): identify the acquirer, determine the acquisition date, recognise and measure identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any NCI, and recognise goodwill or a bargain purchase gain.

\[ \text{Goodwill} = \text{Consideration transferred} + \text{NCI} + \text{FV of previously held interest} - \text{FV of identifiable net assets} \]

  • Consideration at fair value, including contingent consideration at acquisition-date fair value (103.39); later changes in a liability-classified earn-out go to P&L, not goodwill (103.58). Acquisition costs are expensed (103.53).
  • NCI measured at fair value (full goodwill) or proportionate share of identifiable net assets, a transaction-by-transaction choice (103.19).
  • Identifiable intangibles (customer relationships, brands, technology, order backlog, non-compete) are recognised separately if they meet the separability or contractual-legal criterion (103.B31–B34); assembled workforce is subsumed in goodwill. Valuation methods: MEEM for the primary intangible, relief-from-royalty for brands, with-and-without for non-competes.
  • Deferred tax on fair value step-ups (no tax step-up in a share acquisition in India) increases goodwill.
  • Measurement period up to 12 months to finalise provisional amounts retrospectively (103.45).
  • Bargain purchase — Ind AS carve-out: after reassessment, the gain is recognised in OCI and accumulated in capital reserve if there is clear evidence of the reason for bargain purchase; otherwise directly in capital reserve (103.34–36A). IFRS 3 takes it to P&L.
  • Common control combinations use the pooling of interests method (Appendix C): carrying values, no goodwill, difference to capital reserve — relevant for Indian group restructurings via NCLT schemes.

Illustration: acquire 80% of Target for ₹800 crore; FV of net assets ₹700 crore after a ₹150 crore customer intangible uplift and DTL of ₹37.8 crore (25.168%). NCI at proportionate share = ₹140 crore; goodwill = 800 + 140 − 700 = ₹240 crore.

Ind AS 110 — control

An investor controls an investee when it has all three (Ind AS 110.7): power over relevant activities, exposure or rights to variable returns, and the ability to use power to affect returns. Power derives from rights — voting rights, potential voting rights that are substantive (B22–B25), contractual arrangements; de facto control is possible with less than 50% if other holdings are widely dispersed (B41–B46). Protective rights (vetoes over fundamental changes) do not confer power. Principal vs agent analysis for fund managers (B58–B72) considers decision-making scope, removal rights, remuneration and other interests.

Interview angles: a 49% stake with the right to appoint the majority of the board (control); a 51% stake where a minority investor's SHA affirmative rights cover budgets and CEO appointment (possibly joint control under Ind AS 111); an ESOP trust funded by the company (typically consolidated). Under Companies Act 2013 Sec 129(3) consolidation follows Ind AS, while the legal definition of subsidiary (Sec 2(87)) differs and matters for restrictions on layers of subsidiaries.

Ind AS 7 — cash flow classification

India-specific (carve-out from IAS 7): for entities other than financial institutions, interest paid is a financing activity and interest and dividends received are investing activities; dividends paid are financing (Ind AS 7.33–34). Financial institutions classify interest paid/received in operating. Taxes paid are operating unless specifically identifiable with investing or financing (7.35).

Other frequently tested points: cash and cash equivalents include investments with original maturity of three months or less; bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form part of cash management are included (7.8); non-cash transactions (ROU additions, debt-equity swaps, capex on credit) are excluded and disclosed (7.43); the reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities (7.44A–44E); and the IASB's 2023 supplier finance arrangements amendment to IAS 7/IFRS 7 requiring disclosures of reverse factoring (check whether and from when MCA has notified the corresponding Ind AS change — date-sensitive). Interviewers also ask why CFO in India often looks better than under IFRS peers: interest paid sits outside CFO.

Ind AS 21 — foreign currency

  1. Determine functional currency — the primary economic environment (21.9–14); for Indian GCCs/subsidiaries of US groups it is usually INR because costs are INR even if billing is USD.
  2. Foreign currency transactions: initial recognition at spot rate (21.21); at reporting date, monetary items at closing rate, non-monetary items at historical rate (or FV-date rate) (21.23). Exchange differences on monetary items go to P&L (21.28).
  3. Translation to presentation currency (foreign operations): assets/liabilities at closing rate, income/expenses at transaction or average rates, differences to OCI — foreign currency translation reserve — reclassified to P&L on disposal (21.39, 21.48).
  4. Net investment in a foreign operation: FX on a monetary item that is part of net investment goes to OCI in consolidated statements (21.32).

India-specific: Ind AS 101 D13AA allowed first-time adopters to continue the AS 11 para 46/46A policy (capitalising or amortising FX on long-term foreign currency monetary items) for items recognised before the beginning of the first Ind AS reporting period. The lack of exchangeability amendment (21.8A, 19A–19C) was notified in India effective 1 April 2025. Advance consideration in foreign currency uses the date of initial recognition of the advance (Appendix B).

Illustration: USD 1 million receivable booked at ₹83.50; closing rate ₹85.80 → exchange gain ₹23 lakh in P&L. A USD 1 million machinery advance paid at ₹83.50 is non-monetary — no restatement.

Schedule III (Division II, Ind AS)

Presentation framework under Sec 129: current/non-current split (operating cycle test), statement of changes in equity, and the 2021 amendments (effective FY22) requiring:

  • Promoter shareholding and changes during the year;
  • Ageing schedules for trade receivables, trade payables, CWIP and intangibles under development;
  • Title deeds of immovable property not held in the company's name;
  • Reconciliation of quarterly returns/statements filed with banks for borrowings secured on current assets;
  • Wilful defaulter status, relationships with struck-off companies, charges not registered, compliance with layers of companies;
  • Eleven ratios (current, debt-equity, DSCR, ROE, inventory turnover, trade receivables turnover, trade payables turnover, net capital turnover, net profit, ROCE, return on investment) with explanation for changes above 25%;
  • Undisclosed income, crypto/virtual currency holdings, Benami property proceedings, CSR details, and utilisation of borrowed funds and share premium (intermediary rules).

Rounding thresholds by turnover (lakhs/crores) and MSME dues disclosure under MSMED Act Sec 22 are frequent tie-ins — the Sec 43B(h) income-tax disallowance for payments to micro/small enterprises beyond 45 days links Schedule III, tax and working capital.

CARO 2020

Issued under Sec 143(11); applies to companies other than exempt classes (banking, insurance, Sec 8, OPCs, small companies, and private companies meeting all the conditions on paid-up capital plus reserves ≤ ₹1 crore, borrowings ≤ ₹1 crore and revenue ≤ ₹10 crore). Twenty-one clauses (3(i)–3(xxi)). High-yield interview clauses:

Clause Subject
3(i) PPE records, physical verification, title deeds, revaluation, Benami proceedings
3(ii) Inventory verification (discrepancy ≥ 10% per class); quarterly returns for working capital limits above ₹5 crore agreeing with books
3(iii) Loans, guarantees, advances to companies, firms, LLPs and others; repayable-on-demand loans
3(vii) Statutory dues deposited; disputed dues
3(ix) Defaults to lenders; wilful defaulter; diversion of term loans; short-term funds used for long-term purposes
3(xi) Fraud, whistle-blower complaints, ADT-4 filings
3(xvii) Cash losses in current and preceding year
3(xix) Material uncertainty about meeting liabilities within one year (ratio-based)
3(xx) Unspent CSR transfers
3(xxi) Qualifications/adverse remarks in CARO reports of group companies included in consolidated statements

CARO does not apply to consolidated statements except clause 3(xxi). Position your audit experience: "I tested clause 3(ii)(b) quarterly stock statement reconciliations, which is the same skill as a month-end inventory reconciliation in R2R."

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Reverse acquisitions (103.B19–B27): the legal acquirer is the accounting acquiree — common in SPAC-like or listed shell mergers.
  • Step acquisitions: remeasure the previously held interest to fair value through P&L (103.42).
  • Loss of control: derecognise, remeasure retained interest at fair value, gain/loss in P&L; FCTR recycles.
  • Ind AS 7 for FX on cash: unrealised FX on cash balances is shown separately in the reconciliation, not in CFO.
  • Schedule III vs Ind AS 1: where a specific Ind AS requires a different presentation, the standard prevails (Schedule III general instructions).

Key takeaways

  • PPA: fair value net assets with intangibles and DTL; goodwill as the residual; bargain purchase goes to capital reserve in India.
  • Control = power + variable returns + link; protective rights do not count.
  • Ind AS 7.33: interest paid financing, interest/dividend received investing for non-financial entities.
  • Ind AS 21: monetary items at closing rate through P&L; foreign operations translated through OCI.
  • Know the 2021 Schedule III additions and the high-yield CARO clauses with thresholds.

Practice Questions

Q1. PPA with NCI choices [L2]

A listed Indian company acquires 70% of a specialty chemicals company for ₹1,050 crore cash plus an earn-out with acquisition-date fair value ₹60 crore. Book net assets are ₹600 crore; fair value uplifts are customer relationships ₹200 crore, brand ₹80 crore and land ₹50 crore (all without tax step-up; tax rate 25.168%). NCI fair value is ₹420 crore. Compute goodwill under both NCI measurement options and state how a ₹20 crore later increase in the earn-out fair value is accounted for.

💡 Hint: Compute the DTL on all uplifts before determining fair value of net assets.

Q2. Control fact patterns [L2]

For each, conclude on control, joint control or significant influence with reasons: (a) 45% voting interest, the remaining 55% held by 2,000 retail shareholders, no shareholder agreement; (b) 60% stake where the 40% investor's consent is required for annual budget, CEO appointment and capex above ₹10 crore; (c) 30% stake plus currently exercisable warrants to acquire another 30% at a deep-out-of-the-money price.

Q3. Classify cash flows [L1]

An Indian manufacturing company reports: interest paid ₹45 crore (₹5 crore capitalised under Ind AS 23), dividend received from an associate ₹12 crore, dividend paid ₹30 crore, income tax paid ₹60 crore, lease principal ₹18 crore, lease interest ₹7 crore, purchase of a machine on 90-day credit ₹25 crore, and FX gain on USD cash balances ₹2 crore. Classify each under Ind AS 7 and identify non-cash items for disclosure.

Q4. FX remeasurement and translation [L2]

An Indian parent with a US subsidiary (functional currency USD) has: a USD 5 million export receivable booked at ₹84.00; a USD 2 million loan to the subsidiary not planned to be settled in the foreseeable future; closing rate ₹85.50; average rate ₹84.80. Subsidiary net assets USD 20 million (opening rate ₹83.00), profit USD 3 million. Compute the exchange differences in standalone P&L, consolidated P&L and consolidated OCI.

Q5. Audit-to-industry pitch with CARO [L3]

You are interviewing for a GCC controllership role. Prepare a structured two-minute answer to "Tell me about a control weakness you found" using a CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) or 3(ix) finding from your audit experience, quantifying the issue and linking it to a month-end close control you would design.
