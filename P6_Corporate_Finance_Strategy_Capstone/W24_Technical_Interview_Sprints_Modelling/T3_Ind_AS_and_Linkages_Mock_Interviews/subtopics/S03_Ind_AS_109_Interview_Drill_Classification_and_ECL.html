Ind AS 109 Interview Drill: Classification and ECL Staging

Scope: Ind AS 109: SPPI and business-model tests, amortised cost/FVOCI/FVTPL, ECL staging, simplified approach for receivables

Why it matters

Ind AS 109 questions appear in every technical interview because they sit at the junction of accounting, credit and modelling. Big 4 FAAS teams build ECL models for NBFCs and corporates; GCC controllership teams run the receivables provision matrix every quarter; valuation analysts need to know whether an investment's fair value changes hit P&L or OCI. Interviewers test two skills: can you classify an instrument with the two tests in the right order, and can you compute an expected credit loss and explain its staging.

Core notes

The classification decision for financial assets

Debt instruments are classified on two tests (Ind AS 109.4.1.1):

  1. Business model test — how the entity manages groups of assets to generate cash flows (B4.1.1–B4.1.6), assessed at portfolio level with evidence such as how performance is evaluated and reported to key management, how managers are compensated, and the frequency, volume and reasons for past sales. - Hold to collect (HTC): collect contractual cash flows; sales infrequent or insignificant, or due to credit deterioration, are consistent. - Hold to collect and sell (HTC&S): both collecting and selling are integral (e.g., liquidity portfolio of a treasury). - Other: held for trading, managed on a fair value basis.
  2. SPPI test — contractual cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal outstanding (4.1.2(b), B4.1.7–B4.1.26). Interest must be consideration for time value, credit risk, other basic lending risks and costs, and a profit margin — consistent with a basic lending arrangement. Leverage or links to equity prices, commodity prices or the issuer's profits fail SPPI.
Business model SPPI met Classification Gains/losses
Hold to collect Yes Amortised cost (4.1.2) Interest (EIR), ECL, derecognition gains in P&L
Collect and sell Yes FVOCI — debt (4.1.2A) Interest, ECL and FX in P&L; other FV changes in OCI, recycled on derecognition
Other Either FVTPL (4.1.4) All in P&L
Any No FVTPL All in P&L

Override options: the fair value option to eliminate an accounting mismatch (4.1.5); and for equity investments not held for trading, an irrevocable instrument-by-instrument election to present FV changes in OCI with no recycling to P&L (4.1.4, 5.7.5) — only dividends go to P&L. Equity instruments, mutual fund units (puttable, generally fail SPPI and do not qualify as equity for the holder) and derivatives are typically FVTPL.

SPPI examples interviewers use:

  • Plain fixed-rate bank FD or corporate bond: passes.
  • Floating rate linked to MCLR/repo with a reset tenor mismatch (e.g., quarterly reset to 1-year rate): requires the modified time value assessment (B4.1.9B–B4.1.9D).
  • Compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs): conversion to equity fails SPPI → FVTPL for the holder.
  • Prepayment at par plus reasonable compensation: passes; the prepayment-features-with-negative-compensation amendment (notified in India in 2019) allows negative compensation too.
  • Loan where interest is a share of borrower's profits: fails.

Reclassification happens only when the business model changes, prospectively from the first day of the next reporting period (4.4.1, 5.6.1) — rare.

Amortised cost mechanics

Measure at fair value plus transaction costs, then apply the effective interest rate (EIR) — the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash receipts to the gross carrying amount (Appendix A). Processing fees on loans reduce the carrying amount and are amortised through EIR, not taken upfront — a frequent NBFC audit point.

Excel: EIR of a ₹100 lakh loan, ₹1 lakh fee, 10% coupon, 3 years, bullet:
=RATE(3, 10, -99, 100)   → about 10.41%

ECL: the general three-stage approach

ECL applies to amortised cost and FVOCI debt assets, lease receivables, contract assets, loan commitments and financial guarantee contracts (5.5.1). It is a probability-weighted, discounted estimate reflecting reasonable and supportable forward-looking information (5.5.17).

Stage Trigger Loss allowance Interest revenue basis
1 Performing; no significant increase in credit risk (SICR) since initial recognition 12-month ECL (5.5.5) Gross carrying amount
2 SICR since initial recognition (rebuttable presumption at 30 days past due, 5.5.11) Lifetime ECL (5.5.3) Gross carrying amount
3 Credit-impaired (objective evidence; default presumed at 90 dpd, B5.5.37) Lifetime ECL Net (amortised cost after allowance) (5.4.1(b))

Key points: SICR compares lifetime default risk at reporting date with that at initial recognition — it is relative, not absolute. A low credit risk simplification (investment grade) allows Stage 1 without the SICR test (5.5.10). Purchased or originated credit-impaired (POCI) assets use a credit-adjusted EIR and recognise only changes in lifetime ECL. Movement between stages is symmetrical: an asset can move back to Stage 1 when SICR no longer exists.

In India, NBFCs (Ind AS phase-wise since FY19) run PD-LGD-EAD models and must compare the Ind AS 109 allowance with the RBI IRACP prudential provision, transferring any shortfall to an impairment reserve (RBI's March 2020 Ind AS circular for NBFCs). Scheduled commercial banks remain on IRACP; RBI has proposed an ECL framework for banks with a target start of 1 April 2027 — date-sensitive, check final directions.

Simplified approach for trade receivables

For trade receivables and contract assets without a significant financing component, the entity must always recognise lifetime ECL — no staging, no SICR tracking (5.5.15(a)(i)). For receivables with a significant financing component and for lease receivables, the simplified approach is an accounting policy choice (5.5.15(a)(ii), (b)). The practical tool is the provision matrix (B5.5.35): historical loss rates by ageing bucket, adjusted for forward-looking conditions.

Historical loss rates are usually derived with a roll-rate (migration) analysis across 8–12 quarters. Specifically identified credit-impaired customers (insolvency under IBC, disputes) are carved out and provided individually. If the opening allowance was ₹40 lakh, the entry is Dr Impairment loss on financial assets (P&L) ₹9.3 lakh, Cr Allowance for ECL ₹9.3 lakh. Schedule III requires trade receivables split into considered good – secured/unsecured, having significant increase in credit risk, and credit impaired, with an ageing schedule.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Intercompany loans interest-free or below market: recognise at fair value; the difference is a capital contribution (parent) or distribution — and ECL still applies.
  • Business model "sales" test: selling receivables under a factoring arrangement with derecognition implies the portfolio is not purely HTC; it may be HTC&S or FVTPL.
  • Government dues (DISCOM receivables, GST refunds): GST refunds are statutory, not contractual, so outside Ind AS 109; DISCOM receivables are financial assets and need ECL even if the counterparty is a state utility — time value from delays matters because ECL is discounted.
  • Forward-looking overlays (post-pandemic or sectoral stress) must be documented and back-tested; auditors challenge unexplained management overlays.
  • Write-off reduces gross carrying amount when no reasonable expectation of recovery (5.4.4); recoveries later go to P&L.

Key takeaways

  • Classify debt assets on business model then SPPI; failing SPPI means FVTPL regardless of business model.
  • FVOCI debt recycles to P&L on sale; FVOCI equity never recycles.
  • General approach: Stage 1 = 12-month ECL; Stages 2 and 3 = lifetime ECL; Stage 3 interest on net carrying amount.
  • 30 dpd and 90 dpd are rebuttable presumptions, not rules.
  • Trade receivables without a significant financing component always use lifetime ECL, typically via a provision matrix.
  • Quote a provision matrix with a forward-looking adjustment and the journal entry in any receivables answer.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify five instruments [L1]

Classify and state the P&L/OCI treatment for: (a) 7.4% NCDs of an AAA PSU held by a manufacturer's treasury that sells them to fund capex when needed; (b) units of a liquid mutual fund; (c) unlisted equity shares in a strategic supplier; (d) CCDs of a start-up; (e) an employee loan at 4% when market rate is 10%.

💡 Hint: Run the SPPI test first for instruments with conversion or equity-like features.

Q2. Staging an NBFC loan [L2]

An NBFC disbursed a ₹50 lakh loan with 12-month PD of 1.5% at origination (lifetime PD 4%). At 31 March 2026 the loan is 45 days past due; 12-month PD is now 6% and lifetime PD 14%. LGD is 40%, EAD ₹48 lakh and the EIR is 13%. Determine the stage, the loss allowance (ignore discounting beyond one year and state your simplifying assumption) and the interest revenue basis for FY27.

Q3. Build a provision matrix [L2]

Receivable ageing at 31 March 2026 (₹ crore): not due 42, 1–60 days 18, 61–120 days 7, 121–270 days 3, above 270 days 2. Three-year average loss rates: 0.3%, 1.8%, 6.5%, 22%, 70%. A forecast slowdown in the auto sector justifies a 20% uplift in all rates. One customer in the above-270 bucket (₹0.8 crore) was admitted to CIRP. Build the matrix in Excel terms, compute the closing allowance and the FY26 charge if the opening allowance is ₹1.9 crore.

💡 Hint: Carve the CIRP customer out of the matrix and provide for it specifically.

Q4. Interview: simplified vs general [L3]

An interviewer says: "Our receivables are all current, so we only provide 12-month ECL under the simplified approach." Respond, correcting the error, explaining when a corporate can and cannot use the simplified approach, and how the answer changes for a lease receivable and a loan to a subsidiary.
