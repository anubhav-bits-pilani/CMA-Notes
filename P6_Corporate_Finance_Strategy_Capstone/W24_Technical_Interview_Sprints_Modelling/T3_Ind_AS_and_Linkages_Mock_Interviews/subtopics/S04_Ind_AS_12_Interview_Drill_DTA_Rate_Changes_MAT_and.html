Subtopic note

Ind AS 12 Interview Drill: DTA, Rate Changes, MAT and 115BAA

Scope: Ind AS 12: balance sheet approach, DTA recognition criteria, rate changes, MAT credit, Sec 115BAA transition

Why it matters

Deferred tax is where interviewers separate people who memorised "timing differences" (the old AS 22 income-statement approach) from those who can work the Ind AS 12 balance sheet approach. In India the topic carries extra local complexity: MAT credit, the 2019 concessional regime under Sec 115BAA, ITFG clarifications, and from 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbering the provisions. FAAS teams compute deferred tax on Ind AS transition and PPA; controllers prepare the tax rate reconciliation each quarter; valuation analysts must know whether a DTA on losses is worth anything in a DCF.

Core notes

The balance sheet approach

Deferred tax arises from temporary differences — differences between the carrying amount of an asset or liability and its tax base (Ind AS 12.5). The tax base of an asset is the amount deductible for tax against future taxable economic benefits (12.7); of a liability, its carrying amount less amounts deductible in future (12.8).

\[ \text{Deferred tax} = (\text{Carrying amount} - \text{Tax base}) \times t \]

where \(t\) is the rate expected to apply when the difference reverses, based on rates enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date (12.47). For assets, a positive difference is a taxable temporary difference (DTL); a negative one is deductible (DTA). For liabilities, the signs flip.

Typical Indian temporary differences:

Item Carrying amount vs tax base Result
PPE with higher income-tax depreciation (WDV block rates) CA > TB DTL
Provision for gratuity/leave encashment (deductible on payment, Sec 43B) Liability CA > TB (TB = 0) DTA
ECL allowance on receivables (tax deduction only on write-off) Receivable CA < TB DTA
Lease: ROU asset and lease liability (tax deduction for rent paid) ROU CA > TB 0 → DTL; liability CA > TB 0 → DTA Both, recognised gross since the 2023 amendment
FVTPL gains on investments taxed on sale CA > TB DTL (rate may be capital gains rate)
PPA fair value uplift on intangibles (no tax step-up) CA > TB DTL, increases goodwill

Permanent differences (e.g., CSR expenditure disallowed under Explanation 2 to Sec 37(1), penalties) affect current tax and the rate reconciliation but create no deferred tax.

Initial recognition exemption (12.15, 12.24): no deferred tax on initial recognition of goodwill, or of an asset/liability in a transaction that is not a business combination, affects neither accounting nor taxable profit, and — since the amendment notified in India with effect from 1 April 2023 — does not give rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences. The amendment means leases and decommissioning provisions now carry deferred tax.

Outside basis differences (investments in subsidiaries, branches, associates, JVs; 12.39, 12.44): DTL recognised unless the parent controls the timing of reversal and reversal is not probable in the foreseeable future — relevant for undistributed profits of subsidiaries, though since dividend distribution tax was abolished (FY21), Indian dividends are taxed in the shareholder's hands with a Sec 80M deduction for onward distributions.

DTA recognition criteria

A DTA is recognised for deductible temporary differences, unused tax losses and unused tax credits to the extent it is probable that taxable profit will be available against which they can be utilised (12.24, 12.34). Sources of taxable profit (12.28–12.31):

  1. Reversal of existing taxable temporary differences in the same jurisdiction and entity, in the same or appropriate periods;
  2. Future taxable profits (excluding new deductible differences);
  3. Tax planning opportunities.

For a history of recent losses, a DTA on losses requires convincing other evidence (12.35). Indian business losses carry forward for 8 years (Sec 72) while unabsorbed depreciation carries forward indefinitely (Sec 32(2)) — the expiry pattern drives the scheduling. Unrecognised DTAs are reassessed at each reporting date (12.37) and the carrying amount reviewed for recoverability (12.56). DTA and DTL are offset only where there is a legally enforceable right and the same taxation authority (12.74). Deferred tax is never discounted (12.53).

Rate changes

Deferred tax balances are remeasured when rates change. Use enacted or substantively enacted rates (12.46–12.48). In India, a Finance Bill is generally regarded as substantively enacted once passed by both Houses of Parliament (assent is then a formality), so a Finance Bill introduced in February but passed after 31 March does not affect 31 March balances — an event after the reporting period, disclosed if material. The remeasurement goes to P&L, except to the extent it relates to items previously recognised in OCI or equity, which follow those items (12.58, 12.61A — "backwards tracing").

Where different rates apply to different levels of income or the manner of recovery (use vs sale, 12.51–12.51A), measure using the expected manner — e.g., land held for long-term appreciation at the long-term capital gains rate.

MAT credit

Under Sec 115JB (1961 Act), a company pays the higher of normal tax and Minimum Alternate Tax (15% of book profit plus surcharge and cess); the excess of MAT over normal tax is creditable against future normal tax for up to 15 years (Sec 115JAA). The ICAI Guidance Note on accounting for MAT credit under Ind AS treats MAT credit as a deferred tax asset, presented within deferred tax assets (net), recognised only to the extent it is probable that the company will pay normal tax within the credit period (12.34 criteria). Book profit under Ind AS includes OCI items in specified ways (Sec 115JB(2A)) and Ind AS transition amounts are spread over five years (Sec 115JB(2C)) — common FAAS technical questions.

Sec 115BAA transition

The Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 introduced Sec 115BAA from AY 2020-21: domestic companies may opt for tax at 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = 25.168%, without MAT, on forgoing specified deductions and exemptions (Sec 80-IA, additional depreciation, Sec 35(2AB) and others) and without set-off of losses attributable to those deductions. The option, once exercised, cannot be withdrawn. Brought-forward MAT credit lapses on opting.

Accounting effects (per ICAI's 2019 clarifications and Ind AS 12 principles):

  • Deferred tax is measured at the rate expected to apply on reversal. A company that has decided to opt from a future year should schedule reversals: differences reversing after the switch at 25.168%, those before at the old rate (e.g., 34.944% for a company above ₹10 crore income on the old regime).
  • The remeasurement is recognised in the period in which the company decides to opt (board approval / evidence of intent), through P&L except backwards-traced items.
  • MAT credit that will not be utilised before the switch is written off (charged to tax expense).
  • The effective tax rate reconciliation shows a one-off "effect of change in tax rate" line.

Illustration: net DTL of ₹200 crore at 34.944%, i.e., underlying net taxable differences of ₹572.3 crore. On deciding to opt for 115BAA from FY26: new DTL = 572.3 × 25.168% = ₹144.0 crore — a deferred tax credit of ₹56.0 crore to P&L. If unused MAT credit of ₹40 crore cannot be absorbed before the switch, it is written off: net P&L credit ₹16.0 crore.

Date sensitivity: the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27 (1 April 2026) and renumbers the sections; the concessional regime and its trade-offs continue in substance — cite the old section with "corresponding provision of the 2025 Act" in current-year work.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Tax holiday units (old Sec 80-IA/10AA): temporary differences reversing within the holiday period carry no deferred tax (rate nil); scheduling is required.
  • Uncertain tax treatments (Appendix C to Ind AS 12): assume the authority examines with full knowledge; measure at most likely amount or expected value; disclose. Common for transfer pricing and Sec 14A disallowances.
  • Business combinations: DTL on fair value step-ups increases goodwill; acquirer DTAs newly recoverable because of the combination go to P&L, not goodwill (12.67).
  • Share-based payments: DTA based on intrinsic value at reporting date; excess over cumulative expense goes to equity (12.68C).
  • Pillar Two: the temporary exception from deferring tax on global minimum top-up tax (amendment notified for Ind AS 12) matters for Indian subsidiaries of large MNC groups.

Key takeaways

  • Deferred tax = (carrying amount − tax base) × rate expected on reversal; never discounted.
  • DTA requires probable future taxable profit; losses need convincing evidence; reassess each period.
  • Rate changes hit P&L except backwards-traced OCI/equity items; use substantively enacted rates.
  • MAT credit is a DTA under Ind AS, subject to the same probability test.
  • Opting for 115BAA triggers remeasurement at 25.168% and write-off of unusable MAT credit in the period of the decision.
  • Always present the answer as a computation plus a rate reconciliation line.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the deferred tax position [L2]

At 31 March 2026 a company (tax rate 25.168%) has: PPE carrying amount ₹480 crore, tax WDV ₹390 crore; gratuity provision ₹22 crore (deductible on payment); ECL allowance ₹9 crore; ROU asset ₹60 crore and lease liability ₹66 crore; disallowed CSR spend ₹4 crore during the year. Compute the net DTA/DTL, identify which items are permanent differences and present the closing deferred tax line.

💡 Hint: Tabulate carrying amount, tax base and difference for each balance before applying the rate.

Q2. DTA on losses [L2]

A loss-making EV start-up has unabsorbed business losses of ₹300 crore (expiring FY29–FY33), unabsorbed depreciation of ₹120 crore and taxable temporary differences of ₹50 crore reversing over FY27–FY30. Its board-approved plan shows taxable profits only from FY30. Explain how much DTA you would recognise, what evidence you would need, and how an auditor would challenge it.

Q3. Rate change after year-end [L1]

The Finance Bill introduced on 1 February 2026 proposes a rate change effective from tax year 2026-27; it is passed by both Houses on 25 March 2026 and receives assent on 30 March 2026. A second change is announced on 10 April 2026. State which rate applies to deferred tax at 31 March 2026 and how each change is treated in the FY26 financial statements.

Q4. 115BAA decision memo [L3]

A company pays tax under the old regime at 34.944%, has MAT credit of ₹65 crore, a net DTL of ₹140 crore (at 34.944%) and expects normal tax liability of ₹30 crore a year for the next three years. Draft the outline of a technical memo: the decision to opt now or in three years, the deferred tax remeasurement and MAT credit write-off amounts under each option, and the disclosures.

💡 Hint: Compare the MAT credit utilisable before the switch with the rate saving on current tax and the deferred tax remeasurement.

Q5. Interview rapid-fire [L1]

In one line each: why is deferred tax not discounted; why does goodwill not carry deferred tax at initial recognition; why does a lease now carry both a DTA and a DTL; and where does a deferred tax rate change on an FVOCI equity reserve go?
