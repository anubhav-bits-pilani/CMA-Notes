Subtopic note

Ind AS 115 Interview Drill: Five Steps to Contract Balances

Scope: Ind AS 115: five-step model, principal vs agent, variable consideration constraint, significant financing component, contract assets/liabilities

Why it matters

Revenue is the first line every interviewer probes, because it touches almost every client: Big 4 FAAS teams write Ind AS 115 memos for IPO-bound companies, GCC controllership teams book deferred revenue and unbilled revenue every month, and FP&A teams must explain why billings and revenue diverge. Interviewers rarely ask "list the five steps". They ask a fact pattern ("a platform sells third-party goods, offers volume rebates and gives 18 months' credit — how much revenue?") and listen for whether you can reach a number and a journal entry. This note arranges the standard in that exam-style order.

Core notes

The five-step model

Step Question Key paragraphs Typical interview trap
1. Identify the contract Is there an enforceable contract meeting all five criteria (approval, rights identifiable, payment terms, commercial substance, collection probable)? Ind AS 115.9; combination 115.17; modifications 115.18–21 Side letters and verbal extensions; collectability failing so cash received is a liability
2. Identify performance obligations Is each promised good/service distinct — capable of being distinct and distinct in the context of the contract? Ind AS 115.22–30 Installation bundled with equipment; software licence plus significant customisation becomes one PO
3. Determine the transaction price Fixed + variable consideration, adjusted for financing, non-cash consideration and consideration payable to customer Ind AS 115.47–72 Slotting fees/listing fees paid to retailers reduce revenue, not marketing expense
4. Allocate the price Relative stand-alone selling price (SSP); estimate SSP by adjusted market, expected cost-plus-margin or residual (residual only if SSP highly variable/uncertain) Ind AS 115.73–86 Allocating discounts entirely to one PO without meeting 115.82 criteria
5. Recognise revenue When (or as) control transfers — over time if any 115.35 criterion met, otherwise at a point in time using 115.38 indicators Ind AS 115.31–45 Using billing milestones as the progress measure without testing that they depict performance

A good 30-second answer: "Revenue is recognised when control of a distinct good or service passes to the customer, at the amount the entity expects to be entitled to. I work through contract, POs, price, allocation and timing — the judgement usually sits in steps 2, 3 and 5."

Principal vs agent

The test is whether the entity controls the specified good or service before it is transferred to the customer (Ind AS 115.B34–B38). Indicators (B37): primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk before or after transfer, and discretion in establishing the price. Credit risk is not an indicator in the current standard. A principal recognises gross revenue and the supplier cost in cost of sales; an agent recognises only the commission or net margin.

Indian context: e-commerce marketplaces (agent for third-party seller goods, principal for own inventory models), travel aggregators (agent for airline tickets; possibly principal for pre-bought hotel inventory), and staffing or freight forwarders where the analysis depends on who bears fulfilment risk. Interviewers like "Gross Merchandise Value vs revenue" — GMV is an operating metric; revenue under agent accounting is the take rate.

Illustration: a marketplace sells a ₹10,000 phone on behalf of a seller, keeps ₹1,200 commission and remits ₹8,800. As agent, revenue = ₹1,200. If it had bought the phone into its own warehouse at ₹8,800, it would be principal: revenue ₹10,000, COGS ₹8,800. Gross margin in rupees is identical; revenue, margin % and working capital profile are not.

Variable consideration and the constraint

Variable consideration includes discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, performance bonuses, penalties and rights of return (115.51). Estimate using either the expected value (portfolio of similar contracts, many outcomes) or the most likely amount (binary outcomes), applied consistently (115.53).

The constraint (115.56–58): include variable consideration only to the extent that it is highly probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognised will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved. Factors increasing reversal risk include susceptibility to factors outside the entity's influence, long resolution periods, limited experience and a broad range of outcomes. Exception: sales- or usage-based royalties on licences of IP are recognised only when the sale/usage occurs (115.B63). Reassess at each reporting date (115.59).

Illustration: an FMCG company sells ₹50 lakh of goods to a distributor with a 4% rebate if annual purchases exceed ₹6 crore. Based on history, achievement is 80% likely. Using the most likely amount, the rebate is expected; revenue = ₹48 lakh and a refund liability of ₹2 lakh.

Sales returns: recognise revenue net of expected returns, a refund liability, and a right-to-recover asset for the goods (measured at former carrying amount less recovery costs), with COGS reduced correspondingly (115.B21–B27).

Significant financing component

If payment timing provides the customer or the entity with a significant financing benefit, adjust the promised amount for the time value of money using the rate that would apply to a separate financing transaction with the customer (115.60–65). Practical expedient: ignore it if, at inception, the gap between transfer and payment is one year or less (115.63). Factors indicating no SFC (115.62): advance payment where timing is at customer's discretion, substantial variable consideration based on future events, or difference arising for reasons other than financing (e.g., retention money as performance security).

Illustration: equipment delivered 1 April 2025, ₹1,21,00,000 payable on 31 March 2027, customer's borrowing rate 10%. Revenue at delivery = ₹1,21,00,000 ÷ 1.21 = ₹1,00,00,000; interest income of ₹10 lakh in FY26 and ₹11 lakh in FY27. In reverse (customer prepays two years ahead for a long construction), the entity recognises interest expense and revenue grows above cash received — a favourite interviewer twist, common in Indian real estate advance-funded projects.

Contract assets, receivables and contract liabilities

Balance Definition Indian label in practice Presentation
Receivable Unconditional right to consideration — only passage of time required (115.108) Trade receivables (billed or unbilled where right is unconditional) Financial asset; Ind AS 109 ECL
Contract asset Right conditional on something other than time, e.g., future performance (115.107) "Unbilled revenue" or "contract assets" for IT services and EPC Subject to Ind AS 109 impairment; Schedule III other financial/other current asset
Contract liability Obligation to transfer goods/services for which consideration received or due (115.106) Advances from customers, deferred revenue, income received in advance Other current/non-current liabilities

Presentation is on a net contract position basis per contract: a single contract is either a net asset or a net liability. Disclosures (115.113–129) include disaggregation of revenue, opening/closing contract balances, revenue recognised from opening contract liability, remaining performance obligations, and — India-specific — the reconciliation of revenue recognised with the contracted price showing adjustments for discounts, rebates and similar items (Ind AS 115.126AA).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Bill-and-hold (115.B79–B82): revenue only if the reason is substantive, goods are separately identified, ready for transfer and cannot be redirected. Auditors use CARO-style cut-off testing to catch quarter-end bill-and-hold.
  • Material rights: loyalty points or renewal discounts that a customer would not get without the contract are separate POs; defer part of the price.
  • Customer-funded upfront fees (onboarding in SaaS): usually not a separate PO; recognise over the expected service period.
  • Real estate: over-time recognition only if 115.35(c) holds — no alternative use and an enforceable right to payment for performance to date, which depends on the allotment letter and RERA terms.
  • Consideration payable to a customer (listing fees, co-op advertising) reduces revenue unless in exchange for a distinct good/service at fair value.
  • Penalties for delay in EPC contracts are variable consideration, not finance cost or other expense.

Key takeaways

  • Answer with the five steps, then go straight to the judgement step the fact pattern is testing.
  • Principal vs agent turns on control before transfer; the indicators support, they do not decide.
  • Variable consideration is estimated then constrained to the highly probable non-reversal amount and reassessed each period.
  • The financing component practical expedient applies when transfer and payment are within one year.
  • Contract asset = conditional right; receivable = unconditional right; contract liability = cash ahead of performance.
  • Always close an answer with the journal entry and the Schedule III line item.

Practice Questions

Q1. Marketplace gross vs net [L1]

An Indian e-commerce company lists a ₹2,400 kurta from a seller. It collects ₹2,400 from the customer, retains ₹360 as commission plus ₹60 as a shipping fee (shipping is performed by a third-party courier engaged by the company at ₹50), and remits the rest to the seller, who sets the price and owns the inventory. Determine revenue, cost and the principal/agent conclusion for each promise and write the journal entries.

💡 Hint: Analyse the kurta and the shipping service as separate specified services before applying the control test.

Q2. Volume rebate with the constraint [L2]

A pharma distributor's supply contract (FY ending 31 March 2026) provides a 2% rebate on all purchases if annual purchases exceed ₹20 crore and 5% if they exceed ₹30 crore. Q1 purchases are ₹6 crore; management expects full-year purchases of ₹24 crore with 70% probability, ₹32 crore with 20% and ₹18 crore with 10%. Compute Q1 revenue under the expected value and most likely methods, decide which is more appropriate, and explain how the constraint applies if a key product faces a pending NPPA price-control notification.

Q3. Deferred payment and advance payment [L2]

Company A delivers a machine on 1 April 2025 for ₹2.42 crore payable on 31 March 2027; the cash price is ₹2.00 crore. Company B receives ₹1.00 crore on 1 April 2025 for a customised machine to be delivered on 31 March 2027; its incremental borrowing rate is 9%. Prepare FY26 and FY27 journal entries for both and state the contract balance each presents at 31 March 2026.

💡 Hint: Derive the implied rate for A from the cash price; for B accrete the contract liability.

Q4. Contract asset or receivable [L1]

An IT services company completed milestone 1 of a fixed-price contract (₹80 lakh) on 25 March 2026, but under the contract it can invoice only after the customer signs off milestone 2 in May 2026. It also completed ₹30 lakh of time-and-material work in March, invoiced on 5 April 2026. Classify each balance at 31 March 2026, identify the Schedule III line and state how Ind AS 109 impairment applies.

Q5. Interview: "Why can revenue exceed cash for three years?" [L3]

A hiring manager at a GCC asks you to explain to a non-finance business head why a three-year SaaS-plus-implementation deal shows revenue growing faster than collections, while a sister deal shows the opposite. Prepare a two-minute answer using Ind AS 115 concepts (performance obligations, contract assets/liabilities, financing component) and one illustrative set of numbers you choose.
