Subtopic note

Three-Statement Linkage Drills for Interviews

Scope: Three-statement linkage drills: depreciation, inventory build, capex, debt raise, dividend, write-off scenarios

Why it matters

"Walk me through a ₹10 increase in depreciation" is the single most common technical question in Indian FAAS, KPO valuation and FP&A interviews, and its variations test whether you truly understand how accounting adjustments move through the model you will build on the job. A clean answer is fast, always includes tax, always ends with "and the balance sheet balances because…", and uses the indirect cash flow method. This note sets a fixed answering routine and runs it across the six scenarios interviewers use most.

Core notes

The answering routine

Use the same four-line sequence every time, in this order:

  1. Income statement: what changes in EBITDA, EBIT, PBT, tax and PAT.
  2. Cash flow statement: start from PAT change, add back non-cash items, adjust working capital, then investing and financing lines; state the net change in cash.
  3. Balance sheet — assets: cash plus the specific asset affected.
  4. Balance sheet — liabilities and equity: retained earnings (via PAT and dividends), debt, tax payable/deferred tax; then confirm assets = liabilities + equity.

Assumptions to state up front: tax rate (use 25% for round numbers, or 25.168% for a 115BAA Indian company), whether the change is also deductible for tax, and that tax is paid in cash in the same period (otherwise tax payable absorbs it). Interviewers reward stating assumptions more than speed.

Scenario 1 — Depreciation +₹10 (tax-deductible, 25%)

Statement Effect
IS EBIT −10; tax −2.5; PAT −7.5
CFS PAT −7.5; add back depreciation +10; CFO +2.5; net cash +2.5
BS assets Cash +2.5; PPE −10 → total −7.5
BS L+E Retained earnings −7.5 → total −7.5. Balances.

If book depreciation changes but tax depreciation does not (the usual Indian case — tax follows WDV block rates), current tax is unchanged and a deferred tax effect arises: PAT still −7.5 (deferred tax credit 2.5), CFO change is nil (−7.5 + 10 − 2.5 non-cash deferred tax), cash unchanged, PPE −10, DTL −2.5, equity −7.5. Mentioning this distinction marks you as someone who has done real closes.

Scenario 2 — Inventory build: purchase ₹100 of inventory on credit, not yet sold

No IS impact (inventory is capitalised until sale). CFS: increase in inventory −100, increase in payables +100, CFO nil. BS: inventory +100, trade payables +100. If bought for cash: CFO −100, cash −100, inventory +100 — total assets unchanged. Follow-up: when sold for ₹150, revenue +150, COGS −100, PBT +50, tax −12.5, PAT +37.5; CFO: PAT 37.5 + decrease in inventory 100 − increase in receivables 150 (if on credit) = −12.5 (the tax payment); BS: receivables +150, inventory −100, cash −12.5, equity +37.5.

Inventory write-down ₹20 (Ind AS 2 NRV): COGS/other expense +20; PAT −15 (tax deductible assumption); CFO: PAT −15 + non-cash write-down 20 (or via change in inventory) = +5; cash +5 (lower tax); inventory −20; equity −15. In India a provision for obsolescence is usually not tax-deductible until actual write-off/sale, which converts the tax effect into a DTA — state it.

Scenario 3 — Capex ₹100 for cash at year-end

No IS impact in the year (if purchased on the last day). CFI −100; cash −100; PPE +100. Next year: depreciation (say 10-year life, ₹10) flows as in Scenario 1. Variations: capex on credit (capital creditors +100, no cash; non-cash investing activity disclosed under Ind AS 7.43); capex funded by debt (combine with Scenario 4); borrowing costs capitalised under Ind AS 23 increase PPE and are shown within investing or financing per policy — interest paid capitalised is still interest paid.

Scenario 4 — Raise debt ₹100 at 10%, held as cash

Day 1: no IS; CFF +100; cash +100; borrowings +100. End of year 1: interest 10 → PBT −10, tax −2.5, PAT −7.5; CFS: PAT −7.5 (interest paid is a CFF outflow for Indian non-financial companies under Ind AS 7.33, so in the indirect method you add back finance cost in CFO and show interest paid −10 in CFF: CFO +2.5, CFF −10); net cash −7.5; equity −7.5. Transaction costs (₹2 processing fee) are netted from borrowings and amortised through EIR under Ind AS 109, not expensed upfront.

Scenario 5 — Dividend ₹50 declared and paid

No IS impact (dividends are distributions, Ind AS 32.35). CFF −50; cash −50; retained earnings −50. Declared after the reporting date: no liability; disclose (Ind AS 10.12–13). Declared but unpaid: dividend payable +50, no cash change. Interview follow-ups: TDS on dividends (Sec 194) affects only the split of payment between shareholder and government; interim dividends declared by the board create a liability when declared. Under Companies Act 2013 Sec 123, dividends are paid out of current profits after depreciation or free reserves, subject to the Companies (Declaration and Payment of Dividend) Rules, 2014.

Scenario 6 — Write-offs

Bad debt written off ₹40 with an existing ECL allowance of ₹40: gross receivable −40, allowance −40 → no IS, no cash, no net BS change. Without an allowance: impairment loss 40 → PAT −30; CFO: −30 + 40 (non-cash, or via decrease in receivables) = +10 if deductible; cash +10; receivables −40; equity −30.

Goodwill impairment ₹100: impairment expense 100, no tax effect (goodwill has no tax base and no deferred tax) → PAT −100; CFO: −100 + 100 = 0; cash nil; goodwill −100; equity −100. Interviewers deliberately ask this to see whether you blindly apply 25%.

PPE write-off ₹30 (asset scrapped, no proceeds): loss on disposal 30; PAT −22.5 if tax WDV block allows (in India the block concept means the asset stays in the block unless the block is extinguished — so usually a deferred tax effect); CFO add-back +30; PPE −30; equity reflects PAT.

Model implementation

In a model, each scenario maps to a schedule: depreciation and capex to the PPE roll-forward, inventory to the working capital schedule, debt to the debt schedule with interest on average balances (watch circularity — use an iteration switch or opening balances), dividends to the equity roll-forward. A balance check row (=ROUND(TotalAssets-TotalLiabEquity,2)=0) and a cash proof (=OpeningCash+NetCashFlow-ClosingCash) should both return zero.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Tax paid vs tax expense: if the scenario says tax is payable next year, the PAT effect is the same but cash goes to the tax payable line — state your assumption.
  • Working capital sign conventions: an increase in an asset is a cash outflow; an increase in a liability is an inflow. Say the rule aloud to avoid sign slips.
  • Revaluation of PPE (Ind AS 16 revaluation model): OCI and revaluation surplus, deferred tax through OCI — no PAT effect.
  • Stock-based compensation: expense reduces PAT, added back in CFO, credited to equity (share options outstanding) — equity net effect only the tax portion.
  • Unrealised FX loss on a foreign currency loan: P&L loss, non-cash add-back in CFO, borrowings up — no cash.

Key takeaways

  • Always answer IS → CFS → BS assets → BS liabilities/equity and close with "balances because…".
  • State the tax assumption; recognise when tax is deferred rather than current.
  • Goodwill impairment has no tax effect; dividends and capex have no IS effect.
  • Indian Ind AS 7.33 places interest paid in financing for non-financial companies.
  • Use the summary table as a flashcard set and drill each in under 60 seconds.

Practice Questions

Q1. Five rapid linkages [L1]

At a 25% tax rate, walk through all three statements for: (a) prepaid insurance of ₹12 paid in cash on 31 March; (b) accrued salary of ₹30 at year-end; (c) a ₹60 customer advance received; (d) a ₹25 impairment of an intangible asset with no tax base; (e) issuing equity shares of ₹200 at par.

💡 Hint: Separate items that touch the income statement from pure balance-sheet swaps before computing tax.

Q2. Depreciation with deferred tax [L2]

A company changes its useful-life estimate, increasing FY26 book depreciation by ₹40 lakh; tax depreciation is unchanged and the rate is 25.168%. Show the effects on all three statements, including the deferred tax line, and contrast with an interviewer's "standard" answer that assumes the extra depreciation is tax-deductible.

Q3. Debt-funded capex across two years [L2]

On 1 April 2025 a manufacturer borrows ₹500 crore at 9% and spends it on a plant commissioned the same day (10-year life, straight-line, nil residual). Tax rate 25%, tax and interest paid in cash at year-end, book and tax depreciation equal. Show the FY26 IS, CFS and BS effects and the cumulative position at 31 March 2026. Identify where interest paid sits under Ind AS 7.

Q4. Write-off chain [L2]

A receivable of ₹80 lakh is fully provided under ECL in FY25 (the allowance was not tax-deductible). In FY26 it is written off and becomes tax-deductible. Tax rate 25%. Show the three-statement and deferred tax effects in FY25 and FY26.

💡 Hint: The FY25 charge creates a DTA; the FY26 write-off converts it into a current tax benefit.

Q5. Build-and-explain drill [L3]

In a blank Excel sheet, build a mini three-statement block (IS, BS, indirect CFS) with opening cash ₹100, and add toggles for each of the six scenarios in this note. Design the balance check and cash proof rows and describe how you would use them to diagnose an interviewer-introduced error in under five minutes.
