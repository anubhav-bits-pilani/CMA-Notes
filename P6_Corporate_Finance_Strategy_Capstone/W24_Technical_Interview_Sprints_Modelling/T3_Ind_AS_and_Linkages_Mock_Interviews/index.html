Roadmap topic: Accounting & Ind AS Deep-Dive Mock Interviews: Mastering tricky questions on Ind AS 115, 116, 109, 12, and 3-statement linkages

Overview

Technical accounting interviews for Big 4 FAAS/CMAAS teams, GCC controllership and reporting roles, and listed-company financial reporting teams in India follow a recognisable pattern. A first round screens standards knowledge (Ind AS 115, 116, 109, 12), a second round runs fact patterns that require numbers and journal entries, and a third round tests three-statement linkages and how accounting choices affect KPIs that the business and investors watch. Interviewers are not looking for recitation of paragraphs; they want a candidate who can reach a defensible answer, show the entry, quantify the effect on EBITDA, PAT, net debt and cash flow, and flag the judgement and its disclosure.

For a CMA with audit experience, the edge is that you have seen how these standards behave in real ledgers: unbilled revenue that never converts, lease registers missing renewal options, receivables ageing with stale overrides, MAT credit lingering after a 115BAA switch. The mock interview drills in this topic convert that experience into structured answers. Every answer should follow the same skeleton: principle (one line, with paragraph) → numbers (worked in your head or on paper) → entry or three-statement effect → judgement/disclosure/KPI angle.

The learning sequence runs from standards to linkages. Start with Ind AS 115 (s01) because revenue questions open most interviews, then Ind AS 116 (s02), whose KPI effects are the most commonly misunderstood, then Ind AS 109 (s03) classification and ECL, and Ind AS 12 (s04) with Indian tax overlays. The linkage drills (s05) bring these together in the "walk me through" format, and s06 covers the second-tier areas (PPA, control, cash flow classification, FX, Schedule III, CARO) that fill the remaining interview time. The six key concepts are the flashcards you should be able to deliver in under a minute each.

Prepare by recording yourself answering each practice question in two minutes, then scoring against the week's rubric: technical accuracy, structure, time and communication. A mock interviewer should be allowed to interrupt with "what if tax is deferred?" or "what does that do to net debt?" — the follow-ups are where marks are won.

Related concepts to study

Ind AS close pack and technical memos

→ Week 1–4: Ind AS close pack and technical accounting memos

The close pack you built in Weeks 1–4 is your evidence base for this topic. Each memo (revenue for a bundled contract, lease identification, ECL policy, deferred tax reconciliation) is a ready-made interview story: issue, standard references, alternatives considered, conclusion, and journal entry. In mock interviews, when asked "how would you account for X", answer in memo order — that structure signals FAAS-readiness. Revisit the memos and extract a one-paragraph summary and one number from each, so you can reference "in my portfolio close pack, the provision matrix produced a ₹49 lakh allowance with a forward-looking overlay of…" Interviewers trust candidates who have applied the standard over those who have only read it.

Accounting adjustments in the three-statement model

→ Week 13–15: how accounting adjustments flow through the 3-statement model

Weeks 13–15 built the integrated model; this topic tests whether you can narrate the flows verbally. Every accounting adjustment has a home schedule: Ind AS 116 adds a lease schedule (ROU roll-forward, liability with interest), Ind AS 115 contract balances live in working capital, ECL in the receivables roll-forward, and deferred tax in a temporary-difference schedule linking book and tax depreciation. Practise opening your model and changing one input — a useful-life revision, a new lease, a higher ECL rate — and tracing the result to the balance check. The verbal linkage answer and the model mechanics should match exactly; if they do not, either your model or your answer has an error.

Ind AS vs IFRS carve-outs

Ind AS vs IFRS carve-outs (bargain purchase, FCCB conversion options, real estate revenue)

Interviewers at Big 4 and MNC GCCs ask about carve-outs because group reporting often requires IFRS-to-Ind AS reconciliations. Bargain purchase: Ind AS 103 routes the gain to OCI and capital reserve (or directly to capital reserve) instead of P&L. FCCB conversion options: Ind AS 32.11 treats a conversion option in a foreign currency convertible bond with a fixed conversion price in any currency as equity, whereas IFRS treats it as a derivative liability at FVTPL because the number of rupees is not fixed. Real estate revenue: under the pre-Ind AS 115 regime, Ind AS 11 scope and the ICAI Guidance Note allowed percentage-of-completion for real estate; under Ind AS 115 developers must pass the 115.35(c) test, consistent with IFRS 15. Also know Ind AS 7.33 (interest classification) and Ind AS 101 D13AA (long-term FX monetary items).

US GAAP comparisons for GCC roles

US GAAP comparisons (ASC 606, ASC 842 operating vs finance leases) for GCC interviews

GCCs of US multinationals in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune report to US GAAP ledgers, so interviewers test the bridge. ASC 606 is converged with Ind AS 115 on the five-step model, with differences in practical expedients (shipping and handling election, sales tax presentation), licensing renewals and the collectability threshold ("probable" means a higher likelihood under US GAAP). ASC 842 retains a dual model for lessees: finance leases look like Ind AS 116 (amortisation plus interest), while operating leases recognise an ROU asset and liability but a single straight-line lease cost in operating expenses — EBITDA does not rise and payments stay in operating cash flow. When a GCC prepares Ind AS statutory accounts and US GAAP reporting packs, the lease difference is a standing GAAP adjustment you must explain.

Common pitfalls

Reciting standards without numbers

Reciting standards without a numeric example or journal entry. Candidates fresh from exams list the five steps or the three ECL stages and stop. It happens because exam preparation rewards recall, and because thinking of numbers under pressure feels risky. The correct approach is to attach a small, round example to every answer — "₹100 sale with a 5% rebate expected: revenue 95, refund liability 5" — and the Dr/Cr entry. In mock reviews, detect this by checking whether every recorded answer contains at least one number and one entry; if not, mark it down for structure even if technically correct.

Ignoring tax in linkage questions

Forgetting tax effects in linkage questions. The commonest linkage error is "depreciation up ₹10, cash unchanged, PAT down ₹10". People forget the tax shield or forget that a book-only change creates deferred tax instead of current tax. The fix is to state the tax assumption at the start of every answer (rate, deductibility, timing of payment) and to treat goodwill impairment, dividends and capex as the explicit "no-tax" or "no-IS" exceptions. In review, verify that PAT changes are always at \(1 - t\) of pre-tax changes unless you stated why not, and that the balance sheet tie-out includes tax payable or deferred tax lines.

Mixing simplified and general ECL

Confusing ECL simplified approach (lifetime from day one) with the general three-stage approach. Candidates say "receivables are current so we provide 12-month ECL" or "we move receivables to Stage 2 at 30 days". The confusion arises because NBFC-style staging is widely discussed. Correct treatment: trade receivables and contract assets without a significant financing component must use the simplified approach — lifetime ECL always, no staging — typically through a provision matrix; staging applies to loans, debt investments and, by choice, lease receivables or receivables with financing components. In review, check the policy note and the matrix: any "12-month" language for trade receivables is an error.

Claiming Ind AS 116 raises total lease cost

Saying Ind AS 116 increases total lease expense over the term (timing changes, total is broadly the same). Because early-year expense (depreciation plus front-loaded interest) exceeds rent, candidates conclude the standard makes leases more expensive. Over the full term, total depreciation equals the ROU asset and total interest equals payments minus the initial liability, so total expense equals total rent (adjusted for initial direct costs, incentives and restoration). The right answer separates timing (front-loaded PBT impact) from totals, and separates P&L classification (EBITDA up) from cash (unchanged). In review, ask the candidate to sum the five-year schedule — the totals should reconcile to rent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Retail chain technical interview case [L3]

A listed apparel retailer (FY ending 31 March 2026, tax rate 25.168%) has: (i) 120 stores on 9-year leases with 3-year lock-ins, total annual rent ₹180 crore, of which ₹30 crore is revenue-share rent; (ii) a loyalty programme where customers earn points worth 2% of spend redeemable within 12 months (70% expected redemption), with FY26 sales of ₹2,400 crore; (iii) a sale-or-return arrangement with franchisees of ₹150 crore at a 30% margin, where 8% is expected to be returned; (iv) trade receivables from franchisees of ₹220 crore with no financing component. (a) Explain the lease term judgement and which rent is capitalised. (b) Compute the revenue deferred for loyalty points and returns and pass the entries. (c) State the ECL approach and outline the provision matrix. (d) Explain the overall effect on EBITDA, PAT, net debt and CFO versus pre-Ind AS 116/115 accounting.

💡 Hint: Allocate the transaction price between goods and points on relative stand-alone selling prices before considering redemption.

Q2. Three-statement chain with deferred tax [L2]

A manufacturer at 25% tax makes four changes in FY26: (a) raises ₹200 crore of 8.5% NCDs on 1 April 2025, paying ₹2 crore issue costs; (b) spends ₹150 crore on capex on 1 April 2025, 10-year straight-line book life, tax depreciation at 15% WDV; (c) writes off ₹12 crore of receivables already fully provided; (d) declares and pays a final dividend of ₹40 crore. Walk through IS, CFS and BS effects for the year, including current and deferred tax, and show that the balance sheet balances.

Q3. Financial instruments and tax for an NBFC subsidiary [L3]

A corporate group's NBFC subsidiary holds (i) a ₹500 crore retail loan book, 85% Stage 1 (12-month PD 1.8%), 10% Stage 2 (lifetime PD 12%), 5% Stage 3, LGD 45% throughout; (ii) ₹100 crore of G-secs managed for liquidity and sold as needed; (iii) ₹50 crore of CCDs in a fintech. The parent opted for Sec 115BAA from FY26 and has MAT credit of ₹18 crore it cannot use. Compute the ECL (ignore discounting), classify the investments, and explain the consolidated deferred tax and MAT credit effects in FY26.

💡 Hint: Treat Stage 3 PD as 100% and handle MAT credit under the Ind AS 12 probability test.

Q4. Acquisition with leases and revenue contracts [L3]

An Indian IT services company acquires 100% of a Hyderabad GCC services firm for ₹900 crore. Target book net assets ₹300 crore; identified customer contracts and relationships ₹250 crore; order backlog ₹40 crore; lease liabilities of ₹60 crore and ROU assets of ₹55 crore on the target's books (acquirer's IBR gives a lease liability of ₹64 crore with no favourable/unfavourable terms); contract liabilities of ₹25 crore; tax rate 25.168%. (a) Compute goodwill. (b) Explain how the target's leases and contract liabilities are measured in the PPA. (c) Explain the post-acquisition effects on EBITDA and PAT that the interviewer should expect in the first year.

Q5. Mock interview script and self-scoring [L2]

Write out a 10-question rapid-fire mock interview covering every subtopic in this topic (at least one each on Ind AS 115, 116, 109, 12, linkages and the other frequent areas). For each question, note the headline answer you expect to give, the number you will use, and the follow-up an interviewer is likely to ask. Record yourself and score against technical accuracy, structure, time management and communication.
