Key concept

The EBITDA Bridge from Prior Period to Current

Concept: EBITDA bridge: Prior EBITDA ± price ± volume ± mix ± input cost ± FX ± opex = Current EBITDA

Statement

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{\text{cur}} = \text{EBITDA}_{\text{prior}} + V_{P} + V_{Vol} + V_{Mix} + V_{IC} + V_{FX} + V_{Opex} \]

where, with the prior period (or budget) as base:

  • \(V_P = \sum_i (AP_i - PP_i) \times AQ_i\) — price/realisation in base currency;
  • \(V_{Vol} = (AQ - PQ) \times \overline{PCM}\) — volume at the prior weighted-average contribution per unit;
  • \(V_{Mix} = \sum_i (AQ_i - AQ \times PM_i) \times PCM_i\) — mix at prior unit contribution;
  • \(V_{IC} = \sum_i AQ_i \times (PVC_i - AVC_i)\) — input (variable) cost change per unit on actual volumes;
  • \(V_{FX}\) — translation/transaction effect of exchange rates on revenue and imported costs versus the prior rate;
  • \(V_{Opex}\) — change in fixed operating costs (employee, A&P, freight not in variable cost, power, IT, other).

\(A\) denotes current actual and \(P\) prior; \(PCM_i\) is prior contribution per unit and \(PM_i\) prior mix share. Favourable bars increase EBITDA, adverse bars reduce it.

Explanation

The bridge is the visual form of a variance analysis, ordered so that each bar answers a management question: did we charge more (price), sell more (volume), sell better (mix), buy better (input cost), benefit from currency (FX), and control overheads (opex)? The algebra must reconcile exactly — the sum of bars equals the change in EBITDA — or the chart loses credibility.

Design choices to state:

  • Base: prior year (investor presentations, analyst calls) or budget (internal MIS). The algebra is the same.
  • Ordering: revenue-side bars first (price, volume, mix), then cost (input, FX, opex). Some companies show FX first to present "constant currency" performance.
  • FX split: separate FX from price to avoid crediting commercial teams for currency moves — crucial for exporters and IT services; hedging gains may be a separate bar.
  • Opex granularity: split into inflation, investments (headcount, capability) and one-offs to make the bar actionable.
  • Ind AS 116 consistency: compare EBITDA on the same basis in both periods; rent reclassification is not a performance bar.
  • One-offs: show exceptional items separately so the underlying trend is clear.

Illustration

FY25 to FY26 EBITDA bridge for an Indian pharma exporter (₹ crore):

Bar ₹ crore Driver
FY25 EBITDA 520
Price (constant currency) −40 US generics price erosion of ~4%
Volume +95 New launches and higher domestic volumes
Mix +30 Higher share of specialty injectables
Input cost −25 API and solvent inflation
FX +35 INR depreciation from ₹83.0 to ₹85.5 per USD on export sales net of imported inputs
Opex −45 Wage inflation (−25), new US field force (−30), lower travel (+10)
FY26 EBITDA 570 Check: 520 − 40 + 95 + 30 − 25 + 35 − 45 = 570 ✓

Narrative: "EBITDA grew ₹50 crore to ₹570 crore. Volume and mix added ₹125 crore and FX ₹35 crore, offset by ₹40 crore US price erosion, ₹25 crore input inflation and ₹45 crore opex, mainly the new US field force. Constant currency EBITDA growth was 3%."

In Excel, use the native waterfall chart (set start and end bars as totals); in Power BI, the waterfall visual with a "Bridge Category" column and a sort-order column.

Connections

Combines the price/volume and mix concepts with cost variances; it is the output format for the PVM narratives subtopic and the "headline, three drivers" answer structure. It also connects to Week 19 variance mechanics, Week 20 MIS commentary and the business-partnering margin-decline case. In valuation interviews, the same bridge explains historical margin evolution and supports forecast margin assumptions in the DCF.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a bridge from data [L2]

A consumer durables company's FY25 EBITDA was ₹300 crore. FY26: volume up 8% at prior average contribution of ₹2,500 per unit on 6 lakh units; mix favourable ₹18 crore; realisation −1.5% on revenue of ₹3,240 crore; commodity costs −₹42 crore; FX on imported compressors −₹12 crore; opex +₹20 crore (adverse). Build the bridge, compute FY26 EBITDA and draft a three-sentence commentary.

💡 Hint: Compute volume variance on prior units before applying the other bars.

Q2. Constant currency messaging [L2]

An IT services company's EBITDA rose from ₹1,800 crore to ₹1,950 crore; ₹120 crore of the increase came from INR depreciation and ₹40 crore from hedge gains reclassified from OCI. Design the bridge bars and write the headline a CFO would use on the earnings call.

Q3. Bridge integrity review [L1]

A draft bridge for an FMCG company shows bars of +60 (price), +45 (volume), −20 (mix), −35 (input cost), −15 (opex) between prior EBITDA ₹400 crore and current EBITDA ₹420 crore. Identify the problem, list three likely causes, and explain how you would fix the bridge before it goes to the CFO.
