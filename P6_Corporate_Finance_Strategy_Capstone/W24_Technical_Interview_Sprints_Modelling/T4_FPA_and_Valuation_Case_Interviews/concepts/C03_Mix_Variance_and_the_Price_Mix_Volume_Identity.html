Key concept

Mix Variance and the Price + Mix + Volume Identity

Concept: Mix variance = Σ (Actual qty_i − Actual total qty × Budget mix %_i) × Budget price_i (or margin_i); price + mix + volume = total variance

Statement

\[ \text{Mix variance} = \sum_i \left(AQ_i - AQ \times BM_i\right) \times BP_i \]

where \(AQ_i\) is the actual quantity of product \(i\), \(AQ\) the actual total quantity, \(BM_i = BQ_i \div BQ\) the budget mix share of product \(i\), and \(BP_i\) its budget price (use budget margin per unit \(BCM_i\) for a margin bridge). Then:

\[ \underbrace{\sum_i (AP_i - BP_i) AQ_i}_{\text{Price}} + \underbrace{\sum_i (AQ_i - AQ \cdot BM_i) BP_i}_{\text{Mix}} + \underbrace{(AQ - BQ)\,\overline{BP}}_{\text{Volume}} = \sum_i AQ_i AP_i - \sum_i BQ_i BP_i \]

Explanation

Proof of the identity (worth reproducing in an interview if asked): expand the mix term as \(\sum AQ_i BP_i - AQ \sum BM_i BP_i = \sum AQ_i BP_i - AQ \times \overline{BP}\). Adding price (\(\sum AQ_i AP_i - \sum AQ_i BP_i\)) and volume (\(AQ \times \overline{BP} - BQ \times \overline{BP}\)) leaves \(\sum AQ_i AP_i - BQ \times \overline{BP}\), and \(BQ \times \overline{BP} = \sum BQ_i BP_i\) = budget revenue. So the three variances exactly partition the total, with no unexplained residual — only if price uses actual quantities, mix uses actual total at budget mix and budget prices, and volume uses the budget weighted-average price.

Intuition: mix asks "given the total units we actually sold, how much more or less did we earn because the proportions differed from budget?" Selling more of the high-price (or high-margin) product than budget mix implies is favourable. Mix can be adverse on revenue but favourable on margin when the lower-priced product has the higher margin — a common interview trap, which is why EBITDA bridges compute mix on margin.

Conditions: mix is meaningful only among substitutable products sold in a common unit (litres, tonnes, packs); for unrelated products, show product-level volume variances instead. Define the mix level (product, channel, region) and apply it consistently. CMA standard costing calls this the sales mix variance.

Illustration

A two-wheeler maker (units in thousands; ₹ thousand per unit; margins are contribution per unit):

Model BQ BM BP BCM AQ
Commuter 300 75% 80 12 330
Premium 100 25% 160 40 70
Total 400 400

Total volume is on budget (AQ = BQ = 400), so volume variance = 0; actual mix at budget shares would be 300 and 100.

  • Revenue mix = (330 − 300) × 80 + (70 − 100) × 160 = 2,400 − 4,800 = −2,400 A (₹24 crore)
  • Margin mix = (330 − 300) × 12 + (70 − 100) × 40 = 360 − 1,200 = −840 A (₹8.4 crore)

Narrative: "Volumes were on plan, but premium share fell from 25% to 17.5%, costing ₹8.4 crore of contribution."

Excel: =SUMPRODUCT(AQ - SUM(AQ)*BQ/SUM(BQ), BP)
DAX:   Mix Var = SUMX(Products, ([AQ] - [AQ Total] * [Budget Mix %]) * Products[BudgetPrice])

Connections

Completes the PVM narrative subtopic alongside the price/volume concept and feeds the EBITDA bridge concept (mix at budget margin). It addresses the pitfall on mixing budget and actual quantities, connects to Week 19 variance mechanics and CMA standard costing (sales mix and quantity variances), and is a frequent Power BI build task in live modelling tests (Week 24 t2) where the dataset is given at SKU level.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-product mix [L2]

Budget: X 500 units at ₹200, Y 300 at ₹300, Z 200 at ₹500. Actual: X 450 at ₹210, Y 380 at ₹290, Z 220 at ₹480. Compute price, mix and volume variances, prove the identity numerically and identify which product drove the mix result.

💡 Hint: Compute the budget mix shares and the actual quantities at budget mix before applying budget prices.

Q2. Revenue vs margin mix [L2]

A pharma company sells branded generics (BP ₹120, BCM ₹70) and trade generics (BP ₹60, BCM ₹15), budget 40:60 on 10 lakh units. Actual: 11 lakh units at 45:55. Compute mix on revenue and on margin and explain to a sales head why the margin view matters more.

Q3. Interview: prove it [L3]

An interviewer asks you to prove algebraically, on a whiteboard, that price + mix + volume equals the total variance, and then to explain what goes wrong if volume is computed at actual weighted-average price. Prepare the proof and a numerical counter-example of your own.
