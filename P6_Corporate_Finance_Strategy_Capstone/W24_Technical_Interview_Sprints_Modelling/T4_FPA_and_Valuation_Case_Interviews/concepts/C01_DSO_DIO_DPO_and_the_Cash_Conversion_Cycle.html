Key concept

DSO, DIO, DPO and the Cash Conversion Cycle

Concept: DSO = Receivables ÷ Revenue × 365; DIO = Inventory ÷ COGS × 365; DPO = Payables ÷ COGS × 365; CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO

Statement

\[ DSO = \frac{\text{Trade receivables}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 365, \qquad DIO = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365, \qquad DPO = \frac{\text{Trade payables}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365 \]

\[ CCC = DSO + DIO - DPO \]

where receivables, inventory and payables are period-end (or average) balances; revenue and COGS are for the same period (annualised if the period is shorter than a year — use the number of days in the period instead of 365 for quarters or months). DSO (days sales outstanding) measures how long customers take to pay; DIO (days inventory outstanding) how long stock is held; DPO (days payables outstanding) how long the company takes to pay suppliers. CCC (cash conversion cycle) is the number of days cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.

Explanation

The ratios convert balance sheet amounts into time, which managers can act on and which can be benchmarked across companies of different sizes. A lower CCC means less working capital per rupee of sales; a negative CCC (FMCG leaders with distributor advances, quick-commerce, airlines with advance bookings) means suppliers and customers fund the business.

Conditions and conventions to state in interviews:

  • Denominators: DSO on revenue (receivables include margin); DIO and DPO on COGS (inventory and payables are at cost). Some analysts use purchases for DPO — more accurate when inventory moves sharply.
  • GST: receivables include GST billed but revenue excludes it — for precision, gross up revenue or strip GST from receivables (an 18% GST rate can inflate DSO by about 18%).
  • Average vs closing: closing balances react faster; averages smooth seasonality. Be consistent.
  • Schedule III data: use trade receivables net of ECL allowance, and include contract assets (unbilled revenue) for project and IT businesses; payables include MSME and non-MSME trade payables but not capital creditors.
  • Indian benchmarks vary widely: IT services DSO of 60–80 days including unbilled, FMCG CCC often negative, capital goods/EPC CCC above 100 days.

In models, drive balances from days (Receivables = Revenue × DSO ÷ 365), so that the forecast is interpretable and sensitivities are meaningful.

Illustration

FY26 data for an Indian electrical goods company (₹ crore): revenue 1,460; COGS 1,022; receivables 240; inventory 210; payables 168.

  • DSO = 240 ÷ 1,460 × 365 = 60.0 days
  • DIO = 210 ÷ 1,022 × 365 = 75.0 days
  • DPO = 168 ÷ 1,022 × 365 = 60.0 days
  • CCC = 60 + 75 − 60 = 75 days

Each day of DSO is worth 1,460 ÷ 365 = ₹4.0 crore; each day of DIO or DPO is worth 1,022 ÷ 365 = ₹2.8 crore. Reducing DIO by 15 days releases about ₹42 crore of cash.

Excel: =Receivables/Revenue*365   ;  CCC: =DSO+DIO-DPO
Forecast: Receivables_t =Revenue_t*DSO_t/365

Connections

Used directly in the working capital subtopic ("profits up, cash down"), in DCF defence (terminal working capital assumptions must be consistent with growth), in the business-partnering cases (capex requests forget working capital), and in Week 13–15 three-statement models where working capital schedules are day-driven. It also links to Schedule III ageing disclosures, CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) on quarterly stock statements to banks, and the Sec 43B(h) MSME payment rule which compresses DPO.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and interpret [L1]

A quick-commerce company reports (₹ crore) revenue 5,475, COGS 4,380, receivables 75, inventory 360, payables 720. Compute DSO, DIO, DPO and CCC, and explain what the result implies about how its growth is funded.

💡 Hint: A negative CCC means the business is funded by its suppliers.

Q2. Quarterly days with seasonality [L2]

An air-conditioner maker's Q4 (Jan–Mar, 90 days) revenue is ₹900 crore and COGS ₹630 crore; closing receivables ₹420 crore, inventory ₹560 crore, payables ₹350 crore. Full-year revenue is ₹2,700 crore. Compute the quarter's days using the correct day count, compare with days computed on annual revenue, and explain which an analyst should use for a March year-end.

Q3. GST distortion [L2]

A company's receivables of ₹236 crore include 18% GST; revenue (net of GST) is ₹1,200 crore. Compute DSO with and without the GST adjustment, and explain how you would make the KPI consistent across business units with different GST rates.
