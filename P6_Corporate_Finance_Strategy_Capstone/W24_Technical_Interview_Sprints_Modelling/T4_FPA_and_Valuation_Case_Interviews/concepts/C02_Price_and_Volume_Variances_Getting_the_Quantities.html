Key concept

Price and Volume Variances: Getting the Quantities Right

Concept: Price variance = (Actual price − Budget price) × Actual qty; Volume variance = (Actual total qty − Budget total qty) × Budget weighted-average price (or margin)

Statement

\[ \text{Price variance} = \sum_i (AP_i - BP_i) \times AQ_i \]

\[ \text{Volume variance} = (AQ - BQ) \times \overline{BP}, \qquad \overline{BP} = \frac{\sum_i BQ_i \times BP_i}{BQ} \]

where \(AP_i, BP_i\) are actual and budget prices of product \(i\); \(AQ_i\) actual quantity of product \(i\); \(AQ = \sum AQ_i\) and \(BQ = \sum BQ_i\) actual and budget total quantities; and \(\overline{BP}\) the budget weighted-average price. For a margin (contribution or EBITDA) bridge, replace \(\overline{BP}\) with the budget weighted-average margin per unit \(\overline{BCM}\); the price variance stays the same because a price change flows fully to margin.

Explanation

Each variance isolates one factor by changing it while holding everything else at defined levels:

  • Price uses actual quantities — it measures what the price difference was worth on what was actually sold. This matches the CMA standard costing sales price variance.
  • Volume uses total quantities and the budget mix and price (embedded in \(\overline{BP}\)) — it measures what the change in total units would have been worth if sold at budget mix and price.
  • The remainder, caused by selling a different proportion of products, is the mix variance; price + mix + volume equals the total revenue variance.

For a single-product business, mix is zero and price + volume = total. With multiple products, using \(\overline{BP}\) (not individual prices or actual prices) is what keeps mix separate. In CMA terms, this volume variance is the sales quantity variance and, with mix, sums to the sales volume variance.

Interview checks: say "favourable/adverse", state the base (budget or prior year), and show the reconciliation. If FX applies, compute price in constant currency and show FX separately.

Illustration

Two products, ₹ per unit, lakh units:

Product BQ BP AQ AP
A 60 100 55 104
B 40 50 55 49
Total 100 110

Budget revenue = 6,000 + 2,000 = ₹8,000 lakh; actual = 5,720 + 2,695 = ₹8,415 lakh; total variance +415.

  • \(\overline{BP}\) = 8,000 ÷ 100 = ₹80
  • Price = (104 − 100) × 55 + (49 − 50) × 55 = 220 − 55 = +165 F
  • Volume = (110 − 100) × 80 = +800 F
  • Mix (residual, or by formula) = (55 − 66) × 100 + (55 − 44) × 50 = −1,100 + 550 = −550 A
  • Check: 165 + 800 − 550 = 415 ✓

Narrative: "Volume added ₹8 crore, but a shift towards lower-priced B cost ₹5.5 crore; net price gains contributed ₹1.65 crore."

Excel: Price  =SUMPRODUCT(AP-BP,AQ)
       Volume =(SUM(AQ)-SUM(BQ))*SUMPRODUCT(BQ,BP)/SUM(BQ)

Connections

This concept is half of the PVM narrative subtopic and pairs with the mix variance concept; its margin version feeds the EBITDA bridge concept. It underpins Week 19 variance mechanics and Week 20 MIS commentary, and the "mixing budget and actual quantities" pitfall in this topic. In Power BI, price and volume variances are implemented as DAX measures using SUMX over a product table with budget and actual columns.

Practice Questions

Q1. Single product reconciliation [L1]

A cement company budgeted 12 lakh tonnes at ₹5,600 per tonne and sold 12.6 lakh tonnes at ₹5,450. Compute price and volume variances, prove the reconciliation and state the narrative in one sentence.

Q2. Margin version [L2]

Using the two-product illustration above with budget contribution per unit of ₹40 for A and ₹15 for B, compute the volume variance at budget weighted-average contribution and the price variance on contribution, and explain why the price variance is unchanged from the revenue version.

💡 Hint: Compute the budget weighted-average contribution per unit from budget quantities.

Q3. Prior year bridge [L2]

Restate the illustration as a prior-year bridge where prior-year quantities and prices are A 50 units at ₹98 and B 30 units at ₹52, and actual is as given. Compute price and volume variances against prior year and explain how investor presentations typically label these bars.
