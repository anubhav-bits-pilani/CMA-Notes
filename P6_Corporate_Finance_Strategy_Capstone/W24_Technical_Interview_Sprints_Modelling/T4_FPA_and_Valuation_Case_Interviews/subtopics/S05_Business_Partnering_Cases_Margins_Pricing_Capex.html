Subtopic note

Business-Partnering Cases: Margins, Pricing, Capex, Headcount

Scope: Business-partnering cases: margin decline diagnosis, pricing decision, capex request review, headcount plan

Why it matters

FP&A roles are increasingly hired on "business partnering" — the ability to sit with a sales head, plant head or HR leader and turn a question into a decision. Final-round interviews at Indian conglomerates, consumer companies and GCCs (where FP&A partners US business units) often run a 20–30 minute case: "Gross margin fell 300 bp; diagnose", "Should we cut price by 5%?", "Review this ₹40 crore capex request", "Sales wants 25 more people". The evaluation is about structure, numbers and a clear recommendation, not about the "right" answer. Your costing background (marginal costing, CVP, relevant costing, capital budgeting) maps directly onto these cases.

Core notes

A universal case structure

  1. Clarify the objective, time horizon and constraints (two or three questions, not ten).
  2. Structure the problem with a tree (profitability, driver or decision tree).
  3. Quantify with the data given; make explicit, reasonable assumptions where it is missing.
  4. Synthesise into a recommendation with the financial impact, risks and next steps.
  5. Communicate headline first, then drivers, then risks — within two minutes for the summary.

Case 1 — Margin decline diagnosis

Prompt: "EBITDA margin fell from 18% to 15% year on year for a ₹1,200 crore consumer company."

Tree: margin = (price × volume × mix − variable cost − fixed cost) ÷ revenue. Work through:

  • Gross margin: price realisation (discounts, schemes, channel mix — modern trade and quick-commerce margins vs general trade), product mix, input cost inflation (palm oil, packaging, crude-linked inputs), FX on imported inputs, plant utilisation (fixed overhead absorption).
  • Opex: A&P spend as % of sales, freight (diesel, longer distribution), wage inflation, new capability costs (D2C, digital).
  • Accounting/one-offs: Ind AS 115 reclassification of trade schemes from expense to revenue reduction, inventory write-downs, ESOP charge, provisions.

Quantify: 300 bp on ₹1,200 crore = ₹36 crore. A good answer: "₹20 crore of the ₹36 crore is gross margin — ₹14 crore input cost (palm oil +18%), ₹6 crore adverse channel mix to quick-commerce; ₹10 crore is A&P stepped up for the new launch; ₹6 crore is operating deleverage at the new plant running at 55% utilisation. Two are structural (input cost, channel), two are timing (launch, ramp-up). Recommend a 3% price increase on the core range, renegotiating quick-commerce terms, and an A&P payback review at month six."

Case 2 — Pricing decision

Prompt: "Sales proposes a 5% price cut to regain share. Contribution margin is 40%."

Break-even volume increase for a price change:

\[ \text{Required } \Delta Q\% = \frac{-\Delta P\%}{CM\% + \Delta P\%} \]

with \(\Delta P\%\) the price change (negative for a cut) and \(CM\%\) the current contribution margin ratio. For a 5% cut at 40% CM: \(5\% \div 35\% = 14.3\%\) more volume just to hold contribution. Then ask: what is the price elasticity (historical data, A/B tests by region)? What will competitors do (price war risk)? Is there capacity? What is the effect on the brand and on channel inventory (distributors' stock revaluation claims)? Alternatives: targeted promotions, pack-price architecture (₹10/₹20 price points in India), value-added bundles, and pricing by channel. Recommendation format: "Do not cut list price; test a 5% scheme in two underperforming states for eight weeks with a volume hurdle of +15%."

Case 3 — Capex request review

Prompt: "Plant head requests ₹40 crore for a new line; claims 3-year payback."

Review checklist:

  • Strategic fit and alternatives: debottleneck existing lines, outsource/contract manufacturing, phased capex.
  • Volume assumptions: are incremental volumes supported by the sales plan and market data, or double counted with another project?
  • Incremental cash flows only: incremental contribution, incremental fixed costs, working capital build (often forgotten), tax on profits and tax depreciation shield (WDV rates), terminal value/salvage, excluding allocated overheads and sunk costs.
  • Metrics: NPV at the hurdle rate (WACC + project premium), IRR, discounted payback; sensitivity to volume, price and delay.
  • Execution risk: commissioning delays, ramp-up curve, approvals (environmental clearance, factory licence), PLI scheme eligibility.

Illustration: ₹40 crore capex, incremental EBITDA ₹14 crore p.a. from year 2 (₹7 crore in year 1), working capital ₹6 crore, 8-year life, tax 25.168%, hurdle 13%. Simple "payback" of three years ignores working capital, tax and ramp-up; after tax and WC, payback stretches beyond four years — the FP&A partner's role is to restate it, not to reject the project.

Case 4 — Headcount plan

Prompt: "Sales wants 25 additional reps; each costs ₹12 lakh CTC fully loaded."

Approach:

  • Cost: 25 × ₹12 lakh = ₹3 crore per year at full run-rate; with phased hiring (5 per month from April), FY cost is lower — build a monthly hiring curve with ramp-to-productivity (e.g., 0% month 1, 50% months 2–4, 100% from month 5) and attrition (Indian sales attrition often 25–35%).
  • Benefit: incremental revenue per productive rep × contribution margin; break-even revenue per rep = ₹12 lakh ÷ CM%. At 30% CM: ₹40 lakh per rep per year.
  • Benchmarks: revenue per rep, territory coverage, sales cost as % of revenue, quota attainment of existing reps (are current reps at capacity?).
  • Alternatives: inside sales, distributor-led coverage, digital channels.
  • Recommendation: approve in tranches (10 now, 15 after hitting productivity milestones), with a monthly dashboard tracking ramp, attainment and attrition.

Also consider the Indian cost loading: employer PF, gratuity provision (Ind AS 19), ESIC where applicable, variable pay, travel, devices — and, since the Labour Codes took effect in late 2025, the wage definition (basic at least 50% of total remuneration) that can increase PF and gratuity costs. Date-sensitive: check final central and state rules.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Data gaps: state assumptions explicitly and show how the conclusion changes if they are wrong.
  • Conflicting stakeholder goals: sales wants share, finance wants margin — frame trade-offs in contribution and cash terms.
  • Sunk costs and allocated overheads: exclude from decisions; include only incremental, avoidable costs.
  • Short-term vs long-term: a price cut may raise volume this quarter but reset customer expectations.
  • Non-financial factors: compliance (environmental clearance), brand, employee morale — mention, but keep the numbers central.

Key takeaways

  • Use one structure for every case: clarify, structure, quantify, synthesise, communicate.
  • Margin cases: separate price, mix, input cost, opex and one-offs; quantify in rupees and basis points.
  • Pricing: compute break-even volume, test elasticity and competitive response, prefer targeted actions.
  • Capex: incremental after-tax cash flows including working capital; NPV and sensitivities over simple payback.
  • Headcount: phased cost, ramp-up productivity, attrition and break-even revenue per head.

Practice Questions

Q1. Margin bridge case [L2]

A ₹2,000 crore auto components supplier's EBITDA margin fell from 14.0% to 11.5%. You are told: steel prices rose 12% (steel is 40% of COGS; COGS is 70% of revenue; 60% of the increase is passed through with a one-quarter lag), utilisation dropped from 82% to 70% (fixed manufacturing costs ₹220 crore), and wage settlement added ₹18 crore. Quantify each driver in ₹ crore and bp, identify the residual, and recommend actions.

💡 Hint: Compute the unrecovered steel cost first; fixed cost deleverage shows up as fixed costs spread over lower revenue.

Q2. Price cut decision [L2]

A water purifier brand sells 2 lakh units a month at ₹12,000 with variable cost ₹7,800. Sales proposes a 7% cut, expecting volume to rise 18%. Compute the break-even volume increase and the contribution impact of the proposal, and draft a recommendation including at least two alternatives.

Q3. Capex request review [L3]

Review this request: capex ₹60 crore on 1 April 2026; incremental revenue ₹50 crore in year 1 and ₹90 crore p.a. for years 2–8 at 30% contribution margin; incremental fixed costs ₹8 crore p.a.; working capital at 15% of incremental revenue; tax depreciation at 15% WDV; tax 25.168%; salvage ₹5 crore; hurdle 12.5%. Build the NPV, IRR and discounted payback in Excel, run sensitivities on volume (−20%) and a 6-month delay, and write a one-paragraph recommendation.

Q4. Headcount plan for a GCC [L2]

A Hyderabad GCC proposes 40 additional analysts at ₹15 lakh fixed CTC, 10% variable, 20% additional employer costs, hired 10 per quarter from Q1 FY27, with 20% annual attrition and three months to full productivity. Build the FY27 and FY28 cost forecast and identify the KPIs you would ask the US business unit to commit to in return.
