Subtopic note

PVM, FX and Cost Bridges: Variance Narratives That Land

Scope: PVM variance narratives: price, volume, mix, FX and cost bridges for revenue and EBITDA

Why it matters

FP&A interviews at Indian listed companies and GCCs almost always include a variance case: "Revenue was ₹50 crore above budget but EBITDA was ₹12 crore below — explain." The interviewer wants three things: correct arithmetic where price, volume, mix and FX add exactly to the total; a narrative that ranks drivers by size and attributes them to business causes; and a recommendation. Your CMA training in standard costing (sales price, sales mix and sales quantity variances; material and labour variances) is a direct advantage — the FP&A versions are the same algebra presented as a bridge chart rather than a variance statement.

Core notes

Revenue PVM decomposition

Notation: for product \(i\), \(AP_i, BP_i\) = actual and budget price; \(AQ_i, BQ_i\) = actual and budget quantity; \(AQ\), \(BQ\) = total quantities; \(BM_i = BQ_i \div BQ\) = budget mix share; \(\overline{BP} = \sum BQ_i BP_i \div BQ\) = budget weighted-average price.

\[ \text{Price} = \sum_i (AP_i - BP_i) \times AQ_i \]

\[ \text{Mix} = \sum_i (AQ_i - AQ \times BM_i) \times BP_i \]

\[ \text{Volume} = (AQ - BQ) \times \overline{BP} \]

\[ \text{Price} + \text{Mix} + \text{Volume} = \sum_i AQ_i AP_i - \sum_i BQ_i BP_i \]

The identity holds because each step changes exactly one factor while holding the others at the levels used in the adjacent step. Using budget quantities in the price formula or actual mix in the volume formula breaks the reconciliation — the most common interview error.

FX variance

For companies with foreign currency revenue (IT services, pharma exports, auto components), separate FX from price:

\[ \text{FX} = \sum_i AQ_i \times AP_i^{FC} \times (\text{Actual rate} - \text{Budget rate}) \]

\[ \text{Price (constant currency)} = \sum_i AQ_i \times (AP_i^{FC} - BP_i^{FC}) \times \text{Budget rate} \]

where \(AP_i^{FC}\) is the actual price in foreign currency. Indian IT companies report "constant currency growth" precisely to strip this out. Hedging gains/losses (cash flow hedges reclassified from OCI under Ind AS 109) are often shown as a separate bridge bar so that operational FX and treasury outcomes are not mixed.

EBITDA (margin) bridge

To bridge EBITDA, compute volume and mix at budget contribution margin per unit rather than price, then add cost variances:

\[ \text{EBITDA}_{A} = \text{EBITDA}_{B} + \text{Price} + \text{Volume}_{m} + \text{Mix}_{m} + \text{Input cost} + \text{FX} + \text{Opex} \]

where \(\text{Volume}_m = (AQ - BQ) \times \overline{BCM}\), \(\text{Mix}_m = \sum (AQ_i - AQ \times BM_i) \times BCM_i\), and input cost = \(\sum AQ_i \times (BVC_i - AVC_i)\) for variable costs per unit (favourable when actual cost is lower). Price variance flows to EBITDA in full (price has no incremental cost). Opex variances (employee costs, marketing, freight, power) are shown separately, often split into inflation, headcount and one-offs.

Worked example

A two-product paint company, budget vs actual for Q3 FY26 (prices ₹ per litre, volumes in lakh litres):

Product BQ BP BVC AQ AP AVC
Premium 40 400 240 36 420 250
Economy 60 200 140 74 195 145
Total 100 110

Budget revenue = 16,000 + 12,000 = ₹28,000 lakh; actual = 15,120 + 14,430 = ₹29,550 lakh; variance +1,550.

  • Budget mix: premium 40%, economy 60%; \(\overline{BP}\) = ₹280.
  • Volume = (110 − 100) × 280 = +2,800
  • Mix = (36 − 44) × 400 + (74 − 66) × 200 = −3,200 + 1,600 = −1,600
  • Price = (420 − 400) × 36 + (195 − 200) × 74 = 720 − 370 = +350
  • Check: 2,800 − 1,600 + 350 = 1,550 ✓

Contribution bridge (budget CM: premium 160, economy 60; \(\overline{BCM}\) = (6,400 + 3,600) ÷ 100 = ₹100):

  • Volume = 10 × 100 = +1,000; Mix = (−8) × 160 + 8 × 60 = −800; Price = +350; Input cost = 36 × (240 − 250) + 74 × (140 − 145) = −360 − 370 = −730.
  • Budget contribution 10,000 → actual = 36 × 170 + 74 × 50 = 6,120 + 3,700 = 9,820. Bridge: 10,000 + 1,000 − 800 + 350 − 730 = 9,820 ✓

Turning numbers into a narrative

Structure: headline → ranked drivers → cause → action.

"Revenue beat budget by ₹15.5 crore, but contribution missed by ₹1.8 crore. Volume added ₹10 crore as economy demand surged in tier-2 towns, but adverse mix cost ₹8 crore because premium share fell from 40% to 33% after a competitor's launch. Input costs, mainly titanium dioxide and crude-linked solvents, cost ₹7.3 crore, only partly offset by a net ₹3.5 crore price gain (premium +₹7.2 crore, economy −₹3.7 crore from a ₹5 per litre discount). Recommendation: protect premium share with targeted dealer schemes rather than broad price cuts, and withdraw the ₹5 per litre economy discount from January, since economy volumes are running well above plan."

Bridge charts: build in Excel with stacked-column waterfalls (or the native waterfall chart) and in Power BI with the waterfall visual; label bars with values and colour favourable/adverse consistently.

Excel: Price variance  =SUMPRODUCT(AP-BP, AQ)
       Mix variance    =SUMPRODUCT(AQ-SUM(AQ)*BQ/SUM(BQ), BP)
       Volume variance =(SUM(AQ)-SUM(BQ))*SUMPRODUCT(BQ,BP)/SUM(BQ)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mix level matters: mix at product level differs from mix at channel or region level; define the hierarchy and compute mix at the lowest meaningful level, with the residual shown at the next level.
  • New or discontinued products: no budget price — treat as volume/"new product" bar rather than forcing into mix.
  • Discounts and schemes: decide whether trade schemes (Ind AS 115 variable consideration) are part of net price or a separate bar; consistency across months matters more than the choice.
  • Units vs value mix: for multi-unit portfolios (litres vs kg) use a common unit or compute mix on revenue share.
  • Order of decomposition: price-first vs volume-first changes the joint variance allocation; state the convention (CIMA/standard costing uses actual quantity for price).
  • Prior year vs budget bridges: same algebra with prior year as the base; investor presentations usually bridge to prior year.

Key takeaways

  • Price uses actual quantities; mix uses actual total quantity at budget mix and budget price/margin; volume uses budget weighted-average price/margin.
  • Price + mix + volume (+ FX) must equal the total — always show the check.
  • EBITDA bridges value volume and mix at budget margin and add input cost, FX and opex bars.
  • Narrate in size order with business causes, then give an action.
  • Your CMA standard costing variances are the same mathematics — say so in interviews.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-product PVM [L2]

A snacks company's budget and actual for October 2025 (volume in lakh packs, price ₹ per pack): Chips BQ 50 at ₹20, AQ 48 at ₹21; Namkeen BQ 30 at ₹30, AQ 36 at ₹29; Biscuits BQ 20 at ₹15, AQ 26 at ₹15. Compute price, mix and volume variances, prove they reconcile and write a three-sentence narrative.

💡 Hint: Compute the budget weighted-average price first and check the sum of variances equals actual minus budget revenue.

Q2. FX separation for an IT services firm [L2]

Budget: 1,000 billable FTEs at USD 4,000 per month at ₹83.0/USD. Actual: 1,050 FTEs at USD 3,950 at ₹85.5/USD. Bridge monthly revenue from budget to actual into volume, price (constant currency) and FX, and explain which bars an India-based delivery head controls.

Q3. EBITDA bridge build [L3]

Using the paint company example in this note, add opex: budget ₹6,000 lakh, actual ₹6,250 lakh (₹150 lakh wage inflation, ₹180 lakh new hires, −₹80 lakh lower advertising). Build the full EBITDA bridge from budget to actual in Excel, design the waterfall chart and draft the CFO commentary with a recommendation.

Q4. Fix the broken variance [L1]

A colleague computed price variance as (AP − BP) × BQ and volume variance as (AQ − BQ) × AP for a single product with BQ 1,000, BP ₹50, AQ 1,200, AP ₹48. Show why the variances do not reconcile with the total and restate them correctly.
