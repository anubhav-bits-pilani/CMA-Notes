FP&A Frameworks: Trees, 30-60-90 Plans and KPI Selection

Scope: Frameworks: profitability tree, driver trees, 30-60-90 day FP&A plan, KPI selection

Why it matters

Interviewers use frameworks to test structured thinking: "How would you think about why profit fell?", "How would you model our revenue?", "What would you do in your first 90 days?", "Which five KPIs would you put on the CEO's dashboard?" A candidate who draws a clean tree, quantifies the branches and picks KPIs that drive decisions signals that they will run the monthly cycle well. These frameworks also become the skeleton of your Power BI reporting pack and driver-based forecast — so in a portfolio defence you can show the tree, the model and the dashboard as one system.

Core notes

Profitability tree

The profitability tree decomposes profit into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive (MECE) branches:

\[ \text{Profit} = \underbrace{(\text{Price} \times \text{Volume})}_{\text{Revenue}} - \underbrace{(\text{Variable cost per unit} \times \text{Volume} + \text{Fixed costs})}_{\text{Costs}} \]

Level 1 Level 2 Level 3 (examples)
Revenue Volume Market size × share; customers × frequency × units per order
Price/realisation List price, discounts, schemes, channel mix, FX
Mix Product, channel, geography, customer segment
Variable cost Materials Input prices, yields, wastage, FX on imports
Conversion Power, consumables, variable labour
Distribution Freight per unit, commissions
Fixed cost Manufacturing Plant overheads, maintenance, depreciation
SG&A Employee costs, A&P, IT, rent (or Ind AS 116 depreciation/interest)

Use it to diagnose: identify which branch explains most of the variance, then drill down. In interviews, quantify each branch with given data before going to the next level — this prevents a list of hypotheses with no ranking.

Driver trees

A driver tree links a financial outcome to the operational drivers managers control, with mathematical relationships at each node. Unlike the profitability tree (accounting-led), it is business-model-specific:

  • Retail: Revenue = stores × sq ft per store × sales per sq ft; or footfall × conversion × average bill value (ABV).
  • IT services: Revenue = billable FTEs × utilisation × billing rate × working days; margin driven by pyramid (fresher ratio), offshore mix, utilisation, wage hikes, FX.
  • SaaS: ARR = opening ARR + new + expansion − contraction − churn; revenue recognised ratably (Ind AS 115 over-time).
  • Quick-commerce/D2C: orders = monthly transacting users × order frequency; revenue = orders × AOV × take rate; contribution per order = AOV × gross margin − delivery − packaging − payment gateway − discounts.
  • Manufacturing: Revenue = capacity × utilisation × realisation per tonne; EBITDA per tonne as the KPI (cement, steel).

Build principles: each node is a formula, leaves are inputs owned by a named business owner, and the tree reconciles to the P&L. In Excel, keep drivers in an inputs sheet with scenario columns; in Power BI, implement drivers as what-if parameters or field parameters for sensitivity.

Example (retail): 200 stores × 1.2 lakh footfall a year × 18% conversion × ₹2,500 ABV = ₹1,080 crore revenue. A 1-point conversion improvement adds ₹60 crore — showing managers where to focus.

30-60-90 day FP&A plan

A standard final-round question for FP&A manager roles. Structure:

Phase Objective Activities Deliverable
Days 1–30: Learn Understand business, people, data Meet business heads and finance team; map the close and reporting calendar; review last 12 months' MIS, budget and forecast accuracy; understand ERP (SAP/Oracle) and planning tools (Anaplan, Adaptive, Excel); identify top 5 pain points Stakeholder map, process map, pain-point list
Days 31–60: Diagnose and quick wins Improve reliability and timeliness Fix one recurring reporting issue (e.g., reconcile MIS EBITDA to GL); automate a manual report with Power Query/Power BI; introduce a variance commentary template; agree KPI definitions One automated report, KPI dictionary, first business review led
Days 61–90: Build and propose Shape the forward agenda Propose a driver-based rolling forecast; design the monthly business review cadence; present roadmap (tools, team, timelines) to CFO FP&A roadmap, forecast model prototype, agreed calendar

Interview tips: tailor the plan to the company (listed company → quarterly results and guidance support; GCC → US business unit partnering, time-zone calendars, SOX awareness), and quantify an outcome ("cut monthly MIS turnaround from WD+8 to WD+5").

KPI selection

Good KPIs are decision-relevant, controllable, measurable, timely and few. Selection steps:

  1. Start from strategy and the driver tree — pick nodes with the highest value sensitivity.
  2. Balance lagging (EBITDA, ROCE, FCF) and leading indicators (pipeline coverage, order intake, conversion, utilisation).
  3. Assign an owner and a target for each; define formula, source system and frequency (KPI dictionary).
  4. Limit to 5–8 for the CEO dashboard; cascade detailed KPIs to functions.
  5. Avoid vanity metrics (GMV without take rate, app downloads without retention) and conflicting incentives.

Examples by business:

Business Headline KPIs
FMCG Volume growth, price/mix, gross margin, A&P %, distribution reach, working capital days
IT services Constant currency revenue growth, utilisation, attrition, deal TCV, EBIT margin, DSO
Cement/steel Volume, realisation per tonne, EBITDA per tonne, capacity utilisation, net debt/EBITDA
SaaS ARR growth, net revenue retention, gross margin, CAC payback, Rule of 40
Retail Same-store sales growth, sales per sq ft, gross margin, inventory turns, store EBITDA

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Non-MECE trees: overlapping branches (e.g., "discounts" in both price and marketing) double count; define each branch once.
  • Accounting vs operational definitions: Ind AS revenue (net of schemes, agent net) may differ from sales MIS (gross invoiced) — reconcile in the KPI dictionary.
  • Ind AS 116 and EBITDA KPIs: decide whether management EBITDA is pre- or post-Ind AS 116 and apply consistently.
  • KPI overload: more than 15 KPIs on a dashboard signals poor prioritisation.
  • Goodhart's law: when a KPI becomes a target, it can be gamed (DSO improved by delaying invoicing) — pair KPIs to counterbalance.

Key takeaways

  • Profitability trees diagnose; driver trees model and forecast — both must reconcile to the P&L.
  • Quantify each branch before drilling deeper to rank causes.
  • A 30-60-90 plan moves from learning to quick wins to a forward roadmap with measurable outcomes.
  • Choose few, controllable, decision-relevant KPIs with owners, definitions and targets, balancing leading and lagging.
  • Reuse these frameworks as the skeleton of your forecast model and Power BI pack.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draw a driver tree [L2]

For an Indian multiplex chain with 900 screens, draw a revenue and EBITDA driver tree to three levels (ticketing, F&B, advertising; occupancy, average ticket price, spend per head; film hire costs, rent/Ind AS 116 effects). Using assumptions you state, compute annual revenue and identify the two drivers with the highest sensitivity.

💡 Hint: Express each revenue stream per screen per show before scaling to the network.

Q2. Diagnose with a profitability tree [L2]

A specialty chemicals company's PBT fell from ₹180 crore to ₹120 crore. Given: volume −4%, realisation −6%, raw material cost per kg −3% (RM is 55% of revenue), fixed costs +₹15 crore, finance cost +₹8 crore. Base revenue ₹1,500 crore. Build the profitability tree, quantify each branch and identify any unexplained residual.

Q3. 30-60-90 plan for a GCC [L3]

You are joining a Bengaluru GCC as FP&A Manager supporting a US medical-devices business unit (US GAAP, SAP S/4HANA, Anaplan). Write a 30-60-90 day plan with specific deliverables, stakeholders and measurable outcomes, and explain how it would differ for an FP&A role at an NSE-listed Indian manufacturer.

Q4. KPI dashboard selection [L2]

Select six KPIs for the CEO dashboard of an Indian D2C apparel brand selling via its own website, marketplaces and 40 stores. For each, give the formula, data source, owner, frequency and the decision it informs, and name one KPI you deliberately excluded and why.
