Defending WACC Inputs in an Indian Valuation Interview

Scope: WACC defence: Rf (10-year G-sec), beta source and adjustment, ERP choice, Kd from rating spread, target capital structure

Why it matters

Every valuation, corporate finance and FP&A interview with a capex or M&A angle eventually asks "what WACC did you use and why?" The number itself matters less than the chain of evidence: where each input came from, why it is consistent with the cash flows (INR, nominal, FCFF), and how sensitive value is to it. Registered valuer firms and Big 4 deal teams expect sources; KPO equity research teams expect consistency with house views; corporate FP&A teams expect a hurdle rate the board will accept. A CMA who can cite the Ind AS 113 fair value hierarchy, the ICAI Valuation Standards and SEBI/RBI pricing rules alongside a clean CAPM build stands out.

Core notes

The formula and the consistency principle

\[ \text{WACC} = \frac{E}{D+E} \times K_e + \frac{D}{D+E} \times K_d \times (1 - t) \]

\[ K_e = R_f + \beta_L \times \text{ERP} + \text{(size/specific risk premium, if justified)} \]

where \(E\) and \(D\) are target market values of equity and debt, \(K_e\) cost of equity, \(K_d\) pre-tax cost of debt, \(t\) the marginal tax rate (25.168% for a 115BAA company), \(R_f\) the risk-free rate, \(\beta_L\) levered beta and ERP the equity risk premium. Every input must match the currency and inflation basis of the cash flows: INR nominal FCFF → INR nominal WACC.

Risk-free rate: 10-year G-sec

Use the yield on the benchmark 10-year Government of India security as of the valuation date (sources: RBI, CCIL/FBIL, NSE debt segment, Bloomberg/Refinitiv). Defence points:

  • Tenor: 10-year approximates the duration of equity cash flows and is the most liquid point; some valuers use a longer tenor or an average yield curve for long-life infrastructure assets.
  • Spot vs normalised: spot is standard and market-consistent; normalising (e.g., 1–2 year average) must be justified and disclosed. In 2025–26 the 10-year yield has traded in roughly the 6.2–6.8% range — date-sensitive, always cite the date.
  • Sovereign default risk: the G-sec is INR-denominated and treated as risk-free in INR; you do not subtract a default spread as you would with USD-denominated sovereign bonds.

Beta: source and adjustment

Sources: regression of stock returns against Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 (weekly over 2 years or monthly over 5 years are common); data providers (Bloomberg adjusted beta, Capitaline, Prowess, Ace Equity). For unlisted targets, build a peer beta: unlever each comparable's beta, take median, relever at the target structure (Hamada):

\[ \beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - t)\frac{D}{E}}, \qquad \beta_L^{\text{target}} = \beta_U \times \left(1 + (1-t)\frac{D}{E}\right) \]

Adjustment: the Blume adjustment (\(0.67 \times \text{raw} + 0.33\)) pulls betas towards 1 on the empirical observation of mean reversion. Defend the choice of index (broad index reduces concentration bias), frequency (weekly avoids daily illiquidity noise; Dimson adjustment for thinly traded stocks), and check the regression \(R^2\) and standard error — an R-squared below 0.1 means the beta is weakly supported, so prefer peer betas.

ERP choice

Options and defences:

Approach Typical India range Source to cite
Historical (Sensex/Nifty return over G-sec, long period) 6–8% Own computation; academic studies
Implied ERP (from index level, expected earnings growth) 6.5–8% Damodaran implied ERP for mature market plus India country risk premium
Mature market ERP + country risk premium (CRP) ~4.3–4.6% + ~2.0–2.5% Damodaran annual country ERP data (January update); sovereign rating Baa3/BBB-
Survey-based 7–8% Fernandez survey, KPMG/Big 4 cost-of-capital studies for India

A defensible interview answer: "I used 7.0–7.5%, triangulated from Damodaran's India total ERP and the implied ERP on the Nifty; using the 10-year G-sec as Rf, the CRP is already partly embedded in Rf because the G-sec is INR-denominated, so I avoided double counting by using a total ERP rather than adding CRP separately to a USD-based build." Stating the date of the ERP data and the double-counting check earns marks.

Kd: rating spread build

\[ K_d^{\text{pre-tax}} = R_f + \text{Credit spread for rating}, \qquad K_d^{\text{post-tax}} = K_d^{\text{pre-tax}} \times (1 - t) \]

Use the company's (or a synthetic) rating from CRISIL, ICRA, CARE or India Ratings; spreads from traded corporate bonds of similar rating and tenor (FIMMDA/FBIL valuation matrices), or from interest coverage → synthetic rating tables. Cross-check against the actual marginal borrowing cost (MCLR + spread on bank loans, NCD coupons) — not the average historical coupon on the books. Illustrative spreads for 10-year INR paper: AAA ~0.6–0.9%, AA ~1.2–1.8%, A ~2.5–3.5% (date-sensitive). For loss-making companies with no tax capacity, use a lower effective tax rate for the shield.

Target capital structure

Weights should be target market-value weights, not book values or current weights if the company is mid-deleveraging. Sources: peer median D/(D+E) at market values, management's stated leverage policy, credit rating thresholds (e.g., Net debt/EBITDA below 2.5× for AA). Market value of debt is usually approximated by book value for floating-rate bank debt. Include lease liabilities in debt only if lease cash flows are excluded from FCFF (consistency with Ind AS 116 treatment).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Size and company-specific premiums: common in Indian unlisted valuations (1–5%); must be explicitly justified (key-person dependence, customer concentration) and not double count risks already in cash flow forecasts.
  • USD vs INR builds: a USD WACC uses US Treasury Rf plus CRP; converting cash flows requires consistent inflation differentials (approximate INR rate = (1 + USD rate) × (1 + INR inflation)/(1 + USD inflation) − 1).
  • Regulatory pricing contexts: SEBI ICDR preferential pricing, FEMA pricing guidelines (internationally accepted pricing methodology for unlisted shares) and Rule 11UA (income-tax) valuations each have their own requirements — the WACC must still be defensible to the regulator.
  • Changing capital structure (LBO, project finance): use APV or year-specific WACC instead of a constant WACC.
  • Negative or very low betas from illiquid stocks: reject and use peers.

Key takeaways

  • Defend each input with a source and date: G-sec yield, peer/adjusted beta, triangulated ERP, rating-based Kd.
  • Keep currency, inflation and tax consistency between WACC and FCFF.
  • Use target market-value weights and relever peer betas at that structure.
  • Avoid double counting country risk when Rf is the INR G-sec.
  • Show a WACC sensitivity (±1%) and explain which input you are least confident about.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build and defend a WACC [L2]

For an unlisted Indian specialty chemicals company: peers' levered betas 1.10, 0.95, 1.25, 0.85 with D/E of 0.30, 0.10, 0.50, 0.20; tax 25.168%; target D/E 0.30; 10-year G-sec 6.5%; ERP 7.25%; company-specific premium 2%; synthetic rating A with a spread of 2.8%. Compute the WACC and write one sentence of justification for each input.

💡 Hint: Unlever each peer at its own D/E before taking the median.

Q2. Rf and ERP challenge [L2]

An interviewer argues: "The G-sec already includes country risk, and you also added a 2.3% CRP to a 4.5% mature-market ERP — you have double counted." Prepare a structured response explaining when this criticism is right, when it is wrong, and how you would restate the cost of equity consistently.

Q3. Kd from rating vs book coupon [L1]

A company's average book interest rate is 7.2% on legacy term loans; its current rating is A+ and recent A+ 5-year NCDs priced at 8.9% (G-sec 6.6%). Its marginal tax rate is 25.168% but it has unabsorbed losses for the next two years. Determine the pre- and post-tax Kd you would use and explain why.

Q4. Sensitivity table [L3]

Build an Excel two-way data table showing equity value per share for ERP (6.0%–8.0% in 0.5% steps) against relevered beta (0.8–1.2 in 0.1 steps), holding other inputs constant (Rf 6.6%, pre-tax Kd 8.0%, tax 25%, D/V 20%), for a company with FCFF next year ₹120 crore valued as a growing perpetuity at 5%, net debt ₹300 crore and 10 crore shares. Explain to a hiring manager which input you would spend most time validating and why.

💡 Hint: Link beta through Ke into WACC so the data table can flex beta directly.
