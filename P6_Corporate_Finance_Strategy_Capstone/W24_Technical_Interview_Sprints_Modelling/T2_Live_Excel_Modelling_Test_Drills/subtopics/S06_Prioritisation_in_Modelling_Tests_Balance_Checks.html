Prioritisation in Modelling Tests: Balance, Checks, Then Polish

Scope: Prioritisation: balance first, then checks, then formatting and write-up; flag assumptions explicitly

Why it matters

A modelling test is scored on what you submit, not on what you intended to build. The most common failure is not technical ignorance but poor sequencing: a beautifully formatted model that does not balance, or a balanced model with no checks and no explanation of assumptions. A fixed priority order protects the marks that matter most and gives you a rule to fall back on when the clock is running out.

Core notes

The priority order

  1. Balance: a complete chain from inputs through IS, BS roll-forwards and CFS to a balance sheet that balances in every forecast year (c01 order). This is the threshold for credibility; an unbalanced model is usually judged as failed regardless of other qualities.
  2. Checks: visible integrity checks proving the model works: balance check per year and a master check; cash on CFS equals cash on BS; retained earnings roll-forward; debt roll-forward; for DCF, TV % of EV and sanity of implied multiples; for variance tasks, components sum to the total.
  3. Required outputs: the specific outputs the brief asks for (value per share, sensitivity table, variance commentary, chart). Strictly, these sit alongside balancing; build them as soon as the model balances.
  4. Formatting: consistent number formats, input colour coding, headers, column widths, freeze panes, grouping.
  5. Write-up: a notes tab and email covering assumptions, checks, outputs and limitations.

Formatting comes after balance and checks because it adds no information. Write-up comes last in the sequence but is time-boxed in advance (the final 10 minutes of the c03 split) so that it is never squeezed out.

Minimal formatting during the build

Some formatting is functional and should be applied as you go, because it prevents errors and takes seconds:

  • Inputs in blue font (or a light fill) so you can see what is hard-coded.
  • One number format for the whole block (e.g. #,##0.0).
  • Timeline headers and units label at the top ("₹ crore, FY ending 31 March").
  • A consistent column structure: same years in the same columns on every sheet, so that formulas are consistent across rows.

Everything else (borders, fills, fonts, titles, print settings) waits.

Building checks efficiently

A check row typically returns zero when correct. Make them easy to read and aggregate:

=ROUND(TotalAssets - TotalLiabilities - TotalEquity, 3)

Master check (one cell that summarises all checks):

=IF(SUMPRODUCT(ABS(Checks!C5:G12))<0.001,"OK","ERROR")

Use conditional formatting (green/red) on the master check and put a link to it at the top of every sheet. Keep checks on one Checks sheet or at the bottom of each statement, but reference them centrally.

Typical check list for a 3-statement plus DCF test:

Check Formula logic
BS balances Assets − Liabilities − Equity = 0
Cash ties CFS closing cash − BS cash = 0
Retained earnings Opening + PAT − dividends − closing = 0
Debt Opening + drawdowns − repayments − closing = 0
Revolver non-negative MIN(revolver row) ≥ 0
Minimum cash MIN(cash row) ≥ minimum cash input
TV share TV PV / EV between, say, 50% and 85% (warning, not error)
Variance sum Price + volume + mix − total variance = 0

Flagging assumptions explicitly

Every brief leaves gaps. The rule: any assumption not given in the brief is (1) placed in the inputs block, (2) labelled with "Assumption" and a short reason, and (3) listed in the notes. Examples:

  • "Interest computed on opening debt to avoid circularity; average-balance version available via switch in Inputs!C20."
  • "Tax on negative PBT set to zero; no DTA recognised on losses (conservative)."
  • "Days ratios on 365-day year; inventory days on COGS."
  • "Shares outstanding assumed constant; no dilution from ESOPs."
  • "Valuation date 31 March 2025; cash flows discounted at year-end; mid-year switch available."

Flagging protects you twice: a reviewer who disagrees with an assumption sees it was deliberate and can change it in one cell; and you avoid being marked wrong for an undocumented choice.

The write-up

A short note (on a Notes tab and pasted into the email) of about 150–250 words:

  1. Outputs: headline results (e.g. "EV ₹1,544 crore; equity value ₹1,194 crore; ₹119 per share; TV 83% of EV").
  2. Structure: sheets and flow in one line.
  3. Assumptions: those not given in the brief.
  4. Checks: list and status ("all checks OK; master check on every sheet").
  5. Limitations and next steps: what you would add with more time (e.g. scenario switch, average-balance interest, quarterly phasing).

Triage when time runs short

If at minute 70 of 90 the model does not balance:

  • Spend up to 10 more minutes debugging using the c05 method.
  • If still unbalanced, stop. Write the note, state the imbalance amount and your hypothesis for its cause, and submit on time. A transparent, diagnosed imbalance scores better than a hidden plug or a late submission.
  • Never hard-code a plug to force balance; reviewers check for it and treat it as an integrity failure.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Brief demands formatting: if the prompt says "presentation quality will be assessed", raise formatting priority slightly, but still after balance.
  • Live tests: narrate the priority order ("I'll get it balancing first, then add checks and tidy up") so the interviewer understands why the sheet looks rough.
  • Required outputs that do not depend on balancing: e.g. a revenue build or PVM; deliver them early, then return to the balancing model.
  • Checks that fail by rounding: use ROUND in the check, not in the model, and set a tolerance.
  • Too many assumptions: if you need more than five or six undocumented assumptions, re-read the brief; you may have missed given data.

Key takeaways

  • Order: balance, checks, required outputs, formatting, write-up; time-box the write-up in advance.
  • Apply only functional formatting during the build (blue inputs, one number format, units label).
  • Build a master check visible on every sheet; zero-based check rows with rounding tolerance.
  • Put every unstated assumption in the inputs block, label it and list it in the notes.
  • If the model will not balance in time, diagnose, document and submit; never plug.

Practice Questions

Q1. Check sheet design [L1]

Design a Checks sheet for a 5-year 3-statement and DCF model with a revolver. List each check, its formula logic, tolerance and whether it is an error or a warning, and write the master check formula.

Q2. Assumption log [L2]

A brief gives revenue growth, margins, capex, days ratios, debt repayment and tax rate, but is silent on: interest calculation basis, dividends, minimum cash, depreciation method for new capex, treatment of tax losses, and discounting convention. Write the six assumption entries as they would appear in the inputs block and the notes.

💡 Hint: For each, state the choice, the reason and how a reviewer could change it.

Q3. Triage decision [L2]

At minute 72 of a 90-minute test: the model balances in FY26 and FY27 but is out by ₹6 crore in FY28 and ₹6 crore in FY29 and FY30; the DCF and sensitivity table are not built; nothing is formatted; no note exists. Prioritise the remaining 18 minutes minute by minute and justify your sequence.

Q4. Write the note [L2]

Write the 200-word submission note for a test where you produced: EV ₹2,340 crore, net debt ₹410 crore, equity value per share ₹386 on 5 crore shares, TV 74% of EV, sensitivity range ₹330–455 per share; assumed interest on opening debt, no dividends in FY26, mid-year convention; all checks passing except that the revolver goes negative in FY30 by ₹2 crore, which you could not fix in time.
