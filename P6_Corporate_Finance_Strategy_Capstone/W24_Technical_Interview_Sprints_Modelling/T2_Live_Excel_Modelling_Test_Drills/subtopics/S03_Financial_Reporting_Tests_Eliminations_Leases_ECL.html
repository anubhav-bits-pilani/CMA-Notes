Subtopic note

Financial Reporting Tests: Eliminations, Leases, ECL and Deferred Tax

Scope: Financial reporting tests: consolidation eliminations, lease schedule, ECL provision matrix, deferred tax computation

Why it matters

Big 4 FAAS teams, listed-company reporting teams and GCC controllership teams test technical accounting through computations rather than modelling. A typical test gives 45–90 minutes to complete three or four exercises in Excel with journal entries. These tests check whether you can apply Ind AS mechanics accurately, lay out a schedule an auditor can follow, and state judgements explicitly. For a CMA with audit experience, they are an opportunity to differentiate, provided the schedules are built quickly and cleanly.

Core notes

Consolidation eliminations (Ind AS 110)

Typical prompt. Parent and subsidiary trial balances or financial statements, ownership %, acquisition-date data, and intra-group transactions (sales, unsettled balances, unrealised profit in inventory, intra-group loans and interest, dividends). Required: elimination entries and a consolidated statement.

Layout. A worksheet with columns Parent | Subsidiary | Adjustments Dr | Adjustments Cr | Consolidated, and an eliminations log listing each entry with a reference. Ind AS 110 (B86) requires full elimination of intra-group assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows, including unrealised profits.

Key entries.

Item Dr Cr
Investment vs equity at acquisition Share capital, reserves of S (pre-acq), goodwill Investment in S, NCI
Intra-group sales ₹50 lakh Revenue 50 COGS 50
Unrealised profit: goods ₹20 lakh at 25% margin on sale held by buyer COGS (or retained earnings) 5 Inventory 5
Intra-group receivable/payable ₹15 lakh Payables 15 Receivables 15
Intra-group loan interest ₹3 lakh Interest income 3 Interest expense 3
Dividend from S to P ₹10 lakh (P owns 80%) Dividend income 8; NCI 2 Retained earnings/dividends of S 10

Traps. Allocating unrealised profit to NCI on downstream sales (downstream: entire elimination against parent; upstream: share with NCI in proportion); forgetting deferred tax on unrealised profit eliminations under Ind AS 12; NCI measured inconsistently (fair value vs proportionate share, a choice under Ind AS 103).

Lease schedule (Ind AS 116)

Typical prompt. Lease term, payments (arrears/advance), escalation, IBR, initial direct costs, incentives, restoration costs. Required: lease liability and ROU schedules, journal entries, P&L and cash-flow effects.

Mechanics. Lease liability at commencement = PV of unpaid lease payments at IBR (Ind AS 116.26). ROU = lease liability + payments at or before commencement − incentives received + initial direct costs + restoration estimate (Ind AS 116.24). Liability schedule: opening + interest − payment = closing. ROU depreciated straight-line over the shorter of lease term and useful life (unless ownership transfers).

Illustration. Annual payment ₹10 lakh in arrears, 3 years, IBR 9%. PV \(= 10 \times (1 - 1.09^{-3}) / 0.09 = 25.31\).

Year Opening Interest 9% Payment Closing
1 25.31 2.28 10.00 17.59
2 17.59 1.58 10.00 9.17
3 9.17 0.83 10.00 0.00

With no IDC, ROU ₹25.31 lakh depreciated at ₹8.44 lakh a year. Year 1 P&L charge \(8.44 + 2.28 = 10.72\), above the ₹10 rent: the front-loading effect. Excel: =PV(rate,nper,-pmt) for the liability; use =PV(rate,nper,-pmt,0,1) for payments in advance.

Traps. Using payments in advance with an arrears formula; omitting IDC from ROU; deducting the first advance payment twice; ignoring escalation (use a cash flow row and NPV rather than PV when payments vary).

ECL provision matrix (Ind AS 109)

Typical prompt. Receivables ageing and historical loss data, sometimes with forward-looking adjustment. Required: loss rates by bucket, ECL provision, journal entry for the movement.

Mechanics. For trade receivables without a significant financing component, Ind AS 109.5.5.15 requires the simplified approach (lifetime ECL from initial recognition); a provision matrix is an acceptable practical expedient (Ind AS 109.B5.5.35). Steps: (1) bucket historical receivables by ageing; (2) compute historical loss rates (often by roll-rate or "ultimately written off ÷ balance in bucket"); (3) adjust for forward-looking information (e.g. a macro overlay); (4) apply rates to current buckets; (5) compare with existing provision and book the movement.

Illustration.

Bucket Balance ₹ lakh Loss rate ECL ₹ lakh
Not due 400 0.5% 2.0
1–30 days 150 2% 3.0
31–90 days 80 6% 4.8
91–180 days 40 20% 8.0
Over 180 days 20 60% 12.0
Total 690 29.8

If the opening allowance is ₹22 lakh, book Dr Impairment loss (P&L) 7.8, Cr Allowance for ECL 7.8. Excel: =SUMPRODUCT(B2:B6,C2:C6).

Traps. Applying the general three-stage approach to trade receivables; ignoring forward-looking adjustment entirely; specific provisions for credit-impaired customers double-counted with the matrix (exclude them from buckets).

Deferred tax computation (Ind AS 12)

Typical prompt. A list of items with carrying amounts and tax bases (or enough data to derive them), tax rate, opening deferred tax balances, sometimes losses carried forward and a rate change. Required: DTA/DTL schedule, P&L vs OCI split, journal entry, and sometimes a tax rate reconciliation.

Mechanics.

\[ \text{Deferred tax} = (\text{Carrying amount} - \text{Tax base}) \times \text{Enacted or substantively enacted rate} \]

For assets, carrying amount above tax base gives a taxable temporary difference (DTL); for liabilities, carrying amount above tax base gives a deductible temporary difference (DTA). Recognise DTA only to the extent probable future taxable profit exists (Ind AS 12.24, 12.34). The movement goes to P&L unless the underlying item is in OCI or equity (Ind AS 12.61A).

Layout.

Item Carrying amount Tax base Temporary difference DTL/(DTA) at 25.168%
PPE 500 380 120 30.2
ECL allowance (liability-like) 29.8 0 (29.8) (7.5)
Lease liability 25.31 0 (25.31) (6.4)
ROU asset 25.31 0 25.31 6.4
Gratuity provision (OCI remeasurements included) 18 0 (18) (4.5)

Net DTL ₹18.2 lakh. The 2023 amendment to Ind AS 12 (narrowing the initial recognition exemption) requires deferred tax on leases and decommissioning obligations where equal taxable and deductible differences arise, so ROU and lease liability are recognised gross (offset in presentation if conditions are met).

Traps. Wrong sign for liabilities; applying the old 34.944% rate after opting for Sec 115BAA; recognising DTA on losses without convincing evidence of future profits; putting the deferred tax on OCI remeasurements through P&L.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rounding: state precision (₹ lakh to two decimals) and use ROUND only in presentation, not in calculations.
  • Missing data: e.g. no IBR given; assume and state, or derive from a given borrowing rate.
  • Presentation in tests: journal entries in Dr/Cr tables with narration; reviewers value clarity over decoration.
  • Interaction effects: an unrealised profit elimination creates a deferred tax effect; lease accounting creates deferred tax; mention these even if not asked.
  • Indian tax specifics: Sec 43B items (e.g. bonus, leave encashment on payment basis) create deductible differences; Sec 36(1)(vii) bad debts are deductible on write-off, so ECL allowances create DTAs.

Key takeaways

  • Consolidation tests: full elimination of intra-group items, correct NCI allocation and deferred tax on unrealised profit.
  • Lease tests: PV at IBR, ROU components, arrears vs advance, front-loaded expense.
  • ECL tests: simplified approach and provision matrix for trade receivables, with forward-looking adjustment.
  • Deferred tax tests: carrying amount minus tax base, correct sign, enacted rate, P&L vs OCI.
  • Lay out every schedule so a reviewer can trace it; state assumptions explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Consolidation eliminations [L2]

P acquired 75% of S on 1 April 2024. During FY25: P sold goods to S for ₹80 lakh at a 20% margin on sales, of which S holds ₹30 lakh in inventory at 31 March 2025; S sold goods to P for ₹40 lakh at 25% margin on cost, of which P holds ₹10 lakh; S owes P ₹12 lakh at year-end; P lent S ₹100 lakh at 8% on 1 October 2024; S declared a dividend of ₹20 lakh. Tax rate 25.168%. Prepare the elimination entries, the NCI share of unrealised profit, and the deferred tax entries.

💡 Hint: Margin on sales and margin on cost give different unrealised profit fractions; compute each separately.

Q2. Lease schedule with escalation [L2]

A 5-year warehouse lease with annual payments in advance of ₹20 lakh escalating 5% per year, IBR 10%, IDC ₹1 lakh, lease incentive received ₹2 lakh, restoration estimate PV ₹1.5 lakh. Build the lease liability and ROU schedules, year-1 journal entries and the P&L charge for each year.

Q3. ECL matrix with overlay [L2]

Historical loss rates by bucket are: not due 0.4%, 1–30 days 1.5%, 31–60 days 4%, 61–90 days 9%, 91–180 days 25%, over 180 days 70%. Management expects a sector slowdown and applies a forward-looking uplift of 15% to all rates. Balances (₹ lakh): 900, 300, 150, 80, 50, 30; a ₹20 lakh balance in the over-180 bucket relates to a customer in insolvency proceedings, fully provided specifically. Opening allowance ₹48 lakh. Compute the closing allowance and the P&L charge.

Q4. Deferred tax with a rate change [L3]

At 31 March 2025 a company has: PPE carrying amount ₹1,200 lakh, tax WDV ₹860 lakh; leave encashment provision ₹45 lakh (deductible on payment under Sec 43B); ECL allowance ₹60 lakh; unabsorbed depreciation ₹90 lakh. It opted for Sec 115BAA from FY25 (opening deferred tax measured at 34.944%). Compute the closing net deferred tax at 25.168%, the effect of the rate change on opening balances, the treatment of unabsorbed depreciation (consider any restriction under Sec 115BAA on brought-forward losses from specified deductions), and the journal entry.
