Mock Drill Regime: Three Timed Builds a Week with Rubric Scoring

Scope: Mock drills: three timed builds per week with self-scoring against a rubric

Why it matters

Speed and composure under test conditions come from repetition, not from reading. A structured drill regime turns the techniques in this topic into reflexes and produces evidence of improvement you can track. Three builds a week is enough to make progress visible within a month without displacing interview preparation and applications. Self-scoring against a fixed rubric keeps the drills honest and points each next drill at your weakest area.

Core notes

The weekly cycle

Day Drill Duration Focus
Monday Full 3-statement (± DCF) from a new prompt 90 min + 20 min review Balance speed, linkages, checks
Wednesday Targeted drill (revenue build, DCF from FCFF, or financial reporting computation) 60 min + 15 min review The weakest rubric dimension from Monday
Friday FP&A data drill (PVM, budget vs actual, Power Query cleanup) or live-style build narrated on recording 60 min + 15 min review Data speed, commentary, narration

Rotate prompt types over a four-week block so every type from s02 and s03 appears at least twice.

Sourcing prompts

  • Annual reports and investor presentations of NSE-listed companies: extract three years of history and set your own assumptions (or use consensus-like ranges).
  • Modelling competition cases and practice sets (FMWC-style), and practice cases from training providers.
  • Peer-exchanged prompts: a study partner writes a prompt for you and you for them; this also trains you to read unfamiliar briefs.
  • Past portfolio work rebuilt from a blank sheet with changed parameters.

Keep a prompt bank sheet: ID, type, source, difficulty, date used, score.

Test conditions

  • Real timer, visible; no pausing.
  • Blank workbook; no templates; no reference to earlier models.
  • Same Excel environment as expected in tests; notifications off.
  • Record the screen for live-style drills (OBS, Teams or the built-in screen recorder) to review hesitation and navigation.
  • Log timestamps: start, skeleton complete, IS complete, BS complete, CFS complete, first balance, checks complete, outputs complete, note complete.

Self-scoring rubric

Use the c04 rubric, scoring each dimension 1–5 with written evidence:

Dimension 1 3 5
Correctness Major errors in outputs Minor errors; outputs within 5% of key Outputs match key; correct treatments
Integrity checks None or failing Balance check only Full check set, master check OK
Structure/readability Hard-codes, mixed layout Mostly clean; some inconsistencies Inputs separate, consistent columns, clear flow
Speed Unfinished Finished on time with gaps Finished with 5+ minutes spare
Clarity of assumptions No note Note with partial assumptions Full note: outputs, assumptions, checks, limits

Total out of 25. Where an answer key is unavailable, check correctness by building the same prompt twice a week apart, or by swapping with a peer.

Review protocol (15–20 minutes after each drill)

  1. Score each dimension with evidence.
  2. Identify the single biggest time loss from timestamps (e.g. 14 minutes debugging a sign error in the CFS).
  3. Identify the root cause (knowledge gap, mechanical slip, poor plan).
  4. Write one specific rule for next time ("CFS working capital lines: opening minus closing, always").
  5. Pick the next drill's focus based on the lowest dimension.

Tracking progress

A drill log in Excel with one row per drill: date, prompt ID, type, minutes to first balance, total minutes, each rubric score, total, biggest time loss, rule written. Useful formulas:

=AVERAGEIFS(Log[Total],Log[Type],"3-statement",Log[Date],">="&TODAY()-28)

=MINIFS(Log[MinsToBalance],Log[Type],"3-statement")

Chart minutes to first balance and total score over time. A realistic progression for a candidate starting from a slow baseline is first balance moving from 70–80 minutes towards 40–50 minutes over 4–6 weeks of three drills a week, with totals rising to 20+ of 25.

Exit criteria

You are test-ready when, over the last three full builds: the model balanced within 55 minutes, all checks passed, a note was submitted on time, and no rubric dimension scored below 3. Continue one maintenance drill per week during active interviewing.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Plateaus: if scores stop improving, change prompt difficulty or type; repeating easy prompts trains memory, not skill.
  • Grading your own correctness: bias towards leniency is natural; use peer review or an answer key at least once a week.
  • Burnout: three drills plus interviews and applications can be heavy; reduce to two drills in interview-dense weeks rather than skipping entirely.
  • Over-fitting to one style: alternate formats (take-home vs narrated live) so you do not only practise silent building.
  • Recording consent: if a peer plays interviewer, agree on recording and storage.

Key takeaways

  • Three drills a week: full build, targeted weakness drill, FP&A data or narrated live drill.
  • Simulate test conditions strictly: timer, blank sheet, no templates, same environment.
  • Score every drill on the five-dimension rubric with evidence and timestamps.
  • Use the review to find the biggest time loss and write one rule for next time.
  • Track minutes to first balance and total score; stop intensive drilling once exit criteria are met consistently.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build your four-week plan [L1]

Create a 12-drill plan for the next four weeks, specifying for each drill the prompt type, source, duration and focus dimension, ensuring every prompt type from s02 and s03 appears at least twice.

Q2. Score a drill [L2]

A drill result: model balanced at minute 63 of 90; balance and cash-tie checks present, no debt or retained-earnings checks; inputs mostly separated but tax rate hard-coded in two formulas; value per share ₹412 against a key of ₹436; note submitted listing outputs but not assumptions; finished at minute 89. Score each rubric dimension with evidence, identify the biggest improvement lever and write the rule for the next drill.

💡 Hint: Compute the percentage gap to the answer key before scoring correctness.

Q3. Drill log analytics [L2]

Design the drill log table and write formulas for: average total score over the last 4 drills; best minutes to first balance by prompt type; count of drills where any dimension scored below 3; and a flag showing whether the exit criteria are met over the last three full builds.

Q4. Peer-exchange prompt [L3]

Write a complete 90-minute prompt for a study partner based on a real NSE-listed company of your choice: three years of summarised history (from the annual report), forecast assumptions, required outputs, and an answer-key checklist (not the answers) you will use to score them.
