Subtopic note

Typical Modelling Test Prompts and How to Approach Each

Scope: Typical prompts: 3-statement from assumptions, revenue build, DCF from given FCFF inputs, budget vs actual variance, PVM from a dataset

Why it matters

Modelling tests draw from a small set of recurring prompt types. Recognising the type in the first two minutes tells you the structure to build, the checks to include and the outputs the reviewer expects. Each type also has a characteristic trap that separates candidates. Building a mental playbook for the five common prompts is the most efficient preparation for the formats in s01.

Core notes

Prompt 1: 3-statement model from assumptions

Typical brief. One to three years of historical IS and BS (sometimes only an opening BS), and forecast assumptions: revenue growth, margins, opex ratios, capex, depreciation, working capital days, debt terms, tax rate, dividend policy, minimum cash. Output: forecast IS, BS, CFS for 3–5 years with a balance check.

Structure. Inputs → IS → supporting schedules (WC, PPE, debt) → BS → CFS → balance check (the c01 order). Cash or revolver is the only plug (c02).

Characteristic traps. Using the wrong base for days (DIO and DPO on COGS, DSO on revenue, 365 vs 360 days as the brief says); interest on opening vs average debt (average creates circularity, needing a switch); forgetting dividends in CFF and retained earnings; tax on negative PBT.

Mini example. Revenue FY26 ₹1,000 crore, COGS ₹620 crore, DSO 60, DIO 75, DPO 50 (365-day year): receivables \(1{,}000 \times 60/365 = 164.4\); inventory \(620 \times 75/365 = 127.4\); payables \(620 \times 50/365 = 84.9\); net working capital ₹206.9 crore. The CFS change is opening NWC minus closing NWC.

Prompt 2: revenue build

Typical brief. Build revenue from drivers: stores × revenue per store; subscribers × ARPU with churn; volume × price by segment; capacity × utilisation × realisation for manufacturing; GMV × take rate for platforms.

Structure. A driver block per segment with opening count, additions, churn/closures, closing count, average count, unit economics and revenue. Sum segments to total revenue; show growth % as a sanity check.

Characteristic traps. Applying a monthly rate to an annual period; using closing rather than average count for flow revenue; ignoring part-year contribution of new units; mixing price escalation and volume growth.

Mini example. A SaaS business has 2,000 customers at the start of FY26, adds 600, churns 10% of the opening base; ARPU ₹1.2 lakh a year. Closing customers \(2{,}000 + 600 - 200 = 2{,}400\); average 2,200; revenue \(2{,}200 \times 1.2 = 2{,}640\), i.e. ₹2,640 lakh.

Prompt 3: DCF from given FCFF inputs

Typical brief. Forecast EBIT (or EBITDA), tax rate, D&A, capex and change in NWC are given (or FCFF directly); plus WACC inputs or WACC itself, terminal growth, net debt and shares. Output: EV, equity value, value per share, sensitivity table.

Structure.

\[ \text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta \text{NWC} \]

Discount factors \(1/(1+\text{WACC})^{n}\) (or \(n - 0.5\) under mid-year convention); terminal value by Gordon growth on \(\text{FCFF}_{N} \times (1+g)\); EV = PV of explicit FCFF + PV of TV; equity = EV − net debt − other claims (minority interest, preference, lease liabilities if FCFF is pre-lease) + non-operating assets; per share = equity / diluted shares.

Characteristic traps. Discounting TV with the wrong period (it is discounted from year N, not N+1); using mid-year on TV inconsistently; growth above WACC; double-counting leases (lease liability deducted but rent also deducted in FCFF); forgetting to state TV % of EV.

Mini example. FCFF years 1–3: ₹100, 110, 120 crore; WACC 12%; g 5%. TV at year 3 \(= 120 \times 1.05 / (0.12 - 0.05) = 1{,}800\). PV of TV \(= 1{,}800 / 1.12^{3} = 1{,}281.2\). PV of FCFF \(= 89.3 + 87.7 + 85.4 = 262.4\). EV \(\approx 1{,}543.6\); TV share about 83%.

Prompt 4: budget vs actual variance

Typical brief. A budget and actual P&L (sometimes by month or cost centre). Output: variance table (₹ and %), favourable/adverse flags, flex budget, and commentary on the top drivers.

Structure. Columns: Budget, Actual, Variance ₹, Variance %, F/A. Sign convention stated explicitly (e.g. positive = favourable for both revenue and costs). Optionally a flexed budget at actual volume to split volume and efficiency effects.

Characteristic traps. Inconsistent sign convention between revenue and cost lines; percentage variance on a negative or zero base; commentary that restates numbers without causes; failing to flex variable costs.

Excel pattern. Favourable flag that handles revenue and cost lines with a type column:

=IF((D5-C5)*IF($B5="Revenue",1,-1)>=0,"F","A")

Prompt 5: PVM from a dataset

Typical brief. A table of budget (or prior year) and actual volume and price by product or segment. Output: price, volume and mix variances that reconcile to the total, and a short narrative.

Structure. Per product: budget qty, budget price, actual qty, actual price, budget mix %, actual qty at budget mix. Then:

  • Price variance \(= (P_a - P_b) \times Q_a\)
  • Volume variance \(= (Q_{a,\text{total}} - Q_{b,\text{total}}) \times \bar{P}_b\), with \(\bar{P}_b\) the budget weighted-average price
  • Mix variance \(= \sum_i (Q_{a,i} - Q_{a,\text{total}} \times m_{b,i}) \times P_{b,i}\)

Check: price + volume + mix = actual revenue − budget revenue.

Characteristic traps. Mixing budget and actual quantities so components do not sum; computing mix at actual prices; ignoring new products with zero budget (treat separately or define a budget price assumption).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ambiguous brief: if the prompt omits a needed assumption (e.g. tax on losses), choose a reasonable treatment, put it in the inputs block, and state it in the notes.
  • Historical data that does not tie: reconcile or flag; do not force.
  • Combined prompts: many tests chain types (revenue build → 3-statement → DCF). Build in that order and get each stage working before the next.
  • Units and scale: ₹ lakh vs crore and 365 vs 360 days must match the brief.
  • Required outputs only: do not build an LBO or a comparable analysis that was not asked for; extra content does not compensate for missing required outputs.

Key takeaways

  • Five prompt types cover most tests: 3-statement, revenue build, DCF from FCFF, budget vs actual, PVM.
  • Identify the type in the first two minutes and apply its standard structure.
  • Each type has a characteristic trap; build the relevant check (balance, TV %, variance sum, sign convention).
  • State any assumption the brief omits in the inputs block and notes.
  • Chain prompts stage by stage, getting each working before moving on.

Practice Questions

Q1. Revenue build [L2]

Build a 3-year revenue forecast for a two-wheeler dealership chain: 40 showrooms at the start of FY26, opening 8 per year evenly through the year; mature showroom sells 1,800 units a year at an average realisation of ₹95,000 growing 4% a year; new showrooms reach 60% of mature volume in their first full year and 100% thereafter. Specify the layout and compute FY26 revenue.

💡 Hint: Even openings through the year mean a new showroom contributes roughly half a year in its opening year; state this as an assumption.

Q2. DCF from FCFF [L2]

Given EBIT for FY26–FY30 of ₹180, 200, 225, 245, 260 crore; D&A ₹40, 44, 48, 52, 55 crore; capex ₹60, 62, 65, 66, 68 crore; increase in NWC ₹12, 13, 14, 12, 10 crore; tax 25.168%; WACC 11.5%; g 5%; net debt ₹350 crore; 25 crore diluted shares; mid-year convention for explicit flows and end-year for TV. Compute EV, equity value per share and TV % of EV, and build a 3×3 sensitivity (WACC 10.5/11.5/12.5%, g 4/5/6%).

Q3. Budget vs actual with flex [L2]

Budget: 50,000 units, revenue ₹25 crore, variable cost ₹15 crore, fixed cost ₹6 crore. Actual: 46,000 units, revenue ₹23.46 crore, variable cost ₹14.26 crore, fixed cost ₹6.3 crore. Prepare a variance table with a flexed budget, state your sign convention, and write a 4-line commentary.

Q4. PVM with a new product [L3]

Budget: Product A 10,000 units at ₹500, Product B 6,000 units at ₹800. Actual: A 9,000 units at ₹520, B 7,500 units at ₹780, C (new, not budgeted) 1,000 units at ₹1,000. Compute price, volume and mix variances for A and B, propose and justify a treatment for C, and show the reconciliation to total variance.
