Modelling Test Formats: Take-Home, Live Build and Platform Tests

Scope: Test formats: 60–90 min take-home, live screen-share build, Excel skills tests on platforms such as Mercer Mettl or iMocha

Why it matters

Each test format measures something different and rewards a different preparation strategy. Candidates who prepare only for one (usually the take-home) are caught out when a GCC interviewer says "share your screen and open a blank workbook", or when the first filter is a 40-minute proctored Excel MCQ test. Knowing the format in advance lets you allocate preparation time, set up your environment and avoid procedural failures (wrong Excel version, missed submission window, webcam issues) that cost offers independent of skill.

Core notes

Format 1: 60–90 minute take-home

How it works. You receive an email or portal link at an agreed time with a prompt (PDF or Excel) and data. You build in your own Excel and email or upload the workbook within 60–90 minutes (occasionally 2–3 hours or 24 hours for case studies). Common at valuation KPOs, boutique advisory and transaction services firms, equity research, and some FP&A teams.

What is evaluated. Output correctness (EV, value per share, balancing BS), integrity (checks, no hard-codes), structure and readability, adherence to the brief (units, periods, required outputs), and a written note. Reviewers often open the file and trace two or three formulas; some compare outputs to an answer key within a tolerance.

Preparation and execution.

  • Confirm the start time, duration, file format and submission method in writing; ask whether internet use is allowed.
  • Use desktop Excel (Microsoft 365) with iterative calculation settings known; avoid Excel for the web if possible because shortcuts and some features differ.
  • Save every 10 minutes with versioned names; submit a few minutes early with the file named as instructed.
  • Include a Notes tab and a short email body: outputs, assumptions, checks, limitations.

Format 2: live screen-share build

How it works. On Teams, Zoom or Google Meet, the interviewer shares a prompt or describes one verbally and watches you build, typically for 30–60 minutes. Variants: a mini 3-statement link exercise, a revenue build from drivers, a DCF from given FCFF, a variance or PVM table, or "here is a model; find the error". Common at GCC FP&A and analytics teams and some Big 4 advisory teams.

What is evaluated. Thinking process as much as output: whether you plan before typing, explain choices aloud, use inputs and references properly, use shortcuts fluently, recover from mistakes calmly, and ask clarifying questions. Interviewers may interrupt with changes ("now make growth 8%") to test whether the model is driven by inputs.

Preparation and execution.

  • Test screen-share in advance; close notifications, email and other workbooks; set zoom to 100–120% so the interviewer can read cells.
  • Narrate: "I'll put assumptions here in blue, then build revenue as volume times price." Silence for minutes reads as being stuck.
  • Ask clarifying questions at the start (units, periods, tax rate, whether to use mid-year discounting).
  • If you get stuck, state the logic and proceed with a placeholder rather than freezing.

Format 3: platform-based Excel skills tests

How it works. Assessment platforms such as Mercer Mettl, iMocha, HackerEarth-style tools or in-house portals deliver a proctored, timed test (typically 30–60 minutes). Formats include MCQs on functions and outputs ("what does this INDEX-MATCH return?"), simulation questions in an embedded spreadsheet (build a SUMIFS, create a pivot, apply conditional formatting), and finance-domain MCQs (ratio calculations, accounting entries). Proctoring may use webcam, screen monitoring, browser lockdown and tab-switch detection. Common as a first filter at large GCCs, shared service centres and some listed companies.

What is evaluated. Breadth of function knowledge and accuracy under time pressure; usually an automated score against a cut-off.

Preparation and execution.

  • Practise functions in an embedded or web spreadsheet, since the platform environment may not support all desktop shortcuts or newer functions; know VLOOKUP/INDEX-MATCH fallbacks if XLOOKUP is unavailable.
  • Read the platform's instructions on navigation, negative marking and whether questions can be revisited.
  • Ensure a stable connection, a quiet room, ID ready and no second screen; tab-switching can flag or terminate the test.
  • Budget time per question (e.g. 40 questions in 40 minutes means about a minute each); skip and return if allowed.

Comparison

Dimension Take-home Live screen-share Platform test
Typical duration 60–90 min 30–60 min 30–60 min
Environment Your Excel Your Excel, observed Embedded spreadsheet or MCQ
Main signal Complete, correct, checked model Process, fluency, communication Function breadth, accuracy
Biggest risk Not balancing in time; missing outputs Freezing; silent building; hard-codes exposed Unfamiliar interface; time per item
Write-up Notes tab and email Verbal summary None

A decision rule for preparation time

If you do not know the format, ask the recruiter: "Is the modelling round a take-home, a live build, or an online test, and how long is it?" Recruiters usually answer. Weight preparation by the formats you are likely to face: KPO/valuation pipelines lean towards take-homes; GCC FP&A towards live builds and platform tests; Big 4 FAAS towards technical computations plus interviews.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mac vs Windows Excel: shortcuts differ (Cmd vs Ctrl, no Alt ribbon keys on Mac). If you practise on Windows, test on Windows.
  • Use of templates in take-homes: if allowed, a personal skeleton may be acceptable, but many firms forbid it or can detect it; when in doubt, build from scratch.
  • Use of AI tools: some firms now explicitly forbid AI assistants in take-homes; follow the instruction and assume proctoring or follow-up questions will test understanding.
  • Errors in the prompt: if data is inconsistent (e.g. opening BS does not balance), do not silently fix it; document the adjustment in the notes or ask during a live session.
  • Overrunning: for take-homes, submit on time with what you have plus a note rather than late with a complete model, unless the firm has said time is flexible.

Key takeaways

  • Take-homes test a complete, checked model and a written note; live builds test process and narration; platform tests test function breadth and speed.
  • Ask the recruiter for the format and duration, then weight preparation accordingly.
  • Prepare the environment: desktop Excel, tested screen-share, stable connection, notifications off.
  • In live builds, narrate and ask clarifying questions at the start.
  • In platform tests, know fallback functions and the platform's navigation and proctoring rules.

Practice Questions

Q1. Format identification and plan [L1]

For each scenario, identify the likely format and write a 5-point preparation plan: (a) a Mumbai valuation boutique says "we'll send you a case at 10 am; return by 11:30"; (b) a Bengaluru GCC says "the second round is with the FP&A manager, bring your laptop and be ready to share your screen"; (c) a Pune shared services centre sends a link to a 45-minute proctored assessment valid for 48 hours.

Q2. Live build narration [L2]

Script your narration for the first 8 minutes of a live screen-share where the prompt is: "Build a 3-year revenue forecast for a quick-service restaurant chain: 120 stores in FY25, 25 net new stores per year, average revenue per store ₹2.4 crore growing 6% a year, new stores contribute 50% of a full year in their opening year." Include the clarifying questions you would ask.

💡 Hint: Clarify whether store openings are spread evenly and whether the per-store figure applies to mature stores only.

Q3. Platform test timing [L2]

A platform test has 30 questions in 45 minutes with negative marking of 0.25 for wrong MCQs, comprising 20 MCQs and 10 simulation tasks. Design a time allocation and a skip strategy, and state the minimum confidence level at which you would attempt an MCQ.

Q4. Handling a flawed prompt [L3]

During a 90-minute take-home you discover that the prompt's opening balance sheet is out by ₹3 crore (assets exceed liabilities and equity) and the required output is equity value per share. Describe how you would handle it in the model and in the submission note, and how you would handle the same issue in a live screen-share.
