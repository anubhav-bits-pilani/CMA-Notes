Key concept

Five-Dimension Scoring Rubric for Modelling Tests

Concept: Scoring rubric: correctness, integrity checks, structure/readability, speed, clarity of assumptions

Statement

Score each modelling test or drill on five dimensions, each 1–5, for a total out of 25:

\[ \text{Score} = C + I + S + T + A \]

where \(C\) = correctness, \(I\) = integrity checks, \(S\) = structure/readability, \(T\) = speed (time management), \(A\) = clarity of assumptions, each an integer from 1 to 5.

Dimension What a 5 looks like
Correctness Outputs match the key within rounding; correct accounting and finance treatments; no logic errors
Integrity checks Balance check every period, cash tie-out, roll-forward checks, master check visible; all pass
Structure/readability Separate inputs block with colour coding; consistent columns across sheets; left-to-right, top-to-bottom flow; no hard-codes in calculations; labelled units
Speed All required outputs and note delivered within time with a small buffer
Clarity of assumptions Notes listing outputs, unstated assumptions with reasons, checks and limitations; assumptions also flagged in the inputs block

A practical pass threshold is 18/25 with no dimension below 3.

Explanation

These five dimensions mirror how firms actually review submissions. A reviewer typically opens the outputs (correctness), looks for a check row (integrity), clicks into two or three formulas to see whether they reference inputs (structure), notes whether everything required was delivered (speed), and reads the email or notes (assumptions). Firms rarely publish their rubric, but feedback from tests consistently clusters on these points.

Correctness is necessary but not sufficient. A model with the right answer but hard-coded numbers and no checks may score worse than a transparent, checked model that is slightly off, because the reviewer cannot trust or reuse the former. Integrity and structure are what make a model auditable, a point a CMA with audit background can make explicitly in interviews.

Speed is scored on delivery, not on minutes left: finishing early with missing outputs scores lower than finishing on time with all outputs. Clarity of assumptions is the most underrated dimension; it is cheap to score well on (10 minutes) and expensive to skip.

Scoring with evidence matters. "Structure 3: inputs separate but tax rate hard-coded in IS!D18 and IS!E18" is actionable; "Structure 3" is not. In s07 drills, the lowest-scoring dimension sets the next drill's focus.

The same rubric can score the week's three mocks, with "integrity checks" replaced by "structure of answer" for interview-style mocks, as in the week's cross-cutting exercise.

Illustration

Two submissions for the same prompt (key: value per share ₹436):

Dimension Candidate A Candidate B
Correctness 5 (₹436) 4 (₹428; depreciation on closing rather than opening PPE)
Integrity checks 1 (no checks) 5 (full set, master OK)
Structure 2 (growth and tax hard-coded in formulas) 4 (one hard-code in DCF)
Speed 4 (on time, no sensitivity) 4 (on time, all outputs)
Assumptions 1 (no note) 5 (full note)
Total 13 22

Candidate A had the correct answer but would likely fail the test; Candidate B would likely pass despite a 1.8% error.

Practice Questions

Q1. Score a submission [L2]

A submission: outputs within 3% of key; a balance check row but no cash tie-out or debt check; inputs on a separate sheet but capex % typed into the PPE schedule; required sensitivity table missing; a three-line note listing outputs only. Score each dimension with evidence and state whether it passes the 18/25 threshold.

💡 Hint: Score speed on delivery of required outputs, not on whether the candidate submitted on time.

Q2. Rubric to improvement plan [L2]

Over six drills, your average scores are: correctness 4.2, integrity 4.5, structure 3.8, speed 2.7, assumptions 3.0. Design the next four drills to address the weakest dimensions, stating the specific technique you would practise in each.

Q3. Interview use [L1]

An interviewer asks: "How do you make sure a model you hand over is reliable?" Answer in 60 seconds using the rubric dimensions as structure and one example from your portfolio.
