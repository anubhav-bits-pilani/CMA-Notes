Key concept

The Balance-Sheet Plug Rule: Cash or Revolver Only

Concept: Balance-sheet plug rule: cash (or revolver) is the only allowed balancing item, driven by CFS

Statement

In an integrated 3-statement model, every balance sheet line except cash (and, where used, the revolving credit facility) must be computed independently from its own roll-forward or driver. Cash is then the result of the CFS:

\[ \text{Cash}_{t} = \text{Cash}_{t-1} + \text{CFO}_{t} + \text{CFI}_{t} + \text{CFF}_{t} \]

where \(\text{CFO}\), \(\text{CFI}\), \(\text{CFF}\) are operating, investing and financing cash flows for period \(t\). If a minimum cash balance is required, a revolver draws or repays the shortfall or surplus:

\[ \text{Revolver draw}_{t} = \max\left(0,\ \text{Min cash} - \text{Cash before revolver}_{t}\right) \text{ (subject to repayment logic)} \]

with repayments limited to the opening revolver balance and the cash available above minimum cash. No other line may be back-solved as "total liabilities and equity minus everything else".

Explanation

The rule works because the CFS is, by construction, the sum of changes in every other balance sheet line plus profit. If each non-cash BS line is driven by its own logic and each movement is captured once on the CFS, then cash derived from the CFS must close the balance sheet. A balance check of zero is therefore evidence that the model is internally consistent.

A hard plug (e.g. "other liabilities = total assets − all other liabilities − equity") forces a zero check regardless of errors, so the check proves nothing. Reviewers search for this, and in a test it is treated as an integrity failure. Equally, plugging retained earnings or "other current assets" hides errors in the IS or CFS.

The revolver extension is needed when cash would go below a minimum operating level. The standard design: compute cash before revolver (opening cash plus all cash flows except revolver), then draw to restore minimum cash or repay from surplus. Interest on the revolver, if computed on average balance, creates circularity (interest affects PAT, which affects cash, which affects the revolver); use opening-balance interest or a circularity switch with iterative calculation and a breaker.

Sign convention matters: an increase in an asset reduces cash; an increase in a liability or equity increases cash. Keep CFS signs consistent so every line is a simple difference of BS balances.

Illustration

FY26 for a company in ₹ crore: opening cash 20, minimum cash 25. PAT 40, depreciation 15, increase in working capital 30, capex 50, debt repayment 10, dividend 8.

Cash before revolver \(= 20 + (40 + 15 - 30) - 50 - 10 - 8 = -23\).

Revolver draw \(= \max(0, 25 - (-23)) = 48\). Closing cash \(= -23 + 48 = 25\); revolver closing balance 48 on the liabilities side. Both cash and revolver are driven by the CFS; no other line moves to make the BS balance. If FY27 generates surplus cash above 25, the revolver is repaid first, up to its balance of 48.

Connections

Underpins c01 (CFS after BS roll-forwards), c05 (an imbalance is meaningful only if no plug exists) and s06 (never plug to force balance at the end of a test). Links to Week 13–15 debt schedules and circularity handling, and to Week 16, where the revolver and cash affect net debt in the equity bridge. In FP&A, the same logic underlies cash-flow forecasting for treasury: the funding requirement is the plug, not an arbitrary balance.

Practice Questions

Q1. Revolver mechanics [L2]

Opening cash ₹15 crore; minimum cash ₹20 crore; opening revolver ₹30 crore; FY26 cash flow before revolver is +₹22 crore; FY27 is −₹18 crore. Compute revolver draws/repayments and closing cash and revolver balances for both years, and write a single Excel formula for the revolver movement that handles both draws and repayments.

💡 Hint: Repayment is limited by both the opening revolver balance and the cash above minimum.

Q2. Spot the plug [L2]

A model's BS shows "Other current liabilities" calculated as =TotalAssets-SUM(Debt,Payables,Provisions,Equity) and the balance check always shows zero. Explain why this is unacceptable, what errors it could be hiding, and how to rebuild the line properly.

Q3. Interview explanation [L1]

An interviewer asks: "Why is cash the only plug in a 3-statement model, and when do you need a revolver?" Prepare a 60-second answer with one numeric example.
