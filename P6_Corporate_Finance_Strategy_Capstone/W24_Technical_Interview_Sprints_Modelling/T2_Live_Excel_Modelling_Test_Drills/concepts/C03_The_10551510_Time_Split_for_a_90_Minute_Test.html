Key concept

The 10/55/15/10 Time Split for a 90-Minute Test

Concept: Time split for 90 min: 10 read/plan, 55 build, 15 check/debug, 10 summary of assumptions and outputs

Statement

Allocate a 90-minute modelling test as follows:

Phase Minutes Clock Deliverable at end of phase
Read/plan 10 0–10 Brief read twice; list of required outputs, units, periods; inputs block populated; sheet structure decided
Build 55 10–65 IS, BS roll-forwards, CFS, cash linked, required outputs calculated
Check/debug 15 65–80 All checks built and passing (or imbalance diagnosed)
Summary 10 80–90 Notes tab and email: outputs, assumptions, checks, limitations; minimal formatting; file saved and sent

For other durations, scale proportionally: approximately 11% plan, 61% build, 17% check, 11% summary. For a 60-minute test: about 7, 37, 10 and 6 minutes.

Explanation

The split protects the two phases most often skipped under pressure. Planning feels like lost time but prevents the most expensive errors (missed units, wrong fiscal year, missing outputs), and populating inputs first means the build phase is pure formula work. The summary is fixed at 10 minutes because a model without a note loses an entire rubric dimension (c04) and invites misinterpretation.

The 55-minute build is the longest phase but has internal milestones (from the c01 order): skeleton and IS by about minute 22, BS roll-forwards by 40, CFS by 52, balance by 58–65. If a milestone slips by more than 5 minutes, simplify (e.g. drop average-balance interest, collapse minor BS lines) rather than continuing at full detail.

The check/debug phase assumes some errors; if the model balances first time, use spare minutes to add checks, the sensitivity table or a chart. If debugging runs over, cut formatting first, never the summary.

Hard stops: set alarms at 10, 40, 65 and 80 minutes. At 80, stop building regardless of state.

Adaptations:

  • Take-home with generous time (2–3 hours): keep the proportions but add a self-review pass.
  • Live screen-share (30–45 minutes): plan aloud for 3–5 minutes with clarifying questions; summary is verbal (1–2 minutes).
  • Multi-part tests: allocate time by marks if stated; otherwise by expected complexity, with a reserve.

Illustration

A candidate's actual log versus target in a 90-minute test:

The candidate also continued building at full detail after the 40-minute milestone slipped. Rule for next drill: full 10-minute read; simplify if a milestone slips by over 5 minutes.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Scale the split [L1]

Compute the time split for a 60-minute, a 120-minute and a 45-minute live test using the proportional rule, and list the milestones you would set alarms for in each.

Q2. Mid-test recovery [L2]

At minute 45 of 90 you have finished the IS and PPE schedule but not working capital, debt, equity or the CFS. Re-plan the remaining 45 minutes, stating what you would simplify, what you would drop, and the new milestone times.

💡 Hint: Decide first which required outputs are non-negotiable, then work back.

Q3. Allocate by marks [L2]

A 90-minute test has three parts: (a) 3-statement model, 50 marks; (b) DCF and sensitivity, 30 marks; (c) a 200-word investment view, 20 marks. Part (b) depends on (a). Allocate time to plan, build, check and summary for each part and justify the allocation.
