Key concept

Debugging a 3-Statement Imbalance by Sign, Size and Pattern

Concept: Debugging the imbalance: check difference sign and size; if constant, opening BS; if growing, a missing CFS line

Statement

Define the imbalance for each period \(t\):

\[ D_{t} = \text{Total assets}_{t} - \left(\text{Total liabilities}_{t} + \text{Total equity}_{t}\right) \]

Diagnose using three properties:

  1. Sign: \(D_{t} > 0\) means assets are overstated or liabilities/equity understated (or cash overstated by a CFS error); \(D_{t} < 0\) means the reverse.
  2. Size: compare \(\lvert D_{t} \rvert\) (and \(\lvert D_{t} \rvert / 2\)) with specific line items; a match points to the culprit.
  3. Pattern over time: - Constant \(D_{t} = D\) for all forecast periods (and the first forecast year): the opening balance sheet does not balance or a historical/opening figure is mislinked. - Growing \(D_{t}\) with a roughly constant increment \(\Delta D_{t} = D_{t} - D_{t-1}\): a BS line moves each year without a corresponding CFS line (missing or double-counted CFS item); the increment equals that line's annual movement. - Starting in a later year: a formula break or inconsistency in that year's column.

Explanation

Because cash is the only plug (c02), any imbalance means a balance sheet movement is not reflected in the CFS, or a CFS item does not correspond to a BS movement, or an opening balance is wrong. The pattern reveals which.

A constant difference cannot arise from a recurring flow error, because a flow error accumulates. It must come from the starting point: the opening BS is out, or the first forecast year links to the wrong opening value (e.g. opening equity picks up a subtotal). Check the historical/opening BS balance first.

A growing difference means an error repeats each period. The increment \(\Delta D_{t}\) is the size of the missing flow in that period. Compare it to annual movements: depreciation, capex, dividends, debt repayments, each working capital change, deferred tax movement. If \(\Delta D_{t}\) equals the dividend each year, dividends are deducted from retained earnings but missing from CFF (or vice versa).

The halving test catches sign errors: if \(\lvert D_{t} \rvert\) equals twice a line item, that item is probably added where it should be subtracted (it is counted with the wrong sign, a swing of 2×).

Systematic debugging sequence:

  1. Confirm the opening BS balances.
  2. Tabulate \(D_{t}\) and \(\Delta D_{t}\).
  3. Compare \(\lvert \Delta D_{t} \rvert\) and \(\lvert \Delta D_{t} \rvert / 2\) to every annual BS movement and CFS line.
  4. For each BS line, check that closing − opening equals the CFS line (with the correct sign) or the IS item that drives it.
  5. Check consistency of formulas across columns (Ctrl+grave accent for formula view; look for breaks).
  6. Check cash: CFS closing cash links to BS cash in the same period.

Excel helper rows, placed under the balance check:

=D50-C50

for the period-on-period change in imbalance, and a movement-reconciliation row per BS line, e.g. for receivables (BS row 12, CFS row 30, where the CFS shows an increase in receivables as a negative number):

=(D12-C12)+'CFS'!D30

expected to be zero in every period.

Illustration

A model shows imbalances (₹ crore): FY26 +6.0, FY27 +13.5, FY28 +21.8, FY29 +31.0. Increments: 6.0, 7.5, 8.3, 9.2, growing with revenue. Candidate lines: dividends (₹5, 6, 7, 8: close but not matching), increase in receivables (₹6.0, 7.5, 8.3, 9.2: exact match). Diagnosis: the receivables increase is on the BS but missing from CFO, so cash (and assets) are overstated by the cumulative receivables build. Sign check: \(D_{t} > 0\) means assets too high, consistent with cash overstated. Fix: add the change in receivables to CFO with a negative sign.

Contrast: an imbalance of −2.4 in every year from FY26 to FY30 points to the opening BS. A negative \(D_{t}\) means liabilities plus equity exceed assets; inspection shows the FY25 provisions of ₹2.4 crore were included twice in the liabilities total (once in current provisions and again in other current liabilities). Removing the duplicate restores balance in every year with no CFS change, which is what the constant pattern predicted.

Connections

Depends on c02 (no plugs, so \(D_{t}\) is informative) and c01 (build order tells you which block to test first). Used in the 15-minute check/debug window of c03 and the integrity dimension of c04. Topic Practice Question Q4 and the Saturday capstone Q6 apply it directly. In practice, the same logic is used in month-end close to diagnose why a trial balance or a cash-flow reconciliation is out.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pattern diagnosis [L2]

Imbalances (₹ crore) for FY26–FY30: +4.0, +4.0, +4.0, +4.0, +4.0. Opening FY25 BS shows total assets ₹640 crore and liabilities plus equity ₹636 crore. Explain the diagnosis, the fix and why no CFS line is implicated.

Q2. Growing imbalance [L2]

Imbalances (₹ crore): FY26 −10.0, FY27 −21.0, FY28 −33.1, FY29 −46.4. Depreciation is ₹10.0, 11.0, 12.1, 13.3; dividends ₹4, 5, 6, 7; increases in payables ₹5.0, 5.5, 6.1, 6.7. Identify the most likely error, explain using sign and increment, and state the fix.

💡 Hint: Compute the year-on-year increments first and compare them with each candidate line, including twice the line where a sign error is possible.

Q3. Error starting mid-forecast [L2]

A model balances in FY26 and FY27, then shows +₹3.2 crore in FY28 and +₹3.2 crore in FY29 and FY30. Explain what this pattern indicates, list the three most likely causes, and describe the Excel steps to locate the error in under five minutes.

Q4. Debug narration [L3]

In a live screen-share, the interviewer deliberately breaks your model and says "it doesn't balance — find it". Script your narrated debugging sequence for the first three minutes, including what you would say and which Excel tools you would use at each step.
