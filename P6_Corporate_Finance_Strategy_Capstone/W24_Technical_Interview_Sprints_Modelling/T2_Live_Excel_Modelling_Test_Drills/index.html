Roadmap topic: Live Excel Modelling Test Drills: Practicing 60-to-90 minute take-home and live screen-share modelling tests under strict time constraints

Overview

Most financial modelling, valuation and FP&A shortlists in India now include a practical test. Valuation KPOs and boutique advisory firms set 60–90 minute take-home builds; GCC FP&A teams run live screen-share sessions where an interviewer watches you build a revenue model or a variance analysis; Big 4 FAAS teams set financial reporting computations (consolidation eliminations, lease schedules, ECL matrices, deferred tax); and many large employers screen first with timed online Excel assessments on platforms such as Mercer Mettl or iMocha. Knowing how to build a 3-statement model is necessary but not sufficient: these tests reward speed, structure, integrity and the ability to explain assumptions under time pressure.

This topic treats the test as a performance skill to be trained. The subtopics move from understanding the formats (s01) and the typical prompts for modelling (s02) and financial reporting (s03), through the data skills (s04) and keyboard speed (s05) that determine how fast you can build, to the prioritisation discipline (s06) that ensures a balancing, checked model is submitted on time, and finally the drill regime (s07) that turns this into habit. The key concepts are the rules you apply in every test: the minimum viable model order (c01), the cash/revolver plug rule (c02), the 10/55/15/10 time split (c03), the scoring rubric (c04) and the imbalance debugging method (c05).

For the target learner, the advantage is technical depth: a CMA with audit experience already understands linkages, Ind AS mechanics and reconciliations. The usual gap is fluency: building from a blank sheet without a template, quickly, while keeping inputs separate and checks visible. Three timed builds a week for the remaining job search will close that gap.

The deliverable of the topic is a personal drill log showing at least six timed builds with rubric scores, and a reusable mental routine (not a file template) for any modelling test.

Related concepts to study

Excel, Power Query and 3-statement linkages

→ Week 9–11: Excel and Power Query techniques; → Week 13–15: 3-statement linkages

The tests in this topic are timed applications of Weeks 9–11 and 13–15. From Weeks 9–11, recall lookup functions, SUMIFS, dynamic arrays, data tables, named ranges and Power Query transforms (promote headers, change type, unpivot, merge, append, group by). From Weeks 13–15, recall the linkage logic: PAT to retained earnings, non-cash add-backs in CFO, working capital movements from BS changes, capex and depreciation through the PPE roll-forward, debt and interest through the debt schedule, and cash from the CFS to the BS. Revise these before drilling; speed comes from not having to think about the mechanics.

PVM and variance formulas

→ Week 19: PVM and variance formulas for FP&A-style tests

FP&A tests often hand you a dataset of budget and actual volumes and prices by product, region or channel and ask for a price-volume-mix analysis with commentary. Recall the Week 19 formulas: price variance at actual quantity; volume variance on total quantity at budget weighted-average price; mix variance at budget prices on the difference between actual quantities and actual total quantity at budget mix. The sum must equal the total variance, and you should build that check into the test. For EBITDA bridges, apply the same logic using budget margin per unit and add cost, FX and opex steps.

Template-free building

Template-free building: practise from a blank sheet to avoid template dependency

Many candidates practise by filling in a downloaded template, which trains recognition, not construction. In a live screen-share test there is no template, and in a take-home test using a recognisable template signals dependence. Practise from a blank sheet: type the timeline, build the inputs block, then each statement, forcing yourself to recall the structure. The goal is a mental template (the order in c01, the layout conventions in s06) that you can reproduce in about 10 minutes for a skeleton. Once you can do this, you can adapt to any prompt's structure rather than forcing the prompt into a template.

Modelling competitions and practice sets

Modelling competitions/practice sets (e.g. FMWC-style cases) for speed

The Financial Modeling World Cup (FMWC) and similar competitions publish case-based challenges: a 2–4 page prompt with data, solved under a time limit, with multiple-choice questions on the outputs. These cases are excellent speed drills because they combine reading under pressure, data manipulation, modelling logic and accuracy checking. Past cases and practice sets from modelling training providers can be used as weekly drills. Treat the score as secondary; the value is in exposure to unfamiliar business logic (e.g. a mining royalty or subscription cohort) and in training you to read the full prompt before building.

Common pitfalls

Over-formatting early

Over-formatting early and running out of time before the model balances

The mistake: spending the first 20 minutes on colours, borders, number formats and headers, then running out of time with an unbalanced model. Why it happens: formatting feels productive and reduces anxiety; candidates fear an ugly model will be judged harshly. Correct treatment: apply only minimal formatting during the build (blue font for inputs, a consistent number format for the whole block), and leave cosmetic formatting until after the model balances and checks pass (s06). How to detect: in your drill recordings, note the minute when the balance check first shows zero; if more than 10 minutes of formatting precede it, you have this habit.

Not reading the brief fully

Not reading the brief fully (missed units, fiscal year ends or required outputs)

The mistake: starting to build after skimming, then discovering that figures were in ₹ lakh not ₹ crore, that the fiscal year ends 31 December (for a GCC parent) not 31 March, or that the required output was equity value per share rather than EV. Why it happens: time pressure pushes candidates to start typing immediately. Correct treatment: spend the first 10 minutes (c03) reading the whole prompt twice, listing required outputs, units, periods and conventions at the top of the inputs sheet. How to detect: before submitting, tick each required output against the list.

Hard-coding prompt numbers in formulas

Hard-coding numbers from the prompt inside formulas instead of an inputs block

The mistake: formulas like =C10*1.12 or =D15*0.25168 instead of referencing growth and tax inputs. Why it happens: it is faster to type in the moment. Correct treatment: every assumption from the prompt goes in an inputs block, labelled and coloured blue; formulas reference those cells. This makes the model auditable, allows sensitivities and is a core rubric item. How to detect: use Go To Special (F5, Special, Constants) on calculation sheets to highlight hard-codes, or search formulas for *1. or *0. patterns; Ctrl+grave accent (the key left of 1) toggles formula view.

Submitting without a note

Submitting without a short note on assumptions and checks

The mistake: emailing the workbook with no explanation. Why it happens: the clock runs out, or candidates think the model speaks for itself. Correct treatment: reserve the last 10 minutes for a short note (on a Notes tab and in the email body): assumptions made where the brief was silent, checks performed and their status, key outputs, and known limitations. How to detect: the drill rubric (c04) scores "clarity of assumptions"; a missing note scores zero on that line.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full 90-minute 3-statement and DCF test [L3]

Build from a blank sheet in 90 minutes. Company: Indian specialty chemicals manufacturer, FY ending 31 March, all figures in ₹ crore. FY25 actuals: revenue 800; COGS 496; employee and other opex 136; depreciation 40; interest 18; tax at 25.168%. FY25 closing BS: net PPE 420; inventory 110; receivables 132; payables 82; cash 30; debt 200; equity 410. Assumptions FY26–FY30: revenue growth 14%, 13%, 12%, 10%, 8%; gross margin 38%; opex 17% of revenue; capex 7% of revenue; depreciation 9% of opening net PPE; DIO 80 days on COGS; DSO 60 days on revenue; DPO 60 days on COGS; debt repayment 25 per year; interest 9% on opening debt; dividend 20% of PAT; minimum cash 25 with a revolver at 10% interest on opening balance. DCF: WACC 12%, terminal growth 5%, end-year discounting, 10 crore shares, valuation date 31 March 2025. Required: IS, BS, CFS, balance check, FCFF, EV, equity value per share, and a 5×5 sensitivity of value per share to WACC (10–14%) and g (4–6%). Submit with a notes tab.

💡 Hint: Build the revolver last, after the model balances with debt repayments and dividends, so that you can isolate any imbalance it introduces.

Q2. Financial reporting test pack [L2]

In 60 minutes compute: (a) Ind AS 116 schedule for a 5-year lease of ₹30 lakh per year payable in arrears, IBR 10%, initial direct costs ₹1.5 lakh, with year-1 journal entries; (b) a provision matrix ECL on receivables of ₹12 crore aged 0–30 days ₹6 crore (0.8%), 31–60 days ₹3 crore (2.5%), 61–120 days ₹2 crore (8%), 121–365 days ₹0.8 crore (30%), over 365 days ₹0.2 crore (100%); (c) the deferred tax on the lease (assume tax deduction on payment basis) and on the ECL (assume tax deduction only on write-off) at 25.168%; (d) a one-paragraph note of assumptions.

Q3. FP&A data test [L2]

You receive a 4,800-row CSV of monthly sales by SKU (40 SKUs), region (4) and channel (2) for FY25 with columns: Month (text like "Apr-24"), SKU code with trailing spaces, Region in mixed case, Units, Net Revenue (₹), and a separate budget file at SKU × month level. In 60 minutes: clean the data in Power Query, build a budget vs actual table by region with SUMIFS, compute PVM at SKU level aggregated to region, and write a 5-line commentary. Specify the M steps you would use and the checks that the PVM sums to the total.

Q4. Debug and present [L3]

A candidate's 3-statement model has an imbalance of ₹4.2 crore in FY26, ₹8.9 crore in FY27 and ₹13.1 crore in FY28. Interest on the revolver is calculated on average balance with iterative calculation enabled. (a) Diagnose the likely causes using the pattern of the difference. (b) Describe the step-by-step debugging sequence with Excel tools. (c) Write the note to the hiring manager if the error cannot be fixed within the remaining 5 minutes.

💡 Hint: Compute the year-on-year change in the imbalance and compare it with candidate line items on the CFS.
