Subtopic note

KPO Research and Boutique Valuation Firms as a Target Segment

Scope: KPO and research/valuation firms (e.g. Acuity Knowledge Partners, Evalueserve, Crisil's analytics arm) and boutique valuation/registered-valuer firms

Why it matters

Knowledge process outsourcing (KPO) and research firms are a major entry point into modelling and valuation careers in India. They provide equity research, credit research, investment banking support, private equity portfolio monitoring and valuation services to global banks, asset managers and corporates, from delivery centres in Bengaluru, Gurugram, Mumbai, Pune and Chennai. Boutique valuation firms and registered-valuer entities, on the other hand, prepare valuations required under Indian law (Companies Act, IBC, SEBI, FEMA, Income-tax). Both segments test 3-statement and DCF skills directly, making your portfolio models highly relevant — but they assess you very differently from FP&A or FAAS.

Core notes

KPO and research firms

Examples: Acuity Knowledge Partners (research, data and analytics for financial services, formerly part of Moody's), Evalueserve (research and analytics across corporate and financial services), Crisil's global research and analytics business (research and risk solutions for global banks, historically including Irevna and Pipal Research), and others such as SG Analytics, Aranca and the research arms of global banks (captive "research centres", e.g., investment bank offices in Mumbai and Bengaluru).

Typical roles and work:

Role Work Skills tested
Equity research associate Maintain company models (3-statement, DCF, SOTP), earnings updates, initiation reports, comps sheets for a global analyst Model building and updating from filings, accounting adjustments (IFRS/US GAAP), writing
Credit research analyst Leverage, coverage and liquidity analysis, covenant tracking, credit memos for banks and private credit funds Ratio analysis, debt schedules, cash flow analysis, credit judgement
Investment banking support Pitch books, trading/transaction comps, merger models, CIMs Valuation multiples, Excel/PowerPoint speed, attention to detail
PE/portfolio analytics Portfolio company monitoring, fund reporting, valuation updates (IPEV guidelines) DCF, multiples, quarterly valuation, data handling
Financial data/ESG research Data extraction, standardisation, ESG scoring Accuracy, financial statement literacy, tools

Selection process: online aptitude and financial accounting test, Excel/modelling test (build a three-statement model or update a model for new quarterly results in 60–120 minutes), written test (summarise an earnings release), and technical/HR interviews. Some use platforms such as Mercer Mettl or HackerEarth.

What they value: speed and accuracy in Excel, ability to read IFRS/US GAAP filings (10-K, 10-Q, annual reports), clear written English, consistency with client templates, and CFA progress (CFA Level I/II is a strong signal in this segment).

Positioning a CMA: emphasise financial statement analysis, costing insight (margin drivers), audit-grade accuracy, and your DCF/3-statement portfolio. Prepare a two-page equity research-style note on an NSE-listed company from your portfolio model.

Boutique valuation and registered-valuer firms

Legal triggers for valuation in India — the source of most boutique work:

Law / regulator Valuation requirement (examples)
Companies Act 2013 Sec 247 valuations by a registered valuer: preferential allotments (Sec 62(1)(c)), mergers and schemes (Sec 230–232), non-cash consideration, buy-backs in specified cases
IBC 2016 Fair value and liquidation value under CIRP (Regulation 27/35 of CIRP Regulations) by two registered valuers per asset class
SEBI ICDR pricing for preferential issues of listed companies where required, open offers (SAST), REIT/InvIT valuations under SEBI regulations
FEMA / RBI Pricing of share issues and transfers involving non-residents using an internationally accepted pricing methodology (by a CA, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant)
Income-tax Act Rule 11UA fair market value for share issues (Sec 56(2)(viib) angel tax — omitted from AY 2025-26 by Finance (No. 2) Act 2024) and transfers (Sec 50CA, 56(2)(x))
Ind AS Fair value for Ind AS 103 PPA, Ind AS 36 impairment, Ind AS 109 unquoted instruments, Ind AS 102 ESOPs — often by valuers supporting auditors

Firms: IBBI-registered valuer entities and boutique advisory firms, valuation practices of global advisory firms in India, and merchant bankers' valuation teams. Small firms run by individual registered valuers in Securities or Financial Assets (SFA) are common in Mumbai, Delhi, Ahmedabad, Kolkata and Bengaluru.

Work: DCF, comparable company and transaction methods; NAV for holding companies; option pricing (Black-Scholes, Monte Carlo) for ESOPs and convertibles; valuation reports compliant with ICAI Valuation Standards (for ICAI RVO members) or IVS/RVO standards; regulatory filings.

What they value: valuation methodology depth, report writing, knowledge of the legal context, comfort defending assumptions to regulators and courts — and, longer term, your own registration as a Registered Valuer (SFA).

Positioning a CMA: ICMAI members can enrol with the ICMAI Registered Valuers Organisation; practising cost accountants are also permitted under FEMA pricing guidelines. Emphasise cost audit experience (asset valuation, cost structures), DCF portfolio and an interest in the RV route.

Comparing the two

Dimension KPO/research Boutique valuation/RV
Clients Global banks, asset managers, PE funds, corporates Indian corporates, promoters, resolution professionals, auditors
GAAP/law focus IFRS/US GAAP filings Indian law, Ind AS fair value, ICAI/IVS standards
Output Models, notes, pitch books, data Valuation reports with regulatory use
Pace High volume, client deadlines across time zones Deal-driven, regulatory deadlines
Credential signal CFA, FMVA IBBI Registered Valuer (SFA), CFA, ICAI/ICMAI valuation courses
Pay Structured; mid-range at entry, rising with domain Varies widely; smaller firms may pay less but offer breadth

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Process-heavy roles: some KPO roles are data extraction rather than analysis — check the role mix during interviews.
  • Client concentration: KPO teams dedicated to one client can be restructured when the client contract changes.
  • Non-compete and confidentiality: KPO contracts restrict sharing client work — anonymise all portfolio items; Indian law (Sec 27, Indian Contract Act) generally voids post-employment non-competes, but confidentiality obligations remain enforceable.
  • Regulatory change: angel tax removal and FEMA amendments change boutique demand — keep current.
  • Independence for valuers: registered valuers must comply with the IBBI Code of Conduct (no conflict, no contingent fee).

Key takeaways

  • KPOs hire for modelling speed, filing literacy (IFRS/US GAAP) and writing; expect timed model tests.
  • Boutique valuation firms work on legally triggered valuations across Companies Act, IBC, SEBI, FEMA, Income-tax and Ind AS.
  • A CMA can access valuation work through FEMA pricing roles and the ICMAI RVO route to Registered Valuer (SFA).
  • Prepare an equity research-style note and a regulatory-style valuation summary from your portfolio.
  • Check role content (analysis vs data) and client concentration before accepting KPO offers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Match triggers to valuation types [L1]

For each situation, state the law/regulation, who may perform the valuation and the likely method(s): (a) a private company issuing shares to a Singapore investor; (b) a merger of two group companies under Sec 230–232; (c) CIRP of a textile company; (d) an ESOP grant by an unlisted start-up for Ind AS 102 accounting.

💡 Hint: Several of these allow or require different professionals; note the eligibility for each.

Q2. KPO model update test [L2]

You are given an NSE-listed company's model with FY25 actuals and a Q1 FY26 results release (revenue ₹4,120 crore, EBITDA ₹610 crore, depreciation ₹140 crore, finance cost ₹55 crore, tax rate 25.168%). Describe step by step how you would update the model in 60 minutes, which checks you would run and how you would summarise the quarter for a global analyst in five bullets.

Q3. Equity research note [L2]

Using your portfolio DCF, write the structure of a two-page initiation-style note: rating logic, price target derivation (DCF and multiple cross-check), three investment arguments, three risks, and the key model assumptions table. Explain how you would adapt it for a KPO interview submission.

Q4. Choosing between offers [L3]

You hold two offers: a credit research analyst role at a Bengaluru KPO serving a US private credit fund (₹11 lakh fixed) and an analyst role at a Mumbai valuation boutique run by a registered valuer (₹8.5 lakh fixed plus 10% variable). Build a decision matrix with weighted criteria (learning, credential path, pay, stability, long-term options) and justify your choice in a two-minute answer.
