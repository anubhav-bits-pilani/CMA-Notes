Subtopic note

Pipeline Tracking: 50 Targets, Conversion Metrics, Follow-Ups

Scope: Pipeline tracking: target list of ~50 employers, stage conversion metrics, follow-up cadence

Why it matters

A job search is a sales pipeline: a list of prospects, stages, conversion rates and follow-ups. Without tracking, candidates overestimate activity, forget follow-ups, lose referral threads and cannot tell whether the problem is the CV, the outreach message or interview performance. An FP&A professional should run their own search the way they would run a forecast — with a dataset, KPIs and variance analysis. Building the tracker in Excel or Power BI also becomes a small portfolio piece and a good interview story about applying FP&A discipline.

Core notes

Building the target list (~50 employers)

Fifty is large enough to absorb normal drop-off and small enough to research properly. Suggested split by segment and tier:

Segment Tier A (dream, strong fit) Tier B (good fit) Tier C (backup, quick wins) Total
Big 4 / mid-tier accounting advisory 3 4 3 10
Corporate FP&A — Indian listed companies 3 5 4 12
GCC FP&A 4 5 4 13
KPO / valuation boutiques 2 3 3 8
Controllership / R2R in GCCs 2 3 2 7
Total 14 20 16 50

Selection criteria: role fit (from the role-fit matrix), city preference, industry alignment (manufacturing, pharma, SaaS, BFSI support), compensation range, growth path, and availability of a warm contact. Sources: Nasscom GCC lists and news, NSE indices (Nifty 500 constituents by sector), Big 4 and KPO career pages, LinkedIn company searches, ICMAI placement drives, recruiter conversations.

Tracker design

One row per employer-role combination, with columns:

Column Purpose
Employer, segment, tier, city Filtering and prioritisation
Role title, job ID, link, date posted Specific opportunity
Channel (referral, alumni, hiring manager, platform, recruiter) Channel analysis
Contact name and relationship Referral tracking
CV variant used Variant performance
Stage and stage date Funnel position
Next action and due date Follow-up discipline
Notes (interview feedback, salary range, notice-period constraints) Learning

Stages: Researched → Contacted → Applied/Referred → Responded → Screening → Technical interview → Final round → Offer → Accepted/Declined/Rejected. Record the date each stage is reached to calculate cycle times.

Excel: Days in stage  =IF([@[Stage Date]]="", "", TODAY()-[@[Stage Date]])
       Overdue flag   =IF(AND([@[Next Action Due]]<>"", [@[Next Action Due]]<TODAY()), "OVERDUE", "")
       Stage count    =COUNTIFS(Tracker[Stage], "Technical interview", Tracker[Segment], "GCC FP&A")

In Power BI, a funnel visual by stage with slicers for segment, channel and CV variant, plus a table of overdue actions, gives a weekly review dashboard.

Stage conversion metrics

\[ \text{Conversion}_{s \to s+1} = \frac{\text{Count reaching stage } s+1}{\text{Count reaching stage } s} \]

Illustrative benchmarks for a focused, referral-led search (they vary by market and seniority): contacted → responded 30–50% for warm outreach, 5–10% for cold applications; responded → interview 40–60%; interview → final round 30–50%; final → offer 30–50%. Track by segment, channel and CV variant to find where the funnel leaks:

Leak location Likely cause Fix
Low response CV/LinkedIn not matching JD keywords, weak outreach message, wrong channel Re-tailor CV variant, use referrals, sharpen three-line message
Response but no interview Screening mismatch on CTC, notice period, location or must-have skills Clarify constraints upfront; target better-fit roles
Interview but no final round Technical gaps, weak structure Mock interviews, gap log from the Interview Bank
Finals but no offers Fit, communication, compensation expectations Practise the 2-minute structure; benchmark CTC

Follow-up cadence

Situation Follow-up timing Message content
Outreach with no reply Day 5–7, then day 14; stop after two follow-ups Short nudge with one new piece of value (a relevant insight or portfolio link)
Referral submitted Day 7 check with referrer; day 14 polite status request Thanks and any update
Application with no response Day 10–14, message hiring manager or recruiter Reference job ID, one-line fit
After interview Thank-you email within 24 hours; status check after the stated timeline plus 2–3 days Specific point from the discussion
Rejection Within a week Thank, request feedback, ask to be considered for future roles

Weekly rhythm: Monday — review dashboard and plan 10–15 new contacts; Tuesday to Thursday — outreach, applications, interviews; Friday — follow-ups and tracker update; Saturday — mock interview and gap log.

Worked example

After four weeks: 50 employers researched; 42 contacted; 18 responded (43%); 9 screening calls (50% of responses); 5 technical interviews (56%); 2 final rounds (40%); 1 offer (50%). By channel: referrals 9 contacted → 6 responded → 3 interviews; hiring-manager messages 15 → 7 → 2; platforms 18 → 5 → 0. Diagnosis: platforms produce no interviews — shift time to referrals and hiring managers; technical-to-final conversion at 40% suggests improving case answers.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Quality vs quantity: hitting 50 contacts a week with generic messages lowers response rates; track response rate alongside activity.
  • Multiple roles at one employer: track each role separately but coordinate messaging to avoid appearing indiscriminate.
  • Long cycle times: Big 4 and GCC processes can take 4–8 weeks; plan pipeline depth accordingly.
  • Data hygiene: update the tracker the same day; stale trackers mislead.
  • Offer timing: align offer dates where possible; an early lower-preference offer may need a polite extension request.
  • Confidentiality: do not store others' personal data beyond what is needed; keep the tracker private.

Key takeaways

  • Build a tiered list of about 50 employers across segments, with warm contacts identified.
  • Track each opportunity by stage, channel, CV variant and next action date.
  • Measure stage conversion rates to locate funnel leaks and fix the right problem.
  • Follow a defined follow-up cadence and a weekly review rhythm.
  • Use Excel/Power BI for the tracker — it doubles as an FP&A discipline story.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the target list [L2]

Create your 50-employer target list using the segment-tier table in this note. For each Tier A employer, record the role, city, reason for fit, warm contact (if any) and the CV variant you will use.

💡 Hint: Start with Tier A and B, then fill Tier C with employers that have frequent, structured hiring.

Q2. Tracker build [L2]

Build the tracker in Excel as a structured table with data validation for stages and channels, an overdue-action flag, and a summary sheet showing counts and conversion rates by stage, segment and channel. Describe how you would extend it into a Power BI funnel dashboard.

Q3. Funnel diagnosis [L2]

After six weeks: 60 contacted, 12 responded, 8 screenings, 6 technical interviews, 1 final round, 0 offers. Compute stage conversions, identify the main leak and draft a two-week remediation plan.

Q4. Follow-up messages [L1]

Draft follow-up messages for: (a) a hiring manager who has not replied in 7 days; (b) a recruiter after a final round with no update beyond the promised date; (c) a rejection where you want feedback and future consideration. Each under 80 words.
