Key concept

The 70–80% Truthful Keyword Match Rule

Concept: Keyword match: mirror 70–80% of a target JD's hard-skill terms where truthful

Statement

For each application, extract the JD's hard-skill terms and compute your match rate:

\[ \text{Match rate} = \frac{\text{Number of JD hard-skill terms present and evidenced in your CV}}{\text{Total JD hard-skill terms}} \times 100\% \]

Target a match rate of 70–80%, using only terms you can evidence. Hard-skill terms are standards (Ind AS 116, IFRS 9), processes (consolidation, R2R, intercompany), techniques (variance analysis, DCF), and tools (SAP S/4HANA, Power Query, DAX). Soft skills ("communication", "stakeholder management") are excluded from the count.

Explanation

The band reflects two constraints. Below roughly 60–70%, ATS ranking and recruiter Boolean searches are likely to miss you. Above about 80–85%, either the JD is an unusually close fit, or you are copying terms you cannot defend; a near-100% mirror also reads as copy-pasted to human reviewers. The 70–80% target captures the core of the JD while leaving honest gaps, which you can address in a cover note or interview ("I have not used BlackLine; I have done the same reconciliations in Excel and SAP").

"Mirror" means using the JD's exact phrasing where it is equivalent to yours: if the JD says "month-end close" and you wrote "monthly book closure", change to "month-end close". If it says "IFRS 16" and your work is Ind AS 116, write "Ind AS 116 (IFRS 16)". Mirroring wording is legitimate; adding unearned skills is not.

"Where truthful" is a hard rule. Each term must be supported by an experience bullet, a project or a certification. Interviewers test JD keywords directly, and Big 4 and GCC interviews often include a technical round built from the JD.

Recompute for each JD. Role-specific CV variants (Topic 5) usually start at 60–70% and reach the target with wording changes and bullet reordering.

Illustration

A GCC "Financial Reporting Analyst" JD contains 15 hard-skill terms: US GAAP, IFRS, Ind AS, consolidation, intercompany reconciliation, month-end close, journal entries, accruals, fixed assets, lease accounting (ASC 842/IFRS 16), SAP S/4HANA, BlackLine, Excel (advanced), Power BI, SOX.

Your master CV evidences: Ind AS, consolidation, month-end close (as "book closure"), journal entries, accruals, fixed assets, Ind AS 116, Excel, Power BI. That is 9 evidenced terms, but "book closure" and "Ind AS 116" do not match the JD wording literally, so the literal match is 7/15 = 47%.

Changes: rename "book closure" to "month-end close" (+1); add "lease accounting (IFRS 16)" beside Ind AS 116 (+1); add "intercompany reconciliation" from your consolidation audit work (+1); add "IFC (SOX-equivalent)" from IFC testing (+1); add "IFRS" in the summary because you audited an IFRS reporting-pack for a foreign subsidiary (+1). New match: 12/15 = 80%. Gaps left honestly: US GAAP, SAP S/4HANA (you only used ECC as an audit user, so write "SAP ECC" and leave S/4HANA as a gap), BlackLine.

Connections

Implements s05 (ATS keyword alignment) at the level of a single application. Depends on c02, because a keyword in a passing bullet is more credible than one in a skills list. Feeds Topic 5's role-fit matrix (gaps by role type) and outreach messages. The gap terms become the learning backlog, e.g. a short US GAAP comparison module for GCC roles (Topic 3 related item on ASC 606/842).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute the match rate [L1]

A JD lists 18 hard-skill terms. Your CV evidences 11 of them, of which 3 are worded differently from the JD. Compute the literal match rate before and after rewording, and state how many further truthful terms you would need to reach 75%.

Q2. Truthful or not [L2]

For each, decide whether adding the JD term is truthful and how you would word it: (a) JD says "Anaplan"; you have built driver-based forecasts in Excel only; (b) JD says "SOX testing"; you have done IFC testing under Sec 143(3)(i); (c) JD says "US GAAP"; you studied ASC 606 vs Ind AS 115 differences in a course but have never applied US GAAP; (d) JD says "Hyperion"; you uploaded data to Hyperion once during an audit.

💡 Hint: Separate "used in work", "studied", and "observed"; only the first supports a skills-section claim.

Q3. Full JD alignment exercise [L3]

Take a real current JD for an FP&A or financial reporting role in an Indian GCC or listed company. Extract the hard-skill terms, compute your current match rate, list the rewording and reordering changes, compute the new match rate and list the honest gaps with a 4-week plan to close the two most important ones.
