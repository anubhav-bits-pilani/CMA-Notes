Key concept

The Four-Part Bullet Test for Finance CVs

Concept: Bullet test: action verb + scope (₹, entities, frequency) + tool/standard + quantified result

Statement

Every experience or project bullet must pass a four-element test:

\[ \text{Bullet} = \text{Action verb} + \text{Scope} + \text{Tool or standard} + \text{Quantified result} \]

where:

  • Action verb is a past-tense (or present for current role) verb describing what you did: reconciled, built, automated, identified, quantified, tested, modelled, led, reduced.
  • Scope is the size or frequency of the work: ₹ amounts (turnover, balance, portfolio value), number of entities, SKUs, controls, invoices, and frequency (monthly, quarterly, year-end).
  • Tool or standard names the instrument: Ind AS 116, Ind AS 109 provision matrix, CARO 2020, Power Query, SUMIFS, SAP FBL3N, DAX.
  • Quantified result is the measurable outcome: ₹ value identified or saved, % or days reduced, error rate, findings count, output delivered.

A bullet missing any element fails and must be rewritten or removed.

Explanation

The test is a stricter, finance-specific version of the XYZ formula (s03). XYZ ensures an outcome and a measure; the bullet test also requires scope and a named tool or standard. Scope matters because the same task signals very different capability at ₹20 crore and at ₹2,000 crore, or for one entity versus twelve. The tool/standard element matters because it is where ATS keywords sit and where interviewers anchor technical questions.

Verb choice signals ownership and level. Weak verbs ("assisted", "helped", "was responsible for", "involved in", "handled") hide what you did. Use them only when accuracy demands, and then pair them with your specific contribution. For auditors, prefer "tested", "identified", "quantified", "evaluated", "reconciled", "reported"; avoid "prepared" or "posted" for client accounts unless you actually did (which raises independence questions).

Exceptions: a scope-only bullet is acceptable where a result is genuinely unmeasurable and the scope itself is the signal (e.g. "Led statutory audit fieldwork for a ₹1,200 crore listed client under Ind AS, managing a team of 3"). Keep these to one per role.

Illustration

Scoring three bullets against the test:

Bullet Verb Scope Tool/standard Result Verdict
"Responsible for lease accounting." No No Partial No Fail
"Prepared lease schedules under Ind AS 116 for clients." Weak No Yes No Fail
"Recomputed Ind AS 116 schedules for 22 leases (₹48 crore liability) at a retail client; corrected IBR from 7% to 9.5%, reducing lease liability by ₹3.1 crore before sign-off." Yes Yes Yes Yes Pass

The passing bullet also invites a natural interview question ("why would a higher IBR reduce the liability?"), which you can answer with the PV logic.

Connections

Applies to every bullet written in s02 (translated audit work), s03 (XYZ bullets) and s04 (project one-liners). The tool/standard element is how s05 and c03 keywords enter bullets with context. In the interview topics (t3, t4), each passing bullet is the seed of an anchored answer, consistent with the week's cross-cutting rule that every answer carries a number, a reference or a portfolio example.

Practice Questions

Q1. Score and fix [L1]

Score these bullets on the four elements and rewrite those that fail: (a) "Handled month-end closing activities"; (b) "Tested 64 key controls across P2P and O2C under the ICAI IFC Guidance Note for a ₹700 crore client, reporting 5 deficiencies, all remediated before year-end"; (c) "Involved in preparation of consolidated financial statements".

Q2. Build bullets from facts [L2]

Facts: you prepared the ECL provision matrix for a ₹62 crore receivables book at a chemicals client using 3 years of ageing data; the computed provision was ₹2.9 crore against ₹1.4 crore booked; management accepted ₹2.6 crore after discussion. Write one bullet for a FAAS CV and one for a credit-control/controllership CV, each passing the test.

💡 Hint: The difference between the variants should be in the verb and result emphasis, not in the facts.

Q3. Verb audit [L2]

List ten weak verbs commonly found in Indian audit CVs and provide a stronger, truthful replacement for each, stating where the replacement would be over-claiming for an auditor.
