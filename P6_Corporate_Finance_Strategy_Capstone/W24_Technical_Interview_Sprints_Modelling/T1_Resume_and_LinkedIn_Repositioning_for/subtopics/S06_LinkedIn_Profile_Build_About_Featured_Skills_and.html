Subtopic note

LinkedIn Profile Build: About, Featured, Skills and Open to Work

Scope: LinkedIn: About section narrative, Featured items, skills ordering, recruiter-only Open to Work, posting short modelling insights

Why it matters

For finance roles in India, LinkedIn is both a search engine for recruiters and a credibility check for hiring managers. Search firms and in-house talent teams at Big 4 firms and GCCs source heavily through LinkedIn Recruiter, filtering by title, skills, location and "open to work" signals. After a CV lands, the hiring manager usually opens your profile to see whether it matches and whether you appear credible. A profile that mirrors your CV positioning, shows work through Featured items and posts short, accurate insights gives you inbound opportunities in addition to applications.

Core notes

About section narrative

The About section (up to 2,600 characters) is read as a short story, not a keyword list. Only the first two to three lines are visible before "see more", so the opening must carry the positioning. A five-part structure works:

  1. Hook and positioning (2 lines): who you are and what you do. "CMA (ICMAI) moving from statutory and cost audit into financial reporting and FP&A. I build Ind AS close packs, 3-statement/DCF models and Power BI reporting that finance teams can actually use."
  2. Background proof (2–3 lines): years, entity sizes, standards. "Three years auditing 12 companies (₹50–800 crore turnover) across Ind AS 115, 116 and 109, cost audit of 4 manufacturers, and IFC testing for listed clients."
  3. What you build now (3–4 lines): the portfolio with one result each.
  4. What you are looking for (1–2 lines): role types and locations. "Open to Financial Reporting, FAAS and FP&A roles in Pune, Bengaluru or Mumbai."
  5. Call to action: "Happy to share my models; message me or email name@example.com."

Write in first person, keep paragraphs short, include the key terms naturally (LinkedIn search indexes About text), and avoid adjectives like "passionate", "dynamic", "results-driven".

Featured items

Featured appears near the top of the profile and is the most visible place for evidence. Choose three to four items:

Item type Example Why
Document (PDF carousel) "3-statement + DCF model of an auto ancillary: 8-slide summary" Recruiters view without downloading Excel
Link GitHub/Drive folder with all six projects One click to the full portfolio
Post Your best-performing modelling insight post Shows communication and engagement
Video 2-minute Power BI dashboard walkthrough Demonstrates tool fluency and explanation

Title each item with the outcome ("Lease close pack: EBITDA +₹6.2 crore, PBT −₹0.9 crore in year 1"), not "My project".

Skills ordering

LinkedIn allows up to 100 skills; the top few are the most visible and can be pinned or reordered (the interface changes periodically, so check the current controls). Recruiter search uses skills as a filter. Order the top five deliberately for the target role family:

  • FAAS/financial reporting: Ind AS, Financial Reporting, IFRS, Consolidation, Technical Accounting.
  • FP&A: Financial Planning & Analysis, Financial Modelling, Variance Analysis, Budgeting, Power BI.
  • Controllership/R2R: Record to Report, Month-end Close, Account Reconciliation, Internal Controls, SAP.

Then add the remaining relevant hard skills (Power Query, DAX, DCF, Valuation, Cost Accounting, Statutory Audit, GST, Excel). Associate each skill with the relevant role or project so endorsements are contextual. Remove irrelevant soft skills ("Teamwork", "Leadership") from the top; they add nothing to search.

Open to Work: recruiter-only

Open to Work has two visibility modes: all LinkedIn members (adds the green "#OpenToWork" photo frame) or recruiters only (visible to users of LinkedIn Recruiter, with LinkedIn stating it takes steps to hide the signal from recruiters at your own company, though this is not guaranteed). For an employed candidate, recruiter-only is the default choice: it surfaces you in recruiter searches without signalling to your employer, clients or network. Fill in job titles (up to five, e.g. "Financial Reporting Analyst", "FP&A Analyst", "Assistant Manager FAAS"), locations (cities plus "remote"), start date (immediately/flexible) and job types. The public frame can suit candidates between jobs, but it can also weaken negotiating position with some recruiters; use it as a deliberate choice.

Posting short modelling insights

Posting builds visibility and gives interviewers something to discuss. Practical rules:

  • Format: 100–200 words, one idea, one visual (a screenshot of a formula, a bridge chart, a table).
  • Cadence: one post every 1–2 weeks; consistency beats volume.
  • Topics: "Ind AS 116 in one chart: why EBITDA rises but PAT falls in year 1"; "An XLOOKUP pattern that replaces nested IFs in a provision matrix"; "PVM bridge for an NSE-listed FMCG company from its investor presentation"; "Three checks I put in every 3-statement model".
  • Accuracy: cite the standard or source; do not post client data; add a "not investment advice" line for listed-company valuations.
  • Engagement: reply to comments within a day; comment thoughtfully on posts by finance leaders in target companies.

Other profile elements

  • Photo and banner: professional photo; banner can show your headline or a dashboard image.
  • Custom URL: linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname-cma.
  • Experience entries: same titles and dates as CV; two to three XYZ bullets each.
  • Licenses & certifications: ICMAI membership with month/year; add others below it.
  • Recommendations: request two from former managers or seniors, guiding them to mention specific outcomes.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Employer social media policy: some audit firms and companies restrict posting about clients or industry views; check before posting.
  • Recruiter-only is not foolproof: recruiters from affiliated entities or third-party agencies may see it; assume the information could reach your employer.
  • Notice periods in About: do not state notice period or CTC publicly; those belong in recruiter conversations and Naukri fields.
  • Switching headline per application: you cannot have role-specific LinkedIn variants; choose the broad positioning and let CV variants specialise.
  • Viral posts with errors: an incorrect technical post can do more harm than none; have a peer review complex Ind AS posts.

Key takeaways

  • The About section is a five-part narrative whose first two lines carry the positioning.
  • Feature three to four outcome-titled items, including a PDF carousel and a portfolio link.
  • Order the top five skills for your primary target role family.
  • Use recruiter-only Open to Work while employed; fill titles, locations and start date.
  • Post short, accurate, sourced modelling or Ind AS insights every 1–2 weeks.
  • Keep every date, title and number identical to the CV.

Practice Questions

Q1. Draft the About section [L1]

Write a LinkedIn About section of 180–220 words for a CMA with 4 years of internal and cost audit in a manufacturing group (6 entities, ₹1,800 crore consolidated revenue) who has built the six curriculum projects and is targeting FP&A roles in Pune and Bengaluru.

Q2. Featured and skills plan [L2]

For a candidate targeting FAAS roles at Big 4 firms, specify (a) four Featured items with outcome titles, (b) the top five skills in order with reasons, and (c) ten additional skills to list.

Q3. Four-post content calendar [L2]

Plan four posts for the next eight weeks, each with a title, a 3-line outline, the visual you would use and the source you would cite. At least one post must use public data from an NSE-listed company.

💡 Hint: Investor presentations and annual reports usually give segment revenue and volume data sufficient for a simple PVM or margin bridge.

Q4. Open to Work decision [L3]

A CMA employed at a mid-size audit firm in Mumbai wants to move to a GCC FP&A role within 4 months. Her firm's partner is active on LinkedIn and connected to several GCC finance leaders. Advise on Open to Work settings, job titles, location choices and other visibility steps, and the risks of each option.
