Subtopic note

Translating Audit and Cost-Audit Work into Close and Controls Language

Scope: Translating audit/cost-audit experience (CARO 2020, cost records rules, IFC testing) into close, controls and analysis language

Why it matters

Hiring managers in FAAS, controllership and FP&A rarely think in audit vocabulary. A controller in a Pune GCC thinks in terms of month-end close calendars, balance sheet reconciliations, control owners and SOX deficiencies. An FP&A head thinks in terms of product margins, cost drivers and variance commentary. Your audit experience contains exactly this content, but described from the reviewer's side. Translating it is not spin: it is restating the same work in the language of the function that owns it, so the reader can see transferability. It is also the main way to counter the "audit is not commercial" bias.

Core notes

The translation principle

Audit work answers "is this right?"; close and FP&A work answers "make it right, on time, and explain it". The translation therefore shifts the verb from verified/checked to reconciled/assessed/identified/quantified/recommended, and adds what happened as a result. It must remain truthful: you tested controls, you did not own them; you identified adjustments, you did not post them in the client's ledger. Use verbs that are accurate for an auditor but meaningful to a preparer: "identified", "quantified", "evaluated", "reconciled", "recommended", "reported".

CARO 2020

The Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 requires the statutory auditor to report on specified matters. Each clause maps to a finance-function capability:

CARO 2020 clause area What you actually did Close/controls/analysis translation
3(i) PPE records, physical verification, title deeds, revaluation Reviewed fixed asset register, physical verification reports, title deeds Fixed asset register reconciliation to GL; capitalisation and tagging controls
3(ii) Inventory physical verification; quarterly returns to banks vs books Compared stock statements filed with banks to books Inventory reconciliation; drawing-power and working-capital reporting to lenders
3(iii) Loans/advances/guarantees to related parties Tested terms, repayment, overdue amounts Related-party and intercompany balance review; ageing of loans
3(vii) Statutory dues deposited; disputed dues Tested GST, TDS, PF, ESI deposits and dispute schedules Statutory compliance controls; tax provisioning and contingent liability schedules
3(ix) Defaults, end-use of loans, short-term funds for long-term use Analysed fund flows Cash-flow and funding analysis; covenant monitoring
3(xi) Fraud, whistle-blower complaints Reviewed fraud reporting Fraud-risk and whistle-blower control awareness
3(xvii) Cash losses; 3(xix) material uncertainty on meeting liabilities Assessed ageing and ratios for going concern Liquidity analysis, ratio analysis and going-concern assessment

Example translation: "Reported on CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) for 5 clients" becomes "Reconciled quarterly stock and receivable statements filed with banks (₹15–120 crore drawing power) to books for 5 manufacturing clients; quantified differences up to ₹3.4 crore and drove explanatory disclosures."

Cost records and cost audit

The Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014 require specified companies to maintain cost records (Form CRA-1) and, above thresholds, to undergo cost audit with the report in Form CRA-3 and filing in CRA-4. Cost audit work is directly FP&A-relevant:

Cost audit task Translation
Product-wise cost sheets, reconciliation of cost and financial records Product costing and margin analysis by SKU; cost-to-financial reconciliation
Abnormal and normal loss analysis, capacity utilisation Yield and capacity variance analysis
Overhead absorption and apportionment by cost centre Cost-centre allocation design; activity-based driver review
Related-party transaction pricing (arm's length) in cost statements Transfer pricing and intercompany margin review
Performance appraisal annexure (CRA-3 Annexure), ratio analysis KPI reporting and benchmarking

Example translation: "Conducted cost audit under CRA-3" becomes "Built product-wise cost sheets for 18 SKUs of a ₹600 crore steel company; reconciled cost and financial profit (difference ₹2.7 crore explained by stock valuation and non-cost items); flagged 4 SKUs with negative contribution."

IFC testing

Under Sec 143(3)(i) of the Companies Act 2013 the statutory auditor reports on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of internal financial controls over financial reporting, using the ICAI Guidance Note on Audit of Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting. Directors separately confirm IFC under Sec 134(5)(e) for listed companies. This is the Indian analogue of SOX 404, and the language maps closely:

IFC task SOX/controllership translation
Walkthroughs of process (P2P, O2C, R2R) Process documentation and risk-control matrices (RCMs)
Test of design (ToD) and test of operating effectiveness (ToE) Control design and operating-effectiveness testing
Deficiency evaluation (deficiency, significant deficiency, material weakness) Deficiency remediation tracking and control-owner follow-up
ITGC review (access, change management) ERP access and segregation-of-duties review

Example translation: "Performed IFC testing" becomes "Tested 85 key controls across O2C, P2P and R2R for 3 listed clients (₹400–1,500 crore revenue); identified 6 deficiencies including SoD conflicts in SAP; tracked remediation to closure before year-end sign-off."

Analysis language for FP&A

Audit analytical procedures (ratio analysis, trend analysis, expectation vs actual) are variance analysis by another name. "Performed analytical review of revenue and expenses" becomes "Analysed month-on-month gross margin movement (34% to 29%) for a ₹250 crore FMCG client; attributed 3.2 points to raw material price and 1.8 points to mix; supported management's inventory valuation review."

A step-by-step translation routine

  1. List every engagement: entity type, size (turnover), sector, period, your role.
  2. For each, list the tasks in audit language.
  3. Map each task to the target function's vocabulary using the tables above.
  4. Add scope (₹, number of entities, frequency) and a result (adjustment, finding, time saved, disclosure improved).
  5. Check truthfulness: could the engagement partner confirm this exact sentence?

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Confidentiality: name sectors and size bands, not clients, unless the client is public and the work is publicly known. Audit confidentiality obligations under the ICAI/ICMAI codes of ethics continue after you leave.
  • Adjustments you proposed: say "identified" or "proposed", not "posted". Saying you posted entries implies management responsibility, which an auditor must not take.
  • Over-claiming ownership: "Designed controls" is false for a tester; "evaluated design of controls" is accurate.
  • Cost audit in non-manufacturing roles: for a SaaS FP&A role, emphasise cost allocation and margin analysis; drop industry-specific terms like "conversion cost per tonne".
  • Junior roles: if you were an article or trainee, write the scope you personally handled, not the engagement's total scope.

Key takeaways

  • Translate verbs from "verified/checked" to "reconciled/quantified/identified/recommended", keeping them truthful for an auditor.
  • CARO 2020 clauses map to fixed-asset, inventory, related-party, statutory-dues and liquidity controls.
  • Cost audit under the 2014 Rules maps directly to product costing, margin and variance analysis.
  • IFC testing under Sec 143(3)(i) is the Indian SOX analogue; use RCM, ToD/ToE and deficiency language.
  • Always add scope and a result to the translated line.
  • Never claim management ownership of entries or control design you only audited.

Practice Questions

Q1. CARO clause translation [L1]

Translate the following into close/controls language, adding plausible scope: (a) "Reported on CARO 2020 clause 3(vii)(a) regarding deposit of undisputed statutory dues"; (b) "Verified title deeds of immovable property under clause 3(i)(c)"; (c) "Examined loans given to related parties under clause 3(iii)".

Q2. Cost audit to FP&A bullet [L2]

You audited cost records of a ₹420 crore auto components company with 3 plants and 26 SKUs. The cost-financial reconciliation showed a difference of ₹1.9 crore (₹1.1 crore stock valuation difference, ₹0.8 crore non-cost income). You identified that 5 SKUs had contribution margin below 8% against a company average of 22%. Write two CV bullets for an FP&A Analyst role and one for a cost controller role.

Q3. IFC to SOX interview answer [L2]

A US-headquartered GCC interviewer asks: "You haven't worked on SOX. Why should I believe you can support our SOX 404 testing?" Prepare a 90-second answer that maps your IFC experience (Sec 143(3)(i), ICAI Guidance Note) to SOX terminology, with one specific example of a deficiency you found.

💡 Hint: Structure the answer as framework equivalence, process coverage, and a concrete deficiency with its remediation.

Q4. Truthfulness review [L2]

Review these bullets written by a statutory auditor and mark each as accurate, over-claimed or under-claimed, rewriting where needed: (a) "Owned month-end close for 6 clients"; (b) "Designed revenue recognition controls under Ind AS 115"; (c) "Checked vouchers"; (d) "Quantified ₹4.6 crore of unrecorded liabilities through cut-off testing, adopted by management as year-end provisions".
