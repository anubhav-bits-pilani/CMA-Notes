Subtopic note

Accounting for Deal Fees: Ind AS 103, Ind AS 109 and Ind AS 32

Scope: Accounting treatment of fees: Ind AS 103 (expense), Ind AS 109 (debt costs in EIR), Ind AS 32 (equity issue costs to equity)

Why it matters

A typical Indian deal generates three different kinds of cost that look alike in a Sources & Uses table but are accounted for in three different ways. Getting this right decides the Year 1 PAT (and so whether the deal is reported as accretive), the carrying amount and interest expense of the acquisition debt over its life, and the acquirer's equity. Auditors check this closely, because capitalising advisory fees into goodwill is a common and material error.

Core notes

The three buckets

Cost Standard Treatment Where it lands
Acquisition-related costs: advisory, legal, accounting, valuation, DD, finder's fees, stamp duty on acquisition, general admin costs Ind AS 103.53 Expensed as incurred, when services are received P&L (often exceptional item)
Costs to issue debt: arrangement fees, underwriting, lender legal, rating fee for the issue, stamp duty on debentures Ind AS 109 (transaction costs, para 5.1.1 and B5.4.8) Deducted from initial carrying amount; amortised through the EIR Finance cost over the life of the debt
Costs to issue equity: merchant banker fees for the issue, legal, listing fees, stamp duty on issue Ind AS 32.37 Deducted from equity, net of related income tax Securities premium or other equity

Ind AS 103.53 explicitly excludes costs to issue debt or equity securities from the expense rule. Those follow Ind AS 32 and Ind AS 109.

Acquisition-related costs (Ind AS 103.53)

The rationale is that these costs are not part of the fair-value exchange between buyer and seller; they are payments for services. Consequences:

  • Not added to goodwill. Under old Indian GAAP (AS 14), some entities capitalised such costs. Ind AS does not allow this.
  • Timing: expensed when the service is received, which may be before closing. Costs incurred in the year before closing hit that year's P&L.
  • Allocation for mixed engagements: if an investment bank's fee covers both M&A advice and debt arranging, split it on a reasonable basis (for example the fee schedule in the engagement letter). Only the debt-arranging part goes into the EIR.
  • Costs paid by the seller on behalf of the buyer: if the acquirer reimburses the seller's costs as part of the price, that part is not consideration. It is an acquisition cost, expensed (Ind AS 103.51–52 on separate transactions).
  • Tax: acquisition costs on a share purchase are generally part of the cost of acquisition of shares (capital in nature), so not deductible. No DTA arises on the expense in the consolidated P&L. Merger costs may qualify under Sec 35DD.
  • Separate financial statements: in the acquirer's standalone accounts, the investment in subsidiary is carried at cost under Ind AS 27. Whether directly attributable costs can be included in the cost of investment is a policy question (Ind AS 27 does not define cost). Many Indian companies capitalise them in the standalone investment while expensing them in the consolidated P&L. Be clear which financial statements you are modelling.

Debt transaction costs (Ind AS 109)

Financial liabilities at amortised cost are initially recognised at fair value minus directly attributable transaction costs. The EIR is the rate that discounts the contractual cash flows to that net amount.

\[ \text{Net proceeds} = L - F = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + \text{EIR})^t} \]

where \(L\) is the principal, \(F\) is the fees and \(CF_t\) is interest and principal paid at time \(t\). Interest expense each period = opening amortised cost × EIR. The fee is therefore recognised gradually as extra interest.

Illustration: ₹2,000 crore 5-year bullet term loan at 9% annual interest, fees ₹30 crore (1.5%). Net proceeds ₹1,970 crore. EIR solves \(1{,}970 = \sum 180/(1+r)^t + 2{,}000/(1+r)^5\), giving about 9.39%.

Year Opening amortised cost Interest at EIR 9.39% Cash coupon Closing
1 1,970.0 185.0 180.0 1,975.0
2 1,975.0 185.4 180.0 1,980.4
3 1,980.4 186.0 180.0 1,986.4
4 1,986.4 186.5 180.0 1,992.9
5 1,992.9 187.1 180.0 2,000.0 (repaid)

(Rounded to one decimal; the EIR of 9.3895% brings the closing balance to exactly 2,000.) Straight-line amortisation of ₹6 crore a year is a common model simplification (t4 c06), and the difference is small for bullet loans.

Prepayment and modification: if the loan is repaid early, unamortised fees are written off to P&L. If it is modified (for example after a refinancing with the same lender), test for substantial modification (the 10% test in Ind AS 109 B3.3.6). A substantial modification is an extinguishment, so unamortised fees are written off. A non-substantial one adjusts the carrying amount.

Commitment fees on facilities not expected to be drawn are expensed over the commitment period. If drawdown is probable, they are deferred and included in the EIR on drawdown.

Equity issue costs (Ind AS 32.37)

Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new shares (QIP, preferential issue or shares issued in a scheme) are deducted from equity, net of tax. Costs related to listing existing shares, or joint costs relating to both a listing and an issue, are allocated on a rational basis (Ind AS 32.38). Under Sec 52 of the Companies Act, securities premium can be used to write off expenses of an issue.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Success fees: contingent on closing. Recognise when the obligation arises (on completion). They are still expensed in P&L, not capitalised.
  • Fees in an abandoned deal: always expensed. In a model, they are a sunk cost.
  • Bridge loans: fees on a bridge loan expected to be refinanced within months are amortised over the expected life, which accelerates the expense.
  • Mixed-use fees: an arranger's fee for a debt facility that also covers M&A advice should be split. Auditors will challenge a full allocation to the EIR intended to avoid the P&L hit.
  • Tax-deductibility of debt fees: generally deductible over the loan tenor under the Income-tax Act, which can create small timing differences.
  • NCLT scheme costs: expensed in an acquisition. In a common-control merger under Appendix C, they are also expensed.

Key takeaways

  • Advisory, legal, DD and stamp-duty costs of an acquisition are expensed under Ind AS 103.53, not capitalised into goodwill.
  • Debt fees reduce the loan's initial carrying amount and are recognised over its life through the EIR (Ind AS 109).
  • Equity issue costs are deducted from equity, net of tax (Ind AS 32.37).
  • Split mixed engagement fees by their nature.
  • In the model, Year 1 PAT carries the acquisition costs (often adjusted out), and interest expense includes fee amortisation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying deal costs [L1]

Classify and state the accounting for: ₹18 crore M&A advisory success fee; ₹6 crore legal fees for the SPA; ₹4 crore legal fees for the loan agreement; ₹22 crore arrangement fee on a ₹2,200 crore loan; ₹9 crore merchant banker fee for a QIP; ₹0.4 crore stamp duty on the share transfer; ₹3 crore internal M&A team salaries.

Q2. EIR schedule [L2]

An NCD of ₹1,500 crore is issued at par with a 9.5% annual coupon, 4-year bullet, issue costs of ₹18 crore and a redemption premium of 2% at maturity. Compute the EIR and prepare the amortised cost schedule. Compare annual interest expense with a straight-line approach.

💡 Hint: The redemption premium is part of the contractual cash flows, not a fee.

Q3. Early repayment [L2]

The ₹2,000 crore loan in the core notes (fees ₹30 crore, EIR 9.39%) is repaid at the end of Year 2 from the proceeds of an NCD issue. Compute the P&L charge on repayment and show the journal entry.

Q4. Auditor's challenge [L3]

The acquirer's draft PPA includes ₹42 crore of advisory and DD fees in consideration transferred, increasing goodwill. The CFO argues that the fees were "necessary to acquire the business". Write the auditor's response, including the correct entries and the impact on consolidated PAT and EPS.
