Subtopic note

Stamp Duty and Transfer Costs in Indian Deals

Scope: Stamp duty and transfer costs: 0.015% on delivery-based share transfers; state stamp duty on NCLT-sanctioned scheme orders

Why it matters

Stamp duty is a real cash cost that belongs in Uses and that can change the choice of structure. On a share purchase it is small (0.015%). On a merger scheme it can be large, because several states treat the NCLT order as a conveyance and levy duty on the value of shares issued or property transferred. On a slump sale or asset deal, conveyance duty on land and buildings at 5–7% plus registration charges can run to hundreds of crores. Because Ind AS 103 requires transaction costs to be expensed, stamp duty on a share purchase hits the acquirer's P&L in the year of the deal.

Core notes

Legal framework

Stamp duty is a concurrent subject: Parliament fixes rates for certain instruments (including transfer and issue of securities, under the Indian Stamp Act 1899 as amended by the Finance Act 2019), and states fix rates for other instruments (conveyance, including NCLT orders treated as conveyance, under state stamp Acts such as the Maharashtra Stamp Act 1958 or the Karnataka Stamp Act 1957).

Securities: the Finance Act 2019 regime (from 1 July 2020)

Transaction Rate Collected by Paid by
Transfer of securities, delivery basis (on or off exchange) 0.015% of consideration (or market value) Stock exchanges / clearing corporations for exchange trades; depositories for off-market transfers Buyer
Transfer of securities, non-delivery (intraday) 0.003% Stock exchanges Buyer
Issue of securities (shares, debentures) 0.005% Depositories or the issuer's state (for physical) Issuer
Transfer of debentures 0.0001% Depositories / exchanges Buyer
Derivatives Specified low rates Stock exchanges Buyer

So for an SPA purchase of ₹2,700 crore of demat shares, stamp duty is ₹2,700 × 0.015% = ₹0.405 crore (₹40.5 lakh). Open-offer shares tendered through the exchange mechanism also attract 0.015%.

Physical share transfers and unlisted company shares not in demat: the Finance Act 2019 regime still applies at 0.015% on transfer, collected through the depository if dematerialised. Many unlisted public and private companies are now required to dematerialise shares (Rule 9A and 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules).

Schemes of arrangement: state duty on NCLT orders

The Supreme Court (Hindustan Lever v State of Maharashtra, 2003) held that a court order sanctioning an amalgamation is an "instrument" through which property is transferred and can be charged with stamp duty by the states. The Companies Act 2013 shifted sanction to NCLT, but the principle carries over.

  • Maharashtra (Article 25(da) of the Maharashtra Stamp Act Schedule I): duty on amalgamation or demerger orders is computed with reference to the aggregate market value of shares issued or allotted plus any other consideration, subject to a ceiling linked to the market value of immovable property in the state and an overall cap. Check the current rate and caps.
  • Other states (Gujarat, Karnataka, West Bengal, Delhi and others) have their own entries, often at a percentage of the value of shares allotted or of immovable property in that state, with caps.
  • Multiple states: if the transferor has property in several states, duty may be claimed by each. Credit for duty paid in one state may be available in some cases. This is a planning point for multi-state businesses.
  • Merger of WOS into parent: where no shares are issued (cancellation of cross-holding), duty is usually computed on the value of the immovable property transferred, and some states offer concessions for intra-group schemes.

Slump sale and asset deals

A business transfer agreement for an undertaking attracts duty on each component that is conveyed:

  • Immovable property: conveyance duty at state rates (typically 5–7% of market value, plus registration fee around 1%, and in some states a metro cess or surcharge). This is often the single largest transfer cost.
  • Movables: duty on the agreement, or delivery by hand to avoid it.
  • Assignment of intangibles, contracts and receivables: separate instruments and duty.
  • Leases: duty on fresh leases or assignment.

GST: transfer of a business as a going concern is exempt. In an itemised asset deal, GST applies to movable goods, so the buyer must check input tax credit eligibility.

Other transfer costs

  • Registration fees on conveyances.
  • ROC filing fees for allotment, increase in authorised capital (fees plus stamp duty on increased authorised capital in some states), and for scheme orders.
  • SEBI and stock-exchange fees for open offers, schemes (processing fees on draft schemes) and listing of new shares.
  • Depository and registrar charges.
  • Transfer of licences and approvals (factory licence, pollution consents, FSSAI, drug licences).

Model treatment

Cost Where in Uses Accounting
Stamp duty on share purchase (0.015%) Transaction fees Expensed (Ind AS 103.53)
Stamp duty on issue of new shares (0.005%) Equity issue costs Deducted from equity (Ind AS 32.37)
Stamp duty on NCLT order for an acquisition Transaction fees Expensed under Ind AS 103
Stamp duty on order for a common-control merger Transaction fees Expensed (Appendix C has no special rule)
Conveyance duty on assets bought (asset deal outside a business combination) Asset cost Capitalised to the asset (Ind AS 16 directly attributable cost)
Conveyance duty in a slump sale accounted as a business combination Transaction fees Expensed

The last two rows show a distinction worth noting: if the purchase is a business combination, transaction costs are expensed even if they relate to land. If it is an asset acquisition, they are capitalised.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Valuation for duty: market value as assessed by the state can exceed the deal value. The stamp authority may adjudicate and demand more.
  • Delay penalties: duty must be paid within the state's time limit after the NCLT order, or penalties apply (often a percentage per month).
  • Inbound cross-border mergers: the order may need to be stamped in the state where the Indian company's registered office is, even if shares issued are to non-residents.
  • Tax deductibility: stamp duty on share purchases is part of the cost of acquisition for capital gains, not a deductible business expense. Stamp duty on a merger may be claimed under Sec 35DD (amortised over 5 years) as amalgamation expenditure.
  • Structure arbitrage: buying shares of a property-owning company avoids conveyance duty, but some states levy duty on transfers of controlling interest in companies owning property in the state. Check state law.

Key takeaways

  • Transfer of securities on delivery basis attracts 0.015% stamp duty under the Finance Act 2019 regime, collected by exchanges or depositories. Issue of securities attracts 0.005%.
  • NCLT orders sanctioning mergers and demergers are instruments chargeable by states, often by reference to the value of shares allotted or property transferred, with caps.
  • Slump sales and asset deals bear heavy conveyance duty and registration on immovable property.
  • In a business combination, stamp duty is expensed. In an asset acquisition, it is capitalised. Duty on issue of shares is an equity issue cost.
  • Model stamp duty explicitly in Uses and in the Year 1 P&L.

Practice Questions

Q1. Share deal stamp duty [L1]

An acquirer buys 4.2 crore shares of a listed target at ₹760 under an SPA (off-market, demat), and 2.6 crore shares at ₹780 in the open offer through the exchange mechanism. It issues 1.1 crore new shares at ₹1,450 under a QIP to fund part of the deal. Compute each stamp duty amount and state the accounting treatment.

Q2. Structure comparison [L2]

A business with land and buildings in Maharashtra (market value ₹300 crore) and other net assets of ₹500 crore can be bought by (a) buying all shares of the company for ₹800 crore, or (b) a slump sale for ₹800 crore. Assume conveyance duty of 6% plus 1% registration on immovable property in (b), and 0.015% on the share transfer in (a). Compare transfer costs and the P&L treatment in each case.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the slump sale is a business combination before deciding on expensing.

Q3. Multi-state merger [L2]

The transferor company has immovable property in Maharashtra (₹120 crore), Gujarat (₹80 crore) and Karnataka (₹50 crore). The scheme issues shares worth ₹900 crore. Explain how stamp duty would be assessed, the risk of duplication and how you would estimate a range for the model.

Q4. Tax treatment of stamp duty [L2]

Explain the income-tax treatment of stamp duty in (a) a share purchase, (b) an amalgamation under Sec 232, and (c) a slump sale (in the buyer's hands), and the effect on the deferred tax computation in the PPA.
