Subtopic note

Equity Value vs Enterprise Value and Fully Diluted Shares

Scope: Equity value vs enterprise value bridge and fully diluted shares (treasury stock method for ESOPs)

Why it matters

A deal is negotiated in one currency (often EV, or a price per share) and paid in another (cash to shareholders plus debt repaid). Converting between them is where many funding models go wrong. A price per share applied to basic instead of diluted shares understates the cheque. An EV quoted without deducting debt overstates what shareholders receive. The same bridge is used in DCF valuation (Week 16), in transaction multiples (EV/EBITDA paid) and in the PPA (consideration transferred).

Core notes

The bridge

\[ \text{EV} = \text{Equity value} + \text{Debt} + \text{Preference capital} + \text{NCI} + \text{Debt-like items} - \text{Cash and equivalents} - \text{Non-operating assets} \]

and in reverse:

\[ \text{Equity value} = \text{EV} - \text{Net debt} - \text{Preference} - \text{NCI} - \text{Debt-like items} + \text{Non-operating assets} \]

Each item is a claim on (or asset outside) the operating business that EBITDA generates:

Bridge item Why it is in the bridge Measurement notes
Debt (borrowings, NCDs, CC/OD) Claim of lenders on operating cash flows Book value usually; market value for listed bonds if materially different
Lease liabilities Claim if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 Include only if EBITDA excludes rent
Preference shares Senior to equity Redemption value plus arrears of dividend
NCI in target's subsidiaries Consolidated EBITDA includes 100% of subsidiaries Market value if listed; else book value or multiple-based
Debt-like items Gratuity deficits, overdue statutory dues, deferred consideration, probable tax demands DD-based (t1 s05)
Cash Surplus cash belongs to shareholders Exclude trapped and minimum operating cash
Non-operating assets Investments in associates, surplus land Fair value, after tax if sold

Consistency rule: the EV multiple and the bridge must use the same perimeter. If EV/EBITDA is computed on consolidated EBITDA (100% of subsidiaries), deduct NCI. If associates' profits are excluded from EBITDA, add their value back.

Fully diluted shares

The purchase price must cover all equity claims that will exist at closing. These include:

  • Basic shares outstanding (excluding treasury shares held by an ESOP trust, if any).
  • In-the-money ESOPs, including unvested options that vest on change of control (check the scheme terms).
  • Warrants and convertible instruments (CCPS, FCCBs, convertible debentures) that are in the money at the offer price.
  • RSUs and performance shares, usually counted in full.

Treasury stock method (TSM)

The TSM assumes that options are exercised and the exercise proceeds are used to buy back shares at the current (or offer) price:

\[ \text{Net new shares} = N_{\text{opt}} - \frac{N_{\text{opt}} \times K}{P} = N_{\text{opt}} \times \left(1 - \frac{K}{P}\right) \quad \text{if } P > K \]

where \(N_{\text{opt}}\) is the number of options, \(K\) is the exercise price and \(P\) is the share or offer price. Out-of-the-money options (\(K \geq P\)) add nothing.

In a deal, use the offer price for \(P\), since that is the price at which equity is acquired. Because the diluted share count depends on price and the price per share depends on diluted shares when an equity value is fixed, the calculation is circular. Solve by iteration or algebra (c02).

Convertibles: use the if-converted method. If the conversion value exceeds the redemption value at the offer price, count the conversion shares and remove the instrument from debt. Otherwise, treat it as debt.

Tranches: options usually come in tranches with different strikes. Apply TSM tranche by tranche.

Illustration

Target: 50 crore basic shares; ESOP tranches: 1.2 crore options at ₹180, 0.8 crore at ₹320, 0.5 crore at ₹480. Offer price ₹400. Debt ₹2,200 crore; cash ₹600 crore (₹100 crore trapped); lease liabilities ₹300 crore (EBITDA post-Ind AS 116); NCI in a subsidiary ₹250 crore; unfunded gratuity ₹50 crore.

Tranche Options (crore) Strike In the money? Net new shares (crore)
1 1.2 180 Yes 1.2 × (1 − 180/400) = 0.66
2 0.8 320 Yes 0.8 × (1 − 320/400) = 0.16
3 0.5 480 No 0
Total 0.82

Fully diluted shares = 50.82 crore; equity purchase price = 50.82 × 400 = ₹20,328 crore.

Implied EV = 20,328 + 2,200 + 300 + 250 + 50 − (600 − 100) = ₹22,628 crore.

Using basic shares would understate the equity price by 0.82 × 400 = ₹328 crore.

Excel implementation

Net_new_shares   =SUMPRODUCT((Strike_range<Offer_price)*Options_range*(1-Strike_range/Offer_price))
FD_shares        =Basic_shares + Net_new_shares + Convertible_shares*(Conv_value>Redemption)
Equity_price     =FD_shares * Offer_price
EV               =Equity_price + Debt + Leases + Pref + NCI + DebtLike - (Cash - Trapped_cash)

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • ESOP trust shares: if an ESOP trust already holds shares to satisfy options, those shares are outstanding but the options do not create new shares. Avoid double counting.
  • Cash-settled SARs: treat them as a debt-like cash payment at closing, not as shares.
  • Unvested options: if they do not accelerate on change of control and will be replaced by acquirer options, the replacement awards are partly consideration and partly post-combination expense (Ind AS 103 B56–B62).
  • Market vs book values: use market value for listed NCI and listed bonds when they differ materially.
  • Circularity: TSM at the offer price with a fixed equity value is circular. A data-table-friendly model solves it with algebra rather than iterative calculation.

Key takeaways

  • EV = equity value + debt + preference + NCI + debt-like items − free cash − non-operating assets, using one consistent perimeter.
  • Lease liabilities belong in the bridge only if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116.
  • The price must cover fully diluted shares: basic + in-the-money options (TSM) + convertibles (if-converted) + RSUs.
  • TSM: net new shares = options × (1 − strike/price), tranche by tranche, at the offer price.
  • Basic-share pricing understates the purchase price and the open-offer size.

Q1. TSM with tranches [L1]

Basic shares 8 crore. Options: 0.3 crore at ₹150, 0.25 crore at ₹260, 0.2 crore at ₹410. Offer price ₹300. Compute fully diluted shares and the equity purchase price.

Q2. Full EV bridge [L2]

Equity price ₹6,500 crore. Borrowings ₹1,800 crore; CCPS (to be redeemed) ₹200 crore; cash ₹900 crore, of which ₹150 crore is margin money; lease liabilities ₹420 crore; EBITDA is pre-Ind AS 116; NCI ₹180 crore; 26% stake in an associate worth ₹350 crore whose profits are excluded from EBITDA; disputed tax demand ₹80 crore (probable). Compute EV and EV/EBITDA if EBITDA is ₹960 crore.

💡 Hint: Decide first whether the lease liability belongs in the bridge given the EBITDA basis.

Q3. Circular TSM at a fixed equity value [L2]

The acquirer agrees a fixed equity value of ₹4,000 crore for a target with 10 crore basic shares and 1 crore options at a strike of ₹200. Derive the offer price per share algebraically and verify it.

Q4. Convertible in the target's capital [L2]

The target has ₹500 crore of compulsorily convertible debentures convertible at ₹250 per share, and ₹300 crore of optionally convertible debentures convertible at ₹450 per share or redeemable at par. Offer price ₹380. Determine how each instrument enters the diluted share count or the debt bridge.
