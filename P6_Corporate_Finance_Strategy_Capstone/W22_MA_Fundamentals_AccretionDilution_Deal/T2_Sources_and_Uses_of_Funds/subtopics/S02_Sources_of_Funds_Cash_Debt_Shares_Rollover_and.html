Subtopic note

Sources of Funds: Cash, Debt, Shares, Rollover and Earn-outs

Scope: Sources: acquirer balance-sheet cash, new term loans/NCDs/ECBs, new shares issued, seller rollover, vendor financing, earn-outs

Why it matters

The funding mix determines EPS accretion (t4), credit metrics, the rating and the risk the acquirer takes. Cash is cheap in EPS terms (only foregone interest is lost) but depletes liquidity. Debt adds interest and covenants. New shares dilute ownership and EPS. Seller rollover, vendor financing and earn-outs shift part of the risk to the seller and bridge valuation gaps. In India the choice is shaped by regulation: restrictions on banks financing share acquisitions, ECB end-use rules, ICDR pricing for preferential issues and FEMA limits on deferred consideration for cross-border deals.

Core notes

Layout

ECB (where permitted) Interest + hedging cost × (1 − t), withholding tax

Vendor financing / deferred consideration Interest or unwinding of discount
Earn-out (contingent) Fair-value liability, remeasured through P&L
Total sources Must equal total uses

Acquirer balance-sheet cash

Only excess cash above the operating minimum is available (c06). Include liquid investments (debt mutual funds, FDs) if they can be redeemed without penalty. Remember tax on redemption gains, and that cash in overseas subsidiaries may be trapped. The cost in the EPS model is the after-tax interest income lost.

New debt

  • Term loans: from banks (subject to RBI rules on acquisition finance, s05) or NBFCs. Usually floating rate linked to an external benchmark or MCLR, secured, with amortisation and maintenance covenants (Net Debt/EBITDA, DSCR, interest cover).
  • NCDs: listed or privately placed under SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations 2021. Often issued by an acquisition holding company with share pledges, or by the acquirer on balance sheet. Fixed coupon, bullet or amortising.
  • ECBs: foreign-currency or rupee-denominated borrowing under the RBI ECB framework. Historically, using ECB proceeds for equity investment in Indian companies or for acquiring shares of Indian companies was on the negative list. ECBs are used for overseas acquisitions (ODI). RBI proposed a liberalised ECB framework in 2025, so check the current end-use list. Include hedging cost and withholding tax.

In the model, new debt is usually the plug (c01): fix cash and equity, and debt balances the table.

New shares issued

Shares can be issued to the target's shareholders as consideration (share swap via a preferential issue or a scheme) or to new investors for cash (QIP, rights issue, preferential issue). The share count rises, diluting EPS. Pricing must respect ICDR floors (c04). In a scheme, shares are issued at the swap ratio fixed by the registered valuer (s04).

Seller rollover

The seller (often the founder or a PE fund) keeps or reinvests a stake. Two forms:

  • Retains shares in the target: the acquirer buys less than 100%, so the retained stake becomes NCI. The SHA gives put/call options. A put option held by the NCI may need to be recognised as a financial liability at the present value of the redemption amount (Ind AS 32.23).
  • Receives acquirer shares: part of the consideration is in acquirer shares (a partial share swap).

Rollover reduces upfront cash needs and aligns the seller's incentives with integration.

Vendor financing

The seller defers part of the price as a loan to the buyer (a vendor note) or as deferred consideration at a fixed date. It is debt-like: a liability measured at amortised cost, with interest or unwinding of discount in finance cost. For cross-border deals, FEMA NDI Rules limit deferred consideration to 25% of total consideration, paid within 18 months.

Earn-outs

Part of the price is payable only if targets (revenue, EBITDA, product approval) are met. Under Ind AS 103, earn-outs are contingent consideration: measured at acquisition-date fair value and included in consideration transferred. If classified as a liability, they are remeasured at fair value through P&L until settled (t3 c05). They are not a cash source at closing, but they reduce the cash required upfront. In Sources & Uses, show them as "deferred / contingent consideration" in Sources if the Uses include the full headline price. Payments linked to the seller's continued employment are remuneration, not consideration.

Illustration

Total uses ₹5,000 crore. Acquirer cash ₹1,900 crore, minimum cash ₹400 crore. Board wants new shares to fund no more than ₹1,000 crore. The seller agrees to roll over 10% of the equity price of ₹4,000 crore into acquirer shares, and to an earn-out with fair value ₹150 crore.

Sources ₹ crore
Excess acquirer cash (1,900 − 400) 1,500
New shares to market (QIP) 1,000
Seller rollover into acquirer shares (10% × 4,000) 400
Earn-out (fair value) 150
New term loan (plug) 1,950
Total sources 5,000

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Earn-out classification: equity-settled earn-outs with a fixed number of shares can be equity (Ind AS 32 fixed-for-fixed). Others are liabilities, with P&L volatility.
  • Holding-company debt: debt raised at an acquisition SPV is structurally subordinated to target creditors. Lenders may require a merger of the SPV into the target later (debt pushdown), which needs NCLT approval.
  • Target cash as a source: in a share deal, surplus target cash can be used post-closing (through dividends or a merger), but it is not available to pay the seller at closing unless the structure allows it.
  • Rollover vs NCI: a seller rollover in the target creates NCI. A rollover into the acquirer creates dilution. They have different EPS effects.
  • Funding certainty: for SAST offers, Reg 25 requires firm financial arrangements, and the manager to the offer must be satisfied that funds are available.

Key takeaways

  • Sources = excess cash + new debt (term loan, NCD, ECB where permitted) + new shares + seller rollover + vendor financing + earn-outs (at fair value).
  • New debt is usually the plug once cash and equity are fixed.
  • Each source carries a different EPS cost: foregone interest, interest, or share dilution.
  • Earn-outs are contingent consideration at fair value, and remeasured through P&L if liability-classified.
  • Indian rules (RBI, ECB end-use, ICDR pricing, FEMA deferral limits) constrain the mix.

Practice Questions

Q1. Funding plug [L1]

Total uses ₹3,600 crore. Acquirer cash ₹1,100 crore, minimum cash ₹300 crore. New shares ₹900 crore. Vendor note ₹200 crore. Compute the debt plug and the funding mix in percentages.

Q2. Earn-out in Sources & Uses [L2]

The headline price is ₹2,000 crore, of which ₹300 crore is an earn-out payable in two years if EBITDA exceeds ₹220 crore. Probability of payment 60%; discount rate 10%. Show how the earn-out appears in Sources & Uses, what is recognised in the PPA, and what happens in P&L if the probability rises to 90% after one year.

💡 Hint: Measure the fair value at acquisition first, then remeasure with one year less to discount.

Q3. Rollover alternatives [L2]

A founder holding 100% of a target valued at ₹1,500 crore (equity) agrees to roll over 20%. Option A: she keeps 20% of the target. Option B: she receives acquirer shares worth ₹300 crore at ₹1,200 per share (acquirer has 50 crore shares; target PAT ₹110 crore). Compare the effect on acquirer cash needed, share count, NCI and pro-forma PAT attributable to acquirer shareholders.

Q4. Funding strategy memo [L3]

A listed Indian acquirer with Net Debt/EBITDA of 1.2x and a AA rating wants to buy a ₹6,000 crore target. The covenant limit is 3.0x and the rating threshold for AA is 2.5x. Combined EBITDA is ₹2,400 crore. Recommend a funding mix (cash, bank term loan, NCDs, QIP, rollover, earn-out), with reasons tied to cost, EPS, ratings and regulation.
