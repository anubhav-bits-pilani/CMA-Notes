Subtopic note

Uses of Funds: Price, Refinancing, Open Offer, Fees and Cash

Scope: Uses: equity purchase price, refinancing target debt, open-offer consideration, transaction fees, financing fees, minimum cash

Why it matters

The Uses side of the Sources & Uses table is the full cash bill for the deal. Getting it wrong leads to a funding gap discovered at closing, or to a debt quantum (and so interest, covenants and EPS) that is understated. In Indian listed-target deals the most common misses are the open-offer consideration, the target debt that must be repaid because of change-of-control clauses, and fees. The Uses list also maps directly to the funds flow memorandum at closing and to the Ind AS 103 and Ind AS 109 accounting entries.

Core notes

Layout

Uses ₹ crore Notes
Equity purchase price (SPA stake) Offer price × diluted shares acquired
Open-offer consideration Reg 7/8 SAST: 26% × total shares × offer price (scenario-driven)
Refinancing of target debt Debt repaid at closing, plus prepayment penalties and accrued interest
Transaction fees Advisory, legal, DD, stamp duty, open-offer costs
Financing fees Arrangement, underwriting, commitment, legal for lenders
Cash to balance sheet / minimum cash Cash left for operations post-close
Total uses Must equal total sources

Equity purchase price

\[ \text{Equity purchase price} = \text{Offer price per share} \times \text{Fully diluted shares acquired} \]

Use fully diluted shares (c02, s03): in-the-money ESOPs (treasury stock method), convertibles and warrants that will convert. If the SPA is EV-based (private deals), the equity price is EV minus net debt and debt-like items plus or minus the NWC adjustment against the peg (t1 s05).

Refinancing target debt

In an equity purchase, target debt stays in the target unless it is repaid. Repay it (making it a Use) when:

  • loan agreements have change-of-control clauses or covenants that will be breached;
  • the target's debt is more expensive than the acquirer's (a financial synergy);
  • the acquisition debt needs a clean security package or structural seniority.

Add prepayment premiums, accrued interest and break costs on swaps. Target debt that is assumed rather than refinanced does not appear in Uses, but still appears in pro-forma net debt.

Open-offer consideration

For a listed target where SAST is triggered, include 26% × total shares × Reg 8 price. Uses should show the maximum (100% acceptance) for funding commitments, because the acquirer must have firm financial arrangements before the offer (Reg 25). The ownership and EPS models then run acceptance scenarios. The Reg 17 escrow is a timing item: cash or bank guarantees are blocked from before the detailed public statement until payment. It is not an extra use, but the cash cost of a guarantee and the blocked cash must be planned.

Transaction fees

  • Investment banking and advisory (often 0.5–2% of deal value in India, with success fees).
  • Legal, financial, tax, technical and ESG DD.
  • Manager to the open offer, registrar, escrow agent, newspaper advertisements, SEBI filing fees.
  • CCI filing fee (₹30 lakh for Form I and ₹90 lakh for Form II under the 2024 regulations; check the current schedule).
  • Stamp duty on share transfers (0.015% for delivery-based transfers) or on NCLT orders (s06).

Under Ind AS 103.53 these are expensed in the period incurred (except debt and equity issue costs). They reduce the acquirer's PAT in the closing year and its equity, but are not part of goodwill (s07, c05).

Financing fees

Arrangement and underwriting fees for term loans or NCDs (typically 0.5–1.5% of the facility), commitment fees, lender legal and security creation costs, and rating fees. Under Ind AS 109 these are transaction costs of the liability, deducted from its initial carrying amount and amortised through the effective interest rate (EIR). Equity issue costs (for new shares) are deducted from equity under Ind AS 32.37.

Minimum cash

The combined business needs operating cash after closing (c06). If the acquirer uses its balance-sheet cash as a source, it can only use cash above the minimum. Some models show a "cash to balance sheet" use when new debt is raised to fund operating cash for the target, for example where the target's own cash was swept by the seller in a cash-free, debt-free deal.

Illustration

An acquirer buys 45% of a listed target through an SPA and makes an open offer for 26% (assume full acceptance, so 29% stays with the public). Diluted shares 10 crore; price ₹600; target debt ₹900 crore with change of control clauses; prepayment premium 1%; transaction fees ₹38 crore; financing fees 1% on new debt of ₹3,000 crore.

Uses ₹ crore
SPA stake: 4.5 crore × ₹600 2,700
Open offer: 2.6 crore × ₹600 1,560
Refinance target debt 900
Prepayment premium (1%) 9
Transaction fees 38
Financing fees (1% × 3,000) 30
Total uses 5,237

The acquirer ends at 71%, so a 29% NCI remains. The target debt is still repaid in full, because the change-of-control clauses would otherwise let lenders accelerate it. The acquirer typically funds the repayment through an inter-company loan to the target. Financing fees are often circular with the debt amount (fees depend on debt, debt is the plug). Solve with algebra or a circularity switch (c01).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Open offer at a lower acceptance: commit funding to the maximum, but show expected funding, interest and EPS at the expected acceptance level in the base case.
  • Cash-free, debt-free private deals: there is no target debt line in Uses because the seller repays it out of the EV. The equity price already reflects it. Avoid double counting.
  • Target cash: target cash acquired can be a source (if surplus and accessible) or simply reduce net debt acquired. It cannot be used to pay the seller before closing.
  • Fees on equity issued: for share-swap deals, issue costs (merchant banker, listing fees, stamp duty on issue at 0.005%) go to equity, not the P&L.
  • Debt-like items: deferred consideration and earn-outs are not cash uses at closing but are liabilities at fair value in the PPA (t3 c05).

Key takeaways

  • Uses = equity price + open offer + target debt refinanced (with break costs) + transaction fees + financing fees + cash to balance sheet.
  • Equity price uses fully diluted shares. EV-based deals bridge to equity through net debt and the NWC peg.
  • Fund the open offer at 100% acceptance for commitments; model ownership and NCI by scenario.
  • Transaction fees are expensed (Ind AS 103.53); financing fees go into the EIR (Ind AS 109); equity issue costs go to equity (Ind AS 32).
  • Financing fees that depend on the debt plug create circularity. Handle it explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a Uses table [L1]

An acquirer buys 100% of an unlisted target. Diluted shares 4 crore at ₹850 per share. Target debt ₹600 crore must be repaid with a 2% prepayment premium and ₹6 crore of accrued interest. Advisory and DD fees ₹22 crore; stamp duty at 0.015% on the share price; financing fee 1.25% on a ₹2,500 crore term loan; minimum cash to be left in the target ₹50 crore (the target has no cash). Build the Uses table.

Q2. Open-offer scenarios [L2]

A listed target has 20 crore diluted shares. The acquirer buys 35% from promoters at ₹300 and triggers an open offer for 26% at the same price. Compute total uses at 100%, 60% and 20% acceptance, and the acquirer's ownership in each case. Explain which figure the funding commitment letters must cover.

Q3. Circular financing fees [L2]

Uses excluding financing fees are ₹4,850 crore. Sources excluding new debt are ₹1,600 crore. The financing fee is 1.2% of new debt. Solve for new debt algebraically and show the Excel approach with and without iterative calculation.

💡 Hint: Write debt as a function of itself and rearrange before reaching for iteration.

Q4. Refinance or assume [L3]

The target has ₹1,200 crore of NCDs at 10.2% with a 2% make-whole premium and no change-of-control clause, maturing in 4 years. The acquirer can borrow at 8.4% (pre-tax). Tax 25.17%. Should the NCDs be refinanced at closing? Show the NPV of refinancing and the effect on Uses and on Year 1 PAT.
