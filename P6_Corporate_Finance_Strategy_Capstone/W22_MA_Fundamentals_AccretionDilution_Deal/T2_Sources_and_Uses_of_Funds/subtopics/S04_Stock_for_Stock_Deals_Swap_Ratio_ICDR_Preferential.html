Subtopic note

Stock-for-Stock Deals: Swap Ratio, ICDR Preferential Issue, Valuers

Scope: Stock-for-stock deals: exchange (swap) ratio, preferential issue under SEBI ICDR Chapter V, and registered valuer reports

Why it matters

Paying in shares conserves cash and debt capacity and shares the risk of the deal with the target's shareholders, who become shareholders of the combined company. But it dilutes ownership and, if the acquirer's P/E is lower than the P/E paid, it dilutes EPS (t4 c04). In India, stock consideration is delivered either through an NCLT scheme at a swap ratio fixed by a registered valuer, or through a preferential issue under SEBI ICDR Chapter V for consideration other than cash (a share swap). Each route has pricing, valuation and approval rules that set the minimum issue price and so the number of new shares.

Core notes

Exchange (swap) ratio

\[ \text{ER} = \frac{\text{Offer price per target share}}{\text{Acquirer share price}} \]

\[ \text{New shares issued} = \text{Target diluted shares} \times \text{ER} \]

Example: offer price ₹540 per target share, acquirer price ₹1,800. ER = 0.30, which is 3 acquirer shares for every 10 target shares. With 20 crore diluted target shares, new shares = 6 crore.

Two variants:

  • Fixed exchange ratio: the number of shares is fixed at signing. The value received by target shareholders moves with the acquirer's share price. Common in Indian schemes, where the ratio is in the scheme document.
  • Fixed value (floating ratio): the rupee value is fixed and the ratio adjusts to the acquirer's price at closing, often with a collar. More common in US deals. In India the ICDR floor-price rules make it awkward for preferential issues.

Implied premium: compare \(\text{ER} \times P_{\text{acq}}\) with the target's undisturbed price.

Ownership split: post-deal, target shareholders own

\[ \frac{\text{New shares}}{\text{Acquirer shares} + \text{New shares}} \]

of the combined company. This should be compared with the target's contribution to combined PAT, EBITDA and value (contribution analysis, t4 s06).

Swap ratio in schemes (Sec 230–232)

In a merger scheme, the swap ratio is set by a registered valuer (Sec 247 and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules 2017). For listed companies, the SEBI Master Circular requires:

  • a valuation report from a registered valuer, using methods such as market price, comparable multiples, DCF and NAV, with weights justified;
  • a fairness opinion from an independent SEBI-registered merchant banker on the valuation;
  • audit committee and independent directors' committee recommendations;
  • disclosure in the scheme documents and the stock exchange filing (Reg 37).

The valuer usually derives a per-share value for each company and divides them. The result often differs from the market-price ratio, especially for illiquid or group companies.

Preferential issue for consideration other than cash (ICDR Chapter V)

A listed acquirer can issue shares to the target's shareholders in exchange for their target shares (a swap) by way of a preferential issue:

  • Approval: special resolution of shareholders. The relevant date is 30 days before the date of the general meeting (Reg 161).
  • Floor price, frequently traded shares (Reg 164(1), c04): not less than the higher of the 90-trading-day VWAP and the 10-trading-day VWAP preceding the relevant date. If the articles prescribe a method, the higher of that and the formula applies.
  • Valuation report (Reg 166A): if the allotment results in a change of control, or more than 5% of the post-issue fully diluted capital is allotted to an allottee or allottees acting together, a valuation report from an independent registered valuer is required, and the price must be at least the higher of the floor price and the valuer's price.
  • Consideration other than cash (Reg 163(3)): the valuation of the assets (target shares) received as consideration must be done by an independent registered valuer and the report submitted to the exchanges.
  • Lock-in (Reg 167): shares allotted to promoters are locked in for 18 months (beyond the 20% minimum promoter contribution threshold, a shorter period applies to the excess). Non-promoter allottees: 6 months. Pre-preferential holdings are also locked in for specified periods.
  • Allotment: within 15 days of the shareholder resolution or of the last regulatory approval.
  • SAST interaction: if the allottees (target shareholders) cross 25% of the acquirer, they trigger an open offer for the acquirer itself. Conversely, a share swap to acquire a listed target triggers SAST for the target.

Unlisted targets and FEMA

If the target's shareholders are non-residents, the swap must comply with FEMA NDI Rules: the acquirer's new shares are issued at not less than fair value, and the target shares acquired are valued at not more than their fair value (if the acquirer is buying from non-residents). Valuation by a SEBI-registered merchant banker or an investment banker outside India (for swaps). Sectoral caps and Press Note 3 apply.

Accounting for shares issued

  • Consideration transferred: shares are measured at fair value on the acquisition date (the closing price, not the signing price, under Ind AS 103.37). If the acquirer's price has risen between signing and closing, consideration and goodwill increase.
  • Issue costs: deducted from equity (Ind AS 32.37), net of tax.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Price drift between signing and closing: in a fixed-ratio scheme, consideration and goodwill change with the acquirer's share price at the effective date. Model goodwill at a range of prices.
  • ICDR floor above the negotiated price: if the 90-day or 10-day VWAP rises above the agreed issue price, the issue price must go up to the floor. For the same value of target shares, fewer acquirer shares can be issued, so the target's shareholders receive less than the agreed number. Negotiate a cash top-up mechanism or fix the relevant date and ratio carefully.
  • Fractional entitlements: schemes usually consolidate fractions and sell them through a trustee, distributing the cash.
  • Cross-holdings: shares held by the acquirer in the target are cancelled in a merger, so no new shares are issued against them. Do not include them in "target diluted shares" for the swap.
  • Reverse merger: if the target's shareholders end up controlling the combined entity, the legal acquirer may be the accounting acquiree (reverse acquisition under Ind AS 103 B19–B27).

Key takeaways

  • Exchange ratio = offer price ÷ acquirer price; new shares = target diluted shares × ratio.
  • Schemes fix the swap ratio through a registered valuer report, plus a merchant banker fairness opinion for listed companies.
  • Preferential issues need a special resolution, the Reg 164 floor price, Reg 166A valuation for large or control allotments, and lock-in.
  • Shares issued are measured at fair value on the acquisition date. Issue costs go to equity.
  • Watch for SAST triggers on both sides of a swap.

Practice Questions

Q1. Swap ratio and ownership [L1]

The acquirer has 40 crore shares at ₹950. The target has 12 crore diluted shares and the offer price is ₹380 per share. Compute the exchange ratio, new shares and the target shareholders' ownership of the combined company.

Q2. ICDR floor vs negotiated price [L2]

The parties agree to issue acquirer shares at ₹1,150. On the relevant date, the 90-trading-day VWAP is ₹1,120 and the 10-trading-day VWAP is ₹1,205. A registered valuer's report values the share at ₹1,180, and the allotment exceeds 5% of post-issue capital. What is the minimum issue price, and how many shares are issued for target shares valued at ₹2,410 crore?

💡 Hint: Reg 166A adds the valuer's price to the comparison.

Q3. Goodwill and price drift [L2]

In a fixed-ratio deal, 5 crore acquirer shares are issued. The acquirer's price is ₹700 at signing and ₹780 at closing. Fair value of the target's net identifiable assets is ₹2,900 crore. Compute goodwill at both prices and state which one Ind AS 103 uses.

Q4. Valuer methodology [L3]

Two listed group companies are merging. Market-price ratio: 0.42. DCF ratio: 0.55. NAV ratio: 0.30. Draft the valuer's weighting rationale and explain the public-shareholder approval requirements that might apply.
