Key concept

Exchange Ratio and New Shares Issued in a Stock Deal

Concept: Exchange ratio = Offer price per target share ÷ Acquirer share price; new shares issued = Target diluted shares × exchange ratio

Statement

\[ \text{ER} = \frac{P^{T}_{\text{offer}}}{P^{A}} \]

\[ N_{\text{new}} = N^{T}_{\text{FD}} \times \text{ER} \times s \]

where \(P^{T}_{\text{offer}}\) is the offer value per target share, \(P^{A}\) is the acquirer's share price used for issuance (at least the ICDR floor price for a preferential issue, or the price implied by the valuer's swap ratio in a scheme), \(N^{T}_{\text{FD}}\) is fully diluted target shares (excluding any target shares already held by the acquirer, which are cancelled in a merger), and \(s\) is the share of consideration paid in stock (1 for all-stock).

Post-deal ownership of target shareholders:

\[ \omega_T = \frac{N_{\text{new}}}{N^{A}_{\text{FD}} + N_{\text{new}}} \]

Explanation

The exchange ratio translates a rupee offer price into acquirer shares. It embeds the premium: if the target's undisturbed price is \(P^{T}_{0}\), the premium is \(\text{ER} \times P^{A} / P^{T}_{0} - 1\).

  • Fixed ratio vs fixed value: with a fixed ratio, target shareholders bear the acquirer's price risk between signing and closing. With a fixed value, the acquirer bears dilution risk because more shares are issued if its price falls.
  • Acquirer price basis: in a preferential issue, the issue price cannot be below the Reg 164 floor (c04), which can force a higher issue price than negotiated. For a fixed rupee value of target shares, that reduces \(N_{\text{new}}\) and so the market value target shareholders actually receive. In a scheme, the ratio comes from the valuer's relative values, not necessarily market prices.
  • EPS link: \(N_{\text{new}}\) goes into the pro-forma denominator (t4 c02). The higher the target's P/E paid relative to the acquirer's P/E, the more dilutive the deal (t4 c04).
  • Cross-holdings and fractions: cancel shares already held by the acquirer. Fractions are pooled and sold.
  • Mixed consideration: for a cash-and-stock deal, apply ER only to the stock portion.

Illustration

Acquirer: 80 crore diluted shares at ₹1,250. Target: 25 crore diluted shares, undisturbed price ₹400, of which the acquirer already holds 2 crore. Offer value ₹480 per share. 60% stock, 40% cash.

  • ER = 480 / 1,250 = 0.384 acquirer shares per target share.
  • Target shares to be exchanged = 25 − 2 = 23 crore.
  • New shares = 23 × 0.384 × 60% = 5.30 crore.
  • Cash = 23 × 480 × 40% = ₹4,416 crore.
  • Premium = 480 / 400 − 1 = 20%.
  • Target shareholders' ownership = 5.30 / (80 + 5.30) = 6.2%.

Connections

This concept underpins s04 (stock-for-stock deals and ICDR) and is constrained by c04 (preferential pricing). It feeds t4 s03 (pro-forma share count), t4 s06 (contribution analysis and ownership split) and t4 c04 (P/E rule). In t3, the new shares are valued at the acquisition-date fair value to measure consideration transferred.

Practice Questions

Q1. All-stock ratio [L1]

Target: 9 crore diluted shares, offer ₹720 per share. Acquirer: 36 crore shares at ₹1,800. Compute ER, new shares and the post-deal ownership split.

Q2. Fixed ratio vs fixed value [L2]

Using Q1, the acquirer's price falls to ₹1,600 by closing. Compute the value received by target shareholders under a fixed ratio and the shares issued under a fixed value. Which party bears the price risk in each case?

Q3. Floor-price constraint [L2]

The negotiated acquirer issue price is ₹1,750, but the ICDR floor at the relevant date is ₹1,820. The deal is ₹6,480 crore of stock consideration. Compute the shares issued at the negotiated price and at the floor, and the change in market value received by target shareholders if the market price at closing is ₹1,800. Suggest a contractual fix.

💡 Hint: Value transferred depends on the market price of the shares received, not on the issue price.
