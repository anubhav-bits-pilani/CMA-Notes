Key concept

Equity Purchase Price and the EV Identity

Concept: Equity purchase price = Offer price per share × fully diluted target shares; EV = Equity value + debt + preference + NCI − cash

Statement

\[ E = P_{\text{offer}} \times N_{\text{FD}} \]

\[ \text{EV} = E + D + \text{Pref} + \text{NCI} - \text{Cash} \]

where \(E\) is the equity purchase price for 100% of the target, \(P_{\text{offer}}\) is the offer price per share, \(N_{\text{FD}}\) is the fully diluted share count at the offer price (basic shares plus in-the-money options under the treasury stock method plus convertibles under the if-converted method), \(D\) is the target's debt (including lease liabilities if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116 and debt-like items), Pref is preference capital, NCI is the non-controlling interest in the target's consolidated subsidiaries, and Cash is free cash and equivalents.

Implied transaction multiple: \(\text{EV} \div \text{Target EBITDA}\), or \(E \div \text{Target PAT}\) for the P/E paid.

Explanation

The offer price per share is what the market sees. But the acquirer pays for every share that will exist after closing, and the business it acquires also carries debt, preference capital and minority claims, less cash. The first identity converts price per share into the equity cheque. The second converts equity into the value of the whole operating business, which is what multiples and DCF values describe.

Circularity with TSM. \(N_{\text{FD}}\) depends on \(P_{\text{offer}}\). If the price per share is fixed, compute \(N_{\text{FD}}\) directly. If the equity value \(E\) is fixed and the price is derived, solve:

\[ E = P \times \left[ N_{\text{basic}} + N_{\text{opt}} \left(1 - \frac{K}{P}\right) \right] = P(N_{\text{basic}} + N_{\text{opt}}) - N_{\text{opt}} K \]

\[ P = \frac{E + N_{\text{opt}} K}{N_{\text{basic}} + N_{\text{opt}}} \]

for a single in-the-money tranche with strike \(K\).

Partial acquisitions: for an SPA of \(x\%\) plus an open offer, the equity cheque is \(P \times N_{\text{FD}} \times (\text{stake acquired})\). The EV identity is still computed at 100% for multiples and for the PPA (NCI measured separately).

Illustration

Offer price ₹650. Basic shares 15 crore. Options 0.9 crore at ₹400 and 0.3 crore at ₹700. Debt ₹1,400 crore, CCPS treated as preference ₹150 crore, NCI ₹120 crore, free cash ₹350 crore. EBITDA ₹1,050 crore.

  • Net new shares = 0.9 × (1 − 400/650) = 0.346 crore; second tranche out of the money.
  • \(N_{\text{FD}} = 15.346\) crore; \(E = 15.346 \times 650 = ₹9{,}975\) crore.
  • EV = 9,975 + 1,400 + 150 + 120 − 350 = ₹11,295 crore; EV/EBITDA = 10.8x.

Practice Questions

Q1. Equity price and multiple [L1]

Offer price ₹1,200. Basic shares 6 crore; options 0.4 crore at ₹800. Debt ₹900 crore; free cash ₹250 crore; no preference or NCI. EBITDA ₹780 crore; PAT ₹420 crore. Compute the equity price, EV, EV/EBITDA and P/E paid.

Q2. Price from a fixed equity value [L2]

The parties agree an equity value of ₹5,500 crore. Basic shares 20 crore; options 1.5 crore at ₹180. Derive the offer price per share and verify that it is consistent with TSM. What if a second tranche of 0.5 crore options at ₹300 exists?

💡 Hint: Check whether the second tranche is in the money at the derived price before including it.

Q3. Open-offer sizing error [L2]

An analyst sizes the 26% open offer on basic shares of 30 crore. Options of 1.8 crore at ₹220 are outstanding and vest on change of control. The offer price is ₹540. Quantify the funding shortfall and explain the SAST basis for computing offer size.
