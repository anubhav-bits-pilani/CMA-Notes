Key concept

Sources Equal Uses: New Debt as the Balancing Plug

Concept: Total sources = Total uses; new debt is typically the plug once cash and equity are fixed

Statement

\[ \sum \text{Sources} = \sum \text{Uses} \]

\[ D_{\text{new}} = \text{Total uses} - (C_{\text{excess}} + E_{\text{new}} + R + V + K) \]

where \(D_{\text{new}}\) is new acquisition debt (the plug), \(C_{\text{excess}}\) is acquirer cash above the minimum operating level, \(E_{\text{new}}\) is new equity issued (to the market or to sellers), \(R\) is seller rollover, \(V\) is vendor financing and \(K\) is contingent consideration at fair value (if the headline price including earn-out is in Uses).

If financing fees are a percentage \(f\) of new debt and are themselves a Use:

\[ D_{\text{new}} = \frac{\text{Uses excluding financing fees} - \text{Other sources}}{1 - f} \]

Explanation

Sources & Uses is a cash identity: every rupee spent at closing must come from somewhere. The modelling convention is to fix the policy-driven sources first (how much cash the board will use, how much equity it is willing to issue, what the seller will roll over), and let debt absorb the difference. That reflects how deals are usually funded: equity and cash are decided by the board and the seller, and lenders size debt to fill the gap, subject to leverage limits.

The plug approach must then be checked against constraints:

  • Leverage covenants: pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA below the covenant and rating thresholds (Week 21 t5).
  • Lender sizing: bank framework limits (for example around 70% of acquisition value under the 2025 RBI draft framework), NBFC LTV limits.
  • Interest cover and DSCR in the downside case.

If the plug breaches a constraint, change the mix (more equity, more rollover, an earn-out) rather than accepting it.

Alternative conventions: in all-stock deals, new shares are the plug. In sponsor (LBO) deals, sponsor equity is the plug after maximum debt is set by leverage multiples.

Circularity: financing fees depend on debt, and debt depends on uses including fees. Solve algebraically (the second formula) or with a circuit-breaker switch. Avoid iterative calculation if data tables are used.

Illustration

Uses ₹ crore Sources ₹ crore
Equity purchase price 3,200 Excess acquirer cash 900
Refinance target debt 600 New shares (QIP) 800
Transaction fees 40 Seller rollover 300
Financing fees (1.25% of new debt) \(f \times D\) New term loan (plug) \(D\)
Total 3,840 + 0.0125D Total 2,000 + D

\[ D = \frac{3{,}840 - 2{,}000}{1 - 0.0125} = ₹1{,}863.3 \text{ crore}; \quad \text{fees} = ₹23.3 \text{ crore} \]

Check: Uses 3,840 + 23.3 = 3,863.3; Sources 2,000 + 1,863.3 = 3,863.3.

Practice Questions

Q1. Simple plug [L1]

Uses: equity price ₹2,450 crore, transaction fees ₹30 crore, refinancing ₹400 crore. Sources: acquirer cash ₹1,200 crore (minimum ₹250 crore), new shares ₹500 crore. Financing fees are 1% of new debt. Compute new debt, fees and the balanced table.

Q2. Plug vs covenant [L2]

Using Q1, the acquirer's existing net debt is ₹900 crore, combined pro-forma EBITDA is ₹1,050 crore, and the covenant limit is 2.75x Net Debt/EBITDA. Does the plug breach the covenant? If so, how much additional equity is needed to comply, holding everything else constant?

💡 Hint: Remember that using acquirer cash also increases net debt.

Q3. Model design [L3]

Explain how you would design the Sources & Uses block in Excel so that the funding mix can be switched between "debt plug", "equity plug" and "fixed percentage mix" with a single selector, while keeping the table balanced and free of circular references.
