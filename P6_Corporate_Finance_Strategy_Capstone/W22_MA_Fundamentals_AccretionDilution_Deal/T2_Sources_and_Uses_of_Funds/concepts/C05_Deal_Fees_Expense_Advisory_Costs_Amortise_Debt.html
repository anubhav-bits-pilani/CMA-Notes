Key concept

Deal Fees: Expense Advisory Costs, Amortise Debt Fees via EIR

Concept: Advisory/legal/DD fees: expensed in P&L on the acquisition (Ind AS 103); debt arrangement fees: netted from loan, amortised via EIR

Statement

Acquisition-related costs (Ind AS 103.53):

\[ \text{P\&L expense}_{t} = \text{Advisory} + \text{Legal} + \text{DD} + \text{Valuation} + \text{Acquisition stamp duty} \quad \text{(in the period services are received)} \]

These costs are not part of consideration transferred and do not change goodwill.

Debt arrangement fees (Ind AS 109): the loan is initially recognised at

\[ L_0 = L - F \]

and interest expense in each period is

\[ I_t = \text{Opening amortised cost}_t \times \text{EIR} \]

where \(L\) is the principal, \(F\) is directly attributable transaction costs, and the EIR is the rate that equates the present value of contractual cash flows to \(L_0\). Total fee recognised over the life = \(F\).

Explanation

The two treatments reflect what the costs are for. Advisory fees buy services that help complete the deal. They are not paid to the seller, so they are not part of the exchange for the business, and capitalising them would overstate goodwill. Debt fees are part of the cost of borrowing, so they are spread over the period in which the borrowing is outstanding, increasing the effective interest rate above the coupon.

Practical consequences:

  • Year 1 PAT bears the full advisory fees, usually in an exceptional item. Brokers' adjusted EPS removes them, but statutory EPS includes them.
  • Interest expense each year includes fee amortisation. For a ₹2,000 crore loan with ₹30 crore fees over 5 years, the annual P&L charge is roughly ₹6 crore above the coupon.
  • Tax: advisory fees on a share purchase are generally capital in nature (cost of shares), so there is no tax deduction. Debt fees are generally deductible over the loan term.
  • Equity issue costs follow neither rule: they are deducted from equity under Ind AS 32.37.
  • Early repayment writes off the unamortised fees immediately.

Connections

This concept summarises s07 (accounting for fees) and drives the t2 pitfall on capitalising advisory fees. It feeds t3 (consideration transferred excludes fees, so goodwill is unaffected), t4 c06 (financing fee amortisation in pro-forma PAT) and the week's exercise requirement that advisory fees be expensed and debt fees EIR-amortised in Sources & Uses.

Practice Questions

Q1. Entries and P&L effect [L1]

An acquirer pays ₹19 crore of advisory fees, ₹5 crore of legal fees for the SPA and ₹16 crore of arrangement fees on a ₹1,600 crore 4-year loan at 9%. Pass the journal entries at closing and compute the Year 1 P&L charge for these items (straight-line approximation for the fee).

Q2. Pre-closing fees across a year-end [L2]

DD work costing ₹8 crore is performed in February–March 2026, and the deal closes in June 2026 with a success fee of ₹20 crore. Debt fees of ₹12 crore are paid in June. In which financial year is each cost recognised and where in the P&L?

💡 Hint: Recognition follows the receipt of services and the creation of the obligation, not the closing date.

Q3. Adjusted vs statutory EPS [L2]

Acquirer standalone PAT ₹1,200 crore, 60 crore shares. Pro-forma PAT before acquisition costs ₹1,290 crore. Acquisition costs ₹45 crore (non-deductible). Compute statutory and adjusted pro-forma EPS and explain how each would be presented to the board.
