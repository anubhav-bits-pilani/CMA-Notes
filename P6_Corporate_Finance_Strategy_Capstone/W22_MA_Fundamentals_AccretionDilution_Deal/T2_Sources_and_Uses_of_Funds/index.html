Roadmap topic: Sources & Uses of Funds Table: Structuring cash, new acquisition debt, and stock-for-stock consideration alongside transaction & financing fees

Overview

The Sources & Uses table is the first schedule in any deal model and the bridge between the negotiated price and the post-deal balance sheet. Uses list everything the acquirer must pay at closing: the equity purchase price on fully diluted shares, any SAST open-offer consideration, refinancing of target debt (with break costs), transaction fees, financing fees and any cash left for operations. Sources list how it is paid for: excess acquirer cash, new term loans, NCDs or ECBs, new shares, seller rollover, vendor financing and contingent consideration. The two sides must balance, and by convention new debt is the plug once the board has fixed cash and equity.

The table is more than arithmetic. Each line has a regulatory and accounting overlay that changes the numbers. The equity price depends on the EV-to-equity bridge and the treasury stock method. Stock consideration depends on the exchange ratio, ICDR Chapter V pricing floors and registered valuer reports. Debt availability depends on RBI rules: banks were historically barred from funding share acquisitions, and the 2025 framework now opens a regulated route. Stamp duty depends on whether shares are transferred (0.015%) or an NCLT order conveys property (state duty). The fees are accounted for in three different ways: acquisition costs are expensed under Ind AS 103, debt fees go into the EIR under Ind AS 109, and equity issue costs go to equity under Ind AS 32.

For FP&A and transaction roles, this topic connects directly to the debt schedule (Week 15), the pro-forma balance sheet and covenants (Week 21), the PPA (t3) and the EPS model (t4). A good study sequence: start with the identity and plug (c01), then Uses (s01) with the equity price and bridge (c02, s03) and minimum cash (c06); then Sources (s02), stock deals (s04, c03, c04) and Indian acquisition finance (s05); and finish with transfer costs (s06) and fee accounting (s07, c05).

Related concepts to study

Debt schedule and circularity

→ Week 15: debt schedule, interest and circularity reused in the pro-forma model

The new debt from Sources & Uses becomes the opening balance of the acquisition tranche in the Week 15 debt schedule: mandatory amortisation, optional cash sweep, interest on average or opening balances, and fee amortisation via EIR. Interest on average balances creates circularity (interest affects cash, which affects repayment, which affects interest), and financing fees as a percentage of the debt plug create a second loop. Reuse the Week 15 techniques: a circuit-breaker switch, an opening-balance interest convention for stability, or algebraic solutions. Keep the deal model free of iterative calculation if t5 data tables will run on it.

Post-deal leverage and coverage covenants

→ Week 21 t5: post-deal Net Debt/EBITDA and interest coverage vs covenants and rating

The debt plug must be tested against the acquirer's existing loan covenants and rating thresholds. Pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA uses combined net debt (acquirer debt + new debt + assumed target debt − remaining cash) over combined EBITDA, sometimes including run-rate synergies if the loan agreement allows. Interest cover uses pro-forma EBIT or EBITDA over total interest including new debt. Week 21 t5 covered how covenant definitions (lease treatment, synergy add-backs, LTM vs forward) change the result. A deal that breaches covenants needs lender consent, more equity or a smaller debt quantum.

Earn-outs and deferred consideration

Earn-outs and deferred consideration as contingent consideration under Ind AS 103 (→ w22_t3)

Earn-outs bridge valuation gaps by making part of the price depend on future performance. In Sources & Uses, they reduce upfront cash. Under Ind AS 103.39–40 they are contingent consideration: recognised at acquisition-date fair value as part of consideration transferred, so they affect goodwill. A liability-classified earn-out is remeasured at fair value through P&L each period (t3 c05), creating earnings volatility unrelated to operations. Deferred consideration that is fixed in amount is a financial liability at amortised cost, with unwinding of discount in finance cost. Payments linked to the seller's continued employment are remuneration, not consideration.

FEMA pricing for cross-border deals

FEMA NDI Rules pricing guidelines for cross-border share deals

When a non-resident buys shares from a resident, or receives a fresh issue, the price must be at least fair value. When a non-resident sells to a resident, the price must be at most fair value. For listed shares, fair value follows SEBI pricing guidelines (preferential pricing, open-offer price). For unlisted shares, a valuation by a chartered accountant, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant using an internationally accepted method is needed. Deferred consideration is capped at 25% of total consideration, payable within 18 months. Share swaps are permitted under the automatic route subject to valuation. These rules can set the floor or ceiling for the purchase price in Uses.

Locked-box vs completion accounts

Locked-box vs completion-accounts pricing mechanisms

In a locked-box deal, the equity price is fixed by reference to a historical balance sheet (the locked-box date). Economic risk and reward pass to the buyer from that date. The seller undertakes not to extract value ("leakage") and may receive a ticker (daily interest) until closing. There is no post-closing adjustment, which gives price certainty and suits sellers and PE exits. In a completion-accounts deal, an estimated price is paid at closing and trued up afterwards for actual net debt and NWC against the peg. For Sources & Uses, the locked box fixes the equity cheque. Completion accounts require funding headroom for upward adjustments.

Common pitfalls

Basic instead of fully diluted target shares

What happens: the equity price and the open-offer size are computed on basic shares, ignoring in-the-money ESOPs, warrants and convertibles. Why: basic shares are easy to find in the shareholding pattern, and ESOP details sit in notes. Correct treatment: use the treasury stock method at the offer price for options (tranche by tranche), the if-converted method for convertibles, and check which unvested options accelerate on change of control. Detection: reconcile the share count to the ESOP note and the diluted EPS denominator in the target's annual report.

Mixing EV and equity value

What happens: the model pays an EV-based price to shareholders, or pays the equity price and forgets that the target's debt must be repaid because of change-of-control clauses. Why: deal headlines quote EV, while SPAs for listed targets quote price per share. Correct treatment: decide which basis the price is on and bridge explicitly (c02). If target debt is refinanced, add it, with break costs, to Uses. Detection: check that Uses include either a refinancing line or an explicit assumption that target debt is assumed, and that pro-forma net debt includes it.

Capitalising M&A advisory fees into goodwill

What happens: advisory, legal and DD fees are added to consideration transferred, increasing goodwill and avoiding a P&L hit. Why: legacy Indian GAAP practice and the wish to show a better Year 1 PAT. Correct treatment: Ind AS 103.53 requires expensing acquisition-related costs when services are received. Only debt and equity issue costs are excluded from that rule (EIR and equity respectively). Detection: compare the PPA's consideration with the SPA price; any excess should be explained by contingent or deferred consideration, not fees.

Ignoring SAST escrow and acceptance scenarios

What happens: the model funds only the expected open-offer acceptance, or forgets the Reg 17 escrow that must be in place before the detailed public statement. Why: acceptance in Indian open offers is often low, so analysts assume it away. Correct treatment: commit funding for 100% acceptance (Reg 25 firm financial arrangements), deposit escrow of 25% of the first ₹500 crore and 10% of the balance (100% if completing the SPA early under Reg 22), and run ownership, NCI and EPS at 0%, 50% and 100% acceptance. Detection: the funding plan should show committed facilities at least equal to maximum offer consideration and an escrow timeline.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full Sources & Uses for a listed target [L3]

An NSE-listed acquirer buys 40% of a listed target from the promoters at ₹520 per share and makes a 26% open offer at the Reg 8 price, which equals ₹520. Target: 18 crore basic shares; ESOPs 0.9 crore at ₹300 and 0.4 crore at ₹560 (vest on change of control); debt ₹1,100 crore with change-of-control clauses and a 1.5% prepayment premium. Advisory and legal fees ₹36 crore; stamp duty at 0.015% on all shares acquired; financing fee 1.25% of new debt. Acquirer cash ₹2,000 crore; minimum cash ₹600 crore; the board allows a QIP of ₹1,000 crore with issue costs of 2%. (a) Compute fully diluted shares and the open-offer size. (b) Build Sources & Uses at 100% acceptance with debt as the plug (solve the fee circularity). (c) Compute the Reg 17 escrow. (d) Show the acquirer's holding and debt at 30% acceptance. (e) Classify every fee for accounting.

Q2. Stock-for-stock via a scheme [L3]

Two listed companies merge. Acquirer: 50 crore shares at ₹900, PAT ₹2,250 crore. Target: 20 crore diluted shares at ₹300 undisturbed, PAT ₹450 crore. The registered valuer recommends 4 acquirer shares for every 10 target shares; the acquirer already holds 2 crore target shares. Stamp duty on the NCLT order is estimated at 0.7% of the value of shares allotted. (a) Compute new shares, premium and ownership split. (b) Compute stamp duty and explain its accounting. (c) List the SEBI and NCLT approvals and documents needed. (d) Compute pro-forma EPS without synergies and comment.

Q3. Funding choice under Indian acquisition-finance rules [L3]

A listed acquirer with consolidated equity ₹6,000 crore and debt ₹5,500 crore wants to buy an unlisted company for ₹4,000 crore (equity value) and refinance ₹800 crore of target debt. Options: (i) bank acquisition loan under the 2025 framework (assume the draft parameters: up to 70% of acquisition value, post-deal debt-to-equity of up to 3:1) at 8.6% with a 1% fee; (ii) privately placed HoldCo NCDs at 10.75% with a 1.5% fee; (iii) a preferential issue at ₹1,400 to a strategic investor. Tax 25.17%. (a) Determine the maximum bank loan under both constraints. (b) Propose a funding mix and build Sources & Uses. (c) Compute Year 1 after-tax interest cost including fee amortisation. (d) Identify the regulatory risks in each source.

Q4. Earn-out, locked box and FEMA [L2]

A Singapore-based seller sells 100% of an unlisted Indian SaaS company to an Indian acquirer on a locked-box basis (locked-box date 31 March 2026, closing 30 June 2026, ticker 8% a year on an equity price of ₹900 crore). An earn-out of up to ₹200 crore is payable in 24 months; fair value at closing ₹110 crore. Fair value per the merchant banker's valuation is ₹1,050 crore for 100% of the shares. (a) Compute the closing cash payment. (b) Check FEMA pricing and deferred-consideration limits. (c) Show how the earn-out appears in Sources & Uses and in the PPA.
