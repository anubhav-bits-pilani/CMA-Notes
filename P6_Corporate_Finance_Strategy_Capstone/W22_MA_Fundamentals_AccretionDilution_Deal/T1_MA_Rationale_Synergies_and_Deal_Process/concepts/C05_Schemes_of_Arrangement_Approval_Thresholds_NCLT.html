Key concept

Schemes of Arrangement: Approval Thresholds, NCLT and LODR Reg 37

Concept: Schemes of arrangement: Sec 230–232, approval by majority in number representing three-fourths in value of creditors/members, NCLT sanction, SEBI/stock-exchange observation letter (LODR Reg 37)

Statement

A compromise, arrangement, merger or demerger under Sec 230–232 of the Companies Act 2013 binds all members and creditors when:

\[ \frac{N_{\text{for}}}{N_{\text{present and voting}}} > 50\% \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{V_{\text{for}}}{V_{\text{present and voting}}} \geq 75\% \]

for each class of members and creditors (Sec 230(6)), where \(N\) is the number of persons and \(V\) is the value of their shares or debt. Voting may be in person, by proxy or by postal ballot or e-voting. After approval, the NCLT sanctions the scheme by order (Sec 230(6), 232(3)). It becomes effective when the certified copy of the order is filed with the ROC (Sec 232(5)).

For listed companies, LODR Reg 37 requires that the draft scheme be filed with the stock exchanges, and the company must obtain an observation letter (or no-objection letter) before filing the application with NCLT. SEBI's Master Circular on schemes of arrangement prescribes the documents.

Explanation

Process steps.

  1. Board approval of the scheme, valuation report (registered valuer, Sec 247) and, for listed companies, a fairness opinion from a SEBI-registered merchant banker, audit committee report and independent directors' committee report.
  2. Listed companies: file with the exchanges under Reg 37. SEBI comments through the exchanges. The observation letter is valid for a set period within which the NCLT application must be made.
  3. First motion application to NCLT: directions for convening meetings, or dispensing with them where consents are obtained (for example creditors representing 90% in value under Sec 230(9)).
  4. Notices to the Central Government (Regional Director), ROC, income-tax authorities, Official Liquidator, RBI, SEBI, CCI and sectoral regulators, who have 30 days to object (Sec 230(5)).
  5. Meetings of each class. Results reported to NCLT.
  6. Second motion petition, hearing and sanction order. The NCLT considers objections. Only persons holding at least 10% of shareholding or 5% of total outstanding debt (as per the latest audited financial statements) can object (Sec 230(4)).
  7. File the order with the ROC within 30 days. Pay stamp duty on the order as required by the state (t2 s06).

Public-shareholder protection. Under the SEBI Master Circular, certain schemes (for example where shares are allotted to promoters, where the scheme involves the listed company merging with a promoter-group entity, or where a subsidiary of the listed company acquired from promoters is merged) need approval by a majority of public shareholders: votes cast by public shareholders in favour must exceed those against.

Accounting certificate. The statutory auditor must certify that the accounting treatment in the scheme complies with the applicable Ind AS (Sec 230(7), 232(3) proviso). For mergers, this means Ind AS 103 or Appendix C.

Dissenting shareholders. There is no general appraisal right in Indian schemes, but the sanction hearing considers fairness. Under Sec 230(11)–(12) a takeover offer scheme for unlisted companies requires 3/4 shareholding support.

Illustration

Class: unsecured creditors. 400 creditors with total debt ₹500 crore. At the meeting, 260 creditors holding ₹420 crore are present and voting. In favour: 150 creditors holding ₹330 crore.

  • Majority in number: 150 / 260 = 57.7% > 50%. Passed.
  • Three-fourths in value: 330 / 420 = 78.6% ≥ 75%. Passed.

If instead 120 creditors holding ₹340 crore voted in favour, value would pass (81%) but number would fail (46.2%), and the class would not approve the scheme.

Practice Questions

Q1. Voting test [L1]

Equity shareholders: 1,20,000 holders with 50 crore shares. At the meeting, 3,400 holders with 32 crore shares vote; 1,900 holders with 23 crore shares vote in favour. Public shareholders cast 9 crore votes, of which 4.2 crore are in favour. The scheme involves allotment of shares to the promoter group. Is the scheme approved?

💡 Hint: Apply both the Sec 230(6) dual test and the public-shareholder majority condition.

Q2. Objection rights [L1]

A shareholder holding 6% of the shares and a lender holding 3% of total outstanding debt want to object to a scheme at NCLT. Can they? What other avenue does a small shareholder have?

Q3. Timeline for the model [L2]

A listed acquirer's board approves a merger scheme on 20 May 2026. Estimate a realistic timeline to the NCLT order, listing each step and its duration. Explain how the appointed date (1 April 2026) and the effective date affect the FY27 consolidated accounts and EPS under Ind AS 103.
