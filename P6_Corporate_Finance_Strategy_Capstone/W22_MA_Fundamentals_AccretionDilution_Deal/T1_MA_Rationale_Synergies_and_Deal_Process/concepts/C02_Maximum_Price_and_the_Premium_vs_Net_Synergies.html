Key concept

Maximum Price and the Premium vs Net Synergies Test

Concept: Maximum price (acquirer view) = Target standalone value + PV of net synergies; premium paid should be < PV of net synergies

Statement

\[ P_{\max} = V_B + V_{\text{syn}} \]

\[ \text{NPV}_{\text{acquirer}} = (V_B + V_{\text{syn}}) - P - \text{Transaction costs} \]

\[ \text{Value created for acquirer if } \text{Premium} = P - V_B < V_{\text{syn}} - \text{Transaction costs} \]

where \(V_B\) is the target's standalone intrinsic value (DCF or a market price that is not already inflated by bid speculation), \(V_{\text{syn}}\) is the PV of after-tax synergies net of costs to achieve and dis-synergies (c01), \(P\) is the purchase price for the equity (or EV, if \(V_B\) is also measured as EV), and transaction costs are advisory, legal, stamp duty and financing fees.

Explanation

The acquirer creates value only if what it pays is less than what it gets. What it gets is the target's standalone value plus the synergies that the combination unlocks. So the premium over standalone value is effectively a payment to target shareholders for synergies that the acquirer will have to deliver. If the premium equals \(V_{\text{syn}}\), all synergy value goes to the seller and the acquirer's NPV is zero before fees.

Practical points:

  • Measurement consistency: compare equity value with equity price, or EV with EV. In a listed-target deal, premium is usually quoted relative to the undisturbed share price (for example the 1-day or 60-day VWAP before rumours). That market price is only a proxy for \(V_B\).
  • Who gets the synergies: in competitive auctions, sellers capture most synergies because bidders compete them away (the "winner's curse"). Discipline means walking away at \(P_{\max}\).
  • Unique synergies: synergies only this acquirer can achieve justify a higher bid than other bidders can afford, but paying for them hands that advantage to the seller.
  • Regulatory price floors: a SAST Reg 8 floor or FEMA fair-value floor can force a price above \(P_{\max}\). Then the deal should be rejected or restructured.
  • Partial acquisition: if the acquirer buys only part of the target, NCI shares in target-level synergies. Scale the test to the acquirer's share.

Illustration

Target: standalone DCF equity value ₹4,000 crore; 10 crore shares; undisturbed price ₹380 per share (market value ₹3,800 crore). PV of net synergies ₹1,050 crore. Transaction costs ₹60 crore.

  • \(P_{\max} = 4{,}000 + 1{,}050 = ₹5{,}050\) crore gross; net of transaction costs the walk-away price for the equity is \(5{,}050 - 60 = ₹4{,}990\) crore, or ₹499 per share.
  • Offer at ₹460 per share (₹4,600 crore): premium over the undisturbed price is 21%. Premium over DCF value is ₹600 crore. Acquirer NPV \(= 4{,}000 + 1{,}050 - 4{,}600 - 60 = ₹390\) crore. Value is created.
  • Offer at ₹520 per share: acquirer NPV \(= -₹210\) crore, even though the deal may still be EPS-accretive if funded with cheap debt.

Connections

This links to c01 (computing \(V_{\text{syn}}\)), s02–s03 (what goes into net synergies), and the pitfalls on revenue synergies and integration costs. It is the value test that t4 and t5 contrast with EPS accretion, and it reuses Week 16 DCF and comps for \(V_B\). In board papers, FP&A presents premium paid against risked synergy NPV as the headline value metric.

Practice Questions

Q1. Maximum price per share [L1]

Target standalone equity value ₹2,500 crore, 5 crore diluted shares, PV of net synergies ₹600 crore, transaction costs ₹40 crore. Compute the maximum price per share and the acquirer's NPV at an offer of ₹560 per share.

Q2. Accretive but value-destroying [L2]

An acquirer with a P/E of 30x buys a target with PAT of ₹200 crore for ₹5,000 crore using debt at 8% pre-tax (tax 25.17%). The target's standalone value is ₹3,800 crore and PV of net synergies is ₹700 crore. Show that the deal is EPS-accretive in Year 1 but destroys value, and explain why both statements can be true.

💡 Hint: Compare after-tax interest with target PAT for EPS, and premium with synergy value for NPV.

Q3. Price floor above the walk-away price [L3]

The SAST Reg 8 price for a listed target works out at ₹720 per share because the acquirer bought shares at that price in the market 20 weeks ago, while \(P_{\max}\) is ₹680 per share. Discuss the options available to the acquirer.
