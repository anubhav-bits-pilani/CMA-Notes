Key concept

SAST Open-Offer Triggers: Reg 3(1), Reg 3(2) and Reg 4

Concept: SAST triggers: Reg 3(1) acquiring 25% voting rights; Reg 3(2) creeping >5% per FY for holders between 25% and 75%; Reg 4 control

Statement

Under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations 2011, an acquirer, together with persons acting in concert (PACs), must make a public announcement of an open offer if:

\[ \text{Reg 3(1)}: \quad H_{\text{pre}} < 25\% \ \text{and} \ H_{\text{post}} \geq 25\% \]

\[ \text{Reg 3(2)}: \quad 25\% \leq H_{\text{pre}} < 75\% \ \text{and} \ \Delta H_{\text{FY}} > 5\% \]

\[ \text{Reg 4}: \quad \text{acquisition of control, directly or indirectly, irrespective of } H \]

where \(H\) is the aggregate voting-rights holding of the acquirer and PACs, \(\Delta H_{\text{FY}}\) is the gross acquisition in a financial year (April–March), and 75% is the maximum permissible non-public shareholding (because SCRR Rule 19A requires 25% minimum public shareholding).

Explanation

  • Reg 3(1) is the classic threshold. Crossing 25% gives the power to block special resolutions, so minority shareholders get an exit opportunity at a fair price.
  • Reg 3(2) is the creeping-acquisition limit. An existing holder of 25% or more may add up to 5% in each financial year without an offer. Acquiring more than 5% triggers an offer. The 5% is computed on gross acquisitions (sales in the year are not netted off), and includes subscriptions to preferential allotments. Passive increases from a buy-back are exempt under Reg 10(3) and 10(4)(c) only if the stated conditions are met (for example reducing the excess within 90 days, or not voting in favour of the buy-back). An acquirer cannot use the creeping route to go beyond 75%.
  • Reg 4 applies to acquisitions of control regardless of shareholding. "Control" (Reg 2(1)(e)) includes the right to appoint a majority of directors or to control management or policy decisions, through shareholding, management rights, shareholders' agreements or voting agreements. Affirmative rights over operating matters can amount to control. Purely protective rights generally do not.
  • Indirect acquisitions (Reg 5): acquiring a parent that holds shares or control in a listed company triggers an offer for the listed company. If the listed company is a significant part of the parent (over 80% of NAV, turnover or market cap), it is treated as a direct acquisition.
  • Exemptions (Reg 10): inter-se transfers among promoters or group companies (subject to conditions and pricing caps), schemes of arrangement sanctioned by NCLT, IBC resolution plans, and others. Many need a report to SEBI and some need a prior exemption application.
  • Timing: for an SPA, the public announcement is made on the date of agreeing to acquire. For a market purchase, it is made on the date the trigger is crossed.

Breach consequences include directions to make an offer with interest, divestment and penalties.

Illustration

A promoter group holds 48% of a listed company (20 crore shares). During FY27 it buys 0.6 crore shares in the market (3%) and subscribes to a preferential allotment that raises its stake by another 2.4% on the enlarged capital.

  • Gross acquisition = 3% + 2.4% = 5.4% > 5%, so Reg 3(2) is triggered, even if the group sold 1% during the year.
  • A separate case: a strategic buyer acquires 22% from a PE fund and gets the right to appoint the CEO and 3 of 5 directors. That is control under Reg 4, so an offer is required even though the stake is below 25%.
  • A third case: an investor at 24.5% buys 1% more. Its holding crosses 25%, so Reg 3(1) is triggered.

Practice Questions

Q1. Trigger test [L1]

For each case, state whether an open offer is triggered and under which regulation: (a) 18% to 26% by an SPA; (b) 40% to 44.5% via market purchases in one FY; (c) 60% to 66% via a preferential allotment in one FY; (d) 72% to 76% via a market purchase; (e) 12% plus the right to appoint the majority of the board.

Q2. Creeping calculation [L2]

A holder at 31% buys 3.5% in April, sells 1.5% in August and buys 2.2% in January of the same FY. Separately, a buy-back in December increases its percentage by 0.4%. Is Reg 3(2) triggered? Show the computation.

💡 Hint: Check whether disposals are netted and how passive increases are treated.

Q3. Control through rights [L3]

A PE investor acquires 19% and negotiates affirmative votes on the annual budget, appointment of the CFO and any acquisition above ₹50 crore. Discuss whether this is "control" under Reg 4 and what the deal team should do in structuring the SHA.
