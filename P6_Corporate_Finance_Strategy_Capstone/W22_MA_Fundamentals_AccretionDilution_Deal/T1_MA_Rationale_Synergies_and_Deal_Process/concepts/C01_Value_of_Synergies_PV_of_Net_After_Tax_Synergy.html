Key concept

Value of Synergies: PV of Net After-Tax Synergy Cash Flows

Concept: Value of synergies = PV of after-tax synergy cash flows − PV of after-tax costs to achieve

Statement

\[ V_{\text{syn}} = \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{S_t \times (1 - T) - \Delta \text{Capex}_t - \Delta \text{NWC}_t}{(1 + r)^t} + \frac{TV_N}{(1+r)^N} - \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{C_t \times (1 - T)}{(1 + r)^t} \]

where:

  • \(S_t\) = phased, probability-weighted pre-tax EBITDA synergies in year \(t\), net of dis-synergies;
  • \(T\) = marginal tax rate of the entity where synergies arise (25.17% for a Sec 115BAA company);
  • \(\Delta \text{Capex}_t\), \(\Delta \text{NWC}_t\) = incremental investment needed to deliver the synergies (often small for cost synergies, material for revenue synergies);
  • \(C_t\) = one-time pre-tax costs to achieve (severance, IT integration, site closure);
  • \(r\) = discount rate reflecting synergy risk;
  • \(TV_N\) = terminal value of run-rate synergies, for example \(\frac{S_{N+1}(1-T)}{r - g}\).

Explanation

Synergies are incremental cash flows, so they are valued like any project: after tax, net of the investment needed, discounted at a rate that matches their risk. Three points distinguish good practice:

  1. After tax on both sides. A ₹100 crore pre-tax saving is worth ₹74.83 crore after tax at 25.17%. Costs to achieve that are tax-deductible (severance, consultants) reduce tax too. Capitalised integration capex gives tax depreciation over time, not an immediate deduction.
  2. Risk-adjusted discount rate. Cost synergies under management control are often discounted at the combined WACC. Revenue synergies carry more risk: either apply a probability weight to the cash flows (preferred, transparent) or add a premium to the rate. Do not do both.
  3. Terminal value. Recurring synergies usually continue beyond the forecast period. Many practitioners use a zero or low growth rate, or a fade, because competitors catch up and savings get competed away.

The resulting \(V_{\text{syn}}\) is the ceiling on the premium the acquirer can pay without destroying value (c02). It is an economic number, independent of EPS. A deal can be EPS-accretive while \(V_{\text{syn}}\) is smaller than the premium.

Illustration

Cost synergies (pre-tax) of ₹60 crore in Year 1, ₹120 crore in Year 2 and ₹150 crore from Year 3, growing at 2% after Year 3. Costs to achieve: ₹90 crore in Year 1 and ₹50 crore in Year 2. Tax 25.17%, discount rate 11%. No capex or NWC.

₹ crore Year 1 Year 2 Year 3
After-tax synergies 44.9 89.8 112.2
After-tax CTA (67.3) (37.4) 0
Net (22.4) 52.4 112.2
Discount factor at 11% 0.901 0.812 0.731
PV (20.2) 42.5 82.0

Terminal value at end of Year 3 \(= \frac{112.2 \times 1.02}{0.11 - 0.02} = 1{,}271.6\), with a PV of \(1{,}271.6 \times 0.731 = 929.7\).

\[ V_{\text{syn}} \approx -20.2 + 42.5 + 82.0 + 929.7 = ₹1{,}034 \text{ crore} \]

About 90% of the value is in the terminal value, which is why the sustainability and growth assumption on run-rate synergies is the most important judgement.

Practice Questions

Q1. Synergy NPV with probability weights [L2]

Cost synergies: ₹40 crore (Year 1), ₹80 crore (Year 2 onwards). Revenue synergy EBITDA: ₹0, ₹20 crore, ₹50 crore from Year 3, with 40% probability. CTA: ₹70 crore in Year 1. Tax 25.17%, discount rate 12%, perpetual growth 0% after Year 3. Compute the value of synergies.

Q2. Tax treatment of CTA [L2]

Of ₹100 crore CTA in Year 1, ₹60 crore is severance (deductible) and ₹40 crore is ERP capex depreciated for tax at 40% WDV. Tax 25.17%, discount rate 11%. Compare the PV of after-tax CTA with the simplification "CTA × (1 − T)" in Year 1, and comment on the error.

💡 Hint: Treat the ERP capex as an outflow in Year 1 with a stream of tax-depreciation shields.

Q3. Discount rate vs probability weighting [L3]

A colleague discounts revenue synergies at 18% and applies a 50% probability haircut. Explain the double counting, and propose a consistent approach for the board paper.
