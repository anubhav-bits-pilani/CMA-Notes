Subtopic note

Indian M&A Regulatory Map: CCI, SAST, NCLT Schemes and FEMA

Scope: Indian regulatory map: CCI (deal value threshold ₹2,000 crore), SEBI SAST open offers, Companies Act Sec 230–234 schemes via NCLT, FEMA pricing

Why it matters

Regulation decides how long a deal takes, how much cash it needs and what price can be paid. A listed target brings an open offer that may require funding of a further 26% of the company. A merger needs an NCLT scheme that takes most of a year. A horizontal deal above the CCI thresholds cannot close until approval. A foreign buyer or seller must comply with FEMA pricing floors and ceilings. A deal model that ignores these will understate funding needs, misstate the timeline (and so the stub-period EPS) and miss conditions precedent.

Core notes

Competition Commission of India (CCI)

The Competition Act 2002 (Sec 5 and 6) requires notification of "combinations" (acquisitions of shares, voting rights, control or assets, and mergers or amalgamations) that cross the thresholds, and prohibits closing until CCI approves or 150 days pass (the review period was shortened from 210 days by the 2023 amendment). CCI must form a prima facie opinion within 30 days.

Threshold tests (as revised by MCA notifications in March 2024, which are indexed periodically, so check the latest):

Test Parties (India) Group (India) Worldwide with India nexus
Assets > ₹2,500 crore > ₹10,000 crore Global assets above the USD threshold with at least ₹1,250 crore in India
Turnover > ₹7,500 crore > ₹30,000 crore Global turnover above the USD threshold with at least ₹3,750 crore in India

De minimis (small target) exemption: an acquisition is exempt if the target has Indian assets of ₹450 crore or less, or Indian turnover of ₹1,250 crore or less. The exemption is granted for a fixed period and has been extended before, so check the current notification.

Deal value threshold (DVT): from 10 September 2024, a transaction must be notified, even if the asset and turnover tests are not met and the de minimis exemption would apply, if (a) the value of the transaction exceeds ₹2,000 crore and (b) the target has substantial business operations in India. The Combination Regulations 2024 set out the SBO tests: for digital services, at least 10% of global users or GMV or turnover in India; for other businesses, at least 10% of global GMV or turnover in India together with the absolute floors in the regulations. Deal value includes all consideration: direct and indirect, deferred, non-compete payments and earn-outs.

Other features: Green Channel automatic approval for deals with no horizontal, vertical or complementary overlaps; Schedule I exemptions (for example minority investments below 25% made solely as an investment, and intra-group restructurings); gun-jumping penalties up to 1% of turnover or assets.

SEBI SAST Regulations 2011 (listed targets)

The Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers Regulations apply when shares or control of a listed company are acquired.

  • Triggers (c03): Reg 3(1) acquisition taking holding to 25% or more of voting rights; Reg 3(2) creeping acquisition of more than 5% in a financial year by someone already holding 25% or more but below the maximum permissible non-public shareholding (75%); Reg 4 acquisition of control, regardless of the shareholding. Indirect acquisitions (buying a parent company) are covered by Reg 5.
  • Offer size and price (c04): at least 26% of total shares (Reg 7), at the highest of the Reg 8 price parameters.
  • Process: public announcement (PA) on the day of agreement to acquire, detailed public statement within 5 working days, draft letter of offer to SEBI, tendering period of 10 working days, and payment within 10 working days of the tendering period closing.
  • Escrow (Reg 17): 25% of the first ₹500 crore of offer consideration plus 10% of the balance. Deposit in cash, bank guarantee or securities, with at least 1% in cash where a guarantee is used. This must be in place before the detailed public statement.
  • Completion of the SPA (Reg 22): the acquirer cannot complete the underlying acquisition until the offer period ends. It can complete earlier only if it deposits 100% of the open-offer consideration in escrow (after 21 working days from the DPS).
  • MPS consequence: if the acquirer's holding exceeds 75% after the offer, it must bring public shareholding back to 25% within 12 months (SCRR Rule 19A), or it can delist. Since the 2021 amendments, an acquirer may announce delisting intention with the open offer.

Companies Act 2013 Sec 230–234 schemes via NCLT

  • Sec 230: compromise or arrangement with creditors or members (includes capital reorganisation and takeover of the remaining shares of unlisted companies under Sec 230(11)–(12)).
  • Sec 231: NCLT's power to enforce the scheme.
  • Sec 232: merger and amalgamation, including demerger. The scheme needs the approval of a majority in number representing three-fourths in value of each class of creditors and members present and voting (c05), followed by NCLT sanction. Notices go to the Central Government (RD), ROC, Official Liquidator, income-tax authorities, RBI, SEBI, CCI and stock exchanges where relevant.
  • Sec 233: fast-track mergers (small companies, holding with wholly owned subsidiary, start-ups, and other classes notified), approved by the Regional Director without NCLT (s08).
  • Sec 234: cross-border mergers between Indian and foreign companies, read with FEMA (Cross-Border Merger) Regulations 2018. Outbound mergers need the foreign company to be in a notified jurisdiction.
  • Listed companies: before filing with NCLT, they need an observation letter from the stock exchanges under LODR Reg 37 and the SEBI Master Circular on schemes. This includes a valuation report from a registered valuer, a fairness opinion from a SEBI-registered merchant banker, audit committee and independent director reports, and in specified cases approval by a majority of public shareholders.

Typical timeline: 8–14 months from board approval to the NCLT order. The appointed date (accounting effective date) can be earlier than the effective date (filing of the order with ROC).

FEMA pricing for cross-border share deals

Under the FEM (Non-debt Instruments) Rules 2019:

  • Resident to non-resident transfer (or fresh issue to a non-resident): price not less than fair value.
  • Non-resident to resident transfer: price not more than fair value.
  • Fair value: for listed companies, the price worked out under SEBI guidelines (for example preferential pricing under ICDR, or SAST price for open offers). For unlisted companies, valuation under an internationally accepted pricing methodology on an arm's-length basis by a chartered accountant, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant.
  • Deferred consideration: up to 25% of total consideration can be deferred, escrowed or paid as indemnity for up to 18 months.
  • Swaps: share swaps are permitted under the automatic route subject to valuation and sectoral caps.
  • Press Note 3 (2020): investment from entities of countries sharing a land border with India (or where the beneficial owner is from such a country) needs government approval.
  • Reporting: Form FC-TRS for transfers, and FC-GPR for fresh issue, on the FIRMS portal within the prescribed time.

Putting it together

Deal fact Regulatory consequence Model consequence
Listed target, acquiring > 25% or control SAST open offer Fund up to 26% extra; escrow; NCI scenarios
Combined size or deal value above thresholds CCI filing CP; closing date pushed out; stub period
Merger of two companies Sec 230–232 scheme 8–14 month timeline; stamp duty on order; swap ratio
Foreign party involved FEMA pricing Price floor or ceiling; deferred consideration limit

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • DVT and global deals: a foreign-to-foreign acquisition of a digital business with a large Indian user base can need CCI approval even with tiny Indian revenue.
  • Control without 25%: affirmative voting rights or board nomination rights can be "control" under Reg 4. SEBI's view of protective vs participative rights matters.
  • Pricing conflict: a FEMA floor (fair value) and a SAST price can differ. The higher binding constraint applies.
  • Scheme vs open offer: a scheme of arrangement sanctioned by NCLT is exempt from the open offer under Reg 10(1)(d), subject to conditions, which is why many restructurings use schemes.
  • Indexation of CCI thresholds: thresholds are revised periodically. Always check the current notification date.

Key takeaways

  • CCI: asset/turnover thresholds, de minimis exemption, and since September 2024 a ₹2,000 crore deal value threshold tied to substantial business operations in India. No closing before approval.
  • SAST: 25% / creeping 5% / control triggers, 26% open offer, Reg 8 pricing, Reg 17 escrow and Reg 22 completion restrictions.
  • Sec 230–232 schemes need three-fourths in value approval and NCLT sanction. Listed companies also need the Reg 37 observation letter.
  • FEMA: resident-to-non-resident at no less than fair value, and the reverse at no more than fair value.
  • Each regulatory step changes the funding need, the timeline or the CP list in the model.

Practice Questions

Q1. CCI notifiability [L2]

Acquirer group: Indian assets ₹12,000 crore, Indian turnover ₹18,000 crore. Target: Indian assets ₹380 crore, Indian turnover ₹560 crore, global turnover ₹4,000 crore (so 14% is from India). Deal value ₹2,600 crore. Is the deal notifiable? Walk through each test, including the de minimis exemption and the DVT.

Q2. SAST escrow [L2]

Open offer for 26% of 8 crore shares at ₹640 per share. Compute the minimum escrow under Reg 17 and the 100% escrow the acquirer would need if it wants to complete the SPA before the offer period ends.

Q3. FEMA pricing check [L2]

A Singapore investor (not covered by Press Note 3) buys 20% of an unlisted Indian company from resident promoters at ₹410 per share. A merchant banker's DCF gives fair value of ₹455 per share. Is the price compliant? What if the investor is selling to residents at ₹480?

Q4. Choosing the route [L2]

A listed company holds 68% of a listed subsidiary and wants 100%. Compare (a) creeping acquisition plus delisting, (b) a scheme of amalgamation under Sec 230–232 and (c) a voluntary open offer, on regulatory steps, timeline and cash need.

Q5. Regulatory timeline in the model [L3]

SPA signed on 15 June 2026 for a listed target; CCI Phase I approval expected in 60 days; SAST open offer tendering period expected to close on 30 September 2026. Build a timeline showing when the SPA shares and offer shares are acquired. Explain how the ownership percentage, NCI and stub-period EPS in FY27 depend on it.
