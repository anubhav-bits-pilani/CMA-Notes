Subtopic note

Synergy Taxonomy: Cost, Revenue and Financial Synergies

Scope: Synergy taxonomy: cost (procurement, plant consolidation, SG&A), revenue (cross-sell, pricing, distribution), financial (tax losses, cost of capital)

Why it matters

Synergies are the only reason an acquirer can pay more than the target's standalone value and still create value. The premium paid is effectively a prepayment for synergies. Boards, lenders and the stock market all ask the same question: which synergies, how big, how certain, and by when? In the model, synergies feed the EPS bridge (t4), break-even analysis (t5) and the value test (c02). In practice, cost synergies are bankable and revenue synergies are discounted, so classifying each synergy correctly matters as much as sizing it.

Core notes

Definition

A synergy is the increase in combined cash flows (or decrease in combined risk) that neither company could achieve alone:

\[ V_{AB} = V_A + V_B + \text{PV}(\text{net synergies}) \]

where \(V_A\) and \(V_B\) are standalone values. Anything either party could do alone, such as cutting its own overheads, is not a synergy. It belongs in that party's standalone plan. This "standalone test" is the first filter in synergy review.

Cost synergies

Cost synergies have the highest realisation rates because management controls them.

Procurement. Combined volumes give better prices on raw materials, packaging, freight, power and indirect spend. Size it by spend category × addressable share × expected saving %. Example: combined coal and petcoke spend ₹1,800 crore, 70% addressable, 2.5% price improvement = ₹31.5 crore a year. Watch for contracts that cannot be renegotiated until expiry, and for categories where the target already buys better than the acquirer.

Plant and network consolidation. Close sub-scale plants, shift volume to lower-cost sites, merge depots and warehouses, optimise freight lanes (important in cement, FMCG and logistics). Savings come from fixed overhead removed plus variable-cost differences between plants. Costs to achieve are large: VRS, asset write-downs (Ind AS 36 impairment of plants to be closed), shifting costs and sometimes state subsidy clawbacks.

SG&A. Remove duplicate corporate functions (finance, HR, legal, IT, listing costs if the target is delisted), sales-force overlap and marketing spend. A common sizing method is overlapping headcount × average cost to company, plus non-people costs such as rent, audit fees and software licences.

Revenue synergies

Revenue synergies are harder to achieve because they depend on customers. Analysts usually probability-weight or exclude them from base-case valuation.

  • Cross-selling: selling the acquirer's products to the target's customers and vice versa (bancassurance, a paint company selling the acquired brand's adhesives through its dealer network).
  • Pricing: better price realisation from a stronger brand portfolio or reduced price competition. In horizontal deals this overlaps with competition-law risk, so a pricing-power argument in a board paper can become evidence in CCI proceedings.
  • Distribution: pushing the target's products through the acquirer's wider network (rural reach, modern trade, exports) or entering new geographies.

Always convert revenue synergies to EBITDA using the contribution margin of the incremental sales, net of incremental variable costs, selling costs and working capital. A ₹200 crore revenue synergy at a 25% contribution margin is ₹50 crore of EBITDA, before any probability haircut.

Financial synergies

  • Tax losses: the acquirer may use the target's unabsorbed depreciation and business losses. In an amalgamation this depends on Sec 72A conditions (s07). In a share deal it depends on Sec 79 for closely held companies. Value it as PV of tax saved, timed to when the combined entity can actually use the losses. Under Ind AS 12, recognising a DTA needs probable future taxable profit.
  • Cost of capital: a larger, more diversified group may borrow at a lower spread, get a better rating, or move the target's debt from high-cost NBFC funding to bank or bond funding. Refinancing the target's 12% debt at 8.5% is a measurable saving. Pure diversification of equity risk is not a synergy, because shareholders can diversify themselves and CAPM prices only systematic risk.
  • Debt capacity / tax shield: a combined entity with more stable cash flows may carry more debt, adding interest tax shields. Treat this cautiously. It is a financing decision, not an operating synergy.
  • Working capital and capex: rationalising inventory norms, combining treasury and avoiding duplicate capex. These are one-off cash releases and should be shown separately from recurring EBITDA synergies.

Classification summary

Category Examples Typical realisation confidence Model treatment
Cost: procurement Raw material, freight, power High 100% base case, phased
Cost: plant consolidation Closing sites, depot merger Medium to high, slow Phased with costs to achieve
Cost: SG&A Corporate overlap, headcount High Phased, offset by severance
Revenue: cross-sell/distribution Network expansion Low to medium Probability-weighted, often excluded from base
Revenue: pricing Portfolio pricing Low; regulatory risk Usually excluded
Financial: tax losses Sec 72A carry-forward Binary on eligibility PV of tax saved, separately disclosed
Financial: cost of debt Refinancing at lower spread Medium Through interest line, not EBITDA

Sizing and documentation in the model

Build a synergy schedule with one row per initiative: category, run-rate amount, phasing % by year, probability weight, cost to achieve and owner. Sum by category. Key outputs:

\[ \text{Risked run-rate EBITDA synergy} = \sum_i \text{Run-rate}_i \times p_i \]

where \(p_i\) is the probability weight. Show synergies as a percentage of the target's revenue and of the combined cost base. A sanity benchmark is that cost synergies above about 3–5% of the combined addressable cost base need specific evidence (t5 c03).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative synergies are part of the taxonomy: customer overlap losses, supplier dual-sourcing policies and key-person exits (s03).
  • Tax-loss synergies in a share deal of an unlisted target can disappear under Sec 79 if more than 49% of voting power changes, unless an exception applies.
  • Revenue synergies vs competition law: a "price increase" synergy in a horizontal deal is commercially sensitive. Frame it as mix and premiumisation where that is genuine.
  • Double counting: procurement savings may already be in the acquirer's standalone plan, and revenue synergies can overlap with a distribution synergy on the same customers.
  • Where synergies land: if both entities are not wholly owned, part of the synergy belongs to NCI. Attribute synergies to the entity where they arise.

Key takeaways

  • A synergy must pass the standalone test. Value either party could create alone does not count.
  • Cost synergies (procurement, plant consolidation, SG&A) are the most reliable and get full base-case weight after phasing.
  • Revenue synergies (cross-sell, pricing, distribution) must be converted to EBITDA at contribution margin and probability-weighted.
  • Financial synergies (tax losses, lower cost of debt) are real but conditional. Diversification alone is not a synergy.
  • Keep a line-by-line synergy schedule with category, phasing, probability and cost to achieve.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and screen synergies [L1]

A management deck lists: (a) ₹40 crore saving from closing the target's head office; (b) ₹25 crore from the acquirer's own planned ERP upgrade; (c) ₹60 crore EBITDA from selling the target's products through the acquirer's 18,000 dealers; (d) ₹15 crore lower interest from refinancing the target's NBFC loans; (e) "lower risk from diversification". Classify each and say whether it passes the standalone test.

Q2. Procurement synergy sizing [L2]

Combined spend: steel ₹900 crore (80% addressable, 3% saving), packaging ₹220 crore (60% addressable, 5%), freight ₹500 crore (50% addressable, 4%), indirect ₹150 crore (90% addressable, 6%). Compute run-rate procurement synergies, and the value at a 30% realisation in Year 1, 70% in Year 2 and 100% from Year 3.

Q3. Revenue synergy to EBITDA [L2]

Cross-sell revenue synergies are projected at ₹120 crore (Year 2) and ₹250 crore (Year 3). Contribution margin is 32%, incremental selling cost is 6% of revenue, and incremental NWC is 15% of incremental revenue. Apply a 50% probability weight. Compute risked EBITDA synergy and the cash-flow impact (before tax) for each year.

💡 Hint: NWC investment is a cash outflow on the change in incremental revenue, not on the level.

Q4. Financial synergy valuation [L2]

The target has ₹480 crore of unabsorbed depreciation (no time limit) and ₹220 crore of business losses with 3 years of life left. The combined entity can absorb ₹200 crore a year of losses starting Year 1, using the business losses first. Tax rate 25.17%, discount rate 11%. Compute PV of tax savings and explain how the result changes if Sec 72A conditions fail.

Q5. Board challenge [L3]

You are FP&A lead. The deal team's base case has 45% of total synergies from revenue and pricing in a horizontal FMCG deal. Draft five challenge questions to the deal team and propose how the base, upside and downside cases should treat each synergy category.
