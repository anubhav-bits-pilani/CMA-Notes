Subtopic note

Synergy Phasing, Costs to Achieve and Dis-synergies

Scope: Synergy phasing, costs to achieve (one-time integration costs) and dis-synergies (customer attrition, retention bonuses)

Why it matters

A run-rate synergy number in a press release ("₹500 crore of annual synergies") is not the value of synergies. Value depends on when the savings arrive, what it costs to unlock them and what is lost along the way. Integration costs can exceed the first year's synergies, so most deals reduce reported profit in Year 1. Dis-synergies such as customers leaving because they do not want a single supplier, or retention bonuses needed to keep key people, are often left out of models. That makes the deal look better than it is. Analysts and auditors (Ind AS 36 impairment reviews of acquisition goodwill) test the phasing assumptions against actual delivery.

Core notes

Phasing

Synergies ramp up because contracts expire, plants close in sequence, IT systems take time to merge and employee consultations take time. A typical phasing profile:

Synergy type Year 1 Year 2 Year 3 Comment
Procurement 40–60% 80–100% 100% Starts with contract renewals
SG&A / corporate 30–50% 80–100% 100% Depends on legal entity merger and headcount actions
Plant consolidation 0–20% 40–70% 100% Needs capacity transfer, regulatory consents
Revenue synergies 0–10% 20–40% 50–100% Slowest; probability-weighted

In a model, store run-rate synergy and a phasing row of percentages for each line. Then:

\[ \text{Synergy}_{t} = \text{Run-rate} \times \text{Phasing}_{t} \times p \times \text{Stub factor}_{t} \]

where \(p\) is the probability weight and the stub factor is the fraction of the first fiscal year after closing (for example 0.5 for a 1 October close with a 31 March year-end). Phasing is relative to the closing date, but the model usually reports by fiscal year, so map closing-anniversary years to fiscal years.

Costs to achieve (CTA)

Costs to achieve are the one-time cash costs needed to deliver synergies:

  • Severance and VRS (an Ind AS 19 termination benefit when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer, or when it recognises related restructuring costs).
  • Restructuring under Ind AS 37: recognise a provision only when there is a detailed formal plan and a valid expectation in those affected. Planning to restructure is not enough.
  • Site closure: lease exit costs, plant dismantling, asset impairment (non-cash), relocation of equipment.
  • IT integration: ERP migration, data cleansing, licence duplication during transition.
  • Rebranding, signage, legal entity rationalisation, consultants and integration-office costs.

A common rule of thumb is that total CTA is roughly 1.0–1.5x run-rate cost synergies, concentrated in Years 1 and 2. In the P&L these are often shown as "exceptional items", which Schedule III allows as a separate line when items are of such size, nature or incidence that disclosure is relevant. Brokers will exclude them in "adjusted" EPS, but they are real cash costs, so the value test (c01) must include them.

Important distinction. Integration costs incurred after closing are different from transaction costs (advisory, legal and DD fees for doing the deal). Transaction costs are expensed on the acquisition under Ind AS 103.53 and appear in Sources & Uses (t2). Integration costs are post-deal operating or exceptional costs of the combined group and appear in the projections.

Dis-synergies

Dis-synergies are value lost because of the combination:

  • Customer attrition: customers who dual-source for risk reasons may cut their share of wallet, and competitors of the acquirer who bought from the target may leave. Model it as lost revenue × contribution margin.
  • Retention bonuses and key-person costs: payments to keep the target's management and technical staff through integration. These are post-combination expenses, not part of consideration, when they are linked to continued service (Ind AS 103 B55).
  • Harmonisation of pay and benefits upwards: aligning the target's lower wage scales or gratuity and leave policies with the acquirer's.
  • Loss of incentives and status: MSME status (and its payment protection), state industrial incentives, export benefits or start-up tax holidays may be lost once the target becomes part of a larger group.
  • Regulatory remedies: CCI-imposed divestitures remove part of the revenue and the synergy base.
  • Contract change-of-control: customers or licensors may renegotiate terms on change of control.

Excel implementation

Synergy_t  =Runrate * INDEX(Phasing_row, MATCH(Year_t, Year_row, 0)) * Prob * StubFactor_t
StubFactor =IF(Year_t = Close_FY, (FY_End - Close_Date) / (FY_End - FY_Start), 1)

Keep CTA on a separate row with its own timing so that sensitivity tables can vary CTA timing independently of synergy phasing (t5 s05).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Front-loaded CTA, back-loaded synergy: Year 1 EPS can be dilutive even when the deal is clearly value-accretive. Show both views.
  • Restructuring provisions at acquisition: under Ind AS 103 the acquirer cannot recognise a liability for its own planned restructuring of the target in the PPA, unless it was the acquiree's present obligation at acquisition date. Integration provisions are post-combination expenses.
  • Retention vs consideration: retention payments to selling shareholders that are forfeited on leaving are remuneration. Payments not linked to service may be consideration.
  • Synergy slippage: a one-year delay in plant consolidation can cut synergy NPV by 10–15% at typical discount rates. Run a timing scenario.
  • Double counting with NCI: where the target is less than 100% owned, NCI shares in synergies that arise inside the target.

Key takeaways

  • Value depends on phased, risked synergies net of costs to achieve and dis-synergies, not run-rate headlines.
  • Phase by synergy type and adjust the first fiscal year for a stub period.
  • CTA are real cash costs, often 1.0–1.5x run-rate cost synergies, and must be in the value test even if brokers exclude them from adjusted EPS.
  • Integration costs (post-deal) are different from transaction costs (expensed on acquisition under Ind AS 103).
  • Dis-synergies include customer attrition, retention bonuses, pay harmonisation, lost incentives and CCI remedies.

Practice Questions

Q1. Phased synergies with a stub year [L2]

A deal closes on 1 January 2027 (FY ends 31 March). Run-rate cost synergies are ₹200 crore, phased 35% / 75% / 100% over the first three years after closing. Map the phasing to FY27, FY28, FY29 and FY30 assuming synergies accrue evenly within each anniversary year, and compute the synergy for each fiscal year.

💡 Hint: Each fiscal year can contain parts of two anniversary years.

Q2. Net synergy NPV [L2]

Run-rate cost synergies ₹90 crore (phasing 50% / 100%), revenue synergy EBITDA ₹30 crore (phasing 0% / 30% / 60% / 100%, probability 60%), customer attrition ₹8 crore a year from Year 1, retention bonuses ₹12 crore in each of Years 1 and 2, and CTA ₹70 crore in Year 1 and ₹40 crore in Year 2. Tax 25.17%, discount rate 12%, terminal growth 3% from Year 5. Compute the NPV of net synergies.

Q3. Classifying post-deal costs [L1]

Classify each as transaction cost, integration cost (CTA), dis-synergy or consideration: merchant banker fee for the open offer; ERP migration; ₹5 crore paid to the selling founder if she stays 18 months; legal fees for the SPA; rebranding of 300 outlets; VRS for 400 workers; loss of a customer contract worth ₹30 crore of revenue.

Q4. Restructuring provision at acquisition [L2]

At the acquisition date the target has announced nothing. The acquirer's integration plan foresees closing one target plant at a cost of ₹45 crore. The deal team wants to include a ₹45 crore provision in the PPA. Explain whether this is permitted and the impact on goodwill and post-deal P&L.

Q5. Integration scorecard [L3]

Design an integration tracking sheet for a horizontal cement deal that compares planned vs actual synergies, CTA and dis-synergies by initiative and quarter. Specify the columns, the RAG thresholds and how variances should roll into the quarterly board pack.
