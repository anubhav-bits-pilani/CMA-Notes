Subtopic note

Tax Structuring: Sec 72A Losses, Sec 47 Neutrality and Sec 50B

Scope: Tax structuring: Sec 72A carry-forward of losses on amalgamation (conditions on asset holding and business continuity), Sec 47 tax neutrality, Sec 50B slump sale

Why it matters

Tax can make or break a deal's economics. If an amalgamation qualifies, neither the companies nor the shareholders pay capital gains tax, and the target's accumulated losses can shelter the combined entity's profits. If it fails, the losses lapse and the transfer may be taxable. A slump sale triggers tax for the seller on a deemed fair-market-value consideration. These outcomes feed the synergy case (tax-loss synergies), the price negotiation (who bears the tax) and the post-deal tax line in the pro-forma P&L.

Date sensitivity. The section numbers in this note are those of the Income-tax Act 1961, which governs transactions up to 31 March 2026. The Income-tax Act 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 (tax year 2026-27) and broadly carries these provisions forward with new section numbers. Check the mapping table before you cite a provision for a current-year transaction.

Core notes

Amalgamation definition: Sec 2(1B)

For tax neutrality, an amalgamation must meet all of these:

  1. All property of the amalgamating company becomes property of the amalgamated company.
  2. All liabilities of the amalgamating company become liabilities of the amalgamated company.
  3. Shareholders holding at least three-fourths in value of shares in the amalgamating company (excluding shares already held by the amalgamated company or its subsidiary) become shareholders of the amalgamated company.

The acquisition of property by purchase, or on liquidation, is not an amalgamation.

Sec 47: transactions not regarded as transfer

Clause Transaction Condition
47(vi) Transfer of capital assets by the amalgamating company to the amalgamated company Amalgamated company is an Indian company
47(vii) Shareholder transfers shares in the amalgamating company for shares in the amalgamated company Allotment of shares in the amalgamated company (which is Indian); cash consideration for fractional entitlements is tolerated, but other cash consideration breaks neutrality for that shareholder
47(vib) Transfer of capital assets in a demerger by the demerged company to the resulting company Resulting company is Indian; demerger meets Sec 2(19AA)
47(vid) Shareholder receives shares of the resulting company in a demerger Allotment in consideration of the demerger

Consequences: the amalgamated company takes over the tax WDV of depreciable assets (Explanation 7 to Sec 43(1) for cost and Explanation 2 to Sec 43(6) for WDV), and shareholders' cost and holding period carry over to the new shares (Sec 49(2) and Sec 2(42A)). So a tax-neutral merger gives no tax step-up, which is why fair-value uplifts in the PPA create a DTL (t3 s05).

Sec 72A: carry-forward of losses on amalgamation

Sec 72A allows the accumulated business loss and unabsorbed depreciation of the amalgamating company to be treated as the amalgamated company's own loss and depreciation for the previous year in which the amalgamation was effected. The 8-year carry-forward period for business loss restarts from that year. Unabsorbed depreciation has no time limit.

Eligible amalgamations (Sec 72A(1)): the amalgamating company owns an industrial undertaking or a ship or a hotel, or is a banking company, or is a specified public-sector airline or telecom company, among other specified cases. "Industrial undertaking" means manufacturing or processing goods, computer software, power generation or distribution, telecom services, mining, or construction of ships, aircraft or rail systems. Pure service and trading companies generally do not qualify.

Conditions on the amalgamating (loss-making) company (Sec 72A(2)(a)):

  • It has been engaged in the business in which the loss or depreciation arose for 3 or more years.
  • It has held continuously, as on the date of amalgamation, at least three-fourths of the book value of fixed assets it held two years before the date of amalgamation.

Conditions on the amalgamated company (Sec 72A(2)(b) with Rule 9C):

  • It holds continuously, for at least 5 years from the date of amalgamation, at least three-fourths of the book value of fixed assets of the amalgamating company acquired in the scheme.
  • It continues the business of the amalgamating company for at least 5 years.
  • It achieves production of at least 50% of the installed capacity of that undertaking before the end of 4 years from the date of amalgamation, and maintains that until the end of 5 years. This applies to industrial undertakings, and the Central Government can relax it in specified cases.
  • It furnishes the prescribed audit report (Form 62) with the return of income.

Consequence of breach (Sec 72A(3)): if conditions are not met later, the set-off allowed is deemed to be the income of the amalgamated company for the year in which the default occurs. This is a clawback, not a simple disallowance going forward.

Related provisions: Sec 72A(4)–(5) for demergers (loss attributable to the undertaking transferred passes to the resulting company). Sec 72AA for banking amalgamations under government schemes. Sec 79: for closely held companies a change of more than 49% in beneficial voting power disallows carry-forward of business losses (not unabsorbed depreciation). IBC and certain start-up cases are exempt. Sec 79 is the relevant test in share purchases, Sec 72A in amalgamations.

Sec 50B: slump sale

Slump sale (Sec 2(42C), as amended by Finance Act 2021): transfer of one or more undertakings by any means for a lump-sum consideration without values being assigned to individual assets and liabilities. The 2021 amendment ("by any means") brings exchange and other non-sale transfers in.

Computation (Sec 50B):

\[ \text{Capital gain} = \text{FMV}_{\text{Rule 11UAE}} \text{ (deemed full value of consideration, if higher than actual)} - \text{Net worth of the undertaking} \]

  • Net worth = aggregate value of total assets of the undertaking minus liabilities of the undertaking as in the books, where depreciable assets are taken at tax WDV and other assets at book value. Self-generated goodwill is taken at nil, and any revaluation is ignored.
  • FMV: Rule 11UAE prescribes FMV1 (assets at specified values minus liabilities) and FMV2 (actual consideration). The higher applies.
  • Holding period: undertaking held for more than 36 months gives a long-term capital gain (taxed at 12.5% without indexation for transfers on or after 23 July 2024; earlier 20%). Otherwise short-term, at normal rates. Indexation is never available (Sec 50B(2)).
  • Report: a CA's report in Form 3CEA certifying net worth.

For the buyer, a slump sale gives a cost basis for depreciable assets (allocated on a reasonable basis, often supported by valuation), so plant and eligible intangibles generate tax depreciation. Goodwill acquired is not depreciable after Finance Act 2021 (t3 s08).

Structuring comparison

Route Seller tax Buyer tax base Losses Typical use
Amalgamation (Sec 232) Neutral under Sec 47 Carry-over (no step-up) Sec 72A if eligible Group consolidation, merger of loss-making manufacturer
Demerger Neutral under Sec 47 Carry-over Pass with the undertaking Separating businesses before sale
Slump sale Sec 50B on FMV − net worth Step-up on depreciable assets Stay with seller Sale of a division
Share purchase Capital gains on shares No change Sec 79 for closely held Most acquisitions

Modelling Sec 72A benefits

Model the loss pool as a schedule: opening losses, utilisation (capped by taxable profit of the combined entity, business losses first if they are expiring), closing losses and expiry. Tax saved = utilisation × tax rate. Present the value separately and attach a probability for compliance with the 5-year conditions.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Service companies: an IT services company is an industrial undertaking only if it is in "computer software". Services such as BPO or consulting may not qualify.
  • Asset disposal post-merger: selling more than 25% of the acquired fixed assets (by book value) within 5 years triggers Sec 72A(3) clawback. Integration plans that close plants must be checked against this.
  • Reverse merger: merging a profitable company into a loss-making one keeps the losses in the same entity (no Sec 72A needed). The tax authorities may question it under GAAR if the main purpose is a tax benefit.
  • GAAR (Chapter X-A): schemes whose main purpose is obtaining a tax benefit can be challenged.
  • Cash in a merger: cash consideration other than for fractional shares can take a shareholder outside Sec 47(vii) and make the exchange taxable for them.
  • Stamp duty and GST are separate from income-tax neutrality (t2 s06).

Key takeaways

  • Sec 2(1B) amalgamation plus Sec 47(vi) and (vii) gives tax neutrality at company and shareholder level, but no step-up in tax base.
  • Sec 72A lets losses and unabsorbed depreciation carry forward only for eligible undertakings and only if the 3-year business, 3/4 asset holding, 5-year continuity and 50% capacity conditions are met.
  • A breach of Sec 72A conditions claws back the set-off as income in the year of default.
  • Sec 50B taxes slump sales on the higher of Rule 11UAE FMV and actual consideration minus net worth, with no indexation.
  • Section numbers are those of the 1961 Act. Map them to the Income-tax Act 2025 for transactions from 1 April 2026.

Practice Questions

Q1. Sec 72A eligibility screen [L2]

Loss-making target: textile manufacturer, in business for 6 years. Book value of fixed assets two years before the amalgamation ₹400 crore; held on the date of amalgamation ₹280 crore (the rest sold). Business loss ₹150 crore, unabsorbed depreciation ₹90 crore. Is Sec 72A available? What would change if ₹320 crore were still held?

Q2. Clawback calculation [L2]

Losses of ₹200 crore were set off under Sec 72A in Years 1 and 2 (₹120 crore and ₹80 crore). In Year 3, the amalgamated company sells a plant representing 30% of the book value of the acquired fixed assets. Tax rate 25.17%. Explain and quantify the Year 3 consequence.

Q3. Slump sale gain [L2]

Undertaking: plant tax WDV ₹160 crore (book ₹210 crore), land book value ₹50 crore (revalued in the books to ₹120 crore), inventory ₹40 crore, debtors ₹35 crore, liabilities ₹70 crore. Actual consideration ₹380 crore; FMV1 under Rule 11UAE ₹420 crore. Held for 10 years. Compute net worth and the capital gain, and the tax at 12.5% (ignore surcharge and cess).

💡 Hint: Check which values Sec 50B uses for depreciable assets and what happens to revaluation.

Q4. Shareholder neutrality [L1]

In a merger, shareholders of the transferor receive 3 shares of the transferee for every 5 shares, plus ₹20 per share in cash. Is Sec 47(vii) available? What would you change in the scheme to preserve neutrality?

Q5. Structuring advice [L3]

A profitable listed FMCG company wants to acquire an unlisted, loss-making food-processing company with ₹350 crore of losses. Compare a share purchase, a share purchase followed by amalgamation, and a direct amalgamation on tax-loss availability, conditions, timing and risks. Recommend a route.
