Subtopic note

Due Diligence Focus Areas: QoE, Net Debt, NWC Peg and Beyond

Scope: DD focus areas: quality of earnings (normalised EBITDA), net debt and debt-like items, NWC peg, tax, legal, HR, IT, ESG

Why it matters

Price in most deals is set as EV = Multiple × normalised EBITDA, then bridged to equity by subtracting net debt and adjusting for NWC. Each of these three numbers is a DD output. A ₹10 crore overstatement of EBITDA at a 12x multiple is ₹120 crore of overpayment, and every unrecorded debt-like item reduces equity value rupee for rupee. For someone with audit and GL experience, financial DD is the natural entry into deal work: the skills are the same, but the question changes from "is it true and fair?" to "is it sustainable and is it priced?".

Core notes

Quality of earnings (QoE)

QoE turns reported EBITDA into a normalised (adjusted) EBITDA that reflects recurring, run-rate earnings of the business as it will be acquired. Common adjustments (c06):

Adjustment Direction Example
Non-recurring items Add back costs / remove gains One-off litigation settlement, plant fire loss, profit on sale of land, insurance claim
Related-party normalisation Usually reduce Promoter salary below or above market, rent-free use of promoter property, purchases from group firms at non-arm's-length prices
Ind AS 116 Reduce (for comparability) Lease rentals moved below EBITDA; deduct for "pre-Ind AS 116" EBITDA if the multiple is on that basis
Ind AS 115 Either Bill-and-hold, variable consideration, principal vs agent, year-end channel stuffing
Accounting policy alignment Either Capitalisation of R&D or overheads, inventory costing, provisioning policy
Run-rate / pro-forma Either Full-year effect of a price increase or plant commissioned mid-year; lost customer
Standalone costs Reduce Carve-outs: costs of services previously provided by the parent group

Also test: revenue cut-off, credit notes after year-end, provisions released to profit, capitalised costs, ESOP cost treatment and auditor qualifications or CARO remarks. Analyse monthly EBITDA (LTM) for seasonality and year-end spikes.

Net debt and debt-like items

The buyer pays EV and assumes or repays debt, so every claim that functions like debt reduces the equity price.

  • Financial debt: term loans, working-capital borrowings (CC/OD often treated as debt, depending on the peg definition), NCDs, ICDs, preference shares classified as liabilities, accrued interest, prepayment penalties.
  • Cash: only free cash. Exclude trapped cash (margin money, escrow, cash in subsidiaries with repatriation limits, unpaid dividend accounts) and cash needed for operations.
  • Debt-like items: unfunded gratuity and leave encashment, deferred consideration on earlier acquisitions, overdue statutory dues (GST, TDS, PF), disputed tax demands likely to crystallise, customer advances not linked to normal trade, capex creditors for completed assets, declared but unpaid dividends, Ind AS 116 lease liabilities (consistent with whether EBITDA is pre- or post-Ind AS 116), and MSME dues overdue beyond 45 days (also tax-relevant under Sec 43B(h)).
  • Cash-like items: income-tax refunds due, surplus non-operating assets.

NWC peg

The SPA defines a target (normal) level of net working capital, the peg, usually the average of the last 12 monthly balances to remove seasonality. At closing:

\[ \text{Equity price} = \text{EV} - \text{Net debt}_{\text{closing}} + (\text{NWC}_{\text{closing}} - \text{NWC}_{\text{peg}}) \]

If the seller runs down inventory or delays creditor payments before closing, NWC falls below the peg and the price is reduced. This stops the seller extracting cash through working capital. Definitions must be consistent: an item cannot sit in both NWC and net debt.

Illustration: EV ₹900 crore, closing net debt ₹220 crore, peg ₹140 crore, closing NWC ₹118 crore. Equity price = 900 − 220 + (118 − 140) = ₹658 crore.

Tax DD

  • Pending assessments, demands and litigation under income tax, GST and customs. Classify as probable, possible or remote, and decide on specific indemnity, escrow or a price reduction.
  • Availability of tax losses and MAT credit, and whether the structure preserves them (Sec 72A, Sec 79).
  • Transfer pricing exposures on related-party transactions; GST input credit eligibility and reconciliation (GSTR-2B vs books); TDS defaults (disallowance under Sec 40(a)(ia)).
  • Withholding tax on the purchase price if the seller is a non-resident (Sec 195), and Sec 281 (transfers during pending proceedings may be void against tax claims, so a Sec 281 certificate is often a CP).
  • Indirect transfer and stamp duty exposure on the structure.

Legal DD

Title to land and key assets, licences and environmental consents, material contracts (change-of-control, exclusivity), litigation, charges registered with the ROC, compliance with the Companies Act (related-party approvals under Sec 188, managerial remuneration limits), FEMA filings for foreign investment and IP ownership.

HR DD

Headcount and cost structure, key-person dependence, union agreements, contract labour compliance, PF/ESI compliance, gratuity and leave actuarial valuations (Ind AS 19), ESOP schemes and their treatment on change of control, and pending labour disputes. The four Labour Codes (in force from 21 November 2025) change wage definitions and gratuity bases, so check their impact on liabilities.

IT DD

ERP landscape and integration complexity, licence transferability, cybersecurity incidents, data-protection compliance (Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its 2025 Rules), dependence on group shared services, and the cost of separating systems in a carve-out (TSAs).

ESG DD

Environmental liabilities (contaminated land, pollution-control board notices), emissions and energy intensity, BRSR disclosures for listed entities (SEBI LODR Reg 34(2)(f)), BRSR Core assurance for the largest listed companies, governance red flags and supply-chain labour practices. ESG findings increasingly flow into the price, the CP list and post-deal capex.

From findings to the SPA

Finding Typical outcome
EBITDA overstated Price reduction (multiple × EBITDA adjustment)
Debt-like item quantified Deducted in the equity bridge
Contingent exposure (tax demand) Specific indemnity, escrow or holdback
Missing licence CP to closing
Ongoing risk (IT, ESG) Post-closing covenant, integration plan, capex in the model

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Consistency of the multiple: if comparable multiples are on post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA, keep EBITDA post-Ind AS 116 and treat lease liabilities as debt. Mixing the two creates double counting or omission.
  • Working-capital debt: CC/OD balances that swing with seasons can be put in the peg or in net debt, but not both. Agree definitions in the SPA.
  • Seasonal businesses: a March-end peg in a business that peaks in Q4 distorts NWC. Use a 12-month average.
  • Promoter-driven normalisation: removing promoter-related income that will not continue post-deal reduces EBITDA. Sellers present only the favourable adjustments.
  • Disputed tax demands: price deduction vs indemnity is a negotiation. Indemnities depend on the seller's creditworthiness, so escrows are often preferred.

Key takeaways

  • DD supplies the three inputs that set price: normalised EBITDA, net debt (including debt-like items) and the NWC peg.
  • QoE adjustments cover non-recurring items, related-party normalisation, Ind AS 116/115 effects and run-rate changes.
  • Debt-like items (gratuity deficits, overdue statutory dues, deferred consideration, probable tax demands) reduce equity value directly.
  • The NWC peg protects the buyer against cash extraction through working capital before closing.
  • Tax, legal, HR, IT and ESG findings convert into indemnities, escrows, CPs or price changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Normalised EBITDA [L2]

Reported EBITDA FY26 ₹185 crore includes: profit on sale of a warehouse ₹12 crore; a one-off flood loss ₹9 crore; promoter director salary ₹1 crore (market rate ₹4 crore); rent-free use of a promoter-owned office (market rent ₹3 crore a year); ₹8 crore of March sales reversed in April through credit notes; and Ind AS 116 lease rentals of ₹14 crore excluded from EBITDA. Compute Adjusted EBITDA on a post-Ind AS 116 basis and on a pre-Ind AS 116 basis.

Q2. Net debt and debt-like items [L2]

Target balance sheet: term loan ₹240 crore, CC ₹60 crore, cash ₹75 crore (of which ₹20 crore margin money and ₹5 crore unpaid dividend account), unfunded gratuity ₹18 crore, overdue GST ₹6 crore, income-tax refund due ₹9 crore, lease liabilities ₹40 crore, and a disputed tax demand of ₹30 crore assessed as 60% likely. EV is ₹1,100 crore on a post-Ind AS 116 basis; CC is in net debt. Compute adjusted net debt and the equity price.

💡 Hint: Decide first which cash is free and how to treat a probability-weighted exposure versus an indemnity.

Q3. NWC peg [L2]

Monthly NWC (₹ crore) for the last 12 months: 120, 128, 135, 142, 150, 146, 138, 132, 125, 118, 112, 105. EV is ₹750 crore, closing net debt ₹180 crore, and closing NWC ₹105 crore. Compute the peg as the 12-month average and the equity price. Explain why the seller might argue for a March-only peg.

Q4. DD finding to SPA mechanism [L2]

For each finding, recommend a price adjustment, specific indemnity, escrow, CP or post-closing covenant: (a) pollution-control board closure notice for one plant; (b) ₹45 crore GST demand under appeal; (c) ERP licence not transferable on change of control; (d) EBITDA overstated by ₹6 crore through capitalised salaries; (e) key customer contract with a change-of-control termination right.

Q5. Building a QoE workbook [L3]

Design a Power Query and Excel workflow that imports the target's monthly trial balances from the VDR (36 Excel files), maps them to a standard chart, produces monthly LTM EBITDA and NWC and flags year-end anomalies. Specify the queries, the mapping table and the checks.
