Subtopic note

Alternative Routes: IBC Resolution Plans and Sec 233 Fast-Track Mergers

Scope: Alternative routes: IBC resolution plans (Sec 29A eligibility), fast-track mergers under Sec 233

Why it matters

Not every Indian acquisition is a negotiated share purchase or a full NCLT scheme. Since 2016, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) has been a major acquisition channel in steel, power, cement, real estate and consumer businesses. Distressed assets are bought through resolution plans that give the buyer a clean slate. At the other end, group restructurings such as merging a wholly owned subsidiary into its parent no longer need a full NCLT process: Sec 233 lets the Regional Director approve them in a few months. Each route changes the price-setting process, the timeline, the accounting (often a bargain purchase in IBC cases) and the tax position.

Core notes

IBC resolution plans

Process outline. A financial creditor, operational creditor or the corporate debtor applies under Sec 7, 9 or 10. Once NCLT admits the application, the corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) starts and a moratorium applies (Sec 14). The resolution professional (RP) runs the company, collects claims and forms the committee of creditors (CoC) of financial creditors. The RP publishes an invitation for expressions of interest (Form G) with eligibility criteria, issues the information memorandum and the request for resolution plans (RFRP), and evaluates plans against an evaluation matrix.

  • Approval: CoC by at least 66% of voting share (Sec 30(4)), then NCLT approval under Sec 31.
  • Timeline: 180 days, extendable by up to 90 days, with an overall limit of 330 days including litigation (Sec 12). In practice many cases take longer.
  • Mandatory content (Sec 30(2)): payment of CIRP costs; operational creditors to get at least the amount they would receive in liquidation (or under the waterfall, if higher); dissenting financial creditors to get at least their liquidation value; management and implementation plan.
  • Clean slate: once approved, the plan binds all stakeholders including government authorities (Sec 31(1)). Claims not included in the plan are extinguished, as the Supreme Court held in Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel v Satish Kumar Gupta (2019) and Ghanashyam Mishra (2021). Sec 32A gives the corporate debtor immunity from liability for prior offences once control changes to a non-related party.

Sec 29A ineligibility. A person cannot submit a resolution plan if it, or any person acting jointly or in concert, or any connected person:

  • (a) is an undischarged insolvent;
  • (b) is a wilful defaulter under RBI guidelines;
  • (c) has an account classified as an NPA for one year or more and has not cleared overdue amounts with interest before submitting the plan. Financial entities that are not related parties of the corporate debtor are exempt, and there are exceptions for accounts acquired under IBC;
  • (d) has been convicted of an offence punishable with imprisonment of two years or more (seven years for offences under specified laws), until two years after release;
  • (e) is disqualified from acting as a director under the Companies Act;
  • (f) is prohibited by SEBI from trading in securities or accessing capital markets;
  • (g) was a promoter or in management of a corporate debtor in which a preferential, undervalued, extortionate or fraudulent transaction occurred and an NCLT order was made;
  • (h) has executed a guarantee for a creditor of a corporate debtor under IBC and the guarantee has been invoked and remains unpaid;
  • (i) is subject to any of the above disabilities in a jurisdiction outside India.

"Connected persons" include promoters and management of the applicant, holding companies, subsidiaries, associates and related parties, which is why former promoters generally cannot bid for their own companies. Sec 240A relaxes clauses (c) and (h) for MSMEs, so MSME promoters can bid if not otherwise ineligible. Resolution applicants file an affidavit of eligibility, and the RP and CoC verify it.

Pre-packaged insolvency (Sec 54A onwards): for MSMEs, a debtor-in-possession process with a base resolution plan, to be completed within 120 days.

Valuation and accounting for the bidder.

  • The RP appoints two registered valuers to determine fair value and liquidation value, which are shared with the CoC (not bidders) and anchor negotiations.
  • Bids are usually expressed as upfront cash plus deferred payments, equity to creditors and capex commitments. The model should compare the NPV of total payments with the EV of the business.
  • On acquisition, apply Ind AS 103 at fair value. Because creditors take large haircuts, fair value of net identifiable assets often exceeds consideration, giving a bargain purchase gain recognised in OCI and accumulated in capital reserve if there is clear evidence of the underlying reasons, or else directly in capital reserve (t3 s07). The forced-sale context is usually that evidence.
  • Debt waived under the plan creates gains in the corporate debtor's books, with tax implications. Sec 79 does not apply to companies whose resolution plans are approved under IBC (Sec 79(2)(c)), so losses survive the change in shareholding. The MAT provisions give relief for brought-forward losses and unabsorbed depreciation in IBC companies (Sec 115JB). Consider also the CBDT clarifications on Sec 41 and on waivers.

Fast-track mergers under Sec 233

Eligible classes (Sec 233(1) with Rule 25 and Rule 25A of the Companies (CAA) Rules 2016, as amended):

  • Two or more small companies (private companies within the Sec 2(85) paid-up capital and turnover limits prescribed by rules: ₹4 crore and ₹40 crore under the 2022 rules, with the Act allowing up to ₹10 crore and ₹100 crore, so check the current notification; holding/subsidiary companies and others as specified are excluded).
  • A holding company and its wholly owned subsidiary (the transferor is the WOS).
  • Two or more start-ups, or a start-up with a small company.
  • From 17 September 2024: a foreign holding company merging into its Indian wholly owned subsidiary (inbound reverse flip), subject to RBI and FEMA approvals.
  • Further classes notified in 2025: for example mergers between unlisted holding companies and subsidiaries (not only wholly owned) within borrowing limits, and between subsidiaries of the same holding company. Listed companies are excluded. Check the latest rule amendments for the exact limits.

Procedure.

  1. Board approval of the scheme; notice inviting objections from the ROC, Official Liquidator and other regulators (30 days).
  2. Declaration of solvency (Form CAA.10) by each company with the ROC.
  3. Approval by members holding at least 90% of the total number of shares (by number, not those present and voting) at a general meeting.
  4. Approval by creditors representing nine-tenths in value (meeting or written consent).
  5. Filing with the Regional Director, ROC and Official Liquidator. If no objections, the RD confirms the scheme. If the RD considers it against public interest or creditors' interest, it may refer the scheme to NCLT, which can apply the normal Sec 232 procedure.

Typical duration is 3–6 months, compared with 8–14 months for an NCLT scheme. The 90% member and 9/10 creditor approvals are tougher than the three-fourths test under Sec 230, but in intra-group cases they are easily met.

Accounting. Mergers of a WOS into its parent are common-control combinations accounted under Ind AS 103 Appendix C (pooling of interests at carrying amounts, t3 s07).

Tax. A Sec 233 merger is an amalgamation in substance. If Sec 2(1B) conditions are met, Sec 47 neutrality applies, and Sec 72A applies if the undertaking is eligible.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Connected-person reach: Sec 29A can disqualify a bidder through a related entity's NPA. Run a group-wide eligibility check early. A failed check late in the process wastes months.
  • Deferred payments in resolution plans: they are contingent or deferred consideration under Ind AS 103, measured at fair value at the acquisition date.
  • Plan implementation risk: approval can be challenged before NCLAT or the Supreme Court. Model a delayed close.
  • Fast-track referral: if the ROC or OL objects, the RD can refer the scheme to NCLT, losing the timing advantage. Clean compliance history helps.
  • Listed entities: Sec 233 excludes listed companies, so a listed parent merging a listed subsidiary must use Sec 230–232.

Key takeaways

  • IBC resolution plans need CoC approval by 66% and NCLT sanction, and they deliver a clean slate under Sec 31 and Sec 32A.
  • Sec 29A bars wilful defaulters, long-standing NPA holders, disqualified persons and their connected persons. MSMEs get partial relief under Sec 240A.
  • IBC acquisitions often produce bargain purchase gains, which go to capital reserve under Ind AS 103.
  • Sec 233 fast-track mergers suit small companies, start-ups, holding-WOS and other notified intra-group cases, with 90% member and 9/10 creditor approvals and RD confirmation.
  • Fast-track saves time but excludes listed companies, and the RD can refer the scheme to NCLT.

Practice Questions

Q1. Sec 29A screening [L2]

A bidder's group includes: (a) a sister company whose loan was classified as an NPA 14 months ago with ₹35 crore overdue; (b) a director disqualified under Sec 164(2) who resigned 3 years ago; (c) a holding company that guaranteed a loan of a corporate debtor now under CIRP, with the guarantee invoked and unpaid. Assess eligibility and the cure available for each.

Q2. Valuing a resolution plan bid [L2]

A bidder offers ₹2,400 crore: ₹1,500 crore upfront, ₹600 crore in equal instalments at the end of Years 1, 2 and 3 (at 9% interest on the outstanding balance), and 10% equity in the corporate debtor to creditors, valued at ₹300 crore. The bidder also commits ₹500 crore of capex in Year 1. EV of the business after capex is ₹3,600 crore. Discount rate 13%. Compute the PV of the bidder's total outlay and the implied value gap.

Q3. Bargain purchase in IBC [L2]

Consideration ₹1,800 crore; fair value of identifiable net assets after IBC haircuts ₹2,150 crore, after a DTL of ₹110 crore. Draft the acquisition journal entry and explain the capital reserve presentation and disclosure.

Q4. Fast-track eligibility [L1]

Which of these can use Sec 233? (a) merger of two private companies each with paid-up capital ₹3 crore and turnover ₹35 crore, neither a holding or subsidiary; (b) a listed parent merging a 100% unlisted subsidiary; (c) a Delaware parent merging into its Indian WOS; (d) two DPIIT-recognised start-ups.

Q5. Route selection for a group simplification [L3]

A listed parent has three unlisted WOS and one 80% unlisted subsidiary. It wants to simplify the structure within 9 months. Recommend routes for each entity, explain the approvals, accounting under Appendix C, and tax neutrality checks.
