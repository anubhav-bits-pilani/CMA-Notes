Subtopic note

The M&A Deal Process from NDA to Closing

Scope: Deal process: NDA, teaser, CIM, non-binding offer, VDR access, confirmatory DD, binding offer, SPA/SHA, conditions precedent, closing

Why it matters

FP&A and finance professionals usually join a deal at a specific stage: building the model for the non-binding offer, answering VDR questions on the sell side, or preparing the closing balance sheet. Knowing the sequence tells you what information is available at each stage, how precise the model needs to be, and which numbers become contractually binding. In India the process also has regulatory waypoints (SAST public announcement, CCI filing, NCLT sanction) that set the timeline and affect conditions precedent.

Core notes

The typical auction (sell-side led) process

Stage Document / event What finance does Typical timing
1. Preparation Vendor DD, information memorandum, data room build Sell side: normalise EBITDA, prepare business plan 6–10 weeks
2. Marketing Teaser (anonymous 1–2 page summary) Buy side: quick screen against strategy Week 0
3. Confidentiality NDA Legal: scope, non-solicit, standstill Week 1
4. Information CIM / information memorandum, management projections Buy side: outside-in model, trading comps, preliminary DCF Weeks 2–5
5. First round Non-binding offer (NBO) / indicative offer Price range, structure, key assumptions, funding Week 5–6
6. Second round VDR access, management presentations, site visits Confirmatory DD, refined model, synergy case Weeks 6–12
7. Final offer Binding offer with mark-up of draft SPA Final price, adjustments, funding certainty Week 12–14
8. Signing SPA / SHA executed; for listed targets, SAST public announcement Board approvals, disclosure under LODR Reg 30 Signing date
9. Interim Conditions precedent (CPs) satisfied: CCI, open offer, lender consents Interim covenants monitoring, completion accounts prep 2–9 months
10. Closing Payment, share transfer, board reconstitution Closing balance sheet, PPA kick-off, funds flow Closing date

In a bilateral (negotiated) deal the stages are the same in substance but there is no competitive auction pressure, and exclusivity is usually granted after a term sheet.

Key documents

Teaser. A short, often anonymised overview: sector, scale (revenue band, EBITDA margin), investment highlights. Purpose: generate interest without revealing identity.

NDA (non-disclosure agreement). Covers confidentiality of information, permitted use, return or destruction of documents, non-solicitation of employees and, for listed targets, a standstill restricting the bidder from buying shares in the market. For listed companies, sharing unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI) is allowed only for legitimate purposes under SEBI PIT Regulations (Reg 3) with a board-approved structure, and recipients become insiders. The deal team must keep a structured digital database of UPSI recipients.

CIM (confidential information memorandum). A detailed document covering business description, market, historical financials (often 3 years plus current year), management projections, normalised EBITDA bridge, organisation and process letter. Treat projections as a sell-side case.

Non-binding offer. States an indicative value (often as EV with a net debt and NWC basis), structure (share or asset deal, percentage acquired), funding sources, key assumptions, DD scope, approvals needed and timetable. It is "non-binding" except for confidentiality and exclusivity clauses, if any.

VDR (virtual data room). An online repository of legal, financial, tax, HR, IT and commercial documents. Access is tiered: "clean team" access for competitively sensitive data in horizontal deals (to avoid gun-jumping and information exchange concerns under competition law). Finance tracks Q&A logs, which often reveal the real issues.

Confirmatory DD. Detailed financial, tax, legal, commercial, HR, IT and ESG review (s05). Outputs are price adjustments, specific indemnities, CPs and SPA warranties.

Binding offer. Firm price and a marked-up SPA. Usually backed by evidence of funding (commitment letters from banks, board approval).

SPA (share purchase agreement). Covers price and price mechanism (locked box or completion accounts), representations and warranties, indemnities (general and specific, for example a known tax demand), limitations (de minimis, basket, cap, time limits), CPs, interim covenants (conduct of business between signing and closing), termination rights (long-stop date, MAC clause) and escrow or holdback.

SHA (shareholders' agreement). Needed when the seller keeps a stake or there are co-investors: board seats, reserved matters, transfer restrictions (ROFR, tag-along, drag-along), put/call options and exit. In listed companies, special rights for shareholders need shareholder approval by special resolution under LODR Reg 31B (inserted 2023) and periodic renewal.

Conditions precedent

Typical CPs in Indian deals:

  • CCI approval (or confirmation that no filing is needed), as the Competition Act prohibits closing before approval (standstill obligation).
  • Completion of the SAST open offer, or the escrow and offer process reaching the stage where the SPA can complete under Reg 22 (s06).
  • Sector regulator approvals: RBI (banks, NBFCs), IRDAI (insurers), SEBI (intermediaries), TRAI/DoT.
  • Government approval for FDI under the approval route, including Press Note 3 (2020) for investors from countries sharing a land border with India.
  • Lender consents and release of charges; change-of-control waivers from key customers.
  • No material adverse change; warranties true at closing (bring-down).

Closing mechanics

At closing: the buyer pays the price (net of escrow), shares are transferred (demat instruction, stamp duty paid), resignations and appointments of directors happen, and lender charges are released. Finance prepares a funds flow memorandum that mirrors the Sources & Uses (t2): who pays whom, which accounts, and which fees. The acquisition date for Ind AS 103 is usually the closing date, when control passes (t3 s01).

Price mechanisms

  • Locked box: price fixed by reference to a historical balance sheet (the locked-box date). No post-closing adjustment. The seller covenants against "leakage" (dividends, management fees) and may charge a ticker (interest) from the locked-box date to closing.
  • Completion accounts: price adjusted after closing for actual net debt and NWC against the agreed peg (s05).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Gun-jumping: integrating operations, exchanging pricing data or exercising control before CCI approval can attract penalties of up to 1% of total turnover or assets. Clean-team protocols are needed in horizontal deals.
  • UPSI and listed targets: DD of a listed target requires a board-approved process, and the information must be made public (generally via the open-offer documents) before the acquirer trades.
  • MAC clauses are narrowly interpreted. Do not rely on them for business downturns.
  • Long-stop date: set it with enough time for CCI (up to 150 days for Phase II-type review) and NCLT timelines. A scheme can take 9–15 months.
  • NBO precision: the NBO price is usually EV-based. If net debt or NWC differs at DD, the equity price changes even though the "headline" EV stays the same.

Key takeaways

  • The sequence (teaser, NDA, CIM, NBO, VDR and confirmatory DD, binding offer, SPA/SHA, CPs, closing) sets what information and model precision are available.
  • NDA and PIT compliance are critical for listed targets. Recipients of UPSI become insiders.
  • The SPA turns DD findings into price adjustments, indemnities, escrows and CPs.
  • Indian CPs typically include CCI approval, SAST open offer, sector regulators, FDI approvals and lender consents.
  • The funds flow at closing mirrors the Sources & Uses table. The acquisition date drives PPA.

Practice Questions

Q1. Process sequencing [L1]

Put these in order and say what finance produces at each step: binding offer; VDR access; teaser; closing; CIM; SPA signing; NBO; NDA; CCI approval.

Q2. NBO equity value adjustment [L2]

An NBO values the target at an EV of ₹1,200 crore on a cash-free, debt-free basis with a normalised NWC of ₹150 crore. Confirmatory DD finds net debt of ₹310 crore (not ₹260 crore as in the CIM), debt-like items of ₹25 crore (unpaid gratuity, disputed tax), and expected NWC at closing of ₹128 crore. Compute the equity price at the NBO basis and after DD.

Q3. Drafting CPs [L2]

A listed Indian acquirer is buying 51% of an unlisted NBFC whose promoter holds 15% of a listed subsidiary. Draft a list of CPs and explain why each is needed.

Q4. Locked box vs completion accounts [L2]

Explain how each mechanism allocates risk between buyer and seller between the reference date and closing. When would you, as buyer's FP&A lead, prefer completion accounts?

Q5. Gun-jumping case [L3]

Before CCI approval, the acquirer's sales head asks the target to share customer-wise pricing and to align discount policies "to be ready on Day 1". Advise on the risks and design a clean-team protocol.
