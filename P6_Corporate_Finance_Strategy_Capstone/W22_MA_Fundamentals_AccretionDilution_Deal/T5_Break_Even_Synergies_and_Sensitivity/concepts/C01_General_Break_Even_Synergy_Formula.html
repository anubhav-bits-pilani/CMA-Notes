Key concept

General Break-Even Synergy Formula

Concept: Required pre-tax synergies S = [Acquirer standalone EPS × Pro-forma shares − Pro-forma PAT excluding synergies] ÷ (1 − t)

Statement

\[ S^{*} = \frac{\text{EPS}_{A} \times (N_{A} + N_{\text{new}}) - \text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S}}{1 - t} \]

where:

  • \(S^{*}\) = pre-tax synergies required for pro-forma EPS to equal standalone EPS in the year;
  • \(\text{EPS}_{A}\) = acquirer standalone forecast EPS for the same year (s01 definition);
  • \(N_{A} + N_{\text{new}}\) = pro-forma weighted diluted shares (standalone plus consideration and equity-raise shares);
  • \(\text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S}\) = pro-forma PAT attributable to owners with synergies set to zero (acquirer PAT + target PAT − after-tax foregone interest, new interest, PPA amortisation and fees − NCI);
  • \(t\) = tax rate applicable to the synergies.

If \(S^{*} \le 0\), the deal is accretive without synergies; report break-even as nil and show the surplus.

Explanation

The numerator is the PAT gap: the earnings the enlarged share base needs to hold EPS at the standalone level, minus what the combined business earns before synergies. Dividing by \((1-t)\) converts the after-tax gap into pre-tax operating synergies, the unit in which management guides. The formula is valid for any funding mix — cash, debt, stock or combination — because the share count captures stock dilution and the PAT term captures funding costs.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Linearity. It assumes PAT is linear in synergies. Rating ratchets, tax-deductibility caps or synergy-linked NCI acceptance break linearity; use Goal Seek (c05) as a check.
  • NCI. If synergies arise in a partly owned subsidiary, divide by \((1-t)(1 - \text{NCI}\%)\).
  • Different tax rate. Use the rate where synergies arise (e.g. a foreign target).
  • Revenue synergies. \(S^{*}\) is an EBIT-level figure; required revenue = \(S^{*}\) ÷ contribution margin.
  • Basis consistency. Reported vs adjusted EPS, full-year vs time-weighted — the same basis on both terms.

Illustration

Mixed deal: Bharat Polymers pays ₹2,700 crore for Deccan Packaging, 50% in shares at ₹500 (2.7 crore new shares) and 50% in debt at 9%. Standalone EPS ₹20, 60 crore shares. Target PAT ₹150 crore; after-tax PPA ₹60 crore; tax 25%.

\[ \text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S} = 1{,}200 + 150 - 60 - 1{,}350 \times 9\% \times 0.75 = 1{,}290 - 91.13 = 1{,}198.88 \]

\[ S^{*} = \frac{20 \times 62.7 - 1{,}198.88}{0.75} = \frac{1{,}254.00 - 1{,}198.88}{0.75} = ₹73.5 \text{ crore} \]

Comparison: all-stock \(S^{*}\) = ₹24.0 crore; 60/40 debt/cash \(S^{*}\) = ₹100.9 crore (topic 5 s02).

Practice Questions

Q1. Mixed funding break-even [L2]

Acquirer standalone EPS ₹36, 80 crore shares, share price ₹720. Target equity price ₹4,320 crore: 40% stock, 35% new debt at 9.1%, 25% cash yielding 6.9%. Target PAT ₹205 crore; after-tax PPA ₹48 crore; fee amortisation ₹3 crore pre-tax; tax 25.168%. Compute break-even pre-tax synergies.

Q2. Synergies in a 60%-owned target [L2]

An acquirer owns 60% of a target after an open offer. \(\text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S}\) (after NCI) is ₹1,480 crore; standalone PAT ₹1,520 crore; no new shares; tax 25%. All synergies arise in the target. Compute break-even synergies, and compare with the case where synergies arise in the acquirer.

💡 Hint: Only the parent's share of target-level synergies reaches owners.

Q3. Negative break-even [L1]

A deal has \(\text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S}\) of ₹845 crore, 41 crore pro-forma shares and standalone EPS of ₹20.20. Compute \(S^{*}\), interpret the result, and state how it should be reported in the board paper.
