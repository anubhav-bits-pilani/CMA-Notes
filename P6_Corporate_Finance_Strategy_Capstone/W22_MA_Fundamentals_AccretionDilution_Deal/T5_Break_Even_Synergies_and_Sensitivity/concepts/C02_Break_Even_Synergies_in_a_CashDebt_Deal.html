Key concept

Break-Even Synergies in a Cash/Debt Deal

Concept: Cash/debt deal (no new shares): S = max[0, (Foregone interest + New debt interest + PPA amortisation) × (1 − t) − Target PAT] ÷ (1 − t)

Statement

\[ S^{*} = \frac{\max\left[0,\; (I_{c} + I_{d} + A_{PPA})(1-t) - \text{PAT}_{T}\right]}{1-t} \]

where \(I_{c}\) = pre-tax foregone interest on cash used, \(I_{d}\) = pre-tax interest on new debt (including fee amortisation if not shown separately), \(A_{PPA}\) = pre-tax PPA amortisation and step-up depreciation, \(\text{PAT}_{T}\) = target PAT included in the year (time-weighted, after NCI if relevant), and \(t\) = tax rate.

Explanation

With no new shares, EPS-neutrality means pro-forma PAT equals acquirer standalone PAT. The deal adds target PAT and subtracts the after-tax "carrying cost" of the acquisition. The break-even synergy simply covers the shortfall, grossed up for tax:

\[ \text{Carrying cost}_{\text{AT}} = (I_{c} + I_{d} + A_{PPA})(1-t) \]

If target PAT already exceeds the after-tax carrying cost, the deal is accretive without synergies and the floor at zero applies. Equivalently, using topic 4 c05, the shortfall is \(\text{Price} \times (k_{\text{acq}} - y_{T}^{\text{adj}})\) where \(y_{T}^{\text{adj}}\) is the target's post-PPA earnings yield at the offer.

Points to watch:

  • Add all deal costs to the bracket: financing-fee amortisation, commitment fees, interest on refinanced target debt (net of the reversal of target's own interest), lost interest on target cash swept.
  • Non-deductible interest. Use \(I_{d}\) without the \((1-t)\) factor for the non-deductible portion; the formula becomes \(\max[0, (I_{c} + A_{PPA})(1-t) + I_{d}^{\text{non-ded}} + I_{d}^{\text{ded}}(1-t) - \text{PAT}_{T}] / (1-t)\).
  • Year 2. \(I_{d}\) falls with debt repayment; \(\text{PAT}_{T}\) grows — so \(S^{*}\) usually falls (s02).
  • Equity raise. If "cash" comes from a QIP, new shares exist and the general formula (c01) must be used.

Illustration

60% debt / 40% cash acquisition of Deccan Packaging (₹2,700 crore), tax 25%.

₹ crore Year 1 Year 2
\(I_{c}\) (1,080 × 6.5%) 70.20 70.20
\(I_{d}\) incl. fee amortisation 150.66 114.66
\(A_{PPA}\) 80.00 80.00
Carrying cost after tax 225.65 198.65
Target PAT 150.00 162.00
Shortfall 75.65 36.65
\(S^{*}\) 100.9 48.9

Year 2 debt is ₹1,220 crore after a ₹400 crore repayment from target cash flow. If 30% of Year 1 interest were non-deductible, the Year 1 shortfall would rise by 30% × 150.66 × 25% = ₹11.30 crore, and \(S^{*}\) to ₹115.9 crore.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the formula [L1]

Cash used ₹600 crore at 7%; new debt ₹1,400 crore at 9.6%; PPA amortisation ₹52 crore; target PAT ₹105 crore; tax 25.168%. Compute break-even pre-tax synergies.

Q2. Year 2 with debt paydown [L2]

In Q1, ₹350 crore of debt is repaid at the end of Year 1 from target cash flow, target PAT grows 10%, and PPA amortisation is unchanged. Compute Year 2 break-even, and recompute assuming the repayment came instead from acquirer treasury cash that was earning 7%.

💡 Hint: In the second case the interest saved is partly offset by additional foregone interest.

Q3. When is break-even zero? [L2]

Derive the maximum PPA amortisation (pre-tax) at which the deal in Q1 is EPS-neutral with zero synergies, and explain what that implies about the share of premium allocated to amortising intangibles versus goodwill.
