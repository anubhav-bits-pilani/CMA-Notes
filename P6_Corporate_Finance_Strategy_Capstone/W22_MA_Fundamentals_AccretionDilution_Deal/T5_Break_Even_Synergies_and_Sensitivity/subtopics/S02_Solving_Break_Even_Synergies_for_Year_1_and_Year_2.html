Subtopic note

Solving Break-Even Synergies for Year 1 and Year 2

Scope: Solving break-even synergies algebraically and with Goal Seek; Year 1 vs Year 2 differences from debt paydown and synergy phasing

Why it matters

Break-even synergy is the single number that best summarises how demanding a deal is. Boards compare it with management's synergy guidance; analysts compare it with peer deals; lenders use it to judge whether the equity story depends on heroic assumptions. It must be computed exactly and dynamically: exactly, because a ₹10 crore error on a ₹100 crore break-even is a 10% misstatement of the cushion; dynamically, because the number must update every time the offer price, funding mix or interest rate changes during negotiation. And it must be shown for Year 1 and Year 2, because the financing burden and the synergy ramp change materially between them.

Core notes

Algebraic solution — general case

Write pro-forma PAT as PAT excluding synergies plus after-tax synergies:

\[ \text{PAT}_{PF} = \text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S} + S(1-t) \]

Setting pro-forma EPS equal to standalone EPS (s01):

\[ \frac{\text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S} + S(1-t)}{N_{A} + N_{\text{new}}} = \text{EPS}_{A} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S^{*} = \frac{\text{EPS}_{A}\,(N_{A}+N_{\text{new}}) - \text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S}}{1-t} \]

This is concept c01. \(S^{*}\) is pre-tax; a negative value means the deal is accretive with zero synergies (report as nil, with the "headroom" shown separately).

NCI complication. If synergies arise in a partly owned target, only \((1 - \text{NCI}\%)\) of them reach owners, so divide by \((1-t)(1-\text{NCI}\%)\) for target-level synergies.

Cash/debt special case. With \(N_{\text{new}} = 0\), \(\text{EPS}_{A} N_{A} = \text{PAT}_{A}\), and the formula reduces to concept c02:

\[ S^{*} = \frac{\max\left[0,\;(I_{c} + I_{d} + A_{PPA} + F)(1-t) - \text{PAT}_{T}\right]}{1-t} \]

Goal Seek

Goal Seek (Data → What-If Analysis → Goal Seek) finds the input that sets a formula cell to a target value:

  • Set cell: a difference cell =PF_EPS - SA_EPS (better than setting EPS to a typed value, which would become stale).
  • To value: 0.
  • By changing cell: the synergy input for that year.

Goal Seek overwrites the input with a constant, so it is a check on the algebraic formula, not a substitute (concept c05). For multi-year break-evens, run it once per year with that year's synergy input, or automate with a short macro.

Why Year 2 differs from Year 1

Driver Direction of Year 2 break-even Mechanism
Debt paydown from cash flow Lower Lower average balance → lower interest
Target PAT growth Lower More earnings to cover a largely fixed financing cost
Acquirer PAT growth Higher (stock deals) Standalone EPS benchmark rises, so new shares need more earnings
Synergy phasing Not in break-even, but in the cushion Guided synergies ramp (e.g. 40% → 80% → 100%), so the gap to break-even widens
Costs to achieve Lower (if front-loaded in Year 1) Fewer one-offs in Year 2 if they are included in the neutrality definition
Fee write-off on refinancing Higher in the refinancing year Unamortised fees expensed
Floating-rate resets Either Rate path
PPA amortisation Usually flat; lower once inventory step-up has flowed through Inventory step-up hits Year 1 only

The cash used to repay debt matters: if repayments come from target cash flow, the full interest saving is a deal benefit. If they come from acquirer cash that would otherwise have earned \(r_{c}\), the net benefit is only \((r_{d} - r_{c})\) on the amount repaid — an integrated three-statement model captures this automatically, a stand-alone accretion tab does not.

Worked illustration

Bharat Polymers / Deccan Packaging, 60% debt (₹1,620 crore at 9%) / 40% cash (₹1,080 crore at 6.5%), PPA amortisation ₹80 crore, fee amortisation ₹4.86 crore, tax 25%. Year 1 target cash flow repays ₹400 crore of debt at year-end.

₹ crore Year 1 (FY27) Year 2 (FY28)
Acquirer standalone PAT (= EPS × 60 crore shares) 1,200.00 1,320.00
Target PAT 150.00 162.00
Foregone interest (pre-tax) 70.20 70.20
New debt interest (pre-tax) 145.80 109.80
PPA amortisation 80.00 80.00
Fee amortisation 4.86 4.86
Total pre-tax deal costs 300.86 264.86
After-tax deal costs 225.65 198.65
PAT shortfall = after-tax costs − target PAT 75.65 36.65
Break-even pre-tax synergies = shortfall ÷ 0.75 100.9 48.9
Guided synergies (phased) 100.0 160.0
Cushion (0.9) 111.1

Year 1 is just short of neutral; Year 2 is comfortably accretive because interest falls ₹36 crore, target PAT grows ₹12 crore and guided synergies ramp up.

Check with the general formula for Year 1: \(S^{*} = (20 \times 60 - 1{,}124.35) \div 0.75 = 100.87\).

All-stock comparison

All-stock at ₹500 (5.4 crore new shares): Year 1 PAT ex-synergies = 1,200 + 150 − 60 = ₹1,290 crore; \(S^{*} = (20 \times 65.4 - 1{,}290) \div 0.75 = ₹24.0\) crore. Year 2 (acquirer EPS ₹22, target PAT ₹162 crore): \(S^{*} = (22 \times 65.4 - 1{,}422) \div 0.75 = ₹22.4\) crore. Stock break-even barely moves between years because there is no debt to repay and acquirer EPS growth raises the bar for new shares.

Excel implementation

Break-even synergies (Year n):
=MAX(0, (SA_EPS * (SA_Shares + New_Shares) - PF_PAT_exSyn) / (1 - Tax_Rate))

Check cell:
=ROUND((PF_PAT_exSyn + BE_Syn * (1 - Tax_Rate)) / (SA_Shares + New_Shares) - SA_EPS, 6)

The check cell must return 0 (or the deal is accretive without synergies and the MAX floor is binding).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Synergy-dependent items. If synergies change working capital or tax rates, or if lenders' margin ratchets depend on leverage (which depends on synergies), PAT is no longer linear in \(S\) and Goal Seek (or iteration) is required.
  • Losses at target. Negative target PAT increases the shortfall; break-even is still well defined.
  • Revenue synergies. The break-even is in EBIT terms; to express it as revenue, divide by the contribution margin.
  • Non-deductible interest. If some interest is non-deductible, after-tax costs rise, but synergies still taxed at \(t\) — the shortfall widens and the break-even rises disproportionately.
  • Time-weighted Year 1. For a mid-year closing, break-even on the reported basis is for the stub period; annualise for comparison with run-rate guidance.

Key takeaways

  • \(S^{*} = [\text{EPS}_{A}(N_{A}+N_{\text{new}}) - \text{PAT}_{PF}^{\text{ex-}S}] / (1-t)\); for cash/debt deals it reduces to after-tax deal costs minus target PAT, grossed up.
  • Compute break-even with a live formula; use Goal Seek on a difference cell only to verify.
  • Year 2 break-even typically falls in debt deals (deleveraging, target growth) and stays flat or rises in stock deals.
  • Compare break-even with phased guided synergies each year to show the cushion.
  • Divide by \((1-\text{NCI}\%)\) as well when synergies arise in a partly owned target.

Practice Questions

Q1. Year 1 and Year 2 break-even [L2]

Acquirer standalone PAT ₹1,050 crore (Year 1) and ₹1,150 crore (Year 2), 70 crore shares. Target PAT ₹130 crore and ₹140 crore. Funding: ₹1,000 crore cash at 7% yield and ₹1,800 crore loan at 9.4%, of which ₹450 crore is repaid at the end of Year 1 from target cash flow. PPA amortisation ₹75 crore per year; fee amortisation ₹6 crore. Tax 25.168%. Compute break-even pre-tax synergies for both years and explain the change.

Q2. Stock deal break-even [L2]

Acquirer EPS ₹30 (Year 1) and ₹34 (Year 2), 50 crore shares, share price ₹750. It issues shares for 100% of a target worth ₹3,000 crore. Target PAT ₹120 crore (Year 1) and ₹126 crore (Year 2); after-tax PPA amortisation ₹30 crore. Tax 25%. Compute break-even synergies for both years and explain why they rise.

Q3. NCI and synergy location [L2]

Repeat Q1 assuming the acquirer owns 70% of the target (consideration and debt scaled to 70%) and all synergies arise at the target. Compute the Year 1 break-even and compare it with the 100% case.

💡 Hint: Target-level synergies reach owners only after both tax and the NCI share.

Q4. Goal Seek vs formula [L1]

List the exact Goal Seek inputs you would use to verify Q1's Year 2 break-even, and explain two ways in which relying on Goal Seek alone would make the model unreliable.
