Subtopic note

Scenario Overlays: Synergy Realisation, Cost Timing, Rating Risk

Scope: Scenario overlays: synergy realisation % (50/75/100%), cost-to-achieve timing, rating downgrade spread

Why it matters

Sensitivity tables move one or two inputs mechanically. Scenarios tell coherent stories: "integration goes badly — only half the synergies arrive, costs are higher and later, and the rating agency downgrades us". Investment committees and lenders ask for exactly these combined downsides. Empirical studies and practitioner surveys consistently show that many acquirers fall short on synergy delivery, revenue synergies more often than cost synergies, and that costs to achieve frequently exceed plan. A deal that is neutral only at 100% realisation, with costs to achieve excluded and the current spread held constant, is a fragile deal. Overlays make that fragility visible.

Core notes

Overlay 1 — synergy realisation %

Apply a realisation factor to guided synergies:

\[ S_{y}^{\text{scenario}} = \rho \times S_{y}^{\text{guided}} \qquad \rho \in \{50\%, 75\%, 100\%\} \]

Refinements:

  • Separate factors by type. Cost synergies are more controllable (procurement, plant consolidation, SG&A); revenue synergies (cross-sell, pricing, distribution) are less certain and should carry a lower factor or be excluded from the base case.
  • Timing slip as well as level. A "delay" scenario shifts the phasing curve one year to the right rather than reducing run-rate.
  • Dis-synergies. Customer attrition and retention bonuses often scale with integration intensity; include them in the downside.
  • Break-even reframed. The required realisation % = break-even synergies ÷ guided synergies; if it exceeds ~80% in Year 1, the deal depends on near-perfect execution.

Overlay 2 — cost-to-achieve timing

One-time integration costs (severance, plant closure costs, IT migration, rebranding, consultants) are expensed as incurred under Ind AS (restructuring provisions only when Ind AS 37 criteria — a detailed formal plan and a valid expectation in those affected — are met). Their timing drives reported EPS:

  • Front-loaded (most in Year 1): Year 1 reported EPS hit is large; Year 2 clean.
  • Even split across Years 1–2.
  • Delayed (most in Year 2): smaller Year 1 hit, but synergies usually arrive later too — link cost timing and synergy phasing rather than moving one without the other.

Total costs to achieve are commonly benchmarked at 1–1.5x run-rate cost synergies; also run a "cost overrun" case (e.g. +25%). Present run-rate EPS (excluding these costs) and reported EPS separately; the board must see both.

Overlay 3 — rating downgrade spread

A debt-funded deal may move the acquirer's leverage beyond rating-agency thresholds (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings publish sector-wise guidance on leverage and coverage). A downgrade raises:

  • the spread on the new acquisition debt (if priced after the rating action, or via a ratings-linked margin ratchet);
  • the cost of existing floating-rate debt with ratings-linked pricing, and refinancing costs of maturing debt;
  • possibly triggers covenants or rating-based prepayment clauses in existing NCDs.

\[ \Delta I = D_{\text{new}} \times \Delta s_{\text{new}} + D_{\text{exist, repricing}} \times \Delta s_{\text{exist}} \]

where \(\Delta s\) are spread increases. The after-tax EPS hit is \(\Delta I (1-t) / N\), and break-even synergies rise by \(\Delta I\) pre-tax (for debt deals).

Worked illustration

Bharat Polymers / Deccan Packaging, 60/40 debt/cash, guided synergies ₹100 crore (Year 1) and ₹160 crore (Year 2), costs to achieve ₹120 crore pre-tax, tax 25%. Pro-forma PAT ex-synergies and ex-costs: ₹1,124.35 crore (Year 1) and ₹1,283.36 crore (Year 2). Standalone EPS ₹20 and ₹22; 60 crore shares.

Realisation (run-rate, excluding costs to achieve):

Realisation Year 1 EPS Year 1 accretion Year 2 EPS Year 2 accretion
50% 19.36 (3.2%) 22.39 1.8%
75% 19.68 (1.6%) 22.89 4.0%
100% 19.99 (0.1%) 23.39 6.3%

Cost-to-achieve timing (100% realisation, reported basis):

Timing (Y1 / Y2) Year 1 EPS Year 1 accretion Year 2 EPS Year 2 accretion
100% / 0% 18.49 (7.6%) 23.39 6.3%
50% / 50% 19.24 (3.8%) 22.64 2.9%
25% / 75% 19.61 (1.9%) 22.26 1.2%

Rating downgrade: one notch adds 40 bps on the ₹1,620 crore new loan and 25 bps on ₹3,000 crore of existing floating debt: \(\Delta I = 6.48 + 7.50 = ₹13.98\) crore pre-tax, ₹10.49 crore after tax, −₹0.17 EPS. Year 1 accretion at 100% realisation falls to (0.9%), and Year 1 break-even synergies rise from ₹100.9 crore to ₹114.9 crore.

Combining overlays

Build a scenario table where each named scenario sets all three overlays:

Scenario Realisation Cost timing Cost overrun Downgrade
Base 100% 50/50 0% No
Management 110% 75/25 0% No
Downside 75% 50/50 +25% No
Severe 50% 25/75 with 1-year synergy delay +25% Yes

Drive the model with a single scenario selector:

=INDEX(Scenario_Table[Realisation], MATCH(Selected_Scenario, Scenario_Table[Scenario], 0))

and use a data table with the scenario number as the single input to show all scenarios side by side without switching manually (a one-variable data table works well here).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Correlation between overlays. Poor integration typically means lower realisation, higher and later costs and more dis-synergies together — do not combine "best" and "worst" inputs randomly.
  • Probability weighting. Some boards ask for an expected-value case; weight scenarios explicitly and disclose the weights.
  • Downgrade feedback. Lower synergies raise leverage, which raises the downgrade probability — model the downgrade as triggered by a leverage threshold rather than as an independent switch for internal analysis.
  • Tax on costs to achieve. Severance and restructuring costs are generally deductible when paid; VRS payments are deductible over five years under Sec 35DDA — a timing difference affecting current tax, not consolidated PAT if deferred tax is recognised.
  • Capitalisable costs. IT system implementation costs may be partly capitalised (Ind AS 38), shifting the P&L hit into amortisation.

Key takeaways

  • Overlay realisation (50/75/100%), cost-to-achieve timing and a rating-downgrade spread on the base pro-forma.
  • Separate cost and revenue synergy factors; model delays as phasing shifts, not just lower levels.
  • Present run-rate and reported EPS; cost-to-achieve timing can swing Year 1 reported accretion by several points.
  • A downgrade raises both new and existing debt costs; break-even synergies rise by the pre-tax interest increase.
  • Use coherent named scenarios driven by one selector, reflecting correlated downside drivers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Realisation and required % [L2]

Break-even pre-tax synergies are ₹145 crore (Year 1) and ₹95 crore (Year 2). Guided synergies are ₹160 crore and ₹220 crore. Compute the required realisation % for each year and, for an acquirer with 80 crore shares, 25.168% tax and standalone EPS of ₹28 (Year 1), the Year 1 EPS at 50%, 75% and 100% realisation.

Q2. Cost timing and reported EPS [L2]

Costs to achieve of ₹180 crore pre-tax are planned. Pro-forma PAT ex-costs at 100% realisation is ₹2,450 crore (Year 1) and ₹2,720 crore (Year 2); standalone PAT ₹2,400 crore and ₹2,600 crore; 120 crore shares (no new shares); tax 25%. Compute reported accretion in both years for timing splits of 100/0, 60/40 and 30/70.

Q3. Downgrade-triggered scenario [L3]

A debt-funded acquirer's pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA is 3.1x; the rating agency's downgrade trigger for its sector is sustained leverage above 3.0x. New debt ₹4,000 crore; existing ratings-linked debt ₹2,500 crore. A one-notch downgrade adds 50 bps to new debt and 30 bps to existing debt. Tax 25.168%; 90 crore shares. Compute the EPS impact, explain how you would model the downgrade as an endogenous trigger, and identify one action that could avoid it.

💡 Hint: Link the downgrade switch to the leverage output with an IF test rather than a manual flag.

Q4. Designing the severe case [L3]

For an Indian FMCG acquisition with guided synergies of 60% cost (distribution and procurement) and 40% revenue (cross-selling in rural channels), design a severe-downside scenario: specify realisation factors by type, phasing changes, cost overrun, dis-synergies and rating effect, and justify each assumption briefly.
