Defining EPS-Neutral for Break-Even Analysis

Scope: Defining EPS-neutral: pro-forma EPS equals acquirer standalone EPS in each forecast year

Why it matters

"Break-even" is only meaningful once the target condition is precisely defined. Two analysts can report different break-even synergies for the same deal simply because one compares pro-forma EPS with last year's actual EPS and the other with the acquirer's own forecast; or because one uses adjusted EPS (ex-PPA amortisation) and the other reported Ind AS EPS; or because one uses a full-year pro-forma and the other a time-weighted reported basis. Board papers, fairness opinions and investor presentations must state the definition, and an FP&A analyst asked to "make the deal EPS-neutral" needs to know which EPS, which year and which basis before touching the model.

Core notes

The definition

A deal is EPS-neutral in year \(y\) when

\[ \text{EPS}_{PF,y} = \text{EPS}_{A,y} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\text{PAT}_{PF,y}}{N_{A,y} + N_{\text{new},y}} = \frac{\text{PAT}_{A,y}}{N_{A,y}} \]

where \(\text{EPS}_{A,y}\) is the acquirer's standalone forecast EPS for the same year (what it would have earned without the deal), \(\text{PAT}_{PF,y}\) is pro-forma PAT attributable to owners (topic 4 c01) and \(N_{\text{new},y}\) is the weighted number of new shares.

Key elements of the definition:

  1. Benchmark = standalone forecast, same year. Not prior-year actual EPS. Organic growth is not a deal effect. A deal that leaves EPS at ₹21 versus ₹20 last year but ₹22 standalone forecast is 4.5% dilutive.
  2. Each forecast year separately. Year 1 and Year 2 neutrality are distinct conditions because the financing burden, synergy phasing and target growth change (s02). Report break-even for each year.
  3. Same EPS measure on both sides. If pro-forma is diluted EPS, standalone must be diluted EPS. If adjusted EPS (excluding PPA amortisation and one-off deal costs) is used for pro-forma, the standalone benchmark must exclude the acquirer's own existing acquired-intangible amortisation too, for consistency.
  4. Same time basis. Full-year pro-forma ("as if closed on day 1 of the fiscal year") or reported basis (time-weighted per Ind AS 33.22). Do not mix.
  5. Attributable to owners. NCI deducted where ownership is below 100% (topic 4 s04).
  6. Run-rate vs reported. State whether one-time costs to achieve, transaction costs expensed under Ind AS 103.53 and inventory step-up are included. Banks typically define neutrality on run-rate EPS, excluding one-offs, and show reported EPS separately.

EPS-neutral vs neutral PAT

For a cash/debt deal, \(N_{\text{new}} = 0\), so EPS-neutral means \(\text{PAT}_{PF} = \text{PAT}_{A}\): the deal's incremental after-tax earnings exactly offset its after-tax costs. For a stock deal, neutrality requires pro-forma PAT to grow in proportion to shares:

\[ \text{PAT}_{PF} = \text{EPS}_{A} \times (N_{A} + N_{\text{new}}) \]

so new shares "need" earnings of \(\text{EPS}_{A} \times N_{\text{new}}\). This is why the general break-even formula (c01) multiplies standalone EPS by pro-forma shares.

Tolerance and rounding

Indian companies report EPS to two decimals (₹). A pro-forma EPS of ₹19.996 against ₹20.00 rounds to neutral. Bankers commonly describe accretion within ±0.5–1% as "broadly neutral". For break-even calculations, solve exactly (to the rupee crore of synergies) and present rounded figures, but never let rounding drive a "neutral" headline.

Illustration

Bharat Polymers standalone FY27 EPS forecast ₹20.00 (FY26 actual ₹18.20). After a 60/40 debt/cash acquisition, pro-forma FY27 EPS before synergies is ₹18.74.

Comparison Pro-forma EPS Benchmark Verdict
vs FY26 actual (wrong benchmark) 18.74 18.20 "+3.0% accretive" — misleading
vs FY27 standalone forecast 18.74 20.00 6.3% dilutive
Adjusted EPS (ex-PPA ₹60 crore after tax) vs adjusted standalone (acquirer has no PPA) 19.74 20.00 1.3% dilutive

Only the second and third rows are valid comparisons; the definition chosen determines the break-even synergy (₹100.9 crore on reported basis, ₹20.9 crore on adjusted basis in this example).

Model implementation

Create an explicit "neutrality test" row per year:

=ROUND(PF_EPS - SA_EPS, 6)

and a flag =IF(ABS(PF_EPS/SA_EPS-1)<=Tolerance,"Neutral",IF(PF_EPS>SA_EPS,"Accretive","Dilutive")). Have a single basis switch (Reported / Adjusted; Full-year / Time-weighted) that changes both pro-forma and standalone EPS together, so the two sides can never be on different bases.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Acquirer standalone loss or near-zero EPS. Percentage accretion explodes or changes sign; switch to rupee EPS change, or to EBITDA- or cash-EPS-based neutrality.
  • Standalone plan includes a buyback that the deal replaces. Neutrality should be tested against the standalone case including the buyback — the deal must beat the alternative use of cash, not "do nothing".
  • Mid-year closing. A deal can be neutral on the reported FY27 basis (only six months of costs) and dilutive on a full-year basis; state both.
  • Different year-ends. If the target's year differs, calendarise (topic 4 s01) before testing neutrality.
  • Dividend-per-share neutrality. Some boards also test DPS — whether the combined company can maintain the dividend policy; this is a cash test, not an EPS test.

Key takeaways

  • EPS-neutral means pro-forma EPS equals the acquirer's standalone forecast EPS for the same year — not last year's actual.
  • Test neutrality year by year; Year 1 and Year 2 differ.
  • Use the same EPS measure (reported vs adjusted, basic vs diluted) and time basis on both sides, driven by one switch.
  • For stock deals, neutrality requires pro-forma PAT = standalone EPS × pro-forma shares.
  • State whether one-offs (costs to achieve, transaction costs, inventory step-up) are included.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choosing the right benchmark [L1]

An acquirer reported FY26 EPS of ₹42.10; its FY27 standalone forecast is ₹47.50. The deal's pro-forma FY27 EPS is ₹45.90. Compute accretion/dilution against each benchmark and state which one should appear in the board paper.

Q2. Stock-deal neutrality condition [L2]

Acquirer FY27 standalone PAT ₹1,500 crore, 75 crore shares. It issues 6 crore new shares for a target. What pro-forma PAT is required for EPS-neutrality? If target PAT is ₹95 crore and after-tax PPA amortisation ₹25 crore, compute the after-tax synergies needed.

💡 Hint: New shares need earnings equal to standalone EPS times the number of new shares.

Q3. Adjusted vs reported neutrality [L2]

The acquirer already has ₹40 crore per year of after-tax amortisation from previous acquisitions. A new deal adds ₹55 crore after-tax PPA amortisation. Acquirer standalone PAT ₹800 crore, 50 crore shares; pro-forma reported PAT before synergies ₹770 crore, no new shares. Compute accretion on reported basis and on an adjusted basis that excludes all acquired-intangible amortisation, and explain why the adjusted standalone benchmark must be adjusted too.

Q4. Defining neutrality in a term sheet [L3]

You are asked to draft a one-paragraph definition of "EPS-neutral" for an internal investment committee policy that requires all acquisitions to be EPS-neutral by Year 2. Draft it, specifying benchmark, EPS measure, time basis, treatment of one-offs and NCI, and justify each choice.
