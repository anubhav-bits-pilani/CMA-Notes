Subtopic note

Maximum Offer Price for EPS-Neutrality

Scope: Maximum offer price for EPS-neutrality at a given synergy level

Why it matters

Break-even synergy answers "given the price, what synergies do we need?". In negotiation, the question is usually inverted: "given the synergies we believe in, how high can we bid without diluting EPS?". The answer is a walk-away price on an EPS basis — one of the inputs, together with the value-based maximum price (standalone value plus PV of net synergies, topic 1 c02) and regulatory floors, that defines the bidding range. In India, the EPS-based ceiling also has to be compared with the SAST minimum open-offer price (Reg 8) for listed targets and the ICDR floor for any shares issued; if the regulatory floor exceeds the EPS-neutral ceiling, the deal cannot be both compliant and non-dilutive.

Core notes

The exact relationship

Let \(X\) be the target's annual contribution to pro-forma PAT before funding costs:

\[ X = \text{PAT}_{T} + S(1-t) - A_{PPA}(1-t) - \text{NCI} \]

and let \(k_{\text{acq}}\) be the weighted earnings cost of funding per rupee of equity price (topic 4 c05), including any fee cost:

\[ k_{\text{acq}} = w_{d}\left[r_{d} + \tfrac{f}{n}\right](1-t) + w_{c}\,r_{c}(1-t) + w_{s}\,\frac{1}{\text{P/E}_{A}} \]

where \(f\) = upfront financing fee as a fraction of debt and \(n\) = tenor. Setting pro-forma EPS equal to standalone EPS gives, for any funding mix (exactly, when adjustments do not depend on price):

\[ E_{\max} = \frac{X}{k_{\text{acq}}} \qquad P_{\max} = \frac{E_{\max}}{N_{T}} \qquad \text{Max premium} = \frac{P_{\max}}{P_{\text{undisturbed}}} - 1 \]

Proof sketch. Neutrality requires \(\text{PAT}_{A} + X - E(w_{d}k_{d} + w_{c}k_{c}) = \text{EPS}_{A}(N_{A} + w_{s}E/P_{A})\). Since \(\text{PAT}_{A} = \text{EPS}_{A}N_{A}\) and \(\text{EPS}_{A}/P_{A} = 1/\text{P/E}_{A}\), this simplifies to \(X = E\,k_{\text{acq}}\).

For an all-stock deal this is the intuitive result \(E_{\max} = \text{P/E}_{A} \times X\): the acquirer can pay its own multiple for the target's adjusted earnings.

When PPA depends on price

A higher price raises the excess over book value, part of which is allocated to amortising intangibles and PPE step-ups (the rest to goodwill, which is not amortised). Model this as

\[ A_{PPA} = a\,(E - B) \]

where \(B\) = target book net assets (excluding existing goodwill) and \(a\) = fraction of excess allocated to amortising assets ÷ average life. Substituting:

\[ E_{\max} = \frac{\text{PAT}_{T} + S(1-t) + a\,B\,(1-t)}{k_{\text{acq}} + a\,(1-t)} \]

This keeps the solution closed-form. If fees, NCI or tax limits make the relationship non-linear, use Goal Seek on the price input (setting EPS difference to zero) as a check.

Diluted target shares

The equity price depends on fully diluted target shares, and the treasury stock method makes in-the-money options depend on the offer price. Iterate: compute \(P_{\max}\) with basic shares, recompute diluted shares at \(P_{\max}\), repeat (two passes are usually enough), or let Excel iterate with a circularity switch.

Worked illustration

Bharat Polymers / Deccan Packaging. Target PAT ₹150 crore; synergies ₹100 crore pre-tax; tax 25%; 10 crore target shares; undisturbed price ₹225; book net assets ₹1,100 crore. Funding 60% debt at 9% (fee 1.5%, 5 years) / 40% cash at 6.5%.

\[ k_{\text{acq}} = 0.6 \times (9\% + 0.3\%) \times 0.75 + 0.4 \times 6.5\% \times 0.75 = 4.185\% + 1.95\% = 6.135\% \]

Case A — PPA fixed at ₹80 crore pre-tax: \(X = 150 + 75 - 60 = ₹165\) crore. \(E_{\max} = 165 \div 6.135\% = ₹2{,}689\) crore → \(P_{\max} = ₹268.9\) per share (19.5% premium).

Case B — PPA scales with price (50% of excess over book allocated to 10-year assets, so \(a = 0.05\)):

\[ E_{\max} = \frac{150 + 75 + 0.05 \times 1{,}100 \times 0.75}{0.06135 + 0.05 \times 0.75} = \frac{266.25}{0.09885} = ₹2{,}693.5 \text{ crore} \]

→ \(P_{\max} = ₹269.4\). (Here the two cases nearly coincide because the base PPA was calibrated at ₹2,700 crore; at higher synergy levels, Case B gives a noticeably lower ceiling than Case A because each extra rupee of price adds amortisation.)

All-stock, PPA fixed: \(E_{\max} = 25 \times 165 = ₹4{,}125\) crore → ₹412.5 per share (83% premium). The 25x acquirer currency allows a much higher EPS-neutral price — but says nothing about value.

Comparing ceilings

Ceiling / floor Basis Illustration (₹ per share)
EPS-neutral max, 60/40 debt/cash Accounting 268.9
EPS-neutral max, all-stock Accounting 412.5
Value-based max = standalone value + PV net synergies Economic (topic 1 c02) e.g. 225 + 70 = 295
SAST Reg 8 minimum offer price Regulatory floor e.g. 236 (60-day VWAP)

The negotiating range is between the regulatory floor and the lower of the value-based and (if the board insists on neutrality) EPS-based ceilings. In the all-stock case the binding constraint is value, not EPS.

Excel implementation

k_acq   =wD*(rD+Fee/Tenor)*(1-t) + wC*rC*(1-t) + wS/PE_A
E_max   =(PAT_T + Syn*(1-t) + a*BookNA*(1-t)) / (k_acq + a*(1-t))
P_max   =E_max / Diluted_Shares_T
MaxPrem =P_max / P_Undisturbed - 1

Put \(P_{\max}\) in a two-way data table against synergy level and funding mix to give the board a "maximum price map".

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Regulatory floor above ceiling. If the SAST Reg 8 price (highest of the negotiated price, the volume-weighted average price paid by the acquirer in the preceding 52 weeks, the highest price paid by the acquirer in the preceding 26 weeks, and the 60-trading-day VWAP for frequently traded shares) exceeds \(P_{\max}\), the acquirer must accept dilution, find more synergies, change the funding mix, or walk away.
  • Stock consideration and ICDR pricing. The acquirer cannot issue preferential shares below the ICDR floor; if its share price falls, \(\text{P/E}_{A}\) falls and so does \(E_{\max}\) for stock deals.
  • Year choice. \(P_{\max}\) differs by year (Year 2 typically higher for debt deals). Boards usually anchor on Year 1 or "neutral by Year 2" policies.
  • Partial ownership. For an open offer that may achieve less than 100%, compute \(P_{\max}\) per share for the incremental stake, with NCI in \(X\).
  • Debt capacity. A higher price with fixed mix raises leverage; the EPS ceiling may exceed the price at which covenants bind.

Key takeaways

  • For any funding mix, the EPS-neutral maximum equity price is \(E_{\max} = X / k_{\text{acq}}\), where \(X\) is target PAT plus after-tax synergies less after-tax PPA and NCI.
  • For all-stock deals, \(E_{\max} = \text{P/E}_{A} \times X\).
  • When PPA amortisation scales with price, solve the linear equation \(E_{\max} = [\text{PAT}_{T} + S(1-t) + aB(1-t)] / [k_{\text{acq}} + a(1-t)]\).
  • Compare the EPS ceiling with the value-based ceiling and SAST/ICDR floors to define the negotiating range.
  • Iterate for diluted target shares under the treasury stock method.

Q1. Maximum price under three funding mixes [L2]

Target PAT ₹240 crore, 20 crore diluted shares, undisturbed price ₹150. Synergies ₹80 crore pre-tax; after-tax PPA amortisation fixed at ₹45 crore. Acquirer P/E 22; debt 9.4% pre-tax, fee 1% over 5 years; cash yield 6.9%; tax 25.168%. Compute \(P_{\max}\) and the maximum premium for: all-stock; all-debt; 50% cash / 50% debt.

Q2. PPA scaling with price [L2]

For the all-debt case in Q1, assume target book net assets of ₹1,800 crore and that 40% of any excess of equity price over book is allocated to intangibles amortised over 8 years. Recompute \(P_{\max}\) with the closed-form formula and verify that pro-forma PAT impact is zero at that price.

💡 Hint: Compute \(a\) as the allocation fraction divided by the life.

Q3. Regulatory floor versus EPS ceiling [L3]

A listed target's 60-trading-day VWAP is ₹410, its 26-week market high is ₹438 (the acquirer has not bought any target shares in the market), and the acquirer agreed to pay the promoter ₹425 under the SPA. The acquirer's EPS-neutral ceiling at its base synergy case is ₹415 for a 70/30 debt/cash deal. Determine the minimum open-offer price under SAST Reg 8 and set out the board's options, quantifying the additional synergies needed to restore neutrality if each target share bought (26% offer of 12 crore shares plus 30% SPA) is paid at the Reg 8 price. Assume \(k_{\text{acq}}\) = 6.4% and tax 25.168%.

Q4. Interview: why the all-stock ceiling is so high [L1]

Explain in four sentences why the EPS-neutral maximum price in an all-stock deal equals the acquirer's P/E times the target's adjusted earnings, and why a board should not treat it as a bidding limit.
