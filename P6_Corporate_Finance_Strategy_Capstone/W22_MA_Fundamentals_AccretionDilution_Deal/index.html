How this week fits

Week 22 opens the corporate-finance strategy block of Phase 6 (Corporate Finance Strategy, Capstone & Career Launch). It pulls together almost everything built so far. Ind AS 103, 110 and 12 from Weeks 1–4 supply the accounting for acquisitions. Forensic analysis and benchmarking from Weeks 5–8 become due diligence. EPS mechanics from Week 14 and debt schedules from Week 15 become the pro-forma P&L and financing schedule. The DCF, comps and EV-to-equity bridge from Week 16 value the target. The leverage and covenant analysis from Week 21 tests whether the acquirer can carry the deal.

The week runs in five topics. t1 covers the strategic rationale, synergies, deal process and the Indian regulatory and tax map (CCI, SAST, NCLT schemes, FEMA, Sec 47/72A/50B, IBC). t2 builds the Sources & Uses table and covers acquisition finance and fee accounting. t3 covers purchase price allocation and goodwill. t4 builds the EPS accretion/dilution model, and t5 solves for break-even synergies and runs sensitivities. The hands-on exercise combines all five into a 2-year model for an Indian listed acquirer, and the Saturday case tests the funding-mix logic (P/E versus after-tax cost of debt) under time pressure. Later capstone weeks reuse this model for an end-to-end deal pitch and for interview case practice.

Refresh before starting

Ind AS 103, Ind AS 110 and Ind AS 12 basics

Ind AS 103, Ind AS 110 consolidation and Ind AS 12 deferred tax basics (→ Weeks 1–4)

  • Ind AS 103: acquisition method in four steps (identify acquirer, acquisition date, recognise and measure net identifiable assets and NCI, goodwill or bargain purchase). Acquisition costs are expensed (103.53). Contingent consideration is at fair value. India carve-out: bargain purchase gain to OCI/capital reserve. Appendix C: common control at carrying amounts.
  • Ind AS 110: control = power + exposure to variable returns + ability to use power to affect returns. Consolidate from the date control is obtained; eliminate intra-group balances and unrealised profit; present NCI within equity; changes in ownership without loss of control are equity transactions (110.23).
  • Ind AS 12: temporary difference = carrying amount − tax base. Business combinations create DTL on step-ups (12.19, 12.66). No DTL on initial recognition of goodwill (12.15(a)). Acquirer's own DTAs made recoverable by the deal go to P&L (12.67). Rate: 25.17% under Sec 115BAA.

EPS, P/E and earnings yield

EPS (Ind AS 33), P/E and earnings yield relationships

  • Basic EPS = profit attributable to equity holders of the parent ÷ weighted average number of shares outstanding (Ind AS 33.10, 33.19). Shares issued as consideration in a business combination are included from the acquisition date (33.22).
  • Diluted EPS adjusts for dilutive potential shares (options via treasury stock method, convertibles via if-converted), with antidilutive items excluded (33.41).
  • P/E = price ÷ EPS. Earnings yield = EPS ÷ price = 1 ÷ P/E. A 25x P/E is a 4% earnings yield.
  • The after-tax "cost" of issuing stock in EPS terms is the acquirer's earnings yield. The cost of debt is \(r_d(1 - t)\). The cost of cash is the after-tax yield foregone. A deal is accretive (before synergies and PPA) when the target's earnings yield at the offer price exceeds the weighted cost of funding.

Debt schedules and interest conventions

Debt schedules and interest calculation conventions (→ Week 15)

  • Debt schedule rows: opening balance, drawdowns, mandatory repayment, optional repayment (cash sweep), closing balance, interest.
  • Interest on average balance is more accurate but creates circularity with cash. Use a circuit-breaker switch or an opening-balance convention. Avoid iterative calculation in models that run data tables.
  • Fees: amortised through the EIR (Ind AS 109), often approximated straight-line in models.
  • Day-count and stub periods: a loan drawn on 1 October accrues interest for 182 of 365 days in FY27.
  • Floating rates: link to a rate curve input. Model a rating-downgrade spread as a scenario.

EV-to-equity bridge and diluted share count

EV-to-equity bridge and fully diluted share count from DCF work (→ Week 16)

  • Equity value = EV − debt − lease liabilities (if EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116) − preference − NCI − debt-like items + free cash + non-operating assets.
  • Fully diluted shares = basic + in-the-money options under the treasury stock method (options × (1 − strike ÷ price)) + convertibles if converted + RSUs.
  • In a deal, compute target dilution at the offer price, and acquirer dilution at the acquirer's current price.
  • Keep the perimeter consistent: an EV multiple on consolidated EBITDA needs NCI in the bridge.

Cross-cutting concepts

Tax-effecting and time-weighting every pro-forma line

Every pro-forma line must be tax-effected and time-weighted consistently

Pro-forma PAT is built from pre-tax adjustments: synergies, foregone interest on cash, new debt interest, financing fee amortisation, PPA amortisation and depreciation, and inventory step-up. Each must be multiplied by (1 − t) before it is added to or subtracted from PAT, using the marginal rate of the entity where it arises (25.17% for a Sec 115BAA company). Items that are not tax-deductible, such as acquisition advisory fees on a share purchase, are not tax-effected. Time-weighting is the second rule: if a deal closes on 1 October and the fiscal year ends on 31 March, only six months of the target's PAT, synergies, new interest and amortisation belong in FY27, and any new shares are time-weighted under Ind AS 33 (shares issued as consideration count from the acquisition date). The classic error is to include a full year of target PAT while time-weighting only interest, or to time-weight PAT but not the share count. Build a single "stub factor" cell for each year and multiply every deal-related line by it, so that the convention is consistent and auditable. Interest on debt drawn at closing follows the same factor, adjusted for the day-count convention.

Accounting outcome vs economic outcome

Accounting outcome (EPS, goodwill) vs economic outcome (NPV of synergies minus premium)

EPS accretion and goodwill are accounting outcomes. Value creation is an economic outcome: the acquirer creates value only if the PV of net synergies exceeds the premium paid over standalone value plus transaction costs. The two can point in opposite directions. A high-P/E acquirer paying with stock for a low-P/E target will report accretion even with no synergies, because it is swapping expensive paper for cheap earnings. That is arithmetic, not value. A cash deal funded with cheap debt can be accretive while the premium exceeds every rupee of synergy. Conversely, a deal with large long-term synergies can be dilutive in Year 1 because of costs to achieve, inventory step-up and backlog amortisation. Goodwill is also not "value": it is the residual of the price over identifiable net assets and includes any overpayment, which later surfaces as an Ind AS 36 impairment. Board papers should therefore show both lenses side by side: accretion/dilution by year and the NPV test (synergy NPV vs premium), and explain any disagreement.

No new shares in a 60% debt / 40% cash deal

Exercise: 60% debt / 40% cash means no new shares; dilution comes only from interest, foregone interest and PPA amortisation

With 60% debt and 40% cash, the acquirer issues no shares, so the EPS denominator stays at the acquirer's diluted share count. Pro-forma EPS rises if the target's earnings (plus after-tax synergies) exceed the after-tax cost of funding plus the after-tax PPA charges. The dilutive items are: new debt interest × (1 − t) (including fee amortisation), the interest foregone on cash used × (1 − t), PPA amortisation and depreciation × (1 − t), the Year 1 inventory step-up, and NCI's share of target profit if ownership is below 100%. For a quick test, compare the target's earnings yield at the offer price (target PAT ÷ equity price paid) with the weighted after-tax cost of funding (60% × debt rate × (1 − t) + 40% × cash yield × (1 − t)). If the yield is higher, the deal is accretive before PPA and synergies. With Indian debt at 8.5–9.5% and cash at 6.5–7%, the after-tax funding cost is about 5.8–6.6%. A target bought at more than about 16–17x earnings is likely to be dilutive before synergies.

SAST open-offer funding and NCI when the peer is listed

Exercise: if the peer is listed, model SAST open-offer funding (26%) and NCI for scenarios below 100% ownership

If the target is listed and the SPA takes the acquirer to 25% or more, or gives control, SAST Reg 3/4 triggers an open offer for at least 26% of the total shares (Reg 7) at the highest of the Reg 8 parameters. The model must commit funding for 100% acceptance, place escrow under Reg 17 (25% of the first ₹500 crore and 10% of the rest) before the detailed public statement, and run ownership scenarios: for example SPA 45% plus 0%, 50% or 100% acceptance of the 26% offer, giving 45%, 58% or 71% ownership. Below 100%, NCI takes its share of target PAT, of target-level synergies and of PPA amortisation that sits in the target, so attributable pro-forma PAT falls. Interest on acquisition debt stays fully with the acquirer. Lower acceptance therefore cuts debt and interest but also cuts attributable earnings. If ownership exceeds 75%, the model must show a plan to restore minimum public shareholding within 12 months, or a delisting route.

Stamp duty, advisory fees and debt fees in Sources & Uses

Exercise: include stamp duty, advisory fees (expensed) and debt fees (EIR-amortised) in Sources & Uses

Fees are cash uses at closing, so they increase the funding requirement. Their accounting differs and must be reflected in the pro-forma statements. Stamp duty on the share transfer (0.015% of consideration for delivery-based demat transfers, on both SPA and open-offer shares) and advisory, legal and DD fees are acquisition-related costs: expensed in P&L under Ind AS 103.53 in the period services are received, not added to goodwill. On a share purchase they are generally capital in nature and not tax-deductible. Debt fees (arrangement, underwriting, lender legal) are deducted from the loan's initial carrying amount and amortised through the EIR under Ind AS 109, so they increase interest expense over the tenor. In the model, a straight-line amortisation over the tenor is an acceptable approximation, shown on its own row. Because debt fees are a percentage of the debt plug, solve the circularity algebraically: debt = (uses before debt fees − other sources) ÷ (1 − fee %).

Year 1 and Year 2 accretion, break-even synergies and covenant test

Exercise: show Year 1 and Year 2 accretion, break-even synergies, and post-deal Net Debt/EBITDA vs covenant

The output page must show, for each of Year 1 and Year 2: standalone EPS, pro-forma EPS, accretion or dilution in rupees and %, and the break-even pre-tax synergies needed for EPS neutrality. For a debt and cash deal with no new shares, the break-even synergy is

\[ S = \max\left[0, \ \frac{(\text{Foregone interest} + \text{New debt interest} + \text{PPA amortisation}) \times (1 - t) + \text{NCI share} - \text{Target PAT}}{1 - t}\right] \]

on a time-weighted basis. Year 2 usually improves on Year 1 because a full year of target PAT is included, synergies ramp up, costs to achieve fall away, short-life intangibles (backlog) and inventory step-ups are fully amortised, and debt amortisation reduces interest. Alongside EPS, show pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA (combined net debt after the deal ÷ combined EBITDA, LTM or forward as the covenant defines) against the covenant (for example 2.5x) and the rating threshold, and pro-forma interest cover. A deal that is accretive but breaches covenants is not financeable as structured.

Hands-on exercise brief

Roadmap exercise: Build a 2-year M&A Accretion/Dilution model for an Indian listed acquirer buying a smaller peer using 60% debt and 40% cash, including PPA goodwill and break-even synergies.

Scenario

Acquirer Ltd (NSE-listed specialty chemicals, FY ends 31 March) agrees on 1 July 2026 to buy the 45% promoter stake in Target Ltd (NSE-listed peer) at ₹480 per share and triggers a SAST open offer for 26%. Assume closing of both the SPA and the open offer on 1 October 2026. Year 1 = FY27 (six months of ownership), Year 2 = FY28.

Inputs and assumptions

Item Acquirer Target
Basic shares (crore) 50.0 10.0
Options (crore) / strike (₹) 1.0 at ₹900 0.4 at ₹250
Current share price (₹) 1,200 400 (undisturbed)
PAT FY27E / FY28E (₹ crore) 3,000 / 3,360 420 / 470
EBITDA FY27E / FY28E (₹ crore) 5,500 / 6,100 900 / 990
Revenue FY27E / FY28E (₹ crore) 30,000 / 33,000 4,800 / 5,250
Cash (₹ crore) 4,500 (minimum 1,000) 250 (minimum 150)
Debt (₹ crore) 3,000 600 (change-of-control clause; prepay at 1%)
Book net assets (₹ crore) – 2,600 (includes goodwill 100)

Deal and funding:

  • Offer price ₹480 for SPA and open offer (assume Reg 8 parameters do not exceed this). Base case: 100% acceptance (71% ownership). Scenarios: 0% and 50% acceptance.
  • Refinance target debt at closing, funded by the acquirer through an inter-company loan.
  • Advisory, legal and DD fees ₹32 crore; stamp duty 0.015% on all shares acquired; debt arrangement fee 1.0% of new debt, 5-year tenor.
  • Funding of total uses: 60% new term loan at 8.75% (amortising 10% a year from FY28), 40% acquirer excess cash (pre-tax yield 7.0%).
  • Tax 25.17% for both companies.
  • PPA (100% basis): brand ₹500 crore (indefinite), customer relationships ₹400 crore (10 years), plant step-up ₹200 crore (8 years), inventory step-up ₹30 crore (sold within 3 months). NCI at proportionate share (base) and at fair value of ₹400 per share (alternative).
  • Synergies (pre-tax, arise in the target): run-rate ₹180 crore; realised 30% of run-rate during FY27 (applied to the six-month stub) and 70% in FY28. Costs to achieve ₹120 crore in FY27 and ₹60 crore in FY28 (tax-deductible).
  • Covenant: Net Debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5x on LTM pro-forma EBITDA including 100% of the target; rating threshold 2.0x.

Required tabs

  1. Inputs: all assumptions above in one place, colour-coded, with scenario selectors (acceptance level, NCI method, synergy realisation %, interest rate shift).
  2. Target valuation: fully diluted shares by TSM at ₹480, equity value, EV bridge, implied EV/EBITDA and P/E paid, premium over the undisturbed price.
  3. SAST: open-offer size, maximum consideration, Reg 17 escrow, ownership by acceptance scenario, MPS check.
  4. Sources & Uses: SPA price, open offer, target debt refinancing plus prepayment, advisory fees, stamp duty, debt fees (solved algebraically), 60/40 split, excess cash check.
  5. PPA: book net assets, removal of existing goodwill, step-ups and intangibles, DTL, FV of net identifiable assets, NCI (both methods), goodwill.
  6. Debt schedule: term loan drawdown, amortisation, interest (with stub factor), fee amortisation.
  7. Pro-forma P&L: acquirer PAT + attributable target PAT (time-weighted) + after-tax synergies − after-tax CTA − after-tax foregone interest − after-tax new interest and fees − after-tax PPA charges − expensed acquisition costs − NCI share. Show both statutory and adjusted (excluding one-off deal costs and inventory step-up).
  8. EPS and accretion: standalone vs pro-forma EPS, accretion % for FY27 and FY28.
  9. Break-even synergies: algebraic formula for each year, and a Goal Seek check; synergies required as % of target revenue and of combined cost base.
  10. Credit: pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA and interest cover vs covenant and rating threshold.
  11. Sensitivities: data tables on (a) offer premium × funding mix, (b) synergy realisation × interest rate, all on the same sheet as their inputs.

Required checks

  • Sources = Uses (difference cell = 0).
  • Excess cash used ≤ acquirer cash − minimum cash.
  • Goodwill = consideration + NCI − FV of net identifiable assets, with a flag if negative.
  • DTL = (step-ups + intangibles) × tax rate.
  • Pro-forma share count = acquirer diluted shares (no new shares in the 60/40 case).
  • Stub factor applied to every deal-related P&L line in FY27.
  • Pro-forma EPS at break-even synergies = standalone EPS (difference = 0).
  • No goodwill amortisation anywhere in the model.

An Excel workbook with the tabs above and a one-page output dashboard: accretion/dilution for FY27 and FY28 (base and two acceptance scenarios), goodwill under both NCI methods, break-even synergies vs management's run-rate, Net Debt/EBITDA vs covenant, and a heat map from the sensitivity tables. Add a half-page memo stating whether the deal is accretive, whether it creates value (synergy NPV vs premium), and the key risks.

Saturday assessment paper

Roadmap assessment: Saturday 3-Hour Case Test: Determine whether an all-stock vs all-debt acquisition is accretive to EPS using P/E vs after-tax cost of debt rules.

Instructions

  • Time: 3 hours. Total: 100 marks. Answer all questions.
  • Tax rate 25.17% unless stated otherwise. FY ends 31 March. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated.
  • Show workings. Excel is allowed for Q4 and Q6; state formulas used.
  • Where a regulatory rule is applied, cite the regulation or section.

Case data (applies to Q1–Q5)

Bharat Polymers Ltd (acquirer, NSE-listed) is considering acquiring 100% of Deccan Resins Ltd (unlisted, so no open offer) on 1 April 2027.

Item Bharat Polymers Deccan Resins
Diluted shares (crore) 40 8
Share price / offer price (₹) 1,500 600 offer
PAT FY28E (₹ crore) 2,400 300
PAT FY29E (₹ crore) 2,640 345
EBITDA FY28E (₹ crore) 4,000 560
Net debt at 31 March 2027 (₹ crore) 2,000 0 (debt-free)

Funding alternatives: (A) all-stock at ₹1,500 per Bharat share; (B) all-debt at 9.0% pre-tax, bullet, 1% upfront fee over 5 years. PPA: intangibles of ₹900 crore amortised over 10 years (DTL recognised). Synergies: none in the base case. Covenant: Net Debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.0x.

Q1. P/E rule of thumb [10 marks]

(a) Compute Bharat's P/E and the P/E paid for Deccan. (b) Using the rule of thumb, state whether the all-stock deal is accretive or dilutive before synergies and PPA. (c) Compute Deccan's earnings yield at the offer price and Bharat's after-tax cost of debt, and state whether the all-debt deal is accretive before PPA.

Q2. Pro-forma EPS for both structures [20 marks]

For FY28 and FY29, compute pro-forma PAT, pro-forma shares, pro-forma EPS and accretion % under (A) and (B), including PPA amortisation and (for B) interest and fee amortisation. Present the results in a single table.

💡 Hint: Tax-effect every adjustment; the share count changes only in one structure.

Q3. Break-even synergies [15 marks]

For each structure and each year, compute the pre-tax synergies required for EPS neutrality. Express each as a % of Deccan's FY28E EBITDA and comment on plausibility.

Q4. Mixed funding and the weighted cost test [15 marks]

(a) Compute the weighted after-tax cost of acquisition for funding mixes of 0/100, 25/75, 50/50, 75/25 and 100/0 (stock/debt), using 1 ÷ P/E as the cost of stock. (b) Identify the mix at which the deal is EPS-neutral before PPA. (c) Build (or describe) a one-way data table showing FY28 accretion % across the five mixes, including PPA.

Q5. Credit and value [15 marks]

(a) Compute pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA at 1 April 2027 under (B) and state whether the covenant holds. (b) Deccan's standalone DCF equity value is ₹4,300 crore and the PV of achievable net synergies is ₹350 crore. Compute the premium and the acquirer's NPV. (c) Write a short paragraph explaining why structure (B) can be accretive while destroying value, or why it does not, based on your numbers.

Q6. Listed-target variation with SAST [15 marks]

Assume instead that Deccan is NSE-listed (8 crore shares, undisturbed price ₹500) and Bharat buys the 40% promoter stake at ₹600 under an SPA. The 60-trading-day VWAP is ₹560; Bharat bought 1% in the market 20 weeks ago at ₹615. (a) Determine the open-offer price and size. (b) Compute the maximum offer consideration and the Reg 17 escrow. (c) Under all-debt funding, compute FY28 pro-forma EPS at 0% and 100% acceptance (ignore PPA differences for partial ownership other than scaling by ownership), including NCI. (d) State the minimum public shareholding consequence at 100% acceptance.

Q7. Short notes [10 marks]

Write brief notes (about 100 words each) on: (a) why advisory fees cannot be added to goodwill under Ind AS 103; (b) Sec 72A conditions if Deccan had accumulated losses and were merged into Bharat; (c) when CCI approval would be needed for this deal under the deal value threshold.
