Subtopic note

NCI Options, Step Acquisitions, Contingent Consideration, Measurement Period

Scope: NCI measurement options (fair value vs proportionate share), step acquisitions, contingent consideration, 12-month measurement period

Why it matters

Indian listed-target deals rarely end at 100% ownership. An SPA for the promoter stake plus a 26% open offer often leaves the acquirer between 51% and 75%, so NCI measurement decides how much goodwill appears on the balance sheet. Many acquirers also build up stakes over time (associate to subsidiary), which triggers a remeasurement gain or loss on the old stake. Earn-outs and deferred payments are common in founder-led deals. Finally, the PPA is usually provisional at the first reporting date, and the 12-month measurement period rules decide whether later changes hit goodwill or P&L.

Core notes

NCI measurement options (Ind AS 103.19)

For each business combination, the acquirer may measure NCI that are present ownership interests (entitling holders to a proportionate share of net assets on liquidation) at either:

  1. Fair value ("full goodwill" method): NCI measured at its acquisition-date fair value, usually from the market price of shares not acquired (for a listed target) or a valuation. Fair value per share for NCI may be lower than the price paid by the acquirer, because the acquirer's price includes a control premium.
  2. Proportionate share of identifiable net assets ("partial goodwill" method): NCI = NCI % × FV of net identifiable assets. No goodwill is attributed to NCI.

The choice is made deal by deal. Other NCI components (for example equity components of convertibles, share-based payment awards) are measured at fair value or under the relevant standard.

Illustration: consideration for 70% = ₹1,400 crore. FV of net identifiable assets ₹1,500 crore. NCI fair value (30% at the market price) = ₹540 crore.

₹ crore Proportionate method Fair value method
Consideration 1,400 1,400
NCI 30% × 1,500 = 450 540
Less FV net identifiable assets (1,500) (1,500)
Goodwill 350 440

Consequences: the fair value method gives higher goodwill and higher NCI. Impairment losses on goodwill are allocated between parent and NCI on the basis used for profit allocation under the fair value method, but under the proportionate method the goodwill for impairment testing is grossed up to include notional NCI goodwill (Ind AS 36 Appendix C). Later purchases of NCI (equity transactions under Ind AS 110.23) produce differences in equity whose size depends on the initial NCI measure.

Step acquisitions (Ind AS 103.41–42)

When control is obtained in stages (for example from a 30% associate to 65% control), the acquirer:

  1. Remeasures its previously held equity interest at acquisition-date fair value.
  2. Recognises the resulting gain or loss in P&L (or in OCI if the earlier interest was an equity investment designated at FVOCI under Ind AS 109; amounts in OCI are not recycled but may be transferred within equity).
  3. Reclassifies amounts previously in OCI from the associate (for example FX translation reserve) as if the interest had been disposed of.
  4. Includes the fair value of the previously held interest in the goodwill computation (c01).

Illustration: the acquirer holds 30% of an associate (equity-method carrying amount ₹420 crore). It buys another 35% for ₹980 crore (implying ₹2,800 crore for 100%). Fair value of the 30% stake on the acquisition date = ₹750 crore (minority basis, below the control price). Remeasurement gain = 750 − 420 = ₹330 crore in P&L. This gain is non-cash and should be excluded from adjusted EPS.

Contingent consideration (Ind AS 103.39–40, 58)

Contingent consideration (earn-outs) is recognised at acquisition-date fair value as part of consideration transferred. Classification:

  • Financial liability (cash-settled, or settled in a variable number of shares): remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes in P&L (c05).
  • Equity (a fixed number of the acquirer's shares, meeting Ind AS 32 fixed-for-fixed): not remeasured; settlement accounted within equity.
  • Asset (for example a right to return consideration if conditions are met): remeasured through P&L.

Changes in fair value from facts that existed at the acquisition date, identified within the measurement period, adjust goodwill. Changes from post-acquisition events (meeting an earnings target, a share price move) go to P&L.

Distinguish contingent consideration from remuneration: payments forfeited if the seller-employee leaves are remuneration for post-combination services (B55(a)), expensed over the service period.

The measurement period (Ind AS 103.45–50)

If the initial accounting is incomplete at the end of the reporting period in which the combination occurs, the acquirer reports provisional amounts. During the measurement period, it adjusts them retrospectively (as if completed at the acquisition date) for new information about facts and circumstances that existed at that date.

  • The measurement period ends when the acquirer has the information it was seeking, or learns that more information is not obtainable, and cannot exceed one year from the acquisition date.
  • Adjustments change goodwill, with comparative information revised (including additional depreciation or amortisation from the acquisition date).
  • After the measurement period, changes are made only to correct errors under Ind AS 8.
  • Disclosure (B67): the reasons why the initial accounting is incomplete, the assets, liabilities, equity interests or items of consideration affected, and the nature and amount of adjustments.

Illustration: acquisition on 1 November 2026. At 31 March 2027, the customer relationship valuation is provisional at ₹200 crore. In August 2027, the valuer finalises it at ₹260 crore based on attrition data as at acquisition. Adjust retrospectively: intangible +60, DTL +15.1, goodwill −44.9, and restate FY27 amortisation from 1 November 2026. If the change arose from a customer loss after acquisition, it would instead be an impairment in P&L.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • NCI fair value in a listed target after an open offer: low liquidity and the offer itself distort the market price. Use the undisturbed price or a valuation with a discount for lack of control.
  • Put options over NCI: the SHA gives the seller a put on the remaining stake. A financial liability is recognised (Ind AS 32.23) at the PV of the redemption amount. Policy choices on whether NCI continues to be recognised need disclosure.
  • Step acquisition via open offer: if a 20% holder crosses into control through an SPA plus open offer, the 20% is remeasured.
  • Deferred vs contingent: fixed deferred consideration is a financial liability at amortised cost (unwinding in finance cost), not remeasured to fair value.
  • Measurement period for deferred tax: DTA recognition within the period, based on acquisition-date facts, adjusts goodwill (Ind AS 12.68).

Key takeaways

  • NCI can be measured at fair value (full goodwill) or proportionate share of net identifiable assets (partial goodwill), chosen deal by deal.
  • In a step acquisition, the previously held interest is remeasured to fair value with the gain or loss in P&L, and is part of the goodwill computation.
  • Contingent consideration is at fair value. Liability-classified earn-outs are remeasured through P&L; equity-classified ones are not.
  • Provisional PPA amounts can be adjusted retrospectively against goodwill for up to one year, only for facts existing at the acquisition date.
  • Post-acquisition events go to P&L, not goodwill.

Practice Questions

Q1. NCI methods [L1]

Consideration ₹2,100 crore for 60%. FV of net identifiable assets ₹2,500 crore. Market price of shares implies fair value of the 40% NCI of ₹1,250 crore. Compute goodwill and NCI under both methods.

Q2. Step acquisition [L2]

The acquirer holds 25% of a listed company (equity-method carrying amount ₹310 crore; associate's FCTR in OCI attributable ₹12 crore credit). It acquires 26% more under an SPA for ₹780 crore and 15% through an open offer at the same price per share. Fair value of the original 25% on the acquisition date ₹700 crore. FV of net identifiable assets ₹2,400 crore; NCI at proportionate share. Compute the remeasurement gain, the OCI reclassification and goodwill.

💡 Hint: Work out the price per percentage point from the SPA, then count the total consideration for 41%.

Q3. Earn-out remeasurement [L2]

An earn-out pays ₹150 crore in 2 years if EBITDA exceeds ₹90 crore. At acquisition, the probability is 50% and the discount rate 10%. After one year, the probability rises to 80% because a new contract was won after closing. Compute the fair value at acquisition and at the end of Year 1, and the P&L effect, including unwinding.

Q4. Measurement period adjustment [L2]

Acquisition on 1 December 2026; provisional PPA at 31 March 2027 shows PPE step-up ₹100 crore (10-year life). In September 2027, an appraisal of PPE as at 1 December 2026 gives ₹160 crore. Tax 25.17%. Show the retrospective adjustment and the restated FY27 depreciation, and explain what changes if the appraisal arrives in January 2028.
