Subtopic note

Deferred Tax on Fair Value Adjustments and No DTL on Goodwill

Scope: Deferred tax on fair value adjustments under Ind AS 12; no DTL on initial recognition of goodwill

Why it matters

In a share purchase or amalgamation in India, fair-value uplifts in the PPA do not change the tax base of the target's assets. So every rupee of step-up creates a taxable temporary difference and a deferred tax liability, which reduces net identifiable assets and increases goodwill by the same amount. On a ₹1,000 crore intangible allocation at 25.17%, that is ₹252 crore of extra goodwill. In the model, the DTL also unwinds as the step-ups are depreciated or amortised, which is why the after-tax EPS drag of amortisation is amortisation × (1 − t), not the full amount. Missing the DTL is one of the most common PPA errors.

Core notes

Principle

Ind AS 12.19 and 12.66: temporary differences arise in a business combination when the carrying amounts of assets acquired and liabilities assumed (at fair value) differ from their tax bases. The resulting deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognised at the acquisition date and affect goodwill (or the bargain purchase gain).

\[ \text{DTL}_{\text{PPA}} = \sum_i (\text{FV}_i - \text{TB}_i) \times t_i \quad - \quad \text{Existing DTL on the same items in the target's books} \]

where \(\text{FV}_i\) is the fair value, \(\text{TB}_i\) the tax base and \(t_i\) the rate expected to apply when the difference reverses (Ind AS 12.47 and 12.51: the rate consistent with the expected manner of recovery).

In practice the target already has deferred tax on book vs tax differences. The PPA adds deferred tax on the incremental step-up (FV − book value), so the full position is (FV − TB) × t.

What the tax base is in Indian structures

Structure Tax base of target assets after the deal DTL on step-ups?
Share purchase Unchanged (target entity continues) Yes, on all step-ups and new intangibles
Amalgamation (Sec 2(1B), Sec 47) Carries over: WDV of block (Explanation 2 to Sec 43(6)); cost for capital assets (Sec 49) Yes
Slump sale Buyer's actual cost allocated to assets Little or none on depreciable assets; DTL on goodwill not recognised
Asset purchase Price paid per asset Little or none

The rate is the target entity's rate: 25.17% for a domestic company under Sec 115BAA (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess), or 34.94% under the normal regime with surcharge, or the capital-gains rate where recovery is through sale.

No DTL on goodwill (Ind AS 12.15(a))

Ind AS 12.15(a) prohibits recognising a DTL on the initial recognition of goodwill. The reason is circularity: goodwill is a residual, so recognising a DTL on it would increase goodwill, which would increase the DTL, and so on. In a share deal, goodwill's tax base is nil anyway (goodwill does not exist for tax purposes in the target). After Finance Act 2021, goodwill is not depreciable for tax in any structure, so there is no tax-deductible goodwill in Indian deals after AY 2021-22 (s08). Subsequent DTL on goodwill that is tax-deductible (in other jurisdictions) is recognised as the temporary difference arises, but that rarely applies in India now.

DTAs in a business combination

  • The target's own tax losses and unabsorbed depreciation: recognise a DTA if it is probable that taxable profit will be available (Ind AS 12.34), considering the combined group's profits and the legal availability of the losses (Sec 72A in an amalgamation, Sec 79 in a share deal of a closely held company).
  • Contingent liabilities recognised in the PPA that will be deductible when paid create DTAs.
  • Acquirer's own DTAs (Ind AS 12.67): if the combination makes it probable that the acquirer can now use its own previously unrecognised tax losses, the acquirer recognises that DTA in P&L, not in goodwill.
  • Subsequent recognition of the target's DTAs (Ind AS 12.68): within the 12-month measurement period, if based on new information about facts at the acquisition date, adjust goodwill. Otherwise recognise in P&L.

Unwinding

As a step-up is depreciated or amortised in the consolidated books, there is no matching tax deduction, so the temporary difference falls and the DTL releases to P&L as deferred tax income:

\[ \Delta \text{DTL}_t = \Delta \text{Dep or Amort}_t \times t \]

Net after-tax P&L effect = \(\Delta \text{Dep}_t \times (1 - t)\). The current tax charge is unchanged, because tax depreciation is unaffected. So the DTL unwinding affects EPS, but not cash taxes.

Illustration

Share purchase of 100% for ₹3,000 crore. Target book net assets ₹1,100 crore; existing goodwill nil. Fair value adjustments: brand ₹600 crore (indefinite life), customer relationships ₹400 crore (10 years), plant +₹200 crore (5 years remaining). Tax rate 25.17%. Land none.

₹ crore Without DTL (wrong) With DTL (correct)
Book net assets 1,100 1,100
Fair value adjustments 1,200 1,200
DTL on 1,200 × 25.17% – (302.0)
FV of net identifiable assets 2,300 1,998.0
Goodwill = 3,000 − FV net assets 700 1,002.0

Year 1 amortisation and depreciation = 400/10 + 200/5 = ₹80 crore; DTL release = 80 × 25.17% = ₹20.1 crore; net PAT drag = ₹59.9 crore. The brand's DTL (₹151 crore) stays until the brand is impaired or sold.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Indefinite-life intangibles and land: the DTL is measured using the rate for the expected manner of recovery. For land, recovery through sale suggests the capital-gains rate (12.5% LTCG plus surcharge and cess, for a long-held asset). For a brand used in the business, recovery through use suggests the corporate rate. Document the judgement.
  • Outside basis differences: deferred tax on the difference between the carrying amount of the investment in the subsidiary and its tax base (undistributed profits) is not recognised if the parent controls the timing of reversal and reversal is not probable in the foreseeable future (Ind AS 12.39).
  • Rate change: if the target moves to Sec 115BAA after the deal, remeasure DTL; the effect goes to P&L.
  • Goodwill on amalgamation in the acquirer's standalone books: not depreciable for tax after FA 2021, so it has a nil tax base, and the 12.15(a) exemption applies to its initial recognition.
  • Exemption interaction: the initial recognition exemption for assets (12.15(b)) does not apply to business combinations. DTL on intangibles is always recognised in a PPA.

Key takeaways

  • In share deals and amalgamations, step-ups do not change the tax base, so DTL = (FV − tax base) × t on every step-up and new intangible.
  • The DTL reduces net identifiable assets and increases goodwill by the same amount.
  • No DTL is recognised on the initial recognition of goodwill (Ind AS 12.15(a)).
  • The DTL unwinds as step-ups are depreciated or amortised, so the after-tax drag is the charge × (1 − t), with no effect on cash tax.
  • The acquirer's own DTAs made recoverable by the deal go to P&L, not goodwill.

Practice Questions

Q1. DTL and goodwill [L1]

Consideration ₹1,800 crore for 100%. Book net assets ₹700 crore; intangibles identified ₹650 crore; PPE step-up ₹150 crore. Tax rate 25.17%. Compute goodwill with and without the DTL and explain the difference.

Q2. Unwinding schedule [L2]

Customer relationships ₹300 crore (6 years), technology ₹120 crore (4 years), plant step-up ₹90 crore (9 years). Tax rate 25.17%. Prepare a 4-year schedule of amortisation and depreciation, DTL release, closing DTL and after-tax P&L drag.

Q3. Acquirer's unrecognised losses [L2]

The acquirer has ₹400 crore of unrecognised tax losses. After acquiring a profitable target, the combined group's forecast makes it probable that the acquirer can use ₹250 crore within the carry-forward period. Is the DTA recognised in goodwill or in P&L? Compute the amount at 25.17%.

💡 Hint: Ind AS 12.67 deals specifically with the acquirer's own deferred tax assets.

Q4. Structure and DTL [L3]

The same ₹2,500 crore target can be bought as a share purchase or a slump sale. In the slump sale the buyer allocates ₹1,400 crore to plant and technology (tax-depreciable) and the balance to goodwill. Compare the PPA (DTL and goodwill), the Year 1 P&L, and the cash tax over five years under each structure, assuming tax depreciation at 15% WDV for plant and 25% WDV for technology, and a 25.17% tax rate.
