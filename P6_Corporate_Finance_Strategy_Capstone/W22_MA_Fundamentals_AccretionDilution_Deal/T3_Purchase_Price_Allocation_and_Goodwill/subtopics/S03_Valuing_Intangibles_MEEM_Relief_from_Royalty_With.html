Subtopic note

Valuing Intangibles: MEEM, Relief from Royalty, With-and-Without, Cost

Scope: Valuation approaches: MEEM (customer relationships), relief-from-royalty (brands), with-and-without (non-compete), replacement cost (assembled software)

Why it matters

The fair value of each intangible decides how much of the premium is amortised (and over what life) and how much stays in goodwill. These are Level 3 fair values under Ind AS 113, built on management forecasts, royalty rates, attrition and discount rates, so they are among the most judgemental numbers in financial reporting and are reviewed closely by auditors and regulators. A deal analyst does not need to be the valuer, but must understand the methods well enough to estimate the PPA in the model and challenge the valuer's report.

Core notes

Three approaches under Ind AS 113

  • Income approach: PV of future cash flows attributable to the asset (MEEM, relief from royalty, with-and-without, greenfield).
  • Market approach: prices of comparable transactions (rare for intangibles; used for spectrum, licences, some brands).
  • Cost approach: cost to replace the asset's service capacity, adjusted for obsolescence (software, assembled workforce for CAC purposes).

MEEM: multi-period excess earnings method (customer relationships)

MEEM isolates the cash flows of one asset (usually the primary value driver, often customer relationships) by deducting returns on all other assets that contribute to those cash flows.

Steps:

  1. Start with revenue from existing customers only, declining with an attrition rate (for example 10% a year).
  2. Deduct operating costs to get EBITDA, then deduct tax to get after-tax operating profit (excluding costs of acquiring new customers, which do not relate to existing relationships).
  3. Deduct contributory asset charges (CACs): a fair return on (and of) the other assets used: NWC, fixed assets, the brand or technology (often as a royalty), and assembled workforce.
  4. The remainder is the excess earnings attributable to customer relationships. Discount at a rate above WACC (reflecting intangible risk).
  5. Add a tax amortisation benefit (TAB) if a market participant would get tax deductions for the asset. In India, for a share deal there is no step-up, and customer relationships may not be depreciable intangibles under Sec 32 in any case, so TAB is usually excluded. Decide on a market-participant basis and disclose.

\[ \text{FV}_{\text{CR}} = \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{\left[\text{EBITDA}_t (1 - T) - \text{CAC}_t\right]}{(1 + r_{\text{CR}})^{t - 0.5}} \ (+\ \text{TAB}) \]

with mid-year discounting \((t - 0.5)\).

Illustration (₹ crore, simplified 3-year view of a longer schedule). Existing-customer revenue Year 1 ₹500, attrition 15% a year, EBITDA margin 20%, tax 25.17%, CACs 6% of revenue after tax, discount rate 14%.

Year 1 Year 2 Year 3
Revenue from existing customers 500.0 425.0 361.3
EBITDA (20%) 100.0 85.0 72.3

The full schedule continues until attrition makes the cash flows immaterial (for example 15–20 years).

Relief from royalty (brands, technology)

The value of owning a brand equals the royalties the owner is spared from paying to license it. Steps:

  1. Forecast revenue attributable to the brand (including growth for the brand's life, or into perpetuity for indefinite-life brands).
  2. Apply a market royalty rate from comparable licensing agreements (for example 2–5% for FMCG brands, higher for luxury or pharma), adjusted for the brand's profitability (cross-check that the royalty is a sensible share of EBIT, often 25–33% under the "profit split" rule of thumb).
  3. Deduct tax on the royalty saved.
  4. Discount at a brand-specific rate and add TAB if applicable.

\[ \text{FV}_{\text{brand}} = \sum_{t} \frac{\text{Revenue}_t \times \rho \times (1 - T)}{(1 + r_B)^{t - 0.5}} + \text{TV} \]

Example: brand revenue ₹1,200 crore growing 6%, royalty 3%, tax 25.17%, discount rate 13%, indefinite life. Year 1 after-tax royalty = 1,200 × 3% × 0.7483 = ₹26.9 crore. Gordon value (mid-year) ≈ 26.9 / (0.13 − 0.06) × 1.13^0.5 ≈ ₹409 crore.

With-and-without method (non-compete)

Value the business twice: with the non-compete in place and without it (assuming the seller competes). The difference in PV, multiplied by the probability that the seller would actually compete (and be able to), is the value of the agreement.

\[ \text{FV}_{\text{NC}} = p_{\text{compete}} \times \left[\text{PV}(\text{CF}_{\text{with}}) - \text{PV}(\text{CF}_{\text{without}})\right] \]

Inputs: lost revenue and margins from the seller's competition, time for the seller to ramp up, enforceability (non-competes on employees are unenforceable in India under Sec 27 of the Indian Contract Act, but restraints on the seller of goodwill of a business are allowed as an exception), and the seller's capacity to compete.

Example: without the non-compete, cash flows fall by ₹12 crore, ₹18 crore and ₹20 crore in Years 1–3 (after tax). At 13% and a 50% probability of competition, FV ≈ 0.5 × (10.6 + 14.1 + 13.9) ≈ ₹19.3 crore.

Replacement cost (assembled software)

For internally developed software that is not sold to customers (for example a core platform or ERP customisation), the cost approach estimates what it would cost to rebuild an equivalent:

\[ \text{FV} = \text{Effort (person-months)} \times \text{Loaded cost per person-month} \times (1 + \text{Developer's profit}) \times (1 - \text{Obsolescence \%}) \times (1 - T \text{ if no TAB}) \]

Example: 600 person-months, ₹2.5 lakh per month, 15% developer's profit, 30% obsolescence. FV pre-tax = 600 × 2.5 × 1.15 × 0.7 = ₹1,207.5 lakh ≈ ₹12.1 crore. Practice varies on whether the cost is computed pre- or post-tax; be consistent with the market-participant view and with how the DTL is computed.

Technology sold to customers (SaaS, licensed software) is usually valued by relief from royalty or MEEM instead.

Reconciliation checks

  • WARA vs WACC vs IRR: the weighted average return on assets (returns on working capital, fixed assets, intangibles and goodwill weighted by their fair values) should reconcile to the WACC and the deal IRR. Goodwill should carry the highest implied return.
  • Only one MEEM: apply MEEM to one asset only. Using it for two assets double counts.
  • Consistency with the deal model: forecasts used for valuing intangibles should be market-participant forecasts without acquirer-specific synergies.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Royalty rate selection: databases give wide ranges. Validate with profit-split and with the brand's margin premium over unbranded peers.
  • Attrition estimates: use historical customer data (cohorts by year of acquisition). One large customer may need separate valuation.
  • TAB in India: include only if a market participant buying the asset in a taxable asset transaction could claim tax depreciation (for example on technology, licences or trademarks under Sec 32). Exclude for goodwill and where no tax amortisation is available.
  • Discount rates: intangibles generally carry higher rates than tangible assets. A rate lower than WACC for customer relationships needs explanation.
  • Non-compete enforceability: if the restraint is not enforceable or the seller cannot compete, the value is small.

Key takeaways

  • MEEM values the primary intangible (usually customer relationships) as excess earnings after contributory asset charges, with attrition.
  • Relief from royalty values brands and technology as the after-tax royalty saved.
  • With-and-without values non-competes as the probability-weighted loss avoided.
  • Replacement cost values internally used software as the cost to rebuild less obsolescence.
  • Reconcile WARA to WACC and IRR, apply MEEM to only one asset, and use market-participant assumptions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Relief from royalty [L2]

Brand revenue Year 1 ₹800 crore, growing 8% for 5 years and 4% thereafter; royalty rate 4%; tax 25.17%; discount rate 14%; indefinite life; no TAB. Value the brand using mid-year discounting.

Q2. MEEM build [L3]

Existing-customer revenue Year 1 ₹300 crore with 12% attrition; EBITDA margin 18%; CACs: NWC at 1.5% of revenue, fixed assets at 3%, brand royalty at 2% (all after tax); tax 25.17%; discount rate 15%; horizon 10 years. Build the MEEM schedule in Excel and compute fair value. State the formulas you would use for attrition and mid-year discounting.

💡 Hint: Revenue in year t is Year 1 revenue × (1 − attrition) to the power (t − 1).

Q3. With-and-without [L2]

Without a 4-year non-compete, the target would lose after-tax cash flows of ₹8, ₹15, ₹20 and ₹22 crore in Years 1–4. Probability that the promoter would compete: 60%. Discount rate 13% (year-end). Value the non-compete and discuss enforceability under Indian law.

Q4. Challenging a valuer [L3]

A valuer's PPA assigns 85% of the premium to an indefinite-life brand, values customer relationships with MEEM using a discount rate below WACC, and also uses MEEM for technology. List the challenges you would raise and the reconciliation you would request.
