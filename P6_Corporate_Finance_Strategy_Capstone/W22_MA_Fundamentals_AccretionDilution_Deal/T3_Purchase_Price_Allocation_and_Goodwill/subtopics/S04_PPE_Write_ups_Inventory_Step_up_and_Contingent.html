Subtopic note

PPE Write-ups, Inventory Step-up and Contingent Liabilities

Scope: Fair value write-ups of PPE, inventory step-up (flows through COGS quickly) and contingent liabilities recognised at acquisition

Why it matters

Tangible step-ups are often the second-largest PPA adjustment after intangibles, especially in manufacturing, cement, steel, real estate and infrastructure deals, where land and plant carried at decades-old cost are worth far more today. Each step-up has a distinct P&L pattern: land never depreciates, plant depreciates over its remaining life, and inventory step-up reverses within months through cost of goods sold, creating a one-off margin dip right after closing. Contingent liabilities that the target never recorded become balance-sheet liabilities in the acquirer's consolidated accounts. All three must be modelled to get post-deal EBITDA, EBIT, PAT and the DTL right.

Core notes

PPE fair value write-ups

Under Ind AS 103.18 and Ind AS 113, PPE is measured at fair value at the acquisition date, irrespective of the target's carrying amount or the cost model it uses.

  • Land: market approach (comparable sales, circle rates as a floor reference, valuer reports). Freehold land is not depreciated, so its step-up creates a permanent DTL that unwinds only on sale. Leasehold land (long-term leases from industrial development corporations) is an ROU asset under Ind AS 116; its step-up is amortised over the remaining lease term.
  • Buildings and plant: usually the cost approach, as depreciated replacement cost (DRC): current replacement cost new less physical deterioration, functional obsolescence and economic obsolescence. For specialised plant with an active second-hand market, the market approach is used. Economic obsolescence matters when the business earns less than the cost of capital on replacement cost (the "income cap").
  • Capital work in progress: at cost if recently incurred, adjusted for impairment or overruns.
  • Useful lives: the acquirer reassesses remaining useful lives and residual values at the acquisition date. The step-up is depreciated over the remaining life, not the original life.

Incremental depreciation:

\[ \Delta \text{Dep}_t = \frac{\text{FV} - \text{Book value}}{\text{Remaining useful life}} \]

for straight-line assets (land excluded).

Inventory step-up

Inventory is measured at fair value:

  • Finished goods and merchandise: estimated selling price less costs of disposal, less a reasonable profit allowance for the selling effort of the acquirer (Ind AS 103 illustrative guidance; the profit for the acquirer's future selling effort is excluded from fair value).
  • Work in progress: selling price less costs to complete, costs of disposal and profit on completing and selling.
  • Raw materials: current replacement cost.

Because fair value exceeds the target's cost, the step-up flows through COGS as the inventory is sold, usually within one or two inventory turns (weeks or months). This reduces post-deal gross margin and EBITDA once, then disappears. In the model:

\[ \Delta \text{COGS}_{\text{Year 1}} = \text{Inventory step-up} \times \min\left(1, \frac{\text{Months post-close in Year 1}}{\text{Inventory holding period in months}}\right) \]

Brokers and management usually exclude it from adjusted EBITDA. In statutory EPS it is a real charge in the first quarter after closing. The related DTL reverses at the same speed, so the after-tax hit is step-up × (1 − t).

Contingent liabilities (Ind AS 103.22–23)

Ind AS 37 recognises a provision only if an outflow is probable. Ind AS 103.23 overrides this in a business combination: the acquirer recognises a contingent liability assumed if

  1. it is a present obligation arising from past events, and
  2. its fair value can be measured reliably,

even if it is not probable that an outflow will be required. Possible obligations (whose existence depends on uncertain future events) are not recognised.

Typical Indian examples: tax demands under appeal (income tax, GST, customs), environmental claims, product liability suits, labour disputes, and guarantees given.

Measurement: fair value, which is the price a market participant would demand to assume the obligation. It is often estimated as the probability-weighted expected outflow, discounted, plus a risk margin.

Subsequent measurement (103.56): until settled, cancelled or expired, measured at the higher of (a) the amount that would be recognised under Ind AS 37, and (b) the amount initially recognised less cumulative income recognised under Ind AS 115, if applicable.

Indemnification assets: if the seller indemnifies the buyer for the contingent liability (a specific indemnity in the SPA), the acquirer recognises an indemnification asset on the same basis as the liability, subject to a collectability allowance (103.27–28). This offsets the effect on goodwill.

Illustration

Target book values: land ₹60 crore (FV ₹260 crore), plant ₹400 crore (FV ₹520 crore, remaining life 8 years), inventory ₹150 crore (FV ₹174 crore, holding period 2 months). A GST demand of ₹50 crore is under appeal; the probability of loss is 30% and fair value is estimated at ₹17 crore. The seller gives a full indemnity. Tax rate 25.17%. Closing date 1 October (FY ends 31 March).

Item Step-up (₹ crore) Year 1 P&L impact (pre-tax) DTL at acquisition
Land 200 Nil 50.3
Plant 120 120/8 × 6/12 = 7.5 depreciation 30.2
Inventory 24 24 through COGS (within 2 months) 6.0
Contingent liability (17) Remeasured each period (4.3) DTA, if deductible when paid
Indemnification asset 17 Mirrors liability 4.3

Year 1 pre-tax drag = 7.5 + 24 = ₹31.5 crore; after-tax = 31.5 × (1 − 0.2517) = ₹23.6 crore. From Year 2, the drag is ₹15 crore a year of plant depreciation (₹11.2 crore after tax) until Year 8.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Circle rates vs market value: stamp-duty ready reckoner rates are not fair value. Use registered valuer reports and comparable transactions.
  • Economic obsolescence: in a loss-making target, DRC can exceed what the business can support. Apply an income-based test, or the PPE may be impaired on day one.
  • Inventory under weighted average costing: the step-up blends into the weighted average and is released as that inventory is consumed. Track it separately in the model.
  • Contingent liabilities vs provisions: if the target already recognised a provision under Ind AS 37, the acquirer remeasures it at fair value. Do not count it twice.
  • Indemnities and credit risk: an indemnification asset from a promoter group with weak finances needs a valuation allowance. An escrow is better evidence of collectability.
  • Tax base of plant: in an amalgamation, the tax WDV carries over (Explanation 2 to Sec 43(6)), so plant step-ups create a DTL. In a slump sale the buyer gets a new tax base, so less DTL arises.

Key takeaways

  • PPE is measured at fair value (market or DRC) with reassessed remaining lives. Land step-ups are not depreciated; plant step-ups are depreciated over the remaining life.
  • Inventory step-up flows through COGS within one or two turns, creating a one-off margin dip after closing.
  • Contingent liabilities that are present obligations with reliably measurable fair value are recognised even if an outflow is not probable (Ind AS 103.23).
  • Seller indemnities create indemnification assets measured on the same basis.
  • Every step-up carries a DTL that unwinds in line with its P&L charge.

Practice Questions

Q1. Step-up schedule [L2]

Plant book ₹300 crore, FV ₹390 crore, remaining life 6 years; buildings book ₹100 crore, FV ₹160 crore, remaining life 20 years; freehold land book ₹20 crore, FV ₹140 crore. Closing on 1 July 2026, FY ends 31 March. Compute incremental depreciation for FY27 and FY28 and the DTL at acquisition at 25.17%.

Q2. Inventory step-up timing [L2]

Inventory FV ₹96 crore vs cost ₹80 crore; inventory holding period 45 days. Closing on 15 February 2027. Compute the COGS impact in FY27 and FY28, the after-tax impact, and explain how adjusted EBITDA would be presented.

💡 Hint: Work out how much of the inventory is sold before 31 March.

Q3. Contingent liability and indemnity [L2]

Pending income-tax demand ₹80 crore under appeal (probability of loss 25%, expected payment in 3 years, discount rate 9%). Seller indemnity for 60% of any loss, backed by an escrow of ₹30 crore. Compute the fair value of the contingent liability and the indemnification asset, and the net effect on goodwill.

Q4. Day-one impairment risk [L3]

The valuer's DRC for a loss-making target's plant is ₹1,100 crore against book ₹700 crore, but a DCF of the business gives an EV of ₹900 crore. Discuss how economic obsolescence should be applied, the effect on goodwill, and the risk of an Ind AS 36 impairment at the first year-end.
