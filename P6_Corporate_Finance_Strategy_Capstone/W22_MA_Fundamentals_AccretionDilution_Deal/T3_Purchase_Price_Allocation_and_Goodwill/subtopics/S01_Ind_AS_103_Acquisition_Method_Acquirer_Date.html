Subtopic note

Ind AS 103 Acquisition Method: Acquirer, Date, Recognition

Scope: Ind AS 103 acquisition method: identify acquirer, acquisition date, recognise and measure identifiable assets/liabilities at fair value

Why it matters

The acquisition method decides what appears on the consolidated balance sheet the day after closing: which assets and liabilities are recognised, at what values, and how much is left as goodwill. Those values drive post-deal depreciation and amortisation, deferred tax, impairment testing and EPS for years. Identifying the acquirer decides whose assets are revalued. The acquisition date decides from when the target's results are consolidated, which is the time-weighting point for pro-forma EPS. For an analyst, the PPA is the bridge between the deal model and the reported numbers.

Core notes

Scope: is it a business combination?

Ind AS 103 applies when an acquirer obtains control of a business (Ind AS 103.3, Appendix B7–B12D). A business is an integrated set of activities and assets that includes, at minimum, an input and a substantive process that together significantly contribute to the ability to create outputs. The optional concentration test (B7A–B7C): if substantially all of the fair value of gross assets acquired is concentrated in a single identifiable asset or group of similar assets, the set is not a business, and the transaction is an asset acquisition (cost allocated by relative fair values, no goodwill, no deferred tax on initial recognition).

Excluded from Ind AS 103: joint arrangements' formation, acquisitions of assets that are not businesses, and combinations of entities under common control (dealt with in Appendix C, s07).

The four steps (Ind AS 103.5)

  1. Identify the acquirer.
  2. Determine the acquisition date.
  3. Recognise and measure the identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any NCI.
  4. Recognise and measure goodwill or a gain from a bargain purchase (c01, s07).

Step 1: identify the acquirer (103.6–7, B13–B18)

The acquirer is the entity that obtains control as defined in Ind AS 110 (power, exposure to variable returns, and ability to use power to affect returns). If that is not clear:

  • In a cash or debt-funded deal, the acquirer is usually the entity that transfers cash or incurs liabilities.
  • In a share exchange, consider: relative voting rights after the combination (who holds the larger share), existence of a large minority voting block, composition of the governing body, composition of senior management, and the terms of the exchange (who pays a premium).
  • The acquirer is usually the entity whose relative size (assets, revenue, profit) is significantly larger.
  • A new entity formed to issue shares is not the acquirer. One of the combining entities is.

Reverse acquisition (B19–B27): the legal acquirer (issuer of shares) is the accounting acquiree when the target's shareholders gain control. The consolidated accounts are a continuation of the legal subsidiary's accounts. This is common when a large unlisted company merges into a small listed shell.

Step 2: acquisition date (103.8–9)

The date on which the acquirer obtains control, normally the closing date when consideration is paid and shares are transferred. It can be earlier or later if a written agreement transfers control on another date.

Indian schemes need care. The appointed date in an NCLT scheme is a legal effective date, often in the past, but control for Ind AS 103 usually passes only when the scheme becomes effective (the order is filed with ROC) or when regulatory approvals are complete. ICAI guidance (ITFG clarifications) is that, for business combinations not under common control, the acquisition date is when control is obtained, which may differ from the appointed date. For common-control mergers, Appendix C restates comparatives anyway.

Why it matters: results are consolidated from the acquisition date, fair values are measured at that date, and the share price for consideration in shares is the price on that date.

Step 3: recognise and measure identifiable assets and liabilities

Recognition principle (103.10–14): recognise identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed if they meet the Conceptual Framework definitions at the acquisition date and are part of the exchange (not separate transactions). This leads to recognition of items not on the target's balance sheet: internally generated brands, customer relationships, technology and contingent liabilities.

Measurement principle (103.18): measure at acquisition-date fair value under Ind AS 113 (the price received to sell an asset in an orderly transaction between market participants). Use market-participant assumptions, not acquirer-specific synergies.

Exceptions (103.21–31A):

Item Treatment
Contingent liabilities Recognised if a present obligation from past events and fair value is reliably measurable, even if outflow is not probable (103.23)
Income taxes Ind AS 12 (deferred tax on temporary differences, not fair value)
Employee benefits Ind AS 19
Indemnification assets Same basis as the indemnified item, subject to collectability
Reacquired rights Value based on remaining contractual term only
Share-based payment awards Ind AS 102 market-based measure
Assets held for sale Ind AS 105 (fair value less costs to sell)
Leases where acquiree is lessee Lease liability at PV of remaining payments; ROU asset equal to it, adjusted for favourable/unfavourable terms

Classification (103.15–16): classify and designate assets and liabilities (for example financial instrument categories, hedge designations) based on conditions at the acquisition date, except lease and insurance contract classifications, which keep their original basis.

Separate transactions (103.51–52, B50): amounts that are not part of the exchange are accounted separately, for example settlement of pre-existing relationships, remuneration to selling employees and reimbursement of the acquirer's costs.

Target book net assets ₹1,200 crore (including existing goodwill of ₹80 crore). Fair value adjustments: PPE +₹250 crore, inventory +₹30 crore, brand ₹300 crore (not on book), customer relationships ₹180 crore, contingent liability ₹40 crore. Tax rate 25.17%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Share swap between similar-sized companies: weigh all B13–B18 indicators. Board and management composition often decide.
  • Control before legal completion: an SPA that gives the buyer power over relevant activities from signing (for example via interim covenants) does not usually transfer control; protective covenants are not power.
  • Step acquisitions: if the acquirer already held an associate stake, remeasure it at fair value on the acquisition date (s06).
  • Restructuring provisions: the acquirer's own plans are not liabilities of the acquiree at acquisition date. They are post-combination expenses.
  • Fair value vs synergies: an asset the acquirer does not intend to use (a competing brand to be retired) is still measured at market-participant fair value (defensive value).

Key takeaways

  • Ind AS 103 applies only to acquisitions of a business; the concentration test can make it an asset acquisition.
  • Four steps: identify the acquirer, set the acquisition date, recognise and measure net identifiable assets and NCI, then compute goodwill or a bargain purchase.
  • The acquirer is the entity that obtains control; in share exchanges use the B13–B18 indicators, and watch for reverse acquisitions.
  • The acquisition date is when control passes, which may differ from the scheme's appointed date.
  • Fair value is the rule, with exceptions for contingent liabilities, taxes, employee benefits, leases and share-based payments.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identifying the acquirer [L2]

Company A (market cap ₹6,000 crore) issues shares to the shareholders of Company B (market cap ₹9,000 crore). After the merger, B's former shareholders hold 58%, B's CEO becomes CEO, and the board has 5 B nominees and 4 A nominees. A is the listed legal acquirer. Identify the accounting acquirer and explain the consequences for the consolidated financial statements.

Q2. Acquisition date in a scheme [L2]

A scheme of amalgamation (not under common control) has an appointed date of 1 April 2026. NCLT sanctions it on 20 November 2026 and the order is filed with ROC on 5 December 2026. CCI approval was obtained on 30 July 2026. Determine the acquisition date and explain how FY27 consolidated results are affected.

Q3. Business or asset? [L2]

An acquirer pays ₹520 crore for a company whose assets are a solar plant (fair value ₹480 crore), a PPA with a discom (fair value ₹30 crore) and working capital of ₹10 crore. Operations are outsourced to an O&M contractor. Apply the concentration test and state the accounting consequences.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the plant and its PPA are a single identifiable asset under B7B.

Q4. Fair value of net assets [L3]

Book net assets ₹800 crore include goodwill of ₹50 crore and a deferred tax asset of ₹20 crore. Fair value adjustments: land +₹120 crore (tax base unchanged), technology ₹90 crore, order backlog ₹25 crore, contingent liability ₹15 crore (outflow possible but not probable). Tax rate 25.17%. Compute FV of net identifiable assets and explain each line.
