Subtopic note

Identifiable Intangibles in a Business Combination

Scope: Identifiable intangibles: customer relationships, brands, technology, order backlog, non-compete, licences

Why it matters

In most Indian acquisitions of consumer, pharma, technology or service businesses, the premium over book value is not explained by factories. It is explained by brands, customer relationships, technology and licences that the target built internally and never recognised on its balance sheet. Ind AS 103 requires the acquirer to identify and measure these separately from goodwill. Doing so matters for three reasons: finite-life intangibles are amortised (reducing EPS, t4), they carry deferred tax (s05), and leaving them in goodwill misstates both the balance sheet and the impairment tests.

Core notes

Identifiability criteria (Ind AS 38.12 and Ind AS 103 B31–B34)

An intangible asset acquired in a business combination is recognised separately from goodwill if it is identifiable, meaning it meets either:

  • the contractual-legal criterion: it arises from contractual or other legal rights, whether or not those rights are transferable or separable (licences, patents, trademarks, supply contracts, non-competes); or
  • the separability criterion: it can be separated and sold, transferred, licensed, rented or exchanged, individually or with a related contract, asset or liability (customer lists, unpatented technology, databases).

In a business combination, the probability and reliable measurement criteria of Ind AS 38 are deemed to be satisfied (Ind AS 38.33), so an identifiable intangible is always recognised at fair value.

Not identifiable: the assembled workforce (B37) and potential contracts under negotiation at the acquisition date (B38). Their value stays in goodwill.

The main categories

Customer relationships. Value from expected future business with existing customers beyond any current contract. They meet the contractual-legal criterion if there is a contract (even if no order is currently open) or the separability criterion if they can be sold. In B2B businesses (auto components, specialty chemicals, CDMO pharma, IT services) they are usually the largest intangible. Valued with MEEM (s03). Useful life is based on observed attrition (for example 8–15 years). Distinguish customer contracts (open contracts, including order backlog) from customer relationships (the expected renewals).

Brands and trademarks. Registered trademarks meet the contractual-legal criterion. Brands with strong consumer recognition (FMCG, consumer durables, paints, quick-service restaurants) are valued with the relief-from-royalty method. Useful life can be indefinite if there is no foreseeable limit to the period over which the brand will generate cash flows (Ind AS 38.88), in which case the brand is not amortised but tested annually for impairment. Many acquirers assign a finite life (10–20 years) to regional or secondary brands, and indefinite life to heritage brands with continued investment.

Technology. Patented technology (contractual-legal), unpatented know-how, software, formulations, trade secrets and processes (separable if they can be licensed). Valued with relief-from-royalty or MEEM, or with replacement cost for internally developed software (s03). Useful life reflects technological obsolescence, often 3–8 years.

Order backlog. Contracted orders not yet fulfilled at the acquisition date. Valued as the after-tax excess earnings on the backlog, net of contributory asset charges. Life is short (the period to execute the orders, often less than 2 years), so its amortisation front-loads the EPS drag.

Non-compete agreements. Agreements by the sellers not to compete. They meet the contractual-legal criterion. Valued with the with-and-without method (s03). Life equals the term of the agreement. If the non-compete is paid separately to the promoter, assess whether it is part of consideration, and for listed targets note that the SAST Reg 8 price includes non-compete fees.

Licences and permits. Telecom spectrum, mining leases, broadcasting, FSSAI or drug manufacturing licences, regulatory approvals (ANDA approvals for pharma), airport slots and franchise agreements. Contractual-legal. Values often come from the income approach (greenfield or MEEM) or market comparables (spectrum auction prices). Life is the licence term, including renewals only if renewal is likely without significant cost.

Other intangibles: favourable contracts (supply or lease terms better than market), databases, websites and domain names, internet user bases, and reacquired rights (rights the acquirer had previously granted to the target, valued over the remaining contract term only).

Illustration: allocating a premium

A listed FMCG acquirer buys a regional food company for ₹2,000 crore (equity, 100%). Book net assets ₹500 crore, no existing goodwill. Valuation identifies:

Intangible Method Fair value (₹ crore) Useful life
Brand Relief from royalty 650 Indefinite
Distributor relationships MEEM 220 12 years
Recipes and formulations Relief from royalty 80 8 years
Non-compete with promoter With-and-without 40 5 years

Total identified intangibles ₹990 crore. DTL at 25.17% = ₹249.2 crore (share deal, tax base nil). FV of net identifiable assets = 500 + 990 − 249.2 = ₹1,240.8 crore (ignoring other step-ups). Goodwill = 2,000 − 1,240.8 = ₹759.2 crore.

Annual amortisation = 220/12 + 80/8 + 40/5 = 18.3 + 10 + 8 = ₹36.3 crore pre-tax; ₹27.2 crore after the DTL unwinds (c04). The brand is not amortised.

If all premium were left in goodwill, there would be no amortisation and no DTL. EPS would look better, but the balance sheet would be wrong and the auditor would not accept it.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Indefinite life brands: justify with evidence (brand history, continuing advertising, market share) and review annually (Ind AS 38.109). Moving to finite life triggers amortisation and an impairment test.
  • Overlapping intangibles: a brand and customer relationships can double count the same cash flows. Use contributory asset charges and separate the cash flows carefully.
  • Defensive intangibles: a competing brand that the acquirer will retire is still recognised at market-participant fair value. Its life reflects the period over which it contributes to the acquirer's value (often short).
  • Customer relationships with related parties: a captive customer (for example the seller's group) may have different attrition. Check contract terms.
  • Licences requiring regulatory approval to transfer: if the licence does not transfer, it is not acquired. Factor approval risk into the value.

Key takeaways

  • Intangibles are identifiable if they meet the contractual-legal or separability criterion. In a business combination they are always recognised at fair value.
  • Assembled workforce and potential contracts are not identifiable and remain in goodwill.
  • Main categories: customer relationships, brands, technology, order backlog, non-compete and licences, each with a typical valuation method and useful life.
  • Finite-life intangibles are amortised. Indefinite-life brands are tested annually for impairment.
  • Identifying intangibles creates amortisation drag and DTL and reduces goodwill.

Practice Questions

Q1. Identifiability [L1]

State whether each is recognised separately from goodwill: (a) the target's 1,200 skilled engineers; (b) an unregistered but well-known brand; (c) a customer list that the target's privacy policy prohibits selling; (d) a 3-year supply contract at market rates with an auto OEM; (e) a contract under negotiation at the acquisition date; (f) a pharma ANDA approval.

Q2. Allocating premium and EPS drag [L2]

Purchase price ₹1,500 crore for 100%. Book net assets ₹420 crore (including goodwill of ₹30 crore). Intangibles: customer relationships ₹260 crore (10 years), technology ₹120 crore (6 years), order backlog ₹45 crore (18 months), brand ₹200 crore (15 years). Tax rate 25.17%, share deal. Compute goodwill and the after-tax amortisation in Years 1 and 2.

💡 Hint: The backlog's 18-month life means Year 2 carries only part of a year's amortisation.

Q3. Indefinite-life judgement [L2]

The valuation team proposes an indefinite life for a 40-year-old regional snack brand with stable share. The acquirer plans to migrate the brand's products to its national brand over 4 years. Discuss the useful life and the impact on the PPA and EPS.

Q4. Non-compete and SAST [L3]

In the acquisition of a listed target, the promoter receives ₹520 per share plus a non-compete fee of ₹60 crore (equal to ₹15 per share). Explain the effect on (a) the SAST offer price, (b) the PPA, including whether the non-compete is a separate intangible or part of consideration, and (c) post-deal amortisation.
