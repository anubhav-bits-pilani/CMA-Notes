Post-Deal Goodwill: Ind AS 36 Impairment and Tax Non-Depreciation

Scope: Post-deal: annual goodwill impairment testing per CGU (Ind AS 36); tax: goodwill not depreciable after Finance Act 2021

Why it matters

Goodwill is not amortised under Ind AS. It sits on the balance sheet until it is impaired, and impairment is tested at least annually. For a deal team, this means the synergy and growth case used to justify the price becomes the benchmark the auditors test every year. If synergies slip, an impairment charge follows. Large Indian write-downs of acquisition goodwill (telecom, pharma, overseas acquisitions in steel and auto) have often come two to four years after a deal. On the tax side, Finance Act 2021 removed tax depreciation on goodwill, which changed the economics of slump sales and amalgamations and removed a long-litigated deduction.

Core notes

Allocation of goodwill to CGUs (Ind AS 36.80–87)

From the acquisition date, goodwill is allocated to each cash-generating unit (CGU) or group of CGUs expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination, whether or not other assets of the acquiree are assigned to those units. Each unit or group:

  • represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes, and
  • is not larger than an operating segment under Ind AS 108 (before aggregation).

If the initial allocation cannot be completed by the end of the annual period in which the combination occurs, it must be completed before the end of the first annual period beginning after the acquisition date (36.84).

Goodwill can be allocated to the acquirer's existing CGUs if they benefit from synergies. For example, the procurement savings of a horizontal cement deal may benefit the acquirer's own plants.

Annual testing (Ind AS 36.10(b), 36.90, 36.96)

A CGU to which goodwill is allocated is tested for impairment annually, at the same time each year (any time during the year), and whenever there is an indication of impairment. If goodwill was acquired during the current year, the CGU is tested before the end of the current year.

\[ \text{Impairment loss} = \max\left(0, \ \text{Carrying amount of CGU (incl. goodwill)} - \text{Recoverable amount}\right) \]

\[ \text{Recoverable amount} = \max\left(\text{Value in use}, \ \text{Fair value less costs of disposal}\right) \]

  • Value in use (VIU): PV of pre-tax cash flows from the CGU in its current condition, excluding future restructurings not yet committed and enhancements (36.44), discounted at a pre-tax rate (36.55). Cash flows based on reasonable and supportable assumptions, using the most recent budgets for a maximum of five years unless a longer period is justified (36.33), and a steady or declining growth rate beyond.
  • FVLCOD: Ind AS 113 fair value, often a market-participant DCF or multiples, less disposal costs.

Allocation of an impairment loss (36.104)

  1. First, reduce the carrying amount of goodwill allocated to the CGU.
  2. Then, the other assets of the unit pro rata to their carrying amounts, but not below the highest of their FVLCOD, VIU and zero.

Goodwill impairment is never reversed (36.124), even if conditions later improve.

NCI gross-up (36 Appendix C): if NCI was measured at proportionate share, the goodwill attributable to NCI is not recognised. For testing, gross up the carrying amount of goodwill to include the notional NCI share, compare with recoverable amount, and recognise only the parent's share of any loss in goodwill.

Illustration

A CGU carries goodwill of ₹900 crore (100% acquisition) plus other net assets of ₹2,600 crore. Year 2 forecasts are cut because synergies are 18 months late. VIU = ₹3,050 crore; FVLCOD = ₹2,950 crore.

  • Recoverable amount = ₹3,050 crore. Carrying amount = ₹3,500 crore. Impairment = ₹450 crore.
  • Entirely allocated to goodwill, reducing it to ₹450 crore. No tax effect (no DTL on goodwill, s05).
  • EPS impact: ₹450 crore off reported PAT in that year (usually shown as an exceptional item). Cash flows are unaffected.

Disclosures (Ind AS 36.134)

For each CGU with significant goodwill: carrying amount of goodwill, basis of recoverable amount, key assumptions and management's approach to them, forecast period, growth rate, discount rate, and sensitivity: if a reasonably possible change in a key assumption would cause an impairment, the headroom and the change needed. Analysts read these to see how much headroom a deal has.

Tax: goodwill not depreciable after Finance Act 2021

Before 2021, the Supreme Court in CIT v Smifs Securities (2012) held that goodwill was an "intangible asset" eligible for depreciation under Sec 32(1)(ii) as "any other business or commercial rights of similar nature". Acquirers in amalgamations and slump sales claimed 25% WDV depreciation on goodwill, subject to the 5th proviso to Sec 32(1) and Explanation 7 to Sec 43(1).

Finance Act 2021 (effective from AY 2021-22, that is FY 2020-21):

  • Amended Sec 2(11) and Sec 32 to exclude goodwill of a business or profession from the definition of depreciable assets and from the block of intangible assets.
  • Goodwill already in the block on 1 April 2020 is removed from the block, and the WDV is reduced accordingly (Rule 8AC prescribes the computation). If the block becomes negative, there can be a short-term capital gain.
  • On transfer of purchased goodwill, the cost of acquisition is the purchase price reduced by depreciation allowed earlier (Sec 55(2)(a)).
  • Other intangibles (know-how, patents, copyrights, trademarks, licences, franchises and similar commercial rights) remain depreciable at 25% WDV. So separately identifying and valuing such intangibles in a slump sale or asset purchase still creates a tax shield.

Consequences for deal modelling:

  • In a slump sale or asset deal, allocate consideration to depreciable assets and specific intangibles with care and evidence, because any residual goodwill gives no tax shield.
  • In share deals, the point is mostly moot: there is no step-up anyway.
  • No DTL arises on goodwill at initial recognition, and impairment has no tax effect.
  • Date sensitivity: for tax years from 1 April 2026, check the equivalent provisions in the Income-tax Act 2025, which carries forward the exclusion of goodwill from depreciable assets.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Synergy assumptions in VIU: VIU uses the CGU's current condition, so integration plans not yet committed (restructuring) are excluded. Synergies already achieved can be included. FVLCOD can include synergies a market participant would achieve.
  • Allocating to the acquirer's CGUs: allocating goodwill to the acquirer's strong existing units can delay impairment. Auditors challenge allocations not supported by internal reporting.
  • Pre-tax vs post-tax discount rates: Ind AS 36 requires a pre-tax rate. Most practitioners compute VIU on a post-tax basis and back-solve the pre-tax rate for disclosure.
  • Market capitalisation below net assets: an indicator of impairment that needs to be addressed explicitly (36.12(d)).
  • Brokers' adjusted EPS excludes impairment, but statutory EPS and dividend capacity (Sec 123) are affected.

Key takeaways

  • Goodwill is allocated to CGUs that benefit from the synergies, at no higher than operating-segment level.
  • It is tested at least annually: recoverable amount is the higher of VIU and FVLCOD.
  • Impairment losses reduce goodwill first, then other assets pro rata, and goodwill impairment is never reversed.
  • Disclosures of key assumptions and sensitivity show how much headroom the deal has.
  • Since Finance Act 2021, goodwill is not depreciable for tax. Other intangibles remain depreciable at 25% WDV.

Practice Questions

Q1. Impairment test and allocation [L2]

CGU carrying amount: goodwill ₹600 crore, brand (indefinite life) ₹300 crore, PPE ₹1,100 crore, NWC ₹200 crore. VIU ₹1,750 crore; FVLCOD ₹1,700 crore. The brand's FVLCOD is ₹290 crore. Compute the impairment and allocate it.

Q2. NCI gross-up [L2]

The acquirer owns 70% and measured NCI at proportionate share. Recognised goodwill ₹420 crore; other net assets of the CGU ₹1,500 crore; recoverable amount ₹1,850 crore. Compute the impairment recognised in goodwill.

💡 Hint: Gross up goodwill to 100% before comparing with recoverable amount, then recognise only the parent's share.

Q3. Headroom sensitivity [L2]

VIU of a CGU is ₹4,200 crore at a pre-tax discount rate of 13% and terminal growth of 5%; carrying amount is ₹3,900 crore. Year 5 cash flow is ₹480 crore. Estimate the terminal growth rate at which headroom becomes zero, holding other assumptions constant, and draft the sensitivity disclosure.

Q4. Tax allocation in a slump sale [L3]

A buyer pays ₹1,000 crore in a slump sale. Fair values: plant ₹400 crore, trademarks ₹180 crore, technical know-how ₹70 crore, customer relationships ₹90 crore, NWC ₹60 crore; residual goodwill ₹200 crore. Explain which items are depreciable for tax and at what rates, estimate the tax shield over 5 years at 25.17% (plant 15% WDV, intangibles 25% WDV), and discuss the evidence needed to support the allocation.
