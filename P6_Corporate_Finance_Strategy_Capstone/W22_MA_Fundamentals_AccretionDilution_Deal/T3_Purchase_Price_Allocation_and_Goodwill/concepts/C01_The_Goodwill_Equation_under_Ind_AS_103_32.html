Key concept

The Goodwill Equation under Ind AS 103.32

Concept: Goodwill = Consideration transferred + NCI + FV of previously held interest − FV of net identifiable assets

Statement

\[ GW = CT + NCI + FV_{\text{PHI}} - FV_{\text{NIA}} \]

where:

  • \(CT\) = consideration transferred, measured at acquisition-date fair value: cash, fair value of shares issued (at the acquisition-date price), fair value of contingent consideration, and liabilities incurred to former owners. It excludes acquisition-related costs (expensed) and amounts for separate transactions;
  • \(NCI\) = non-controlling interest, at fair value or at proportionate share of \(FV_{\text{NIA}}\) (s06);
  • \(FV_{\text{PHI}}\) = fair value of the acquirer's previously held equity interest in the acquiree (step acquisitions);
  • \(FV_{\text{NIA}}\) = net of acquisition-date amounts of identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed, measured under Ind AS 103 (mostly fair value, with Ind AS 12 deferred tax), c02.

If \(GW < 0\), reassess, then recognise a bargain purchase in capital reserve (c06).

Explanation

Goodwill is a residual. The left-hand terms measure what was given up for 100% of the business: what the acquirer pays now, what the minority keeps (valued on the chosen basis), and what the acquirer already owned. The right-hand term measures what can be separately identified. The difference is the value that cannot be attributed to identifiable assets: the assembled workforce, expected synergies, going-concern value, and any overpayment.

Key implications:

  • Because goodwill is a residual, every error in identifying intangibles or deferred tax shows up in goodwill.
  • Goodwill is not amortised; it is tested for impairment annually (s08).
  • The goodwill number changes with the NCI measurement choice (higher under the fair value method).
  • Shares issued as consideration are measured at their fair value on the acquisition date (not signing), so goodwill moves with the acquirer's share price between signing and closing.
  • Contingent consideration is included at fair value at the acquisition date; later changes from post-acquisition events do not change goodwill.

Illustration

The acquirer previously held 20% of the target (fair value at acquisition date ₹500 crore). It buys another 50% for ₹1,400 crore in cash plus contingent consideration with a fair value of ₹100 crore. NCI (30%) at fair value ₹780 crore. FV of net identifiable assets ₹2,200 crore. Acquisition costs ₹25 crore.

\[ GW = (1{,}400 + 100) + 780 + 500 - 2{,}200 = ₹580 \text{ crore} \]

The ₹25 crore of costs is expensed and not included. Under the proportionate method, NCI = 30% × 2,200 = ₹660 crore and goodwill = ₹460 crore.

Connections

This is the endpoint of the PPA: c02 computes \(FV_{\text{NIA}}\), s06 covers NCI and the previously held interest, t2 c05 explains why fees are excluded from \(CT\), and s08 covers the subsequent impairment testing. The pitfalls on existing goodwill and DTL both act through this equation. In the week's exercise, the goodwill line in the pro-forma balance sheet is computed here, and it links to Week 1–4 consolidation (Ind AS 110).

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic goodwill [L1]

Cash ₹900 crore plus 2 crore acquirer shares (price ₹450 at signing, ₹480 at closing) for 80%. NCI at proportionate share. FV of net identifiable assets ₹1,500 crore. Compute goodwill.

Q2. Step acquisition with contingent consideration [L2]

The acquirer held 15% (FVOCI, fair value at acquisition date ₹240 crore). It buys 45% for ₹810 crore cash plus an earn-out (fair value ₹60 crore). NCI 40% at fair value ₹600 crore. FV of net identifiable assets ₹1,450 crore. Transaction costs ₹18 crore. Compute goodwill and state where the gain on the 15% interest is recognised.

💡 Hint: An FVOCI equity investment's remeasurement goes to OCI, not P&L.

Q3. Interview: what is goodwill? [L3]

"Explain to a non-finance board member what the ₹1,200 crore of goodwill in our latest acquisition represents, why it is not amortised, and what could make us write it down." Prepare a two-minute answer with an Indian example.
