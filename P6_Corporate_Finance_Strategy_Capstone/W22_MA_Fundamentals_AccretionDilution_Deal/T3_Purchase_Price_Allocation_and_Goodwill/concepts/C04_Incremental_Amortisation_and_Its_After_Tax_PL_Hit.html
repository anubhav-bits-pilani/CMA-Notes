Key concept

Incremental Amortisation and Its After-Tax P&L Hit

Concept: Annual incremental amortisation = Intangible value ÷ useful life; after-tax P&L hit = Amortisation × (1 − t) when DTL unwinds

Statement

For each finite-life intangible \(j\) recognised in the PPA:

\[ A_j = \frac{FV_j - RV_j}{L_j} \]

\[ \text{After-tax P\&L hit} = \sum_j A_j \times (1 - t) + \sum_k \Delta Dep_k \times (1 - t) \]

where \(FV_j\) is the fair value at acquisition, \(RV_j\) is residual value (normally nil for intangibles under Ind AS 38.100), \(L_j\) is the useful life in years, \(\Delta Dep_k\) is incremental depreciation on tangible step-ups, and \(t\) is the tax rate at which the related DTL was recognised. Indefinite-life intangibles and goodwill have \(A = 0\).

For the first fiscal year after a mid-year closing:

\[ A_j^{\text{Year 1}} = \frac{FV_j}{L_j} \times \frac{\text{Months from closing to year-end}}{12} \]

Explanation

The PPA turns part of the premium into finite-life assets that are expensed over time. Because the tax base did not change (share deal or amalgamation), there is no tax deduction for this amortisation. But the DTL created at acquisition unwinds as deferred tax income at exactly \(A \times t\). So the net P&L effect after tax is \(A \times (1 - t)\), which is the figure that enters pro-forma PAT (t4 c01).

Points to watch:

  • Method: straight-line is the default, but Ind AS 38.97 requires a pattern that reflects consumption. Customer relationships with high early attrition may be amortised on an accelerated basis, and order backlog over its short execution period.
  • Life: contractual or legal life, shortened by expected use; renewals only if likely without significant cost (Ind AS 38.94).
  • Cash vs reported: amortisation is non-cash and the DTL release is non-cash. Cash EPS (adding back amortisation) is common in Indian broker reports, but statutory EPS includes it.
  • If no DTL was recognised (for example an asset deal where amortisation is tax-deductible), the after-tax hit is still \(A \times (1 - t)\), but through current tax savings rather than DTL release. If the amortisation is neither tax-deductible nor backed by a DTL (rare in a correct PPA), the hit is the full \(A\).

Illustration

Closing on 1 October 2026; FY ends 31 March. Intangibles: customer relationships ₹360 crore (12 years), technology ₹150 crore (5 years), order backlog ₹48 crore (12 months), brand ₹500 crore (indefinite). Tax 25.17%.

The backlog makes FY28 the heaviest year, which matters for Year 1 and Year 2 accretion in t4.

Connections

This concept takes the PPA outputs from s02, s03 and c03 and delivers the amortisation line for t4 (pro-forma PAT and the pitfall on tax-effecting amortisation) and t5 (break-even synergies). It also links to the t3 pitfall on leaving all premium in goodwill, which removes this line and overstates EPS, and to Cash EPS conventions in Indian broker reports.

Practice Questions

Q1. Amortisation schedule [L1]

Intangibles: distributor network ₹240 crore (10 years), software ₹60 crore (3 years), non-compete ₹45 crore (5 years). Closing 1 January 2027 (FY ends 31 March). Tax 25.17%. Compute amortisation and after-tax hit for FY27, FY28 and FY29.

Q2. Accelerated pattern [L2]

Customer relationships of ₹400 crore have attrition of 20% a year. The valuer proposes amortisation in proportion to the expected excess earnings, which decline with attrition. Compute the first three years' amortisation using a pattern proportional to \(0.8^{(n-1)}\) over 10 years, and compare with straight-line.

💡 Hint: Normalise the weights so that they sum to 1 over the 10-year life.

Q3. EPS presentation [L2]

Pro-forma PAT before PPA amortisation is ₹1,450 crore; amortisation is ₹96 crore; tax 25.17%; shares 75 crore. Compute statutory pro-forma EPS and "cash EPS" excluding amortisation, and explain which one a credit analyst and an equity analyst would focus on.
