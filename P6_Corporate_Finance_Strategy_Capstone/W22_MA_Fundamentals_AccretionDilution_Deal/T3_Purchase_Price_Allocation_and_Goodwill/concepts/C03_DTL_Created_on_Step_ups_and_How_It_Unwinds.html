Key concept

DTL Created on Step-ups and How It Unwinds

Concept: DTL created = (Fair value − Tax base) of stepped-up assets × tax rate; unwinds as the step-up is depreciated/amortised

Statement

At the acquisition date:

\[ DTL_0 = \sum_i (FV_i - TB_i) \times t \]

In each later period:

\[ DTL_t = DTL_{t-1} - \left(\Delta \text{Book D\&A}_t - \Delta \text{Tax D\&A}_t\right) \times t \]

where \(FV_i\) is the PPA fair value of asset \(i\), \(TB_i\) is its tax base, \(t\) is the applicable tax rate, \(\Delta \text{Book D\&A}_t\) is the incremental book depreciation or amortisation from the step-up, and \(\Delta \text{Tax D\&A}_t\) is any incremental tax depreciation (zero in a share deal or amalgamation, because the tax base does not change).

For a share deal, \(\Delta \text{Tax D\&A}_t = 0\), so:

\[ \text{DTL release}_t = \Delta \text{Book D\&A}_t \times t \]

Explanation

The DTL represents the extra tax that would be payable if the asset were recovered at its new carrying amount, because the tax deductions available are only the old tax base. As the book carrying amount is depreciated toward the tax base, the temporary difference shrinks and the DTL is released to P&L as deferred tax income. Cash tax is unchanged throughout. The release only offsets, in the P&L, the extra book D&A that the step-up created.

  • Indefinite-life assets (land, indefinite-life brands): no depreciation, so the DTL stays until sale or impairment. On impairment, the DTL is released in proportion.
  • Inventory step-up: the DTL reverses within months as the inventory is sold.
  • Rate changes: remeasure the DTL through P&L if the rate changes (for example a move to Sec 115BAA).
  • Recovery through sale: land DTL may be measured at the capital-gains rate.
  • Slump sale or asset deal: the tax base moves to the new cost, so the DTL is small or zero for depreciable assets.

Illustration

Share deal. Customer relationships ₹500 crore (10 years), plant step-up ₹150 crore (5 years), land step-up ₹100 crore. Tax rate 25.17%.

₹ crore At acquisition Year 1 Year 2 Year 5 Year 10
Customer relationships temporary difference 500 450 400 250 0
Plant step-up temporary difference 150 120 90 0 0
Land step-up temporary difference 100 100 100 100 100
Total temporary difference 750 670 590 350 100
DTL at 25.17% 188.8 168.6 148.5 88.1 25.2
DTL release in the year – 20.1 20.1 20.1 12.6

Year 1 book D&A from step-ups = 50 + 30 = ₹80 crore; DTL release = 80 × 25.17% = ₹20.1 crore; net PAT drag = ₹59.9 crore. From Year 6 only the customer relationships amortise (₹50 crore, release ₹12.6 crore). The land DTL of ₹25.2 crore remains.

Practice Questions

Q1. DTL at acquisition [L1]

Step-ups: brand ₹400 crore (indefinite), technology ₹180 crore (6 years), plant ₹90 crore (9 years), inventory ₹25 crore (sold within 2 months). Tax 25.17%. Compute the DTL at acquisition and at the end of Year 1.

Q2. Slump sale vs share deal [L2]

The same plant step-up of ₹240 crore (book life 8 years) arises in a share deal and a slump sale. In the slump sale, tax depreciation on the stepped-up amount is 15% WDV. Compute the DTL at acquisition and at the end of Years 1 and 2 under each structure.

💡 Hint: In the slump sale the tax base equals fair value at acquisition, so the difference arises only from different depreciation patterns.

Q3. Rate change [L2]

A target with a DTL of ₹150 crore at 34.944% opts for Sec 115BAA (25.168%) in the year after acquisition. Compute the remeasurement and state whether it adjusts goodwill or P&L.
