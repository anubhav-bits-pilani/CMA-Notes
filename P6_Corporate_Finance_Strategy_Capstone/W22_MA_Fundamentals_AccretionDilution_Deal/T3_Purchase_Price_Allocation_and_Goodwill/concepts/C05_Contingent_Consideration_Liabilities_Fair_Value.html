Key concept

Contingent Consideration Liabilities: Fair Value Through P&L

Concept: Contingent consideration classified as liability: remeasured at fair value through P&L each period

Statement

At the acquisition date (Ind AS 103.39):

\[ CC_0 = \sum_s p_s \times \frac{\text{Payout}_s}{(1 + r)^{T}} \]

is included in consideration transferred, where \(p_s\) is the probability of scenario \(s\), \(\text{Payout}_s\) the payment in that scenario, \(T\) the time to payment and \(r\) a discount rate reflecting the time value of money and the risk of the obligation (including own credit risk). More complex earn-outs need option-pricing or Monte Carlo methods.

At each later reporting date (Ind AS 103.58(b)), a liability-classified contingent consideration is remeasured:

\[ \Delta FV_t = CC_t - CC_{t-1} \quad \text{recognised in P\&L} \]

except for measurement-period adjustments that reflect facts existing at the acquisition date, which adjust goodwill.

Explanation

Contingent consideration is part of the price for the business, so its acquisition-date fair value goes into goodwill. After that, changes in its value are post-acquisition events: the target hit or missed its targets, or time passed. They are recognised in P&L, not goodwill.

Classification under Ind AS 32 and 109:

  • Liability: payable in cash, or in a variable number of the acquirer's shares (for example "shares worth ₹100 crore"). Remeasured at FV through P&L, as Ind AS 109 (para 4.2.1(e)) requires for contingent consideration recognised by an acquirer. The rule that sends own-credit changes to OCI applies only to liabilities designated under the fair value option, so it does not apply here.
  • Equity: a fixed number of shares for a fixed condition (fixed-for-fixed). Not remeasured.
  • Asset: a right to the return of consideration (clawback). Remeasured through P&L.

P&L consequence: an earn-out liability increases when the target performs well, creating a loss in the acquirer's P&L in a good year. When the target underperforms, the liability falls and a gain is recognised. This counter-intuitive pattern is why analysts strip earn-out remeasurements out of adjusted EBITDA and EPS, and why an earn-out gain may coincide with a goodwill impairment test trigger.

Unwinding of discount is also a P&L charge, usually presented within finance cost.

Illustration

Earn-out: ₹200 crore payable at the end of Year 2 if Year 2 EBITDA ≥ ₹150 crore. At acquisition, the probability is 40%; discount rate 10%.

  • \(CC_0 = 0.40 \times 200 / 1.10^2 = ₹66.1\) crore, included in consideration and goodwill.
  • End of Year 1: probability now 75% (a large contract was won after closing). \(CC_1 = 0.75 \times 200 / 1.10 = ₹136.4\) crore. Change = ₹70.3 crore, a P&L loss (of which about ₹6.6 crore is unwinding at 10% on the opening balance and ₹63.7 crore is remeasurement).
  • End of Year 2: target met; ₹200 crore paid. Further loss = ₹63.6 crore.

Total P&L charges = ₹133.9 crore over two years, even though the business did well. Goodwill stays at its acquisition-date amount.

Journal at acquisition: Dr Goodwill (part of the residual) / Cr Contingent consideration liability 66.1. Journal at Year 1: Dr P&L – remeasurement 63.7, Dr Finance cost 6.6 / Cr Liability 70.3.

Connections

This concept details the contingent-consideration piece of s06 and links to the t2 related item on earn-outs and deferred consideration (which reduce upfront cash in Sources & Uses). It feeds the pro-forma P&L in t4, where remeasurement is usually excluded from adjusted EPS, and the forecast balance sheet (liability until settled). Payments linked to continued employment are remuneration, not contingent consideration (s06, t1 s01).

Practice Questions

Q1. Initial measurement [L1]

An earn-out pays ₹50 crore if Year 1 revenue exceeds ₹300 crore (probability 60%) and an additional ₹30 crore if Year 2 revenue exceeds ₹380 crore (probability 35%). Discount rate 11%. Compute the acquisition-date fair value.

Q2. Remeasurement through the P&L [L2]

Using Q1, at the end of Year 1 the Year 1 target is met (₹50 crore paid) and the Year 2 probability rises to 70%. Compute the liability at the end of Year 1 and the P&L charge, split between unwinding and remeasurement.

💡 Hint: Unwind both components at 11% for one year before applying the new probability.

Q3. Classification judgement [L2]

Classify each earn-out and state the subsequent measurement: (a) 50 lakh acquirer shares if EBITDA ≥ ₹80 crore; (b) acquirer shares worth ₹40 crore at the then market price; (c) cash of ₹25 crore to founders only if they remain employed for 2 years; (d) a refund of ₹15 crore of the price if a licence is not renewed.
