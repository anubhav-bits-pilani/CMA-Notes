Key concept

Bargain Purchase Gain: OCI and Capital Reserve under Ind AS 103

Concept: Bargain purchase gain: recognised in OCI and accumulated in capital reserve (if clear evidence), else directly in capital reserve

Statement

If, after reassessment (Ind AS 103.36),

\[ FV_{\text{NIA}} - \left(CT + NCI + FV_{\text{PHI}}\right) = BPG > 0 \]

then under Ind AS 103.34 (Indian carve-out from IFRS 3):

\[ \text{If clear evidence of underlying reasons:} \quad BPG \rightarrow \text{OCI on acquisition date} \rightarrow \text{accumulated in Capital reserve} \]

\[ \text{Otherwise:} \quad BPG \rightarrow \text{directly in equity as Capital reserve} \]

where \(BPG\) is the bargain purchase gain and the other symbols are as in the goodwill equation (c01).

Explanation

Under IFRS 3, a bargain purchase gain goes to profit or loss. India departed from this because a gain from buying cheaply is not earned through operations, and because recognising it in profit could inflate distributable profits and EPS. The carve-out keeps the gain in equity.

Clear evidence of the underlying reasons means documented facts that explain why the seller accepted less than fair value. Examples: an IBC resolution plan, a forced sale by a lender, a regulatory divestment order (for example a CCI remedy), a seller's urgent liquidity need, or a foreign parent exiting under a global restructuring deadline. If such evidence exists, the gain goes through OCI (it appears in total comprehensive income, but not in profit), then accumulates in capital reserve. Without such evidence, the excess is credited directly to capital reserve without passing through OCI.

Reassessment first: the standard requires the acquirer to recheck identification and measurement before recognising any gain. Common corrections: omitted contingent liabilities, overvalued intangibles or PPE (economic obsolescence), missed DTL, and liabilities for onerous contracts.

Consequences:

  • No effect on profit, EPS or dividend capacity. Capital reserve is not distributable.
  • The gain is not taxable merely because it is recognised in equity; tax issues arise from the transaction itself (for example Sec 56(2)(x) where shares are bought below fair market value, or debt waivers in IBC cases).
  • Disclosure (B64(n)): the amount of the gain, the line item in which it is recognised, and a description of the reasons why the transaction resulted in a gain.

Illustration

A listed acquirer buys 80% of a distressed auto-component maker from a lender-led sale for ₹400 crore. FV of net identifiable assets after DTL is ₹600 crore. NCI at proportionate share. Reassessment confirms the PPA.

  • NCI = 20% × 600 = ₹120 crore.
  • BPG = 600 − (400 + 120) = ₹80 crore.
  • Evidence: the lender-led sale process under a court-monitored timeline is clear evidence. The gain goes to OCI and then to capital reserve.

Connections

This concept is the accounting end of s07 and links to t1 s08 (IBC resolution plans, where bargain purchases are common) and c01 (the goodwill equation, whose negative result is a BPG). In t4, a BPG does not increase pro-forma PAT, so it cannot make a deal look accretive. It also relates to the Week 1–4 consolidation work on capital reserve under Indian GAAP and Ind AS.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and classify [L1]

Consideration ₹250 crore for 100%. FV of net identifiable assets ₹310 crore. The seller was a promoter who wanted to retire; no other reason is documented. Compute the gain and state its recognition.

Q2. NCI choice and BPG [L2]

Consideration ₹700 crore for 70%. FV of net identifiable assets ₹1,050 crore. NCI fair value ₹330 crore. Compute the result under both NCI methods and explain why the choice matters here.

💡 Hint: Under one method the result may be goodwill and under the other a bargain purchase.

Q3. Reassessment memo [L3]

A provisional PPA for an IBC acquisition shows a ₹420 crore bargain purchase. Draft a reassessment checklist for the audit committee, covering identification, measurement, evidence of the underlying reasons and the disclosure required.
