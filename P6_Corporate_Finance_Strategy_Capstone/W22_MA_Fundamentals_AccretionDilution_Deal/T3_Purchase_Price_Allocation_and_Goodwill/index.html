Roadmap topic: Purchase Price Allocation (PPA) & Goodwill: Allocating offer premium to identifiable intangibles, fair value asset write-ups, deferred tax liability creation, and residual goodwill

Overview

Purchase price allocation is where the deal price meets the financial statements. Under the Ind AS 103 acquisition method, the acquirer identifies itself and the acquisition date, then recognises the target's identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value. Those include brands, customer relationships, technology, order backlog, non-competes and licences that never appeared on the target's own balance sheet, as well as contingent liabilities that the target never provided for. Tangible assets are written up: land and plant to market or depreciated replacement cost, and inventory to selling price less a profit allowance. Because a share purchase or amalgamation in India does not change tax bases, each write-up creates a deferred tax liability. What remains after deducting the fair value of net identifiable assets from consideration, NCI and any previously held interest is goodwill.

For the target roles, PPA matters in three ways. In financial reporting and audit, it is a high-judgement Level 3 fair-value exercise with heavy disclosure and recurring impairment testing under Ind AS 36. In deal modelling, the PPA outputs drive the amortisation and depreciation lines that reduce pro-forma EPS (t4), the DTL that changes goodwill, and the balance sheet that drives leverage and return metrics. In FP&A, the acquisition case becomes the benchmark for annual impairment tests, so missed synergies become write-downs.

The learning sequence: start with the acquisition method (s01) and the goodwill equation (c01, c02). Then work through what is recognised and how: intangibles (s02) and their valuation (s03), tangible write-ups and contingent liabilities (s04), and deferred tax (s05, c03, c04). Next, cover the structural variations: NCI options, step acquisitions, contingent consideration and the measurement period (s06, c05), and the Indian specifics of bargain purchases and common-control combinations (s07, c06). Finish with post-deal goodwill impairment testing and the tax position on goodwill (s08).

Related concepts to study

Ind AS 12, Ind AS 36 and Ind AS 110 foundations

→ Week 1–4: Ind AS 12 deferred tax, Ind AS 36 impairment, Ind AS 110 consolidation

PPA uses three foundations from the first weeks. Ind AS 12 supplies the temporary-difference logic: a fair value above tax base creates a DTL, with the exception for goodwill in 12.15(a) and the business-combination rules in 12.66–68. Ind AS 36 supplies the annual impairment test for goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles at CGU level, with recoverable amount as the higher of value in use and FVLCOD. Ind AS 110 supplies control (identifying the acquirer and the acquisition date), consolidation procedures, NCI presentation and the treatment of later changes in ownership as equity transactions. Revisit the consolidation worksheet: the PPA adjustments become consolidation entries that persist every year.

PPA amortisation and pro-forma EPS

→ Week 22 t4: PPA amortisation and depreciation step-ups reduce pro-forma EPS

The PPA's finite-life intangibles and tangible step-ups produce incremental amortisation and depreciation that enter the pro-forma P&L after tax. For a share deal, the after-tax charge is the pre-tax charge × (1 − t), because the DTL unwinds. Inventory step-ups produce a one-off Year 1 COGS hit. The phasing matters: short-life items such as order backlog concentrate the drag in Years 1–2, which is exactly where accretion/dilution is judged. Leaving premium in goodwill removes the drag and overstates EPS. In t5, the PPA charge is one of the costs that break-even synergies must cover.

Ind AS 113 fair value hierarchy

Ind AS 113 fair value hierarchy and valuation inputs (Level 3 for most intangibles)

Ind AS 113 defines fair value as an exit price between market participants and classifies inputs into three levels: Level 1 (quoted prices in active markets for identical items), Level 2 (observable inputs other than Level 1, such as quoted prices for similar items) and Level 3 (unobservable inputs). Most PPA intangibles (customer relationships via MEEM, brands via royalty rates and forecasts) are Level 3, as is DRC for specialised plant. The standard requires the valuation technique to maximise observable inputs, use market-participant assumptions (not acquirer-specific synergies) and consider highest and best use for non-financial assets. For PPA, Level 3 status means extensive judgement and sensitivity in valuation reports, and audit focus.

Registered valuers and the IBBI framework

Registered Valuers under Companies Act Sec 247 and IBBI framework (ICMAI RVO)

Sec 247 of the Companies Act requires valuations under the Act (schemes, preferential allotments, swap ratios) to be done by a registered valuer. Under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules 2017, valuers register with IBBI through a Registered Valuers Organisation (RVO) in one of three asset classes: land and building, plant and machinery, and securities or financial assets. ICMAI's RVO (ICMAI Registered Valuers Organisation) is one such RVO, which is relevant for CMAs who want to qualify. While Ind AS PPA valuations do not strictly require a registered valuer, Indian companies and auditors usually rely on one, and ICAI's Valuation Standards (ICAI VS) and IVS are the reference standards.

Tax amortisation benefit in asset vs share deals

Tax-amortisation benefit in asset deals vs share deals in India

The tax amortisation benefit (TAB) is the PV of tax savings a buyer gets from depreciating an acquired intangible for tax. Valuers add it to income-approach values when a market participant would obtain it. In India, a share deal or tax-neutral amalgamation gives no step-up, so the acquirer gets no TAB (and recognises a DTL instead). A slump sale or asset purchase gives a cost base for depreciable assets and specified intangibles (know-how, patents, trademarks, licences, franchises at 25% WDV), so TAB exists for those, but not for goodwill after Finance Act 2021. Whether to include TAB depends on the market-participant structure, so disclose the assumption.

Common pitfalls

Leaving all premium in goodwill

What happens: the model or the provisional PPA puts the entire excess of price over book value into goodwill. Why: it is quick, needs no valuation, and makes EPS look better because goodwill is not amortised. Correct treatment: identify every intangible meeting the contractual-legal or separability criterion (s02), value each (s03) and recognise it with its DTL. Only the residual is goodwill. In early-stage models, use benchmark allocations by sector (for example 30–60% of the premium to intangibles in consumer and technology deals) until the valuation arrives. Detection: compare goodwill as a percentage of consideration with sector peers' PPA disclosures; a 90%+ goodwill share in a consumer or B2B deal is a red flag.

Forgetting the DTL on write-ups

What happens: fair value step-ups and intangibles are added to net assets without deducting deferred tax. Why: analysts think of fair value adjustments as pre-tax balance sheet items and forget that tax bases do not change in a share deal. Correct treatment: DTL = step-ups × tax rate (c03), deducted in FV of net identifiable assets, which increases goodwill by the same amount. Then unwind it each year in the P&L. Detection: check that goodwill minus "premium over book less intangibles" equals the DTL; check that the PPA tab has a DTL line and the pro-forma balance sheet shows it.

Not eliminating the target's existing goodwill

What happens: book net assets including the target's old goodwill are used as the starting point, so the old goodwill is effectively counted as an identifiable asset. Why: book equity is taken directly from the balance sheet without examining its composition. Correct treatment: deduct existing goodwill (and its related deferred tax, if any) before adding step-ups (c02). The new goodwill replaces it. Detection: check the target's notes for goodwill from its own acquisitions, especially in serial acquirers and holding companies.

Amortising goodwill in the model

What happens: the model amortises goodwill (often over 5–10 years) as under old Indian GAAP (AS 14 amalgamation goodwill over 5 years) or for tax-driven reasons. Why: habit from AS-era accounts, or confusion with tax depreciation, which was allowed until FY 2019-20. Correct treatment: under Ind AS, goodwill is not amortised. It is tested for impairment at least annually (s08). For tax, goodwill is not depreciable after Finance Act 2021. Detection: search the model's D&A schedule for a goodwill line, and check that the goodwill balance stays constant unless an impairment scenario is selected.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full PPA for a listed-target acquisition [L3]

An NSE-listed acquirer buys 70% of a listed consumer company (40% SPA + 30% open offer) for ₹4,900 crore in cash on 1 October 2026. Target book net assets ₹2,000 crore include goodwill of ₹150 crore. Valuation: brand ₹1,500 crore (indefinite), distributor relationships ₹600 crore (12 years), technology ₹120 crore (6 years), order backlog ₹40 crore (6 months), land step-up ₹250 crore, plant step-up ₹180 crore (9 years), inventory step-up ₹36 crore (sold within 3 months), contingent liability ₹45 crore. Tax 25.17%. NCI fair value ₹1,950 crore. (a) Compute FV of net identifiable assets and goodwill under both NCI methods. (b) Prepare the FY27 and FY28 incremental D&A schedule and after-tax P&L drag. (c) State the DTL at 31 March 2027. (d) Identify which CGUs the goodwill should be allocated to if the acquirer expects procurement synergies in its own plants.

Q2. Step acquisition, earn-out and measurement period [L3]

The acquirer held 25% of an unlisted company (equity-method carrying amount ₹260 crore; fair value at acquisition date ₹480 crore). On 1 December 2026 it buys another 50% for ₹1,000 crore cash plus an earn-out of ₹200 crore payable in 2 years if revenue targets are met (probability 45%, discount 10%). NCI at proportionate share. Book net assets ₹1,300 crore; intangibles ₹500 crore; PPE step-up ₹100 crore; tax 25.17%. (a) Compute the remeasurement gain and goodwill. (b) In June 2027, a valuation as at the acquisition date increases intangibles by ₹80 crore; in December 2027 the earn-out probability rises to 80%. Show the accounting for each. (c) Explain the FY27 and FY28 P&L effects.

Q3. IBC acquisition and bargain purchase [L2]

A resolution applicant pays ₹2,500 crore for 100% of a cement company under an approved IBC plan. Book net assets (after write-offs) ₹1,800 crore; land step-up ₹900 crore (DTL at a capital-gains rate of 14.95%); plant step-up ₹400 crore (25.17%); limestone mining lease ₹300 crore (25.17%); contingent liabilities extinguished by the plan. (a) Compute the result of the PPA. (b) Prepare the journal entry. (c) Explain the presentation and the effect on EPS and dividend capacity. (d) List the reassessment steps.

Q4. Common control vs acquisition method [L3]

A listed parent (P) holds 100% of S1 and 60% of listed S2. It proposes (i) merging S1 into P and (ii) merging S2 into P by issuing P shares to S2's minority. S1 book net assets ₹900 crore, P's investment ₹400 crore, consolidated carrying amounts of S1 net assets including PPA ₹1,250 crore. For S2, P's consolidated goodwill is ₹300 crore. (a) Determine the accounting method for each merger. (b) Show P's standalone accounting for the S1 merger. (c) Explain how the S2 minority's share issuance is treated in consolidated and standalone accounts. (d) Identify the regulatory approvals.
