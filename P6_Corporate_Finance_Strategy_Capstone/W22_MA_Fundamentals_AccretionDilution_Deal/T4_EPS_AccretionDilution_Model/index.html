Roadmap topic: EPS Accretion / Dilution Model: Combining Acquirer + Target standalone PAT + After-tax Synergies – Foregone Interest on Cash – New Debt Interest – Amortization of Intangibles

Overview

An accretion/dilution model answers one narrow but heavily scrutinised question: after the deal closes, will the acquirer's earnings per share be higher or lower than they would have been without the deal? Boards of NSE-listed companies, rating agencies, sell-side analysts and the acquirer's own investor-relations team all ask it within hours of an announcement. The model combines two standalone forecasts (acquirer PAT and target PAT), layers on every incremental income-statement effect created by the transaction — after-tax synergies, interest income lost on cash spent, interest on new acquisition debt, amortisation of intangibles and depreciation on fair-value step-ups from the purchase price allocation, amortisation of financing fees and the share of profit belonging to minority shareholders — and divides the result by a pro-forma share count that includes any shares issued as consideration.

For an FP&A or corporate-development analyst in India, the model sits at the junction of three skill sets built earlier in the curriculum: Ind AS accounting (Ind AS 103 PPA outputs, Ind AS 33 weighted shares, Ind AS 109 effective interest on loans, Ind AS 12 deferred tax unwind), financial modelling (debt schedules, three-statement integration, circularity control) and corporate finance judgement (whether EPS optics are a reliable signal of value creation). It is also the engine behind the break-even synergy analysis in the next topic: once pro-forma EPS is correctly built, solving for the synergy level that makes it equal to standalone EPS is a single algebraic step.

The learning sequence follows the build order of a real model. First, put both standalone PAT forecasts on the same fiscal-year basis and the same definition of earnings (s01). Second, build each pro-forma adjustment line, tax-effected and time-weighted (s02, with the formula concepts c01, c03 and c06). Third, build the denominator — acquirer diluted shares plus new shares, time-weighted under Ind AS 33 for a mid-year closing (s03, c02). Fourth, handle partial ownership and NCI, which is the norm when an Indian acquirer buys a listed company through a share purchase agreement plus a SAST open offer (s04). Fifth, use the shortcut tests — the P/E rule and the weighted cost of acquisition versus target earnings yield — to sanity-check the full model (s05, c04, c05). Finally, round out the board pack with contribution analysis for stock deals (s06) and credit and return metrics that EPS alone does not show (s07).

Related concepts to study

Week 14 EPS and share-count mechanics

→ Week 14: EPS and share-count mechanics in the income statement

Week 14 built the income statement down to EPS, including the weighted average share count, buybacks, bonus issues and the treasury stock method for ESOPs. The accretion/dilution model reuses exactly that machinery, but adds a new source of shares (consideration shares) and a new timing rule: shares issued in a business combination enter the weighted average from the acquisition date. If your Week 14 model already computes basic and diluted weighted shares from a share roll-forward, the pro-forma share count is simply another row in that roll-forward. Any error in the standalone denominator — for example using period-end instead of weighted shares, or omitting dilutive ESOPs — flows straight into the accretion percentage because accretion is measured against standalone EPS computed on the same basis.

PPA outputs feeding the amortisation line

→ Week 22 t3: PPA outputs (intangibles, step-ups, DTL) feed the amortisation line

The purchase price allocation in topic 3 produces three numbers this model needs: the value and useful life of each identified intangible, the PPE (and inventory) step-up with its remaining life, and the deferred tax liability created on those step-ups. Annual amortisation and incremental depreciation become pro-forma deductions; the DTL unwind reduces the deferred tax charge so the after-tax hit is amortisation × (1 − t). The inventory step-up is a one-off hit to COGS in the first months after closing and is usually shown separately because analysts exclude it from "run-rate" EPS. Goodwill is the residual and is not amortised under Ind AS — it never appears as a pro-forma P&L line unless an impairment is being modelled.

Value creation test versus EPS optics

→ Week 16: value creation test (NPV of deal) vs EPS optics

Week 16 established that value is created only when the present value of cash flows received exceeds the price paid. Applied to M&A, the deal NPV for the acquirer's shareholders equals the PV of net synergies minus the premium paid over standalone value (and minus transaction costs). EPS accretion is an accounting outcome driven by relative P/E multiples, the cost of debt and the size of PPA amortisation; it says nothing directly about whether the premium is recovered. A low-interest-rate debt-funded deal for a low-P/E target is almost always accretive, even at a premium that exceeds any plausible synergies. The board pack therefore presents both: accretion in Years 1–2 and the NPV or ROIC-vs-WACC test.

Cash EPS and adjusted EPS in broker reports

Cash EPS / adjusted EPS excluding PPA amortisation, as used in Indian broker reports

Indian sell-side reports (and many company investor presentations) quote "cash EPS" — PAT plus depreciation and amortisation, divided by shares — or "adjusted EPS" that adds back only acquisition-related intangible amortisation, net of tax, plus one-off deal costs. The argument is that PPA amortisation is a non-cash allocation of price already paid, and that a brand or customer list is often maintained by ongoing marketing spend already in the P&L. Your model should therefore show reported (Ind AS) EPS accretion and adjusted EPS accretion side by side, with a reconciliation. SEBI LODR does not prescribe adjusted EPS, so the definition must be disclosed and applied consistently; reported Ind AS 33 EPS remains the audited figure.

Diluted EPS with convertible consideration

Ind AS 33 diluted EPS with convertible instruments issued as consideration

Indian deals sometimes use compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS), compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), warrants or contingently issuable shares (earn-outs settled in shares) as consideration. Under Ind AS 33, mandatorily convertible instruments are included in basic EPS from the date the contract is entered into when conversion is certain; optionally convertible instruments enter diluted EPS using the if-converted method (add back after-tax interest or preference dividend, add potential shares) if dilutive. Contingently issuable shares enter basic EPS only when the conditions are satisfied and diluted EPS if conditions would be met at the reporting date. The pro-forma model must state which EPS it is measuring accretion on and treat the convertible consistently in both numerator and denominator.

Common pitfalls

Not tax-effecting synergies, interest and amortisation

Forgetting to tax-effect synergies, interest and amortisation

The mistake. Adding pre-tax synergies to PAT, or deducting pre-tax interest and amortisation from PAT. Because PAT is already an after-tax number, every adjustment must be after tax as well.

Why it happens. Synergy guidance from management and interest rates from term sheets are quoted pre-tax, and analysts paste them directly into the PAT bridge.

Correct treatment. Multiply every pre-tax adjustment by (1 − t), using the marginal rate of the entity where the item arises (25.168% under Sec 115BAA for most Indian corporates). Where deductibility is doubtful — for instance interest on borrowings used to acquire shares held as investments, where Sec 57 restricts deduction against dividend income — model a no-deduction case.

Detection in review. Build the PAT bridge with explicit pre-tax, tax and after-tax columns; check that the tax column equals the sum of pre-tax adjustments × t (or the documented exceptions).

Ignoring NCI below 100% ownership

Ignoring NCI when the acquirer ends up with less than 100% of a listed target

The mistake. Adding 100% of target PAT to the acquirer's PAT when the acquirer owns, say, 66% after an SPA plus a 26% open offer.

Why it happens. Templates built for private 100% acquisitions are reused for listed targets, and the open-offer acceptance level is uncertain at announcement.

Correct treatment. Consolidate 100% of target PAT and then deduct NCI share of the target's post-PPA profit (NCI participates in fair-value step-up amortisation at the subsidiary level). Acquisition debt interest at the parent is not shared with NCI. Run acceptance scenarios (0%, 50%, 100% of the 26% offer).

Detection in review. Check that pro-forma PAT is labelled "attributable to owners of the parent" and that an ownership % input drives an NCI line.

Annual target PAT for a mid-year closing

Using annual target PAT for a mid-year closing without time-weighting

The mistake. Including twelve months of target PAT in the closing year when the deal closes on, say, 1 October, while also including only six months of the related interest or the full new share count.

Why it happens. Annual columns in the model and the desire to show a "clean" full-year view.

Correct treatment. Either (a) present a full-year "as if closed on 1 April" pro-forma clearly labelled as illustrative, or (b) time-weight every line consistently — target PAT, synergies, interest, amortisation and consideration shares (Ind AS 33 from the acquisition date) — by the post-closing fraction of the year.

Detection in review. One stub-fraction input cell must drive every pro-forma line in the closing year; search for hard-coded 12-month figures.

Treating EPS accretion as value creation

Equating EPS accretion with value creation (a high-premium deal can be accretive yet destroy value)

The mistake. Concluding that a deal is good because it is accretive.

Why it happens. EPS is the metric markets and management incentive plans track, and it is easy to compute.

Correct treatment. Accretion depends on the after-tax cost of funding relative to the target's earnings yield at the offer price; when after-tax debt costs 6–7% and the target trades at 12–15x earnings, almost any debt deal is accretive. Value is created only if ROIC on the total investment exceeds WACC over time, equivalently if PV of net synergies exceeds the premium. Present both tests.

Detection in review. The board pack must contain a deal NPV or ROIC-vs-WACC page; challenge any recommendation supported only by EPS.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full pro-forma build for a 60/40 debt-cash deal [L3]

Shakti Cements Ltd (NSE-listed) plans to acquire 100% of Vindhya Cement Ltd on 1 April 2026. FY27 forecasts: Shakti PAT ₹1,800 crore, diluted shares 75 crore; Vindhya PAT ₹210 crore, diluted shares 12 crore. Offer price ₹300 per Vindhya share. Funding: 60% new term loan at 9.25% (bullet, 5-year tenor, upfront fee 1.2% of loan), 40% from Shakti's surplus cash earning 6.8%. PPA: brand ₹450 crore (15-year life), customer relationships ₹300 crore (10-year life), PPE step-up ₹360 crore (12-year remaining life). Run-rate pre-tax cost synergies ₹90 crore in FY27 and ₹160 crore in FY28. Tax rate 25.168% on all items. Assume Vindhya PAT grows 8% in FY28 and Shakti PAT grows 10%.

(a) Build the FY27 and FY28 pro-forma PAT bridge with pre-tax, tax and after-tax columns. (b) Compute pro-forma EPS and accretion % for both years on reported and on adjusted (ex-PPA amortisation) bases. (c) Explain in three sentences why the two bases differ.

💡 Hint: Compute equity purchase price first; the fee amortisation line uses a straight-line approximation of EIR for a bullet loan.

Q2. Listed target with open offer and NCI [L3]

Orbit Pharma Ltd agrees to buy 45% of Nova Labs Ltd (listed, 20 crore shares) from its promoters at ₹520 per share, triggering a mandatory open offer for 26% at the same price. Closing (SPA and open offer settlement) is on 1 July 2026. Nova FY27 PAT forecast ₹260 crore (accruing evenly). Orbit FY27 PAT ₹1,150 crore, 50 crore diluted shares. All consideration funded by a new NCD issue at 8.6% coupon. PPA intangible amortisation at Nova level ₹40 crore per year. Tax 25.168%.

(a) Compute FY27 pro-forma EPS under 0%, 50% and 100% acceptance of the open offer. (b) State how much NCD funding is required in each scenario and why Reg 17 escrow matters before the offer opens. (c) Comment on whether 100% acceptance raises any minimum public shareholding issue.

Q3. Quick test versus full model reconciliation [L2]

An acquirer trades at 30x earnings (share price ₹900). The target's EPS is ₹18 and the offer price is ₹360. Pre-tax cost of debt 9%, pre-tax yield on cash 6.5%, tax 25%. (a) Using the weighted cost of acquisition, classify each of the following funding mixes as accretive or dilutive before synergies and PPA: all stock; all debt; all cash; 50% stock / 50% debt. (b) List three items that the full model includes but the quick test ignores, and for each say whether it makes the full model more or less dilutive than the quick test.

Q4. Board memo: accretive but value-destructive? [L3]

A consumer-durables acquirer proposes a ₹6,000 crore all-debt acquisition at 10% pre-tax interest of a target with PAT ₹420 crore and EV/EBIT of 20x. Management forecasts 9% Year 1 EPS accretion. The acquirer's WACC is 11.5%; target post-tax EBIT is ₹330 crore and announced synergies are ₹50 crore pre-tax. Combined Net Debt/EBITDA rises from 0.8x to 3.4x against a covenant of 3.5x. Write a one-page memo (structured bullets acceptable) to the audit committee addressing: (a) the source of the accretion, (b) ROIC vs WACC in Year 1, (c) credit headroom and rating risk, (d) what additional analysis you would require before approval.
