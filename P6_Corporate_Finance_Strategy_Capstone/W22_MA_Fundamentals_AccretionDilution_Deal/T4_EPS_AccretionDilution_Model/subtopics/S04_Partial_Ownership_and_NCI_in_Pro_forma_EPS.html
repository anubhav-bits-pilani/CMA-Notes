Subtopic note

Partial Ownership and NCI in Pro-forma EPS

Scope: Partial ownership: deducting NCI share of target profit when less than 100% is acquired (e.g. after a 26% open offer)

Why it matters

Acquisitions of NSE- or BSE-listed companies in India almost never deliver 100% ownership on day one. A typical structure is a share purchase agreement (SPA) with the promoter for a block of shares, which triggers a mandatory SAST open offer for at least 26% of the total shares, and the final stake depends on how many public shareholders tender. Even with full acceptance, the acquirer may need to sell down to keep public shareholding at the 25% minimum under SCRR Rule 19A. The acquirer consolidates 100% of the target's revenue and profit under Ind AS 110 once it has control, but EPS is computed on profit attributable to owners of the parent (Ind AS 33.10), so the minority (NCI) share must be deducted. A model that forgets this overstates accretion — often by several percentage points.

Core notes

Accounting basis

  • Control (Ind AS 110): consolidate line by line; present profit attributable to owners of the parent and to NCI separately (Ind AS 110.B94 and Schedule III Division II).
  • Significant influence, no control (Ind AS 28): equity-account the share of profit as a single line; no NCI arises because nothing is consolidated. A stake of, say, 26–45% without control falls here.
  • De facto control below 50% is possible under Ind AS 110 (dispersed public shareholding, board nomination rights in the SPA, potential voting rights). The control conclusion drives whether you model consolidation with NCI or equity accounting.

For EPS, the numerator in both cases is profit attributable to the parent.

What NCI shares in — and what it does not

The NCI deduction is NCI % × the target's profit as it appears in the consolidated accounts, i.e. after consolidation adjustments that are pushed down to the subsidiary:

Item Where it sits Shared with NCI?
Target standalone PAT Subsidiary Yes
PPA amortisation and step-up depreciation (net of DTL unwind) Subsidiary (fair value adjustments belong to the subsidiary's net assets) Yes
Synergies arising in the target (e.g. target plant savings) Subsidiary Yes
Synergies arising in the acquirer (e.g. acquirer procurement savings) Parent No
Interest on acquisition debt raised by the parent Parent No
Foregone interest on parent cash Parent No
Financing-fee amortisation on parent debt Parent No
Goodwill impairment Subsidiary/CGU Only under the full-goodwill (fair value) NCI option, per Ind AS 36 allocation

Identifiable intangibles and step-ups are recognised at 100% of fair value whichever NCI measurement option is chosen under Ind AS 103.19 (fair value or proportionate share of net identifiable assets); the option changes goodwill and the NCI balance, not the amortisation.

Pro-forma formula with NCI

\[ \text{PAT}_{\text{owners}} = \text{PAT}_{A} + \text{PAT}_{T}^{\text{adj}} + S_{A}(1-t) - \left(I_{\text{cash}} + I_{\text{debt}} + F\right)(1-t) - \text{NCI}\% \times \text{PAT}_{T}^{\text{adj}} \]

where \(\text{PAT}_{T}^{\text{adj}}\) = target PAT + target-level synergies × (1 − t) − PPA amortisation × (1 − t), \(S_{A}\) = acquirer-level synergies, \(I_{\text{cash}}\) = foregone interest, \(I_{\text{debt}}\) = new debt interest, \(F\) = financing fee amortisation and \(t\) = tax rate.

At 0% acceptance, 40% ownership may still amount to control (board seats, dispersed holding); if not, the target would be equity-accounted, which gives the same attributable profit for this simple case but no consolidated EBITDA.

The table shows two things. First, ignoring NCI at 66% would overstate PAT by ₹30.6 crore (2.6% of EPS). Second, dilution scales with stake because, in this example, the after-tax cost of acquisition funding (6.0%) exceeds the target's post-PPA earnings yield at the offer price (90 ÷ 2,700 = 3.3%) — every additional share bought at ₹270 is dilutive.

Modelling acceptance scenarios

Make the open-offer acceptance rate an input (0–100% of the 26% offer size), feeding:

  1. Shares acquired in the offer and total ownership %.
  2. Open-offer consideration in Sources & Uses and the escrow requirement under SAST Reg 17 (25% of the first ₹500 crore of offer consideration plus 10% of the balance, deposited before the offer opens — note the escrow is sized on full acceptance, not on expected acceptance).
  3. Debt drawn (the debt tranche is often a committed facility sized for 100% acceptance with a lower expected draw — model commitment fees on the undrawn portion).
  4. NCI % and the NCI deduction.
  5. A flag if ownership exceeds 75%, triggering the SCRR Rule 19A requirement to restore 25% public shareholding (typically within 12 months), via OFS, QIP or other permitted methods — the sell-down changes ownership in Year 2.

Subsequent buy-out of NCI

Acquiring NCI later while retaining control is an equity transaction under Ind AS 110.23: no goodwill, no P&L gain or loss. In the pro-forma, Year 2 shows a lower NCI deduction and higher funding cost; whether that is accretive is again a comparison of funding cost with the post-PPA earnings yield of the NCI stake at the buy-out price. A delisting offer under SEBI Delisting Regulations 2021 (reverse book-building) is the route to 100% for a listed target.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Synergy leakage to minorities. Transfer of business or pricing changes between a listed parent and a listed subsidiary are related-party transactions requiring audit committee and, if material, shareholder approval under LODR Reg 23 (with related parties barred from voting). Synergies that require shifting margin from target to acquirer may be blocked or must be priced at arm's length; model where synergies land.
  • Dividend leakage. Cash to service parent debt reaches the parent only as dividends, of which NCI receives its share. Accretion may be fine while parent-level debt service capacity is weak — check in s07.
  • Tax on dividends. Dividends from an Indian subsidiary are taxable in the parent's hands but Sec 80M allows a deduction to the extent the parent redistributes dividends; this affects cash, not consolidated PAT.
  • Losses at target. NCI is allocated its share of losses even if this makes NCI negative (Ind AS 110.B94).
  • Potential voting rights or put options to minority holders. A written put over NCI shares can create a financial liability (Ind AS 32) and may lead to an "anticipated acquisition" presentation where NCI is not shown — consider accounting policy and its EPS effect.
  • Equity-accounted stake. No consolidated EBITDA or debt; credit metrics in s07 change dramatically compared with consolidation.

Key takeaways

  • EPS uses profit attributable to owners of the parent; deduct NCI % of the target's post-PPA, post-target-synergy profit.
  • NCI shares PPA amortisation and target-level synergies but not parent-level acquisition financing costs.
  • The NCI measurement choice under Ind AS 103 affects goodwill, not the amortisation of identifiable intangibles.
  • Make open-offer acceptance an input driving ownership, funding, escrow, NCI and the 75% MPS flag.
  • Later buy-outs of NCI are equity transactions (Ind AS 110.23); their EPS effect depends on the funding cost versus the NCI stake's earnings yield.

Practice Questions

Q1. NCI deduction with target-level synergies [L2]

An acquirer ends with 62% of a listed target after an SPA and open offer. Target FY27 PAT ₹300 crore; PPA amortisation ₹72 crore pre-tax; target-level cost synergies ₹50 crore pre-tax; acquirer-level procurement synergies ₹30 crore pre-tax; parent acquisition debt interest ₹110 crore pre-tax. Tax 25.168%. Compute the NCI deduction and pro-forma PAT attributable to owners, given acquirer standalone PAT ₹1,650 crore.

Q2. Acceptance-level scenario grid [L2]

A target has 15 crore shares. The acquirer buys 35% from promoters at ₹400 and makes an open offer for 26% at ₹400. Funding is 100% debt at 9.2%. Target post-PPA PAT ₹180 crore. Acquirer PAT ₹2,000 crore, 80 crore shares. Tax 25%. Build pro-forma EPS for 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% acceptance of the open offer, and compute the Reg 17 escrow amount.

💡 Hint: Escrow is sized on the full open-offer consideration at the tiered percentages.

Q3. Control versus significant influence [L3]

An acquirer buys 38% of a listed target through an open offer only (no promoter seller); remaining shares are held by 40,000 retail holders and three mutual funds holding 6% each. The acquirer obtains the right to appoint 4 of 9 directors. Discuss whether the target is consolidated or equity-accounted under Ind AS 110/28, and how the accretion model and credit metrics differ in each case.

Q4. MPS sell-down in Year 2 [L2]

After full acceptance the acquirer holds 82% of a listed target. To comply with SCRR Rule 19A it sells 7% through an OFS on 1 April of Year 2 at ₹450 per share (target 20 crore shares), using proceeds to repay 9% debt. Target post-PPA PAT ₹250 crore. Tax 25%. Quantify the Year 2 change in NCI deduction and interest saving, and state the accounting for the OFS gain or loss in consolidated accounts.
