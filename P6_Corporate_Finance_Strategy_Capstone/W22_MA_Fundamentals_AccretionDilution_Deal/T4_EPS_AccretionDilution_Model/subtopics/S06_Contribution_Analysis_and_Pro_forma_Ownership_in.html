Contribution Analysis and Pro-forma Ownership in Stock Deals

Scope: Contribution analysis and pro-forma ownership split for stock deals

Why it matters

In a stock-for-stock deal, the target's shareholders do not walk away with cash — they become shareholders of the combined company. The key negotiation variable is therefore the exchange (swap) ratio, which fixes the post-deal ownership split. Contribution analysis asks a simple fairness question: does each group of shareholders own a share of the combined company roughly proportional to what its company contributes in revenue, EBITDA, PAT, cash flow and value? In India, contribution analysis appears in fairness opinions issued by SEBI-registered merchant bankers on schemes of arrangement, in registered valuers' swap-ratio reports, in board notes and in the explanatory statement circulated to shareholders and creditors at NCLT-convened meetings. It is also the mirror image of accretion/dilution: if target holders receive less ownership than their earnings contribution, the deal is EPS-accretive to acquirer holders, and vice versa.

Core notes

Pro-forma ownership split

\[ \text{Ownership}_{A} = \frac{N_{A}}{N_{A} + N_{T} \times \text{ER}} \qquad \text{Ownership}_{T} = \frac{N_{T} \times \text{ER}}{N_{A} + N_{T} \times \text{ER}} \]

where \(N_{A}\) = acquirer fully diluted shares, \(N_{T}\) = target fully diluted shares and ER = exchange ratio (acquirer shares per target share). In a mixed cash-and-stock deal, only the stock component creates ownership; target holders' ownership is (stock consideration ÷ acquirer price) ÷ total post-deal shares.

Fully diluted counts matter on both sides: in-the-money ESOPs of the target typically convert into acquirer options at the same ratio, and unvested awards may be replaced.

Contribution metrics

For each metric \(M\) (same year, same accounting basis):

\[ \text{Contribution}_{T} = \frac{M_{T}}{M_{A} + M_{T}} \]

Typical metrics, ordered from top of income statement to value:

Metric Leverage-neutral? Comment
Revenue Yes (enterprise level) Ignores margins; flatters low-margin businesses
EBITDA Yes (enterprise level) Core metric for capital-intensive Indian sectors; Ind AS 116 lease effects must be aligned
EBIT / NOPAT Yes Reflects D&A intensity
PAT No (equity level) Directly comparable to ownership; ties to EPS accretion
Operating cash flow / FCFE Depends Useful where accruals differ
Market capitalisation (undisturbed) Equity level Market's view before the deal leak
DCF equity value Equity level Intrinsic view; used by registered valuers

Leverage adjustment. Enterprise-level contributions (revenue, EBITDA, EBIT) must be converted to equity-level contributions before comparing with ownership, because ownership is an equity claim. A practical method: apply each company's contribution % to combined EV, subtract that company's own net debt, and divide by combined equity value:

\[ \text{Implied equity share}_{T} = \frac{c_{T} \times (\text{EV}_{A} + \text{EV}_{T}) - \text{ND}_{T}}{(\text{EV}_{A} + \text{EV}_{T}) - \text{ND}_{A} - \text{ND}_{T}} \]

where \(c_{T}\) is the target's contribution % on the metric and ND is net debt.

Illustration

Bharat Polymers (60 crore shares, ₹500) and Deccan Packaging (10 crore shares, undisturbed ₹225, offer ₹270), all-stock at ER 0.54.

FY27 ₹ crore Acquirer Target Target contribution
Revenue 12,000 2,000 14.3%
EBITDA 2,200 300 12.0%
PAT 1,200 150 11.1%
Market cap (undisturbed) 30,000 2,250 7.0%
Market cap at offer 30,000 2,700 8.3%
Pro-forma ownership 60 ÷ 65.4 = 91.7% 5.4 ÷ 65.4 = 8.3% 8.3%

Leverage-adjusted EBITDA share (acquirer net debt ₹1,500 crore, target ₹200 crore; combined EV at market = 31,500 + 2,450 = 33,950): implied equity share = (12.0% × 33,950 − 200) ÷ (33,950 − 1,700) = (4,074 − 200) ÷ 32,250 = 12.0%.

Interpretation: target shareholders receive 8.3% while contributing 11–14% of earnings and operations. That gap is exactly why the deal is EPS-accretive before PPA — the acquirer's 25x P/E "currency" buys earnings priced at 18x. Target shareholders accept because the acquirer's market valuation is higher (they receive a 20% premium to their own market value) and they participate in synergies. A fairness opinion for the target's board would need to explain why a split below earnings contribution is nonetheless fair (higher-quality, higher-growth acquirer earnings; synergy participation; liquidity).

Presentation

The standard board/fairness slide is a "football field" style bar chart: one bar per metric showing target contribution %, with a vertical line at the proposed ownership % and, ideally, the implied exchange ratio for each metric:

\[ \text{Implied ER} = \frac{\text{Ownership}_{T}/(1-\text{Ownership}_{T}) \times N_{A}}{N_{T}} \]

using the contribution % as \(\text{Ownership}_{T}\). Comparing the proposed ER with the range of implied ERs shows the negotiation zone.

Governance and regulatory consequences of the split

  • Promoter dilution. The acquirer promoter's stake falls in proportion; check that it stays above levels needed for special resolutions (75%) or blocking minorities (25%+).
  • SAST on the acquirer. If target promoters receive acquirer shares that take them to 25% or more of the acquirer, an open offer could be triggered for the acquirer itself; schemes sanctioned by NCLT have an exemption under SAST Reg 10(1)(d) subject to conditions, but a preferential-issue share swap does not automatically qualify.
  • Minimum public shareholding. Post-merger public float must stay at least 25% (SCRR Rule 19A).
  • Board seats and lock-in. Consideration shares under an ICDR preferential issue are subject to lock-in; in schemes, lock-in may apply for promoters' shares.
  • Scheme disclosures. SEBI's master circular on schemes requires a valuation report from a registered valuer and a fairness opinion from an independent SEBI-registered merchant banker, which typically include contribution-style analysis.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Differing accounting policies. Contribution on EBITDA is distorted if one company is on Ind AS 116 and the other on AS; align first.
  • Growth differences. A faster-growing target may justify ownership above its current-year contribution; show FY+1 and FY+2 contributions.
  • Cash-rich targets. Large surplus cash in the target inflates equity value relative to EBITDA; the leverage adjustment handles this.
  • Merger of equals. Where ownership is near 50/50, governance (CEO, chair, board split) is often traded against the exchange ratio — contribution analysis anchors the economic side.
  • Cross-holdings. If the acquirer owns target shares, those are cancelled; ownership calculations use shares held by others only.
  • Fixed vs floating exchange ratio. Indian schemes use a fixed ratio; the ownership split is locked but the value received floats with the acquirer's share price until the effective date.

Key takeaways

  • Pro-forma ownership in a stock deal is set by the exchange ratio: target holders own \(N_{T} \times \text{ER}\) ÷ total shares.
  • Contribution analysis compares that ownership with each company's share of revenue, EBITDA, PAT, cash flow and value.
  • Adjust enterprise-level metrics for net debt before comparing with equity ownership.
  • Ownership below earnings contribution means the deal is accretive to the acquirer; it is the mirror of the P/E rule.
  • Check governance consequences: promoter dilution, SAST triggers, MPS and lock-in.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ownership split under alternative ratios [L1]

An acquirer has 120 crore fully diluted shares at ₹850; the target has 30 crore fully diluted shares. Compute pro-forma ownership for exchange ratios of 0.30, 0.35 and 0.40, and the implied offer price per target share in each case.

Q2. Contribution table and implied exchange ratios [L2]

FY27 data (₹ crore): Acquirer revenue 18,000, EBITDA 3,600, PAT 1,980, net debt 2,400, 90 crore shares. Target revenue 4,500, EBITDA 720, PAT 360, net debt 300, 24 crore shares. Proposed ER 0.40. Compute target contribution on each metric, leverage-adjusted contributions for revenue and EBITDA (use an EV/EBITDA of 14x for both to estimate EV), and the implied ER for each metric. Comment on whether the proposed ratio favours acquirer or target holders.

💡 Hint: Convert each contribution % into an ownership share, then back out the ER from the share counts.

Q3. SAST check on a share swap [L2]

The target's promoter group owns 60% of the target (25 crore shares). Under a preferential-issue swap at ER 1.2, they would receive acquirer shares. The acquirer has 100 crore shares and its own promoters hold 42%. Determine the target promoters' resulting stake in the acquirer, whether any SAST trigger arises, and how the analysis would change if the transaction were a scheme of amalgamation sanctioned by the NCLT.

Q4. Fairness opinion narrative [L3]

You are drafting the contribution section of a fairness opinion for a target board where target holders will get 18% of the combined company but contribute 24% of PAT and 21% of EBITDA. Draft five bullet points explaining the factors that could make the ratio fair and two factors that would argue against it.
