Credit and Return Outputs Alongside Pro-forma EPS

Scope: Credit outputs alongside EPS: pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA, interest cover, ROIC vs WACC

Why it matters

An all-debt deal for a low-P/E target is almost always EPS-accretive — and can still be a bad deal if it pushes leverage past covenants or a rating threshold, or if returns on the capital invested stay below the cost of capital for years. Boards, lenders and rating agencies therefore look at three additional outputs next to the EPS line: leverage (Net Debt/EBITDA), debt-service capacity (interest cover, DSCR) and value creation (ROIC versus WACC). In India these outputs are tested directly: bank sanction letters for acquisition finance carry financial covenants, CRISIL/ICRA/CARE/India Ratings issue rating actions within days of large debt-funded announcements, and investors question managements on "payback" of the premium.

Core notes

Pro-forma Net Debt/EBITDA

\[ \text{Pro-forma ND} = \text{ND}_{A} + \text{ND}_{T} + \text{New debt} + \text{Cash used} - \text{Target debt refinanced from new debt already counted} \]

In words: start with both companies' net debt, add new acquisition debt raised and add acquirer cash spent (spending cash raises net debt). If target debt is repaid using new debt, do not count it twice — either leave target debt in and exclude the refinancing tranche, or remove it and include the tranche.

\[ \text{Pro-forma leverage} = \frac{\text{Pro-forma ND}}{\text{EBITDA}_{A} + \text{EBITDA}_{T} + \text{Synergies (phased)}} \]

Conventions to fix and disclose:

  • LTM vs forward EBITDA. Lenders normally test on LTM; banks' sale pitches use forward. A covenant test at the first testing date after closing uses LTM including the target only from closing unless the facility agreement allows pro-forma inclusion of acquired EBITDA (most acquisition facilities do).
  • Synergy credit. Lenders typically allow only realised or "reasonably expected within 12 months" cost synergies, often capped (for example at 10–20% of EBITDA). Rating agencies usually ignore unrealised synergies.
  • Ind AS 116. Lease liabilities are debt under Ind AS; covenants may be on a "frozen GAAP" basis excluding them. Keep numerator and denominator consistent: if lease liabilities are in net debt, use EBITDA after Ind AS 116 (before lease payments).
  • Debt-like items identified in DD (unpaid capex creditors, gratuity deficits, disputed tax deposits) may be included by rating agencies.

Interest cover and DSCR

\[ \text{Interest cover} = \frac{\text{EBITDA (or EBIT)}}{\text{Gross interest expense}} \qquad \text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{PAT} + \text{D\&A} + \text{Interest}}{\text{Interest} + \text{Scheduled principal}} \]

Use gross interest (do not net off interest income — foregone income is already reflected) and include financing-fee amortisation if the covenant definition says "finance costs" as per Schedule III. DSCR matters when the acquisition loan amortises; Indian term loans commonly require DSCR of 1.2–1.5x. Interest cover on EBIT (after PPA amortisation) is lower than on EBITDA, and covenants should be read for which definition applies.

Indian specifics

  • TOL/TNW. Indian banks and agencies track total outside liabilities to tangible net worth. Goodwill and acquired intangibles are deducted from tangible net worth, so a large premium can push TOL/TNW sharply higher even if Net Debt/EBITDA looks fine.
  • Parent-level (structural) subordination. If acquisition debt sits in the parent and target cash can reach it only by dividend, lenders look at standalone parent debt service, upstream dividend capacity after NCI leakage, and Sec 123 restrictions on dividends out of profits.
  • Acquisition finance limits. Historically, banks were restricted from financing promoters' contributions and share acquisitions; the RBI's 2025 framework enabling bank acquisition finance comes with eligibility and leverage conditions (including a cap on the share of acquisition value that can be debt-financed and post-deal debt-to-equity limits). Check the final RBI directions applicable on the deal date and model the constraint explicitly.

ROIC versus WACC

Return on the capital invested in the deal:

\[ \text{ROIC}_{\text{deal}} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_{T} + S(1-t)}{\text{EV paid} + \text{Transaction costs} + \text{Costs to achieve (after tax)}} \]

where \(\text{NOPAT}_{T}\) = target EBIT × (1 − t) and EV paid = equity price + net debt assumed or refinanced. Two variants are common: before PPA amortisation (treating intangible amortisation as non-economic, since the brand or customer base is maintained by ongoing spend) and after it (a stricter accounting view). Compare with the acquirer's WACC adjusted for the target's risk (if materially different, use the target's WACC).

\[ \text{EVA}_{\text{deal}} = (\text{ROIC}_{\text{deal}} - \text{WACC}) \times \text{Invested capital} \]

The year in which ROIC crosses WACC is the "value break-even year"; many acquirers present it alongside EPS-accretion year.

Illustration

Bharat Polymers / Deccan Packaging, 60% debt / 40% cash, equity price ₹2,700 crore, target net debt ₹200 crore left in place, transaction costs ₹40 crore paid from cash.

₹ crore Acquirer Target Deal effects Pro-forma
Net debt 1,500 200 +1,620 debt, +1,080 + 40 cash used 4,440
EBITDA FY27 2,200 300 +100 synergies 2,600
ND/EBITDA 0.68x 0.67x 1.71x (1.78x ex-synergies)
Gross interest 255 36 +145.8 + 4.9 fee 441.7
EBITDA/interest 8.6x 8.3x 5.9x

ROIC (pre-PPA): target EBIT ₹240 crore → NOPAT ₹180 crore; plus synergies after tax ₹75 crore = ₹255 crore. Invested capital = 2,700 + 200 + 40 = ₹2,940 crore. ROIC = 8.7%, versus WACC 11.5% — so although Year 1 EPS is broadly neutral (s02), the deal earns below its cost of capital in Year 1. ROIC after PPA amortisation is (255 − 60) ÷ 2,940 = 6.6%. Run-rate synergies of, say, ₹180 crore by Year 3 and target EBIT growth are needed to reach WACC.

Model layout

Add a "Credit & returns" block under the EPS output with: pro-forma ND, EBITDA (with and without synergies), ND/EBITDA versus covenant, headroom (covenant EBITDA at which breach occurs), interest cover and DSCR, TOL/TNW, ROIC versus WACC by year, and a flag row:

=IF(ND_EBITDA > Covenant_Max, "BREACH", IF(ND_EBITDA > Covenant_Max - 0.5, "TIGHT", "OK"))

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Covenant definitions differ from model definitions. Read the facility agreement: EBITDA may exclude exceptional items, include pro-forma acquired EBITDA and allow capped synergies.
  • Rating thresholds. Agencies publish sector-specific downgrade triggers (e.g. net debt/EBITDA sustained above a level); a downgrade raises the cost of existing and new debt — feed it back into the interest line (t5 s05).
  • Seasonality. Year-end net debt in cash-cyclical businesses (sugar, fertilisers) can be unrepresentative; test average or peak leverage.
  • Equity-accounted stake. If the target is not consolidated (s04), its EBITDA is excluded but acquisition debt is in — leverage looks far worse.
  • ROIC on incremental vs total capital. For bolt-ons, measure on the deal's own invested capital; for transformational deals, also show combined-company ROIC.
  • Indian tax on interest. If acquisition interest is non-deductible, DSCR and PAT both worsen; NOPAT-based ROIC is unaffected (it is unlevered).

Key takeaways

  • Present Net Debt/EBITDA, interest cover/DSCR and ROIC vs WACC next to EPS accretion.
  • Pro-forma net debt adds new debt and cash used; avoid double counting refinanced target debt.
  • Follow covenant definitions (LTM, synergy credit caps, Ind AS 116 treatment) rather than model convenience.
  • In India also check TOL/TNW (goodwill reduces tangible net worth), parent-level debt service and RBI acquisition-finance conditions.
  • An EPS-neutral deal can earn ROIC well below WACC; show the year ROIC crosses WACC.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pro-forma leverage and covenant headroom [L2]

Acquirer net debt ₹3,200 crore, LTM EBITDA ₹2,000 crore. Target net debt ₹450 crore (to be refinanced by new debt), LTM EBITDA ₹520 crore. Equity price ₹4,000 crore funded by ₹1,000 crore cash and the rest by new debt; target debt refinanced by an additional new tranche. Fees ₹60 crore paid from cash. The facility covenant is Net Debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.25x, allowing synergy credit capped at 10% of combined EBITDA; expected synergies ₹180 crore. Compute pro-forma leverage with and without the synergy credit, and the EBITDA fall that would cause a breach.

Q2. ROIC break-even year [L2]

A deal's invested capital is ₹5,000 crore. Target EBIT is ₹380 crore in Year 1, growing 8% a year. Pre-tax synergies phase in at ₹40 crore, ₹110 crore and ₹160 crore in Years 1–3 and grow with EBIT thereafter. Tax 25.168%; WACC 11%. Find the first year ROIC (pre-PPA) exceeds WACC, showing the calculation for each year.

Q3. TOL/TNW after a high-premium deal [L2]

Pre-deal, an acquirer has total outside liabilities of ₹6,000 crore and net worth of ₹5,000 crore (goodwill nil). It acquires a target for ₹3,500 crore funded by debt; PPA recognises intangibles of ₹900 crore, DTL ₹226 crore and goodwill of ₹1,700 crore; target outside liabilities ₹800 crore. Estimate pro-forma TOL/TNW and explain why a bank might be concerned even though Net Debt/EBITDA stays below 2.5x.

Q4. Lender versus board narrative [L3]

The CFO presents a deal as "8% EPS accretive, 2.9x leverage falling to 2.0x in two years". The lender's credit committee notes structural subordination (debt at the parent, target 70% owned and listed), a dividend payout policy at the target of 30%, and LODR Reg 23 constraints. Draft the key questions the credit committee should ask and the additional model outputs you would add to answer them.
