Quick Accretion Test: Funding Cost vs Target Earnings Yield

Scope: Quick accretion test: comparing the cost of each funding source with the target's earnings yield at offer price

Why it matters

Before a full model exists — on a first call with a promoter, in a board strategy session, or in an interview — you need to say within a minute whether a deal is likely to be accretive. The quick test does this by comparing two yields: what the acquirer "pays" per rupee of consideration (the after-tax cost of each funding source) and what it "earns" per rupee (the target's earnings yield at the offer price). It is also the best diagnostic tool for a full model: if the full model says a deal is accretive and the quick test says dilutive, there must be a specific driver (synergies, PPA, NCI, time-weighting) that explains the gap, and you should be able to name it.

Core notes

The two yields

Target earnings yield at offer.

\[ y_{T} = \frac{\text{Target EPS}}{\text{Offer price per share}} = \frac{\text{Target PAT}}{\text{Equity purchase price}} = \frac{1}{\text{Offer P/E}} \]

This is the PAT the acquirer buys per rupee of equity consideration. Use the same forecast year as the accretion test (usually forward FY+1 earnings), on the normalised basis from s01.

After-tax cost of each funding source.

Source Pre-tax cost After-tax cost Why
New debt \(r_{d}\) \(r_{d}(1-t)\) Interest is deductible (subject to Indian restrictions)
Existing cash \(r_{c}\) \(r_{c}(1-t)\) Taxable interest income is forgone
New acquirer shares – \(1 / \text{P/E}_{A} = \text{EPS}_{A} / P_{A}\) Each new share issued at price \(P_{A}\) claims \(\text{EPS}_{A}\) of earnings

The "cost" of stock is the acquirer's earnings yield: issuing ₹100 of shares at a P/E of 25 hands ₹4 of annual earnings to new holders — a 4% earnings cost. It is an accounting cost, not the cost of equity (which is typically 12–15% in India and is the right number for valuation, not for EPS).

Decision rule

For a single funding source, the deal is accretive before synergies and PPA if

\[ \text{After-tax cost of funding} < y_{T} \]

For a mix, compare the weighted cost (concept c05):

\[ k_{\text{acq}} = w_{d}\,r_{d}(1-t) + w_{c}\,r_{c}(1-t) + w_{s}\,\frac{1}{\text{P/E}_{A}} \]

with \(w_{d}+w_{c}+w_{s}=1\). If \(k_{\text{acq}} < y_{T}\), accretive; if equal, neutral; if higher, dilutive.

Why it works. Accretion before synergies requires that the earnings added (target PAT) exceed the earnings "given up" to fund the price (after-tax interest plus earnings handed to new shareholders). Dividing both sides by the purchase price gives the yield comparison. For an all-stock deal, the rule collapses to the P/E rule (concept c04): acquirer P/E > offer P/E.

Illustration

Bharat Polymers: share price ₹500, EPS ₹20 (P/E 25). Deccan Packaging: EPS ₹15, offer ₹270 (offer P/E 18; \(y_{T}\) = 5.56%). Pre-tax debt 9%, cash yield 6.5%, tax 25%.

Funding After-tax cost vs 5.56% Verdict before synergies/PPA
100% stock 1 ÷ 25 = 4.00% below Accretive
100% cash 6.5% × 0.75 = 4.875% below Accretive
100% debt 9% × 0.75 = 6.75% above Dilutive
60% debt / 40% cash 0.6 × 6.75% + 0.4 × 4.875% = 6.00% above Dilutive
50% stock / 50% debt 0.5 × 4% + 0.5 × 6.75% = 5.375% below Marginally accretive

Check for 100% cash with the full arithmetic: pro-forma PAT = 1,200 + 150 − 2,700 × 4.875% = ₹1,218.4 crore; EPS = ₹20.31 (+1.5%), consistent with the quick test.

Extending the test

The quick test ignores several items. Each can be folded in to make a "quick-plus" test:

  1. PPA amortisation. Replace target PAT with target PAT − PPA amortisation × (1 − t). In the illustration, ₹60 crore after-tax amortisation reduces \(y_{T}\) to 90 ÷ 2,700 = 3.33%, and every funding mix above becomes dilutive.
  2. Synergies. Add synergies × (1 − t) to target PAT: with ₹100 crore pre-tax, adjusted \(y_{T}\) = (150 + 75 − 60) ÷ 2,700 = 6.11%.
  3. Fees. Add transaction and financing fees to the "price" denominator (they must be funded) — or treat amortised financing fees as part of the debt cost.
  4. Refinancing of target debt. If Uses include repaying target debt, switch to an EV basis: compare the after-tax cost of funding the EV with the target's unlevered after-tax earnings yield, NOPAT ÷ EV. Comparing PAT with equity price while funding the debt repayment too is a mismatch.
  5. NCI. For partial stakes, use the acquired stake's share of PAT over the consideration actually paid.

Using the test as a model diagnostic

When the full model's accretion % differs in sign from the quick test, reconcile:

\[ \Delta \text{PAT} = \text{Price} \times (y_{T} - k_{\text{acq}}) + S(1-t) - \text{PPA}(1-t) - F(1-t) - \text{NCI} \]

for a cash/debt deal (where \(S\) = synergies, \(F\) = financing fee amortisation, and the stock component is zero). Each term can be checked against the full bridge. For stock deals the denominator also changes, so accretion % ≠ ΔPAT ÷ standalone PAT — the reconciliation is done on EPS.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Which acquirer P/E? Use forward P/E consistent with the forecast year of target EPS. Acquirer share prices often move on announcement; re-run at the undisturbed and post-announcement prices.
  • High-P/E Indian acquirers. Many Indian consumer and technology companies trade at 40–60x, making stock look nearly "free" (1.7–2.5% earnings cost). The accretion is real but tells you nothing about value — the target's holders receive a scarce, highly valued currency.
  • Non-deductible interest. If acquisition interest is not tax-deductible (see s02), use the pre-tax rate as the after-tax cost.
  • Negative or very low target earnings. \(y_{T}\) near zero makes every funding source dilutive; use forward years or EBITDA-based tests.
  • Cash that would otherwise repay debt. Use the existing debt rate rather than deposit yield as the opportunity cost.
  • Rate and rating changes. If the deal pushes leverage beyond rating thresholds, existing debt reprices too; the marginal cost of debt is then higher than the new-loan coupon.

Key takeaways

  • Compare the after-tax cost of each funding source with the target earnings yield at the offer price (target EPS ÷ offer price).
  • Cost of debt = \(r_{d}(1-t)\); cost of cash = \(r_{c}(1-t)\); cost of stock = 1 ÷ acquirer P/E.
  • Weighted cost below target earnings yield means accretive before synergies and PPA; for all-stock this is the P/E rule.
  • Fold in PPA amortisation, synergies, fees, refinancing (EV basis) and NCI for a "quick-plus" test.
  • Use the test to reconcile and challenge the full model, not as a substitute for it.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify funding mixes [L1]

Acquirer P/E 32; target EPS ₹24; offer price ₹480; pre-tax debt cost 8.8%; cash yield 7%; tax 25.168%. Compute the target earnings yield and the after-tax cost of all-stock, all-cash, all-debt and 40% stock / 60% debt funding, and classify each as accretive or dilutive before synergies and PPA.

Q2. Quick-plus with PPA and synergies [L2]

In Q1 the equity purchase price is ₹4,800 crore. PPA amortisation is ₹90 crore pre-tax per year and run-rate synergies ₹70 crore pre-tax. Recompute the adjusted target yield and determine which funding mixes from Q1 remain accretive.

Q3. EV basis for refinancing [L2]

A target with equity value ₹2,000 crore at offer and debt of ₹800 crore (to be refinanced) has EBIT ₹300 crore and PAT ₹165 crore. Tax 25%. The acquirer funds the whole EV with new debt at 9%. Explain why comparing ₹165 crore ÷ ₹2,000 crore with 6.75% is inconsistent, and perform the correct comparison.

💡 Hint: Compare after-tax EBIT over EV with the after-tax cost of funding the whole EV.

Q4. Interview: why is stock "cheap"? [L3]

An interviewer asks: "Our acquirer trades at 55x earnings and the target at 22x. The CFO says paying in stock is accretive so we should stretch the price to 35x. Is stock our cheapest currency?" Prepare a structured answer distinguishing the accounting cost of stock from the economic cost of equity, and explain what the CFO's logic misses.
