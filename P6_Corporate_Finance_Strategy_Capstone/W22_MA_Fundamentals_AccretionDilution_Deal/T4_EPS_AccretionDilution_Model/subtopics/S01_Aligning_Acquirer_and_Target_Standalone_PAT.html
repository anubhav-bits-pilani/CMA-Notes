Subtopic note

Aligning Acquirer and Target Standalone PAT Projections

Scope: Standalone projections: acquirer and target PAT (consensus or own model), aligned to the same fiscal year

Why it matters

Accretion is a ratio of two EPS figures, and both rest on standalone PAT forecasts. If the acquirer's FY27 PAT comes from a consensus that already includes a one-off land sale gain, or the target's numbers run on a December year-end, the accretion percentage can swing by several points for reasons that have nothing to do with the deal. In practice, the first hours of any corporate-development or banking engagement are spent agreeing "whose numbers": the acquirer board wants management's own plan, the fairness-opinion provider prefers consensus, and the lenders will run a downside case. The FP&A analyst's job is to put both companies on an identical basis — same fiscal year, same earnings definition, same accounting policies — before a single pro-forma adjustment is layered on.

Core notes

Sources of standalone PAT

Consensus estimates. For NSE-listed acquirers and targets, consensus PAT and EPS are available from Bloomberg, LSEG (Refinitiv), Capitaline/ACE Equity or a manual average of broker reports. Consensus is neutral and defensible in a public announcement, because it is what the market is already pricing; the fairness opinion and any investor presentation normally use it. Its weaknesses: broker coverage of smaller Indian targets may be two or three analysts, estimates may be stale (dated before the last quarterly result), and brokers differ in what they treat as exceptional.

Own model. For an unlisted target, or where management has better information (the acquirer's own budget, the target's information memorandum and data-room projections), PAT comes from a three-statement model of the kind built in Weeks 13–15. The acquirer's own budget is usually used internally; the target's management case is almost always haircut after quality-of-earnings due diligence, because sellers' projections are optimistic.

Practice. Build both and keep a case switch: Case = 1 consensus, Case = 2 management, Case = 3 acquirer's diligence (haircut) case. Accretion is presented on consensus externally and on the diligence case internally.

=CHOOSE($C$3, Consensus!F12, MgmtCase!F12, DDCase!F12)

Same fiscal year

Indian companies are required to follow an April–March financial year under Sec 2(41) of the Companies Act 2013 (with limited exceptions approved by the NCLT for companies that are holding companies or subsidiaries of foreign entities needing consolidation on a different year). So two Indian listed companies are usually aligned already. Misalignment arises when:

  • the target is a foreign company or an Indian subsidiary of a foreign group with a December or June year-end;
  • the target's last full year is stale and the analyst wants "last twelve months" (LTM) figures;
  • the deal closes mid-year and the stub period must be identified (handled by time-weighting in s03).

Calendarisation. To convert a December year-end target to an Indian FY ending 31 March:

\[ \text{PAT}_{\text{FY Mar-27}} = \tfrac{9}{12}\,\text{PAT}_{\text{CY26}} + \tfrac{3}{12}\,\text{PAT}_{\text{CY27}} \]

where CY26 is the calendar year January–December 2026. The weights equal the months of each calendar year falling within April 2026–March 2027. If quarterly data exist, summing four actual or forecast quarters is more accurate than weighting annual figures, especially for seasonal businesses (cement, FMCG with festive-quarter peaks).

LTM construction. LTM PAT = last full-year PAT + current year-to-date PAT − prior year same-period PAT. LTM is used for valuation multiples; forward years are used for accretion.

Same earnings definition

Both PAT figures must be:

  1. Attributable to owners of the parent — after existing NCI in each company's own consolidated accounts.
  2. Consolidated if each company has subsidiaries; mixing a target's standalone PAT with an acquirer's consolidated PAT omits the target's subsidiaries.
  3. Normalised for non-recurring items — exceptional items disclosed under Schedule III, profit on sale of assets, one-off litigation, the quality-of-earnings adjustments from DD. Accretion is a run-rate concept.
  4. Pre-deal — the acquirer's consensus should not already include any brokers' guess of the deal's effect. Check report dates against any leak or announcement date.
  5. Under the same accounting policies — Ind AS for both; if the target reports under Indian GAAP (AS) as an unlisted company below the Ind AS thresholds, convert key items (Ind AS 116 leases, Ind AS 115 revenue timing, Ind AS 109 expected credit losses, ESOP fair value expensing under Ind AS 102). A target on AS with large operating leases will show higher EBITDA under Ind AS 116 and a different PAT profile.
  6. Fully taxed at its sustainable rate. A target enjoying a tax holiday (e.g. an SEZ unit under Sec 10AA) or still on the old regime with MAT credit should be modelled at its actual rate, with the holiday's expiry explicitly scheduled.

If the acquirer's consensus FY27 PAT is ₹1,200 crore but includes ₹40 crore post-tax profit on sale of a warehouse, the run-rate base is ₹1,160 crore. The standalone EPS used as the accretion benchmark must also be computed on ₹1,160 crore, otherwise the comparison is inconsistent.

Model layout

A clean layout keeps one "Standalone" tab with two blocks (acquirer and target), each showing revenue, EBITDA, D&A, EBIT, interest, PBT, tax, PAT, NCI, PAT to owners and diluted shares, for FY26A, FY27E and FY28E. The pro-forma tab then pulls only PAT to owners and diluted shares. This keeps the audit trail clear: any change in standalone assumptions flows through a single link.

Items that will be adjusted later — do not double count

Some lines in the target's standalone forecast will be replaced in the pro-forma: interest on target debt that will be refinanced, interest income on target cash that will be swept, amortisation of the target's existing acquired intangibles (replaced by the new PPA values), and any related-party management fees to the seller that stop at closing. Flag these in the standalone block so the pro-forma adjustment lines can reverse them explicitly rather than editing the standalone numbers.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Consensus that excludes or includes ESOP cost. Some brokers show EPS before ESOP charges. Ind AS 102 expense is a real cost; use PAT after ESOP expense for both companies.
  • Target in a loss or near-zero PAT. EPS accretion becomes meaningless (dilution is mechanical). Shift emphasis to EBITDA accretion, cash EPS, or the year when PAT turns positive, and to value metrics.
  • Different tax regimes. If one party is on Sec 115BAA (25.168%) and the other still on the old regime using MAT credits, the pro-forma tax rate on synergies depends on where synergies arise.
  • Fiscal-year shift after closing. If an acquired Indian subsidiary of a foreign group had an approved non-March year, it must move to March after becoming a subsidiary of an Indian holding company unless an exemption applies — model the transition period.
  • Stale consensus. If the target's last quarterly result changed the outlook, document an adjusted consensus rather than silently mixing your own numbers into "consensus".

Key takeaways

  • Accretion is only as reliable as the two standalone PAT bases; agree the source (consensus vs own model vs diligence case) explicitly and keep a case switch.
  • Align fiscal years; calendarise foreign targets as 9/12 of the earlier calendar year plus 3/12 of the later one for an April–March FY.
  • Use consolidated, normalised PAT attributable to owners of the parent, after ESOP expense, under Ind AS for both parties.
  • Strip out non-recurring items from both, and compute standalone EPS on the same normalised base used in the pro-forma.
  • Flag target lines (target interest, swept cash income, old intangible amortisation, seller fees) that pro-forma adjustments will reverse.

Practice Questions

Q1. Calendarising a June year-end target [L1]

A target has a July–June fiscal year. Forecast PAT: FY Jun-26 ₹96 crore, FY Jun-27 ₹120 crore. Derive the target's PAT for the acquirer's FY April 2026–March 2027, assuming profits accrue evenly within each target fiscal year.

💡 Hint: Count how many months of each target year fall within April 2026–March 2027.

Q2. Normalising consensus PAT [L2]

Acquirer consensus FY27 PAT is ₹2,450 crore and includes: ₹60 crore pre-tax gain on sale of an investment property; ₹25 crore pre-tax reversal of a legal provision; ESOP expense of ₹30 crore that one broker (out of four equally weighted) excluded. Tax rate 25.168%. Acquirer diluted shares 98 crore. Compute normalised standalone PAT and EPS that should serve as the accretion benchmark, stating each adjustment.

Q3. Indian GAAP target conversion [L2]

An unlisted target reports under Indian GAAP (AS). It has operating lease rentals of ₹48 crore per year; under Ind AS 116 the right-of-use asset would be ₹180 crore depreciated over 5 years and lease liability interest at 9% on an opening liability of ₹180 crore. Its PAT under AS is ₹70 crore; tax 25.168%. Estimate first-year PAT under Ind AS and explain why using the AS PAT in the pro-forma would misstate accretion.

Q4. Choosing the projection source [L3]

You are the FP&A lead of an NSE-listed acquirer. The target (listed, covered by two brokers) has consensus FY27 PAT of ₹310 crore, management projections in the VDR of ₹365 crore, and your QoE team's diligence case of ₹285 crore. Explain which figure you would use for (a) the board approval pack, (b) the public announcement investor presentation, (c) the lenders' base case, and how you would structure the model so all three can be shown without separate files.
