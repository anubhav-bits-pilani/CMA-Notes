Subtopic note

Pro-forma PAT Adjustments in an Accretion/Dilution Model

Scope: Pro-forma adjustments: synergies, foregone interest on cash, new debt interest, PPA amortisation/depreciation, financing-fee amortisation

Why it matters

The sum of acquirer PAT and target PAT is not the combined company's PAT. The transaction itself creates new income-statement lines: the acquirer loses the interest it was earning on cash it spends, pays interest on money it borrows, amortises intangibles and depreciates fair-value step-ups recognised in the purchase price allocation, amortises the upfront fees on the acquisition loan, and (hopefully) earns synergies. These lines determine whether the deal is accretive. Investors, rating agencies and the audit committee will each pick at a different line — the analyst who can defend every adjustment, with its tax effect and timing, is the one whose model gets used.

Core notes

The bridge

Pro-forma PAT is built as a bridge from the simple sum of standalone PAT:

Synergies

Synergies come from the topic 1 synergy schedule: cost synergies (procurement, plant consolidation, SG&A), revenue synergies converted to EBIT at the contribution margin, less dis-synergies. Three rules:

  1. Phase them. Year 1 is commonly 25–50% of run-rate, Year 2 75–100%. Use the phasing schedule, not run-rate.
  2. Probability-weight revenue synergies or exclude them from the base case entirely.
  3. Treat one-time costs to achieve separately. Integration costs (severance, IT migration, rebranding) are usually shown below the line as exceptional items, and accretion is presented both "excluding" and "including" them. Under Ind AS they are expensed when incurred (restructuring provisions only when Ind AS 37 criteria are met), so reported EPS includes them.

After-tax synergies = pre-tax synergies × (1 − t).

Foregone interest on cash

Cash spent on the purchase price, fees or the open offer would otherwise have earned treasury income (bank FDs, liquid funds, T-bills). The lost income is:

\[ \text{Foregone interest} = \text{Cash used} \times r_{\text{cash}} \]

where \(r_{\text{cash}}\) is the pre-tax yield on the acquirer's treasury portfolio. Only cash above minimum operating cash can be used (topic 2). If the cash would otherwise have repaid existing debt, the opportunity cost is the existing debt rate, not the deposit yield — use the higher of the two where that is the realistic alternative.

New debt interest

\[ \text{Interest} = \text{Average debt balance} \times r_{\text{debt}} \]

For a bullet loan the balance is constant; for an amortising term loan or a cash sweep, use the average of opening and closing balances from the Week 15 debt schedule (with a circularity switch if interest depends on cash flow that depends on interest). Floating-rate loans (MCLR/repo-linked, or T-bill-linked NCDs) should use the forward curve or a stated assumption, with a rate sensitivity. Where acquisition debt is raised in the target or an SPV that later merges, interest is in the same tax pool as the target's profits.

Refinanced target debt. If the uses include repaying the target's existing loans, reverse the target's interest expense (it is already deducted in target PAT) and include the new refinancing debt in the new-debt line. Failing to reverse it double counts interest.

PPA amortisation and depreciation

From the topic 3 PPA:

\[ \text{Annual amortisation} = \sum_i \frac{\text{FV of intangible}_i}{\text{Useful life}_i} \qquad \text{Step-up depreciation} = \frac{\text{PPE fair value} - \text{PPE book value}}{\text{Remaining useful life}} \]

In a share acquisition the tax base of the target's assets does not change, so these charges are not tax-deductible. However, a DTL was created at acquisition on the step-ups; as the step-up is amortised, the DTL unwinds and reduces deferred tax expense by amortisation × t. The net after-tax hit to consolidated PAT is therefore amortisation × (1 − t), as if the amortisation were tax-deductible. Remember: goodwill is not amortised under Ind AS (it is tested for impairment under Ind AS 36), and inventory step-up is a one-time COGS charge in the first inventory turn after closing.

Reverse the amortisation of the target's previously recognised acquired intangibles, because in consolidation they are replaced by the new fair values.

Financing-fee amortisation

Upfront arrangement fees, underwriting fees on NCDs and lenders' legal costs are transaction costs of a financial liability measured at amortised cost. Under Ind AS 109 they are deducted from the initial carrying amount and recognised through the effective interest rate over the expected life (Ind AS 109, paras B5.4.1–B5.4.3). In the model, the straight-line approximation fee ÷ tenor is common for bullet loans; for amortising loans compute the EIR schedule (c06). The fee is tax-deductible over the loan life in most cases, so after-tax amortisation = amortisation × (1 − t).

Worked illustration

Bharat Polymers acquires 100% of Deccan Packaging for ₹2,700 crore equity (10 crore shares at ₹270). Funding 60% new debt (₹1,620 crore, 9%, 5-year bullet, fee 1.5%) and 40% cash (₹1,080 crore earning 6.5%). PPA: customer relationships ₹600 crore (10 years); PPE step-up ₹200 crore (10 years). Year 1 synergies ₹100 crore pre-tax. Tax 25%.

₹ crore Pre-tax After-tax
Acquirer PAT 1,200.00
Target PAT 150.00
Synergies 100.00 75.00
Foregone interest (1,080 × 6.5%) (70.20) (52.65)
New debt interest (1,620 × 9%) (145.80) (109.35)
PPA amortisation + step-up depreciation (60 + 20) (80.00) (60.00)
Fee amortisation (24.30 ÷ 5) (4.86) (3.65)
Pro-forma PAT 1,199.35

With 60 crore acquirer shares and no new shares, pro-forma EPS is ₹19.99 against standalone ₹20.00 — almost exactly EPS-neutral with ₹100 crore of synergies. Without synergies pro-forma PAT is ₹1,124.35 crore and EPS ₹18.74 (6.3% dilutive).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Tax deductibility of acquisition interest in India. Interest on borrowings used to buy shares held as investments may be restricted: Sec 57 caps deduction against dividend income at 20% of the dividend, and the tax authorities often challenge Sec 36(1)(iii) claims. Unless the debt is pushed down (e.g. a post-acquisition merger of the SPV with the target), model a downside with interest non-deductible — after-tax cost then equals the pre-tax rate. Sec 94B also limits interest paid to non-resident associated enterprises to 30% of EBITDA.
  • Inventory step-up. A one-off hit in the first quarter or two; show Year 1 accretion including and excluding it.
  • Transaction costs. Advisory and legal fees are expensed under Ind AS 103.53 in the closing period. Exclude them from run-rate accretion but disclose the reported EPS impact.
  • Cash at target. If the target's own cash is swept to the acquirer, reverse the target's interest income on it.
  • Interest on open-offer funding. Escrow deposits under SAST Reg 17 are blocked before closing; funding costs during that period are pre-closing and often ignored, but large escrows for big offers can be material.
  • Hedging. If the new debt is swapped from floating to fixed, use the swapped rate and remember hedge accounting under Ind AS 109.

Key takeaways

  • Build pro-forma PAT as an explicit bridge with pre-tax, tax and after-tax columns for every adjustment.
  • Foregone interest uses the acquirer's realistic opportunity yield on cash; new debt interest uses average balances from the debt schedule.
  • Reverse items replaced by the deal: target interest on refinanced debt, interest on swept cash, old intangible amortisation.
  • PPA amortisation and step-up depreciation hit PAT at amortisation × (1 − t) because the DTL unwinds; goodwill is never amortised.
  • Financing fees are spread through EIR under Ind AS 109; advisory fees are expensed at closing under Ind AS 103.
  • Phase synergies and present integration costs and inventory step-up separately so run-rate and reported EPS can both be shown.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the PAT bridge [L2]

Acquirer FY27 PAT ₹950 crore, 40 crore diluted shares. Target FY27 PAT ₹120 crore (after ₹30 crore pre-tax interest on loans to be refinanced at closing). Equity price ₹1,600 crore; target debt refinanced ₹300 crore. Funding: ₹500 crore acquirer cash (yield 7%), balance new term loan at 9.5% with 2% upfront fee and 6-year bullet tenor. PPA: brand ₹250 crore (20 years), technology ₹180 crore (6 years), PPE step-up ₹90 crore (9 years). Year 1 synergies ₹40 crore pre-tax. Tax 25.168%, all closing on 1 April 2026. Build the full bridge and compute Year 1 pro-forma EPS and accretion %.

💡 Hint: Total funding equals equity price plus refinancing plus the financing fee (ignore other fees); reverse the target's existing interest.

Q2. Journal entries for the financing fee [L1]

A ₹1,000 crore 5-year bullet loan is drawn with a 1.5% arrangement fee paid upfront; coupon 9% payable annually. Pass the journal entries at drawdown under Ind AS 109 and explain how the first-year interest expense differs from the coupon paid.

Q3. Opportunity cost of cash [L2]

An acquirer will use ₹800 crore of cash. Its treasury earns 6.8% in liquid funds, but it also has ₹600 crore of existing term debt at 9.4% that is prepayable without penalty. Discuss which rate should be used for foregone interest and how you would show the choice in the model.

Q4. Non-deductible acquisition interest [L3]

In Q1, assume tax counsel advises that interest on the new acquisition loan held in the acquirer (an investment holding structure with dividend income from the target of ₹60 crore per year) will be deductible only to the extent of 20% of dividend income. Recompute the after-tax cost of new debt and the effect on accretion, and suggest two structuring alternatives that could restore deductibility.

💡 Hint: Consider debt push-down through a subsequent merger and borrowing at the target level for refinancing.

Q5. Inventory step-up presentation [L2]

The PPA includes an inventory step-up of ₹45 crore, all of which will be sold within the first four months after a 1 April closing. Explain how it affects reported Q1 and FY27 EPS, how analysts typically adjust for it, and how you would structure the model columns to show both views.
