Key concept

Weighted Cost of Acquisition vs Target Earnings Yield

Concept: Weighted cost of acquisition = Σ (funding % × after-tax cost), where cost of stock = 1 ÷ acquirer P/E; accretive if below target earnings yield at offer

Statement

\[ k_{\text{acq}} = \sum_{i} w_{i}\,k_{i} = w_{d}\,r_{d}(1-t) + w_{c}\,r_{c}(1-t) + w_{s}\,\frac{1}{\text{P/E}_{A}} \]

\[ y_{T} = \frac{\text{EPS}_{T}}{P_{\text{offer}}} \qquad \text{Accretive (before synergies and PPA)} \iff k_{\text{acq}} < y_{T} \]

where \(w_{d}, w_{c}, w_{s}\) are the shares of the purchase price funded by new debt, existing cash and new acquirer stock (\(\sum w_{i} = 1\)); \(r_{d}\) = pre-tax cost of new debt; \(r_{c}\) = pre-tax yield on cash; \(t\) = tax rate; \(\text{P/E}_{A}\) = acquirer P/E at the issue price; \(y_{T}\) = target earnings yield at the offer price.

Explanation

Each funding source has an earnings cost per rupee of consideration: debt costs its after-tax interest; cash costs the after-tax income it no longer earns; stock costs the earnings per rupee that new shareholders will claim, which is the acquirer's earnings yield \(1/\text{P/E}_{A}\). The target delivers \(y_{T}\) of earnings per rupee paid. If what you pay per rupee is less than what you receive, EPS rises.

For cash and debt the result is exact in rupee terms: \(\Delta\text{PAT} = \text{Price} \times (y_{T} - k_{\text{acq}})\) before synergies and PPA. With stock in the mix, the sign is exact but the magnitude of accretion % depends on the enlarged share base.

Important caveats:

  • The cost of stock here is an accounting cost, not the cost of equity (\(k_{e}\) of 12–15% in India). For value decisions, use WACC and NPV, not \(k_{\text{acq}}\).
  • Adjust \(y_{T}\) for PPA amortisation and synergies: \(y_{T}^{\text{adj}} = [\text{PAT}_{T} + S(1-t) - A_{PPA}(1-t)] / \text{Price}\).
  • Refinancing: if target debt is repaid, compare on an EV basis (NOPAT ÷ EV versus cost of funding the EV).
  • Non-deductible interest changes the debt cost to \(r_{d}\).
  • Funding mix also affects risk: a cheaper accounting mix (more debt) may raise the cost of equity and trigger rating action.

Illustration

Acquirer P/E 25 (stock cost 4.00%); debt 9% pre-tax (6.75% after tax at 25%); cash 6.5% (4.875% after tax). Target EPS ₹15, offer ₹270 → \(y_{T}\) = 5.56%. Equity price ₹2,700 crore.

Mix (debt / cash / stock) \(k_{\text{acq}}\) vs 5.56% ΔPAT before stock dilution (₹ crore)
60 / 40 / 0 6.00% Dilutive 2,700 × (5.56% − 6.00%) ≈ −12.0
0 / 40 / 60 4.35% Accretive n.m. (shares issued)
30 / 30 / 40 5.09% Accretive n.m.
100 / 0 / 0 6.75% Dilutive ≈ −32.3

Check the 60/40 row: 150 − 109.35 − 52.65 = −₹12.0 crore change in PAT, matching the yield method. Adding ₹100 crore synergies and ₹80 crore PPA (both pre-tax) gives \(y_{T}^{\text{adj}}\) = (150 + 75 − 60) ÷ 2,700 = 6.11% > 6.00%, so the 60/40 deal becomes marginally accretive before fees.

Connections

  • Generalises the P/E rule (c04) to any mix; c04 is the case \(w_{s} = 1\).
  • Uses after-tax cash and debt costs defined in c03.
  • s05 applies it as the "quick accretion test" and model diagnostic; the weekly assessment asks you to apply it to all-stock versus all-debt choices.
  • Topic 5 c02: when \(k_{\text{acq}} > y_{T}^{\text{adj, ex-synergies}}\), the break-even synergy is positive.
  • Contrasts with WACC (Week 16): \(k_{\text{acq}}\) answers an EPS question, WACC answers a value question.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choose a funding mix [L2]

Acquirer P/E 20; pre-tax debt 9.5%; cash yield 7.2%; tax 25.168%. Target offer P/E 16. Available: up to ₹1,000 crore surplus cash; debt capacity ₹2,500 crore; equity price ₹4,000 crore. Find the funding mix that maximises accretion before synergies and PPA, and the mix that minimises dilution of EPS if leverage cannot exceed ₹1,800 crore of new debt.

Q2. All-stock vs all-debt [L2]

Acquirer P/E 14 (share price ₹420); pre-tax debt 10%; tax 25%. Target EPS ₹30, offer price ₹480. Compute \(k_{\text{acq}}\) and the verdict for all-stock and all-debt funding, and determine the debt rate at which all-debt becomes EPS-neutral.

💡 Hint: Set the after-tax debt cost equal to the target earnings yield.

Q3. When the cheapest EPS mix is not the best [L3]

For an Indian acquirer whose shares trade at 45x earnings, the bankers recommend 100% stock as "most accretive". Explain three reasons the board might still prefer some debt or cash, referring to cost of equity, control dilution of the promoter group and signalling.
