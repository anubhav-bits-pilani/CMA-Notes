Key concept

Foregone Interest on Cash and New Debt Interest

Concept: Foregone interest = Cash used × pre-tax yield on cash; new debt interest = Debt raised × pre-tax rate (avg balance if amortising)

Statement

\[ I_{c} = C_{\text{used}} \times r_{c} \]

\[ I_{d} = \sum_{j} \frac{D_{j,\text{open}} + D_{j,\text{close}}}{2} \times r_{d,j} \]

where \(C_{\text{used}}\) = acquirer cash applied to the deal (purchase price, open offer, fees), limited to cash above minimum operating cash; \(r_{c}\) = pre-tax yield the cash was earning (or the rate on debt it would otherwise have repaid); \(D_{j,\text{open}}\) and \(D_{j,\text{close}}\) = opening and closing balances of debt tranche \(j\) in the period; \(r_{d,j}\) = pre-tax interest rate of tranche \(j\). For a bullet loan, opening = closing, so \(I_{d} = D \times r_{d}\). Both are deducted from PAT after tax: \(I_{c}(1-t)\) and \(I_{d}(1-t)\).

Explanation

Foregone interest is an opportunity cost: the standalone forecast assumes the cash stays invested in FDs, liquid mutual funds, commercial paper or G-secs and earns treasury income. Once spent on the deal, that income disappears. The appropriate yield is the acquirer's actual treasury yield (often 6–7.5% pre-tax for Indian corporates in recent rate cycles). If the cash would otherwise have been used to prepay existing debt, the opportunity cost is the existing debt rate. If the standalone model's cash balance already earns interest on average cash, the cleaner approach is to reduce the opening cash balance in the combined model and let the existing interest-income formula recompute.

New debt interest depends on the debt structure:

  • Bullet term loan or NCD: balance × coupon.
  • Amortising term loan: average balance × rate; Week 15's debt schedule supplies opening and closing balances.
  • Cash sweep: repayment depends on free cash flow, which depends on interest — a circular reference. Use an average-balance formula with an iteration switch, or compute interest on opening balance to break the loop (and disclose the convention).
  • Floating rate: repo-linked or MCLR-linked bank loans reset; use the forward curve or a stated rate path and sensitise.
  • Mid-year draw: time-weight from drawdown date.

Financing fees are not part of coupon interest but are part of the Ind AS 109 effective interest cost (c06); either include them in \(I_{d}\) via EIR or show them as a separate line. Commitment fees on undrawn facilities (e.g. an open-offer tranche sized for 100% acceptance) are an additional cost.

Illustration

Equity price ₹2,700 crore funded ₹1,080 crore cash (yield 6.5%) and a ₹1,620 crore term loan at 9% repayable ₹270 crore at each year-end from Year 1. Tax 25%.

₹ crore Year 1 Year 2
Opening debt 1,620 1,350
Repayment (270) (270)
Closing debt 1,350 1,080
Average balance 1,485 1,215
Interest at 9% 133.65 109.35
After-tax interest 100.24 82.01
Foregone interest after tax (1,080 × 6.5% × 0.75) 52.65 52.65 + lost interest on cash used for repayments

Using the opening balance in Year 1 (₹145.80 crore) would overstate interest by ₹12.15 crore pre-tax. Note that repayments themselves use cash, so Year 2 foregone interest should reflect the lower cash balance if repayments come from acquirer treasury rather than from operating cash flow — in an integrated model, this is automatic.

Practice Questions

Q1. Amortising loan interest [L1]

A ₹2,400 crore acquisition loan at 8.75% is drawn on 1 April 2026 and repaid in eight equal half-yearly instalments starting 30 September 2026. Compute FY27 and FY28 interest using average balances per half-year, and compare with interest on the opening annual balance.

Q2. Opportunity rate choice [L2]

An acquirer uses ₹1,500 crore of cash for a deal. Its treasury book yields 7.1%, it has ₹900 crore of 9.3% NCDs callable at par next quarter, and the board had approved a ₹500 crore buyback that will now be cancelled. Discuss which opportunity costs should appear in the pro-forma model and how the cancelled buyback affects the share count.

Q3. Circularity in a cash sweep [L3]

Describe how you would model interest on a cash-sweep acquisition loan in Excel so that the model remains stable, auditable and able to run data tables. Include the switch logic you would use and the reason data tables can break with circular references.

💡 Hint: Consider a circuit-breaker cell and an opening-balance fallback.
