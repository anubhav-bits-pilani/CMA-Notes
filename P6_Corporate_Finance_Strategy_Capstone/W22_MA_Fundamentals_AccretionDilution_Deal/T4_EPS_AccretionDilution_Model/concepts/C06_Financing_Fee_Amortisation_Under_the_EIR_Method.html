Key concept

Financing Fee Amortisation Under the EIR Method

Concept: Financing fee amortisation = Upfront fees ÷ loan tenor (EIR method in Ind AS 109)

Statement

Model approximation (straight-line):

\[ F_{\text{annual}} \approx \frac{\text{Upfront financing fees}}{\text{Loan tenor (years)}} \]

Ind AS 109 treatment (effective interest method): the loan is initially recognised at fair value less transaction costs, \(L_{0} = D - F\). The EIR \(r_{e}\) solves

\[ D - F = \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{CF_{k}}{(1+r_{e})^{k}} \]

and each period's finance cost is \(r_{e} \times \text{opening amortised cost}\). Fee amortisation in period \(k\) = finance cost − contractual interest paid.

Here \(D\) = principal drawn, \(F\) = upfront fees integral to the loan (arrangement, underwriting, lenders' legal and processing fees), \(CF_{k}\) = contractual interest and principal cash flows, \(n\) = expected life.

Explanation

Transaction costs directly attributable to issuing a financial liability measured at amortised cost are deducted from its initial carrying amount (Ind AS 109.5.1.1) and recognised over its life through the EIR (Ind AS 109, paras B5.4.1–B5.4.3). They are not expensed upfront, and — unlike M&A advisory fees — they do not fall under Ind AS 103.53 because they relate to the financing, not the business combination.

Practical points:

  • Bullet loans: straight-line is a close approximation; EIR front-loads slightly less amortisation and back-loads more (because the amortised cost grows).
  • Amortising loans: EIR amortisation declines with the balance; straight-line overstates later-year cost. Use the EIR schedule.
  • Prepayment: if the loan is prepaid (common when acquisition bridges are refinanced by NCDs or a QIP), the unamortised fee is written off to finance cost immediately — a one-off hit in the refinancing year.
  • Commitment fees on undrawn facilities are expensed over the commitment period if drawdown is not probable; if drawdown is probable they are deferred and included in EIR on drawdown.
  • Tax: financing fees are generally deductible over the loan period for Indian tax (judicial precedent supports spreading; check the position), so the after-tax PAT cost is \(F_{\text{annual}}(1-t)\).
  • Equity issue costs (QIP, preferential issue) go to equity under Ind AS 32.37, not P&L; they affect neither pro-forma PAT nor fee amortisation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Straight-line vs EIR for an amortising loan [L2]

A ₹1,000 crore 4-year loan at 9.5% is repaid ₹250 crore at each year-end, with a 2% upfront fee. Build the EIR schedule (state the EIR to two decimals) and compare annual fee amortisation with the straight-line approximation.

💡 Hint: Use Excel's =IRR() on net proceeds and contractual cash flows to find the EIR.

Q2. Refinancing write-off [L2]

In the illustration, the loan is fully prepaid at the end of Year 2 from QIP proceeds. Compute the Year 2 finance cost and the after-tax EPS impact of the write-off for an acquirer with 60 crore shares and a 25% tax rate.

Q3. Classify the costs [L1]

Classify each of the following under Ind AS and state where it goes in the pro-forma model: (a) ₹18 crore lead arranger fee on the acquisition loan; (b) ₹12 crore legal fees for the SPA; (c) ₹9 crore merchant banker fees for the open offer; (d) ₹6 crore QIP underwriting fee; (e) ₹2 crore commitment fee on an undrawn open-offer tranche where drawdown is not probable.
