Key concept

P/E Rule of Thumb for All-Stock Deals

Concept: Rule of thumb (no synergies): all-stock deal is accretive if acquirer P/E > target P/E paid (offer price ÷ target EPS)

Statement

For an all-stock acquisition with no synergies, no PPA amortisation and no other adjustments:

\[ \text{Accretive} \iff \text{P/E}_{A} > \text{P/E}_{\text{paid}} \qquad \text{where } \text{P/E}_{A} = \frac{P_{A}}{\text{EPS}_{A}}, \quad \text{P/E}_{\text{paid}} = \frac{P_{\text{offer}}}{\text{EPS}_{T}} \]

\(P_{A}\) = acquirer share price used to set the exchange ratio; \(\text{EPS}_{A}\) = acquirer standalone EPS; \(P_{\text{offer}}\) = offer price per target share; \(\text{EPS}_{T}\) = target EPS (same forecast year). Equal P/Es give EPS-neutrality.

Explanation

Derivation. Let the target have \(N_{T}\) shares. The acquirer issues \(N_{\text{new}} = N_{T} P_{\text{offer}} / P_{A}\) shares. Pro-forma EPS exceeds standalone EPS when

\[ \frac{\text{PAT}_{A} + \text{PAT}_{T}}{N_{A} + N_{\text{new}}} > \frac{\text{PAT}_{A}}{N_{A}} \iff \frac{\text{PAT}_{T}}{N_{\text{new}}} > \text{EPS}_{A} \iff \frac{\text{EPS}_{T}\,P_{A}}{P_{\text{offer}}} > \text{EPS}_{A} \iff \frac{P_{A}}{\text{EPS}_{A}} > \frac{P_{\text{offer}}}{\text{EPS}_{T}} \]

Intuitively, each new acquirer share "costs" \(\text{EPS}_{A}\) of earnings, and buys \(\text{PAT}_{T}/N_{\text{new}}\) of target earnings. A high-P/E acquirer has expensive paper: it gives away fewer earnings per rupee of value delivered.

Conditions and caveats.

  • The target P/E is the P/E paid (at the offer price, including premium), not the target's market P/E.
  • Use consistent EPS years (both forward or both trailing).
  • PPA amortisation reduces the effective target EPS; with PPA the test becomes \(\text{P/E}_{A} > P_{\text{offer}} / (\text{EPS}_{T} - \text{PPA per target share after tax})\).
  • Synergies increase effective target EPS and can make a deal accretive even when \(\text{P/E}_{A} < \text{P/E}_{\text{paid}}\).
  • The rule says nothing about value: a 60x acquirer buying a 40x target is accretive by the rule yet may be overpaying. This is the classic "bootstrapping" illusion of 1960s conglomerates — EPS grows through acquisitions while value does not.
  • Under Indian schemes, the swap ratio is set from a registered valuer's relative valuation, not directly from market prices; the effective \(P_{A}\) is the value per acquirer share implied by the ratio.

Illustration

Acquirer P/E = 500 ÷ 20 = 25x. Target EPS ₹15; offer ₹270 → P/E paid 18x. 25 > 18, so accretive before synergies and PPA. Check: new shares = 10 × 270 ÷ 500 = 5.4 crore; EPS = (1,200 + 150) ÷ 65.4 = ₹20.64 (+3.2%).

If the offer were ₹375 (P/E paid 25x): new shares 7.5 crore; EPS = 1,350 ÷ 67.5 = ₹20.00 — exactly neutral. At ₹450 (30x): EPS = 1,350 ÷ 69 = ₹19.57 (−2.2%).

With ₹60 crore after-tax PPA amortisation (₹6 per target share), effective target EPS is ₹9 and P/E paid at ₹270 becomes 30x — now 25 < 30 and the deal is dilutive (s03 shows −1.4%).

Connections

  • Special case of c05 with 100% stock funding: cost of stock = 1 ÷ P/E.
  • Mirror image of contribution analysis (s06): when \(\text{P/E}_{A} > \text{P/E}_{\text{paid}}\), target holders receive an ownership share below their earnings contribution.
  • Topic 5 s04: maximum offer price for EPS-neutrality in an all-stock deal is \(\text{P/E}_{A}\) × effective target EPS (adjusted for synergies and PPA).
  • Week 16 valuation: high relative P/E may reflect growth or overvaluation — the rule cannot distinguish.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the rule [L1]

Acquirer price ₹1,250, EPS ₹41.67. Target EPS ₹32; undisturbed price ₹760; proposed premium 25%. Determine whether an all-stock deal is accretive before synergies and PPA, and find the premium at which it becomes EPS-neutral.

Q2. Rule with PPA and synergies [L2]

In Q1, the target has 8 crore shares. PPA amortisation is ₹64 crore pre-tax per year and synergies ₹48 crore pre-tax; tax 25.168%. Recompute the effective P/E paid and the verdict.

Q3. Interview: bootstrapping [L3]

Explain, with a two-company numerical example of your own, how a high-P/E acquirer can report rising EPS through a series of stock acquisitions of lower-P/E targets with zero synergies, and why the market should not reward this with a constant P/E.
