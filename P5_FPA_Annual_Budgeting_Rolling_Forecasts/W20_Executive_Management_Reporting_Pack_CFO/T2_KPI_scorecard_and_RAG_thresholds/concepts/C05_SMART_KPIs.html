Key concept

SMART KPIs

Concept: SMART KPIs: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound

Statement

A KPI and its target are SMART when they are:

  • Specific — the measure is precisely defined (what, which population, which basis), leaving no room for interpretation.
  • Measurable — the data exists, is reliable, and can be calculated consistently from an identified source.
  • Achievable — the target is stretching but realistic given resources, market conditions and history.
  • Relevant — the KPI is linked to a strategic objective or value driver, and its owner can influence it.
  • Time-bound — the target has a deadline and a measurement frequency (by when, measured how often).

Explanation

SMART (usually credited to George Doran, 1981) is a quality test applied to each candidate KPI before it enters the scorecard and to each target at AOP time. It catches the weaknesses behind most scorecard failures: vague KPIs ("improve customer service"), unmeasurable ones ("brand love"), demotivating targets ("zero attrition"), vanity metrics with no link to value ("social media followers"), and targets with no deadline ("reduce DSO").

Interpreting each letter in an FP&A context:

  • Specific connects to the KPI definition sheet: formula, inclusions, exclusions, basis.
  • Measurable requires a governed source and a reconciliation — measurement that depends on a manual spreadsheet maintained by one person fails in practice.
  • Achievable is a judgement informed by history, benchmarks and drivers (e.g. reducing DSO from 68 to 45 days in one quarter when channel credit terms are 45–60 days is not achievable). Note the tension with stretch: AOP targets are usually achievable-with-effort; OKR key results deliberately stretch beyond.
  • Relevant links to the KPI tree (s02) and balanced scorecard (s06); "relevant" also means the owner has levers.
  • Time-bound includes both the target date (by 31 March 2027) and the review cadence (monthly in MBR).

Variants add letters — SMARTER (evaluated, reviewed) — but the core test is the same.

Illustration

Draft KPI Fails on SMART rewrite
"Improve collections" S, M, T Reduce DSO (T3M basis, excl. GST) from 68 to 60 days by 31 March 2027, reviewed monthly; owner Head of Credit Control
"Better delivery performance" S, M, T Raise line-level OTIF (customer receipt date, ±0/−2 days) to 95% for modern trade by Q4 FY27; monthly
"Zero attrition in sales" A Reduce voluntary field-sales attrition (annualised, on-roll) from 24% to 18% by March 2027; monthly rolling 12 months
"Grow Instagram followers" R Replace with: e-commerce conversion rate for premium purifiers from 1.8% to 2.4% by Q4; monthly
"Cut freight costs" S, T Reduce outbound freight cost per unit from ₹142 to ₹130 (YTD average) by March 2027 through North re-tender; monthly

Connections

SMART is the quality gate for KPI selection (s01), for target setting and bands (s03) and for entries in the KPI definition sheet (s05). It also applies to actions in the action log (Topic 1 s05) and to corrective actions in commentary (Topic 3 — owner and timeline). It links to the OKR vs KPI discussion: OKR objectives are deliberately qualitative while key results must still be measurable and time-bound.

Practice Questions

Q1. SMART test [L1]

Assess each against SMART and rewrite: (a) "Increase premium mix"; (b) "Maintain good relations with distributors"; (c) "EBITDA margin 15% in FY27" (given LE of 10.9%); (d) "Reduce inventory".

Q2. Achievability evidence [L2]

Shakti's Supply Chain head proposes a FY28 DIO target of 75 days (FY27 LE 80). History: FY24 84, FY25 82, FY26 78; peers range 60–90 days; SKU count is planned to fall 15%; forecast accuracy improving from 68% to 75%. Assess achievability and recommend a target with phasing.

💡 Hint: Combine trend, peer range and the specific drivers that will move DIO before judging the number.

Q3. Relevance challenge [L2]

The HR head wants "training hours per employee" on the MBR scorecard. Argue for or against using SMART, and propose a more relevant alternative KPI if you argue against.
