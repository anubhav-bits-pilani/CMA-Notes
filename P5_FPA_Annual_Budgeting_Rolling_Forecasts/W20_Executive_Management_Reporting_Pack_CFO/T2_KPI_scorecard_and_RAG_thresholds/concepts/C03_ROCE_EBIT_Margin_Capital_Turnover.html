Key concept

ROCE = EBIT Margin × Capital Turnover

Concept: ROCE = EBIT ÷ Capital employed = EBIT margin × Capital turnover

Statement

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{CE}} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{EBIT margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{CE}}}_{\text{Capital turnover}} \]

where EBIT is earnings before interest and tax (operating profit after depreciation and amortisation; whether other income and exceptional items are included is a definition choice), Revenue is revenue from operations, and CE is capital employed, defined either from the financing side as total equity + total borrowings (+ lease liabilities if included) − cash and liquid investments, or from the asset side as net fixed assets (incl. right-of-use assets and CWIP if included) + net working capital. Average CE over the period is preferred; for part-year periods EBIT is annualised.

Explanation

ROCE measures the operating return earned on all the capital — debt and equity — tied up in the business. It is independent of capital structure (unlike ROE) and captures the balance sheet (unlike EBITDA margin), making it the best single financial KPI for capital-intensive businesses and for comparing BUs. A ROCE above the pre-tax cost of capital (for an Indian mid-cap manufacturer roughly 13–15% pre-tax) indicates value creation.

The decomposition tells management where to act: low margin points to pricing, mix and cost; low turnover points to under-utilised capacity, excess inventory or slow collections. Two businesses can have the same ROCE by different routes — a premium brand with high margin and low turnover, a distributor with thin margin and high turnover.

Definition choices to fix in the KPI sheet: pre- or post-tax (pre-tax EBIT is usual for ROCE; post-tax gives ROIC), treatment of leases (if lease liabilities are in CE, EBIT must be post-Ind AS 116, i.e. rent excluded and ROU depreciation included), inclusion of CWIP (assets not yet earning; often excluded to avoid penalising growth capex, but then must be tracked separately), goodwill (include for acquisition accountability), and average vs closing CE.

Illustration

Shakti, YTD October (7 months), ₹ crore: EBIT ₹38.8 crore (EBITDA ₹60.8 crore − D&A ₹22.0 crore); revenue ₹660 crore; average CE ₹470 crore.

Annualised EBIT = 38.8 × 12 ÷ 7 = ₹66.5 crore; annualised revenue = ₹1,131.4 crore.

Component Calculation Value
EBIT margin 66.5 ÷ 1,131.4 5.88%
Capital turnover 1,131.4 ÷ 470 2.41×
ROCE 5.88% × 2.41 14.1%

Target 18%: at the same turnover, EBIT margin would need to be 7.6%; at the same margin, capital turnover would need to be 3.06×, i.e. CE of about ₹370 crore — ₹100 crore less, roughly equivalent to cutting CCC by more than 30 days. Margin recovery is the more realistic lever.

Practice Questions

Q1. ROCE by segment [L2]

Shakti segment data (annualised, ₹ crore): Kitchen — EBIT 38, revenue 518, CE 210; Fans & Lighting — EBIT 22, revenue 405, CE 175; Water Purifiers — EBIT 6.5, revenue 209, CE 85. Compute EBIT margin, capital turnover and ROCE for each and recommend where management attention should go.

Q2. Definition effects [L2]

Shakti's average CE excluding leases is ₹470 crore and EBIT pre-Ind AS 116 is ₹66.5 crore annualised. Lease liabilities average ₹42 crore; annual rent on these leases is ₹12.9 crore; ROU depreciation ₹10.3 crore a year; CWIP averages ₹30 crore. Compute ROCE (a) as defined, (b) including leases consistently, (c) also excluding CWIP from CE. Explain which you would put on the MBR scorecard.

💡 Hint: Including leases changes both EBIT (rent out, ROU depreciation in) and CE.

Q3. Interview: margin or turnover? [L1]

"Your ROCE is 14% against an 18% target. Would you rather fix margin or turnover?" Give a structured 100-word answer using the decomposition.
