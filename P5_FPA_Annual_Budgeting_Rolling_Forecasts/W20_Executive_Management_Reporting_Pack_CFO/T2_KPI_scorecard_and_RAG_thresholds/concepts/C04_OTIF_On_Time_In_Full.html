Key concept

OTIF: On Time In Full

Concept: OTIF = Orders delivered on time and in full ÷ Total orders

Statement

\[ \text{OTIF} = \frac{N_{\text{OT} \cap \text{IF}}}{N_{\text{total}}} \]

where \(N_{\text{total}}\) is the number of orders (or order lines) due for delivery in the period, and \(N_{\text{OT} \cap \text{IF}}\) is the number of those that were delivered both on time (on or before the committed date, within any agreed tolerance window) and in full (100% of the ordered quantity, correct item, often also correct documentation). Each order counts once; partial or late orders count as failures.

Explanation

OTIF measures delivery reliability as the customer experiences it. It is a key internal-process and customer KPI for consumer goods, durables, pharma distribution and B2B manufacturers. Indian modern-trade chains and e-commerce marketplaces increasingly impose OTIF-linked penalties or reduced visibility for suppliers below a threshold, so OTIF has direct P&L effects beyond customer goodwill.

Definition choices that must be fixed (they routinely cause disputes between Sales and Logistics):

  • Unit of measure: order level (strictest — one short line fails the whole order), line level (most common), or unit/value level.
  • Which date: customer-requested date, confirmed/promised date, or dispatch date. Customer-receipt (proof of delivery) is the customer view; dispatch-based OTIF overstates performance by ignoring transit delays.
  • Tolerance: "on time" window (e.g. −2/+0 days; early deliveries may be rejected by modern trade) and "in full" tolerance (e.g. ≥ 98% of quantity for bulk products).
  • Exclusions: customer-caused delays, force majeure, cancelled orders — define narrowly and document.
  • Population: all orders due in the period, not just orders delivered (otherwise undelivered orders vanish from the denominator).

OTIF is the product of on-time and in-full performance only if they are independent; in practice track the two failure modes separately (late but full; on time but short; both) to direct action — short deliveries point to inventory and fill rate; late deliveries point to logistics or production scheduling.

Illustration

Shakti, October 2026, modern-trade and e-commerce order lines due: 48,000.

Outcome Lines
On time and in full 42,240
On time, short-shipped 2,640
Late, full 2,400
Late and short 720
Total 48,000

OTIF = 42,240 ÷ 48,000 = 88.0%. On-time rate = (42,240 + 2,640) ÷ 48,000 = 93.5%; in-full rate = (42,240 + 2,400) ÷ 48,000 = 93.0%. Short shipments (3,360 lines) and late deliveries (3,120 lines) are similar in size, so both the inventory and the transport owner need actions. Against a 95% target, attainment is 92.6% — Red.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute OTIF variants [L1]

November data: 1,200 orders comprising 50,400 lines. Lines on time and in full 46,100; on time but short 2,000; late but full 1,800; late and short 500. Orders with all lines on time and in full: 980. Compute line-level OTIF, order-level OTIF, on-time rate and in-full rate, and explain why order-level OTIF is much lower.

Q2. Penalty economics [L2]

A modern-trade customer buying ₹18 crore a quarter from Shakti charges a penalty of 2% of the value of non-OTIF lines and, below 90% OTIF, cuts shelf space by 15% for the next quarter (contribution margin 18%). Shakti's OTIF to this customer was 87% in Q2 with an average line value equal across lines. Quantify the Q2 penalty and the Q3 contribution at risk, and draft the commentary line for the MBR.

💡 Hint: Separate the direct penalty from the volume consequence, and annualise neither unless asked.

Q3. Gaming the metric [L2]

The logistics team proposes changing OTIF from customer-requested date to Shakti-confirmed date, which would raise October OTIF from 88% to 94%. Evaluate the proposal and recommend how the scorecard should handle both measures.
