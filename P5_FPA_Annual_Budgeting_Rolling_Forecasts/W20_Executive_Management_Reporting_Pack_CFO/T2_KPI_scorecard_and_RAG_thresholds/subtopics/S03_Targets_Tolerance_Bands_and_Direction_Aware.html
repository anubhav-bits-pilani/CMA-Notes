Subtopic note

Targets, Tolerance Bands and Direction-Aware Thresholds

Scope: Target setting and tolerance bands per KPI; direction-aware thresholds (higher-is-better vs lower-is-better)

Why it matters

A RAG colour is only as meaningful as the target and band behind it. Set the band too wide and the scorecard is permanently Green; too narrow and it flickers between colours with random noise; ignore direction and a rising DSO turns Green. Most scorecard credibility problems in Indian corporates trace to this step, not to the formatting. FP&A owns the methodology and must defend it to functional heads whose incentives depend on the colours.

Core notes

Sources of targets

Source Use Caveat
AOP / budget Accountability and incentives Can become outdated mid-year
LE / re-forecast Current expectation; "are we delivering what we now expect?" Moves the goalposts if used for incentives
Prior year / trend Improvement over time Ignores changed conditions
External benchmark Peer comparison (e.g. listed peers' DSO, sector ROCE) Definitions differ between companies
Regulatory / contractual Covenants, SLAs, statutory limits (MSME 45-day payment) Non-negotiable; usually binary
Strategic ambition Multi-year targets (ROCE 18% by FY28) Needs phasing into annual milestones

Targets should be phased monthly for seasonal KPIs (revenue, inventory days) and set at the same granularity as the actual (month, YTD, LE).

Direction-aware thresholds

Every KPI carries a direction flag:

  • Higher-is-better (HIB): revenue growth, GM%, EBITDA %, ROCE, OTIF, fill rate, NPS.
  • Lower-is-better (LIB): DSO, DIO, CCC, cost per unit, attrition, defects, lost-time injuries, Net debt/EBITDA.
  • Target-is-best (range): KPIs where both over- and under-shooting are bad — capex utilisation vs phased plan, inventory within a min–max band, DPO (too low wastes cash, too high damages suppliers and breaches the MSMED 45-day rule).

Attainment is computed direction-aware (c01):

\[ \text{Attainment}_{\text{HIB}} = \frac{A}{T}, \qquad \text{Attainment}_{\text{LIB}} = \frac{T}{A} \]

where \(A\) is actual and \(T\) is target, so that attainment ≥ 100% always means "at or better than target". Alternatively use a signed performance gap expressed in the KPI's own unit:

\[ \text{Gap} = d \times (A - T), \quad d = +1 \text{ (HIB)}, \; d = -1 \text{ (LIB)} \]

which avoids ratio distortions near zero and for percentage KPIs.

Tolerance bands

The band defines how far from target a KPI can be before it turns Amber or Red. Four approaches:

  1. Fixed % of target (default): Green ≥ 100%, Amber 95–< 100%, Red < 95% (c02). Simple, but ignores volatility.
  2. Absolute units: for percentage KPIs, bands in percentage points or bps are more intuitive — GM% Green ≥ target, Amber within 50 bps below, Red > 50 bps below.
  3. Volatility-based: bands set at multiples of the KPI's historical standard deviation of monthly deviations from target, e.g. Amber within 1σ, Red beyond 1σ (or 2σ for very noisy KPIs). Prevents noise from producing Red.
  4. Materiality-based: bands set so that Red corresponds to a ₹ impact that matters (e.g. any deviation worth more than ₹2 crore EBITDA on an annualised basis).

Asymmetric bands and "too good" flags

For HIB KPIs, overshooting is usually fine, but extreme over-performance can signal a data error, a soft target or an unsustainable action (e.g. OTIF 100% with fill rate collapsing because only easy orders were shipped). Some scorecards add a Blue status for "exceeds target by more than X%" to prompt review rather than celebration.

Cumulative vs point-in-time targets

Flow KPIs (revenue, EBITDA) are compared YTD to a YTD-phased target; stock or ratio KPIs (DSO, attrition rate) are compared at a point in time or as rolling averages. Mixing these (e.g. comparing a month's DSO to an annual average target) creates false signals in seasonal businesses.

Governance

  • Targets and bands are proposed by FP&A, agreed with KPI owners and approved by the CFO/CEO at AOP time.
  • Bands are documented in the KPI definition sheet (s05) and not changed mid-year except for errors.
  • If the AOP becomes obsolete, add an LE-based view rather than changing AOP targets (see topic pitfall).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Targets of zero or near zero (injuries, write-offs): ratio attainment is undefined; use count-based rules.
  • Negative targets or actuals (EBITDA loss, negative CCC): ratios flip sign and mislead; use the gap method.
  • Percentage KPIs: 33.5% vs 34% is 98.5% attainment — which looks Amber under a 95% rule even though 50 bps of GM on ₹1,200 crore is ₹6 crore. Use absolute bps bands for margins.
  • Small denominators: a business unit with five customers should not be RAG-rated on NPS.
  • Incentive pressure: owners lobby for wide bands; use volatility data to set them objectively and keep the incentive measure separate from the scorecard colour if needed.

Key takeaways

  • Targets come from AOP, LE, trend, benchmarks, contracts or strategy; state which one each KPI uses.
  • Every KPI needs a direction flag: higher-is-better, lower-is-better or target range.
  • Direction-aware attainment (A/T or T/A) or a signed gap ensures "≥ 100%" or "≥ 0" always means good.
  • Set bands by volatility and materiality, not one universal 95% rule; use bps/pp for percentage KPIs.
  • Handle zero targets, negatives and range KPIs explicitly; govern band changes through the definition sheet.

Practice Questions

Q1. Direction-aware RAG [L1]

Using the default rule (Green ≥ 100%, Amber 95–< 100%, Red < 95% attainment), compute attainment and RAG for: DIO actual 80 days vs target 75; freight per unit ₹142 vs ₹130; revenue growth 4.9% vs 9.7%; OTIF 88% vs 95%; Net debt/EBITDA 1.28× vs ≤ 1.5×.

Q2. Volatility-based bands [L2]

Monthly OTIF deviations from target over the last 12 months (pp): −1.5, +0.8, −2.4, −0.6, +1.2, −3.1, −1.0, +0.4, −2.2, −1.8, +0.5, −0.9. Compute the standard deviation, propose Amber and Red bands at 1σ and 2σ below target 95%, and classify October OTIF of 88% and September OTIF of 93.6%.

💡 Hint: Use the sample standard deviation; think about whether the mean deviation being negative tells you something about the target.

Q3. Percentage KPI trap [L2]

GM% actual 33.6% vs target 34.0%. Under the ratio rule it is Green or Amber? Under a 50 bps absolute band? Quantify the annualised ₹ impact on FY27 revenue of ₹1,170 crore and argue which band is appropriate.

Q4. Range KPI design [L3]

Design direction rules, bands and an Excel formula for DPO (target 60 days, with MSME payables subject to the 45-day limit under the MSMED Act and Sec 43B(h)). Show how you would display both total DPO and MSME DPO on the scorecard, and classify: total DPO 68 days with MSME DPO 52 days.
