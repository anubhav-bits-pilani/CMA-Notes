Balanced Scorecard Perspectives

Scope: Balanced scorecard perspectives: financial, customer, internal process, learning and growth

Why it matters

The balanced scorecard (Kaplan and Norton, 1992) is the most widely used frame for organising a KPI scorecard so that it does not collapse into a list of financial ratios. Its four perspectives force the question: are we building the customer value, processes and capabilities that will produce next year's financial results? In an MBR, organising the scorecard by perspective gives the page a logic that executives recognise, and it exposes gaps — a scorecard with nothing under learning and growth is managing for this quarter only. It also appears in CMA syllabi (strategic performance management), so it bridges your CMA background and FP&A practice.

Core notes

The four perspectives

Perspective Question Typical objectives Typical KPIs
Financial To succeed financially, how should we appear to shareholders? Profitable growth, margin, capital efficiency, cash Revenue growth, EBITDA %, ROCE, FCF, EPS
Customer To achieve our vision, how should we appear to customers? Acquisition, retention, satisfaction, share NPS, market share, customer retention, OTIF (customer view), complaints per 1,000 units
Internal business process What processes must we excel at? Operations, innovation, quality, supply chain Fill rate, production yield, forecast accuracy, cost per unit, new product launch on time, DIO
Learning and growth How will we sustain our ability to change and improve? People, capability, systems, culture Attrition, training hours, engagement score, key-role succession coverage, digital adoption

Some organisations add a fifth perspective (sustainability/ESG — e.g. energy per unit, water intensity, safety incidents), which aligns with SEBI's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) requirements for the top 1,000 listed companies.

Cause-and-effect logic and strategy maps

The perspectives are linked causally from bottom to top: capabilities (learning and growth) enable process excellence, which delivers the customer value proposition, which drives financial outcomes. A strategy map draws these links as arrows between objectives.

Shakti example:

  • Learning and growth: reduce field sales attrition (24% → 18%); train 400 distributor salesmen on premium range.
  • Internal process: improve S&OP forecast accuracy (68% → 80%); raise fill rate (93% → 97%); cut freight cost per unit (₹142 → ₹130).
  • Customer: OTIF 95% to modern trade; NPS 45; premium share in e-commerce.
  • Financial: GM 34%, EBITDA 12.5%, ROCE 18%.

The logic test: if every lower-level KPI hits target, should the financial KPIs follow? If not, a causal link is missing.

Designing the scorecard page by perspective

  1. Group rows by perspective, financial on top (the outcome), then customer, process, learning and growth.
  2. Two to five KPIs per perspective; 12–20 total.
  3. For each KPI show actual, target, attainment, RAG, trend arrow (3-month direction) and owner.
  4. Add a one-line perspective summary ("Customer: 2 of 4 Red — OTIF and NPS driven by e-commerce fulfilment issues").
  5. Link to commentary pages for Red KPIs.

Balanced scorecard vs plain KPI list

A KPI list measures; a balanced scorecard explains. Its value is in: (a) balance between financial and non-financial, short and long term, internal and external; (b) explicit causal links that support root-cause commentary (Topic 3); (c) alignment cascades — corporate scorecard objectives cascade to BU and functional scorecards, so each manager sees their contribution.

Criticisms and practical limits

  • Causality is assumed, not proven: links (training → productivity) may be weak or delayed; test them over time.
  • Weighting: composite scores (e.g. 40% financial, 20% each other perspective) hide Reds; prefer showing each KPI.
  • Rigidity: perspectives can become a form-filling exercise; the strategy must drive the KPIs, not the template.
  • Measurement burden: learning and growth KPIs often lack reliable data; start with few, well-defined measures.

Implementation in Power BI and Excel

Add a Perspective column and sort order to the KPI dimension table; use it as the row group in the matrix. A strategy-map page can be built with shapes and card visuals bound to status measures, so the map's boxes change colour automatically.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • KPIs spanning perspectives: OTIF is a process measure internally but a customer measure externally; place it once, where it is owned and acted on.
  • Non-profit or public-sector units (CSR trusts under Sec 135) may invert the order — mission outcomes on top, financial as a constraint.
  • Incentive linkage: linking bonuses to all four perspectives encourages balance, but subjective measures (engagement) invite disputes; use objective definitions.
  • BRSR/ESG KPIs carry assurance requirements (BRSR Core reasonable assurance for top listed companies, phased in by SEBI); ensure definitions match the BRSR format.

Key takeaways

  • Four perspectives: financial, customer, internal process, learning and growth; often a fifth for ESG.
  • Strategy maps link them causally from capabilities to processes to customers to financial results.
  • Organise the MBR scorecard by perspective with 2–5 KPIs each, RAG, trend and owner.
  • Use the causal links to trace Red financial KPIs to their operational drivers.
  • Beware assumed causality, composite weightings and template-driven KPI choice.

Practice Questions

Q1. Allocate KPIs to perspectives [L1]

Allocate to a perspective and justify: (a) cost per hire; (b) warranty claims per 1,000 units; (c) secondary sales growth; (d) capex utilisation; (e) e-commerce return rate; (f) SAP S/4HANA go-live milestone; (g) Net debt/EBITDA; (h) energy consumption per unit produced.

Q2. Build a strategy map [L2]

Shakti's Water Purifiers strategy is "win in service revenue". Draw (as a table of objectives and arrows) a strategy map with at least two objectives per perspective, and one KPI and target per objective.

💡 Hint: Start from the financial objective and ask what customers must experience, which processes deliver that and which capabilities those processes require.

Q3. Composite score critique [L2]

The CEO proposes a single "Business Health Index" = 40% financial + 20% customer + 20% process + 20% learning, each scored 0–100 from attainment. For October: financial 72, customer 88, process 91, learning 84. Compute the index, explain what it hides, and propose a better summary.

Q4. Cascade [L3]

Cascade Shakti's corporate objective "ROCE 18% by FY28" into a Supply Chain functional scorecard with KPIs in all four perspectives, showing the causal link from each to ROCE.
