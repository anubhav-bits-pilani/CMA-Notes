KPI Definition Sheet: Formula, Source, Owner, Frequency

Scope: KPI definition sheet: formula, source, owner, frequency

Why it matters

Every argument in an MBR about whether a KPI is "really" Red is ultimately an argument about definition. The KPI definition sheet (also called a KPI dictionary or metadata register) settles those arguments in advance: it fixes the formula, the data source, the accountable owner and the reporting frequency for each KPI, and records any change. It is the foundation of a single source of KPI truth, a prerequisite for automation in Power BI, and a document that internal auditors and new joiners rely on. In interviews, being able to produce a crisp definition row for OTIF or attrition is a strong signal of practical experience.

The four bolded fields — formula, source, owner, frequency — are the minimum; without any of them the KPI is not reportable.

Formula — precision rules

  • State numerator and denominator separately, each with inclusions and exclusions.
  • Specify the time basis (point in time, month, YTD, rolling 3 or 12 months) and annualisation method.
  • Specify the aggregation rule: ratio KPIs are recomputed from aggregated components, never averaged across units (an average of plant OTIFs is not the company OTIF).
  • Provide a worked example in the sheet with numbers, so any reader can reproduce the calculation.
  • Align with statutory definitions where they exist (e.g. Schedule III "trade receivables", Ind AS 115 "revenue from contracts with customers") and note deliberate differences.

Source — data lineage

Record the system (SAP, Salesforce, HRMS such as SuccessFactors or Darwinbox, TMS/WMS), the specific table or report, the extraction method (Power Query from SQL view, scheduled export), the refresh time and the reconciliation control (e.g. "AR sub-ledger total reconciles to GL control account 2100 monthly"). Where data passes through Excel manual steps, note them — they are control risks.

Owner — one name, with levers

The owner is the person who can change the result and who answers for it in the MBR — not the person who produces the number. Separate the KPI owner (Head of Supply Chain for OTIF) from the data steward (logistics MIS analyst) and the reporter (FP&A). One owner per KPI; shared ownership dilutes accountability.

Frequency — measurement vs review

Distinguish measurement frequency (OTIF measured daily), reporting frequency (weekly flash, monthly MBR) and target-setting frequency (annual AOP, quarterly LE). Some KPIs are meaningful only quarterly (NPS from a quarterly survey, ROCE on quarter-end balance sheets); show them with the latest available value and the as-of date rather than forcing monthly numbers.

Governance and maintenance

  • The sheet is owned by FP&A, approved by the CFO and functional heads at AOP time, and published (SharePoint list or a Power BI "definitions" page) for everyone.
  • Changes require a change request, approval, an effective date and restatement of comparatives; the version number appears in the scorecard footer.
  • Annual review: remove KPIs no longer used, add new ones linked to strategy.
  • In Power BI, the KPI dimension table (direction, thresholds, owner, unit) is loaded from the same sheet so that the scorecard and the definitions can never diverge.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Same name, different KPIs: "margin" can mean gross, contribution or EBITDA margin; name precisely and avoid unqualified terms.
  • Definitions differing from investor communication: if the MBR "EBITDA" differs from the investor-presentation EBITDA, rename or reconcile.
  • Third-party data (Nielsen market share, rating agency metrics) have their own definitions and lags; document them and do not RAG on data older than one period.
  • Mid-year definition change forced by system migration (e.g. SAP ECC to S/4HANA): run parallel for at least one month and show both.
  • Personal data (attrition by individual, salary-related KPIs) must respect the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023; aggregate appropriately.

Key takeaways

  • Every KPI needs at minimum: formula, source, owner and frequency; a full row adds inclusions, direction, target, thresholds and change log.
  • Write numerator and denominator separately with inclusions/exclusions and an aggregation rule.
  • Document data lineage and reconciliation controls for each source.
  • One accountable owner per KPI, distinct from data steward and reporter.
  • Version-control the sheet and drive Power BI thresholds from it to keep a single source of KPI truth.

Practice Questions

Q1. Complete definition rows [L1]

Write complete definition rows (all fields in the required-fields table) for: fill rate (line), DIO, freight cost per unit and capex utilisation % for Shakti.

Q2. Resolve an attrition dispute [L2]

HR reports attrition of 14% (voluntary, on-roll, annualised on closing headcount); the Sales head says it is 31% in the field sales force. Data: average on-roll headcount 2,900 (closing 3,050); voluntary leavers YTD (7 months) 230; involuntary 60; contract field staff average 800 with 180 leavers. Compute attrition on at least three definitions and draft the definition row and any supplementary KPI you would add.

💡 Hint: Check the denominator (average vs closing) and the population (on-roll vs contract) separately.

Q3. Source reconciliation control [L2]

OTIF is calculated in Power BI from TMS proof-of-delivery data. October order lines: SAP SD 48,000 due; TMS shows 46,900 lines with POD. Describe the reconciliation control, how to treat the 1,100 lines, and how the definition sheet should document the treatment.

Q4. Interview: why a definition sheet? [L1]

In under 150 words, explain to a non-finance interviewer why a KPI definition sheet matters, using a real-looking example from a consumer durables business.
