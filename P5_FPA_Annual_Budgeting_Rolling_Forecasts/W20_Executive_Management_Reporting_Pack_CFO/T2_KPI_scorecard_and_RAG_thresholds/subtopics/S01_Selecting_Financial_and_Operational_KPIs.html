Subtopic note

Selecting Financial and Operational KPIs

Scope: KPI selection: financial (revenue growth, EBITDA %, ROCE) and operational (OTIF, fill rate, attrition, NPS)

Why it matters

The value of a scorecard is decided before any colour is applied — in choosing which 12–20 measures earn a place on it. Financial KPIs tell management whether strategy is producing results; operational KPIs tell them whether results will continue. FP&A is usually asked to propose the KPI set for the MBR scorecard, and is accountable when it misses the issue that matters (a scorecard of financial ratios that is Green while OTIF collapses will be Red next quarter).

Core notes

Selection criteria

A KPI earns a place on the MBR scorecard only if it is:

  1. Strategically relevant — directly linked to a strategic objective or a value driver of the business model.
  2. Actionable — a named owner can influence it with identifiable levers.
  3. Measurable reliably and on time — available by working day 5–8 from a trusted source.
  4. Balanced — together with the others, covers financial outcomes and operational drivers, short and long term.
  5. Not redundant — does not duplicate another KPI (GM% and COGS % of revenue are the same thing).
  6. SMART (c05) — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound target.

A typical mix: 40–50% financial outcome KPIs, 50–60% operational/driver KPIs.

Financial KPIs

Revenue growth — measures top-line momentum.

\[ \text{Revenue growth} = \frac{\text{Revenue}_{t}}{\text{Revenue}_{t-1}} - 1 \]

Report YTD vs PY and, for comparability, organic growth (excluding acquisitions) and, for exporters, constant-currency growth. Decompose into volume and price/mix for insight. Shakti YTD: 660 ÷ 629 − 1 = 4.9% (target 9.7%).

EBITDA % — measures operating profitability before capital structure and depreciation policy.

\[ \text{EBITDA \%} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Revenue from operations}} \]

Define pre- or post-Ind AS 116 and treatment of other income and exceptional items (see Topic 1 appendix). Shakti YTD 9.2% (target 12.1%); report the variance in bps.

ROCE — measures return on all capital employed (c03).

\[ \text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Capital employed}} \]

where capital employed = total equity + total debt (± leases) − cash, or equivalently fixed assets + NWC. ROCE connects the P&L to the balance sheet and is the best single financial KPI for capital-intensive businesses; it captures working capital and capex discipline that EBITDA ignores.

Other common financial KPIs: gross margin %, opex % of revenue, FCF and FCF conversion, DSO/DIO/CCC, Net debt/EBITDA, EPS growth, revenue per employee.

Operational KPIs

OTIF (on time in full) — the share of orders (or order lines) delivered by the committed date and in the full quantity (c04). Measures supply reliability; leading indicator of customer satisfaction and revenue retention, especially in modern trade where retailers levy penalties for OTIF misses.

Fill rate — the proportion of demand met from available stock:

\[ \text{Fill rate (unit)} = \frac{\text{Units shipped against orders}}{\text{Units ordered}} \]

Variants: unit fill rate, line fill rate (lines filled completely ÷ total lines), order fill rate (orders filled completely). Fill rate measures inventory availability; OTIF adds the timeliness dimension. Low fill rate with high DIO signals wrong mix of inventory.

Attrition — employee turnover.

\[ \text{Attrition (annualised)} = \frac{\text{Leavers in period}}{\text{Average headcount}} \times \frac{12}{\text{months in period}} \]

Specify voluntary vs total, and inclusion of contract staff and trainees. High attrition raises hiring and training cost and reduces productivity; it is a leading indicator for service quality and sales capacity (sales force attrition in Indian FMCG and durables is often 25–40%).

NPS (Net Promoter Score) — customer loyalty from the question "How likely are you to recommend us (0–10)?":

\[ \text{NPS} = \%\text{Promoters (9–10)} - \%\text{Detractors (0–6)} \]

ranging from −100 to +100. It is a survey-based leading indicator of repeat purchase and word-of-mouth; sample size and survey method must be stable for trends to mean anything.

Building the set — illustration for Shakti

Strategic objective Financial KPI Operational KPI(s)
Profitable growth Revenue growth %, EBITDA % Premium mix %, distribution reach (outlets)
Margin recovery GM % Price realisation per unit, freight cost per unit
Capital efficiency ROCE, CCC DIO by category, capex utilisation %
Customer service — OTIF, fill rate, NPS
People Employee cost % of revenue Attrition %, sales productivity (₹ per salesperson)

This yields 15 KPIs — within the 12–20 range. Each is assigned an owner: e.g. OTIF to Head of Supply Chain, attrition to CHRO, ROCE to CFO.

Sector variation

The operational half of the scorecard is sector-specific: for an NBFC — disbursements, collection efficiency, GNPA/NNPA, cost of funds; for SaaS — ARR, net revenue retention, CAC payback; for a hospital chain — ARPOB (average revenue per occupied bed) and occupancy; for retail — same-store sales growth and revenue per sq ft. The financial half is more universal.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ratio KPIs can improve for bad reasons: EBITDA % rises when low-margin volume is lost; ROCE rises when capex is deferred. Pair ratios with absolute measures (EBITDA ₹, revenue ₹).
  • Survey KPIs (NPS) move with sample composition; footnote sample size and avoid RAG on very small samples.
  • Attrition denominator: using closing headcount in a growing company understates attrition; use average.
  • Fill rate vs OTIF trade-off: shipping partial orders improves timeliness but lowers fill rate; define which the customer values.
  • Acquired businesses distort growth and ROCE; show organic metrics or annotate.

Key takeaways

  • Select KPIs on relevance, actionability, measurability, balance and non-redundancy; 12–20 on the MBR scorecard.
  • Core financial KPIs: revenue growth, EBITDA %, ROCE, complemented by GM%, CCC and FCF.
  • Core operational KPIs: OTIF, fill rate, attrition and NPS, each with precise definitions.
  • Map each KPI to a strategic objective and a named owner.
  • Pair ratio KPIs with absolute measures to avoid improvement for the wrong reasons.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute core KPIs [L1]

Shakti YTD October: revenue ₹660 crore (PY ₹629 crore); EBITDA ₹60.8 crore; EBIT ₹38.8 crore (7 months); average capital employed ₹470 crore; leavers 410 against average headcount 2,900; NPS survey of 1,200 customers: 610 promoters, 380 passives, 210 detractors; units ordered 2.15 million, units shipped against orders 2.0 million. Compute revenue growth, EBITDA %, annualised ROCE, annualised attrition, NPS and unit fill rate.

Q2. Prune a scorecard [L2]

Shakti's draft scorecard has 28 KPIs, including: revenue, revenue growth, revenue vs budget, GM%, COGS % of revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA %, PAT, EPS, ROCE, ROE, DSO, DIO, DPO, CCC, OTIF, fill rate (unit), fill rate (line), NPS, number of customer complaints, website visits, social media followers, attrition, number of trainings, training hours per employee, headcount, freight per unit, capex spend. Reduce it to 15 and justify each removal.

💡 Hint: Remove redundant pairs, pure vanity measures and KPIs no MBR attendee owns.

Q3. Ratio trap [L2]

Water Purifiers' EBITDA margin improved from 6.0% to 7.5% in October, while revenue fell from ₹21 crore budget to ₹18 crore actual. Explain how this can happen, what complementary KPIs you would show, and how you would comment on it in the MBR.

Q4. Sector scorecard [L3]

You join the FP&A team of an NSE-listed hospital chain with 12 hospitals. Propose a 14-KPI MBR scorecard (financial and operational), with a definition line, direction and owner for each.
