Roadmap topic: KPI Scorecard & Traffic-Light Thresholds: Designing operational + financial KPIs with automated RAG (Red/Amber/Green) alerts

Overview

A KPI scorecard is a one-page grid of the 10–20 measures that best indicate whether the business is on track, each with actual, target, attainment and a Red/Amber/Green (RAG) status. In the MBR it usually sits right after the executive summary: the summary tells the story, the scorecard shows at a glance which parts of the business are off track. Done well, it focuses management attention on exceptions. Done badly — 60 metrics, identical thresholds, colours typed by hand — it creates noise, disputes and false comfort.

For FP&A roles the scorecard is a design problem as much as a reporting one. You must select a balanced set of financial and operational KPIs, link them to strategy through leading and lagging indicators, set targets and tolerance bands that respect each KPI's direction and volatility, automate the RAG logic in Excel and Power BI so colours cannot be manipulated, and document every KPI in a definition sheet so that Sales, Supply Chain and Finance agree on what the number means. The balanced scorecard of Kaplan and Norton provides the organising frame.

The learning sequence: start with KPI selection (s01) and the SMART test (c05), with ROCE (c03) and OTIF (c04) as worked examples of a financial and an operational KPI. Then build KPI trees and the leading/lagging distinction (s02). Next set targets and direction-aware tolerance bands (s03), using attainment % (c01) and the example RAG rule (c02). Automate RAG in Excel and Power BI (s04). Document each KPI in a definition sheet (s05). Finally organise the set into balanced scorecard perspectives (s06).

The running case remains Shakti Home Appliances Ltd, October 2026. Its scorecard will mix financial KPIs (revenue growth, GM%, EBITDA %, ROCE, DSO, CCC) with operational ones (OTIF, fill rate, attrition, NPS, freight cost per unit, capex utilisation).

Kaplan & Norton balanced scorecard and strategy maps

Kaplan & Norton balanced scorecard and strategy maps

Robert Kaplan and David Norton introduced the balanced scorecard in 1992 to counter over-reliance on lagging financial measures. It organises measures into four perspectives — financial, customer, internal business process, and learning and growth — and requires each to be derived from strategy. Their later strategy maps show cause-and-effect links: capabilities (learning and growth) improve processes, which deliver customer value, which produces financial results. In an FP&A scorecard this becomes the structure of the page (s06) and the logic of the KPI tree (s02). The key lesson: a scorecard is a strategy-execution tool, not just a list of metrics.

OKRs vs KPIs in corporate settings

OKRs vs KPIs in corporate settings

Objectives and Key Results (OKRs), popularised by Intel and Google, set qualitative objectives with two to five measurable, ambitious key results per quarter; they are about change and stretch, and achieving 60–70% is often considered success. KPIs measure ongoing health and performance against targets, where 100% is expected. In Indian corporates, OKRs are common in tech, start-ups and GCCs; conglomerates and manufacturers rely on KPIs linked to the AOP and incentive plans. The two can coexist: KPIs on the MBR scorecard track business-as-usual; OKRs track transformation initiatives (e.g. "Reduce cost-to-serve in e-commerce" with key results on returns rate and delivery cost per order). Do not apply KPI-style RAG to stretch OKRs.

Data quality and single source of KPI truth

Data quality and single source of KPI truth

A KPI is only as trusted as its data. Disputes such as "Sales says OTIF is 94%, Logistics says 88%" usually come from different sources, cut-offs or definitions. A single source of truth means each KPI is calculated once, from a governed dataset (data warehouse or Power BI semantic model), using the definition sheet, and every report consumes that calculation. Data quality dimensions to monitor: completeness, accuracy, timeliness, consistency and validity. Practical controls: source reconciliations (e.g. order lines in the TMS vs SAP SD), automated exception reports, owner sign-off before the MBR, and a data-quality flag on the scorecard for KPIs with known issues.

Common pitfalls

Too many KPIs (vanity metrics) without owners or actions

Scorecards grow because every function wants its metrics on the CEO's page, and removing a KPI feels like a loss of status. The result is 50+ metrics, many of them vanity measures (website hits, number of trainings conducted) that look good but drive no decision. Correct treatment: limit the MBR scorecard to 12–20 KPIs, each with one named owner and a defined action when it turns Red; move others to functional dashboards. Detect in review: for each KPI ask "who owns it, and what did we do last time it was Red?" If no one can answer, remove it.

Same RAG thresholds for all KPIs regardless of volatility or direction

Applying "Green ≥ 100%, Amber 95–100%, Red < 95%" to every KPI ignores that a 5% miss on EBITDA margin is serious while a 5% swing in monthly NPS is noise, and that for DSO or attrition a higher number is worse. People do it because one rule is easy to implement. Correct treatment: store a direction flag and KPI-specific bands derived from volatility and materiality (e.g. bands of ±1 standard deviation, or tighter for safety KPIs). Detect: check whether any lower-is-better KPI is Green when above target, or whether a KPI flips colour every month without real change.

RAG based on budget only when budget is outdated; consider LE-based targets

By Month 7, a budget set in February may be irrelevant — a commodity shock or a lost customer makes every KPI Red against AOP, and the scorecard stops discriminating. People keep the budget because it is the incentive baseline. Correct treatment: show RAG against AOP for accountability and against LE (or a re-forecast target) for current performance, or use trend RAG (improving/deteriorating). Detect: if more than 70% of KPIs are Red for three consecutive months against an obsolete budget, the scorecard has lost information value.

KPIs without definitions, causing disputes between departments

Without a definition sheet, each function calculates "fill rate" or "attrition" its own way — order-line vs unit fill, voluntary vs total attrition, annualised vs monthly — and the MBR becomes a debate about numbers instead of actions. Correct treatment: a KPI definition sheet with formula, inclusions/exclusions, source, owner, frequency and change log, approved by the CFO and functional heads, and a single calculation in the data model. Detect: two reports showing different values for the same KPI and period.

Practice Questions

Q1. Design Shakti's scorecard [L3]

Shakti's FY27 strategy is: grow premium appliances, recover gross margin to 34%, reduce working capital, and improve service in e-commerce. October data: revenue growth YTD 4.9% (target 9.7%); GM 32.4% (target 34.0%); EBITDA 9.2% (target 12.1%); ROCE 14.1% (target 18%); DSO 68 days (target 60); CCC 91 days (target 80); OTIF 88% (target 95%); fill rate 93% (target 97%); attrition 24% (target 18%); NPS 41 (target 45); premium mix 31% (target 35%); freight per unit ₹142 (target ₹130). (a) Organise the KPIs into balanced scorecard perspectives and mark each leading or lagging. (b) Set direction and RAG bands for each, justifying two that differ from the default. (c) Compute attainment and RAG. (d) Write the scorecard's three-line summary for the MBR.

Q2. Definition dispute and single source [L2]

Logistics reports October OTIF of 94% (dispatch-date based, order-level); Sales reports 82% (customer-receipt date, line-level). Total order lines 48,000; lines dispatched on time 45,600; lines received by customers on time and in full 39,400; lines received on time but short 2,100. (a) Compute OTIF on each basis. (b) Recommend the definition for the MBR and draft its definition-sheet row. (c) Propose the data-quality controls to prevent recurrence.

💡 Hint: Separate the "on time" test from the "in full" test, and be clear which event (dispatch or receipt) defines on time.

Q3. LE-based RAG [L2]

Against AOP, 10 of Shakti's 14 KPIs are Red in October. Against the October LE (which reflects copper inflation and the e-commerce slowdown), 4 are Red. (a) Explain why both views are needed. (b) Design a scorecard layout showing both without clutter. (c) State which view drives incentives and which drives actions.

Q4. Power BI automation [L3]

Design the data model and DAX for a Power BI scorecard with a KPI dimension table (KPI name, direction, green threshold, amber threshold, unit, owner) and a fact table (KPI, month, actual, target). Write measures for attainment and status, and describe the conditional formatting setup. Include how you would handle KPIs where the target is zero (e.g. lost-time injuries).
