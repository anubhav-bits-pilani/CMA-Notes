Key concept

Customer Profitability After Cost-to-Serve

Concept: Customer profitability = Net revenue − COGS − Cost-to-serve (freight, discounts, service)

Statement

\[ \pi_j = R_j - \text{COGS}_j - \text{CTS}_j \]

where \(\pi_j\) is profit from customer \(j\), \(R_j\) is net revenue (after on-invoice discounts and returns), \(\text{COGS}_j\) is the standard or actual product cost of the mix sold to that customer, and \(\text{CTS}_j\) is cost-to-serve: freight, off-invoice discounts and schemes not already netted in revenue, order processing, warehousing, merchandising, customer service, returns handling, and the financing cost of the customer's credit days.

Explanation

Gross margin by customer reflects only price and product mix. Most of the difference between customers sits in cost-to-serve: a modern-trade chain that demands appointment deliveries, small drops, merchandisers and 60-day credit can be loss-making while showing a healthy gross margin. Cost-to-serve is assigned with activity-based costing: identify activities, pool their costs, choose a driver for each, compute a driver rate and charge each customer by its consumption.

Activity Driver Example rate
Order processing Number of orders ₹350 per order
Picking and dispatch Number of order lines ₹40 per line
Secondary freight Drops × distance band ₹1,800 per drop (local)
Merchandising Store visits ₹600 per visit
Returns handling Return lines ₹250 per line
Credit cost Average receivable × cost of debt 10% per year

Avoid double counting: if schemes are already deducted in net revenue (as Ind AS 115 requires for most trade promotions), do not charge them again as cost-to-serve. Show both contribution after variable cost-to-serve and fully allocated profit — fixed costs such as the depot lease do not disappear when one customer leaves.

Practice Questions

Q1. Driver-based cost-to-serve [L2]

A customer buys ₹4.5 crore a year at 30% gross margin, places 320 orders with 2,400 lines, receives 280 drops, returns 150 lines and has average receivables of ₹55 lakh. Using the driver rates in the table above (local drop rate), compute customer profit and profit %.

Q2. Double counting check [L1]

A cost-to-serve model charges "trade schemes ₹1.2 crore" to a key account whose revenue is already reported net of those schemes under Ind AS 115. Explain the error and its effect on the customer ranking.

Q3. Exit decision [L3]

A customer shows fully allocated loss of ₹40 lakh but positive contribution after variable cost-to-serve of ₹25 lakh. Fixed depot costs of ₹65 lakh are allocated to it. Advise the sales head on whether to exit, reprice or change the service model, and what further data you need.

💡 Hint: Ask which allocated costs would actually disappear if the customer left.
