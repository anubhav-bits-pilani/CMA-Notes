Key concept

Customer Acquisition Cost and CAC Payback Period

Concept: CAC = Sales and marketing spend ÷ New customers acquired; CAC payback (months) = CAC ÷ (ARPU × GM%)

Statement

\[ \text{CAC} = \frac{S\&M}{N_{\text{new}}} \]

\[ \text{CAC payback (months)} = \frac{\text{CAC}}{\text{ARPU} \times \text{GM\%}} \]

where \(S\&M\) is sales and marketing spend attributable to acquisition in the period, \(N_{\text{new}}\) is the number of new customers acquired in the same period, ARPU is average revenue per user per month, and GM% is the gross margin percentage on that revenue (revenue less direct cost of serving the customer).

Explanation

CAC tells a sales or growth head what it costs to win one customer; payback tells the CFO how long that cash is at risk before the customer's gross profit repays it. The numerator should include all acquisition-related spend: performance and brand marketing, sales team salaries and incentives, agency/partner commissions, onboarding offers, and sales tools. Retention and account-management cost belong elsewhere. Two definitions are common and should be stated:

  • Paid CAC — only paid channels divided by customers from those channels.
  • Blended (fully loaded) CAC — all S&M divided by all new customers, including organic.

Blended CAC flatters performance if organic acquisition is large; paid CAC by channel shows where marginal spend is going. Timing matters: spend in month t may acquire customers in months t+1 to t+3 (long B2B sales cycles), so many companies lag the numerator by one quarter.

Payback uses gross margin, not revenue, because revenue is not cash available to repay acquisition cost. If churn is high, customers may leave before payback; compare payback with average customer lifetime \(1/\text{churn}\). Typical benchmarks: under 12 months for SMB/consumer subscriptions, 12–24 months for enterprise SaaS.

Accounting link: under Ind AS 115.91–94 incremental costs of obtaining a contract (sales commissions payable only on winning the contract) are capitalised and amortised if recoverable, using a practical expedient to expense them when the amortisation period is one year or less. A company capitalising commissions reports lower S&M expense than cash spent, so compute CAC on a cash or gross-spend basis for comparability.

Illustration

An Indian edtech subscription business spent ₹9.6 crore on sales and marketing in Q1 and acquired 32,000 new paying subscribers. ARPU is ₹1,250 per month and gross margin is 64%.

\[ \text{CAC} = \frac{9{,}60{,}00{,}000}{32{,}000} = 3{,}000 \text{ per customer} \]

\[ \text{Payback} = \frac{3{,}000}{1{,}250 \times 0.64} = 3.75 \text{ months} \]

By channel, performance marketing CAC was ₹4,800 (payback 6 months) and referral CAC ₹900 (payback about 1.1 months) — the partnering point is to shift budget toward referrals until their marginal CAC rises.

Practice Questions

Q1. Blended vs paid CAC [L1]

A D2C brand spent ₹2.4 crore on paid digital, ₹0.6 crore on influencers and ₹0.9 crore on sales salaries in a quarter. It acquired 48,000 customers, of whom 30,000 came from paid digital and influencers. Compute blended CAC and paid CAC, and explain which should guide next quarter's marketing budget.

Q2. Payback vs lifetime [L2]

A B2B SaaS company has CAC ₹1.8 lakh, ARPU ₹12,000 per month, gross margin 75% and monthly churn 2.5%. Compute CAC payback and expected customer lifetime, and comment on whether the unit economics are sustainable.

💡 Hint: Compare payback months with the average lifetime implied by the churn rate.

Q3. Commission capitalisation effect [L2]

Sales commissions of ₹1.2 crore paid on new three-year contracts are capitalised under Ind AS 115 and amortised over three years. Reported S&M expense for the year is ₹4.0 crore including ₹0.4 crore of commission amortisation. Explain how you would adjust the numerator for CAC and why.
