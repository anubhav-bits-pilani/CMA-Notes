Key concept

Customer Lifetime Value and the LTV/CAC Benchmark

Concept: LTV = ARPU × Gross margin % ÷ Churn rate (per period); LTV/CAC ≥ 3 benchmark

Statement

\[ \text{LTV} = \frac{\text{ARPU} \times \text{GM\%}}{c} \]

\[ \text{LTV/CAC} = \frac{\text{LTV}}{\text{CAC}} \geq 3 \ \text{(benchmark)} \]

where ARPU is average revenue per user per period, GM% is gross margin percentage, and \(c\) is the churn rate per the same period (monthly ARPU with monthly churn, or annual with annual). \(1/c\) is the expected customer lifetime in periods.

Explanation

The formula is the value of a perpetuity of gross profit that decays at the churn rate. It assumes constant ARPU, margin and churn, and ignores discounting. A discounted version, with discount rate \(r\) per period and retention \(1-c\), is:

\[ \text{LTV}_{\text{disc}} = \text{ARPU} \times \text{GM\%} \times \frac{1}{c + r} \]

(this assumes margin is received at the end of each period and churn removes customers from the second period onward). Use the discounted version when lifetimes are long or the cost of capital is high.

The LTV/CAC ratio compares value created by a customer with the cost of acquiring one. A ratio below 1 destroys value; around 3 is the commonly quoted healthy benchmark; much above 5 can mean the company is under-investing in growth. The benchmark is a heuristic from venture investing, not a rule; mature, low-churn businesses can run lower ratios, while high-risk ones need higher.

Key judgement points:

  • Churn definition: logo churn (customers lost) versus revenue churn; net revenue retention above 100% (expansion exceeding churn) makes the simple formula understate LTV.
  • Cohort behaviour: churn is usually higher in the first months; use cohort curves rather than a blended rate.
  • Gross margin scope: include payment gateway fees, hosting, customer support and fulfilment costs.
  • Segment and channel: compute LTV/CAC by acquisition channel and customer segment; averages hide channels with ratios below 1.

Illustration

A broadband operator in Pune has ARPU ₹700 per month, gross margin 55%, monthly churn 2%, and CAC ₹4,500 (including installation subsidy).

\[ \text{LTV} = \frac{700 \times 0.55}{0.02} = 19{,}250 \]

\[ \text{LTV/CAC} = \frac{19{,}250}{4{,}500} \approx 4.3 \]

With a 1.2% monthly cost of capital, discounted LTV is \(385 / (0.02 + 0.012) \approx 12{,}031\) and LTV/CAC falls to about 2.7 — below the 3x benchmark. The partnering message to the sales head: the channel is viable only if churn is reduced or the installation subsidy trimmed.

Practice Questions

Q1. LTV and ratio [L1]

An OTT platform has ARPU ₹199 per month, gross margin 40% and monthly churn 5%. CAC is ₹950. Compute LTV and LTV/CAC and state whether the benchmark is met.

Q2. Period mismatch error [L2]

A colleague computes LTV for an insurance-distribution app using annual ARPU of ₹2,400, gross margin 60% and a monthly churn of 3%, and reports LTV of ₹48,000. Identify the error, recompute correctly, and explain how you would prevent it in the model.

💡 Hint: Express ARPU and churn over the same period before dividing.

Q3. Channel comparison [L3]

Two acquisition channels for a fintech: field agents (CAC ₹2,200, monthly churn 1.5%) and digital ads (CAC ₹800, monthly churn 6%). ARPU ₹300 per month, gross margin 50%, monthly discount rate 1%. Compute simple and discounted LTV/CAC for each and recommend how the marketing budget should be split.
