Key concept

Working Capital Release from Reducing Days

Concept: Working capital release = ΔDays × (Annual revenue or COGS ÷ 365)

Statement

\[ \text{WC release} = \Delta D \times \frac{B}{365} \]

where \(\Delta D\) is the reduction in days (DSO, DIO or DPO increase) and \(B\) is the annual base for that metric: revenue (credit sales, ideally gross of GST if receivables include GST) for DSO, and COGS (or purchases) for DIO and DPO. \(B/365\) is "one day's worth" of the base.

Explanation

Each working capital day is a fixed amount of cash: if annual credit sales are ₹730 crore, each day of DSO ties up ₹2 crore. Translating days into rupees is the single most useful partnering step, because days mean little to an operating head while ₹ crore can be compared with a discount, a freight saving or a capex project.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Consistent base: use the same base as the days definition. If DIO is computed on COGS, the release must use COGS; using revenue overstates inventory release by the gross margin.
  • GST: receivables are invoiced inclusive of GST while revenue excludes it, so DSO computed on ex-GST revenue is overstated; either gross up the base (for example by 18%) or state the convention in the KPI sheet.
  • Forward-looking base: for a forecast, use next year's projected revenue/COGS, since release happens against future activity.
  • Payables: an increase in DPO "releases" cash too, but check supplier impact and MSME rules — payment beyond 45 days to registered micro and small enterprises triggers interest under the MSMED Act and disallowance under Sec 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act until paid.
  • One-time vs recurring: the release is a one-off cash inflow; the recurring benefit is the interest saved (release × cost of debt) or the return on redeployed cash.
  • Growth offsets: if revenue grows, absolute working capital may still rise even as days fall; present both "days effect" and "growth effect".

Illustration

A specialty chemicals company: revenue ₹1,460 crore, COGS ₹1,022 crore, cost of debt 9%. Targets: DSO 68 → 58 days; DIO 75 → 65 days; DPO 55 → 60 days.

Lever ΔDays Base ÷ 365 (₹ crore/day) Release (₹ crore)
DSO 10 4.0 40.0
DIO 10 2.8 28.0
DPO 5 2.8 14.0
Total — — 82.0

Annual interest saving \(= 82 \times 9\% = 7.38\) crore — about 50 bps of EBITDA-equivalent margin on revenue, which makes the case comparable with margin initiatives.

Practice Questions

Q1. Simple release [L1]

An FMCG company has annual COGS of ₹2,920 crore. Finished-goods inventory days fall from 34 to 28. Compute the cash release and the annual interest saving at 8.5%.

Q2. Wrong base [L2]

An analyst computed inventory release using revenue of ₹900 crore instead of COGS of ₹585 crore for a 12-day DIO reduction. Compute the overstatement and explain how a model check could catch it.

Q3. Growth vs days [L2]

Revenue grows from ₹500 crore to ₹620 crore next year while DSO falls from 72 to 60 days. Compute opening and closing receivables, and split the change into a growth effect and a days effect for the MBR cash page.

💡 Hint: Hold days constant to isolate growth, then hold revenue constant to isolate days.
