Subtopic note

Joint Planning Forums: S&OP, Pricing Committee, Capex Committee

Scope: Joint planning forums: S&OP, pricing committee, capex committee

Why it matters

Business partnering scales through forums, not only through one-on-one conversations. Decisions that cut across functions — how much to produce, what price to charge, which projects to fund — need a recurring meeting with a fixed agenda, the right decision-makers and a single set of numbers. FP&A's role in each forum is to provide the financial view of the options, keep the plan reconciled to the latest estimate (LE), and record decisions so they flow into the next forecast. Done well, these forums are where the rolling forecast is actually made; done badly, they become status meetings that finance attends to take notes.

Core notes

Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP)

S&OP is a monthly cross-functional process that balances demand and supply and links them to the financial plan. Mature companies run it as Integrated Business Planning (IBP), where the volume plan is translated into a financial plan every cycle.

Typical monthly cycle (working days relative to month-end close):

Step Timing Owner Output
Data gathering WD 1–3 Demand planning Actual sales, forecast accuracy, statistical baseline
Demand review WD 4–6 Sales / marketing Consensus unconstrained demand plan (volume, mix, promotions)
Supply review WD 7–9 Supply chain / manufacturing Constrained supply plan, capacity, inventory projections
Pre-S&OP (reconciliation) WD 10–11 Planning + FP&A Gaps, scenarios, financial translation (revenue, GM, EBITDA, cash)
Executive S&OP WD 12–13 CEO / BU head, CFO Decisions on gaps, approved plan, updates to LE

FP&A contributions:

  • Financial translation of the consensus volume plan: revenue at expected realisation, contribution, EBITDA, inventory and working capital — compared with AOP and last LE.
  • Gap-to-plan view: "the consensus demand is 4% below AOP; at current mix that is ₹38 crore revenue and ₹9 crore EBITDA; these are the three gap-closing options".
  • Scenario costing: overtime vs subcontracting, building inventory ahead of a shutdown, air vs sea freight.
  • KPIs: forecast accuracy (1 − MAPE), bias, inventory days vs norm, OTIF, capacity utilisation.
  • Decision log: every executive S&OP decision recorded with owner and incorporated into the next rolling forecast.

Forecast accuracy formula commonly used:

\[ \text{MAPE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\lvert A_i - F_i \rvert}{A_i}, \qquad \text{Accuracy} = 1 - \text{MAPE} \]

where \(A_i\) is actual and \(F_i\) the forecast for SKU or segment \(i\). Weighted MAPE (weights by volume or value) is preferred so that small SKUs do not dominate. Bias \(= \sum (F_i - A_i) / \sum A_i\) shows systematic over- or under-forecasting — often more useful to a sales head than accuracy.

Pricing committee

A pricing committee approves list price changes, discount structures, scheme budgets and deviations beyond the authority matrix. Typical members: CEO or BU head (chair), sales, marketing, finance, supply chain (for capacity), and legal for contract terms.

FP&A role:

  • Price change modelling: input cost movement (commodity indices, currency), competitor prices, elasticity estimates, and the PVM impact on revenue and margin.
  • Deal desk for large tenders and key accounts: deal P&L with cost-to-serve, payment terms and working capital cost; a minimum margin floor (for example contribution ≥ 18% after cost-to-serve).
  • Scheme post-evaluation: actual incremental volume vs baseline, cost, ROI; recommending continuation or stopping.
  • Pass-through tracking: what share of a raw-material price increase was recovered through price and how many months it took.

Illustration of pass-through: input cost per unit rose ₹42; price increase taken ₹30 per unit two months later; pass-through is \(30 / 42 = 71\%\) with a two-month lag. On 5 lakh units per month the lag costs about \(42 \times 5 \times 2 = 420\) lakh before the increase and \(12 \times 5 = 60\) lakh per month thereafter.

Compliance note: pricing discussions must not involve competitors; the Competition Act, 2002 (Sec 3) prohibits anti-competitive agreements including price fixing, and resale price maintenance with distributors is also scrutinised.

Capex committee

The capex committee (sometimes a sub-committee of the board for large companies, otherwise a management committee chaired by the CEO or CFO) approves projects within the board-approved capex envelope.

FP&A role:

  • Capex envelope management: annual budget by category (maintenance, growth, strategic, regulatory) and remaining headroom.
  • Standard appraisal template: same WACC, hurdle rates by category, tax assumptions, sensitivity and scenario layout for every proposal, so projects are comparable.
  • Ranking under capital rationing: profitability index (PV of inflows ÷ investment) when the envelope is binding; strategic overrides documented.
  • Tracker and PIR schedule: status of approved projects, overruns needing supplementary approval, PIRs due.
  • Board escalation: projects above the delegation of authority (for example above ₹50 crore) go to the board; related-party elements need Audit Committee approval under Sec 177 and LODR Reg 23.

Making forums effective

  • Fixed calendar linked to the close and forecast cycle.
  • Pre-read circulated 48 hours ahead, with decisions required on page 1.
  • One version of the numbers, reconciled to the GL and the LE.
  • Decision and action log reviewed at the start of each meeting.
  • Clear decision rights: who recommends, who decides, who is consulted (RACI or RAPID).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • S&OP without finance produces volume plans that are never translated into ₹; S&OP with only finance becomes a budget review. Both functions must own it together.
  • Unconstrained vs constrained demand: finance must be clear which version feeds the LE; usually the constrained plan after executive decisions.
  • Pricing committee capture: if sales dominates, deviations become the norm; track deviation volume as a KPI.
  • Capex committee and the envelope: approving projects one by one without ranking leads to the envelope being consumed by early, not best, proposals.
  • Decision drift: forum decisions not reflected in the next forecast break trust; check the LE bridge for every decision.

Key takeaways

  • Forums are where cross-functional decisions and the rolling forecast are made; finance must translate and record.
  • S&OP needs financial translation of volume plans, gap-closing options and forecast accuracy/bias KPIs.
  • Pricing committees need price-change modelling, deal-desk floors, scheme ROI and pass-through tracking.
  • Capex committees need a standard template, envelope control, ranking under rationing and PIR scheduling.
  • Effective forums share one set of numbers, a decision log and clear decision rights.

Practice Questions

Q1. S&OP financial translation [L2]

Consensus demand for Q3 is 9.2 lakh cases against AOP 9.8 lakh cases. Average net realisation is ₹1,150 per case and contribution margin 28%. Supply can deliver only 8.9 lakh cases unless the company pays ₹40 per case extra for third-party manufacturing on up to 0.4 lakh cases. Prepare the pre-S&OP financial summary with the options for the executive meeting.

Q2. Forecast accuracy and bias [L1]

Forecast and actual volumes for five SKUs (in '000 units) are: A 120/100, B 80/90, C 50/40, D 30/35, E 20/10. Compute simple MAPE, volume-weighted MAPE and bias, and explain which metric you would show the sales head and why.

Q3. Pass-through analysis [L2]

Polymer cost rose ₹18 per kg in July. The company sells 2,400 tonnes per month. It raised prices ₹10 per kg from September and another ₹6 per kg from November. Compute the cumulative margin loss from July to December and the pass-through percentage, and draft the pricing committee note.

💡 Hint: Lay out the unrecovered cost per kg month by month before multiplying by volume.

Q4. Capital rationing at the capex committee [L3]

The remaining capex envelope is ₹80 crore. Proposals: P1 ₹40 crore, NPV ₹14 crore; P2 ₹30 crore, NPV ₹9 crore; P3 ₹25 crore, NPV ₹10 crore; P4 ₹15 crore, NPV ₹3 crore (regulatory, mandatory). Projects are indivisible. Recommend a portfolio, explain your ranking method, and describe how you would present the trade-off.

Q5. Forum design [L2]

Your company holds a monthly "business review" that runs three hours and ends without decisions. Redesign it into an effective S&OP-linked forum: agenda, pre-read, participants, decision rights and the finance deliverables.
