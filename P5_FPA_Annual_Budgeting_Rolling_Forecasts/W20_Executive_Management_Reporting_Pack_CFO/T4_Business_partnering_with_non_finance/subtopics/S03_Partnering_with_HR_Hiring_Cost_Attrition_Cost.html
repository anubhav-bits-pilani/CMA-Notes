Subtopic note

Partnering with HR: Hiring Cost, Attrition Cost, Productivity

Scope: HR: cost per hire, attrition cost, productivity, headcount vs revenue

Why it matters

Employee benefits expense is the largest cost line in Indian IT/ITES, financial services, consulting, healthcare and retail, and the second or third largest in most manufacturing companies. HR leaders track hiring funnels, time-to-fill, engagement and attrition; the CFO tracks manpower cost as a percentage of revenue and the headcount plan against the AOP. The FP&A partner connects the two: turning attrition into rupees, hiring into cost per hire and payback, and headcount into productivity per employee. Without this, the annual budget negotiation on headcount becomes a political argument rather than a driver-based one, and the MBR reports "manpower cost over budget" without saying whether the cause is rate (increments, mix), volume (more heads) or timing (joining dates).

Core notes

Cost per hire

\[ \text{Cost per hire} = \frac{\text{External recruiting cost} + \text{Internal recruiting cost}}{\text{Number of hires in the period}} \]

  • External: agency fees (typically 8.33%–15% of first-year CTC in India), job portal subscriptions, campus drive costs, background verification, relocation, joining bonuses.
  • Internal: recruiting team cost, referral bonuses, interviewer time (often estimated at hourly cost), employer branding.

Compute by role family and channel (agency, referral, portal, campus) — referral hires are usually cheaper and stay longer. Pair cost per hire with time-to-fill, because unfilled revenue-generating roles cost more than the fee: a vacant relationship manager seat at a bank or a vacant billable consultant seat is lost revenue every day.

Example: a billable consultant earns ₹18 lakh CTC and bills ₹45 lakh a year. Agency fee is 12% (₹2.16 lakh). Each extra month vacant costs roughly ₹45 lakh ÷ 12 × contribution margin. At a 40% contribution margin that is ₹1.5 lakh per month — so paying the agency to fill 6 weeks faster is justified.

Attrition cost

Attrition rate (annualised):

\[ \text{Attrition \%} = \frac{\text{Leavers in period}}{\text{Average headcount in period}} \times \frac{12}{\text{Months in period}} \times 100 \]

Split voluntary/involuntary and regretted/non-regretted. The cost of each regretted leaver includes:

Component How to estimate
Separation cost Notice-period buyout, leave encashment, gratuity (already accrued under Ind AS 19, so cash rather than P&L)
Vacancy cost Lost output or overtime/contractor premium during vacancy
Replacement cost Cost per hire
Onboarding and training Training cost, bench time, trainer time
Ramp-up productivity loss New hire at, say, 50% productivity for 3 months
Hidden costs Client or customer disruption, knowledge loss, manager time

Rule-of-thumb estimates range from 30% to 150% of annual CTC depending on role seniority. The partner should build a role-level attrition-cost calculator rather than rely on a single percentage.

Illustration: 2,000 frontline sales staff, CTC ₹4.2 lakh, attrition 48%. Estimated cost per leaver = 40% of CTC = ₹1.68 lakh. Annual attrition cost = \(960 \times 1.68 = 1{,}613\) lakh ≈ ₹16 crore. Reducing attrition by 10 points saves about ₹3.4 crore a year — enough to fund a ₹1.5 crore retention bonus and still show a return.

Productivity

Productivity metrics must be specific to the business model:

  • Revenue per employee (FTE-based, annualised): broad but distorted by outsourcing and mix.
  • Value added per employee = (Revenue − bought-in materials and services) ÷ FTEs: better for comparing insourced and outsourced models.
  • Utilisation (billable hours ÷ available hours) and realisation (billed rate ÷ standard rate) in IT services and professional firms.
  • Units per labour-hour or output per shift in manufacturing; sales per sq ft per staff in retail; cases or accounts per FTE in BPO and banking operations.
  • Employee cost as % of revenue, and revenue per ₹ of employee cost.

Headcount versus revenue

The core budget question is whether headcount grows slower than revenue (operating leverage). Track:

\[ \text{Headcount elasticity} = \frac{\%\ \Delta\ \text{Headcount}}{\%\ \Delta\ \text{Revenue}} \]

An elasticity below 1 means productivity is improving. Show headcount by function (revenue-generating, delivery, support/G&A) because support headcount should scale least. Manpower cost variance should be decomposed:

  • Volume (headcount) variance = (Actual average FTE − Budget average FTE) × Budget cost per FTE
  • Rate variance = (Actual cost per FTE − Budget cost per FTE) × Actual average FTE
  • Timing: hires joining later or earlier than the phased plan

Example: budget 500 FTE at ₹12 lakh = ₹60 crore; actual 520 FTE at ₹12.4 lakh = ₹64.48 crore. Volume variance \(20 \times 12 = 2.4\) crore adverse; rate variance \(0.4 \times 520 = 2.08\) crore adverse. HR owns the rate discussion (increment cycle, lateral hiring premium); business heads own the volume.

India-specific items

  • The four Labour Codes (brought into force from 21 November 2025) include a uniform definition of "wages" under which excluded allowances above 50% of total remuneration are added back; this can raise gratuity, PF and leave encashment costs, and the past-service impact on gratuity hits the P&L. State rules and implementation details remain date-sensitive, so check the current position before quoting impacts.
  • Gratuity and leave obligations are actuarially valued under Ind AS 19; remeasurement gains/losses go to OCI, which matters when explaining employee-cost variances.
  • ESOP cost under Ind AS 102 sits in employee benefits expense but is non-cash; show it separately when discussing productivity and cash.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Contractors and outsourced staff are often in "other expenses"; productivity comparisons must include them as FTE equivalents.
  • Bench and trainees: IT companies may report utilisation including or excluding trainees — define it.
  • Attrition denominators: using opening headcount instead of average overstates attrition in fast-growing teams.
  • Non-regretted attrition may be healthy; a target of zero attrition is not the aim.
  • Revenue per employee rises mechanically when you outsource; check value added per employee too.

Key takeaways

  • Cost per hire must be read together with time-to-fill and the revenue cost of vacancy.
  • Convert attrition into ₹ using a role-level calculator; it funds retention business cases.
  • Choose productivity metrics specific to the business model and adjust for outsourcing.
  • Track headcount elasticity to revenue and split manpower variance into volume, rate and timing.
  • Flag date-sensitive regulatory changes (Labour Codes) and non-cash items (ESOP, actuarial) in commentary.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cost per hire by channel [L1]

In FY25 an NBFC hired 600 people: 240 via agencies (fee 10% of average CTC ₹6 lakh), 180 via referrals (bonus ₹25,000 each), 180 via portals (annual subscription ₹54 lakh). The internal recruiting team costs ₹1.8 crore a year and is shared evenly across all hires. Compute cost per hire overall and by channel.

Q2. Attrition business case [L2]

A BPO has 5,000 agents, average CTC ₹3.0 lakh, annual attrition 60%. Replacement and ramp-up cost per leaver is estimated at 35% of CTC. HR proposes a retention programme costing ₹4 crore a year that is expected to cut attrition to 50%. Evaluate the proposal and identify the two assumptions you would test hardest.

💡 Hint: Compute leavers before and after, then the saving net of programme cost; sensitise the cost-per-leaver estimate.

Q3. Manpower cost variance [L2]

Budget for Q1: average 820 FTE, cost per FTE ₹3.1 lakh per quarter. Actual: average 790 FTE, cost per FTE ₹3.32 lakh per quarter. Compute the volume and rate variances, and write a two-line commentary that assigns owners.

Q4. Headcount plan challenge [L3]

A SaaS company plans revenue growth of 30% next year and headcount growth of 35%, with support functions growing 40%. As the HR finance partner, set out the analysis and questions you would bring to the headcount review, and propose productivity targets per function.

Q5. Interview: revenue per employee [L1]

Explain why two IT services companies with identical revenue per employee could have very different productivity, and which additional metrics you would use to compare them.
