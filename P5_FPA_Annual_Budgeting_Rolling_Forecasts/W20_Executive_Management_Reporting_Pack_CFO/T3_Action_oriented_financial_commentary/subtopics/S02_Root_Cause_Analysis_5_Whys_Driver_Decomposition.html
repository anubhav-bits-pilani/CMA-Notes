Subtopic note

Root-Cause Analysis: 5 Whys, Driver Decomposition, PVT Classification

Scope: Root-cause analysis: 5 Whys, driver decomposition, price/volume/timing classification

Why it matters

"Revenue is below budget due to lower volumes" is a description, not a cause. Management cannot act on "lower volumes"; it can act on "two top SKUs were delisted from a marketplace because the listing compliance documents lapsed". Getting from the variance to an actionable cause is the analytical core of FP&A commentary. Three tools do most of the work: driver decomposition (to locate the variance quantitatively), price/volume/timing classification (to type it), and the 5 Whys (to dig from the driver to the operational cause).

Core notes

Step 1 — driver decomposition

Decompose the variance mathematically until each component maps to an owner. Use the PVM engine (Week 19) for revenue and margin and rate/efficiency/volume splits for costs.

Revenue:

\[ \Delta R = \underbrace{(P_a - P_b) Q_a}_{\text{price}} + \underbrace{(Q_a - Q_b) \bar{P}_{b}}_{\text{volume}} + \underbrace{Q_a(\bar{P}_{b,\text{act mix}} - \bar{P}_{b})}_{\text{mix}} \]

where \(P\) is price, \(Q\) is quantity, subscripts \(a\) and \(b\) denote actual and budget, \(\bar{P}_{b}\) is the budget average price at budget mix and \(\bar{P}_{b,\text{act mix}}\) is the budget average price at actual mix.

Costs: split into rate (price per unit of input — ₹/km, ₹/kg copper, salary per head), efficiency/usage (input per unit of output — km per unit shipped, kg per fan) and volume (output). Example for freight:

\[ \text{Freight} = \text{Units shipped} \times \text{Trips per unit} \times \text{Cost per trip} \]

Shakti YTD freight: budget ₹60 crore; actual ₹63 crore. Units shipped 4.2% lower than budget (volume −₹2.5 crore favourable); trips per unit up 6% because of part-load e-commerce dispatches (efficiency +₹3.4 crore adverse); cost per trip up 3.5% from lane rates (rate +₹2.1 crore adverse). Net +₹3.0 crore adverse. The decomposition shows efficiency, not rate, is the largest driver — contrary to the logistics team's "fuel price" explanation.

Decompose also by dimension — segment, channel, region, customer, SKU — to find where the variance concentrates (Pareto: often 20% of SKUs or customers explain 80% of it).

Step 2 — classification: price, volume, timing (and others)

Classify each component, because each class implies a different response:

Class Meaning Typical response in commentary
Price (rate) Realisation per unit or input cost per unit differs from plan Pricing action, negotiation, hedging, pass-through
Volume Quantity sold/consumed differs Demand generation, distribution, capacity, flex costs
Mix Composition differs at constant prices Portfolio and channel actions
Timing Same amount, different period; reverses within the year Reversal date and evidence; no corrective action
Efficiency / usage Input per unit of output differs Process improvement
One-off / non-recurring Will not recur Explain; exclude from run-rate
FX Currency movement on imports/exports Hedging policy review
Accounting / estimate Accrual true-ups, provisions, reclassifications Explain; check for prior-period errors (Ind AS 8)

Timing deserves scrutiny: it is the most abused category. A genuine timing variance has a specific reversal date and evidence (dispatch schedule, signed purchase order, campaign calendar). A "timing" variance that persists for three months is a volume or permanent variance.

Step 3 — the 5 Whys

Ask "why?" iteratively until reaching a cause that an owner can act on — usually three to five levels. Developed at Toyota as part of lean problem-solving, it works in finance too when each answer is checked against data.

Shakti Water Purifiers, YTD revenue ₹13 crore below budget:

  1. Why is revenue below budget? Volume is down; price and mix are near budget (PVM: volume −₹11 crore).
  2. Why is volume down? E-commerce sales fell sharply in August–September; offline sales are on budget.
  3. Why did e-commerce sales fall? The two best-selling RO models were not available on the largest marketplace for six weeks.
  4. Why were they unavailable? The marketplace delisted them because BIS certification documents uploaded to the seller portal had expired.
  5. Why had they expired? Renewal tracking sat with an individual who left in June; no compliance calendar existed for marketplace listings.

Actionable root cause: no ownership of marketplace compliance documentation. Actions: compliance calendar with automated alerts (owner: Head of E-commerce, by 30 November); recovery plan for lost ranking (paid visibility ₹0.4 crore in November–December). Outlook: relisted 2 October; ranking recovery expected by December; ₹3 crore of the lost volume is unrecoverable in FY27.

Combining the tools

  1. Decompose to locate the variance (where, which driver, how much).
  2. Classify to decide the type of response.
  3. Use 5 Whys on the largest non-timing components to reach actionable causes.
  4. Validate each answer with data (s06).
  5. Write the commentary (s01) with the root cause, not the driver.

Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagrams help when causes are multiple — categories such as people, process, systems, suppliers, customers, environment — and are useful in workshops with operations teams.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Multiple interacting causes: a price increase that caused volume loss is one root cause with two effects; describe it once with both effects.
  • Budget error as root cause: sometimes the variance exists because the budget was wrong (unrealistic phasing, missed cost). Say so honestly; the action is to fix the forecast, not to blame operations.
  • 5 Whys that end in blame ("because the manager was careless") stop too early; continue to the process or system gap.
  • Mix definitions: mix computed at budget price vs budget margin gives different answers; use the method in the methodology appendix consistently.
  • Data granularity limits: if SKU-level data is unavailable, state the level of analysis and the limitation.

Key takeaways

  • Decompose first (PVM, rate/efficiency/volume, by dimension) to locate and size the variance.
  • Classify each component — price, volume, mix, timing, efficiency, one-off, FX, accounting — because each implies a different response.
  • Treat "timing" with suspicion: it needs a reversal date and evidence.
  • Use 5 Whys on material non-timing components until reaching a process or decision an owner can change.
  • Validate each step with data; write the root cause, not the driver, in the commentary.

Practice Questions

Q1. Freight decomposition [L2]

Budget: 4.80 million units shipped, 0.0040 trips per unit, ₹31,250 per trip. Actual: 4.60 million units, 0.0043 trips per unit, ₹31,850 per trip. Compute budget and actual freight, and the volume, efficiency and rate variances (sequential: volume at budget efficiency and rate; efficiency at actual volume and budget rate; rate at actual volume and efficiency). Write the root-cause sentence.

Q2. 5 Whys for employee cost [L2]

Employee cost is ₹1.0 crore over budget YTD. Data: on-roll headcount on budget; overtime in the Hosur plant ₹0.6 crore over budget; sales incentive accrual ₹0.5 crore over; savings on vacancies ₹0.1 crore. Plant overtime rose because line-1 changeovers increased from 8 to 14 per week. Carry out a 5 Whys on the overtime component (state your assumed answers at each level) and identify an actionable root cause.

💡 Hint: Ask why changeovers increased and keep going until you reach a planning or process decision.

Q3. Timing or not? [L2]

The Fans BU has classified a ₹4 crore October revenue shortfall as "timing — Diwali dispatches shifted to November" for the second month (September's ₹3 crore was also classified as timing and November's dispatch plan was then ₹40 crore vs budget ₹36 crore). September actual dispatches were ₹1 crore above the September plan. Assess whether the timing classification holds, what evidence you would require, and how you would classify the variance in the MBR.

Q4. Pareto decomposition [L2]

Modern-trade revenue is ₹9 crore below budget YTD across 14 customers. The two largest customers account for −₹7.5 crore, four others −₹2.5 crore and the remaining eight +₹1.0 crore. Describe how you would present this decomposition, which root-cause analysis you would prioritise, and draft the commentary line.
