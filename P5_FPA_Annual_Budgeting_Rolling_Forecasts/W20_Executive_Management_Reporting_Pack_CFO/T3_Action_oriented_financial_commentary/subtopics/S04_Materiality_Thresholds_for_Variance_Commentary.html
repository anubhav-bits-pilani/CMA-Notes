Subtopic note

Materiality Thresholds for Variance Commentary

Scope: Materiality thresholds for which variances need commentary

Why it matters

A P&L with 60 lines across three segments and three time lenses produces hundreds of variances every month. Commenting on all of them produces the "lengthy commentary that buries the key message" pitfall; commenting on none leaves executives guessing. Materiality thresholds decide, objectively and in advance, which variances require an explanation. They save time at close, make the pack shorter and more consistent, and remove arguments about why one item was explained and another was not.

Core notes

Principles

Management-reporting materiality is related to, but different from, audit materiality (SA 320 in India, typically 0.5–1% of revenue or 5% of PBT for the financial statements as a whole). Management thresholds are lower and more granular because the purpose is control and decision-making, not opinion on true and fair view. Good thresholds are:

  • Dual-test: both an absolute ₹ amount and a relative % of budget (c03), so that large lines with small % variances and small lines with large % variances are both handled sensibly.
  • Scaled by level: entity-level pack thresholds are higher than BU or cost-centre thresholds.
  • Applied to each lens: month, YTD and FY LE; YTD and FY usually matter most.
  • Published and stable: in the methodology appendix; changed only at AOP.
  • Overridable upwards: anything strategically or reputationally sensitive is commented regardless of size.

Common threshold designs

Design Rule Strength Weakness
Absolute only \(\lvert \text{Var} \rvert >\) ₹1 crore Simple Ignores small lines with large overruns; flags noise on big lines
Relative only \(\lvert \text{Var \%} \rvert > 5\%\) Normalises size Flags trivial lines (₹5 lakh on ₹20 lakh)
AND (both) ₹ > X and % > Y Filters noise; focuses on material and unusual May miss large ₹ variances with small % on very large lines
OR (either) ₹ > X or % > Y Catches more Too many items
AND with absolute backstop (₹ > X and % > Y) or ₹ > Z Balanced; most used in practice Slightly more complex

Setting X, Y and Z for Shakti (entity MBR, YTD lens): X = ₹1.5 crore (about 2% of YTD EBITDA budget), Y = 5%, backstop Z = ₹5 crore. For BU packs: X = ₹50 lakh, Y = 5%. For cost-centre packs: X = ₹5 lakh, Y = 10%.

A common calibration approach: set X as a percentage of the relevant profit measure (1–3% of EBITDA for the entity pack), Y at 5–10% depending on line volatility, and test on the last six months of data so that 8–15 items per pack trigger commentary.

Applying thresholds across lenses

Rules should state which lens triggers commentary:

  • YTD: primary trigger.
  • Month: trigger only if above a higher threshold or if the month reverses a trend.
  • FY LE vs AOP: trigger at the same ₹ threshold — a small YTD variance with a large FY impact must be commented on.
  • vs PY: trigger for revenue and margin lines where growth is a strategic KPI.

Note the boundary case: freight at exactly 5.0% — the rule must specify "greater than" or "greater than or equal to". The travel variance of ₹4 lakh (12%) falls below the entity threshold, so it belongs in the Sales function pack — which is exactly where the c01 strong commentary would be written.

Excel implementation

=IF(OR(AND(ABS(Var)>X, ABS(VarPct)>Y), ABS(Var)>Z), "Comment", "")

With named cells X, Y, Z on the assumptions tab (and a level lookup if thresholds vary by pack level). Apply it to YTD and LE columns and filter the commentary table on "Comment". Add a manual "Force comment" flag for sensitive items.

In Power BI, a measure returns 1 when the trigger is met; use it as a visual-level filter on the commentary table or as conditional formatting.

Aggregation traps

  • Netting within a line: a line can be within threshold because large components offset (price +₹6 crore, volume −₹6 crore). Apply thresholds to PVM components as well as to lines.
  • Splitting: the same issue spread across several lines (freight in COGS and in other expenses) may fall below threshold on each; review by driver as well as by line.
  • Cumulative creep: a small monthly variance that persists becomes material YTD; the YTD lens catches it, the month lens does not.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Qualitative materiality: related-party transactions, regulatory penalties, safety incidents, fraud indicators and items affecting covenants or guidance are commented regardless of size.
  • Favourable variances: apply the same thresholds — large favourable variances may be timing or errors.
  • Budget = 0: % undefined; rely on the ₹ test.
  • Very volatile lines (FX, commodity hedges): may need higher % thresholds or a separate treatment.
  • Thresholds and auditors: management thresholds do not substitute for audit materiality; but large unexplained management variances are an audit risk indicator (analytical procedures under SA 520).

Key takeaways

  • Use dual ₹ and % thresholds (AND logic) with an absolute backstop; scale by pack level.
  • Calibrate so that roughly 8–15 items per entity pack require commentary.
  • Apply to YTD, FY LE and selected month and PY views; specify boundary conditions (> vs ≥).
  • Test PVM components and drivers, not just lines, to avoid netting and splitting traps.
  • Qualitatively sensitive items are commented on regardless of size.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the trigger [L1]

Using X = ₹1.5 crore, Y = 5%, Z = ₹5 crore (comment if ₹ and % both exceeded, or ₹ > Z), decide which of these YTD variances need commentary: (a) IT costs ₹1.8 crore (22%) over; (b) depreciation ₹0.9 crore (4%) over; (c) Kitchen revenue ₹8 crore (2.6%) under; (d) power and fuel ₹1.6 crore (4.9%) over; (e) legal fees ₹0.6 crore (60%) over.

Q2. Calibrate thresholds [L2]

Over the last six months, Shakti's entity pack had on average 42 YTD line variances. Distribution of absolute ₹ variances (₹ crore): 18 below 0.5, 10 between 0.5 and 1.5, 8 between 1.5 and 5, 6 above 5. Of the 14 above ₹1.5 crore, 10 exceed 5%. Propose thresholds that yield 10–14 commented items, and explain the trade-off.

💡 Hint: Count how many items each candidate rule would select before choosing.

Q3. Netting trap [L2]

Kitchen Appliances revenue variance is −₹0.8 crore (−0.3%), below threshold. PVM shows price +₹7.2 crore, volume −₹9.5 crore, mix +₹1.5 crore. Should it be commented on? Draft the rule amendment for the methodology appendix that handles such cases.

Q4. Pack-level design [L3]

Design a three-tier threshold policy (entity MBR, BU pack, cost-centre pack) for Shakti, including absolute and % tests, lenses, qualitative overrides, the Excel/Power BI implementation and the governance for changes. Justify each number with reference to Shakti's scale (FY27 AOP revenue ₹1,200 crore, EBITDA ₹150 crore).
