Subtopic note

Five-Part Commentary Structure

Scope: Commentary structure: what happened, why (root cause), impact, outlook, action/owner/timeline

Why it matters

A fixed structure is what makes commentary consistently useful rather than dependent on the writer's style that month. Executives learn to read it: the first clause tells them what happened, the next why, then what it costs, what happens next and who is fixing it. It also disciplines the writer — if you cannot fill the "why" or the "owner" slot, you have not finished the analysis. Most FP&A teams that upgrade their MBR start by imposing this five-part template on every material variance.

Core notes

The five parts

Part Question answered Content Typical length
1. What happened What moved, by how much, against what? KPI/line, ₹ and % variance, comparison base (budget, PY, LE), period One clause
2. Why (root cause) What business cause drove it? Driver class (price, volume, mix, timing, rate, one-off) and the underlying operational cause One sentence
3. Impact What does it mean financially? ₹ EBITDA/cash impact, bps of margin, % of budget, FY impact One clause
4. Outlook Will it continue, reverse or worsen? Recurring vs one-off, timing reversal date, LE treatment, risks One clause
5. Action / owner / timeline What are we doing about it? Specific action, named owner, due date, expected ₹ benefit; or "no action — timing, reverses in Nov" One sentence

A complete comment is usually 30–60 words. Example (Shakti, YTD October):

Freight ₹3.0 crore (5%) over budget YTD: North lane rates rose 9% after the July re-contract and part-load dispatches rose to 22% of trips (budget 12%) as e-commerce orders fragmented. Impact −40 bps of EBITDA margin; FY impact ₹5.2 crore if unaddressed, which is in the LE. Head of Logistics to re-tender North lanes (40% of volume) and consolidate e-commerce dispatches to two days a week by 15 December; expected saving ₹1.5 crore in Q4.

Parts mapped: what (₹3.0 crore, 5%, YTD vs budget); why (lane rates, part-load trips); impact (40 bps; FY ₹5.2 crore); outlook (recurring, in LE); action (re-tender and consolidation, owner, date, ₹ benefit).

Part 1 — what happened

State the variance precisely and with direction in words, not only signs: "₹3.0 crore (5%) over budget" or "₹13 crore (9.6%) below budget and 3% below PY". Specify the period (month, YTD, LE) and base. Lead with the most decision-relevant lens — by mid-year that is often YTD plus FY impact rather than the month.

Part 2 — why

The why has two layers: the driver class from the PVM/variance engine (price, volume, mix, rate, efficiency, timing, one-off, FX) and the business cause behind it (e.g. "two best-selling SKUs delisted on a marketplace for six weeks after a listing compliance issue"). A driver class alone ("volume") is not a root cause. s02 covers how to dig.

Part 3 — impact

Translate into the units executives use: ₹ crore or lakh of EBITDA, bps of margin, % of budget, cash impact where different (e.g. a receivables delay has no P&L impact but a cash impact), and the full-year effect. s03 covers the mechanics.

Part 4 — outlook

Classify the variance's future:

  • Timing: will reverse by a specific month — state when and what evidence supports it.
  • Recurring/run-rate: will continue unless action is taken — state whether it is in the LE.
  • One-off: will not recur — state why.
  • Worsening/improving: trend expected to change — state the driver.

This links the backward-looking variance to the LE and R&O log.

Part 5 — action, owner, timeline

A specific, verb-first action; one named owner (role and name in the internal pack); a due date; and the expected ₹ effect. If no action is needed (timing, one-off, external with no lever), say so explicitly — "no action required; reverses in November as dispatches resume". Every action here should appear in the action log with the same ID.

Templates and formatting

A consistent template helps both writers and readers. A simple one-line format:

[Line] ₹X (Y%) [over/under] [base] [period]: [root cause]. Impact [₹/bps], FY [₹]. [Outlook]. [Owner] to [action] by [date]; [benefit].

In Excel, keep a commentary table keyed to variance lines with separate columns for each of the five parts and concatenate them for the pack; this allows completeness checks (blank "owner" for an adverse recurring variance is flagged). In Power BI, store commentary in a SharePoint list or a commentary table joined to the line dimension and display it in a table visual or tooltip.

Positive variances

Favourable variances also need the structure. "A&P ₹2.0 crore under budget" may be a timing deferral (Diwali campaign moved to November) — so outlook says "reverses in November" and no saving should be assumed. A genuine efficiency gain should be explained so it can be replicated.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Several drivers on one line: give the top two with ₹ each and group the rest as "other" — do not list five drivers.
  • Offsetting variances: a net small variance may hide large offsetting ones (price +₹8 crore, volume −₹8 crore); comment if the gross components are material.
  • Unknown root cause at close: say so, give the hypothesis and the date by which it will be confirmed; do not invent a cause.
  • Sensitive causes (fraud suspicion, personnel issues): keep out of the circulated pack; brief the CFO separately.
  • External causes with no lever (commodity prices, monsoon): the action may be mitigation (pricing, hedging) or acceptance with an LE update.

Key takeaways

  • Five parts for every material variance: what, why, impact, outlook, action/owner/timeline.
  • "Why" needs both the driver class and the underlying business cause.
  • Impact is in ₹, bps, % and FY terms; outlook classifies timing vs recurring vs one-off.
  • Actions are specific, owned by a named person, dated and valued — or explicitly "no action" with a reason.
  • Use a structured commentary table so missing parts can be detected automatically.

Practice Questions

Q1. Label the parts [L1]

Identify which of the five parts are present or missing in: "Employee cost ₹1.0 crore (1.8%) over budget YTD due to higher sales incentive accruals as Fans achieved September targets. This is in line with the higher revenue in Fans." Rewrite it to include all five parts (assume FY impact ₹1.4 crore, and incentive plan review by CHRO by 31 January).

Q2. Write five-part comments [L2]

Write five-part comments (30–60 words each) for: (a) Water Purifiers revenue ₹13 crore (9.6%) below budget YTD, of which ₹11 crore volume due to marketplace delisting of two SKUs for six weeks (relisted 2 October); (b) gross margin 160 bps below budget, of which 110 bps copper/aluminium inflation not yet passed through; (c) A&P ₹2.0 crore under budget, Diwali campaign moved to November.

💡 Hint: For (c), think carefully about what the outlook and action parts should say for a timing variance.

Q3. Completeness check formula [L2]

A commentary table has columns: Line, Variance ₹, Favourable? (Y/N), Class (Timing/Recurring/One-off), Why, Impact, Outlook, Owner, Due date. Write an Excel formula that flags a row as "Incomplete" if it is adverse, recurring and lacks either an owner or a due date, or if any row lacks a "Why".

Q4. Offsetting variances [L2]

Kitchen Appliances revenue is ₹8 crore below budget YTD, made up of volume −₹14 crore (mixer-grinder demand weak in South), price +₹9 crore (3% increase in July) and mix −₹3 crore. Write the commentary and explain why commenting only on the net ₹8 crore would mislead.
