Subtopic note

Quantifying Impact: ₹, % of Budget, bps of Margin, FY Impact

Scope: Quantification: ₹ impact, % of budget, bps of margin, FY impact

Why it matters

Executives prioritise by size. A comment that says "freight costs rose significantly" cannot be ranked against "copper inflation hurt margins"; a comment that says "freight −₹3.0 crore, −43 bps, FY −₹5.2 crore" versus "copper −₹7.3 crore, −110 bps, FY −₹11.7 crore" can. Quantification in four complementary units — absolute ₹, % of budget, bps of margin and full-year impact — lets the reader judge materiality, compare items, and connect a monthly variance to the year's outcome and to guidance.

Core notes

1. ₹ impact

State the variance in the pack's standard unit (₹ crore, one decimal, for a company of Shakti's size; ₹ lakh for a division or cost centre pack). Specify which P&L level it affects: revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, PAT or cash. A revenue variance is not an EBITDA variance — ₹13 crore of lost purifier revenue at 14% contribution costs ₹1.8 crore of EBITDA, not ₹13 crore. Always convert revenue variances to their profit impact using the appropriate margin (contribution margin for volume; 100% flow-through for price, net of variable costs linked to price such as commissions).

\[ \text{EBITDA impact of volume} = \Delta Q \times \text{Contribution per unit} \]

\[ \text{EBITDA impact of price} = \Delta P \times Q_a \times (1 - v) \]

where \(v\) is the share of revenue paid away in price-linked variable costs (distributor margin, commissions, royalties).

2. % of budget

\[ \text{Variance \%} = \frac{\text{Actual} - \text{Budget}}{\lvert\text{Budget}\rvert} \]

with sign set by the F/A convention. % of budget normalises across lines of different size: ₹4 lakh on a ₹33 lakh travel budget (12%) is a control issue; ₹4 lakh on a ₹60 crore freight line is noise. Use the absolute value of budget in the denominator to avoid sign errors when the budget is negative (e.g. a budgeted loss). For lines with tiny budgets, % is misleading (₹2 lakh on a ₹1 lakh budget is +200%); combine with ₹ thresholds (s04).

3. Basis points of margin

A margin variance is expressed in bps (1 bp = 0.01 percentage point):

\[ \text{Margin variance (bps)} = (m_a - m_b) \times 10{,}000 \]

where \(m\) is the margin as a decimal. For an individual line, its impact on margin is its ₹ variance divided by revenue:

\[ \text{bps impact of a line} = \frac{\text{Variance of the line (₹)}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 10{,}000 \]

Use actual revenue for actual-margin bridges, consistently. Bps let a reader see how much of the margin gap each item explains. Shakti YTD (actual revenue ₹660 crore): EBITDA margin 9.2% vs 12.1% = −290 bps (exactly 9.21% − 12.12% = −291 bps).

Driver ₹ crore bps of margin (on ₹660 crore)
Copper/aluminium inflation (7.3) (111)

Total (291)

The deleverage row matters: when revenue falls with fixed costs constant, margin falls even with no cost variance. Computing a full bps bridge requires care — the cleanest method is margin at budget cost ratios on actual revenue, then each cost ratio variance.

4. FY impact

The executive question is "what does this do to the year?" FY impact combines the YTD variance with the expected YTG effect:

\[ \text{FY impact} = \text{YTD variance} + \text{YTG expected variance} \]

YTG expected variance depends on the outlook classification:

  • Timing: YTG reverses the YTD variance — FY impact ≈ 0.
  • One-off: FY impact = YTD variance.
  • Run-rate (recurring): FY impact = YTD variance + monthly run-rate × months remaining, adjusted for actions.
  • Worsening/improving: model explicitly.

Example (travel, the c01 case): YTD travel ₹37 lakh vs budget ₹33 lakh — ₹4 lakh (12%) over due to unplanned customer visits; monthly overspend running ₹0.6 lakh; five months left; Sales head caps travel from Q3 to halve the run-rate: FY impact ≈ ₹4 + 0.3 × 5 = ₹5.5 lakh, rounded to ₹6 lakh with risk.

State whether the FY impact is already in the LE. If commentary says "FY impact ₹5.2 crore" but the LE assumes ₹2 crore, the reader needs to know the ₹3.2 crore difference is a risk (and it belongs in the R&O log).

5. Cash impact

For working capital and capex items, give the cash impact alongside or instead of P&L: "DSO 8 days above budget — ₹26 crore cash; no P&L impact beyond ECL ₹0.7 crore". For P&L items with different cash timing (accruals, prepaid), note the difference.

Rounding and precision

Round to the precision that supports decisions: one decimal of ₹ crore for EBITDA lines, whole bps for margins, whole % for variance %. Ensure rounded components in a sentence add up (or say "about"). Spurious precision (₹3.0742 crore) signals a lack of judgement.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Volume variances: never present revenue volume variance as profit impact; convert at contribution.
  • Price increases with volume loss: quantify both effects and net them; the net is the real impact.
  • Mix measured at revenue vs margin: a mix shift to cheaper products may raise or lower margin depending on product margins; quantify at margin for profit commentary.
  • FX on imported inputs: separate FX from commodity price for hedging discussion.
  • Annualising vs FY: "annualised impact ₹12 crore" (run-rate × 12) differs from "FY27 impact ₹5 crore" (remaining months only); label clearly.

Key takeaways

  • Quantify every material variance in ₹ (at the right P&L level), % of budget, bps of margin and FY impact.
  • Convert revenue volume variances to profit using contribution margin; price flows through net of price-linked costs.
  • Use bps of revenue to show each item's contribution to the margin gap, including deleverage.
  • FY impact depends on outlook class: timing ≈ 0, one-off = YTD, recurring = YTD + run-rate × months left.
  • State whether the FY impact is in the LE; anything not in it is an R&O item.

Practice Questions

Q1. Four-unit quantification [L1]

Fans & Lighting: YTD revenue ₹236 crore vs budget ₹245 crore; contribution margin 14.0% vs 16.0%; YTD freight for the BU ₹14.4 crore vs ₹13.0 crore. Compute for freight: ₹ variance, % of budget and bps of BU revenue. Compute the contribution margin variance in bps and in ₹ at actual revenue.

Q2. Volume variance to EBITDA [L2]

Water Purifiers lost 22,000 units against budget at an average price of ₹5,000 per unit; contribution margin is 14% at budget. Price increased 2% on 1 August on actual volume of 2.20 lakh units sold since then, with distributor margin of 12% linked to price. Compute the EBITDA impact of the volume loss and of the price increase, and the net.

💡 Hint: Revenue volume variance × contribution margin; price variance × (1 − price-linked cost share).

Q3. FY impact by class [L2]

For each YTD variance, compute the FY impact (five months left): (a) A&P ₹2.0 crore favourable — campaign moved to November; (b) VRS cost ₹3.0 crore — one-off; (c) freight ₹3.0 crore adverse — run-rate ₹0.45 crore a month, re-tender from 1 January saving ₹0.3 crore a month; (d) copper ₹7.3 crore adverse — copper prices now 4% lower than the YTD average, with 60% of the cost increase still applying.

Q4. Margin bridge in bps [L3]

Build the EBITDA margin bridge in bps from budget 12.1% to actual 9.2% for Shakti using: budget revenue ₹690 crore, actual ₹660 crore; budget fixed costs ₹151 crore (employee, A&P, freight and other treated as fixed for the exercise); actual fixed costs ₹153 crore; budget GM 34.0%, actual 32.4%. Show deleverage, GM rate effect and fixed-cost variance separately and confirm they sum to the total.
