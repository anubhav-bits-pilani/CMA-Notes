Subtopic note

Collecting and Validating Business Explanations

Scope: Collecting inputs from business owners and validating explanations

Why it matters

Finance rarely knows first-hand why a sales region missed, why a plant ran overtime or why a marketing campaign slipped. The explanation must come from the business owner. But owners have incentives to explain variances favourably — "timing", "market", "one-off" — and to avoid committing to actions. FP&A's value is in collecting explanations efficiently within a tight close calendar and then validating them against data before they reach the CEO. Commentary that passes through unvalidated explanations quickly loses credibility; commentary that is validated becomes the most trusted page in the pack.

Core notes

The commentary collection process

A typical MBR calendar (working days after month-end):

WD Activity
WD1–3 Close; preliminary P&L and balance sheet
WD3 FP&A runs variance and PVM engine; applies materiality thresholds; issues commentary requests to owners with data pre-filled
WD4 Owners return explanations (template)
WD4–5 FP&A validates, challenges and drafts; follow-up calls with owners
WD5 Owners review draft commentary ("no surprises")
WD6 CFO review; pack finalised
WD7–8 MBR meeting

The request template

Send owners a structured template, pre-populated with the numbers and the driver decomposition, asking for the remaining parts:

Field Pre-filled by FP&A Completed by owner
Line / KPI, period Yes
Variance ₹ and %, vs budget, PY, LE Yes
Driver decomposition (PVM, rate/efficiency/volume) Yes
Business cause Hypothesis (optional) Yes
Evidence Yes (data, documents)
Outlook: timing / recurring / one-off; reversal date Yes
FY impact and LE treatment Proposed Confirm
Action, owner, due date, ₹ benefit Yes

Pre-filling the numbers prevents owners from disputing the data at the commentary stage and focuses the conversation on causes and actions. Tools: Excel templates on SharePoint, commentary modules in EPM tools (Anaplan, Adaptive, Oracle EPM, OneStream), or a Power Apps form writing to a SharePoint list consumed by Power BI.

Validation — testing explanations against data

Treat each explanation as a hypothesis and test it. Common explanation types and how to test them:

Explanation offered Validation tests
"Market slowdown" Industry data (industry association, Nielsen/retail audit), competitor results, Shakti's market share trend, performance in other channels/regions
"Timing — will catch up next month" Order book, dispatch plan, signed POs, prior-month timing claims and whether they reversed, customer confirmations
"Price increase by supplier / fuel" Invoice-level unit rates, contract terms, index movement (LME copper, diesel price) vs observed cost change
"One-off" Nature of the item, whether similar items occurred before, whether it will recur in the plan period
"Higher volumes caused higher cost" Cost per unit (efficiency) — if cost rose faster than volume, it is not just volume
"Accounting reclassification" Journal entries, GL mapping changes, whether PY/budget are on the same basis
"Budget was wrong" Budget assumptions document; if true, what has changed in the LE

Shakti examples (October):

  • Water Purifiers said "market slowdown". Industry purifier sales grew 6% YoY in Q2; Shakti's offline sales were on budget; e-commerce share dropped from 38% to 31%. Not validated — the cause was Shakti-specific (marketplace delisting).
  • Logistics said "fuel price increase". Diesel in the North rose 2%, but Shakti's freight per unit rose 9%; decomposition showed trips per unit up 6% from part-load dispatches. Partially validated — fuel explains a small part; dispatch efficiency is the main cause.
  • Fans said "timing — Diwali dispatches moved to November". The November dispatch plan is ₹40 crore vs budget ₹36 crore, distributor POs for ₹24 crore are already received, and the September "timing" claim of ₹3 crore had reversed in early October. Validated, with a reversal checkpoint for the November MBR.

Techniques for challenge

  • Ask for numbers, not narratives: "How many units, which customers, what price?"
  • Triangulate: compare to a second source (operational system, external data, another function's view).
  • Look for consistency over time: an explanation used for three months running is suspect.
  • Check the opposite: if "volume" caused higher freight, did freight per unit stay flat?
  • Use the KPI tree: if revenue fell due to "distribution loss", did outlet count fall?
  • Keep a commentary history: track claims (especially timing) and their outcomes month by month.

Escalation and documentation

If an owner's explanation cannot be validated and they insist on it, the commentary should state the facts found and that the explanation is "per BU; FP&A analysis indicates [alternative]", escalated to the CFO before the pack is issued. Keep an evidence file per material commentary (data extracts, emails, POs) — useful for the next month and for internal audit.

Relationship management

Validation should not feel like an audit of the owner. Practices that help: share the variance analysis early, frame challenges as "help me explain this to the CEO", agree the commentary jointly, give owners credit for actions, and make the "no surprises" review standard. Over time, owners who see that validated commentary protects them in the MBR provide better inputs.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Owner unavailable at close: publish with "explanation to follow" and a date, rather than inventing one.
  • Conflicting explanations (Sales blames Supply Chain for OTIF; Supply Chain blames forecast accuracy): present the data for both and agree a joint action; escalate if needed.
  • Sensitive findings during validation (possible channel stuffing, fictitious credit notes): do not put them in the pack; inform the CFO and, where appropriate, internal audit or the Audit Committee under the vigil mechanism.
  • Channel stuffing check: high primary sales with flat secondary sales and rising distributor inventory or DSO indicates pushed sales — revenue recognition under Ind AS 115 and returns provisions need review.
  • EPM-generated explanations: validate them with the same rigour as human ones.

Key takeaways

  • Run a defined commentary calendar: request on WD3 with pre-filled numbers and drivers, validate WD4–5, owner review, CFO sign-off.
  • Use a structured template capturing cause, evidence, outlook, FY impact and action/owner/date.
  • Treat explanations as hypotheses; validate with industry data, operational data, contracts, indices and history.
  • Track timing claims month to month; recurring "timing" is reclassified.
  • Escalate unvalidated explanations transparently; keep an evidence file; manage relationships with "no surprises".

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the request template [L1]

Design the commentary request template for Shakti's BU heads (columns, pre-filled fields, drop-downs and due time) and the e-mail text that accompanies it on WD3.

Q2. Validate three explanations [L2]

For each explanation, state two validation tests and the data you would request: (a) "Kitchen revenue missed because of a competitor's festive discount campaign"; (b) "Hosur plant power cost is higher because of a state tariff increase"; (c) "Receivables increased because modern-trade customers delayed payments at quarter-end — timing". Data available: competitor results not yet published; state electricity regulator order raised industrial tariff by 4%, Shakti's power cost per unit up 11%; modern-trade DSO 62 days (September 58, August 56).

💡 Hint: For each, compare the size of the claimed cause with the size of the variance.

Q3. Channel stuffing indicator [L3]

Fans primary sales in October were ₹33 crore (budget ₹36 crore) but the BU head projects ₹40 crore in November. Distributor inventory days rose from 32 to 41 in October; secondary sales were flat YoY; the BU incentive plan pays on primary sales with a quarterly target. Assess the risk, the validation steps, the accounting considerations under Ind AS 115 and how you would handle it in the MBR and with the CFO.

Q4. Commentary history tracker [L2]

Design an Excel tracker that records each timing claim (month raised, ₹, expected reversal month, actual reversal) and automatically flags claims that have not reversed by the expected month. Write the key formulas.
