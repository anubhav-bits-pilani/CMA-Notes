Roadmap topic: Action-Oriented Financial Commentary: Moving from 'Travel expense increased by ₹4L' to root-cause + business impact + corrective owner/timeline

Overview

Variance commentary is the written explanation that accompanies each MBR page. In most finance teams it is the weakest part of the pack: "Travel expense increased by ₹4L", "Revenue below budget due to lower sales", "Employee cost higher due to increments". Such sentences restate the table and tell the reader nothing they could not see in the numbers. Action-oriented commentary does the opposite: it explains why the variance arose (root cause), quantifies what it means (₹, % of budget, bps of margin, full-year impact), states what will happen next (outlook) and who will do what by when (action, owner, timeline).

This is the core skill that separates an FP&A business partner from a reporting accountant, and it is tested directly in interviews ("here is a variance table — write the CFO commentary"). It draws on everything earlier in the phase: the PVM and timing outputs from Week 19 supply the drivers; the LE supplies the forward view; the KPI scorecard identifies which items are Red; and the MBR structure decides where commentary goes. It also requires judgement: which variances deserve commentary (materiality), how to validate the explanations business owners give, and how to write in a tone executives trust.

The learning sequence: start with the five-part commentary structure (s01) and the weak-to-strong example (c01). Learn to find root causes with 5 Whys and driver decomposition (s02), then to quantify impact in ₹, %, bps and FY terms (s03). Decide what needs commentary using materiality thresholds (s04) and the commentary trigger (c03). Write in the right tone (s05), using the SCR framework for executive narratives (c02) and the "so what" test for every sentence (c04). Finally, collect and validate inputs from business owners (s06).

The running case remains Shakti Home Appliances Ltd, October 2026: YTD EBITDA ₹60.8 crore vs budget ₹83.6 crore; FY27 LE ₹127.1 crore vs guidance ₹150 crore.

Related concepts to study

Minto Pyramid Principle and executive writing

Minto Pyramid Principle and executive writing

The pyramid principle (Topic 1 c04) governs commentary at every level: the first sentence of each commentary box is the conclusion, the following sentences are the supporting reasons, and the data sits in the table above. Executive writing adds sentence-level discipline: lead with the number and its meaning, use active voice and named owners, one idea per sentence, no hedging qualifiers ("somewhat", "slightly") without numbers, and consistent terms. Combined with SCR (c02), the pyramid turns a list of variances into an argument that leads to a decision. Practise by rewriting old commentary: delete every sentence that merely repeats the table and see what is left.

PVM and timing variance outputs as commentary basis

→ Week 19: PVM and timing variance outputs as commentary basis

Week 19's PVM engine splits revenue and margin variances into price, volume and mix, and flags timing variances (phasing differences that reverse within the year). These outputs are the raw material of good commentary: they tell you which driver moved, so the commentary can focus on why. A ₹30 crore revenue shortfall decomposed into volume −₹26 crore, price +₹4 crore and mix −₹8 crore leads to very different commentary than an undifferentiated "sales below budget". Timing classification prevents over-reaction: a variance flagged as timing needs a reversal date in the commentary, not a corrective action. Always reconcile bridge steps to the total before writing.

CFO letter and investor call commentary styles in Indian listed companies

CFO letter and investor call commentary styles in Indian listed companies

Indian listed companies communicate performance through quarterly investor presentations, earnings call transcripts (which SEBI LODR requires to be published on the website and filed with exchanges) and the annual report's MD&A and chairman/CFO letters. Good examples lead with a headline (growth, margin, cash), explain drivers in business terms (volume growth, realisation, commodity costs, mix), and give an outlook with a guidance range. Studying the transcripts of well-regarded NSE companies is a useful benchmark for tone: factual, quantified, balanced. But external commentary is constrained by disclosure rules (no selective disclosure of UPSI, consistency with filed results), whereas internal MBR commentary can and must be blunter about problems, owners and actions.

AI-assisted commentary drafting features in EPM tools (Anaplan, Adaptive, Oracle)

AI-assisted commentary drafting features in EPM tools (Anaplan, Adaptive, Oracle)

Enterprise performance management platforms (Anaplan, Workday Adaptive Planning, Oracle EPM Cloud, OneStream and others) and Power BI's smart narrative increasingly offer generated variance narratives: they detect the largest variances, describe them in sentences and, with generative AI, draft commentary from data and prior narratives. These save time on the "what happened" part. They cannot reliably supply root cause, validated business context or accountable owners — the parts that make commentary valuable — and they may state correlations as causes. Treat generated drafts as a first pass: verify every number against the model, add the validated why, impact, owner and date, and keep human review and sign-off. Data confidentiality settings must be checked before company data is sent to external AI services.

Common pitfalls

Restating numbers without explaining why

"Revenue was ₹660 crore against budget ₹690 crore, a shortfall of ₹30 crore" repeats what the table already shows. It happens because writing the why requires analysis and conversations with the business, and the deadline is tomorrow. Correct treatment: every comment starts from the driver (price, volume, mix, timing, rate, one-off) and the underlying business cause, quantifies impact and states outlook and action. Detect in review: cover the table and read the commentary — if it contains no information beyond the numbers, it fails; also count sentences without a "because" or "due to" followed by a business cause.

Accepting business explanations without validation

Business owners naturally explain variances in ways that protect them ("market slowdown", "timing — will catch up next month"). If FP&A passes these through unchecked, the commentary is unreliable and the same "timing" variance recurs for six months. Correct treatment: test explanations against data — order books, secondary sales, market data, invoice-level detail, prior-month claims — and record the evidence. Detect: track timing claims month to month; if a "timing" variance does not reverse when promised, escalate and reclassify it.

No owner or timeline for corrective action

Commentary such as "Freight costs are being reviewed" names no one and no date, so nothing happens and the item reappears next month. It happens because finance is reluctant to assign actions to operational heads. Correct treatment: every adverse material variance that is not timing has an action with a named owner (person, not department), a due date and an expected ₹ effect, entered into the action log (Topic 1 s05). Detect: scan commentary for passive constructions and words like "reviewing", "monitoring", "exploring" without a name and date.

Lengthy commentary that buries the key message

Long paragraphs that walk through every line item put the crucial point — "full-year EBITDA will miss guidance by ₹23 crore" — in the middle of page 3. It happens because writers fear omitting something and because detail feels safe. Correct treatment: pyramid structure with the key message first, maximum three to five bullets per page, materiality thresholds to exclude small items, and detail moved to the appendix. Detect: ask a reader to state the key message after 30 seconds; check word counts per page (e.g. maximum 120 words per commentary box).

Practice Questions

Q1. Full commentary for the P&L page [L3]

Shakti YTD October (₹ crore): revenue 660 vs budget 690 (PVM: volume −26, price +4, mix −8, of which Water Purifiers volume −11 due to e-commerce marketplace delisting of two SKUs for six weeks); GM 32.4% vs 34.0% (copper/aluminium −₹7.3 crore, mix −₹3.3 crore); freight −₹3.0 crore (North diesel and lane rates up 9%); employee cost −₹1.0 crore (sales incentive accrual); A&P +₹2.0 crore (Diwali campaign shifted to November). FY LE ₹127.1 crore vs guidance ₹150 crore. (a) Apply materiality thresholds of ₹1.5 crore and 5% to decide which items get commentary. (b) Write the P&L commentary box in pyramid form (maximum 120 words). (c) Write the action log entries arising.

💡 Hint: Decide first which items are timing and which are structural; timing items need a reversal date rather than an action.

Q2. Validate the explanations [L2]

The BU heads' explanations are: Water Purifiers — "market slowdown"; Fans — "timing, Diwali dispatches moved to November"; Logistics — "fuel price increase". Available data: industry water purifier sales (industry body data) grew 6% YoY in Q2; Shakti's e-commerce share of purifier sales fell from 38% to 31%; November fan dispatch plan is ₹40 crore against budget ₹36 crore; diesel prices in the North rose 2% while Shakti's freight cost per unit rose 9%. For each, assess the explanation, state what further evidence you would request and draft the validated explanation.

Q3. SCR CFO note [L3]

Write a 250-word CFO note to the CEO in SCR form recommending three actions to close the gap to guidance, each with ₹ FY27 impact, owner and date, and stating whether guidance should be revised at Q3.

Q4. AI draft review [L2]

An EPM tool generates: "Revenue declined 4.3% versus budget, driven primarily by Water Purifiers, which saw a 9.6% decrease. EBITDA margin contracted by 290 bps. Travel expenses increased significantly. The company should consider cost optimisation." List every weakness and rewrite it to MBR standard using the data in Q1.
