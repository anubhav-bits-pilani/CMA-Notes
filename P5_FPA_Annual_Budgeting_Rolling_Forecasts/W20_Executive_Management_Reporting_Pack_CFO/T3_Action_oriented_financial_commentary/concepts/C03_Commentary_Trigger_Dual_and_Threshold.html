Key concept

Commentary Trigger: Dual ₹ and % Threshold

Concept: Commentary trigger: |Variance| > ₹X lakh and > ±Y% of budget

Statement

A variance requires written commentary when both its absolute size and its relative size exceed predefined thresholds:

\[ \text{Comment required} \iff \lvert V \rvert > X \;\; \text{and} \;\; \frac{\lvert V \rvert}{\lvert B \rvert} > Y \]

where \(V = A - B\) is the variance of the actual \(A\) from budget \(B\) for the line and period (YTD or FY LE, as the policy specifies), \(X\) is the absolute threshold (₹ lakh or crore, set by pack level) and \(Y\) is the relative threshold (% of budget). Many policies add an absolute backstop \(Z > X\): comment whenever \(\lvert V \rvert > Z\), regardless of %.

Explanation

The two tests filter different kinds of noise. The ₹ test excludes items too small to matter to the reader's decisions, however large in % (₹2 lakh overrun on a ₹5 lakh line is 40% but immaterial at entity level). The % test excludes items that are large in ₹ but small relative to the line's size, which are usually normal volatility (₹1.6 crore on a ₹200 crore COGS line is 0.8%). Requiring both focuses commentary on items that are material and unusual.

Absolute values are used so the rule applies symmetrically to favourable and adverse variances — large favourable variances often signal timing, errors or soft budgets and deserve explanation too. The denominator uses \(\lvert B \rvert\) so lines with negative budgets (losses, credits) are handled correctly; if \(B = 0\), apply the ₹ test alone.

Calibration: set \(X\) relative to the profit measure the pack serves (for example 1–3% of the entity's YTD EBITDA budget; lower for BU and cost-centre packs) and \(Y\) according to line volatility (5% typical, 10% for volatile lines). Test the rule on recent months so that the pack has a manageable number of commented items (roughly 8–15 at entity level). Document \(X\), \(Y\), \(Z\), the lens (YTD/LE/month) and boundary convention (> vs ≥) in the methodology appendix.

The trigger is a floor, not a ceiling: qualitatively sensitive items (related parties, regulatory, covenants, fraud indicators, guidance-relevant items) are commented regardless.

Excel:

=IF(OR(AND(ABS(D5-C5)>X, ABS(D5-C5)/ABS(C5)>Y), ABS(D5-C5)>Z), "Comment", "")

with C5 budget, D5 actual, and named cells X, Y, Z; wrap the division in IF(C5=0, …) for zero budgets.

Connections

The trigger implements the materiality policy (s04) and determines where the five-part commentary (s01) is required; the travel example (c01) falls below the entity threshold but above the function threshold. It mirrors the dual-band logic of RAG thresholds (Topic 2 s03) and is implemented with the same Excel and DAX patterns. Variance thresholds are also used in internal audit analytics (SA 520 analytical procedures) to set investigation thresholds.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the trigger [L1]

With \(X\) = ₹1.5 crore, \(Y\) = 5% and \(Z\) = ₹5 crore, determine whether each needs commentary: (a) COGS ₹9.2 crore favourable on budget ₹455.4 crore; (b) IT costs ₹1.6 crore adverse on budget ₹7 crore; (c) other income ₹1.4 crore favourable on budget ₹1.0 crore; (d) depreciation ₹0.8 crore adverse on budget ₹21 crore; (e) exceptional VRS cost ₹3.0 crore on budget nil.

💡 Hint: For (e), decide what the rule does when the budget is zero, and whether qualitative materiality applies.

Q2. Write the DAX measure [L2]

Write a Power BI measure Comment Flag that returns 1 when the trigger is met for the selected line and period, using measures [Actual], [Budget] and a parameter table holding X, Y and Z by pack level, and explain how you would use it to filter a commentary table visual.

Q3. Policy critique [L3]

A divisional CFO proposes OR logic (₹ > X or % > Y) "to be safe". Using Shakti's 42 YTD line variances (18 below ₹0.5 crore, 10 between ₹0.5 crore and ₹1.5 crore, 8 between ₹1.5 crore and ₹5 crore, 6 above ₹5 crore; 21 lines in total exceed 5%), estimate the number of comments under OR vs AND-with-backstop logic and argue for a policy.
