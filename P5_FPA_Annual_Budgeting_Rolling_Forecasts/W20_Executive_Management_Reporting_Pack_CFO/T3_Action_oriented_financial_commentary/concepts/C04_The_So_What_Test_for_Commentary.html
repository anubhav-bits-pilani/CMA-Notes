Key concept

The "So What" Test for Commentary

Concept: "So what" test: every sentence must state business impact or decision needed

Statement

For every sentence of commentary, ask "so what?" — why does this matter to the reader? A sentence passes only if it states (or directly supports a sentence that states) at least one of:

  • a business impact — effect on profit, cash, margin, customers, risk or the full-year outcome; or
  • a decision or action needed — what someone must do, decide or approve, by when.

Sentences that only describe what the numbers are — which the reader can see in the table — fail and should be deleted or rewritten.

Explanation

The "so what" test is the editing tool that converts reporting into insight. It forces the writer to climb from data to meaning: "Revenue was ₹660 crore" (fails) → "Revenue was ₹30 crore below budget" (still descriptive) → "The ₹30 crore revenue shortfall costs ₹10 crore of EBITDA and is the main reason the year will miss guidance" (passes — impact) → "…so we need to decide on the fan price increase this week" (passes — decision).

Applying the test repeatedly ("so what?" → "so what?") usually leads to one of three endpoints: an effect on the full-year result or guidance, an effect on cash or covenants, or a decision required. If repeated asking leads nowhere, the item is not material and should leave the commentary.

The test is especially useful against:

  • Restatement: "Employee cost was ₹57 crore vs ₹56 crore budget." So what? If the answer is "nothing much", delete it.
  • Activity reporting: "The team held 14 distributor meetings." So what? "…which secured ₹6 crore of November orders" passes.
  • Vague concern: "We continue to monitor commodity prices." So what? "A further 5% copper rise would cost ₹4 crore in Q4; we propose hedging 50% of Q4 requirement — decision required by 20 November" passes.

Not every sentence needs its own ₹ figure — a supporting sentence that explains the cause of an impact stated in the previous sentence passes because it supports that impact. The test applies to sentences, not to every clause.

Illustration

Draft (Shakti cash page) with test results:

Sentence So what? Verdict
"Receivables at 31 October are ₹212 crore." Reader sees it in the table Fail — delete
"DSO is 68 days against 60 budget." Partly — size of gap, but no consequence Weak — merge
"The 8-day excess locks up ₹26 crore of cash, mostly in two modern-trade accounts disputing ₹5 crore of scheme claims." Cash impact and cause Pass
"At this level, March net debt will be ₹18 crore above the LE, though covenants remain within limits (1.35× vs 2.5×)." FY and covenant impact Pass
"Credit control is following up." No owner, date or value Fail — rewrite
"Head of Sales MT and Credit Controller to settle the scheme dispute by 30 November; expected collection ₹14 crore by 31 December." Action, owner, date, value Pass

The revised commentary keeps the three passing sentences and is half the length.

Connections

The test underlies the five-part structure (s01) — impact, outlook and action are the "so what" — and the tone rules (s05) that require every material item to end in an outlook or action. It complements SCR (c02), where the complication and resolution are "so what" content, and the Pyramid principle, where each grouping must summarise to a point. It also guards against the topic pitfall of restating numbers without explaining why.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the test [L1]

Apply the "so what" test to each sentence and rewrite those that fail (invent plausible Shakti data where needed, stating your assumptions): (a) "Inventory increased by ₹14 crore during the year." (b) "Fan sales in the South were 6% below budget." (c) "The new ERP system went live on 1 October." (d) "We are watching competitor pricing closely."

Q2. Edit a commentary box [L2]

Edit this 95-word commentary to under 50 words so that every sentence passes: "Gross margin for the period was 32.4% compared to budget of 34.0%. This represents a decrease of 160 bps. The decrease is mainly due to commodity prices. Copper prices have increased during the year. Aluminium prices have also increased. Product mix was also adverse. We sold more entry fans. The procurement team is working on this. Management is reviewing pricing options. The margin was also lower than last year, which was 33.1%. We will continue to monitor the situation closely in the coming months."

💡 Hint: Keep the cause with its ₹ size, the FY consequence and a named action; delete every sentence the table already shows.

Q3. Interview exercise [L2]

An interviewer hands you a one-line variance: "Marketing spend ₹1.2 crore below budget YTD." Ask yourself "so what?" three times and write the resulting two-sentence commentary, stating the assumptions you made at each step.
