Key concept

SCR Framework: Situation, Complication, Resolution

Concept: SCR framework: Situation, Complication, Resolution for executive narratives

Statement

SCR structures an executive narrative in three moves:

  • Situation (S) — the shared, uncontroversial context the reader already accepts (plan, target, starting position).
  • Complication (C) — what has changed or gone wrong (or right) that creates tension and a question.
  • Resolution (R) — the answer: what we propose, what we will do or what we need decided.

The implicit Question raised by the complication ("so what do we do?") is answered by the resolution. Barbara Minto presents SCR (Situation–Complication–Question–Answer) as the introduction to a pyramid-structured document; consultancies use SCR for storylines.

Explanation

SCR works because it mirrors how executives process information: they need context to frame the problem, a clear statement of the problem, and then the answer. It prevents two failures: data without a point (all situation, no complication) and recommendations without context (resolution with no reason).

Applying SCR in FP&A:

  • CFO commentary / executive summary: S = AOP and guidance; C = YTD shortfall, causes and LE gap; R = actions, value, decisions required.
  • Deep-dive pages: S = segment plan; C = the variance and root cause; R = recovery plan.
  • Board papers: S = strategy and approved budget; C = market change; R = proposed revision.

Rules of thumb:

  • Keep S short — one or two sentences; the reader knows the context.
  • Make C specific and quantified — the problem in ₹ and its cause.
  • Make R actionable — options and a recommendation, with owners, dates and ₹ effect.
  • The resolution can be placed first (answer-first, Pyramid style) for very senior audiences: R–S–C ("We recommend X. Because… Situation… Complication…"). Many CFO notes open with the recommendation and then use S and C as support.

Variants: SCQA (adds explicit Question), and "What? So what? Now what?" — a similar three-step frame used for commentary bullets.

Illustration

Shakti CFO note, October 2026 (S–C–R, 110 words):

Situation. We guided ₹150 crore EBITDA for FY27 (12.5% margin), in line with the AOP.

Complication. YTD EBITDA is ₹60.8 crore, ₹22.8 crore below budget: copper and aluminium inflation (₹7.3 crore) has not been priced through, fan mix has shifted to entry models (₹3.3 crore), and Water Purifiers lost six weeks of marketplace sales (₹10 crore revenue). The LE is ₹127.1 crore — ₹23 crore short of guidance.

Resolution. Three actions worth ₹11 crore: 2% copper-indexed price increase on fans from 1 December (₹4.5 crore; Head of Sales Fans); freight re-tender and dispatch consolidation (₹1.5 crore; Head of Logistics, 15 December); A&P re-phasing and premium purifier push (₹5 crore; CMO). Decision required: approve the price increase by 15 November; prepare guidance revision to ₹135–140 crore for the Q3 call.

Connections

SCR is the narrative layer on top of the Pyramid principle (Topic 1 c04, and the related item in this topic); it structures the CFO commentary in the week's exercise and the "decisions required" block of the executive summary (Topic 1 s01). Each sentence in C and R should pass the "so what" test (c04). SCR is also the standard structure for interview case presentations (Week 24).

Practice Questions

Q1. Identify S, C and R [L1]

Label each sentence S, C or R and reorder into an SCR narrative: (1) "We propose to defer Hosur line-2 capex by one quarter." (2) "The AOP assumed ₹80 crore capex funded from operating cash flow." (3) "YTD FCF is −₹21 crore against a budget of +₹41 crore because receivables and inventory have absorbed ₹35 crore." (4) "Deferral releases ₹25 crore of FY27 cash with limited risk to FY28 summer capacity." (5) "Net debt is ₹33 crore above budget."

Q2. Write an SCR deep-dive [L2]

Write an SCR narrative (maximum 120 words) for the Water Purifiers segment page using: AOP revenue ₹240 crore and 14% contribution; YTD revenue ₹122 crore vs ₹135 crore; contribution 10% vs 14%; marketplace delisting for six weeks due to expired BIS documents; relisted 2 October; AMC renewal rate 61% vs 70%; proposed actions: compliance calendar (Head of E-commerce, 30 November), paid visibility ₹0.4 crore, AMC renewal call-centre drive (Head of Service, from 15 November, ₹1.2 crore contribution).

💡 Hint: Keep the situation to one sentence and quantify every element of the complication.

Q3. R-first vs S-first [L2]

Rewrite your Q2 answer in answer-first (R–S–C) order. Explain in two sentences which order you would use for the CEO and which for a newly appointed independent director, and why.
