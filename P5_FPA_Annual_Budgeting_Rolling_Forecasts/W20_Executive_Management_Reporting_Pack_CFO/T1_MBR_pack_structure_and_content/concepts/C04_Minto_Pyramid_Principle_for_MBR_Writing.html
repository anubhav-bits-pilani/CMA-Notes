Key concept

Minto Pyramid Principle for MBR Writing

Concept: Minto Pyramid Principle: answer first, then supporting arguments, then data

Statement

The Minto Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto, McKinsey) structures communication as a hierarchy:

  1. Governing thought (the answer) at the top — the single conclusion the reader most needs.
  2. Key line (supporting arguments) — typically three to five points that together justify the governing thought; they should be MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive) and of the same kind (all reasons, or all steps, or all components).
  3. Supporting data under each argument — facts, numbers, analysis.

Rules: ideas at any level summarise the ideas grouped below them (vertical logic); ideas in a group are logically ordered — by time, structure or importance (horizontal logic); the introduction uses Situation–Complication–Question before the answer.

Explanation

Executives read top-down and stop when they have enough. Traditional finance writing is bottom-up: it lists the data (revenue line, then COGS, then opex) and arrives at the conclusion last, if at all. The pyramid reverses this so the reader gets the answer in the first sentence and can choose how far to descend.

Applying it to the MBR:

  • Pack level: the executive summary is the governing thought and key line; variance and segment pages are supporting arguments; the appendix is data.
  • Page level: each page title is a message, not a label. "Water Purifiers drove 43% of the revenue shortfall as e-commerce share fell 7 pp" rather than "Segment performance — Water Purifiers".
  • Paragraph level: the first sentence of each commentary box is the conclusion; following sentences give cause, impact and action.

Grouping logic matters. Three valid ways to organise the key line for "EBITDA is ₹23 crore below budget": by driver (volume, margin rate, opex), by segment (Kitchen, Fans, Purifiers), or by controllability (market-driven, execution, timing). Pick one; mixing them ("Purifiers volume, copper, and the North region") breaks MECE and confuses the reader.

Common errors: burying the answer in the third paragraph; key-line points that overlap (copper inflation and gross-margin decline are the same thing); more than five points at one level; and "summary" sentences that are only topics ("We look at margins") rather than conclusions.

Illustration

Governing thought: "FY27 EBITDA will land at ₹127 crore, ₹23 crore short of ₹150 crore guidance; three actions worth ₹11 crore are proposed, and we recommend preparing for a revision to ₹135–140 crore at Q3."

Key line (by driver):

  1. Volume shortfall of ₹10 crore, mostly Water Purifiers e-commerce — structural, not timing.
  2. Gross-margin erosion of ₹16 crore from copper/aluminium (₹12 crore) and adverse fan mix (₹4 crore) — not yet priced through.
  3. Opex net favourable ₹3 crore, as A&P savings offset freight and employee overruns.

Data under each: PVM bridge, commodity price chart, segment pages, opex schedule.

Connections

The pyramid is the design logic for the executive summary (s01) and for every page title; it links to the standard layout (c05) and to Topic 3's commentary structure, SCR framework and "so what" test. Week 20's cross-cutting storytelling theme (numbers → drivers → actions → decisions) is the pyramid read upwards. It also underpins interview case presentations in Week 24.

Practice Questions

Q1. Restructure a memo [L2]

A draft MBR commentary reads: "Revenue was ₹660 crore against ₹690 crore. COGS was ₹446 crore. Copper prices rose 14% since March. Freight was ₹3 crore over. A&P was ₹2 crore under. EBITDA was ₹61 crore against ₹84 crore. We think the full year will be ₹127 crore. Guidance was ₹150 crore." Rewrite it as a pyramid: one governing thought, three MECE supporting points, and the data under each.

Q2. MECE check [L1]

Identify the MECE violations in this key line and fix it: (a) "Lower sales in Purifiers"; (b) "Weak e-commerce channel"; (c) "Copper inflation"; (d) "Gross margin decline"; (e) "Opex savings".

💡 Hint: Check which items are sub-causes of others and which grouping principle each item belongs to.

Q3. Page titles [L1]

Write message titles (maximum 15 words each) for: the P&L page, the cash tracker, the capex tracker and the R&O log, using the Shakti October data from this topic.
