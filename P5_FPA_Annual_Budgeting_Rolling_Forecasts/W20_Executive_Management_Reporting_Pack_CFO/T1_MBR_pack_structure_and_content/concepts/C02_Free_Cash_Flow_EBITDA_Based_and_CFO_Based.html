Key concept

Free Cash Flow: EBITDA-Based and CFO-Based Definitions

Concept: Free cash flow = EBITDA − Tax paid − ΔNet working capital − Capex (or CFO − Capex)

Statement

EBITDA-based (management) definition:

\[ \text{FCF} = \text{EBITDA} - \text{Tax paid} - \Delta\text{NWC} - \text{Capex} \]

Cash-flow-statement-based definition:

\[ \text{FCF} = \text{CFO} - \text{Capex} \]

where EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation on the MIS definition; tax paid is cash income tax (not the P&L tax charge); \(\Delta\text{NWC}\) is the increase in net working capital (receivables + inventory + other operating current assets − payables − other operating current liabilities), an increase reducing cash; capex is cash spent on PPE and intangibles (including capital advances, net of disposal proceeds if the definition says so); CFO is net cash from operating activities under Ind AS 7.

Explanation

The two definitions converge when CFO ≈ EBITDA − tax − ΔNWC, i.e. when there are no material non-cash items in EBITDA, no other operating cash flows, and interest is classified outside operating activities. Differences arise from:

  • Interest classification: Ind AS 7.33 permits interest paid to be classified as operating or financing for non-financial entities. If in CFO, CFO-based FCF is after interest (a levered measure); EBITDA-based FCF before interest is an unlevered/pre-financing measure.
  • Leases (Ind AS 116): lease principal and interest are financing outflows, so CFO is higher than under old Ind AS 17. Many companies define FCF as CFO − capex − lease payments to restore comparability.
  • Non-cash items in EBITDA: ESOP charges, provisions, unrealised FX — added back in CFO, so FCF definitions diverge if EBITDA is not adjusted.
  • Exceptional cash items: VRS payments, litigation settlements.

The management (EBITDA-based) form is preferred in the MBR because each term maps to an owner: EBITDA to BU heads, NWC to sales/supply chain, capex to project owners, tax to the tax team. The CFO-based form is preferred for external comparability and reconciles to the statutory cash flow statement.

FCF conversion = FCF ÷ EBITDA is a useful KPI; persistent conversion below 50% in a non-capex-heavy year signals working capital problems.

Illustration

Shakti, YTD October FY27 (₹ crore): EBITDA (MIS, pre-Ind AS 116) 60.8; tax paid 9.0; receivables +31; inventory +14; payables +10; capex spent 38. Lease rentals of ₹7.5 crore are deducted in MIS EBITDA but, under Ind AS 116, the statutory cash flow statement shows lease payments in financing activities, so statutory CFO (interest classified as financing) is higher by ₹7.5 crore: post-Ind AS 116 EBITDA ₹68.3 crore − tax ₹9.0 crore − ΔNWC ₹35.0 crore = ₹24.3 crore.

The two definitions reconcile once lease payments are treated consistently. FCF conversion is negative: working capital absorbed more than half of EBITDA and capex the rest — the executive summary must say so.

Connections

FCF is the cash headline in the executive summary (s01) and the cash bridge on the tracker (s03); ΔNWC is driven by the CCC (c01), and capex by the capex tracker (s04). FCF changes net debt (c03). In valuation (DCF in later weeks), unlevered FCF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − ΔNWC − capex is the related but distinct concept; do not confuse the MBR's management FCF with DCF free cash flow to the firm.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both FCF measures [L1]

FY26 Shakti: EBITDA (post-Ind AS 116) ₹142 crore; lease payments (principal ₹9 crore, interest ₹4 crore); tax paid ₹24 crore; ΔNWC increase ₹18 crore; capex ₹55 crore; interest paid on borrowings ₹16 crore classified as financing. Compute CFO, FCF (CFO − capex), FCF after lease payments and FCF conversion.

Q2. Definition choice for the MBR [L2]

Shakti's lenders use "FCF after interest and lease payments"; investor presentations use "CFO − capex". The CEO wants one FCF on the executive summary. Recommend a definition, write the KPI definition row, and describe the reconciliation you would put in the appendix.

💡 Hint: Consider who acts on the MBR number and how each definition is reconciled to the other.

Q3. Diagnose a cash shortfall [L2]

Budget YTD FCF (EBITDA-based) ₹41 crore; actual −₹21.2 crore. Budget: EBITDA ₹83.6 crore, tax ₹10 crore, ΔNWC ₹8 crore, capex ₹24.6 crore. Using the actuals in the illustration, build the FCF variance bridge and write the two-sentence commentary.
