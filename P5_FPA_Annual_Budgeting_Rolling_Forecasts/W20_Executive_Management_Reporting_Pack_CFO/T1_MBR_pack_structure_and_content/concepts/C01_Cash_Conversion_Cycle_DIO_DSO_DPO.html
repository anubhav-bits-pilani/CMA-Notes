Key concept

Cash Conversion Cycle (DIO + DSO − DPO)

Concept: Cash Conversion Cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO

Statement

\[ \text{CCC} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO} \]

\[ \text{DIO} = \frac{\text{Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times D, \qquad \text{DSO} = \frac{\text{Trade receivables}}{\text{Revenue}} \times D, \qquad \text{DPO} = \frac{\text{Trade payables}}{\text{COGS (or purchases)}} \times D \]

where \(D\) is the number of days in the period whose flow is used as the denominator (365 for annual or annualised flows, 91 or 92 for a quarter), inventory, receivables and payables are closing or average balances (one basis chosen and applied consistently), revenue is revenue from operations and COGS is cost of materials consumed plus purchases of stock-in-trade plus changes in inventories (plus conversion cost if the company defines COGS that way).

Explanation

The CCC measures how many days cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Inventory sits for DIO days, the sale takes DSO days to collect, but suppliers fund DPO days of that. A lower CCC means less working capital per rupee of sales and therefore more free cash flow for the same profit. FMCG companies with strong distributor terms and supplier credit often run near-zero or negative CCC; capital goods and consumer durables typically run 60–120 days.

Conditions and conventions that matter:

  • Consistent denominators: DSO uses revenue; DIO and DPO use COGS (DPO on purchases is more precise when inventory levels change materially). Mixing bases makes the arithmetic meaningless.
  • Annualisation in monthly packs: trailing three months × 4 or count-back methods are better than YTD annualised in seasonal businesses.
  • GST: receivables and payables include GST while revenue and COGS do not; adjust or define consistently.
  • Average vs closing: average smooths noise; closing reflects the current position. Lenders and analysts usually use closing year-end balances.
  • Exclusions: advances to suppliers, customer advances, capital creditors, and non-trade receivables are excluded from the classic CCC but affect NWC; show them separately.

Illustration

Shakti, 31 October 2026 (₹ crore). T3M revenue ₹296 crore, T3M COGS ₹200 crore, annualised × 4. Receivables (excl. GST) ₹180 crore; inventory ₹168 crore; trade payables (excl. GST) ₹103 crore.

Component Calculation Days
DSO 180 ÷ 1,184 × 365 55.5
DIO 168 ÷ 800 × 365 76.7
DPO 103 ÷ 800 × 365 47.0
CCC 55.5 + 76.7 − 47.0 85.2

If the target CCC is 75 days, the ₹ cash opportunity by component is: DSO cut of 5 days × ₹3.24 crore/day ≈ ₹16 crore; DIO cut of 5 days × ₹2.19 crore/day ≈ ₹11 crore. One CCC day is not worth the same in each component because the daily denominators differ.

Connections

The CCC is the headline metric of the working capital and cash tracker (s03) and drives the ΔNWC term in free cash flow (c02) and therefore net debt (c03). It links to Phase 2 ratio analysis, to Topic 4 business partnering (working capital release = Δdays × daily revenue or COGS), and to the KPI scorecard in Topic 2 where DSO, DIO and CCC are lower-is-better KPIs needing inverted attainment. In 3-statement models, DSO/DIO/DPO are the standard drivers for forecasting receivables, inventory and payables.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute CCC and cash release [L1]

FY26 Shakti: revenue ₹1,080 crore, COGS ₹713 crore; closing receivables ₹170 crore, inventory ₹150 crore, payables ₹112 crore (all excluding GST). Compute DSO, DIO, DPO and CCC. If FY27 targets are DSO 55, DIO 72, DPO 60 on FY27 revenue ₹1,200 crore and COGS ₹792 crore, compute the target balances and the working capital change.

Q2. Purchases vs COGS for DPO [L2]

In Q3, Shakti builds summer inventory: opening inventory ₹150 crore, closing ₹210 crore, quarterly COGS ₹195 crore, closing payables ₹128 crore. Compute DPO using COGS and using purchases for the quarter (92 days), and explain which better represents supplier credit during a stock build.

💡 Hint: Purchases = COGS + closing inventory − opening inventory.

Q3. Interview question on negative CCC [L2]

An interviewer asks: "A quick-commerce company reports a CCC of −25 days. Is that good, and what are the risks for its FP&A team?" Prepare a structured three-point answer with a numerical example.
