Key concept

Net Debt and Net Debt/EBITDA

Concept: Net debt = Borrowings + Lease liabilities (if included) − Cash and liquid investments; Net debt/EBITDA

Statement

\[ \text{Net debt} = B + L - C \]

\[ \text{Leverage} = \frac{\text{Net debt}}{\text{EBITDA}_{\text{LTM}}} \]

where \(B\) is total borrowings (non-current and current, including current maturities of long-term debt, working capital loans, commercial paper, NCDs, ECBs and accrued interest if the definition requires), \(L\) is lease liabilities under Ind AS 116 (included or excluded by definition), \(C\) is cash and cash equivalents plus liquid investments (bank deposits, liquid and overnight mutual funds, treasury bills) that are freely available, and \(\text{EBITDA}_{\text{LTM}}\) is last-twelve-months EBITDA on a definition consistent with the treatment of leases.

Explanation

Net debt measures the debt a company would still owe if it used its free cash to repay borrowings. Net debt/EBITDA expresses this in years of operating earnings and is the most common leverage covenant in Indian term loans and NCD trust deeds (typical thresholds 2.5×–3.5× for mid-caps), and a key rating metric.

Definitional consistency is everything:

  • Leases in or out: if lease liabilities are in net debt, EBITDA must be post-Ind AS 116 (rent excluded from opex). If leases are out, EBITDA must be pre-Ind AS 116 (rent deducted). Mixing — leases out of debt but post-116 EBITDA — understates leverage. Most Indian bank covenants signed before 2019 use pre-Ind AS 116 ("frozen GAAP") definitions.
  • Cash quality: exclude restricted cash (margin money, earmarked balances, unpaid dividend accounts), cash in subsidiaries subject to exchange controls, and investments that are not liquid (equity shares, long-dated bonds) unless the definition allows.
  • Debt-like items: lenders and analysts may add supplier finance, recourse factoring, guarantees, preference shares classified as liabilities under Ind AS 32, deferred consideration on acquisitions, and unfunded employee benefit obligations.
  • Point in time vs LTM: net debt is a balance at a date; EBITDA is a flow over twelve months. Seasonal businesses (fans peak in March–May) can show very different leverage at different quarter-ends; covenants are usually tested on specified dates.
  • Negative net debt = net cash; leverage is then not meaningful and is reported as "net cash".

Illustration

Shakti, 31 October 2026 (₹ crore): term loans ₹110 crore; working capital demand loans ₹60 crore; commercial paper ₹15 crore; lease liabilities ₹42 crore; cash ₹14 crore; liquid mutual funds ₹22 crore; fixed deposit under lien for a bank guarantee ₹5 crore. LTM EBITDA post-Ind AS 116 ₹128 crore; pre-Ind AS 116 ₹116 crore.

Connections

Net debt and leverage sit on the cash tracker's covenant block (s03) and the executive summary headline (s01); FCF (c02) is the main driver of the movement in net debt month to month. Lease treatment links to Phase 1 Ind AS 116 work; covenant classification consequences link to Ind AS 1 current/non-current rules. In valuation (DCF weeks), net debt is the bridge from enterprise value to equity value, again requiring a consistent treatment of leases and debt-like items.

Practice Questions

Q1. Net debt with debt-like items [L2]

Using the illustration, a rating agency adds: recourse factoring ₹18 crore, supplier finance arrangements ₹12 crore and a ₹10 crore deferred consideration on an acquisition. Compute the adjusted net debt and leverage on both lease bases, and comment on the covenant vs rating view.

Q2. Seasonal leverage [L2]

Shakti's forecast net debt (excl. leases) is ₹149 crore at October, ₹205 crore at 31 December (summer inventory build) and ₹120 crore at 31 March. LTM pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA: ₹116 crore (October), ₹112 crore (December), ₹122 crore (March). The covenant ≤ 2.5× is tested quarterly. Compute leverage at each date and the minimum EBITDA required at December.

💡 Hint: Minimum EBITDA = Net debt ÷ covenant multiple.

Q3. Definition interview question [L1]

"Why can two analysts quote different Net debt/EBITDA multiples for the same NSE-listed company on the same day?" List at least four reasons with a one-line explanation each.
