Key concept

Standard Layout: Same Page Order and Format Every Month

Concept: Standard layout: same page order and format every month for comparability

Statement

A standard MBR layout fixes, for the whole financial year (and ideally across years):

  • the page order (e.g. 1 Executive summary, 2 KPI scorecard, 3 P&L, 4 EBITDA bridge, 5–7 Segments, 8 Cash and WC, 9 Capex, 10 R&O, 11 Action log, A1–A9 Appendix);
  • the page template (title as message, chart area, table area, commentary box, footnotes in the same positions);
  • the column order (Actual | Budget | Var | Var % | PY | Var vs PY | LE | AOP | Var);
  • units, scaling, sign convention and colour rules;
  • the definitions of every metric (appendix) and the version of the template.

Changes are made only through a controlled template version with an effective date and restated comparatives.

Explanation

Comparability is what turns a monthly pack into a time series. When the executive team sees the same page in the same place every month, they read changes, not layouts; they know where the DSO is and immediately notice it has moved. Readers spend their attention on content rather than navigation, meeting time is shorter, and anomalies stand out.

A standard layout also enforces discipline on the producer: the pack cannot omit the cash page in a bad cash month, cannot drop the R&O log when risks are uncomfortable, and cannot switch from YTD to month comparisons to flatter a trend. It makes automation feasible: a fixed template can be populated from the model or from Power BI paginated reports with minimal manual work, which cuts close-to-pack time and errors.

Exceptions are handled without breaking the structure: a one-off deep-dive (e.g. an acquisition integration update) is added as a clearly labelled "special topic" page after the core pages, not inserted between them. Where a page is not relevant in a month (no capex activity), keep it with a single line "No movement" rather than removing it.

Format rules worth fixing explicitly:

Element Standard
Units ₹ crore, one decimal; margins in %, variances in bps
Signs F/A convention; adverse in brackets and red
Periods Oct-26, YTD Oct-26, FY27 LE (Oct), FY27 AOP
Charts IBCS notation: actual solid dark, AOP outlined, PY grey, forecast hatched
Commentary Max 5 bullets per page; each with ₹ impact, cause, owner and date
Footnotes Data source, cut-off date, definition reference

Illustration

Shakti's pack in August placed the cash tracker on page 9; in September a new controller moved it to the appendix as "less relevant this month", and changed DSO from closing to average receivables. The September executive summary reported DSO "improved to 61 days" (average basis) from 66 days (closing basis in August), when on a consistent closing basis it had worsened to 68 days. The CEO approved a dealer credit extension on the strength of the "improvement". A locked template and a definitions appendix would have prevented both changes.

Connections

Standard layout operationalises the consistency cross-cutting theme of the week and the topic pitfall on inconsistent definitions. It relies on the KPI definitions in the appendix (s07) and the Topic 2 KPI definition sheet, follows IBCS conventions (related item), and is implemented in the Power BI paginated reports of Weeks 11–12. It is also a model-integrity issue for Topic 5: a fixed template makes tie-out checks repeatable.

Q2. Handling a structural change [L3]

From January, Shakti will split Fans & Lighting into two segments following a change in CODM reporting. Describe how you would change the MBR layout while preserving comparability: which pages change, how PY and AOP are restated, what you footnote, and how you communicate the change.

💡 Hint: Think about Ind AS 108 restatement of comparatives and about keeping a bridge between old and new structures for at least one quarter.

List five automated or manual checks a reviewer can run each month to confirm the pack follows the standard layout and definitions.
