Working Capital and Cash Tracker Page

Scope: Working capital and cash tracker: CCC, overdue receivables ageing, inventory days, net debt, covenants

Why it matters

Profit is an opinion, cash is a fact. Indian mid-caps rarely fail on EBITDA; they fail when receivables stretch, inventory piles up ahead of a weak season, and lenders call a covenant. The cash tracker is the page that makes working capital and leverage visible to the CEO every month, and it is the page treasury, credit control and supply chain heads use to agree actions. Lenders, rating agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE) and equity analysts all focus on the same metrics: CCC, FCF, Net debt/EBITDA and covenant headroom.

Core notes

Page layout

A standard cash tracker has five blocks:

  1. Cash headline: opening net debt → EBITDA → tax → ΔNWC → capex → lease payments → interest → dividends → closing net debt (a cash bridge), Month and YTD vs budget.
  2. Working capital days: DSO, DIO, DPO and CCC, trend for 13 months, vs budget and PY.
  3. Receivables ageing and overdues: by bucket and by channel/top customers.
  4. Inventory: days by category, ageing, slow-moving and non-moving stock, provisions.
  5. Net debt and covenants: gross debt, leases, cash, net debt, Net debt/EBITDA, DSCR, interest cover, headroom.

Cash conversion cycle and its components

\[ \text{CCC} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO} \]

where DSO = Trade receivables ÷ Revenue × 365, DIO = Inventory ÷ COGS × 365 and DPO = Trade payables ÷ COGS (or purchases) × 365 (concept note c01 covers definitions). In a monthly tracker, the denominator must be annualised consistently. Three common methods:

  • YTD annualised: YTD revenue × 12 ÷ months elapsed. Simple but distorted in seasonal businesses.
  • Trailing three months (T3M) × 4: reflects current run-rate; preferred for monthly tracking.
  • Count-back (exhaustion) method: subtract each month's revenue from receivables going backwards until the balance is exhausted; the number of days used is DSO. Most accurate for seasonal sales.

Illustration (Shakti, 31 October 2026, ₹ crore, YTD annualised; YTD revenue ₹660 crore, COGS ₹446.2 crore, 7 months):

Metric Balance Annualised denominator Days Budget PY
DSO Receivables 212 Revenue 1,131.4 68.4 60 63
DIO Inventory 168 COGS 764.9 80.2 75 78
DPO Payables 121 COGS 764.9 57.7 55 56
CCC 90.9 80 85

Cash locked by the CCC slippage of about 11 days against budget is roughly 11 × (1,131.4 ÷ 365) ≈ ₹34 crore if measured on revenue. More precisely, compute it component by component: DSO excess 8.4 days × ₹3.10 crore/day = ₹26 crore; DIO excess 5.2 days × ₹2.10 crore/day = ₹11 crore; DPO benefit 2.7 days × ₹2.10 crore/day = ₹6 crore; net ₹31 crore.

GST and denominator consistency

Trade receivables include GST (typically 18% on appliances) while revenue from operations is net of GST under Ind AS 115. DSO computed on gross receivables against net revenue overstates days by roughly the GST rate. Either gross-up revenue by the effective GST rate or state the convention in the KPI definition and apply it consistently. The same applies to payables (input GST) and to export receivables (zero-rated).

Overdue receivables ageing

Ageing by days past due (not days since invoice) is what drives collection action and the Ind AS 109 expected credit loss (ECL) provision matrix.

1–30 32 15% 2% 0.6

61–90 18 8% 15% 2.7
> 90 20 9% 40% 8.0
Total 212 100% 12.7

Report overdue > 60 days (₹38 crore) against a target, the top 10 overdue customers with owner and promised dates, and disputed vs undisputed amounts. Schedule III also requires a receivables ageing schedule in the annual financial statements (from date of transaction/due date, with disputed and undisputed split) — align the MBR buckets with it where practical. MSME payables deserve a separate line: payments beyond 45 days to registered micro and small enterprises are disallowed under Sec 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act until paid, and interest is payable under the MSMED Act.

Inventory days and quality

DIO alone hides composition. Show inventory by stage (RM, WIP, FG, stock-in-trade, in-transit), by category and by age (0–90, 91–180, 181–365, > 365 days), with the Ind AS 2 write-down provision for net realisable value. Seasonal businesses should compare DIO with the same month PY rather than with year-end; fans inventory builds from January for the summer season.

Net debt and covenants

\[ \text{Net debt} = \text{Borrowings} + \text{Lease liabilities (if included)} - \text{Cash and liquid investments} \]

Shakti: borrowings ₹185 crore + leases ₹42 crore − cash and liquid funds ₹36 crore = ₹191 crore (management definition); ₹149 crore excluding leases (bank definition). Covenant: Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5× and DSCR ≥ 1.5×. With bank-definition LTM EBITDA (pre-Ind AS 116) of ₹116 crore, leverage is 1.28×, headroom against 2.5× is ₹141 crore of additional net debt or a ₹56 crore EBITDA fall (₹149 ÷ 2.5 = ₹59.6 crore minimum EBITDA). Always compute covenants exactly as defined in the facility agreement (definitions of EBITDA, debt, test dates, LTM basis) and show forward-looking headroom using the LE.

Covenant Threshold Actual Oct Forecast Mar Headroom (Mar) RAG
Net debt / EBITDA ≤ 2.50× 1.28× 1.35× ₹ EBITDA can fall ₹52 crore G
DSCR ≥ 1.50× 1.9× 1.7× 13% A
Interest cover ≥ 3.0× 5.2× 4.8× 38% G

Classification consequences are real. Under Ind AS 1.74, a breach of a long-term loan covenant on or before the reporting date makes the liability current — but the Indian version carves out from IAS 1: the loan is not reclassified if the lender agrees, after the reporting date but before the financial statements are approved for issue, not to demand payment as a consequence of the breach (IAS 1 requires the waiver by the reporting date). Whether MCA has notified amendments mirroring the IASB's 2020/2022 "non-current liabilities with covenants" changes is date-sensitive — check the current notified text. Either way, the tracker gives management months of early warning to seek a waiver or reset.

Free cash flow and cash bridge

FCF = EBITDA − tax paid − ΔNWC − capex (concept c02). The cash bridge from opening to closing net debt is the most effective visual: it shows whether a rise in net debt came from operations, investment or financing.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Channel financing and factoring: receivables sold without recourse leave the balance sheet (Ind AS 109 derecognition), flattering DSO. Show DSO before and after factoring and treat recourse factoring as debt.
  • Supplier finance arrangements: extended DPO via reverse factoring may be debt-like; Ind AS 7 and Ind AS 107 amendments (supplier finance disclosures) require disclosure. Some lenders include them in net debt.
  • Restricted cash, margin money and unpaid dividend accounts are not "free" cash; exclude from net debt cash.
  • Advances to suppliers and customer advances affect NWC but not CCC as usually defined; show them as a separate line.
  • Month-end window dressing (pushing collections, delaying payments) shows up as volatility around quarter-ends; compare average daily balances.

Key takeaways

  • Five blocks: cash bridge, WC days, receivables ageing, inventory quality, net debt and covenants.
  • Choose one annualisation method (T3M or count-back preferred) and apply it every month.
  • Age receivables by days past due and tie buckets to the ECL matrix and Schedule III ageing.
  • Translate day movements into ₹ cash using daily revenue or COGS.
  • Compute covenants on the lender's definitions and show forward headroom on the LE.
  • Watch MSME payables (Sec 43B(h)), factoring and supplier finance for distortions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute WC days by two methods [L2]

Shakti revenue (₹ crore): Aug 92, Sep 104, Oct 100; COGS: Aug 62, Sep 70, Oct 68. Closing 31 October: receivables ₹212 crore (including 18% GST), inventory ₹168 crore, payables ₹121 crore. Compute DSO, DIO, DPO and CCC using (a) T3M × 4 with gross receivables, and (b) T3M × 4 with receivables grossed down for GST. Comment on the difference.

Q2. Count-back DSO [L2]

Receivables at 31 October ₹212 crore (net of GST basis). Monthly revenue: Oct ₹100 crore (31 days), Sep ₹104 crore (30 days), Aug ₹92 crore (31 days), Jul ₹88 crore (31 days). Compute DSO using the count-back method and compare with the YTD-annualised DSO of 68.4 days.

💡 Hint: Exhaust whole months first, then convert the remaining balance into a fraction of the next month's days.

Q3. Covenant headroom and Ind AS 1 [L3]

The facility requires Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5× tested on 31 March on LTM pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA. Forecast 31 March: net debt (excl. leases) ₹165 crore, LTM EBITDA ₹122 crore. Downside case: EBITDA falls by ₹30 crore and net debt rises by ₹25 crore. (a) Compute leverage in base and downside. (b) What EBITDA level triggers a breach in the downside? (c) Discuss classification and disclosure implications under Ind AS 1 if a breach occurs on 31 March and the waiver is received on 20 April.

Q4. Ageing action plan [L2]

Overdue > 60 days is ₹38 crore, of which modern trade ₹21 crore (two customers ₹14 crore, one disputing ₹5 crore of scheme claims), distributors ₹12 crore and institutional ₹5 crore. Draft the receivables section of the cash tracker: table, three actions with owners and dates, and the expected cash collection by 31 December.

Q5. Factoring distortion [L2]

In October Shakti factored ₹25 crore of receivables without recourse at a 1.2% discount. Restate DSO as if no factoring had occurred (YTD annualised revenue ₹1,131.4 crore) and explain how you would present this on the tracker.
