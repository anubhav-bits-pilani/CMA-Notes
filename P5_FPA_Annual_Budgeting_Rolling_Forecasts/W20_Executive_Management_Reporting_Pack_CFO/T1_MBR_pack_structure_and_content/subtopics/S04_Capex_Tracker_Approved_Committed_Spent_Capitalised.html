Subtopic note

Capex Tracker: Approved, Committed, Spent, Capitalised

Scope: Capex tracker: approved vs committed vs spent vs capitalised, project status

Why it matters

Capex is usually the largest discretionary cash outflow after working capital, and the place where FCF forecasts most often go wrong: projects slip, costs overrun, and spend is phased in the last quarter. A capex tracker gives the CFO a single view of where every rupee of the capex budget sits in its life cycle, which projects are late or over budget, and when assets will start depreciating. It is also the basis for capital work-in-progress (CWIP) disclosures, Ind AS 16 capitalisation reviews, Ind AS 36 impairment indicators and the post-investment reviews covered in Topic 4 and Week 21.

Core notes

The capex life cycle — five stages

Stage Meaning Trigger / evidence Accounting effect
AOP / budgeted Amount included in the annual capex plan Board-approved AOP None
Approved Project sanctioned through the capex approval process (CAR/AFE) Signed capital appropriation request per delegation of authority None
Committed Purchase orders/contracts issued; legally or practically obligated Released PO, signed EPC contract None in books; disclose capital commitments (Ind AS 16 and Schedule III "contingent liabilities and commitments")
Spent Goods/services received or advances paid GRN, invoice, milestone certificate, capital advance CWIP (Ind AS 16) or capital advances (other non-current assets)
Capitalised Asset ready for intended use and transferred from CWIP to PPE Commissioning certificate, trial run completion PPE; depreciation starts (Ind AS 16.55)

Key relationships: Approved ≤ AOP (or a variance needs approval); Committed ≤ Approved; Spent ≤ Committed (for disciplined procurement); Capitalised ≤ Spent. The gaps between stages each tell a story:

  • AOP − Approved: projects not yet sanctioned — potential savings or delays.
  • Approved − Committed: approved but not ordered — procurement delays, re-scoping.
  • Committed − Spent: open orders — future cash outflow (critical for the cash forecast).
  • Spent − Capitalised: CWIP — assets not yet in use; ageing matters.

Forecast and cash phasing

The tracker must forecast the full-year spend by project and phase it by month for the cash forecast. A typical pattern is back-ending: 48% spent by October, 100% "forecast" by March. Challenge forecasts with commitment data: spend in the next five months cannot realistically exceed open commitments plus what can be ordered and delivered in time. For Shakti, open commitments are ₹13 crore (51 − 38), so forecasting ₹42 crore of further spend requires ₹29 crore of new orders delivered by March — unlikely. A realistic FY spend forecast might be ₹60–65 crore, which releases ₹15–20 crore of FCF versus AOP but delays capacity.

Project status reporting

For each material project, show:

  • Budget vs estimate at completion (EAC) — cost overrun and its cause.
  • Schedule — planned vs forecast commissioning date, critical-path issues.
  • Benefits — expected incremental EBITDA and start date (links to Topic 4's capex ROI and Week 21's NPV/IRR).
  • RAG on cost, schedule and benefits separately.
  • Owner and next milestone.

Accounting checkpoints the tracker supports

  • Capitalisation date (Ind AS 16.20/16.55): costs stop being capitalised and depreciation starts when the asset is capable of operating as intended by management, not when it is "fully utilised". Delayed capitalisation in the tracker understates depreciation.
  • Directly attributable costs (Ind AS 16.16–17): installation, testing (net of sale proceeds from trial production — under the 2022 amendment, proceeds from selling items produced before intended use go to P&L, not deducted from cost), site preparation, borrowing costs on qualifying assets (Ind AS 23).
  • Capex vs opex: software SaaS configuration costs are generally expensed (IFRIC agenda decision on cloud computing arrangements), which affects the SAP project.
  • CWIP ageing: Schedule III (2021 amendments) requires CWIP ageing (< 1 year, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years) and a completion schedule for projects overdue or over budget. The tracker should produce this directly.
  • Impairment indicators (Ind AS 36): long-stalled CWIP or abandoned projects.
  • Capital commitments: Schedule III requires disclosure of estimated amounts of contracts remaining to be executed on capital account — the "committed − spent" figure, adjusted for advances.

Excel implementation

Keep a project master with one row per project/PO and columns for each stage; derive stage totals with SUMIFS. A status flag:

=IF(AND(EAC<=Approved*1.05, ForecastDate<=PlanDate+30), "G", IF(OR(EAC>Approved*1.10, ForecastDate>PlanDate+90), "R", "A"))

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capital advances are "spent" in cash but not in CWIP; show them separately so cash and accounting views reconcile.
  • Leased assets (Ind AS 116) are not capex in cash terms but add right-of-use assets and lease liabilities; decide whether the tracker includes them and keep it consistent with the net debt definition.
  • Re-approved or re-scoped projects: keep original and revised approvals visible; silent re-baselining hides overruns.
  • Foreign-currency imports: committed amounts move with FX; show at budget rate and actual rate.
  • Capex funded by government grants (PLI, state subsidies): show gross capex and grant separately (Ind AS 20).

Key takeaways

  • Track every project across AOP, approved, committed, spent and capitalised; each gap has a meaning.
  • Use open commitments to sanity-check the back-ended spend forecast and the cash forecast.
  • Report cost, schedule and benefit RAG with EAC and forecast commissioning date per project.
  • Capitalisation timing drives depreciation; the tracker supports Ind AS 16, Ind AS 23 and Schedule III CWIP ageing disclosures.
  • Separate capital advances and leases to reconcile cash and accounting views.

Practice Questions

Q1. Stage analysis [L1]

For Shakti's capex table above, compute for each project the uncommitted approval, open commitments and CWIP added this year. Identify the two projects with the largest future cash outflow in FY27.

Q2. Capitalisation date and depreciation [L2]

The Roorkee purifier line (cost ₹12 crore, useful life 10 years, straight-line, nil residual) completed trial runs on 5 September 2026 and was able to operate as intended. Production started on 20 October because of a raw-material delay. The plant team recorded capitalisation on 1 November. Compute the FY27 depreciation under the correct and the recorded dates and pass the correcting journal entry. Trial production sold for ₹0.4 crore: state its treatment.

Q3. Spend forecast challenge [L2]

The capex forecast submitted by plants shows FY27 spend of ₹80 crore. Open commitments are ₹13 crore; typical PO-to-delivery lead time is 4–6 months for imported machinery and 1–2 months for local items; ₹10 crore of the remaining approved budget is imported machinery and ₹19 crore local. Build a realistic FY spend forecast with assumptions and quantify the FCF impact against AOP.

💡 Hint: Only items ordered by about November can realistically be received by March if lead times are four months or more.

Q4. Board query on CWIP [L3]

An independent director notes that CWIP includes ₹6 crore for a Pune warehouse automation project approved in FY24 and paused since August 2025. Draft the capex tracker note and the accounting assessment (Ind AS 16, Ind AS 36 and Schedule III CWIP ageing), and recommend a course of action.
