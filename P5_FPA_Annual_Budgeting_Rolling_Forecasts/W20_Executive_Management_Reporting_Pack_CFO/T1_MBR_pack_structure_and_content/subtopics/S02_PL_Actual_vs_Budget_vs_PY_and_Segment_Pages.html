Subtopic note

P&L Actual vs Budget vs PY and Segment Pages

Scope: P&L Actual vs Budget vs PY for month, YTD and LE; segment/BU performance pages

Why it matters

The P&L page is the evidentiary core of the MBR: every message on the executive summary about revenue, margin and EBITDA is proved here. Segment and BU pages then show where the variance sits and who owns it. These pages are scrutinised hardest by CXOs and, in condensed form, by the Audit Committee before quarterly results, so they must reconcile to the ledger and to the Ind AS 108 segment note. Building them correctly — three comparison bases, three time lenses, consistent signs — is a daily FP&A skill.

Core notes

The three comparison bases

  • Actual vs Budget (AOP): measures performance against the commitment; drives accountability and incentive plans.
  • Actual vs Prior Year (PY): measures real growth and trend, independent of how the budget was set. A favourable budget variance with a decline vs PY signals a soft budget or a market decline.
  • LE vs AOP (and vs prior LE): measures where the year will land and how the outlook is moving.

Each answers a different question, so a page showing only one is incomplete (see topic pitfall on budget-only comparisons).

The three time lenses

  • Month: noisy (phasing, timing of dispatches, one-off accruals) but useful for spotting turns early.
  • YTD: smooths noise; the basis for most variance commentary.
  • Full year (LE): LE = YTD actual + YTG forecast. From Month 4–5 onwards this is the most decision-relevant lens.

Some packs add a rolling quarter or trailing twelve months (TTM) column for trend, and a QTD column in quarter-end months to support results preparation.

Sign conventions and variance %

Use the favourable/adverse (F/A) convention: revenue and margin variance = Actual − Budget; cost variance = Budget − Actual. Favourable is positive, adverse in brackets. Variance % = Variance ÷ Budget. Margin variances are in basis points, not percentages of percentages (32.4% vs 34.0% is −160 bps, not −4.7%). Colour follows favourability. In Excel, a sign flag per row avoids hand-editing:

=(Act-Bud)*SignFlag      ' SignFlag = +1 for revenue/margin rows, -1 for cost rows

Ind AS and Schedule III alignment

Management P&L lines differ from Schedule III (e.g. "freight" and "A&P" sit inside "other expenses"; employee cost may include contract labour that is statutorily in other expenses). Keep a mapping table from GL to MIS line and a reconciliation from MIS EBITDA to reported profit. Key reconciling items: Ind AS 116 lease rentals (pre-116 MIS EBITDA deducts rent; reported EBITDA does not), other income (often excluded from operating EBITDA), exceptional items, forex gains/losses on borrowings, and Ind AS 115 adjustments such as variable consideration (schemes and rebates netted from revenue under Ind AS 115 but sometimes shown as "trade spend" in MIS).

Segment / BU performance pages

Each segment page mirrors the P&L page at a lower level and adds operating drivers:

  1. Segment P&L summary: revenue, contribution margin (revenue − variable costs − direct fixed costs), segment EBITDA after allocated costs, for Month/YTD/LE vs Budget and PY.
  2. Revenue drivers: volume (units), average realisation per unit, channel mix (general trade, modern trade, e-commerce, exports), new product share.
  3. PVM bridge for revenue and contribution (from the Week 19 engine).
  4. Operating KPIs: e.g. for Water Purifiers — installed base, service revenue, AMC renewal %; for Fans — premium mix %, BEE star-rated share.
  5. Commentary with root cause, owner and action.

Allocation discipline

Segment pages should distinguish controllable results (contribution after direct costs, which the BU head owns) from allocated corporate costs (shared services, corporate brand). Allocation keys (revenue, headcount, square feet, usage) must be stable and disclosed in the appendix. Changing keys mid-year distorts segment trends; if unavoidable, restate PY and budget.

Implementation

In Power BI, a single matrix visual with a Scenario dimension (Actual, Budget, PY, LE) and a Lens calculation group (Month, YTD, FY) avoids duplicate measures. Example DAX for YTD with an April fiscal year:

Revenue YTD = TOTALYTD ( [Revenue], 'Date'[Date], "31/3" )
Revenue PY YTD = CALCULATE ( [Revenue YTD], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR ( 'Date'[Date] ) )

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Budget phasing errors: a monthly variance caused by phasing (budget spread evenly but sales seasonal) is a timing variance; YTD and LE views neutralise it. Fix the phasing in next year's AOP rather than explaining it every month.
  • Acquisitions and disposals: show like-for-like (organic) growth vs PY separately from inorganic contribution.
  • Segment re-organisation: if the CODM changes the segment structure mid-year, Ind AS 108 requires restatement of comparatives where practicable; do the same in the MBR.
  • PY restated for errors (Ind AS 8): use the restated PY and footnote it.
  • Inter-segment sales: eliminate at the total level; show segment revenue including inter-segment with a separate elimination row, consistent with Ind AS 108 disclosures.

Key takeaways

  • Three bases (Budget, PY, LE) answer three different questions; show all three.
  • Three lenses (Month, YTD, FY LE); the FY LE dominates from mid-year.
  • Use F/A sign convention with margin variances in bps and colour by favourability.
  • Keep a GL-to-MIS mapping and reconcile MIS EBITDA to Schedule III/Ind AS reported profit.
  • Segment pages show controllable contribution, drivers, PVM and owner commentary, and reconcile to entity EBITDA.

Practice Questions

Q1. Variance % and bps [L1]

Fans & Lighting YTD: revenue ₹236 crore (budget ₹245 crore, PY ₹222 crore); contribution ₹33.0 crore (budget ₹39.2 crore, PY ₹34.6 crore). Compute revenue variance ₹ and % vs budget and vs PY, contribution margin % for all three, and the margin variance in bps vs budget and PY.

Q2. Reported vs MIS EBITDA reconciliation [L2]

MIS EBITDA YTD is ₹60.8 crore, computed after deducting rent of ₹7.5 crore on leases accounted for under Ind AS 116, and excluding other income of ₹4.2 crore and an exceptional VRS charge of ₹3.0 crore (shown below EBITDA). Reported EBITDA in the investor presentation includes other income and is post-Ind AS 116. Prepare the reconciliation and explain which number should go on the executive summary.

Q3. Phasing vs real variance [L2]

Kitchen Appliances' FY27 AOP is ₹540 crore. The October budget of ₹50 crore and November budget of ₹46 crore were derived by spreading the AOP evenly (₹45 crore a month) plus ad hoc festive uplifts. The five-year average seasonal profile is October 8.5% and November 10.5% of annual sales. October actual is ₹49 crore and the November dispatch plan is ₹58 crore. Recompute the October and November budgets on the seasonal profile and determine whether the October variance is a volume or a timing variance.

💡 Hint: Compare the month and the two-month cumulative view before classifying.

Q4. Design a segment page [L3]

Design the Water Purifiers segment page for Shakti: list the visuals and tables, their order, the five operating KPIs you would show and the questions the page must answer. Revenue YTD ₹122 crore (budget ₹135 crore, PY ₹126 crore); e-commerce share fell from 38% to 31%; AMC renewal rate 61% (target 70%).

Q5. Allocation key change [L2]

Corporate costs of ₹38.8 crore YTD were allocated by revenue in the budget. From October, management wants to allocate by headcount (Kitchen 40%, Fans & Lighting 35%, Purifiers 25%). Revenue shares YTD are 45.8%, 35.8% and 18.5%. Compute the allocation under both keys and explain how you would present the change in the MBR without distorting trends.
