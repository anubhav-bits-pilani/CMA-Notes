Subtopic note

Executive Deck Storyline: Summary to Decisions

Scope: Executive deck storyline: summary, performance, drivers, outlook, risks, decisions

Why it matters

The executive deck is the part of Project 6 that the CFO, CEO or board actually reads. The model and the MBR pack are the evidence; the deck is the argument. Senior audiences give a deck a few minutes and decide from page 1 whether finance has a point of view. A storyline that moves from summary to decisions — rather than from P&L line 1 to line 40 — makes the analysis usable and makes the presenter look like a business partner rather than a reporter. It is also the format of the Saturday assessment: present the MBR and recommend three margin-recovery actions to hit full-year EBITDA guidance.

Core notes

The six-part storyline

Part Question answered Typical content Slides
1. Summary What is the answer? Headline numbers, 3–5 key messages, LE vs AOP/guidance, decisions required 1
2. Performance What happened? Month and YTD P&L actual vs budget vs PY, segment highlights, KPI scorecard summary 1–2
3. Drivers Why did it happen? EBITDA bridge (price, volume, mix, cost, FX), root causes, cash and working capital drivers 1–2
4. Outlook Where are we heading? LE vs AOP, rolling forecast trend, gap to guidance, assumptions changed 1
5. Risks and opportunities What could change it? Quantified R&O list with probability, ₹ impact, timing, mitigation 1
6. Decisions What do we need from you? 2–4 decisions with options, impact, recommendation, owner and date 1

This aligns with the 1-page summary + 5–8 core slides + appendix structure (c04) and the Minto pyramid: the answer (part 1) comes first; parts 2–5 support it; part 6 converts it into action. The SCR framework from t3 maps onto it: Situation (performance), Complication (drivers and outlook gap), Resolution (decisions).

Writing the summary slide

The summary slide should stand alone. Structure:

  • Headline (action title): one sentence with the answer, for example "Q3 EBITDA ₹4.2 crore below AOP on input costs; three actions recover ₹6.5 crore and keep FY guidance of ₹150 crore within reach".
  • Key numbers strip: revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA %, PAT, FCF, net debt — month, YTD, LE, each vs AOP and PY.
  • 3–5 key messages: each a full sentence with a number and a cause.
  • Decisions required: listed explicitly, mirrored on the last slide.

Action titles and the "horizontal read"

Every slide title is a conclusion, not a topic. "EBITDA bridge" is a topic; "Price increases offset only 60% of polymer inflation; volume ahead of plan" is a conclusion. Reading just the titles in sequence (the horizontal read) should tell the whole story. A practical test: paste all titles into one list; if they do not form a coherent argument, the storyline is broken.

Building each core slide

  • Performance: a compact AvBvPY table (IBCS-style notation: actual solid, budget outlined, PY grey) with variance in ₹ and %, and a small KPI RAG strip. Avoid more than 12 rows.
  • Drivers: the EBITDA bridge waterfall from AOP to actual (and AOP to LE), each bar labelled with a cause; a side panel with the top three root causes from the commentary.
  • Outlook: LE vs AOP vs guidance, with the "gap to guidance" quantified; a chart of the rolling 12-month forecast versus prior LE to show momentum.
  • Risks and opportunities: a table with item, ₹ EBITDA impact, probability, timing, mitigation/owner; a risk-adjusted LE range (base ± weighted R&O).
  • Decisions: for each decision, two or three options with EBITDA, cash and KPI impact and a recommendation.

Appendix

Move detail to the appendix: full P&L, segment pages, PVM by SKU group, working capital schedules, capex tracker, KPI definitions and methodology. Reference appendix pages from core slides ("see A4") so detail is available in Q&A without cluttering the storyline.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Good news months: still lead with the answer and the outlook; the decision may be to reinvest upside or bank it.
  • Bad news: state it on slide 1 with the cause and the plan; surprises later in the deck damage trust.
  • Guidance vs AOP: listed companies may have given market guidance that differs from AOP; be explicit about which benchmark the deck uses and why.
  • Audience variation: a board deck emphasises strategy, risk and compliance; an operating review emphasises drivers and actions; keep the same numbers but different depth.
  • Too many decisions: more than four decisions on one slide signals the deck has not prioritised.

Key takeaways

  • Follow summary → performance → drivers → outlook → risks → decisions; answer first, decisions last.
  • Make every title an action title; the titles alone should tell the story.
  • The summary slide must stand alone with headline numbers, key messages and decisions required.
  • Quantify risks and actions with ₹ impact, owner, timing and confidence.
  • Push detail into an appendix referenced from the core slides.

Practice Questions

Q1. Rewrite titles [L1]

Rewrite these topic titles as action titles using plausible numbers: "Revenue analysis", "Working capital", "EBITDA bridge", "Outlook", "Risks".

Q2. Storyline from facts [L2]

Facts for October: revenue ₹212 crore vs AOP ₹205 crore; EBITDA ₹24.1 crore vs AOP ₹26.0 crore; price +₹3.0 crore, volume +₹1.5 crore, raw material −₹5.2 crore, freight −₹1.2 crore; DSO 61 days vs 55; LE EBITDA ₹292 crore vs AOP ₹305 crore. Draft the six slide titles and the three key messages for the summary slide.

💡 Hint: Lead with the EBITDA gap and its cause, not with revenue beating budget.

Q3. Risk-adjusted LE [L2]

Base LE EBITDA is ₹143 crore. Risks: customer insolvency ₹3 crore at 30%; monsoon-linked demand drop ₹5 crore at 20%. Opportunities: export order ₹4 crore at 50%; power tariff refund ₹2 crore at 60%. Compute the risk-adjusted LE and the range, and design the R&O slide.

Q4. Board vs operating review [L3]

Explain how you would adapt the same month's MBR storyline for (a) the monthly CEO operating review and (b) the quarterly board meeting of an NSE-listed company where the Audit Committee will also review quarterly results. Specify what changes and what must stay identical.
