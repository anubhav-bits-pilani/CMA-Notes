Presentation Rehearsal and Q&A Readiness

Scope: Presentation rehearsal and Q&A readiness

Why it matters

A strong model and deck can still fail in the room: the presenter runs out of time on page 3, reads tables aloud, or cannot answer "what if the price increase slips a month?". Senior finance audiences judge competence largely through Q&A — the questions test whether the presenter understands the drivers, owns the assumptions and can think on their feet. The Saturday assessment and real FP&A interviews (case presentations, model walkthroughs) are timed and interrupted; rehearsal and structured Q&A preparation are therefore part of the deliverable, not an extra.

Core notes

Rehearsal plan

  1. Timebox: allocate time by slide. For a 15-minute slot: summary 3 minutes, performance and drivers 4, outlook and risks 3, decisions 3, buffer 2. Leave at least a third of total time for questions in longer slots.
  2. Talk track: write three to five bullet points per slide — the message, the evidence, the transition to the next slide. Do not script word for word; do memorise the opening and the decision request.
  3. First rehearsal alone, with a timer and recording; check for filler words, reading numbers, and overruns.
  4. Second rehearsal with a colleague acting as CFO; ask them to interrupt at random.
  5. Final run-through in the actual format (screen share in Teams, projector, printed pack) to check legibility, chart colours and links to appendix pages.

Presenting numbers well

  • Say the message first, then the number: "We are ₹4.2 crore behind on EBITDA, almost entirely polymer inflation."
  • Round in speech (₹4.2 crore, not ₹4,17,63,000); keep precise numbers on the page.
  • Use at most three numbers per sentence; point to the chart element being discussed.
  • Signpost: "Three points on drivers. First…".
  • Close every section with the implication or the ask.

Q&A preparation: the question bank

Build a question bank of 20–30 likely questions grouped by theme, with a short answer and the backing page or model cell for each:

Theme Example question Preparation
Assumptions Why is volume growth 8% when the market grows 6%? Source (S&OP consensus), share gain logic, sensitivity
Sensitivities What if polymer goes up another ₹10/kg? Pre-computed sensitivity table, ₹ EBITDA per ₹1/kg
Timing What if the price increase is delayed by a month? Monthly phasing; ₹ per month of delay
Root causes Is the freight overrun structural or one-off? Commentary evidence, lane data
Cash Why did FCF fall when EBITDA was flat? FCF bridge: working capital, capex, tax
Covenants What is headroom on net debt/EBITDA? Covenant calculation per sanction letter definitions
Methodology How did you split price and mix? Methodology note; PVM formula order
Accountability Who owns each action and how will we track it? Action log with dates
Accounting Does the scheme accrual change under Ind AS 115? Policy note; variable consideration estimate
Downside What do we do if all three actions slip? Plan B list, discretionary spend levers

Sensitivity and "what if" readiness

Prepare a one-page sensitivity grid from the model for the five highest-ranked assumptions (from the assumptions sheet sensitivity rank). Express each as "₹ EBITDA per unit change":

  • Price: ₹ crore EBITDA per 1% price change.
  • Volume: ₹ crore EBITDA per 1% volume change at current contribution margin.
  • Key input cost: ₹ crore per ₹1 per kg (or per 1% move).
  • FX: ₹ crore per ₹1 move in USD/INR (separate export revenue and imported cost exposures).
  • Timing: ₹ crore per month of delay of a key action.

Example: revenue ₹1,000 crore, contribution margin 35%, polymer consumption 30,000 tonnes. 1% price = ₹10 crore EBITDA (falls entirely to the bottom line before any volume response); 1% volume = ₹3.5 crore; ₹1/kg polymer = \(30{,}000 \times 1{,}000 \times 1 = 3\) crore. With these, most "what if" questions can be answered in seconds. Also keep a two-way data table in Excel (Data > What-If Analysis > Data Table) for price × volume, and the scenario switch ready to show downside live if asked.

Handling questions in the room

  • Listen fully, pause, restate if the question is ambiguous.
  • Answer first, then evidence: "Yes, it is structural — two of the three lanes have new contracts at higher rates; see appendix A6."
  • If you do not know, say so, give what you do know, and commit to a time: "I'll confirm the exact split by 5 pm."
  • Challenge respectfully when a question rests on a wrong premise, using data.
  • Park detailed or off-topic questions for after the meeting.
  • Hostile questions: stay factual, acknowledge the concern, avoid defensiveness about assumptions — explain the source and the sensitivity.

Defending assumptions

Use a consistent pattern: source → reasonableness check → sensitivity → what would change your view. Example: "Volume growth of 8% comes from the December S&OP consensus; it is consistent with our share gain in the last three quarters (7–9%); each point is ₹3.5 crore of EBITDA; if Q4 secondary sales come below 6% we would cut the LE."

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Running out of time: skip to the decisions slide rather than rushing through all slides.
  • Senior interrupts on slide 1: follow their question; the storyline exists to serve the audience.
  • Questions outside finance (for example production yields): answer what the financial impact is and refer the operational detail to the owner.
  • Remote presentation: share only the deck window, number every slide, and avoid animations that lag.
  • Interview setting: interviewers may deliberately challenge a correct assumption to test composure — defend it with evidence rather than changing it immediately.

Key takeaways

  • Rehearse with a timer, a recording and a colleague who interrupts.
  • Say the message before the number and round numbers in speech.
  • Build a 20–30 question bank with answers and backing references.
  • Pre-compute "₹ EBITDA per unit change" for the top five assumptions.
  • Defend assumptions with source, reasonableness, sensitivity and trigger to change.

Practice Questions

Q1. Sensitivity grid [L2]

For a company with revenue ₹640 crore, contribution margin 30%, imported resin of 12,000 tonnes priced at USD 1,300 per tonne, and USD/INR at 84, compute EBITDA sensitivity per 1% price, 1% volume, ₹1 per kg resin and ₹1 move in USD/INR on the resin cost. Present it as a one-page grid.

Q2. Question bank [L2]

Draft ten likely questions a CFO would ask on your Project 6 MBR pack and, for each, name the page or model cell you would point to.

💡 Hint: Cover assumptions, timing, cash, covenants, methodology and accountability.

Q3. Handling a challenge [L3]

During your presentation, the CEO says: "Your LE assumes a 2% price increase from January, but our largest competitor just cut prices. Why should I believe ₹143 crore?" Write your response in under 120 words and list the analysis you would need to have ready.

Q4. Timing plan [L1]

Prepare a slide-by-slide timing plan and talk-track bullets for a 12-minute presentation of a seven-slide MBR deck, leaving time for questions.
