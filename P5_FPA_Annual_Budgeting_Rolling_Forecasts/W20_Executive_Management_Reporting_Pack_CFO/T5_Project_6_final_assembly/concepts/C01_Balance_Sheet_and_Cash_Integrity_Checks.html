Key concept

Balance Sheet and Cash Integrity Checks

Concept: Integrity check: Total assets − (Equity + Liabilities) = 0; CF closing cash = BS cash

Statement

For every period \(t\) and every scenario:

\[ \text{TA}_t - (\text{E}_t + \text{L}_t) = 0 \]

\[ \text{Cash}^{\text{CF}}_{\text{close},t} - \text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t = 0 \]

where \(\text{TA}_t\) is total assets, \(\text{E}_t\) total equity (including non-controlling interest in a consolidated model), \(\text{L}_t\) total liabilities (non-current and current), \(\text{Cash}^{\text{CF}}_{\text{close},t}\) the closing cash from the cash flow statement (opening cash + CFO + CFI + CFF, plus any FX effect on cash) and \(\text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t\) cash and cash equivalents on the balance sheet.

Explanation

In an integrated three-statement model, the balance sheet balances only if every P&L item flows to retained earnings, every balance sheet movement appears in the cash flow statement, and the cash flow statement's closing cash becomes the balance sheet cash line. The two checks are therefore complementary:

  • The balance check fails when a BS line is omitted from totals, a P&L item does not reach equity, or a cash flow item has no BS counterpart.
  • The cash check fails when the BS cash line is not linked to the CF statement (for example, hard-coded or computed separately) or when definitions differ.

Definitional points: under Ind AS 7, cash and cash equivalents include short-term highly liquid investments (usually maturity of three months or less from acquisition) and may be net of bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form part of cash management (Ind AS 7.8). Schedule III, however, presents overdrafts within borrowings and "bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" separately. So the model needs a reconciling line when the CF statement uses cash net of overdraft and the BS shows gross cash. Checks should be computed with a tolerance (for example =ABS(x)<0.01) and summarised as a maximum absolute value across all months so that one failing month is not hidden.

A balancing check that passes does not prove the model is right — offsetting errors balance too — but a failing one proves something is wrong. Never force balance with a plug.

Illustration

March FY26 model output (₹ crore):

Item Amount
Total assets 1,248.6
Equity 612.3
Liabilities 636.3
Balance check 0.0
CF closing cash (net of ₹6.0 crore overdraft) 41.5
BS cash and cash equivalents 47.5
Overdraft in borrowings 6.0
Cash check after overdraft reconciling line 0.0

Without the reconciling line, the cash check would show ₹6.0 crore and a reviewer would waste time hunting an error that is only a presentation difference.

Practice Questions

Q1. Locate the break [L2]

A model balances in all months except those after a ₹15 crore dividend is declared in August and paid in September. In August the check shows −₹15 crore; in September and later it shows −₹15 crore as well. Explain the probable modelling error and the fix.

💡 Hint: Trace the declared dividend through equity, liabilities and cash in each month.

Q2. Write the checks [L1]

Write Excel formulas for the balance check and the cash check in a model with monthly columns G:AD, using named rows TotalAssets, TotalEquity, TotalLiabilities, CF_ClosingCash and BS_Cash, returning a single TRUE or FALSE across all months.

Q3. Overdraft presentation [L2]

Your company uses a cash credit facility of ₹25 crore as part of daily cash management; at year-end it is drawn ₹9 crore and bank balances are ₹14 crore. Explain how cash appears in the cash flow statement and balance sheet under Ind AS 7 and Schedule III, and how your cash check should handle it.
