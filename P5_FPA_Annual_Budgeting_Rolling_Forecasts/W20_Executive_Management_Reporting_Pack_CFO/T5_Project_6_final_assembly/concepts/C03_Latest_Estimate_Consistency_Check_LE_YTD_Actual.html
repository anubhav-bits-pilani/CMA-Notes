Key concept

Latest Estimate Consistency Check: LE = YTD Actual + YTG

Concept: Forecast check: LE = YTD Actual + YTG Forecast

Statement

For a financial year with months \(m = 1, \dots, 12\) (April to March) and last closed month \(n\):

\[ \text{LE} = \sum_{m=1}^{n} A_m + \sum_{m=n+1}^{12} F_m \]

where \(A_m\) is the actual for month \(m\) from the closed ledger and \(F_m\) the current forecast for the year-to-go (YTG) month \(m\). The check is \(\text{LE}_{\text{reported}} - \left(\sum A + \sum F\right) = 0\).

Explanation

The LE (also called forecast outturn or full-year estimate) is the most watched number in the MBR, especially for listed companies managing market guidance. The check guarantees three things:

  • YTD actuals are locked: the LE must use booked actuals for closed months, not forecast numbers left over from the previous cycle.
  • No double counting or gaps: every month is counted exactly once, either as actual or forecast, controlled by the actual/forecast flag.
  • Consistency across pages: the LE on the executive summary, in the bridge, in the segment pages and in the cash tracker are the same number.

Common failure modes: the "last actual month" was updated but the forecast for that month was not replaced; forecasts were not re-phased after a slip, so YTG implicitly contains catch-up that is unrealistic; or the LE was typed into the summary page. A companion reasonableness test compares the YTG run-rate with YTD:

\[ \text{Implied YTG monthly average} = \frac{\text{LE} - \text{YTD}}{12 - n} \]

If it is far above the YTD monthly average without a seasonal or action-based reason, the LE is a "hockey stick" and should be challenged before it reaches the CFO.

Illustration

December close (\(n = 9\)). YTD EBITDA actual ₹101.4 crore; forecast for January, February and March ₹13.2, ₹14.1 and ₹17.3 crore. LE = 101.4 + 44.6 = ₹146.0 crore, matching the summary page. Implied YTG monthly average = ₹14.9 crore versus YTD average of ₹11.3 crore — a 32% step-up. The seasonal pattern from PY shows Q4 typically 18% above the nine-month average, so roughly half the step-up needs specific support (price increase from January, scheme withdrawal). That is the question to resolve before presenting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and test the LE [L1]

After the September close, YTD revenue is ₹612 crore. Forecasts for October–March are ₹108, ₹121, ₹115, ₹104, ₹98 and ₹132 crore. The MBR summary shows LE revenue ₹1,302 crore. Test the LE and state possible reasons for any difference.

Q2. Hockey-stick challenge [L2]

YTD (eight months) EBITDA is ₹64 crore and the LE is ₹112 crore. PY showed the last four months contributing 38% of full-year EBITDA. Assess the reasonableness of the LE and list the evidence you would ask the business for.

💡 Hint: Compare the implied YTG share of full-year EBITDA with the PY seasonal share.

Q3. Model fix [L2]

After rolling the model from November to December, LE revenue jumped by exactly the December forecast value. Diagnose the likely error in the flag or summing logic and describe the fix and the check that prevents recurrence.
