Key concept

Permanent vs Timing Variance Using the Full-Year LE

Concept: Permanent variance = FY budget − FY forecast (LE); Timing variance = YTD variance − Permanent variance

Statement

For a cost line, with positive = favourable:

\[ V_{\text{perm}} = B_{FY} - LE_{FY} \]

\[ V_{\text{timing}} = V_{YTD} - V_{\text{perm}} = \left(B_{YTD} - A_{YTD}\right) - \left(B_{FY} - LE_{FY}\right) \]

where \(B_{FY}\) is the full-year budget, \(LE_{FY} = A_{YTD} + F_{ROY}\) the latest full-year estimate (YTD actual plus rest-of-year forecast), and \(V_{YTD}\) the YTD variance (c01). Equivalently \(V_{\text{timing}} = F_{ROY} - B_{ROY}\), the excess of rest-of-year forecast over rest-of-year budget.

Explanation

The full-year view settles the question "will this variance still exist in March?". Whatever the LE says the year will end at, the difference from the FY budget is, by definition, the variance that will not reverse — permanent. Whatever part of the YTD variance is not permanent must be expected to unwind in the remaining months — timing.

Signs and combinations:

\(V_{YTD}\) \(V_{\text{perm}}\) Reading
+ + (smaller) Underspend partly timing, partly a real saving
+ 0 Pure timing — spend catches up
+ − Underspend now, but the year will overrun — catch-up exceeds the saving (warning sign)
− − (equal) Overrun is permanent, no recovery planned
− 0 Overspend ahead of phasing; will be offset by lower spend later

Conditions for validity:

  • The LE must be genuine (not budget copied forward); test catch-up feasibility against commitments (s04, s05).
  • For variable lines, apply the formula to spending variances: replace \(B_{FY}\) with the FY budget flexed at LE activity, and \(B_{YTD}\) with the YTD flexed budget, so that volume effects (c03) are separated first.
  • One-offs already incurred are part of the permanent component (they are in YTD actual and so in the LE).
  • At year-end \(V_{\text{timing}} = 0\) because YTD = FY.

Illustration

Marketing at 30 September (₹ crore): \(B_{FY} = 12.00\), \(B_{YTD} = 6.00\), \(A_{YTD} = 4.50\), \(F_{ROY} = 7.10\), so \(LE_{FY} = 11.60\).

\[ V_{YTD} = 6.00 - 4.50 = +1.50, \quad V_{\text{perm}} = 12.00 - 11.60 = +0.40, \quad V_{\text{timing}} = 1.50 - 0.40 = +1.10 \]

Check: \(F_{ROY} - B_{ROY} = 7.10 - 6.00 = 1.10\). Commentary: "₹1.5 crore YTD underspend: ₹1.1 crore timing (festive campaign in October–November, in LE), ₹0.4 crore permanent media-rate saving."

Warning case: if the LE were ₹12.40 crore (\(F_{ROY} = 7.90\)), then \(V_{\text{perm}} = -0.40\) and \(V_{\text{timing}} = +1.90\): the business is ₹1.5 crore under budget today but expects to finish ₹0.4 crore over.

Excel: =FY_Bud-FY_LE and =YTD_Var-Permanent, with a check =Timing-((FY_LE-YTD_Act)-(FY_Bud-YTD_Bud)) that must be zero.

Practice Questions

Q1. Four lines [L1]

For each line compute permanent and timing variances (₹ lakh): (a) Training: FY budget 120, YTD budget 60, YTD actual 35, FY LE 110; (b) Rent: FY budget 480, YTD budget 240, YTD actual 240, FY LE 480; (c) Consultancy: FY budget 90, YTD budget 45, YTD actual 62, FY LE 104; (d) Recruitment: FY budget 50, YTD budget 25, YTD actual 12, FY LE 55.

Q2. Variable-line adjustment [L2]

Sales commission is budgeted at 2% of net sales. FY budget sales ₹500 crore, YTD budget sales ₹240 crore, YTD actual sales ₹228 crore; YTD actual commission ₹4.30 crore; LE sales ₹485 crore and LE commission ₹9.90 crore. Compute the volume-driven and spending components, then apply the permanent/timing split to the spending variance only.

💡 Hint: Flex both the YTD and the FY budgets at actual and LE sales respectively.

Q3. Interview: the warning case [L2]

An interviewer says: "A function is ₹2 crore under budget YTD at December, and its 9+3 LE shows it finishing ₹0.5 crore over budget. Is that possible, and what would you do?" Draft your answer with the formula and two follow-up actions.
