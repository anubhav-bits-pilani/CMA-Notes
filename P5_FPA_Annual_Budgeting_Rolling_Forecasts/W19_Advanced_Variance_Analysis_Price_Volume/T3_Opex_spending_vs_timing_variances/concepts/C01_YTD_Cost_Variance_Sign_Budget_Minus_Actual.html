Key concept

YTD Cost Variance Sign: Budget Minus Actual

Concept: YTD variance = YTD budget − YTD actual (positive = underspend for costs)

Statement

For a cost (opex) line at month \(k\) of the fiscal year:

\[ V_{YTD,k} = B_{YTD,k} - A_{YTD,k}, \qquad B_{YTD,k} = \sum_{t=1}^{k} B_t, \quad A_{YTD,k} = \sum_{t=1}^{k} A_t \]

where \(B_t\) is the phased budget and \(A_t\) the actual (accrual-basis) cost for month \(t\), with \(t = 1\) for April in an April–March fiscal year. \(V_{YTD,k} > 0\) is an underspend (favourable), \(V_{YTD,k} < 0\) an overspend (adverse).

Explanation

Writing cost variances as budget − actual gives the same sign meaning as revenue variances written actual − budget: positive always helps profit. A table can then mix revenue, margin and cost lines and still be read in one direction, and the variance column sums to the EBITDA variance (Topic 5).

Points of discipline:

  • Positive is not automatically good news. An underspend is favourable to YTD profit, but it may be timing that reverses (s04), an accrual gap (s05), a mapping error (s06), or a symptom of under-investment (marketing not spent → revenue shortfall). The sign tells you the direction of the profit effect, not whether the business is healthy.
  • YTD vs month. The YTD variance at month \(k\) equals the sum of monthly variances to date; a large monthly variance that reverses next month leaves the YTD variance unchanged — which is why YTD is the primary view for timing analysis and the month is used to spot cut-off errors.
  • Natural signs in ERP extracts. Costs carry positive (debit) signs and revenues negative (credit) signs in a TB; once revenue is flipped to positive presentation, the formula pattern is: revenue variance = A − B, cost variance = B − A. Alternatively, store costs as negatives and use A − B everywhere (Topic 5, c02).
  • Presentation. Show adverse values in brackets with a custom format such as #,##0.0;(#,##0.0);"-", and label the column "Fav/(Adv)" or "Better/(Worse)".

Variance % for costs = \(V_{YTD} / \lvert B_{YTD} \rvert\); show "n.m." when the YTD budget is zero (a new line) — for example, a cost centre whose budget starts in October.

Illustration

Marketing, ₹ crore, flat budget ₹1.0 crore per month.

Month Budget Actual Monthly var YTD budget YTD actual YTD var
April 1.00 0.70 +0.30 1.00 0.70 +0.30
May 1.00 0.65 +0.35 2.00 1.35 +0.65
June 1.00 1.20 −0.20 3.00 2.55 +0.45
...
September 1.00 0.80 +0.20 6.00 4.50 +1.50

At September the YTD underspend is +₹1.50 crore (favourable to YTD EBITDA), 25% of YTD budget. Whether it is good news depends on the LE test in c02.

Excel: =YTD_Bud-YTD_Act with YTD_Bud=SUM($C$5:C5) copied across months; Power BI: YTD Var = [YTD Budget] - [YTD Actual] where both use TOTALYTD(..., 'Date'[Date], "3/31").

Connections

Feeds the timing/permanent decomposition (c02) and is the starting point of every opex classification (s02). Phasing (s01) determines its monthly path. The sign convention is the topic-level application of Topic 5 (c01, c03) and of the cross-cutting rule that positive = favourable across tables, bridges and dashboards. In the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4) opex savings rise and overruns fall because the variance is defined budget − actual. Materiality thresholds (c05) are applied to its absolute value.

Practice Questions

Q1. YTD build [L1]

Monthly budget for travel is ₹40 lakh April–September. Actuals are April 32, May 38, June 51, July 29, August 44, September 47 (₹ lakh). Compute monthly and YTD variances with the budget − actual convention and identify the months with adverse monthly variances.

Q2. Mixed table sign check [L2]

A variance table shows (₹ crore): revenue budget 200, actual 214; COGS budget 120, actual 131; opex budget 50, actual 46. Compute the variance for each line with the convention in this note, confirm that the variances sum to the EBITDA variance, and explain what goes wrong if all lines are computed as actual − budget.

💡 Hint: Compute budget and actual EBITDA directly and compare.

Q3. Interpreting a favourable sign [L2]

A regional sales cost centre shows a YTD underspend of ₹1.1 crore (18% of budget) at December, while the region's revenue is 9% behind budget. List four possible explanations, the evidence that would distinguish them, and how each would change the commentary.
