Key concept

Volume-Driven Opex Variance at Budget Variable Rate

Concept: Volume-driven opex variance = (Actual activity − Budget activity) × Budget variable rate (e.g., freight per tonne)

Statement

For a variable opex line with a budget rate \(r^B\) per unit of activity driver:

\[ \Delta C_{\text{vol}} = \left(X^A - X^B\right) \times r^B \]

where \(X^A\) and \(X^B\) are actual and budget activity (tonnes shipped, orders, net sales ₹, kWh, cases picked) and \(r^B\) the budget variable cost per unit of activity. \(\Delta C_{\text{vol}}\) is the change in cost caused by the activity difference. In a positive = favourable presentation the variance is shown as \(-\Delta C_{\text{vol}}\): higher activity → higher cost → adverse sign on the cost line.

Equivalently, volume-driven variance = static budget − flexed budget, with flexed budget \(= X^A \times r^B\) (plus any fixed element at budget).

Explanation

Many opex lines are not discretionary; they move mechanically with business activity. Secondary freight moves with tonnes shipped, commission with net sales, packing material with cases, warehouse handling with pallets, payment-gateway charges with transaction value, royalty with sales. Comparing these lines with a static budget blames (or credits) the cost owner for volume they do not control.

Separating the volume-driven component achieves three things:

  1. Fair accountability: the logistics head is assessed on the spending variance (c04) — rate per tonne and efficiency — not on the sales team's volume.
  2. Correct bridge placement: on the EBITDA bridge the volume-driven opex is part of the volume story; some companies net it against the volume step (volume at budget contribution after variable selling costs), others show it next to volume.
  3. Clean timing/permanent analysis: the LE test (c02) should be applied after removing volume effects.

Choosing the driver. Use the driver in which the budget was built and which genuinely causes the cost: freight per tonne-km is better than per tonne if lane mix changes; commission per ₹ of collected sales if commission is paid on collections. The driver must be available monthly for both budget and actual.

Semi-variable lines. Split into fixed and variable parts; flex only the variable part. For a warehouse with a fixed lease and variable handling, \(\Delta C_{\text{vol}}\) applies only to handling.

CMA link. This is the flexible budget volume variance applied to selling and distribution overhead; the remainder is the expenditure/spending variance.

Illustration

Secondary freight for the year: budget 50,000 t at ₹1,200/t = ₹6.00 crore. Actual volume 56,000 t; actual freight ₹7.00 crore.

Item ₹ crore
Static budget 6.00
Flexed budget \(= 56,000 \times 1,200\) 6.72
Actual 7.00
Volume-driven variance \(= 6.00 - 6.72\) (0.72)
Spending variance \(= 6.72 - 7.00\) (c04) (0.28)
Total vs static budget (1.00)

Of the ₹1.0 crore adverse freight variance, ₹0.72 crore is the cost of shipping 6,000 more tonnes — offset many times over by the contribution on those tonnes in the revenue/GM volume bar — and only ₹0.28 crore (₹50/t on actual volume, i.e. actual ₹1,250/t vs ₹1,200/t) is a true rate or efficiency overrun for the logistics team to explain (diesel price pass-through, lane mix, part-truck loads).

Excel: =(Act_Tonnes-Bud_Tonnes)*Bud_Rate (cost increase) and presentation =-(Act_Tonnes-Bud_Tonnes)*Bud_Rate.

Connections

Pairs with c04 (spending variance) to explain total variable opex variance; parallels c02 of Topic 1 (volume at budget unit economics) — volume effects are always valued at budget rates. Refreshes the Week 17 flexible budget. Part of the classification framework in s02 (volume-driven class). In the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4) it is either grouped with volume or shown as a separate "variable selling cost — volume" step; in Week 21 it feeds contribution after variable selling costs.

Practice Questions

Q1. Commission flex [L1]

Commission is budgeted at 1.8% of net sales of ₹420 crore. Actual net sales are ₹392 crore and actual commission ₹7.30 crore. Compute the flexed budget, the volume-driven variance and the spending variance with positive = favourable.

Q2. Semi-variable warehouse [L2]

A warehouse budget is ₹2.4 crore fixed (lease and staff) plus ₹85 per pallet handled on 2,00,000 pallets. Actual: 2,30,000 pallets, fixed costs ₹2.5 crore, variable handling ₹2.02 crore. Compute the static-budget variance, the volume-driven variance and the spending variance split into fixed and variable.

💡 Hint: Flex only the per-pallet element.

Q3. Driver choice [L2]

Freight was budgeted per tonne at ₹1,100/t on 80,000 t. Actual tonnes were 78,000 but average lead distance rose from 420 km to 510 km because a new depot opened late. Actual freight cost was ₹9.70 crore. Compute the variances using a per-tonne driver and then using a per-tonne-km driver (budget rate derived from the budget), and explain which split is fairer to the logistics head.
