Key concept

Materiality Thresholds for Variance Commentary

Concept: Materiality thresholds for commentary, e.g., |Variance| > ₹X lakh and > ±5% of budget

Statement

A variance on line \(j\) requires written commentary when it breaches both an absolute and a relative threshold:

\[ \text{Comment}_j = \left[\, \lvert V_j \rvert > T_{\text{abs}} \,\right] \wedge \left[\, \frac{\lvert V_j \rvert}{\lvert B_j \rvert} > T_{\text{rel}} \,\right] \]

where \(V_j\) is the variance (YTD or month, as defined in the policy), \(B_j\) the budget for the same period, \(T_{\text{abs}}\) an absolute threshold in ₹ (e.g., ₹25 lakh at cost-centre level, ₹1 crore at group level) and \(T_{\text{rel}}\) a relative threshold (e.g., 5%). Some policies use OR instead of AND, or add an override list of always-comment lines.

Explanation

Without thresholds, monthly variance commentary either covers every line (hundreds of trivial explanations that bury the important ones) or is left to the preparer's discretion (inconsistent, and adverse variances get less attention than favourable ones). A written materiality policy makes the pack predictable and auditable.

Why two tests?

  • An absolute test alone flags large lines with trivial percentage movements (₹30 lakh on a ₹50 crore salary line is 0.6% — noise).
  • A relative test alone flags tiny lines with big percentages (₹2 lakh on a ₹10 lakh budget is 20% but immaterial).
  • AND focuses commentary on variances that are both large and unusual; OR is more conservative (more comments) and is sometimes used for sensitive lines or at lower levels of the hierarchy.

Design choices to document:

  1. Level: thresholds scale with the hierarchy — cost centre, function, P&L line, group. A ₹10 lakh threshold may suit a cost centre; the board pack might use ₹2 crore or a bps-of-revenue threshold (e.g., > 10 bps of revenue).
  2. Period: month, YTD and FY LE thresholds; many policies require commentary on YTD and LE variances, and on monthly variances only if they breach a higher threshold.
  3. Budget zero or sign flip: the relative test is undefined; use the absolute test alone and show "n.m." for the percentage (Topic 5, c03).
  4. Override lines: always comment on certain lines regardless of size (CSR spend under Section 135, legal and regulatory, related-party transactions, donations, one-offs, any line flagged by audit).
  5. Symmetry: favourable and adverse variances use the same thresholds — favourable variances are often timing or accounting errors (accruals missing), which is exactly what must be explained.
  6. Cumulative small variances: many sub-threshold variances in the same direction can add up; require commentary at the next level if the aggregate breaches.

Relationship to audit materiality. Statutory audit materiality (SA 320) concerns misstatements in financial statements, typically set as a percentage of profit before tax, revenue or total assets. FP&A commentary thresholds are management tools and are usually far lower; do not confuse the two, although audit materiality can inform the group-level threshold.

Excel implementation:

=IF(ABS(Bud)=0, IF(ABS(Var)>Abs_Thr,"Comment",""),
   IF(AND(ABS(Var)>Abs_Thr, ABS(Var)/ABS(Bud)>Rel_Thr),"Comment",""))

with Abs_Thr and Rel_Thr as named input cells so the policy can be changed in one place; an override column (Always_Comment = TRUE) is ORed with the result. In Power BI, the same logic can drive a conditional formatting rule or a "Commentary required" flag measure.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply a policy [L1]

Policy: comment if \(\lvert V \rvert >\) ₹20 lakh AND \(\lvert V \rvert / \lvert B \rvert >\) 5%. Lines (YTD budget, YTD variance in ₹ lakh): Rent 900, (25); IT 350, 40; Freight 640, (31); Printing 30, (9); Insurance 0, (22); Consultancy 210, 18. State which lines require commentary and why.

Q2. AND vs OR [L2]

Using Q1 data, apply an OR policy with the same thresholds. Compare the number of comments, and argue which policy suits (a) a cost-centre owner pack and (b) the board pack.

💡 Hint: Consider the audience's time and the risk of missing a large-but-routine line.

Q3. Designing thresholds [L3]

A listed company with revenue of ₹4,800 crore and EBITDA of ₹620 crore asks you to design commentary thresholds for three levels: cost centre (250 cost centres, typical annual budget ₹2–5 crore), function (12 functions) and board pack (P&L lines). Propose absolute and relative thresholds, period basis, override lines and an aggregation rule, with a one-line rationale for each.
