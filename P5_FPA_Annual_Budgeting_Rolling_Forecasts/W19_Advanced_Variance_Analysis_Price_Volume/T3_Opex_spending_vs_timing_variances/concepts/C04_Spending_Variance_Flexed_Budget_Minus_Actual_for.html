Key concept

Spending Variance: Flexed Budget Minus Actual for Variable Opex

Concept: Spending variance = Flexed budget − Actual (for variable opex)

Statement

\[ V_{\text{spend}} = B_{\text{flex}} - A, \qquad B_{\text{flex}} = F^B + r^B \times X^A \]

where \(F^B\) is the budgeted fixed element (zero for a purely variable line), \(r^B\) the budget variable rate per unit of activity, \(X^A\) actual activity and \(A\) actual cost. Positive = favourable (actual cost below what the actual activity should have cost at budget rates). Together with the volume-driven variance (c03):

\[ B_{\text{static}} - A = \underbrace{\left(B_{\text{static}} - B_{\text{flex}}\right)}_{\text{volume-driven}} + \underbrace{\left(B_{\text{flex}} - A\right)}_{\text{spending}} \]

Explanation

The spending variance is the part of a variable cost variance that the cost owner controls: the rate paid per unit of driver and the efficiency with which the driver is consumed. It can be further decomposed when data allow, exactly like CMA labour and material variances:

\[ V_{\text{spend}} = \underbrace{\left(r^B - r^A\right) \times X^A}_{\text{rate}} \]

for a line where cost is driver × rate; if the driver itself has an input (e.g., freight cost = tonne-km × rate per tonne-km, and tonne-km per tonne is an efficiency measure), split further into rate (at actual input) and efficiency (input change at budget rate) — always rate effects at actual quantity, quantity effects at budget rate.

Interpretation discipline:

  • Fixed lines (rent, salaries) are not flexed; for them spending variance = static variance, and the timing/permanent split (c02) applies directly.
  • Spending variances can still be timing. A freight rate dispute that delays invoices, or a contract-labour bill accrued at the old rate, creates spending variances that reverse; apply the LE test to the spending component.
  • Inflation pass-through: diesel-linked freight contracts (monthly diesel escalation clauses common in Indian road transport) make part of the spending variance an external rate effect; show it as "rate — fuel escalation" separately from operational efficiency.
  • Accrual quality: as with all actuals, verify cut-off before explaining (s03).

Illustration

Freight (from c03): static ₹6.00 crore, flexed ₹6.72 crore at 56,000 t × ₹1,200/t, actual ₹7.00 crore.

\[ V_{\text{spend}} = 6.72 - 7.00 = -0.28 \text{ crore} \]

Further analysis: actual average rate = ₹7.00 crore ÷ 56,000 t = ₹1,250/t. If the budget assumed 400 km average lead at ₹3.00 per tonne-km and actual lead was 410 km at ₹3.0488 per tonne-km (rounded), the ₹50/t overrun splits roughly into efficiency (10 km × ₹3.00 = ₹30/t, i.e. ₹0.168 crore on 56,000 t) and rate (≈ ₹20/t, ≈ ₹0.112 crore) — the rate part reflecting diesel escalation.

Excel: =(Bud_Fixed+Bud_Rate*Act_Driver)-Act_Cost.

Connections

The complement of c03; together they explain the static-budget variance for variable opex. Mirrors the material/labour rate and efficiency variances of Topic 2 (rate at actual quantity, efficiency at standard rate) and the Week 17 flexible budget. Its sign follows c01 (budget − actual). In s02 it is the "spending" class that is further split into timing and permanent with c02. On the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4) it is the controllable "opex efficiency" step, distinct from volume-driven opex.

Practice Questions

Q1. Power cost [L1]

A plant's power budget is ₹1.5 crore fixed demand charges plus ₹7.20 per kWh on 2.4 crore kWh. Actual consumption was 2.1 crore kWh and actual cost ₹17.10 crore, including demand charges of ₹1.6 crore. Compute the flexed budget, the spending variance and its split between fixed and variable elements.

Q2. Rate and efficiency within spending [L2]

Contract labour for loading is budgeted at 0.8 man-hours per tonne at ₹110 per man-hour. Actual: 60,000 tonnes loaded, 52,000 man-hours paid at ₹118. Compute the flexed budget, spending variance, and split it into rate and efficiency components.

💡 Hint: Standard hours for actual output are 0.8 × actual tonnes.

Q3. Commentary [L2]

Using the freight illustration in this note, write a three-sentence commentary for the supply chain head that distinguishes the volume-driven variance, the efficiency effect and the diesel-linked rate effect, and states which part should feature in the logistics team's performance review.
