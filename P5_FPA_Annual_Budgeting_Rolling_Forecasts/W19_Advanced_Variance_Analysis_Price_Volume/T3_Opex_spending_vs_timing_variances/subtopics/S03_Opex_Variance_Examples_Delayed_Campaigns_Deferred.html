Subtopic note

Opex Variance Examples: Delayed Campaigns, Deferred Hiring, Advances, Cut-Off

Scope: Examples: delayed marketing campaign, deferred hiring, advance payments, accrual cut-off errors

Why it matters

Four situations account for a large share of opex variance commentary in Indian companies: marketing campaigns that slip, hiring that lags plan, payments made in advance and expensed incorrectly, and accruals missed or duplicated at month-end. The first two are genuine business timing (sometimes becoming permanent); the last two are accounting artefacts that should never reach the CFO as "variances". An FP&A analyst must recognise each pattern from the numbers, know the right questions to ask the budget owner and the accounts team, and know the correct journal entries so that the actual is fixed before it is explained.

Core notes

1. Delayed marketing campaign

Pattern: large underspend in A&P in the month(s) the campaign was planned; YTD underspend persists until the campaign runs; often accompanied by lower-than-planned revenue if the campaign was meant to drive sales.

Analysis:

  • Confirm the new campaign date and whether media is booked (release orders, agency estimates — see committed spend, s05).
  • Check the LE includes the spend in the new month; if yes, the variance is timing (c02).
  • Ask whether the delay changes the revenue plan: a festive campaign moved from October to December may not recover the lost festive sales — a permanent revenue effect even though the cost is only timing.
  • If the campaign is cancelled, the saving becomes permanent — but so may the revenue shortfall.

Accounting: advertising is expensed when the entity has the right to access the goods or receives the services (Ind AS 38.69–69A: advertising and promotional expenditure is not capitalised). Production costs of an advertisement incurred before the media slot are expensed when the entity has received the production service, not deferred until airing — so a campaign "delay" can still produce production cost in the original month.

2. Deferred hiring

Pattern: employee cost below budget, typically growing month by month; headcount report shows actual heads below budgeted heads.

Analysis: split the employee cost variance into:

  • Vacancy/timing: budgeted roles not yet filled — cost will start when they join. Measure as vacant FTE-months × budgeted monthly CTC.
  • Rate: actual CTC of hires different from budgeted CTC (often adverse in tight talent markets).
  • Mix/grade: hires at a different grade.
  • Permanent: roles cancelled or frozen.

Indian specifics: notice periods of 60–90 days for experienced hires; joining bonuses and notice-period buyouts are additional one-off costs; statutory costs (employer PF, gratuity accrual under Ind AS 19, ESI where applicable) should move with salary in both budget and actual.

Illustration: budget assumes 10 sales officers at ₹1.2 lakh per month CTC from 1 July. By 30 September, 4 joined on 1 August at ₹1.3 lakh, the rest are expected on 1 December. Budget Q2 cost = \(10 \times 1.2 \times 3 = 36.0\) lakh. Actual = \(4 \times 1.3 \times 2 = 10.4\) lakh. Variance +25.6 lakh: vacancy (36 − 8 FTE-months × 1.2 = 26.4) less the rate effect on 8 FTE-months (\(8 \times 0.1 = 0.8\)) → +26.4 − 0.8 = +25.6. With six heads joining on 1 December instead of 1 July, the FY LE is lower than budget, so a large part of the vacancy variance is permanent (months that will never be paid), not timing. Deferred hiring is usually partly permanent — the salary for lost months is never spent.

3. Advance payments

Pattern: a large one-month overspend on a line such as IT software, insurance, rent or a sponsorship, followed by underspends in later months.

Cause: an annual or multi-period payment expensed on payment instead of being recorded as a prepayment and amortised over the period of benefit.

Entry Dr Cr ₹ lakh
April: payment of annual SaaS licence (April–March) Prepaid expenses Bank 120
Each month: amortisation IT software expense Prepaid expenses 10

If the full ₹120 lakh was expensed in April, the correcting entry at April month-end is Dr Prepaid expenses ₹110 lakh, Cr IT software expense ₹110 lakh. Also consider GST input tax credit and TDS on professional or contract payments (Sections 194J/194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961; the provisions are renumbered under the Income-tax Act, 2025 for payments from 1 April 2026 — check the applicable section for the period) — they affect the cash and payable entries, not the expense amount (where ITC is available).

FP&A point: the budget should already be phased evenly (s01); the "variance" is an accounting error, not timing. Distinguish genuine advance payments to vendors for goods/services not yet received (capital or revenue advances) which should sit on the balance sheet.

4. Accrual cut-off errors

Pattern: a line shows underspend in one month and a matching overspend the next, or a quarter-end underspend that reverses after the books are closed. Common for utilities, professional fees, freight, contract labour, events and agency fees, where invoices arrive late.

Correct treatment: expenses are recognised when the service is received (accrual basis under the Conceptual Framework and Ind AS 1.27–28), regardless of invoice timing.

Entry Dr Cr ₹ lakh
Month-end: accrue September freight not yet invoiced Freight expense Accrued expenses (other current liabilities) 45
October: reverse accrual Accrued expenses Freight expense 45
October: book vendor invoice Freight expense Trade payables 47

Net October expense = ₹2 lakh (the true-up). If the September accrual was missed, September is understated by ₹45 lakh and October overstated by ₹45 lakh — a pure artefact. For goods and services received against POs, the GR/IR (goods receipt / invoice receipt) clearing account in SAP accrues automatically on goods/service entry; missing service entry sheets are the usual cause of gaps.

Distinguish accruals (amount and timing reasonably certain, part of trade and other payables) from provisions under Ind AS 37 (uncertain timing or amount, e.g., a disputed claim). Both must be complete at period-end; the FP&A test is the same — is the cost of the period in the period?

Signals that separate the four cases

Signal Delayed campaign Deferred hiring Advance payment error Cut-off error
Shape Underspend until campaign, then overspend Growing underspend Spike then underspend Month-to-month reversal
Operational evidence Media plan date Headcount report Contract term Invoices received after close
Fix LE timing LE: part permanent Journal correction Accrual / reversal

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Campaign delay with committed media: non-cancellable media commitments may require recognising cost or a provision if the slot is lost (Ind AS 37 onerous contract considerations) — check contracts.
  • Hiring freeze announced mid-year: convert vacancy timing to permanent immediately in the LE.
  • Advance to related party: check approvals under Section 188 and disclosure — outside FP&A, but flag.
  • Over-accrual: conservative accruals that are later reversed create favourable variances in the reversal month; track accrual reversals separately.
  • Quarter-end window dressing: deliberately delayed invoice booking to show savings — an internal control issue to escalate.

Key takeaways

  • Delayed campaigns and deferred hiring are business timing; check the LE and whether the revenue plan is affected.
  • Deferred hiring is usually partly permanent: lost salary months are never spent.
  • Advance payments must be prepaid and amortised; a spike then underspend pattern signals an accounting error, not timing.
  • Accrual cut-off errors produce reversing month-to-month variances; fix the books, do not explain the artefact.
  • Use the shape of the monthly variance plus operational evidence to diagnose which case applies.

Practice Questions

Q1. Journal entries for an annual contract [L1]

On 1 April a company paid ₹36 lakh plus 18% GST (input credit available) for a 12-month cloud subscription and booked the whole ₹36 lakh to IT expense. Pass the correcting entry at 30 April and the monthly entry thereafter, and state the April and May IT expense variances against an evenly phased budget of ₹3 lakh per month before and after correction.

Q2. Hiring variance decomposition [L2]

Budget: 15 engineers at ₹2.0 lakh per month CTC from 1 April. Actual: 6 joined on 1 April at ₹2.2 lakh; 5 joined on 1 July at ₹2.1 lakh; 4 positions are expected to join on 1 January. Compute the H1 (April–September) employee cost variance, split it into vacancy and rate components, and estimate the FY permanent variance.

💡 Hint: Count FTE-months budgeted versus filled for each cohort.

Q3. Cut-off diagnosis [L2]

Monthly contract labour cost (₹ lakh) was: budget 30 each month; actual June 18, July 44, August 31, September 16, October 45. The plant's head count of contract workers was stable. Diagnose the likely cause, describe the evidence you would request from accounts payable and the plant, and propose a control.

Q4. Campaign delay with revenue impact [L3]

A ₹5 crore Diwali TV campaign was moved from October to the second week of January because the creative agency missed deadlines. The LE includes the ₹5 crore in January. Sales expects to lose ₹12 crore of festive revenue at a 45% contribution margin, half of which may be recovered in Q4. Write the commentary for the October management pack covering the opex timing variance, the revenue and contribution impact, and the FY EBITDA implication.
