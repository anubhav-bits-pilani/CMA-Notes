Budget Phasing Quality and Its Effect on Monthly and YTD Variances

Scope: Budget phasing quality and its effect on monthly/YTD variances

Why it matters

An annual opex budget is approved as a full-year number, but it is monitored monthly. The split of the annual number across months — the phasing — determines every monthly and year-to-date (YTD) variance reported to management. If phasing does not reflect when the business actually plans to spend, the monthly pack is full of variances that are nothing more than calendar artefacts: a ₹1.5 crore "underspend" in September that is really the Diwali campaign not having started yet. Executives then either celebrate savings that do not exist, or stop reading the variance pack altogether. Good phasing is the cheapest control in FP&A: it is done once, at budget time, and it makes every subsequent month's commentary meaningful.

Core notes

What phasing is

Phasing converts an annual budget \(B_{FY}\) for a line into monthly amounts \(B_1, \dots, B_{12}\) with \(\sum_t B_t = B_{FY}\). YTD budget at month \(k\) is \(B_{YTD,k} = \sum_{t=1}^{k} B_t\). The monthly variance for a cost line is \(B_t - A_t\) and the YTD variance \(B_{YTD,k} - A_{YTD,k}\) (positive = underspend, see c01).

Phasing methods

Method How Suitable for Risk
Flat (1/12) \(B_t = B_{FY}/12\) Genuinely even costs: rent, AMC contracts billed monthly, depreciation Artificial variances for any seasonal or event-driven line
Calendar/working days Weight by working days or calendar days Contract labour, power, security Ignores events
Prior-year profile Use last year's actual monthly shape Stable recurring costs Repeats last year's anomalies (one-offs, delays)
Driver-based \(B_t = \text{driver}_t \times \text{rate}\) Freight (tonnes), commission (sales), power (units produced) Needs a phased volume plan
Event/calendar-based Bottom-up from a plan: campaign calendar, hiring plan, contract renewal dates, audit fees Marketing, recruitment, IT licences, insurance, professional fees Labour-intensive; needs owner input

Indian calendar drivers to build in

  • Festive season: Navratri–Diwali (October–November) drives A&P, trade promotion and secondary freight for consumer companies; wedding season for jewellery and apparel; monsoon for agri-inputs and two-wheelers in rural markets.
  • Increments and bonuses: annual increments commonly effective 1 April or 1 July; statutory bonus under the Payment of Bonus Act paid within eight months of year-end; performance bonus often accrued monthly but paid in May–June.
  • Annual contracts: SaaS licences, insurance premiums and AMC renewals — the expense should be phased evenly (prepaid and amortised), even though the cash is lumpy.
  • Year-end effects: March has audit fees, provisions and true-ups; Q4 often has catch-up spending to "use the budget", which good phasing should not encourage.
  • Hiring: phase salary cost from expected joining dates, not from approval dates; Indian notice periods of 60–90 days mean a role approved in April may join only in August.

Worked illustration

Annual marketing budget ₹12 crore, phased flat at ₹1 crore per month. The approved media plan, however, is: April–September ₹0.50 crore a month (₹3.0 crore), October ₹3.5 crore, November ₹3.0 crore, December–March ₹0.625 crore a month (₹2.5 crore). Total ₹12 crore.

Month-end YTD flat budget YTD plan-based budget YTD actual Variance vs flat Variance vs plan
September 6.00 3.00 4.50 +1.50 −1.50
November 8.00 9.50 9.80 −1.80 −0.30

Against the flat budget, September shows ₹1.5 crore underspend and November ₹1.8 crore overspend — both meaningless. Against the plan-based phasing, September reveals a real issue: H1 actual (₹4.5 crore) is ₹1.5 crore above the plan, suggesting unplanned H1 spends that should be challenged before the festive season. Same annual budget, opposite conclusions.

Effect on YTD vs monthly variances

  • Monthly variances are most sensitive to phasing; YTD variances smooth timing but accumulate phasing error until the event passes.
  • At year-end, phasing differences disappear: FY variance = \(B_{FY} - A_{FY}\) whatever the phasing. Hence the importance of the full-year LE (s04) in mid-year analysis.
  • Quarterly reporting to the board (SEBI LODR Regulation 33 quarterly results) magnifies the effect: a Q2 "saving" from poor phasing becomes a Q3 "overrun".

Re-phasing after approval

Some companies allow re-phasing of the budget during the year (keeping the annual total) when plans change; others freeze the budget phasing and capture the shift in the LE. Re-phasing the budget hides timing variances against the original plan and can be used to manage optics. Best practice: freeze the AOP phasing; show the revised timing in the LE (Week 18 versions), and explain timing variances against the frozen AOP.

Excel implementation

Store phasing as a weights table (line × month, rows summing to 100%) and compute =FY_Budget*Weight. A check column =SUM(Weights)-1 must be zero. For driver-based lines, compute =PhasedVolume*Rate. In Power BI, store monthly budget at the grain of the actual fact table so that time-intelligence YTD measures (TOTALYTD with a fiscal year end of 31 March) work for both.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Lines with lumpy cash but even consumption (insurance, licences) — phase the expense evenly; lumpy phasing usually means the budget was built on a cash basis by mistake.
  • Accrual-based actuals vs cash-based budget owners — if the budget owner phased on payment dates but accounting accrues on consumption, variances arise every month; align the basis.
  • Prior-year profiles containing one-offs — strip them before using the profile.
  • New cost lines with no history — phase from the project plan and revisit at the first LE.
  • Over-engineered phasing for immaterial lines wastes time; apply driver or event phasing to the top lines that make up most of the variance-commentary risk, flat for the rest.

Key takeaways

  • Phasing decides every monthly and YTD variance; poor phasing creates artificial variances that reverse.
  • Use driver-based or event-based phasing for seasonal and event-driven lines, flat only for genuinely even costs.
  • Build Indian calendar drivers: festive season, increment month, bonus payment, contract renewals, hiring lead times.
  • Phase expenses on an accrual basis, not on cash payment dates.
  • Freeze AOP phasing and reflect revised timing in the LE rather than re-phasing the budget.

Practice Questions

Q1. Re-phasing a marketing budget [L1]

A consumer durables company has an annual A&P budget of ₹36 crore. The media plan concentrates 40% in October–November, 20% in March (summer launch of air-conditioners), and spreads the balance evenly across the other nine months. Prepare the monthly phasing and compare the YTD budget at 30 September under flat and plan-based phasing.

Q2. Diagnosing a phasing artefact [L2]

YTD June employee cost: budget ₹15.0 crore (flat-phased from an annual ₹60 crore), actual ₹13.8 crore. The annual budget assumed 8% increments effective 1 July on a base of ₹56 crore annualised and 20 new hires (average CTC ₹18 lakh) joining from 1 April. In reality, 14 of the 20 joined on 1 June and the rest are expected in October. Estimate how much of the YTD variance is a phasing artefact of the flat budget and how much is hiring delay.

💡 Hint: First rebuild what a correctly phased Q1 budget would have been.

Q3. Choosing methods [L2]

For each of the following lines, recommend a phasing method and the driver or source data: (a) secondary freight, (b) statutory audit fee, (c) annual ERP licence renewal of ₹2.4 crore paid in April, (d) sales commission at 2% of net revenue, (e) recruitment agency fees, (f) factory security services.

Q4. Re-phasing governance [L3]

The business unit head asks FP&A to re-phase the AOP marketing budget in August, moving ₹3 crore from Q2 to Q3, "so the monthly pack stops showing a false underspend". Write a short reply explaining the governance concern and propose how the shift should be shown instead, referring to versions and the LE.
