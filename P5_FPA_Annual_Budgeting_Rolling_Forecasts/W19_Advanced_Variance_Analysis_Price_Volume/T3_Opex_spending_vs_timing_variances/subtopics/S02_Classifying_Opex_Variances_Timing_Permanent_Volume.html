Subtopic note

Classifying Opex Variances: Timing, Permanent, Volume-Driven, One-Off

Scope: Classifying variances: timing (shifts between months), permanent (savings/overruns), volume-driven, one-offs

Why it matters

A YTD opex variance number on its own does not tell management what to do. A ₹2 crore underspend could be money that will still be spent next quarter (timing), a genuine saving that can be released to fund something else (permanent), the natural consequence of lower sales volume (volume-driven), or a single non-recurring item that distorts the run rate (one-off). Each class has a different implication for the full-year forecast, for the EBITDA outlook given to the board and analysts, for management incentive calculations, and for the decision whether to re-deploy funds. Classification is therefore the heart of opex variance commentary, and the four classes should appear explicitly in every opex variance table.

Core notes

The four classes

Class Definition Test FY implication
Timing Spend shifts between months; total for the year unchanged YTD variance exists but FY LE ≈ FY budget for that item Reverses later in the year; no change to FY EBITDA
Permanent True saving or overrun that will not reverse FY LE differs from FY budget Changes FY EBITDA; may need corrective action or re-allocation
Volume-driven Variable opex moving with activity (freight, commission, packing, royalty) Variance explained by actual vs budget activity × budget rate Offset by contribution on the volume; judge spending at flexed budget
One-off Non-recurring item (litigation settlement, restructuring, a prior-period invoice, a donation) Identifiable event, not expected to recur Excluded from run rate; may be adjusted in "adjusted EBITDA" (Phase 2)

The classes are not mutually exclusive in a single line; a line's variance is decomposed into components:

\[ \text{Total YTD variance} = \text{Volume-driven} + \text{Spending}, \qquad \text{Spending} = \text{Timing} + \text{Permanent (incl. one-offs)} \]

with one-offs tagged within the permanent component, because a one-off that has happened will not reverse.

The decision sequence

  1. Validate the actual: are accruals complete, is cut-off right, is the cost in the right cost centre? (s03, s05, s06). An accounting artefact is not a variance at all — correct it first.
  2. Flex for volume: for variable lines, compute the volume-driven component at budget rate (c03) and the spending variance against the flexed budget (c04).
  3. Use the LE to split spending into permanent (FY budget − FY LE) and timing (YTD variance − permanent) (c02, s04).
  4. Tag one-offs inside permanent, with event descriptions.
  5. Apply materiality for commentary (c05).

Worked illustration

Half-year (April–September) data, ₹ crore, positive = favourable.

Line H1 budget H1 actual H1 variance FY budget FY LE Permanent Timing
Marketing 6.00 4.50 +1.50 12.00 11.60 +0.40 +1.10
Legal & professional 1.20 1.65 −0.45 2.40 2.85 −0.45 0.00
Secondary freight 3.00 3.36 −0.36 6.00 6.70 −0.70 +0.34

Additional facts: legal includes a ₹0.45 crore one-off arbitration settlement (permanent, one-off). Freight is budgeted at ₹1,200/t on 25,000 t in H1; actual H1 volume was 28,000 t, so the flexed budget is ₹3.36 crore — the entire H1 freight variance is volume-driven (−₹0.36 crore), the spending variance is zero. The freight LE of ₹6.70 crore reflects higher full-year volume; its "permanent" −0.70 is therefore volume-driven, not overspend, and the +0.34 "timing" is a mechanical result of the LE also embedding H2 volume. This shows why volume must be flexed out before applying the timing/permanent split to variable lines: apply the LE test to the spending variance, comparing FY flexed budget with FY LE.

Commentary for marketing: "₹1.5 crore H1 underspend: ₹1.1 crore timing (festive campaign phased to October–November; reflected in LE), ₹0.4 crore permanent saving from renegotiated digital media rates."

Why the classification matters downstream

  • Full-year guidance: only permanent variances change FY EBITDA; timing variances should not move guidance.
  • Incentives: bonuses tied to YTD opex underspend reward deferral; tie them to permanent, FY-based measures.
  • Budget re-allocation: only permanent savings can be released (e.g., to a ZBB package — Week 17).
  • Adjusted EBITDA: one-offs may be excluded in management or analyst "adjusted" metrics; definitions must be consistent and are scrutinised under SEBI guidance on non-GAAP measures in presentations (Phase 2).
  • Volume-driven variances belong with revenue: a freight overrun caused by 12% higher volume is part of a profitable volume story; on the EBITDA bridge it can be grouped with volume (Topic 4).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Timing that becomes permanent: a campaign "delayed to Q4" that is later cancelled converts timing to permanent. Re-test each month; label "timing" only when the LE explicitly includes the spend.
  • Permanent overrun disguised as timing: owners like "it will reverse"; insist on evidence (PO, contract, plan date).
  • Semi-variable lines: power, maintenance, contract labour have fixed and variable parts; flex only the variable part.
  • Recurring "one-offs": if restructuring costs appear every year, they are not one-off; challenge the tag.
  • Prior-period items: an invoice for last year's service booked this year is a one-off this year and, if material, possibly a prior-period error under Ind AS 8 — escalate to financial reporting.

Key takeaways

  • Every opex variance is decomposed into volume-driven, timing and permanent (with one-offs tagged).
  • Validate accounting first, flex for volume second, apply the LE test third, tag one-offs fourth, then apply materiality.
  • Only permanent variances change FY EBITDA; timing variances reverse and should not change guidance.
  • For variable lines, apply the timing/permanent test to spending against the flexed budget, not to the raw variance.
  • Re-test classifications every month; timing can silently become permanent.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify five variances [L1]

For each item, state the class(es) and the FY EBITDA implication: (a) ₹40 lakh underspend on training because a leadership programme moved from August to January; (b) ₹25 lakh overrun on travel due to an unbudgeted customer conference; (c) ₹60 lakh higher sales commission on 8% higher sales; (d) ₹18 lakh saving on telecom from a renegotiated contract for the rest of the year; (e) ₹35 lakh penalty paid to a pollution control board.

Q2. Timing/permanent split with volume [L2]

Q2 YTD (April–September) commission budget ₹2.40 crore at 1.5% of budgeted net sales of ₹160 crore; actual commission ₹2.70 crore on net sales of ₹172 crore. FY budget commission ₹5.10 crore on sales of ₹340 crore; FY LE commission ₹5.50 crore on LE sales of ₹352 crore. Compute the volume-driven and spending variances YTD, and split the spending variance into timing and permanent using the flexed FY budget.

💡 Hint: Flex the FY budget at LE sales before comparing it with the FY LE.

Q3. Commentary rewrite [L2]

Rewrite this commentary so that it classifies the variance properly: "Opex is ₹3.2 crore favourable YTD due to cost control across functions." Supporting facts: ₹1.8 crore from IT projects delayed to Q4 (in LE), ₹0.9 crore lower freight on 7% lower volumes, ₹0.8 crore saving from closing a regional office (permanent), and a ₹0.3 crore adverse one-off for a consumer-court settlement.

Q4. Incentive design [L3]

A company pays functional heads a bonus based on "YTD opex underspend vs budget at the end of Q3". Explain the behaviours this encourages, quantify an example where a head earns a bonus while FY opex overruns, and propose an alternative metric built on the classifications in this note.
