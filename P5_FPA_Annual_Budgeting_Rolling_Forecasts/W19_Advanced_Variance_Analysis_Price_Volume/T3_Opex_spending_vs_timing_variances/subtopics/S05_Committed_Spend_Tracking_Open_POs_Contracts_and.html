Subtopic note

Committed Spend Tracking: Open POs, Contracts and Accrual Completeness

Scope: Committed spend tracking: open POs, contracts and accrual completeness

Why it matters

The general ledger records what has been incurred (and, if accruals are incomplete, only what has been invoiced). Budget owners, however, make spending decisions when they commit — when a purchase order is released, a contract signed or a media slot booked. Between commitment and accounting recognition there can be weeks or months. If FP&A looks only at GL actuals, it sees "underspend" that is already contractually spoken for, and the LE understates the year's cost. Commitment tracking closes that gap: it gives a forward view of spend that is already locked in, it is the evidence base for timing classification (s04), and it is the main tool for testing whether month-end accruals are complete.

Core notes

The spend lifecycle

Stage System event (SAP / Oracle) Recognised in GL? FP&A view
Budget / plan AOP, budget availability control No Budget
Purchase requisition PR created No Pre-commitment (optional)
Purchase order / contract PO released; contract or scheduling agreement No (commitment) Committed
Goods receipt / service entry GRN or service entry sheet posted; GR/IR credited Yes — expense or inventory Dr, GR/IR Cr Incurred (actual)
Invoice receipt Invoice verified; GR/IR cleared to vendor payable Already recognised at GR Actual
Payment Payment run No P&L effect Cash

Key relationship for any cost centre or budget line:

\[ \text{Available budget} = \text{Budget} - \text{Actual (incurred)} - \text{Open commitments} \]

Open commitments are POs and contracted amounts not yet received (goods or services not yet delivered). Commitments do not hit the P&L; they predict when cost will hit.

Systems

  • SAP CO/MM: when a PO is created with an account assignment to a cost centre, internal order or WBS element, SAP CO records a commitment. Commitment line items can be reported by cost centre (for example, transaction KSB2 for cost-centre commitment line items, alongside KSB1 for actual line items); open POs are listed with ME2N/ME2L/ME2K. Budget availability control (on internal orders and projects) can warn or block when actual + commitment exceeds budget.
  • Oracle: encumbrance accounting records requisition and purchase-order encumbrances against budget in Oracle General Ledger/Purchasing; funds checking compares budget with actuals plus encumbrances.
  • Smaller ERPs / Tally: commitments often live in spreadsheets maintained by procurement or functions; FP&A must standardise the template (vendor, PO/contract reference, total value, delivered to date, expected delivery month).

Accrual completeness tests

Commitment data is also the best evidence for testing month-end accruals:

  1. GR/IR review: goods/services received but not invoiced are already accrued via GR/IR — but only if goods receipts and service entry sheets are posted. Unposted service entries for completed services (professional fees, events, maintenance) are the commonest gap.
  2. Open PO aging vs delivery schedule: POs whose delivery date has passed but which show no receipt are either delayed (timing) or delivered but not recorded (missing accrual). Ask the requester.
  3. Contracts with time-based billing (rent, AMC, security, housekeeping, SaaS): expected monthly cost = contract value ÷ term; compare with GL each month.
  4. Recurring vendor analysis: vendors who invoice every month but have no entry this month — accrue on run rate if the service was received.
  5. Subsequent invoices: review invoices received in the first weeks after close for services before the cut-off (the "search for unrecorded liabilities" from audit practice).

Illustration

Marketing cost centre at 30 September, ₹ crore.

Item Amount
FY budget 12.00
YTD actual (GL) 4.50
Open POs for October–November media (released orders) 5.20
Signed agency retainer for October–March not yet invoiced 0.90
Open commitments total 6.10
Available budget \(= 12.00 - 4.50 - 6.10\) 1.40

The LE (₹11.6 crore) implies ₹7.1 crore further spend: ₹6.1 crore is committed, so only ₹1.0 crore of the forecast is uncommitted — the timing classification is well evidenced. If commitments were only ₹2 crore, a ₹7.1 crore rest-of-year forecast would need challenge.

Also: a September event (₹0.35 crore) was completed but the agency invoice and service entry are pending. It sits in open POs, not in GL actuals — so YTD actual is understated and the "underspend" overstated. Correct with an accrual:

Entry Dr Cr ₹ crore
30 September: accrue event services received Marketing expense Accrued expenses / GR/IR 0.35

After the accrual, YTD actual becomes ₹4.85 crore and open commitments ₹5.75 crore; available budget unchanged.

Using commitments in the LE

A robust rest-of-year forecast for discretionary lines is built as:

\[ F_{ROY} = \text{Open commitments due in remaining months} + \text{Planned but uncommitted spend} - \text{Expected cancellations} \]

Planned but uncommitted spend should be supported by an approval stage (approved PR, approved ZBB package — Week 17). The share of the forecast that is committed is a useful LE-quality KPI.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Blanket / framework POs (an annual PO for ₹2 crore of freight "as and when") overstate commitment; use expected consumption, not PO value.
  • Commitments for capex must not be mixed with opex commitments; check account assignment (asset vs cost centre).
  • Cancellable vs non-cancellable: a cancellable media booking is a soft commitment; non-cancellable contracts may become onerous under Ind AS 37 if the benefit is lost.
  • Foreign-currency POs: commitment value moves with the rate; revalue for the LE.
  • Retrospective POs (PO raised after invoice) defeat commitment tracking and are a P2P control weakness to flag to internal audit.
  • Ind AS disclosure: capital commitments are disclosed in financial statements (Schedule III requires disclosure of estimated amount of contracts remaining to be executed on capital account); opex commitments are management information only.

Key takeaways

  • Commitments (POs, contracts, bookings) predict cost before it reaches the GL; available budget = budget − actual − commitments.
  • SAP CO records commitments on account-assigned POs; Oracle uses encumbrance accounting; smaller ERPs need a standard tracker.
  • Commitment and GR/IR data are the best tests of accrual completeness at month-end.
  • Use committed share of the rest-of-year forecast to evidence timing classifications.
  • Treat blanket POs, cancellable bookings and retrospective POs with care.

Practice Questions

Q1. Available budget [L1]

An IT cost centre has an FY budget of ₹8.4 crore, YTD actual of ₹5.1 crore, open POs of ₹2.6 crore (of which ₹0.4 crore relates to a blanket PO expected to be only 50% consumed) and an approved but unreleased PR of ₹0.7 crore. Compute available budget on a strict and on an expected-consumption basis.

Q2. Accrual completeness review [L2]

At 31 December you obtain: GR/IR balance ₹1.8 crore; open POs past delivery date with no GRN ₹0.9 crore (requesters confirm ₹0.5 crore of services were completed in December); monthly security contract ₹12 lakh with no December invoice or entry; a consultant's invoice for ₹22 lakh dated 10 January for work done in November–December. Quantify the missing accruals, pass the journal entries, and state the effect on the YTD opex variance.

💡 Hint: Only services received by 31 December belong in December actuals.

Q3. Commitment-backed LE [L2]

A function's 6+6 LE shows rest-of-year spend of ₹9.0 crore against a remaining budget of ₹7.0 crore. Open commitments for October–March are ₹4.2 crore; approved ZBB packages not yet committed total ₹2.5 crore. Assess the credibility of the timing variance implied, compute the share of the forecast that is committed, and recommend the LE you would accept.

Q4. Designing a commitment tracker [L3]

A mid-size company uses Tally Prime and has no PO-based commitment reporting. Design a commitment tracker for marketing and IT: list its fields, the owner of each field, the monthly process (who updates, when, review by FP&A), the reconciliations to the GL, and the two reports you would produce for the CFO.
