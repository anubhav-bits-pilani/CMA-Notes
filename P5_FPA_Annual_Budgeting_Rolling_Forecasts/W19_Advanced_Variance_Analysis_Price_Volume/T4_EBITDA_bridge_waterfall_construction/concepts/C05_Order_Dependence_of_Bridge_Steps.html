Key concept

Order Dependence of Bridge Steps

Concept: Order dependence: changing sequence of price/volume/mix changes individual bars unless formulas are defined consistently

Statement

If bridge steps are computed by sequential substitution — changing one driver at a time from base to actual while holding later drivers at base — then the value of step \(k\) depends on which drivers were already moved before it. For revenue \(R = \sum_i Q_{\text{tot}}\, s_i\, P_i\) with drivers total volume \(Q_{\text{tot}}\), mix \(s_i\) and price \(P_i\):

\[ \text{Step}_k = R\left(x^{A}_{1},\dots,x^{A}_{k},\,x^{B}_{k+1},\dots,x^{B}_{n}\right) - R\left(x^{A}_{1},\dots,x^{A}_{k-1},\,x^{B}_{k},\dots,x^{B}_{n}\right) \]

where \(x_j\) are the drivers in the chosen order and superscripts A/B denote actual/base values. The total is always exact, but individual steps change with the order because of interaction terms. Bars become order-independent only if each step is defined by a fixed formula (price at actual quantity, volume and mix at base unit values) irrespective of where the bar is drawn.

Explanation

Revenue and margin are products of drivers (quantity × mix × price). Whenever two drivers both change, their product contains an interaction term \(\Delta x \times \Delta y\). Sequential substitution assigns that term to whichever driver is moved later, because by then the other driver is already at actual. Hence:

  • Price first (sequential): price valued at budget quantities; the joint price × quantity term goes to volume and mix.
  • Price last (sequential): price valued at actual quantities — identical to the fixed formula \(\sum_i (P^{A}_i - P^{B}_i) Q^{A}_i\).

The FP&A convention (topic 1 and c02 of this topic) defines each step by formula so that the drawn order of bars is purely presentational. You can then display Mix before Volume, or FX before Price, without changing any number. What remains a real methodological choice is which formula set you adopt — for example, whether the joint term goes to price (standard) or is shown separately as "price/volume interaction" (rare in executive packs, common in academic treatments), and in hierarchical mix (topic 1, s03) whether channel mix is computed before product mix within channel.

Governance implications: document the formula set in the pack methodology, do not change it mid-year, and if you must change it, restate prior months so the trend of bars is comparable. When analysts in different business units use different conventions, consolidated bridges will not add across units.

Illustration

Two SKUs: budget A 60 lakh units at ₹50, B 40 lakh at ₹80 (revenue ₹6,200 lakh); actual A 75 at ₹49, B 35 at ₹84 (revenue ₹6,615 lakh). Revenue sub-bridge by sequential substitution in two orders:

Step Order 1: Price → Volume → Mix Order 2: Volume → Mix → Price
Price 60 × 49 + 40 × 84 − 6,200 = +100 6,615 − 6,550 = +65
Volume 66 × 49 + 44 × 84 − 6,300 = +630 66 × 50 + 44 × 80 − 6,200 = +620
Mix 6,615 − 6,930 = −315 75 × 50 + 35 × 80 − 6,820 = −270
Total +415 +415

Order 2 reproduces the fixed-formula results (price at actual qty +65; volume at budget price +620; mix at budget price −270). Order 1 moves ₹35 lakh of interaction into price and redistributes it across volume and mix. A sales head shown Order 1 one month and Order 2 the next would see a ₹35 lakh "improvement" in pricing that never happened.

Connections

Order dependence explains why bridge integrity (c01) alone is not enough — every order reconciles, but only a fixed formula set gives stable bars. It links to the valuation rules in c02, the margin bridge choice in c04 (sequential recomputation is order-dependent; the margin-neutral method is not), hierarchical mix in topic 1 (s03), and the CMA treatment of sales variances where price variance is on actual quantity.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two orders, one product [L1]

Budget: 2,00,000 units at ₹150. Actual: 2,30,000 units at ₹162. Compute the price and volume steps under (a) price-then-volume sequential substitution and (b) volume-then-price sequential substitution. Identify the interaction term and state which order matches the standard FP&A formula.

Q2. Consolidation across units [L3]

Division North computes its bridge by sequential substitution in the order Price → Volume → Mix; Division South uses the fixed formula set. Group FP&A adds the bars to produce a group bridge. Explain why the group Price bar is not meaningful, and design a methodology note and a check that would detect such inconsistency in future.

💡 Hint: Recompute one division's bars under the other's method and compare the price bars.

Q3. Presenting the interaction term [L2]

A board member asks why there is no "price × volume" bar in your bridge. Explain in four to five sentences where the interaction is, why it is placed there, and when you would show it separately.
