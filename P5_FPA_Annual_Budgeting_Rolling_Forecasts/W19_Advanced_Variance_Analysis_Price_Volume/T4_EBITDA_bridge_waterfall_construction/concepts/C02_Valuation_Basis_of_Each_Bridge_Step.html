Key concept

Valuation Basis of Each Bridge Step

Concept: Volume step at budget unit contribution/margin; price step at actual volume; cost steps at actual volume

Statement

With base B (budget) and comparator A (actual), SKUs \(i\), units \(Q_i\), unit price \(P_i\), unit cost \(c_i\) and unit margin \(m_i = P_i - c_i\):

\[ \text{Price} = \sum_i (P^{A}_i - P^{B}_i)\,Q^{A}_i \qquad \text{Cost} = \sum_i (c^{B}_i - c^{A}_i)\,Q^{A}_i \]

\[ \text{Volume} = \left(Q^{A}_{\text{tot}} - Q^{B}_{\text{tot}}\right)\bar{m}^{B}, \qquad \bar{m}^{B} = \frac{\sum_i Q^{B}_i\, m^{B}_i}{Q^{B}_{\text{tot}}} \]

\[ \text{Mix} = \sum_i \left(Q^{A}_i - Q^{A}_{\text{tot}}\, s^{B}_i\right) m^{B}_i, \qquad s^{B}_i = \frac{Q^{B}_i}{Q^{B}_{\text{tot}}} \]

where \(\bar{m}^{B}\) is the budget weighted-average unit margin and \(s^{B}_i\) the budget mix share. If the bridge is built at contribution level, \(m_i\) is unit contribution (price less all variable costs including variable selling costs).

Explanation

The rule allocates each element of the change to exactly one bar:

  • Price at actual volume answers "what did the price change earn on what we actually sold?". It absorbs the price × volume interaction, so no joint variance remains.
  • Cost at actual volume answers "what did the change in unit cost cost us on what we actually sold?". Same logic, applied to cost.
  • Volume and mix at budget unit margin answer "what would the change in quantity and composition have earned at budget economics?". Using budget margin keeps price and cost changes out of these bars, so the sales team's volume achievement is not flattered or penalised by price or inflation.

Together, price + cost = \(\sum_i (m^{A}_i - m^{B}_i) Q^{A}_i\) (the margin-rate effect on actual units) and volume + mix = \(\sum_i (Q^{A}_i - Q^{B}_i) m^{B}_i\) (the quantity effect at budget margin). Their sum is \(\sum_i (m^{A}_i Q^{A}_i - m^{B}_i Q^{B}_i)\) — exactly the GM variance.

Margin vs contribution. At gross-margin level, \(m_i\) excludes variable selling costs, and freight/commission volume effects appear in opex unless flexed. At contribution level, they sit inside volume. Pick one per pack.

CMA link. This is the same as the CMA sales margin quantity variance (valued at standard margin) and the sales price variance computed on actual quantity, with the quantity variance split into mix and yield (here "volume").

Illustration

Two SKUs as in c01: budget A 60 lakh units (margin ₹15), B 40 lakh (margin ₹32); actual A 75, B 35. Budget average margin \(\bar{m}^{B} = (60 \times 15 + 40 \times 32)/100 = ₹21.8\).

Step Computation ₹ lakh
Volume (110 − 100) × 21.8 +218
Mix (75 − 110 × 0.60) × 15 + (35 − 110 × 0.40) × 32 = 9 × 15 − 9 × 32 −153
Wrong volume (at budget price ₹62 avg) 10 × 62 +620

Using revenue volume (+620) in the EBITDA bridge would overstate volume by 10 × ₹40.2 average budget unit cost = ₹402 lakh, which then reappears as a mysterious negative balancing bar.

Connections

The rule underpins bridge integrity (c01), the stability of bars across sequences (c05) and the margin bridge (c04). It reuses topic 1's revenue PVM formulas with margin replacing price (topic 1 concepts c02 and c03), and topic 2's cost variances supply the split of the cost step into inflation and efficiency. Practice Questions

Q1. Volume at margin vs contribution [L2]

Budget: 5,00,000 cases, price ₹1,000, COGS ₹620 per case, freight and trade commission ₹80 per case. Actual: 5,40,000 cases, all unit values at budget. (a) Compute the Volume bar at unit gross margin and at unit contribution. (b) Under each approach, what does the Opex bar show for freight and commission if opex is otherwise on budget?

Q2. Prove the decomposition [L3]

Using the formulas in the Statement, show algebraically that Price + Cost + Volume + Mix equals actual GM minus budget GM for any number of SKUs. Then explain which term disappears if price is computed at budget quantity and what new term appears.

💡 Hint: Write volume + mix as a single sum over SKUs of the quantity change times budget margin.

Q3. Interview: why budget margin? [L1]

In two or three sentences suitable for an FP&A interview, explain why volume is valued at budget unit margin and not at actual unit margin.
