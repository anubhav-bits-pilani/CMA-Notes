Key concept

EBITDA Margin Bridge in Basis Points

Concept: EBITDA margin bridge: margin % change decomposed in bps per driver

Statement

Let \(E\) be EBITDA, \(R\) revenue, \(M = E/R\) the EBITDA margin, superscripts B (base) and A (actual). Each bridge step \(k\) has an EBITDA effect \(\Delta E_k\) and a revenue effect \(\Delta R_k\), with \(\sum_k \Delta E_k = E^{A} - E^{B}\) and \(\sum_k \Delta R_k = R^{A} - R^{B}\). The margin-neutral decomposition is:

\[ \text{bps}_k = \frac{\Delta E_k - M^{B}\,\Delta R_k}{R^{A}} \times 10{,}000, \qquad \sum_k \text{bps}_k = \left(M^{A} - M^{B}\right)\times 10{,}000 \]

The identity holds exactly because \(\sum_k (\Delta E_k - M^{B} \Delta R_k) = E^{A} - E^{B} - M^{B}(R^{A} - R^{B}) = E^{A} - M^{B} R^{A}\), and dividing by \(R^{A}\) gives \(M^{A} - M^{B}\). One percentage point of margin = 100 bps.

Explanation

A ₹ bridge says how much EBITDA changed; a margin bridge says why the margin % changed. They differ because some drivers change revenue as well as EBITDA. A step that adds revenue at exactly the budget margin (for example, a volume increase where all costs are variable) leaves the margin unchanged and should show 0 bps; the formula achieves this by charging each step's revenue effect at the base margin.

Interpretation by driver:

  • Price: \(\Delta R = \Delta E\), so bps = \(\Delta E (1 - M^{B}) / R^{A}\) — price drops almost fully to margin.
  • Volume: \(\Delta R\) at budget price, \(\Delta E\) at budget unit margin (or contribution). With fixed opex, volume improves EBITDA margin through operating leverage; at gross-margin level with no fixed costs it would be close to zero.
  • Mix: positive if the mix moves to SKUs with margin % above the base, regardless of the ₹ sign.
  • Cost and opex steps: \(\Delta R = 0\), so bps = \(\Delta E / R^{A} \times 10{,}000\).

Alternatives used in practice: (1) sequential recomputation — recompute margin after each step in order; exact but order-dependent (c05); (2) step ÷ actual revenue plus a "revenue dilution" bar \(E^{B}(1/R^{A} - 1/R^{B})\); exact but the dilution bar is hard to explain. The margin-neutral formula is exact and order-independent, which is why it is preferred for executive packs.

Illustration

Two-SKU case (₹ lakh): budget revenue 6,200, EBITDA 800 (\(M^{B}\) = 12.903%); actual revenue 6,615, EBITDA 760 (\(M^{A}\) = 11.489%). Change = −141.4 bps.

Step \(\Delta E\) \(\Delta R\) bps
Price +65 +65 +85.6
Volume +218 +620 +208.6
Mix −153 −270 −178.6
Cost −220 0 −332.6
Opex +50 0 +75.6
Total −40 +415 −141.4

For volume: \((218 - 0.12903 \times 620)/6{,}615 \times 10{,}000 = 208.6\) bps. The message for the CFO: "Margin fell 141 bps although EBITDA was only ₹40 lakh below budget: cost inflation took 333 bps and adverse mix 179 bps; operating leverage from volume and pricing recovered 294 bps."

Connections

The margin bridge complements the ₹ bridge in s05 (where bps-of-revenue labels do not sum to margin change) and appears in the reconciliation table (s06). It extends topic 2's GM bps analysis (concepts c05 and c06 of topic 2) to EBITDA, and it is the analytical answer to the week's assessment question of why margin fell despite higher sales.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute a margin bridge [L2]

Budget revenue ₹1,200 crore, EBITDA ₹180 crore. Steps (₹ crore, EBITDA effect / revenue effect): Price +24 / +24; Volume +30 / +110; Mix −12 / +6; RM inflation −40 / 0; Opex +8 / 0. Compute actual revenue, actual EBITDA, the margin change in bps, and the bps contribution of each step using the margin-neutral formula. Verify the steps sum to the total.

Q2. Why mix can be adverse in bps but favourable in ₹ [L2]

Explain, with a two-SKU numerical example of your own, how a mix shift can increase EBITDA in ₹ while reducing EBITDA margin %, and what sign the margin-neutral mix bps would show.

💡 Hint: Shift volume towards a SKU with a higher ₹ margin per unit but a lower margin %.

Q3. Choosing the method [L3]

Your CFO wants the margin bridge to "add up and not change when we reorder the bars". Compare sequential recomputation, step ÷ actual revenue with a dilution bar, and the margin-neutral method against these two requirements, and recommend one with reasons.
