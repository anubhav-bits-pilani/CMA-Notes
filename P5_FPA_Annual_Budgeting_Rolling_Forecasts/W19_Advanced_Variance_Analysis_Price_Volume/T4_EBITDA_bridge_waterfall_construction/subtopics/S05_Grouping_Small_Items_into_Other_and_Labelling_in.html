Subtopic note

Grouping Small Items into "Other" and Labelling in ₹ Crore and bps

Scope: Grouping small items into "Other" and labelling with ₹ crore and bps

Why it matters

A bridge is a communication device. The engine behind it may have 40 drivers — every commodity, every cost-centre line, every SKU's mix effect — but a CFO or board member can absorb perhaps seven to nine bars. Grouping decides what the audience sees; labelling decides whether they understand scale. Indian management teams think in ₹ crore for absolute amounts and in basis points for margins; a bar labelled "−95.0" means little, while "−₹95 cr | −296 bps" tells the reader both the size and the margin damage. Done badly, grouping hides offsetting items or becomes a plug; done well, it makes the message obvious and keeps detail one click away.

Core notes

Grouping rules

A good grouping policy is written down once, applied every month, and reviewed when the business changes.

  1. Materiality threshold. Show a driver as its own bar if its absolute value exceeds a threshold, for example ₹10 crore or 2.5% of base EBITDA, whichever is lower. Everything below is grouped.
  2. Bar budget. Target six to nine movement bars between the two totals. If the threshold still leaves more, group at the next level of the driver hierarchy (e.g., all raw-material commodities into "RM inflation") and move the detail into a sub-bridge.
  3. Group by nature, not just by size. Small opex items go into "Other opex", small revenue-side items into "Other commercial", miscellaneous below-the-line items into "Others". Do not combine a revenue item with a cost item simply because both are small; the bar loses meaning.
  4. Gross, not net. Apply the threshold to each item's absolute value before netting. Two items of +₹40 cr and −₹38 cr must not disappear into a +₹2 cr "Other"; both are material and need their own bars, or at minimum a footnote.
  5. Stable layout. Keep the same set of bars from month to month where possible (fixed list), letting a bar be zero, rather than dynamically showing a different top-N each month. Dynamic top-N is acceptable for drill-down visuals, not for the headline bridge.
  6. Disclose contents. Every grouped bar carries a footnote or tooltip listing its components, and the reconciliation table (s06) shows them in full.

Labelling in ₹ crore and bps

Two numbers per bar are standard:

  • ₹ crore, one decimal for bars below ₹100 cr, none above (be consistent within a chart). Positive values with a "+" sign; negatives in brackets or with a "−" sign per the pack convention (topic 5).
  • bps of revenue for each step: \( \text{bps}_k = \frac{\text{Step}_k}{\text{Actual revenue}} \times 10{,}000 \). This expresses each bar as a share of the revenue base, which executives compare with the EBITDA margin.

For Godavari, Price is \( 45 / 3{,}210 \times 10{,}000 \approx 140 \) bps, RM inflation \( -95 / 3{,}210 \times 10{,}000 \approx -296 \) bps. The bps labels sum to about +25 bps (= ₹8 cr ÷ ₹3,210 cr), but the EBITDA margin actually fell from 14.0% to 13.3%, i.e. about −67 bps. The gap is the denominator effect: revenue grew, so the budget EBITDA is a smaller share of the larger actual revenue. Do not label ₹-bridge bars with bps and imply they sum to the margin change. Either (a) label bars with "bps of revenue" and state the margin change separately, or (b) build a proper margin bridge whose bars do sum to the margin change (concept c04).

Building labels in Excel

In the stacked-column method, build label text in a helper column and attach it via Format Data Labels → Value From Cells:

=TEXT(B3,"+#,##0.0;(#,##0.0)")&" cr"&CHAR(10)&TEXT(B3/$B$15*10000,"+0;(0)")&" bps"

where $B$15 holds actual revenue. Turn on Wrap text in label options so CHAR(10) breaks the line. For values held in rupees, an Indian-grouping custom number format such as [>=10000000]##\,##\,##\,##0;[>=100000]##\,##\,##0;##,##0 displays lakh/crore separators, but for bridges it is cleaner to convert to crore in the table and use #,##0.0.

Labelling in Power BI

Use a display measure in crore for the data label, and add bps and component lists as tooltip measures. A tooltip page can show the contents of "Others" as a small table filtered to the hovered step.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • "Other" becoming the largest bar. If grouped items sum to more than any named bar, the grouping is wrong; promote the biggest components.
  • Offsetting items inside one group. "Opex +₹22 cr" may combine +₹30 cr A&P underspend (timing) and −₹8 cr employee overrun (permanent). The label is fine, but the commentary must split them; consider separate bars for timing and permanent opex in an LE context.
  • Rounding. Labels rounded to one decimal may not visibly sum to the end bar. Footnote "Totals may not add due to rounding" or compute grouped bars from rounded components.
  • bps on which base? For an AvB bridge, actual revenue is common; some packs use budget revenue. Choose one, state it in the footnote, and apply consistently.
  • Very small companies or segments. A ₹10 cr threshold is meaningless for a ₹40 cr EBITDA segment; use a percentage-of-base threshold.

Key takeaways

  • Write a grouping policy: absolute and percentage thresholds, a bar budget of six to nine, grouping by nature, stable layout.
  • Apply thresholds to gross item values before netting so offsetting items stay visible.
  • Label each bar in ₹ crore and bps, and footnote what bps are measured against.
  • bps-of-revenue labels on a ₹ bridge do not sum to the margin change; use a margin bridge for that message.
  • Every grouped bar must be itemised in the reconciliation table or tooltip.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply a grouping policy [L2]

Budget EBITDA is ₹300 crore. Engine drivers (₹ crore): Price +21, Volume −14, Mix +3, Steel −26, Aluminium −4, Rubber −2, Conversion efficiency +6, Power and fuel −9, Employee cost −3, Travel +1, IT +2, A&P +12, FX +5, Export incentives +2, Warranty provision −7. Policy: separate bar if absolute value ≥ ₹8 crore or ≥ 3% of budget EBITDA, whichever is lower; group by nature. Design the bridge bars, compute actual EBITDA, and list the contents of each grouped bar.

Q2. Gross vs net grouping [L2]

In a month, the "Other opex" group contains a ₹14 crore saving from a cancelled sponsorship and a ₹13 crore overrun from an unbudgeted IT migration, netting to +₹1 crore. Explain why showing "+1" is misleading and how you would present these items in the bridge and the commentary.

Q3. Labels that mislead [L2]

Revenue: budget ₹1,800 crore, actual ₹2,050 crore. EBITDA: budget ₹270 crore, actual ₹281 crore. The analyst labels each bridge bar in bps of actual revenue and writes "net margin impact +54 bps". Compute the actual EBITDA margin change in bps and explain the discrepancy.

💡 Hint: Compare the sum of bps labels with the difference between the two margin percentages.

Q4. Excel label formula [L1]

Write an Excel formula that produces a two-line data label of the form "+45.0 cr" on the first line and "+140 bps" on the second, with negatives shown in brackets, given the step in C7 (₹ crore) and actual revenue in $C$20 (₹ crore). State the chart settings needed to display it.
