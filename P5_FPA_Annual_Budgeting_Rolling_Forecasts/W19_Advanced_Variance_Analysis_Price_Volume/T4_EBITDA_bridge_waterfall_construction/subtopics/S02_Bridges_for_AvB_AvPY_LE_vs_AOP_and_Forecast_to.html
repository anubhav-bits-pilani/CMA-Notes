Subtopic note

Bridges for AvB, AvPY, LE vs AOP and Forecast-to-Forecast

Scope: Bridges for different comparisons: Actual vs Budget, Actual vs PY, LE vs AOP, forecast-to-forecast

Why it matters

The same EBITDA bridge engine answers four different management questions. Actual vs Budget asks "did we deliver what we committed to?". Actual vs Prior Year asks "did the business grow?" — the question analysts and the board's audit committee ask of a listed company. LE vs AOP asks "where will we land for the year against the commitment?". Forecast-to-forecast asks "what changed in our view since last month?". Each comparison uses a different base, a different set of unit economics for valuing volume and mix, and different "special" steps. An FP&A analyst who can switch the engine between them without rebuilding the model saves days at every close.

Core notes

The general rule: value at the base scenario's unit economics

Whatever the comparison, call the left-hand bar the base (B) and the right-hand bar the comparator (C). The generic formulas are:

\[ \text{Price} = \sum_i (P^{C}_i - P^{B}_i)\times Q^{C}_i \qquad \text{Volume} = (Q^{C}_{\text{tot}} - Q^{B}_{\text{tot}})\times \bar{m}^{B} \]

\[ \text{Mix} = \sum_i (s^{C}_i - s^{B}_i)\times Q^{C}_{\text{tot}}\times m^{B}_i \qquad \text{Cost} = \sum_i (c^{B}_i - c^{C}_i)\times Q^{C}_i \]

where \(P_i\) is unit price, \(c_i\) unit cost, \(m_i = P_i - c_i\) unit margin, \(Q_i\) units, \(s_i = Q_i / Q_{\text{tot}}\) mix share, and \(\bar{m}^{B} = \sum_i Q^{B}_i m^{B}_i / Q^{B}_{\text{tot}}\) the base weighted-average unit margin. Superscript B is whatever sits on the left: budget, PY or the earlier forecast. Because the formulas are written generically, you switch comparison by pointing the B and C columns at different scenarios in the data table.

Actual vs Budget (AvB)

  • Base: AOP budget for the same period (month, quarter, YTD).
  • Unit economics: budget price, budget unit cost, budget FX rate.
  • Special steps: usually none beyond FX and one-offs. Timing effects in opex (topic 3) are a key commentary point because budget phasing is a construct.
  • Use: accountability; incentive scorecards; monthly business review.

Actual vs Prior Year (AvPY)

  • Base: last year's actual for the same period.
  • Unit economics: PY realised price and PY unit cost. The "price" bar now captures the year's price increases, and "COGS inflation" captures actual year-on-year input inflation — the same language used in analyst calls.
  • Special steps:
  • Acquisitions/divestments (inorganic). An acquired business has no PY base; show its EBITDA as a separate "Acquisition" bar so the remaining bars are like-for-like (organic). A divested business's PY EBITDA becomes a negative "Divestment" bar.
  • Accounting or definition changes. If PY was prepared on a different basis (for example a reclassification between COGS and opex, or a change in the EBITDA definition), restate PY or show a "Restatement" bar.
  • Calendar effects. An extra selling day, a Diwali shift between October and November, or a leap year can be a separate "Calendar/phasing" bar in quarterly or monthly AvPY bridges.
  • Use: growth story, investor presentations, segment analysis.

LE vs AOP

  • Base: full-year AOP.
  • Comparator: latest estimate (LE) for the full year = YTD actuals + forecast for remaining months.
  • Unit economics: AOP. The bridge shows where the full-year landing differs from the commitment, combining realised variances (YTD) and expected variances (rest of year). A useful refinement splits each bar into "YTD actual" and "Rest-of-year forecast" segments, or shows two bridges side by side.
  • Special steps: "Management actions / initiatives" (price increases announced, cost programmes) and "Risks and opportunities" if the LE is presented before risk adjustments.
  • Use: quarterly board updates, guidance setting, re-forecast sign-off.

Forecast-to-forecast (LE n vs LE n−1)

  • Base: previous forecast (e.g., the September LE).
  • Comparator: current forecast (e.g., the October LE).
  • Unit economics: the previous forecast's assumptions.
  • Special step — "Actualisation": the month that has just closed moved from forecast to actual. Its variance (October actual vs October in the September LE) is a separate bar from changes in the outlook for November–March. This tells management whether the forecast moved because of what happened or because of what they now expect.
  • Use: forecast accuracy tracking, rolling-forecast governance (Week 18).

Illustration

Narmada Polymers Ltd, full-year EBITDA (₹ crore):

Step LE vs AOP Oct LE vs Sep LE
Base AOP 640 Sep LE 612
Actualisation (October) — −6
Price +22 +4
Volume −35 −8
Mix −4 0
COGS inflation (crude derivatives) −41 +5
Efficiency +9 +1
Opex +12 −2
FX +3 0
Comparator Oct LE 606 Oct LE 606

The LE vs AOP bridge says the year is ₹34 cr short of AOP, mainly on volume and feedstock. The forecast-to-forecast bridge says that, in the last month, the view deteriorated by ₹6 cr, entirely due to a weak October (actualisation −6), while the outlook for November–March is unchanged in aggregate (+4 − 8 + 5 + 1 − 2 = 0).

Implementation: one engine, a scenario switch

In Excel, keep the data table in long format (Scenario, Period, SKU, Units, Revenue, COGS, Opex) and pull B and C columns with SUMIFS driven by two dropdown cells (Base scenario, Comparator scenario). For example:

=SUMIFS(Data[Units],Data[Scenario],$C$2,Data[SKU],$A8,Data[Period],$C$3)

In Power BI, use two disconnected scenario slicers (Base, Comparator) and measures that evaluate under each with CALCULATE and TREATAS. Only the special steps (Acquisition, Actualisation) need comparison-specific logic.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • YTD vs month vs full year. A monthly AvB bridge can be dominated by phasing; the YTD bridge is more stable. Packs usually show the month and YTD bridges side by side, and LE vs AOP for the full year.
  • PY price as base for an inflationary year. In an AvPY bridge, a 6% price increase that merely passed through 6% input inflation shows as two large offsetting bars. That is correct and informative — do not net them.
  • Budget rate vs PY rate for FX. In AvPY bridges, FX is measured against the PY average rate, not the budget rate.
  • Acquisition mid-year. Only the post-acquisition months are inorganic in the year of acquisition; the next year, months already owned in PY are organic. Keep a month-level flag.
  • Forecast-to-forecast with re-phased AOP. If the AOP itself was re-phased (not changed in total), the full-year LE vs AOP bridge is unaffected, but month-level bridges are — document re-phasing.

Key takeaways

  • One generic engine, driven by Base and Comparator selectors, serves all four comparisons.
  • Always value volume and mix at the base scenario's unit margin and price/cost at the comparator's volumes.
  • AvPY bridges need inorganic (acquisition/divestment) and restatement steps to stay like-for-like.
  • LE vs AOP combines realised and expected variances; splitting YTD from rest-of-year improves insight.
  • Forecast-to-forecast bridges must isolate actualisation of the closed month from changes in outlook.

Practice Questions

Q1. Matching comparison to question [L1]

For each of the following management questions, state which bridge (AvB, AvPY, LE vs AOP, forecast-to-forecast) answers it and what the base scenario's unit economics are: (a) "Will we hit the full-year EBITDA we guided?"; (b) "How much of this quarter's growth was organic?"; (c) "Why did our year-end view drop ₹10 crore since last month?"; (d) "Did the North region deliver its March plan?"

Q2. Organic vs inorganic AvPY [L2]

Q2 FY26 EBITDA is ₹185 crore versus ₹160 crore in Q2 FY25. On 1 July 2025 the company acquired a brand that contributed ₹12 crore EBITDA in Q2 FY26. In Q4 FY25 it sold a loss-making unit that had EBITDA of −₹3 crore in Q2 FY25. The remaining drivers are Price +₹14 crore, Volume +₹6 crore, Mix −₹2 crore, COGS −₹9 crore, Opex +₹1 crore. Build the AvPY bridge with inorganic steps and compute the organic EBITDA growth %.

Q3. Actualisation bar [L2]

The September LE forecast October EBITDA at ₹48 crore and November–March at ₹260 crore. October actual EBITDA is ₹43 crore. The October LE forecasts November–March at ₹268 crore. YTD April–September actual is ₹290 crore. Compute the September and October full-year LEs and lay out a forecast-to-forecast bridge with an actualisation bar and an outlook-change bar.

Q4. Excel scenario switch [L3]

Design the Excel layout for a bridge engine that switches between AvB, AvPY and LE vs AOP using two dropdowns. Specify the long-format data table columns, the SUMIFS structure for base and comparator units and margins, and the check cells you would include. Explain how the Acquisition step would be switched on only for the AvPY comparison.
