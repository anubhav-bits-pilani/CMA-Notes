Key concept

Revenue and Cost Variance Formulas: Positive Means Favourable

Concept: Revenue/margin variance = Actual − Budget; cost variance = Budget − Actual; positive = favourable in both

Statement

For a revenue, income or margin line \(r\) and a cost line \(c\):

\[ V_r = A_r - B_r, \qquad V_c = B_c - A_c \]

where \(A\) is the actual amount and \(B\) the budget (or other base) amount, both expressed as positive presentation amounts (unsigned convention). Under this definition \(V > 0\) is favourable to profit and \(V < 0\) is adverse for every line, and for any profit subtotal \(P = \sum r - \sum c\):

\[ V_P = A_P - B_P = \sum V_r + \sum V_c \]

so the profit variance is the simple sum of all line variances.

Explanation

The two formulas are the same idea — "effect on profit" — expressed for lines that add to profit (revenue) and lines that subtract from it (cost). More revenue raises profit, so Actual − Budget; more cost lowers profit, so Budget − Actual. The additive property is what makes the convention powerful: a variance column can be summed down the P&L to get the EBITDA variance, and a bridge built from the same numbers adds to the profit difference.

This is the CMA convention in disguise. In standard costing, a material price variance (Standard price − Actual price) × Actual quantity is favourable when positive; a sales price variance (Actual price − Standard price) × Actual quantity is favourable when positive. FP&A simply applies the same logic to every P&L line.

Conditions and exceptions:

  • Margin lines (gross margin ₹, EBITDA) are treated like revenue: Actual − Budget.
  • Margin % lines use the same direction but are expressed in bps: (Actual % − Budget %) × 10,000.
  • Contra-revenue lines (sales returns, discounts shown separately) are cost-like: Budget − Actual.
  • Lines that can change nature (net forex gain/loss, other income/expense) take the formula of the P&L position they are presented in; if a "loss" line becomes a gain, the variance is still computed on the signed amount within that line.
  • Balance sheet lines need a declared lens (s03); the revenue/cost dichotomy does not apply directly.

Illustration

Malabar Spices Ltd, Q1 (₹ lakh):

Line Budget Actual Formula Variance Fav/(Adv)
Revenue from operations 2,400 2,520 A − B 120
Cost of materials consumed 1,440 1,560 B − A (120)
Gross margin 960 960 A − B 0
Employee benefits 210 198 B − A 12
Other expenses 330 351 B − A (21)
EBITDA 420 411 A − B (9)

Check: 120 − 120 + 12 − 21 = −9 = EBITDA variance. Gross margin % fell from 40.0% to 38.1%, i.e. (190) bps — the margin context that a flat ₹ gross margin hides.

Connections

This rule is the foundation of every other concept in the topic: the generic Excel implementation (c02), variance % (c03), the TB flip that produces unsigned amounts (c04) and IBCS colours (c05). It is the sign basis of every bridge step in topic 4 and of the price, volume, mix and cost formulas in topics 1 and 2, and of the YTD opex variance in topic 3 (concept c01: positive = underspend).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and sum [L1]

Budget vs actual (₹ lakh): Revenue 5,600 vs 5,380; Other income 40 vs 52; COGS 3,360 vs 3,170; Employee cost 520 vs 545; Other expenses 760 vs 742. Compute each Fav/(Adv) variance and the EBITDA variance (EBITDA includes other income), and verify the additive property.

Q2. CMA to FP&A translation [L2]

Express the CMA material price variance, labour rate variance and sales price variance in the "positive = favourable" form, and explain in three sentences why the FP&A revenue and cost formulas are consistent with them.

💡 Hint: Compare which of standard and actual appears first in each CMA formula with which of budget and actual appears first in the FP&A formula.

Q3. Contra-revenue [L2]

Trade discounts are booked in a separate GL account and presented within revenue from operations. Budget discounts ₹180 lakh, actual ₹215 lakh; gross sales budget ₹3,000 lakh, actual ₹3,120 lakh. Compute the variances for gross sales, discounts and net revenue, and explain the formula used for discounts.
