Key concept

Generic Excel Variance Formula: Type-Driven or Negative-Cost Storage

Concept: Generic: =IF(Type="Revenue",Act-Bud,Bud-Act) or store costs as negatives so =Act-Bud always gives favourable positive

Statement

Two equivalent designs produce a variance in which positive is always favourable.

Design 1 — type-driven formula on unsigned amounts:

\[ V = d \times (A - B), \qquad d = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{revenue, income, margin, better-if-higher KPI} \\ -1 & \text{cost, contra-revenue, worse-if-higher KPI} \end{cases} \]

implemented as =IF(Type="Revenue",Act-Bud,Bud-Act) or, more generally, =Dir*(Act-Bud).

Design 2 — signed (profit-effect) storage: store revenue as positive and costs as negative, \(A' = d \times A\) and \(B' = d \times B\). Then for every line:

\[ V = A' - B' \]

implemented as =Act-Bud throughout. Here \(A, B\) are unsigned actual and budget amounts, \(A', B'\) are signed amounts and \(d\) is the direction factor.

Explanation

Both designs encode the same fact — whether a line adds to or subtracts from profit — in a different place. Design 1 keeps amounts familiar (all positive, Schedule III look) and puts the logic in the formula via a Type or Dir attribute. Design 2 puts the logic in the data, so formulas are trivially uniform and subtotals are plain sums, at the cost of tables that show costs as negatives unless a display layer converts them.

Practical guidance:

  • The bare IF(Type="Revenue",…) form handles only two types; anything not exactly "Revenue" (e.g., "Other income", "Revenue " with a trailing space, "Margin") falls into the cost branch. Prefer a lookup of a numeric Dir: =XLOOKUP($A8,Map[Line],Map[Dir],"Missing")*(D8-C8), which errors visibly on unmapped lines.
  • Design 2 suits models and Power BI datasets; Design 1 suits presentation tables. Many teams use Design 2 underneath and display ABS values with Design 1-style variances.
  • Never mix the two in one workbook without labelling: applying =Bud-Act to a negatively stored cost double-flips and turns a saving into an adverse variance.
  • KPIs extend Design 1 naturally through Dir; Design 2 is awkward for KPIs (negative DSO is odd), so KPI scorecards usually use Design 1.

Illustration

Same data, both designs (₹ lakh):

Line Dir Budget Actual Design 1 =Dir*(Act-Bud) Signed budget Signed actual Design 2 =Act-Bud
Revenue +1 2,400 2,520 120 2,400 2,520 120
COGS −1 1,440 1,560 (120) (1,440) (1,560) (120)
Employee cost −1 210 198 12 (210) (198) 12
Other expenses −1 330 351 (21) (330) (351) (21)
EBITDA +1 420 411 (9) 420 411 (9)

In Design 2 the EBITDA row is simply =SUM of the signed lines (2,400 − 1,440 − 210 − 330 = 420), and the variance column also sums. The double-flip error: applying Design 1's =Bud-Act to the signed COGS gives −1,440 − (−1,560) = +120, showing a ₹120 lakh overspend as favourable.

Practice Questions

Q1. Robust lookup formula [L1]

Rewrite =IF(B8="Revenue",D8-C8,C8-D8) as a lookup-driven formula that (a) handles revenue, other income, contra-revenue, cost, subtotal and KPI rows, and (b) visibly flags any line missing from the mapping table. State the mapping-table columns required.

Q2. Spot the double flip [L2]

A workbook stores costs as negatives. An analyst copies a variance formula =C8-D8 (Budget − Actual) from an older unsigned template onto all cost rows. Budget employee cost −₹410 lakh, actual −₹435 lakh; budget revenue ₹4,200 lakh, actual ₹4,150 lakh (revenue row uses =D8-C8). Compute what the sheet shows for each line and for EBITDA, compare with the correct values, and describe a check that would have caught it.

💡 Hint: Recompute EBITDA variance directly from the EBITDA row and compare it with the sum of line variances.

Q3. Choosing a design [L3]

You are building (a) a board-pack P&L table, (b) a 3-statement model's P&L, and (c) a Power BI dataset feeding both a P&L matrix and an EBITDA bridge. Recommend Design 1 or Design 2 for each, with reasons, and explain how you would keep their variances identical.
