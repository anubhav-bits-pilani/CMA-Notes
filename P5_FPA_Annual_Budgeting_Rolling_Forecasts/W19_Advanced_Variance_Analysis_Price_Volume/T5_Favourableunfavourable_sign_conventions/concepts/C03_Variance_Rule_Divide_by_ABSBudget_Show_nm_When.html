Key concept

Variance % Rule: Divide by ABS(Budget), Show n.m. When Invalid

Concept: Variance % = Variance ÷ ABS(Budget); show "n.m." when budget = 0 or sign flips

Statement

\[ \text{Var \%} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{V}{\left\lvert B \right\rvert} & \text{if } B \neq 0 \text{ and } \operatorname{sign}(A)\times\operatorname{sign}(B) \geq 0 \\[2ex] \text{n.m.} & \text{if } B = 0 \text{ or } \operatorname{sign}(A)\times\operatorname{sign}(B) < 0 \end{cases} \]

where \(V\) is the Fav/(Adv) variance (positive = favourable), \(B\) the budget (base) amount and \(A\) the actual amount. Optionally, "n.m." also applies when \(\left\lvert B \right\rvert\) is below a materiality floor. Excel:

=IF(OR(Bud=0,SIGN(Bud)*SIGN(Act)<0),"n.m.",Var/ABS(Bud))

Explanation

Why ABS. The sign of a percentage should tell the reader the same thing as the sign of the ₹ variance. With a negative budget (a loss, or a cost stored as a negative), dividing by \(B\) itself reverses the sign. Dividing by \(\left\lvert B \right\rvert\) keeps the percentage's sign equal to \(V\)'s sign in every case, and makes the signed and unsigned storage designs (c02) produce identical percentages.

Why n.m. for zero. Division by zero is undefined; showing #DIV/0! or a huge number is noise. The ₹ variance carries the message ("unbudgeted ₹12 lakh consultancy").

Why n.m. for sign flips. When budget and actual have opposite signs (profit budgeted, loss incurred), the percentage measures distance relative to a base that has crossed zero; values like (250%) or +500% are arithmetically defined but not interpretable as "how much better or worse". Standard practice in FP&A packs and in many investor presentations is to show "n.m." and describe the swing in words.

What remains meaningful. Actual = 0 with a non-zero budget gives exactly ±100% (all revenue lost, or all budgeted cost avoided) — shown, not suppressed. Percentage lines (margins, rates) are compared in bps/percentage points rather than as a % of the budget %.

Two naive results are misleading in sign (start-up EBITDA and signed cost), one is meaningless (flip) and one errors (zero budget).

💡 Hint: For (c), think about whether the line itself changes presentation position and what that means for the sign test.
