Roadmap topic: Favourable vs Unfavourable Sign Conventions: Standardizing variance formulas so revenue/margin upside and cost savings always display consistently

Overview

Variance analysis is only useful if every reader interprets a number the same way. In most Indian FP&A packs the convention is that a positive variance is favourable (good for profit) and a negative or bracketed variance is adverse, whether the line is revenue or cost. That simple rule collides with three realities: ERP trial balances store revenue as credits (negative) and costs as debits (positive); different teams build their own sheets with different habits (cost-centre owners often think "positive = overspend"); and some lines — balance sheet items, KPIs, loss-making segments, zero budgets — do not fit the revenue/cost dichotomy at all. When conventions are mixed, the same overrun shows red on one page and green on another, bridges stop adding up, and executives stop trusting the pack.

For a CMA, the favourable/adverse language is familiar from standard costing: a material price variance is favourable when the actual price is below standard. FP&A takes that idea and turns it into a system — account-type attributes on the chart of accounts, a single variance formula driven by those attributes, a documented approach to variance % on awkward bases, and presentation rules (brackets, colours, "better/(worse)" labels) applied identically in Excel, PowerPoint and Power BI.

The learning sequence runs from data to presentation: (1) understand natural (ERP) sign versus presentation sign and how to flip credit-nature accounts; (2) choose and document the favourable/adverse display convention; (3) implement account-type-driven formulas for revenue, cost, asset and liability lines; (4) handle variance % where the budget is negative, zero or flips sign; (5) enforce consistency across the P&L, KPI scorecard, bridges and Power BI. The concepts state the core formulas, the generic Excel implementation, the variance % rule, the TB flip and the IBCS colour rule.

Related concepts to study

Week 11 DAX sign handling and calculation groups

→ Week 11: DAX sign-handling with account-type attributes and calculation groups

In Week 11 you modelled a chart-of-accounts dimension with attributes such as AccountType and SignFactor, and wrote measures like SUMX(Accounts, [Amount] * Accounts[SignFactor]). Calculation groups (created in Tabular Editor or, in newer Power BI Desktop builds, natively) let you define a "Variance" calculation item once — ( [Actual] - [Budget] ) * SELECTEDVALUE(Accounts[BetterIfHigher]) style logic — and apply it to every base measure, so revenue, cost and KPI variances all follow the same convention. This topic supplies the business rules those DAX patterns implement; Week 11 supplies the mechanics. Revisit how calculation groups interact with format strings, because dynamic format strings can apply bracket formatting for adverse variances consistently.

Chart of accounts design and account-type attributes

Chart of accounts design and account-type attributes (→ Phase 1)

Phase 1 covered GL structure, Schedule III mapping and trial balance finalisation. A well-designed chart of accounts carries attributes beyond the Schedule III line: natural balance (Dr/Cr), statement (P&L/BS), account class (revenue, contra-revenue, COGS, opex, other income, asset, liability, equity) and, for FP&A, a "better if higher" flag. In Tally Prime, groups (Sales Accounts, Direct Expenses, Indirect Incomes) carry nature implicitly; in SAP, G/L account type and the financial statement version hierarchy do. Building the sign logic on these attributes, rather than on account-number ranges or hard-coded lists, means new accounts inherit the right sign automatically.

Conditional formatting and custom number formats

Conditional formatting and custom number formats e.g. #,##0;(#,##0);"-"

Excel custom number formats have up to four sections: positive; negative; zero; text. #,##0;(#,##0);"-" shows positives plainly, negatives in brackets and zero as a dash — the standard finance look. Colour codes can be added, e.g. [Color10]#,##0;[Red](#,##0);"-", but colour in a number format is tied to arithmetic sign, so it is only safe on variance columns already expressed as positive = favourable. Conditional formatting rules (icon sets, data bars, formula-based fills) offer more control, such as colouring a cell red only when the variance is adverse and beyond a materiality threshold. Power BI offers equivalent conditional formatting by rules or field value.

Balance sheet variance interpretation

Balance sheet variance interpretation (higher receivables = adverse for cash)

Balance sheet lines do not have a universal "good" direction. From a cash and working-capital perspective, higher receivables, higher inventory and lower payables than budget are adverse because they absorb cash; higher cash and lower borrowings are favourable. From a credit-risk view, higher provisions may be prudent. FP&A packs therefore attach a "better if higher" flag to each balance-sheet line based on the pack's purpose (usually cash), and analyse working-capital days (DSO, DIO, DPO) alongside ₹ amounts. The same receivable increase may be favourable if it reflects revenue growth at constant DSO — which is why ratio context is needed.

Common pitfalls

Mixed conventions across sheets

Mixing conventions across sheets so the same overrun shows red in one and green in another

The mistake: the P&L tab uses positive = favourable, while the cost-centre tab (often supplied by a functional finance team) uses positive = overspend, and the dashboard colours both with the same red/green rule. People make it because sheets are built by different teams at different times. The correct treatment is one documented convention, applied through a single sign-factor attribute in the mapping table, with each sheet's header stating "Fav/(Adv)". Detect it by tracing one known overrun (e.g., a ₹50 lakh A&P overspend) through every page of the pack and checking it carries the same sign and colour everywhere.

Variance % on negative budgets

Variance % on negative budget values giving misleading signs

If a segment's budget EBITDA is −₹10 crore and actual is −₹5 crore, the variance is +₹5 crore (favourable), but Variance ÷ Budget gives −50%, suggesting an adverse result. It arises because people copy the standard formula without thinking about the denominator. Use Variance ÷ ABS(Budget) and show "n.m." when the budget is zero or actual and budget have opposite signs. Detect it with a review filter on rows where budget ≤ 0 and check every percentage's sign against the ₹ variance sign.

Not flipping credit-nature accounts

Forgetting to flip credit-nature accounts from ERP extracts

ERP extracts (SAP, Oracle, Tally exports) show revenue, other income and liabilities as negative (credit) balances. Loading them directly into a report makes revenue negative; analysts then sometimes flip only the revenue rows, leaving other income or contra-revenue wrongly signed. The correct treatment is a mapping table with natural balance and a presentation sign factor, applied to every account in Power Query or SQL before any calculation. Detect it by checking that total revenue in the report equals the Schedule III revenue from operations and that the trial balance nets to zero before the flip.

Revenue up, margin down treated as favourable

Treating a revenue increase with lower margin as favourable without margin context

A ₹40 crore revenue variance shown in green may hide a margin decline if the growth came from low-margin channels (quick commerce, institutional sales) or price-led discounting. The sign convention is correct at the line level, but the interpretation is incomplete. The correct practice is to pair revenue variances with gross margin ₹ and margin % (bps) variances and to show the PVM decomposition, so a "green" revenue line next to a "red" margin bps line tells the real story. Detect it by reviewing whether every revenue commentary also states the margin effect.

Practice Questions

Q1. Standardise a messy pack [L3]

Sindhu Retail Ltd's monthly pack has three tabs. P&L tab: variances computed as Actual − Budget for all lines. Cost-centre tab: variances computed as Actual − Budget, labelled "Overspend/(Saving)". Dashboard: green when value > 0. October data (₹ lakh): Revenue budget 4,200, actual 4,350; COGS budget 2,940, actual 3,120; Employee cost budget 410, actual 395; Rent budget 180, actual 180; Other income budget 25, actual 12. (a) Compute variances under the current P&L tab method and show which cells the dashboard would colour wrongly. (b) Restate all variances under positive = favourable. (c) Compute gross margin ₹ and margin % variance in bps and write the one-line commentary. (d) Specify the mapping-table columns that would prevent recurrence.

Q2. ERP extract to presentation [L2]

A trial balance extract (natural sign: debit positive, credit negative) shows (₹ crore): Sales −512.4; Sales returns 8.6; Other operating revenue −14.2; Other income −6.1; Cost of materials consumed 301.7; Employee benefits 48.3; Other expenses 77.9; Trade receivables 96.5; Trade payables −64.0. (a) Classify each by account type and natural balance. (b) Produce presentation amounts. (c) Compute revenue from operations as it would appear in Schedule III. (d) Explain how sales returns should be signed and why.

💡 Hint: Sales returns are a debit-nature contra-revenue account; decide whether you flip by account or by group.

Q3. Variance % on awkward bases [L2]

Compute the ₹ variance (positive = favourable) and the variance % you would display for: (a) Segment EBITDA budget −₹8 crore, actual −₹2 crore; (b) Segment EBITDA budget ₹3 crore, actual −₹1 crore; (c) Consultancy cost budget ₹0, actual ₹12 lakh; (d) Interest income budget ₹50 lakh, actual ₹65 lakh; (e) Warranty provision release (credit to P&L) budget −₹20 lakh, actual −₹35 lakh in the expense line. Justify each display choice.

Q4. One convention in Power BI [L3]

Design the Power BI model elements (dimension attributes, base measures, calculation group items, format strings and conditional-formatting rules) that guarantee: positive = favourable for all P&L and KPI variances; brackets for adverse; green/red by good/bad per IBCS; "n.m." for invalid percentages; and balance-sheet variances judged from a cash perspective. Explain how you would test that the bridge page and the scorecard page show identical variances for the same line.
