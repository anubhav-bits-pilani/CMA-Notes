Subtopic note

Account-Type Driven Variance Formulas for P&L and Balance Sheet Lines

Scope: Account-type driven formulas for revenue, cost, asset and liability lines

Why it matters

A monthly pack might contain 300 P&L lines, 80 balance sheet lines and 40 KPIs. Hand-writing "Actual − Budget" for some rows and "Budget − Actual" for others is how sign errors creep in — someone inserts a row, copies the formula from the line above, and a cost line acquires a revenue formula. The robust approach is to write one variance formula for every row, driven by an attribute of the line (its account type or a "better if higher" flag). Then the formula cannot be wrong for a single row without the attribute being wrong, and attributes are reviewed once in a mapping table rather than in hundreds of cells.

Core notes

The four account types and their favourable direction

Account type Examples Favourable when (P&L/profit view) Variance formula
Revenue / income Revenue from operations, other operating revenue, other income Actual > Budget Actual − Budget
Cost / expense COGS, employee cost, other expenses, depreciation, finance cost Actual < Budget Budget − Actual
Asset Cash, receivables, inventory, PPE Depends on the purpose (see below) Direction flag
Liability Payables, borrowings, provisions Depends on the purpose (see below) Direction flag

For P&L lines the direction is unambiguous: more income is better, less cost is better. Contra-revenue lines (sales returns, discounts) behave like costs within revenue — higher is worse — even though they are reported inside revenue from operations. Subtotals (gross margin, EBITDA, PBT) behave like revenue (higher is better).

Balance sheet lines: define the lens

For the balance sheet there is no universal favourable direction; the pack must declare its lens:

  • Cash / working-capital lens (most common in FP&A): higher cash is better; higher receivables, inventory and other current assets are worse (they absorb cash); higher payables are better (they fund operations); higher borrowings are worse.
  • Leverage / credit lens: higher net debt and lower equity are worse; provisions are neutral or judged against adequacy.
  • Capex lens: capex below budget is "favourable" for cash but may signal project delays — usually shown as Increase/(Decrease) without colour.

Under the cash lens:

Line Budget (₹ cr) Actual (₹ cr) Better if higher? Variance Better/(Worse)
Cash and bank 85 102 Yes 17
Trade receivables 240 268 No (28)
Inventories 310 295 No 15
Trade payables 190 176 Yes (14)
Borrowings 400 420 No (20)

A receivable increase can still be "good business" if it follows revenue growth at the same DSO; that is why balance-sheet variances are paired with days metrics (DSO, DIO, DPO) and with a cash-flow bridge.

One formula, driven by an attribute

Store a direction factor \(d\) for each line: \(d = +1\) if higher is better, \(d = -1\) if lower is better. Then:

\[ \text{Variance}_{\text{Fav/(Adv)}} = d \times (\text{Actual} - \text{Budget}) \]

For revenue \(d = +1\) gives Actual − Budget; for cost \(d = -1\) gives Budget − Actual. Excel implementation with an attribute column Dir looked up from a mapping table:

=XLOOKUP($A8,Map[Line],Map[Dir])*(D8-C8)

or the explicit type-driven form for P&L-only reports:

=IF($B8="Revenue",D8-C8,C8-D8)

where column B holds the line type, C budget, D actual. The XLOOKUP form scales better because it handles assets, liabilities and KPIs with the same formula.

The alternative design is to store costs as negative numbers in the data (profit-effect sign, s01). Then every line — revenue, cost, subtotal — uses =D8-C8, and subtotals are simple SUMs. The trade-off is that tables then show costs as negatives, which some audiences dislike; a presentation layer can display ABS amounts with "Less:" labels while keeping the signed data underneath.

Mapping-table design

Line Line type Natural balance Sign factor Better if higher (Dir) Lens
Revenue from operations Revenue Cr −1 +1 P&L
Sales returns Contra-revenue Dr −1 −1 P&L
Cost of materials consumed Cost Dr +1 −1 P&L
EBITDA Subtotal — — +1 P&L
Trade receivables Asset Dr +1 −1 Cash
Trade payables Liability Cr −1 +1 Cash
DSO (days) KPI — — −1 KPI

The Sign factor converts natural sign to presentation (s01, concept c04); Dir converts the arithmetic difference to Fav/(Adv). Keeping them as two separate attributes avoids the common confusion of using the TB sign to decide favourability.

Note the Sales returns row: presentation sign factor −1 (so the ₹ amount reduces revenue) but Dir −1 at its own line level (higher returns are worse). When it rolls into the Revenue subtotal, its effect is already captured with the correct sign.

Illustration

October (₹ lakh). Revenue budget 4,200, actual 4,350 (Dir +1) → +150. Sales returns budget 60, actual 85 (Dir −1) → −1 × (85 − 60) = (25). COGS budget 2,940, actual 3,120 (Dir −1) → (180). Other income budget 25, actual 12 (Dir +1) → (13). DSO budget 52, actual 58 (Dir −1) → (6) days. Every row uses the same formula; only Dir changes.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Lines that change type. Net forex gain/loss, profit/loss on sale of assets and "changes in inventories of FG/WIP" can be income-like or cost-like in different months. Classify by the line's position in the P&L (where it is presented) and keep Dir fixed; a negative "changes in inventories" expense (inventory build) is simply a cost below budget.
  • Variable costs and flexed budgets. For variable cost lines, the variance against the original budget mixes volume and spending effects. Apply the same Dir to the flexed budget variance (topic 3, concept c04).
  • KPIs with a target band. For capacity utilisation or inventory cover, both too high and too low may be bad. Use a target-band rule (distance from target) rather than a simple Dir.
  • Mixed-lens packs. If one page shows the BS under the cash lens and another under a credit lens, label each page; never colour BS variances without a declared lens.
  • Subtotal integrity. With the Dir approach, the variance of a subtotal (EBITDA) must equal the sum of the Fav/(Adv) variances of its components. Build a check; if it fails, a component's Dir is wrong.

Key takeaways

  • Revenue: Actual − Budget; cost: Budget − Actual; positive = favourable in both.
  • Balance sheet lines need a declared lens; under the cash lens, higher receivables and inventory are adverse, higher payables favourable.
  • Use one formula for every row: Variance = Dir × (Actual − Budget), with Dir in a mapping table.
  • Keep the presentation Sign factor and the favourability Dir as separate attributes.
  • Check that subtotal variances equal the sum of component variances.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify and compute [L1]

Assign Dir (+1/−1) and compute Fav/(Adv) variances (₹ lakh): Export revenue budget 620, actual 590; Trade discounts budget 80, actual 72; Power and fuel budget 210, actual 236; Interest income budget 30, actual 41; Depreciation budget 150, actual 150.

Q2. Balance sheet under two lenses [L2]

Budget vs actual at 31 March (₹ crore): Cash 60 vs 45; Receivables 180 vs 165; Inventory 220 vs 260; Payables 150 vs 170; Borrowings 300 vs 280. Compute Better/(Worse) variances under the cash lens. Then explain which variances would be interpreted differently by a credit analyst focused on liquidity and leverage, and why.

💡 Hint: Consider what the payables increase and inventory build imply for the current ratio versus for cash.

Q3. Build the formula [L2]

Design an Excel P&L variance sheet where columns are Line, Type, Budget, Actual, Variance Fav/(Adv), Variance %. Write a single formula for Variance that works for revenue, contra-revenue, cost, subtotal and KPI rows using a mapping table, and a check formula that confirms EBITDA variance equals the sum of its component variances.

Q4. Subtotal check failure [L3]

EBITDA variance computed directly from budget and actual EBITDA is (40) (₹ lakh), but the sum of the component rows in your variance sheet is (15). The component rows are: Revenue from operations (net of returns) +150; Sales returns (25); COGS (180); Employee cost 15; Other expenses +25 (budget 565, actual 590). There are two errors. Identify each (one is an attribute error, one is a hierarchy/mapping error), explain how they partially offset, and describe how you would confirm and prevent them.
