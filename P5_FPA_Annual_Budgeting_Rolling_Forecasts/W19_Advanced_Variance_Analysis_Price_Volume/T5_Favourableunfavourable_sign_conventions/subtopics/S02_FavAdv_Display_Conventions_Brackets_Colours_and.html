Subtopic note

Fav/(Adv) Display Conventions: Brackets, Colours and Better/(Worse)

Scope: Fav/(Adv) conventions: brackets, colours, "better/(worse)" labelling

Why it matters

Once variances are computed with a consistent sign (positive = favourable), they still have to be displayed so that a reader glancing at a page for ten seconds reads the right message. Indian board packs, MIS decks and investor presentations use a mix of brackets, "F/A" suffixes, colours and arrows. Each has strengths and failure modes: brackets are unambiguous in print but invisible in a chart; colours are fast but fail for colour-blind readers and greyscale printing; "better/(worse)" labels remove the need to know whether a line is revenue or cost. A pack that mixes them — brackets meaning adverse on one page and meaning "negative number" on the next — forces readers to decode every table.

Core notes

The underlying rule

All display conventions sit on top of one computational rule: the variance column expresses the effect on profit (or on the KPI's goal). Positive = favourable (better), negative = adverse (worse). The display layer only formats that number; it must never re-interpret it. If the computation is wrong, no formatting will fix it.

Brackets

The finance standard is to show negatives in brackets: (1,250) rather than −1,250. Applied to a variance column built as positive = favourable, brackets mean adverse. Excel custom number format:

#,##0;(#,##0);"-"

Section 1 formats positives, section 2 negatives (brackets), section 3 zero (dash). One decimal in ₹ crore: #,##0.0;(#,##0.0);"-". For alignment of positive numbers with bracketed negatives, add a trailing space placeholder: #,##0_);(#,##0);"-"_).

Brackets work well in tables and print, and survive photocopying and greyscale. Their weakness is that the same format is also used on the Actual and Budget columns, where brackets mean "negative amount" (e.g., a loss), not "adverse". The column header must make the meaning explicit.

Column header labelling: Fav/(Adv) and Better/(Worse)

The header of every variance column should state the convention:

Line Budget Actual Var Fav/(Adv) Var %
Revenue from operations 4,200 4,350 150 3.6%
Cost of materials consumed 2,940 3,120 (180) (6.1%)
Employee benefits 410 395 15 3.7%
EBITDA (simplified: three lines only) 850 835 (15) (1.8%)

"Better/(Worse)" is the same convention in plainer language, preferred by many CFOs because it is meaningful for KPIs that are neither revenue nor cost (DSO, attrition %, OTIF %, customer complaints). Some companies use letter suffixes — "150 F", "180 A" — common in CMA-style operating statements and in manufacturing MIS; they are unambiguous but cannot be summed in Excel unless kept as a separate text column.

Colours

Colour adds speed. The IBCS rule (concept c05) is that green means good for the business and red means bad, irrespective of whether the number is arithmetically positive or negative. Because variance columns are already expressed as positive = favourable, green-for-positive in the variance column is correct; the same rule applied to the Actual column would be wrong (a positive cost is not "good").

Implementation options in Excel:

  • Number-format colours, tied to sign: [Color10]#,##0;[Red](#,##0);"-". Safe only on variance columns built as positive = favourable.
  • Conditional formatting with a formula rule, e.g. red fill when =AND(F5<0,ABS(F5)>Threshold), which adds materiality (only highlight adverse variances above ₹X lakh).
  • Icon sets (arrows): ensure the arrow direction means better/worse, not up/down in value — a cost that went up should show a down-pointing (worse) indicator or a red up arrow; agree which and document it.

Accessibility: around 8% of men have some form of red-green colour vision deficiency. Pair colour with a second cue (brackets, sign, arrow shape or label) and consider a blue/orange palette for dashboards. Printed packs must remain readable in greyscale.

Arrows and variance markers in charts

In charts, brackets disappear. Variance bars in IBCS style are drawn in green (good) or red (bad) extending from a zero line, with labels using "+" and "−" (or brackets) consistent with the table. In a bridge, the bar direction already shows the EBITDA effect (up = favourable), so colour reinforces direction rather than adding new information.

A pack convention sheet

Write the convention on one page at the front (or in the methodology appendix) of the pack:

  • "All variances are shown Fav/(Adv): positive = favourable to profit; brackets = adverse."
  • "Colour: green = favourable, red = adverse beyond materiality (₹25 lakh and 5%)."
  • "KPIs: Better/(Worse) per the KPI's target direction (see scorecard definitions)."
  • "Variance % = Variance ÷ ABS(Budget); n.m. = not meaningful."
  • "Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated; bps = basis points of revenue."

Illustration

A cost-centre owner's sheet reports A&P "Variance +45" meaning ₹45 lakh overspent. The pack convention requires Fav/(Adv). Conversion: Fav/(Adv) = −1 × (Actual − Budget) = Budget − Actual = −45 → displayed as (45) in red. If the cost-centre sheet is pasted in unconverted, the overspend appears as a green +45 — the classic "same overrun, opposite colour" pitfall.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Actual and budget columns with negative values. A loss-making segment's EBITDA of (12) in the Actual column means a loss, not adverse. Keep variance columns visually distinct (separate block, header colour) from amount columns.
  • Zero variances. Use "-" not "0" to reduce clutter, but ensure genuine zeros and blanks (no data) are distinguishable; blanks can be "n.a.".
  • Small variances coloured red. Without a materiality rule, every ₹10,000 adverse variance turns red and the page becomes a sea of colour. Apply thresholds (topic 3, concept c05).
  • External presentations. Investor decks often show YoY growth with "+/−" and no colour; follow the company's disclosure style and avoid colour that implies judgement in regulatory filings.
  • Positive = favourable vs positive = increase. Some treasury and balance-sheet reports use positive = increase. If such a page is included in the pack, label it explicitly ("Increase/(Decrease)") and do not colour it green/red.

Key takeaways

  • Compute variance as effect on profit (positive = favourable); format afterwards — formatting never re-interprets.
  • Brackets for adverse, with "Fav/(Adv)" or "Better/(Worse)" in every variance column header.
  • Green/red mean good/bad, applied only to variance columns or to cells evaluated against target direction.
  • Pair colour with a non-colour cue for accessibility and greyscale printing.
  • Document the convention on one page and apply it to every table, chart and dashboard.

Practice Questions

Q1. Number format design [L1]

Write Excel custom number formats for: (a) a ₹ crore variance column with one decimal, adverse in brackets, zero as a dash and aligned decimals; (b) the same with green for favourable and red for adverse; (c) a variance % column with one decimal and brackets for adverse. State which columns of a P&L table each format may be applied to.

Q2. Convert and colour [L2]

A functional finance team sends (₹ lakh, "Overspend/(Saving)"): Travel 12; Rent (4); IT 31; Training (9); Recruitment 0. Budgets: Travel 60, Rent 120, IT 140, Training 35, Recruitment 20. Convert to the pack convention, compute variance %, and state which cells are coloured red if the materiality rule is |variance| > ₹10 lakh and > 10% of budget.

💡 Hint: Flip the sign first, then apply both legs of the materiality test.

Q3. KPI scorecard labelling [L2]

For these KPIs, define the target direction and show how a Better/(Worse) variance would be displayed: DSO budget 52 days, actual 58 days; OTIF budget 95%, actual 96.5%; Attrition budget 18%, actual 21%; Gross margin budget 38.0%, actual 37.2%. Explain why "Fav/(Adv)" is less natural than "Better/(Worse)" for these.

Q4. Accessibility review [L3]

Your Power BI dashboard uses only red and green fills to show variance status. The CFO prints it in greyscale for a board offsite, and one director is colour-blind. Propose a redesign covering palette, secondary cues, labels and the conventions note, and explain how you would keep it consistent with the Excel pack.
