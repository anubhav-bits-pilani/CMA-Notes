Subtopic note

Commodity Inflation and Pass-Through to Pricing

Scope: Commodity inflation (palm oil, crude derivatives, packaging) and pass-through to pricing

Why it matters

Indian FMCG and consumer-durables margins are driven mostly by commodity cycles. Palm oil (soaps, biscuits, noodles, edible-oil blends), crude derivatives (LAB for detergents, HDPE/LLDPE/PP and PET for packaging, solvents, synthetic rubber) and paper-based packaging (kraft, duplex board, laminates) together can be more than half of COGS. Listed companies routinely explain quarterly gross-margin moves in their earnings calls in terms of "commodity inflation of X% partly offset by calibrated price increases", and analysts model gross margin explicitly from commodity indices.

For FP&A the questions are: how much of the margin decline came from input inflation, how much was recovered through pricing, with what lag, and what the residual exposure is for the rest of the year. Answering them needs a clean split of the material price variance into a market (index-driven) part and a buying-performance part, plus a pass-through analysis that links input inflation to the price element of the revenue PVM.

Core notes

Measuring commodity inflation

For each key commodity, keep in the model:

  • Budget rate: the standard price used in the AOP (e.g., palm oil ₹90,000/t).
  • Index / market rate: an external reference such as Bursa Malaysia crude palm oil futures converted at the INR/MYR or INR/USD rate plus landed cost, the domestic MCX or exchange reference where available, or polymer prices published by domestic producers.
  • Actual landed rate: invoice plus freight, duties and non-creditable taxes. GST is excluded because the input tax credit is available.

Then split the MPV:

\[ \text{MPV} = \underbrace{(SP - IP) \times AQ}_{\text{market inflation}} + \underbrace{(IP - AP) \times AQ}_{\text{buying performance}} \]

where \(IP\) is the index price. Procurement is accountable for the second term (timing of buys, supplier negotiation, hedge effectiveness). The first term is a business-level exposure.

Import duties and agricultural cess on edible oils have changed several times in recent years. Treat any duty assumption as date-sensitive and keep it as a separate input row so its effect can be isolated.

Pass-through: ₹-neutral vs %-neutral

Suppose a pack sells at a net realisation of ₹100 with material and other COGS of ₹60 (GM 40%), and input costs rise 10% to ₹66.

Pricing response New price GM ₹ per unit GM %
No pass-through 100 34 34.0%
₹-neutral (price +₹6, +6%) 106 40 37.7%
%-neutral (price +10%) 110 44 40.0%

A ₹-neutral pass-through protects absolute gross profit per unit but dilutes GM%, by about 230 bps here. Keeping the margin percentage needs a price increase equal to the cost-inflation percentage, which is larger than the ₹ cost increase whenever GM% is positive. Executives who say "we fully passed through inflation" need to be asked which of the two they mean.

Define the pass-through ratio for a period:

\[ \text{Pass-through ratio} = \frac{\text{Price variance (revenue PVM, ₹)}}{\text{Commodity inflation in COGS (₹, at actual volume)}} \]

A ratio of 100% means ₹-neutral. A ratio of \(1/(1 - \text{GM\%})\), about 167% at 40% GM, is needed for %-neutral.

Price-point packs and grammage reduction

A large share of Indian FMCG volume is sold at fixed consumer price points (₹1, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20 packs) through general trade. Those prices cannot be moved without hurting volumes. Companies pass inflation through instead by grammage reduction (indirect price increase). A ₹10 biscuit pack moving from 60 g to 55 g is a 9.1% increase in price per gram.

PVM implications:

  • If the SKU code is unchanged, the revenue PVM shows no price variance per pack. The pass-through then shows up as a favourable material usage/cost per unit in COGS, because each pack uses less material.
  • Better practice is to analyse price and cost per kg (or per litre) for price-point portfolios. Then grammage changes show up correctly as price variance and the cost side remains like-for-like.
  • When grammage changes create a new SKU code, s04 of Topic 1 (new/discontinued SKUs) applies. Map old and new SKUs as a like-for-like pair rather than as a launch and a discontinuation.

Lag analysis

Pass-through is rarely immediate. Price increases are set quarterly, need trade-scheme alignment and distributor inventory to flush through, and modern trade or quick-commerce price changes may need retailer negotiation. Inventory and forward contracts also delay the cost impact. A lag analysis plots, month by month:

  1. Commodity index (and landed cost) vs budget.
  2. Inflation in COGS ₹ at actual volume (cost lags the index by inventory days plus contract cover).
  3. Price variance ₹ from the revenue PVM.
  4. Cumulative net margin impact = 3 − 2.

The cumulative gap shows the margin "lost to lag". The model can then project when the gap closes under the pricing plan. In a falling commodity market the lag works in the company's favour (cost falls before price is cut), and this temporary margin expansion should be labelled as such in the LE.

Hedging

Companies reduce volatility through forward purchase contracts with suppliers (fixed price for a quantity and period), exchange-traded futures (Bursa Malaysia CPO futures for palm; domestic commodity exchanges where contracts are liquid) and currency forwards for the import payable.

Under Ind AS 109, a forward purchase contract for physical delivery used for the entity's own requirements falls under the own-use exemption and is not a derivative at fair value. Financial futures are derivatives. Where hedge accounting is designated (a cash-flow hedge of a highly probable forecast purchase), the effective portion of fair-value changes is held in OCI (cash-flow hedge reserve) and recycled into the cost of inventory (basis adjustment) when the purchase occurs. Without hedge accounting, mark-to-market gains and losses hit P&L in a different period from the hedged cost. In management reporting, show the hedge result beside the MPV so the net effective commodity cost is visible.

Presenting commodity inflation in the margin bridge

The GM bridge should show either:

  • Price (pricing actions) and Commodity inflation (COGS) as separate bars, with commodity inflation taken out of the material price variance; or
  • Material price variance as a single bar, with commentary on the inflation content.

Never show both "MPV" and a separate "commodity inflation" bar computed independently from the same quantities. That double counts (see Topic pitfalls).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mixed-currency commodities: palm oil is priced in MYR/USD. Separate the commodity-price effect (in USD at budget FX) from the FX effect (USD quantity × change in rate), especially when the rupee depreciates in the same quarter.
  • Index basis mismatch: futures prices do not match landed cost (basis risk: grade, freight, duty, refining margin). Calibrate the index-to-landed relationship before attributing variances to buying performance.
  • Pass-through by channel: price increases may be taken in general trade first and in modern trade or e-commerce later. A blended pass-through ratio hides channel-level margin leakage.
  • Trade-scheme reinvestment: a list-price increase partly given back as higher trade schemes or consumer promotions shows up as a smaller net price variance. Under Ind AS 115 trade schemes reduce revenue, so measure pass-through on net realisation, not list price.
  • Competitive response: if a competitor does not follow, the price increase may cost volume. The volume variance in the revenue PVM is then partly a consequence of the pricing decision and should be discussed together with it.

Key takeaways

  • Split MPV into market inflation (index-driven) and buying performance (vs index).
  • ₹-neutral pass-through protects gross profit per unit but dilutes GM%. %-neutral needs price increase % = cost inflation %.
  • Price-point packs pass through via grammage. Analyse per kg so the effect appears as price, not as cost efficiency.
  • Track the lag between index, COGS and pricing; the cumulative gap is margin lost (or gained) to timing.
  • Show hedge results beside MPV, and never present MPV and a separate inflation bar for the same quantities.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pass-through arithmetic [L1]

A detergent pack sells at a net realisation of ₹180 with COGS of ₹117. LAB and packaging inflation raise COGS by ₹13 per pack. Compute the price increase needed for (a) ₹-neutral and (b) GM%-neutral pass-through, and the GM% under each. Explain to a sales head why (b) is larger.

Q2. Index vs buying performance [L2]

A soap maker's budget palm oil rate was ₹88,000/t. In Q2 it consumed 3,600 t at an actual landed rate of ₹97,500/t, while the index-derived landed reference for the quarter averaged ₹99,000/t. Split the material price variance into market inflation and buying performance. Comment on what the procurement team did well or badly.

Q3. Grammage reduction in the PVM [L2]

A ₹10 biscuit pack (net realisation to the company ₹8.20) was reduced from 75 g to 68 g in September with the same SKU code. Monthly volume is 40 lakh packs, and material cost per kg is ₹95 both before and after the change. Explain how the revenue PVM and COGS variances will look if analysed per pack vs per kg. Recommend the reporting basis for the monthly pack.

💡 Hint: Work out what "price per kg" and "material cost per pack" do before and after the change.

Q4. Lag analysis model build [L3]

Design an Excel lag-analysis tab for a company whose main input is HDPE, bought on a 45-day contract cycle with 30 days of inventory. Price increases are taken at most once per quarter with a 30-day trade-communication lead time. Specify the input rows, the monthly calculation rows (index, COGS inflation, price variance, cumulative gap) and the charts. Explain how you would forecast the month in which the cumulative gap turns positive.

Q5. Hedge reporting [L3]

An edible-oil company bought CPO futures to hedge a forecast purchase of 5,000 t for December. In October the futures gained ₹2.4 crore while physical prices rose ₹5,200/t above budget. The company has not designated hedge accounting. Explain the Ind AS 109 accounting outcome in October and December. Show how you would present the commodity cost, MPV and hedge result in the management GM bridge so the CFO sees the economic cost.
