Subtopic note

Purchase Price Variance at Receipt vs at Consumption

Scope: Purchase price variance (PPV) at receipt vs at consumption; inventory timing effects

Why it matters

In most ERP-based Indian manufacturers, the material price variance shown in the management P&L is not the price effect on the goods actually sold. It is the variance captured when goods were received. If procurement front-loads purchases ahead of an expected price rise, or a large shipment of imported palm oil arrives in the last week of March, the entire PPV can land in a month in which almost none of that material was consumed. The FP&A margin bridge then blames or credits the wrong period. In addition, Ind AS 2 requires inventory to be carried at a measure that approximates actual cost, so the PPV on unsold stock cannot simply be expensed.

Understanding where PPV is recognised (in the ERP and under Ind AS) is essential for three things: reconciling management and statutory gross margin, explaining timing swings, and avoiding a mismatch between the price variance and the consumption-based usage variance.

Core notes

Two measurement points

Basis Formula Recognised when Typical system setting
PPV at receipt (purchase basis) \((SP - AP) \times \text{Qty received}\) Goods receipt / invoice receipt SAP material with price control S (standard price); variance to a price-difference account at GR and at IR for invoice-price differences
MPV at consumption (usage basis) \((SP - AP) \times \text{Qty consumed}\) Issue to production / COGS Achieved by deferring PPV in inventory and releasing it as stock is consumed, or by actual costing (e.g., SAP Material Ledger)

With moving average price (V) in SAP, no PPV arises at receipt. The invoice price flows into the inventory's moving average, so price changes reach COGS gradually as stock is consumed. Price effects are still present, but they are buried in the actual consumption cost and must be computed analytically against standard.

Worked illustration

Standard palm oil price ₹90,000/t. In March the company receives 500 t at ₹95,000/t and consumes 300 t of it. Opening stock is nil.

  • PPV at receipt: \((90{,}000 - 95{,}000) \times 500 = -₹25\) lakh, posted in March.
  • MPV at consumption: \((90{,}000 - 95{,}000) \times 300 = -₹15\) lakh relates to March COGS.
  • The remaining −₹10 lakh relates to 200 t still in inventory.

Under price control S, inventory is carried at 200 t × ₹90,000 = ₹1.80 crore, while actual cost is 200 t × ₹95,000 = ₹1.90 crore. If the full ₹25 lakh is expensed in March:

  • March management GM is understated by ₹10 lakh versus a consumption-based view.
  • April GM will look ₹10 lakh better than reality when the 200 t is consumed at the ₹90,000 standard.
  • The balance sheet inventory is understated by ₹10 lakh against Ind AS 2 cost.

Ind AS 2 requirement and capitalisation of variances

Ind AS 2 permits standard costs as a measurement technique only if the results approximate cost (Ind AS 2.21). Standards must take into account normal levels of materials, labour, efficiency and capacity utilisation, and must be regularly reviewed and revised. When actual prices differ materially from standard, the company must capitalise the relevant share of variances into closing inventory at period-end. That share is usually allocated by ratio of closing stock to receipts (or by FIFO layers) for each material.

Period-end adjustment for the illustration (₹ lakh):

Account Dr Cr
Inventory – price variance capitalised (raw materials) 10.0
Material price difference (P&L, cost of materials consumed) 10.0

Next month, reverse it, or release it as the stock is consumed:

Account Dr Cr
Cost of materials consumed (variance released) 10.0
Inventory – price variance capitalised 10.0

The same logic applies to variances in WIP and finished goods. A share of production variances (price, usage, labour, overhead) relating to goods still in FG stock should be carried in FG at period-end. Only abnormal amounts (abnormal wastage, idle capacity) are expensed under Ind AS 2.16.

Statutory auditors test this as part of inventory valuation: compare standard with actual purchase price for top materials, recompute the capitalised variance and check that the standard revision policy (s06) is applied.

Inventory timing effects in the management P&L

FP&A should decide, and document, the basis used in the monthly management P&L:

  1. Receipt basis, with period-end capitalisation only at quarter/year-end. Monthly GM is volatile. Quarter-end GM is closer to statutory. Explain monthly swings as "PPV timing".
  2. Consumption basis every month. The management P&L releases PPV in line with consumption, using a simple ratio or a FIFO roll-forward. GM tracks economic cost of goods sold, and the PPV in inventory becomes a balance sheet KPI ("PPV in stock ₹X lakh, will hit COGS over the next N weeks").

The second basis aligns the price variance with the consumption-based usage variance (s01). Price and quantity variances then refer to the same quantities, and the material variance total reconciles to standard cost of actual output minus actual consumption cost.

Rolling PPV forward: a simple model

For each key material, by month:

Opening PPV in stock      =Prior closing PPV in stock
PPV on receipts           =(StdPrice-ActPrice)*QtyReceived
Released to COGS          =(OpeningPPV+PPVReceipts)*QtyConsumed/(OpeningQty+QtyReceived)
Closing PPV in stock      =OpeningPPV+PPVReceipts-Released

This weighted-average release approximates actual-cost consumption. Use FIFO layers if inventory turns slowly and prices move fast.

Forecasting implications

When building the latest estimate:

  • Forecast PPV on planned receipts at the forecast commodity price.
  • Forecast COGS impact on planned consumption, through the roll-forward.
  • Show "PPV in stock" at each month-end. A large adverse balance in March means FY+1 Q1 margin is already partly locked in.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Year-end stock build: buying ahead before an anticipated duty increase or price rise creates a large PPV (favourable if prices then rise above the purchase price). Under receipt basis that hits the current year, under consumption basis next year. Agree the treatment with the CFO before the event.
  • Imported materials: landed cost includes customs duty, freight and FX at the bill-of-entry date. FX differences between invoice and payment dates on the payable are Ind AS 21 exchange differences, not PPV, and go to finance cost or other gains/losses per policy. Keep them out of the GM bridge unless management policy explicitly includes them.
  • Invoice price differences after GR (IR differences): if the goods have already been consumed, SAP posts the difference to the price-difference account, not to stock. Make sure the roll-forward does not count it twice.
  • NRV write-downs: capitalising adverse PPV raises inventory carrying value. Test that it does not exceed net realisable value (Ind AS 2.28–2.33).
  • Intercompany purchases: PPV on purchases from a group plant may include the selling entity's margin. At consolidation it is unrealised profit in inventory, a different adjustment from PPV.

Key takeaways

  • PPV at receipt is recognised when goods arrive; MPV at consumption is recognised as stock is used. The difference sits in inventory.
  • Ind AS 2.21 allows standard costing only if it approximates cost, so material variances on closing stock must be capitalised.
  • A consumption-based management P&L aligns price and usage variances and avoids timing noise in GM.
  • Track "PPV in stock" as a forward-looking margin indicator in the LE.
  • Keep FX on payables, IR differences on consumed stock and intercompany margin separate from PPV.

Practice Questions

Q1. Receipt vs consumption PPV [L1]

Standard price of PET resin is ₹110/kg. Opening stock is 40,000 kg at standard with nil PPV in stock. In January the company receives 1,20,000 kg at ₹118/kg and consumes 1,00,000 kg. Compute PPV at receipt, PPV released to COGS on a weighted-average basis, and closing PPV in stock.

Q2. Period-end entry [L2]

Using your Q1 results, pass the Ind AS 2 period-end journal entry to capitalise the variance relating to closing stock. Then explain the effect on (a) January management GM under receipt basis, (b) statutory cost of materials consumed and (c) February GM if the entry is reversed on 1 February and consumption basis is not otherwise applied.

Q3. Year-end stock build [L3]

In the last week of March, anticipating a duty increase, a company buys 2,000 t of crude palm oil at ₹96,000/t against a standard of ₹90,000/t. Market prices rise to ₹1,02,000/t in April, the duty increases, and the stock is consumed in April–May. Prepare a note for the CFO explaining how March and Q1 FY+1 gross margins will look under receipt and consumption bases, and recommend the basis for the management pack and the investor presentation.

💡 Hint: Separate the accounting PPV versus standard from the economic gain versus the market price at consumption.

Q4. Roll-forward model build [L3]

Build the specification for a 12-month PPV roll-forward tab covering five key materials. Specify inputs (planned receipts, prices, consumption), calculation rows, the checks (opening + receipts − releases = closing, in both quantity and ₹) and the outputs that feed the LE GM bridge and the balance sheet.

Q5. Audit query [L2]

The statutory auditor notes that your company's standard price for a key resin has been unchanged for 14 months, while actual purchase prices averaged 12% above standard. Year-end raw material stock is ₹38 crore at standard. Explain the Ind AS 2 issue, the adjustment likely required and the change to standard-setting policy you would propose.
