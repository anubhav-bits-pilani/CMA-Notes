Subtopic note

Material Price, Usage, Mix and Yield Variances for FP&A

Scope: Material price, usage, mix and yield variances in a commercial FP&A context

Why it matters

In a manufacturing FMCG, chemicals, pharma or auto-components business, material is usually 45–70% of net revenue. That makes it the biggest gross-margin driver after price. The CMA standard costing framework breaks the material cost variance into price, usage, mix and yield. Plant controllers know this split well. In FP&A it is rarely used well. The usual monthly pack shows one line, "material cost adverse ₹66.6 lakh", and nothing on whether the cause was the commodity market (procurement), recipe drift or wastage (plant) or a planned reformulation (R&D).

Commercially, each component has a different owner and a different lever:

  • Price: procurement, commodity hedging and pricing pass-through (s03).
  • Mix: formulation and R&D decisions, plus substitution when one input runs short.
  • Yield: plant operations, process losses, quality rejections.

The FP&A analyst then turns these ₹ amounts into gross-margin basis points (s04), so the CFO can see that a "2.4% margin hit from palm oil" is a pricing conversation and a "2.8% hit from yield" is an operations conversation.

Core notes

The standard and the sign convention

A standard cost card gives, per unit of output, the standard quantity of each input and its standard price. Throughout this week we use the FP&A sign convention: favourable = positive, adverse = negative. For cost lines that means standard minus actual.

Running example (soap noodles plant, one month):

Input Std qty per 1 t output Std price (₹/t) Std cost per t output (₹)
Palm oil 0.8 t 90,000 72,000
Other (caustic, additives) 0.4 t 30,000 12,000
Total input 1.2 t 84,000

The standard yield is 1 t output from 1.2 t input (83.3%).

Actuals: output 1,000 t; palm oil used 820 t at ₹95,000/t; other inputs used 440 t at ₹29,000/t (total input 1,260 t).

Material price variance (MPV)

\[ \text{MPV} = \sum_i (SP_i - AP_i) \times AQ_i \]

where \(SP_i\) is the standard price, \(AP_i\) the actual price and \(AQ_i\) the actual quantity of input \(i\), either purchased or consumed (s05 covers the choice).

  • Palm oil: \((90{,}000 - 95{,}000) \times 820 = -₹41.0\) lakh
  • Other: \((30{,}000 - 29{,}000) \times 440 = +₹4.4\) lakh
  • MPV = −₹36.6 lakh (adverse)

Material usage (quantity) variance

\[ \text{Usage} = \sum_i (SQ_i - AQ_i) \times SP_i \]

\(SQ_i\) is the standard quantity for actual output: 1,000 t × 0.8 = 800 t of palm oil and 1,000 t × 0.4 = 400 t of other.

  • Palm oil: \((800 - 820) \times 90{,}000 = -₹18.0\) lakh
  • Other: \((400 - 440) \times 30{,}000 = -₹12.0\) lakh
  • Usage = −₹30.0 lakh (adverse)

Splitting usage into mix and yield

Usage splits further when inputs are substitutable (oils in a blend, grades of steel scrap, solvents). Define \(AQ^{\text{std mix}}_i\) as the actual total input split in the standard proportions. Here that is 1,260 t × 2/3 = 840 t palm and 1,260 t × 1/3 = 420 t other.

\[ \text{Mix} = \sum_i (AQ^{\text{std mix}}_i - AQ_i) \times SP_i \qquad \text{Yield} = \sum_i (SQ_i - AQ^{\text{std mix}}_i) \times SP_i \]

  • Mix: palm \((840 - 820) \times 90{,}000 = +₹18.0\) lakh; other \((420 - 440) \times 30{,}000 = -₹6.0\) lakh; Mix = +₹12.0 lakh. The plant used proportionately more of the cheaper input.
  • Yield: palm \((800 - 840) \times 90{,}000 = -₹36.0\) lakh; other \((400 - 420) \times 30{,}000 = -₹6.0\) lakh; Yield = −₹42.0 lakh.

Cross-check on the yield using the output view. 1,260 t of input should have produced 1,260 ÷ 1.2 = 1,050 t, but only 1,000 t came out:

\[ \text{Yield} = (1{,}000 - 1{,}050) \times 84{,}000 = -₹42.0 \text{ lakh} \]

Mix + Yield = +12.0 − 42.0 = −30.0 = Usage.

Reconciliation

Component ₹ lakh Owner
Price (36.6) Procurement / commodity desk
Mix 12.0 R&D / plant formulation
Yield (42.0) Plant operations
Total material variance (66.6)
Standard cost of actual output (1,000 × 84,000) 840.0
Actual material cost (820 × 95,000 + 440 × 29,000) 906.6

The total must equal standard cost of actual output minus actual cost: 840.0 − 906.6 = −66.6. That is the "no unexplained plug" rule from the week overview.

Reading the components commercially

  • Mix favourable + yield adverse together usually means a substitution that did not work. Cheaper "other" inputs replaced palm oil and the process lost output. Report the net (−₹30 lakh) as the real cost of the substitution decision, not the +₹12 lakh mix alone.
  • Price adverse, usage on standard: a market problem. Test it against the commodity index (s03) and the hedge book.
  • Yield adverse with no mix change: process loss, quality rejection, change-over waste or bad raw-material quality. This is often linked to a cheaper supplier, so price favourable / yield adverse can be one decision.

Excel implementation

Lay out one row per input with columns for SQ per unit, SP, AQ, AP and actual output. Then:

Std qty for actual output   =StdQtyPerUnit*Output
MPV                         =(SP-AP)*AQ
Usage                       =(SQ-AQ)*SP
AQ at std mix               =SUM(AQ_range)*StdQtyPerUnit/SUM(StdQtyPerUnit_range)
Mix                         =(AQStdMix-AQ)*SP
Yield                       =(SQ-AQStdMix)*SP
Check (should be 0)         =SUMPRODUCT(SQ_range,SP_range)-SUMPRODUCT(AQ_range,AP_range)-SUM(MPV_range,Mix_range,Yield_range)

Put a zero-check cell at the bottom of the schedule and conditionally format it red if ABS(check)>0.5.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Non-substitutable inputs (a bottle and a cap, a chassis and an engine): a mix variance has no economic meaning. Report usage only, by input.
  • Purchased vs used quantity: if MPV is computed on purchases, the price/usage/mix/yield set will not sum to the consumption-based total. Reconcile through inventory (s05).
  • Joint products and by-product credits (e.g., fatty acid distillate recovered in refining): net the by-product credit at standard into yield, and show by-product price movements separately.
  • Standard yield set too generously: a permanently adverse yield variance usually means the standard is wrong, not the plant. Flag it as a planning issue (s06) rather than escalating it every month.
  • Moisture and grade adjustments: palm oil and agri inputs are bought on a specified basis (FFA, moisture). Quality claims and debit notes should reduce actual price, not sit in "other income".

Key takeaways

  • Material variance = price + usage, and usage = mix + yield. The total must reconcile to standard cost of actual output minus actual cost.
  • Standard quantity is always for actual output. Using budgeted output mixes volume into the cost variance.
  • Mix only makes sense for substitutable inputs. Read mix and yield together, because they often come from one substitution decision.
  • Each component has a different owner (procurement, R&D, plant), which is why FP&A must split it.
  • The ₹ amounts become useful to the C-suite once converted to GM bps (s04).

Practice Questions

Q1. Four-way material variance for a biscuit line [L2]

A biscuit plant's standard per 1,000 kg of output is 700 kg wheat flour at ₹32/kg, 250 kg sugar at ₹42/kg and 150 kg vanaspati at ₹120/kg (total input 1,100 kg). In October, output was 50,000 kg. Actual consumption was 36,000 kg flour at ₹34/kg, 12,000 kg sugar at ₹41/kg and 8,500 kg vanaspati at ₹126/kg. Compute the price, usage, mix and yield variances by input and in total, and show that they reconcile to standard cost of actual output minus actual cost.

Q2. Is mix meaningful here? [L1]

For each of the following, state whether a material mix variance is economically meaningful, and why: (a) a paint plant blending titanium dioxide and calcium carbonate fillers; (b) a two-wheeler assembly line consuming engines and frames; (c) an edible-oil refinery blending palm olein and soybean oil within FSSAI blend limits.

💡 Hint: Ask whether more of one input can physically replace less of another without changing the output specification.

Q3. Substitution decision review [L3]

The procurement head of a detergent company proudly reports a favourable mix variance of ₹45 lakh for Q2. It came from replacing 15% of LAB (linear alkyl benzene) with a cheaper surfactant. The same quarter shows an adverse yield variance of ₹62 lakh and a ₹9 lakh increase in customer complaints-related credit notes. Draft the variance commentary you would present to the CFO, including your recommendation on whether to continue the substitution.

Q4. Excel schedule build [L3]

Design an Excel schedule for a three-input process that takes standard quantities per unit, standard prices, actual quantities, actual prices and actual output as inputs. It should output price, mix and yield variances per input and a zero-check cell. Write the formulas for the "AQ at standard mix" column and the zero-check cell, and explain how the schedule should behave if a fourth input is added mid-year.

Q5. Owner mapping [L2]

A pharma API plant reports, for March, material price −₹18 lakh, mix +₹4 lakh and yield −₹27 lakh against net revenue of ₹9 crore. Map each variance to a likely owner. Express each one as a percentage of net revenue, and list two data points you would request before the monthly business review to confirm the root cause of the yield variance.
