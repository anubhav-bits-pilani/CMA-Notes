Subtopic note

Labour Rate, Efficiency and Idle-Time Variances in FP&A

Scope: Labour rate, efficiency and idle-time variances; contract vs permanent labour

Why it matters

Direct labour is a smaller share of COGS in Indian FMCG (often 3–8% of net revenue). It is far larger in auto components, engineering, garments, leather and electronics assembly. Labour variances matter in FP&A for three reasons:

  1. Wage settlements, minimum-wage revisions and contractor rate escalations are discrete events. They hit the margin in a step that must be explained.
  2. Efficiency and idle time measure how well capacity is used. They are an early warning of volume shortfalls, breakdowns or material shortages upstream.
  3. The cash meaning of an efficiency variance depends on whether the workforce is permanent (a fixed cost in the short run) or contract (variable). A CFO who hears "labour efficiency favourable ₹10 lakh" will assume cash saved. For permanent staff that is usually wrong.

Core notes

Definitions and the hours ladder

Labour variances rest on three hour measures:

  • Hours paid \(H_p\): the payroll or contractor bill basis, including idle time.
  • Hours worked \(H_w = H_p - H_{\text{idle}}\): productive clocked hours.
  • Standard hours for actual output \(SH\): actual output × standard hours per unit.

With standard rate \(SR\) and actual rate \(AR\) (favourable positive):

\[ \text{Rate} = (SR - AR) \times H_p \qquad \text{Idle time} = -H_{\text{idle}} \times SR \qquad \text{Efficiency} = (SH - H_w) \times SR \]

The total labour cost variance is \(SH \times SR - H_p \times AR\), and the three components always sum to it.

Worked illustration

Standard: 2 hours per unit at ₹250/hour. Output 10,000 units. Hours paid 22,000 at ₹260/hour. Idle time 1,000 hours (a 2-day compressor breakdown). Hours worked 21,000. Standard hours for actual output 20,000.

Component Working ₹ lakh
Rate (250 − 260) × 22,000 (2.2)
Idle time 1,000 × 250 (2.5)
Efficiency (20,000 − 21,000) × 250 (2.5)
Total 20,000 × 250 − 22,000 × 260 = 50.0 − 57.2 (7.2)

The rate variance uses hours paid, because the ₹10/hour premium was paid on every hour, idle ones included. Efficiency uses hours worked, because idle hours are already explained separately. If you compute efficiency on hours paid, the idle time lands inside "efficiency" and the plant manager is blamed for a breakdown that maintenance owns.

Causes and owners

Variance Typical causes Owner
Rate Wage settlement, minimum-wage/VDA revision, overtime premium, skill-grade mix, contractor rate card change HR / IR, plant head
Idle time Breakdown, power outage, material shortage, strike, lack of orders Maintenance, supply planning, sales
Efficiency Learning curve on new SKU, line balancing, absenteeism cover by unskilled workers, poor material quality Plant operations, industrial engineering

Overtime is a classic hidden item. Overtime hours at a premium inflate the rate variance, and often signal that standard hours are being exceeded (efficiency) or that the workforce plan is short.

Contract vs permanent labour

Indian plants typically run a core of permanent workmen (covered by long-term wage settlements under the industrial-relations framework) plus contract labour supplied through licensed contractors. The four Labour Codes (Code on Wages, Industrial Relations Code, Social Security Code and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code) consolidate the earlier wage, contract-labour, PF/ESI and factory laws. Their commencement and the state rules under them are date-sensitive, so check the current notification status before modelling statutory cost changes such as the revised "wages" definition for PF and gratuity.

Aspect Permanent labour Contract labour
Cost behaviour Fixed in the short run (monthly wage, settlement-driven) Variable: per man-day, per shift or per piece
Rate variance driver Settlement increments, VDA, grade drift Contractor rate card, statutory minimum wage revision, contractor service charge, PF/ESI loading
Meaning of favourable efficiency Capacity freed, no cash saving unless headcount or overtime falls Fewer man-days billed, real cash saving
Meaning of idle time Paid anyway, a real cost of unused capacity Often not billed (if contract allows), or a minimum-guarantee charge
FP&A model treatment Headcount × CTC by grade, phased to settlement dates Man-days driver × rate per man-day, linked to production volume

For FP&A this means separating labour variances by workforce type. A favourable efficiency variance on a permanent workforce should be shown as "capacity released ₹X lakh, realisable only if overtime or contract man-days are reduced". It should not be banked as a cost saving in the latest estimate.

Linking to gross margin bps

If the ₹7.2 lakh adverse variance relates to a month with net revenue of ₹4 crore:

\[ \text{bps impact} = \frac{-7.2}{400} \times 10{,}000 = -180 \text{ bps} \]

Split it as rate −55 bps, idle −62.5 bps and efficiency −62.5 bps, so the operations review can see the breakdown was worth more margin than the wage increment (s04, c05).

Model implementation

In a driver-based COGS model:

Std hours for output   =Output*StdHrsPerUnit
Rate variance          =(StdRate-ActRate)*HrsPaid
Idle variance          =-IdleHrs*StdRate
Efficiency variance    =(StdHrs-(HrsPaid-IdleHrs))*StdRate
Check                  =StdHrs*StdRate-HrsPaid*ActRate-(Rate+Idle+Eff)

Keep separate input rows for permanent and contract hours. Payroll data (hours paid, rates) and MES or time-office data (hours worked, idle hours, output) usually come from different systems, so add a reconciliation row between payroll hours and time-office hours.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Normal vs abnormal idle time: a standard may already allow for normal idle time (shift change-overs, tea breaks). Then only idle hours above the allowance are variances. Abnormal idle time arising from a strike or flood is, in cost accounting, charged to costing P&L rather than product cost. Under Ind AS 2, abnormal wastage and unallocated fixed overheads caused by low production are expensed, not inventoried.
  • Piece-rate workers: no efficiency variance in ₹ terms arises because pay follows output. The rate per piece is the only lever.
  • Mixed-skill substitution: using semi-skilled workers on a skilled grade gives a favourable rate and an adverse efficiency. This is the labour analogue of material mix/yield. Report the net.
  • Retrospective wage settlement: arrears for prior months booked in the current month distort the rate variance. Show arrears as a one-off, separate from the run-rate rate variance.
  • Learning curve on new lines: set a ramp-up standard (declining hours per unit) rather than the steady-state standard. Otherwise launch months show alarming adverse efficiency that is fully expected.

Key takeaways

  • Rate uses hours paid, idle uses idle hours at standard rate, and efficiency uses hours worked against standard hours for actual output.
  • The three components reconcile to standard cost of actual output minus actual labour cost.
  • Idle time is always adverse and has owners outside the line (maintenance, planning, sales).
  • Favourable efficiency on permanent labour frees capacity but saves no cash. On contract labour it is real.
  • Track arrears, overtime premium and statutory changes as separate, explainable items.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-way labour variance [L1]

An auto-component plant has a standard of 0.5 hours per unit at ₹280/hour. In July it produced 48,000 units. Payroll paid 26,500 hours at ₹290/hour, including 900 hours of idle time due to a die failure. Compute the rate, idle-time and efficiency variances and reconcile them to the total labour cost variance.

Q2. Permanent vs contract efficiency [L2]

A garment unit employs 300 permanent tailors (fixed monthly CTC ₹21,000 each) and hires contract tailors at ₹780 per man-day. In November the standard man-days for actual output were 9,400. Actual man-days worked were 7,200 permanent and 1,900 contract. Compute the efficiency variance at a standard rate of ₹800 per man-day. Then explain how much of it represents a cash saving, and how you would present it in the latest estimate.

💡 Hint: Ask which part of the workforce cost would actually have changed if fewer man-days had been needed.

Q3. Retrospective settlement [L2]

A tyre plant signed a three-year wage settlement in December with an 11% increase effective from 1 April. December payroll includes ₹1.65 crore of arrears for April–November, plus the new monthly rate. The December labour rate variance shows −₹1.86 crore. Explain how you would split this for the December pack and the YTD pack. State the journal entry that should ideally have been made in earlier months if the settlement had been probable and reliably estimable.

Q4. Root-cause narrative [L3]

An electronics assembler reports, for Q3, labour rate −₹6 lakh, idle −₹14 lakh and efficiency −₹11 lakh on net revenue of ₹22 crore. Production planning notes show three days of line stoppage awaiting imported chips, and a new smartphone model launched in the same quarter. Convert each variance to bps of net revenue and write a five-line CFO commentary that assigns ownership. Propose one change to the standards for next year.

Q5. Payroll vs time-office reconciliation [L2]

For a month, payroll shows 41,200 hours paid while the time-office system shows 39,800 hours clocked on production orders and 600 hours booked as idle. List the possible reasons for the 800-hour gap. Explain how each would distort the rate, idle and efficiency variances if left unreconciled.
