Roadmap topic: Direct Material & Labour Cost Variances in FP&A: Translating Material Price/Usage and Labour Rate/Efficiency variances into gross margin basis-point impacts

Overview

CMA standard costing gives you a complete toolkit: material price, usage, mix and yield; labour rate, efficiency and idle time; and reconciliation of standard to actual profit. In a plant cost office these are produced every month. In FP&A the same numbers have to answer a different question: how much gross margin did we lose or gain, why, who owns it, and does it continue?

That needs three translations:

  • from rupees to basis points of margin;
  • from formula components to business drivers (commodity markets, pricing pass-through, plant yield, wage settlements);
  • from the ERP's recognition timing to an economically meaningful period (PPV at receipt vs consumption, standard revisions).

The topic sits between Topic 1 (revenue and margin PVM) and Topic 4 (EBITDA bridge). In the GM PVM of Topic 1, the "cost" bar is \(\sum (BC_i - AC_i) \times AQ_i\). This topic opens that bar. Inside it are material price (split into market inflation and buying performance), material usage (split into mix and yield) and the labour variances, each converted into bps of actual net revenue so it can sit in a GM% bridge next to price and mix.

For a CMA moving into FP&A, the formulas are familiar. The new skills are the timing and accounting overlays (Ind AS 2 variance capitalisation, SAP price control, standard revisions), the commercial reading (pass-through, lag, hedging) and the communication format (bps, owner, run-rate vs one-off).

Suggested learning sequence:

  1. Re-derive the material and labour variance formulas, with standard quantity always flexed to actual output (s01, s02, c01–c04).
  2. Learn the bps conversion and the sequential GM% decomposition (s04, c05, c06).
  3. Add the commercial overlay: commodity inflation and pass-through (s03).
  4. Finish with the timing and accounting overlays: PPV at receipt vs consumption (s05) and mid-year standard revisions (s06).

Related concepts to study

CMA standard costing and profit reconciliation

CMA standard costing and reconciliation of standard to actual profit

The CMA operating statement starts from budgeted profit, adds sales volume (and mix) variances at standard margin to reach standard profit on actual sales, and then adds cost variances (material, labour, variable and fixed overhead) and the sales price variance to reach actual profit. The FP&A GM bridge is the same reconciliation, presented as a waterfall and expressed in bps. Revisit the fixed overhead volume and capacity variances: under absorption costing they show up in GM, while in a contribution-format FP&A bridge fixed overheads are a separate opex-like block. Choose the format deliberately and make sure the GM definition in the pack matches it.

Commodity hedging and pass-through lag

Commodity hedging and price-pass-through lag analysis

Hedging (supplier forward contracts, exchange-traded futures, currency forwards) shifts and smooths the commodity cost curve. Pass-through lag analysis measures how quickly pricing catches up. Together they explain the timing of margin pressure. A company 70% hedged for two quarters will see index inflation hit COGS later, which buys time for price increases. FP&A should model the hedge book (volumes, rates, maturities) beside the commodity forecast. Under Ind AS 109, distinguish own-use contracts (outside derivative accounting) from financial hedges, and cash-flow hedge accounting (OCI, then basis adjustment into inventory) from undesignated hedges (P&L volatility). See s03.

Contribution margin and pricing (Week 21)

→ Week 21: contribution margin and pricing decisions

Material and labour variances feed directly into unit contribution. Week 21 uses contribution margin to decide on price increases, discounts, special orders and SKU rationalisation. The pass-through analysis here (₹-neutral vs %-neutral) is a pricing decision. Contribution per unit, price elasticity and competitor response determine whether a %-neutral increase is achievable. When permanent labour is treated as fixed, labour efficiency variances do not change contribution per unit. Only contract labour and materials do. Keep that classification consistent between this week's bridge and next fortnight's pricing models.

Ind AS 2 and capitalising variances into inventory

Inventory valuation (Ind AS 2) and capitalisation of variances into inventory (→ Phase 1)

Ind AS 2 measures inventory at the lower of cost and NRV. Standard cost is allowed only where it approximates actual cost (Ind AS 2.21), and standards must be reviewed regularly. Material and production variances relating to closing stock therefore need to be capitalised. Abnormal wastage, abnormal idle time and unabsorbed fixed overheads from low production are expensed (Ind AS 2.13 and 2.16). For FP&A, this creates a difference between "variance incurred" and "variance in P&L", which must be reconciled in the GM bridge (s05). A mid-year standard revision triggers stock revaluation, and that must not double-count capitalised variances (s06).

Common pitfalls

Reporting in ₹ only, without bps

Reporting cost variances in ₹ only without margin bps, losing C-suite relevance

Mistake: the pack shows "material adverse ₹4.2 crore, labour adverse ₹0.6 crore" with no scale. Why: plant cost reports are built in ₹, and bps need a revenue denominator that the cost office does not hold. Correct treatment: convert every cost variance to bps of actual net revenue (c05), present a GM% bridge where cost sub-components foot to the cost step (s04, c06), and lead the commentary with bps. Detection: check that the pack has a GM% bridge and that the bps add to Actual GM% − Budget GM%.

Mixing PPV at purchase with P&L variances at consumption

Mixing PPV at purchase with P&L variances at consumption, creating timing mismatches

Mistake: the bridge combines a PPV computed on receipts with usage variances and standard COGS based on consumption. The material line then does not reconcile to actual COGS, and months with stock builds show spurious price swings. Why: the ERP posts PPV at GR/IR, and FP&A takes it straight from the price-difference account. Correct treatment: pick one basis (preferably consumption for the management P&L), roll PPV through inventory, and reconcile to the statutory P&L through a "PPV in stock movement" line (s05). Detection: compare receipts and consumption quantities. If they differ materially while the PPV equals receipts × price difference, the bases are mixed.

Wrong standard quantity basis

Using actual output standard quantities incorrectly (standard qty must be for actual output)

Mistake: usage or efficiency variances are computed against budgeted quantities, or against standard quantities for planned or gross output instead of actual good output. Why: the AOP file holds budgeted consumption and is easy to use. Correct treatment: SQ = actual good output × standard input per unit, and SH = actual output × standard hours per unit (c02, c03). Volume differences are already captured in the GM PVM volume bar. Detection: if usage variances move with production volume (favourable when output is below budget), the basis is wrong.

Double-counting commodity inflation

Double-counting commodity inflation in both price variance and a separate "inflation" bar

Mistake: a GM bridge shows a "commodity inflation" bar (index change × volume) and also the full material price variance. The same rupees appear twice and are offset by an unexplained plug. Why: procurement and FP&A each produce their own view, and they are pasted into one chart. Correct treatment: either show MPV as one bar with inflation commentary, or split MPV into market inflation and buying performance, which sum to MPV (s03). Detection: add up all cost bars and compare with total cost variance (standard cost of actual output minus actual COGS). Any excess means double counting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Soap business GM bridge [L3]

A personal-care company's soap division reports for Q2 (₹ crore unless stated): budget net revenue 180.0 and budget GM 72.0; actual net revenue 186.0 and actual GM 69.3. Net price, volume and mix effects on revenue are +4.5, +6.8 and −5.3 respectively, and the GM PVM gives volume +2.7 and mix −1.6 at budget unit margins. Cost data: palm oil standard ₹90,000/t, actual consumption 7,800 t at ₹96,500/t against a standard quantity for actual output of 7,650 t; the index-derived landed price averaged ₹97,200/t. Packaging price variance was −₹1.9 crore and labour efficiency +₹0.4 crore.

(a) Compute the palm oil price variance, split into market and buying performance, and the palm oil usage variance. (b) Build the GM ₹ bridge, identify any residual and suggest what it might represent. (c) Convert the cost variances to bps of actual net revenue. (d) Write a five-line CFO commentary.

Q2. PPV timing and standard revision [L3]

A detergent maker has an LAB standard of ₹1,15,000/t from April. On 1 October the standard is revised to ₹1,24,000/t. In September it received 1,200 t at ₹1,23,000/t and consumed 700 t. Opening September stock was nil, and the September PPV was expensed at receipt. In October it consumed 800 t (the 500 t carried forward plus 300 t received at ₹1,25,000/t).

(a) Compute the September PPV at receipt and on a consumption basis. (b) Compute the 1 October revaluation of stock on hand and pass the journal entry. (c) Compute the October planning and operational variances vs AOP. (d) Explain how September and October GM appear under the ERP basis versus an economic (consumption, AOP-based) view, and design the bridge rows that make both months reconcile to AOP.

Q3. Labour and capacity in an auto-components plant [L2]

Standard: 0.8 hours per unit at ₹300/hour. Q3 output 1,50,000 units. Permanent workforce paid 1,08,000 hours at ₹305/hour; contract labour paid 18,000 hours at ₹270/hour (standard rate for contract labour ₹260/hour, standard hours not split by workforce type). Idle time of 4,000 hours (permanent) arose from a supplier delay of castings. Net revenue for the quarter was ₹42 crore.

(a) Compute rate (by workforce type), idle and efficiency variances. (b) Convert them to bps. (c) Explain which part represents a cash cost or saving and which represents capacity. (d) Recommend whether the idle time should be shown as a plant variance or a procurement variance in the monthly review.

💡 Hint: Settle the efficiency basis (which standard rate to use for total hours) before you compute anything, and state the assumption.

Q4. Pass-through strategy case [L3]

An FMCG company sells 60% of its biscuit volume in ₹5 and ₹10 price-point packs through general trade and 40% in larger packs through modern trade and e-commerce. Current blended net realisation is ₹160/kg, COGS ₹104/kg (material ₹82/kg), and GM 35%. The commodity outlook shows wheat and palm inflation of 12% on material cost for H2. Evaluate three options: (i) grammage reduction on price-point packs only; (ii) list-price increase of 6% on larger packs only; (iii) both. For each, quantify the GM% outcome assuming no volume loss, identify which variance lines in the PVM and cost bridges will show the effect, and discuss volume and channel risks. Recommend an option.
