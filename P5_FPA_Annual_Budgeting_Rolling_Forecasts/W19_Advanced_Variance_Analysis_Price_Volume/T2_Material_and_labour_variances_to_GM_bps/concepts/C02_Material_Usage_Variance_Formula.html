Key concept

Material Usage Variance Formula

Concept: Material usage variance = (Standard qty for actual output − Actual qty used) × Standard price

Statement

\[ \text{Usage variance} = \sum_i (SQ_i - AQ_i) \times SP_i \qquad\text{with}\qquad SQ_i = Q_{\text{actual output}} \times q^{\text{std}}_i \]

where:

  • \(SQ_i\) = standard quantity of input \(i\) allowed for the actual output;
  • \(q^{\text{std}}_i\) = standard input quantity per unit of output (from the BOM / standard cost card);
  • \(AQ_i\) = actual quantity of input \(i\) consumed;
  • \(SP_i\) = standard price of input \(i\).

Positive = favourable (less material used than allowed); negative = adverse.

Explanation

The usage variance measures physical efficiency of material use, priced at standard so that price movements do not contaminate it. The defining feature is that standard quantity is flexed to actual output. Comparing actual consumption with the budgeted quantity (based on budgeted output) would mix a volume effect into the cost variance. If output is 10% above budget, consumption naturally rises about 10%, and a static comparison would wrongly call that an adverse usage variance. In the GM PVM, the volume effect is already captured by the volume variance at budget unit margin. Using flexed standard quantity keeps the cost variance free of volume.

For substitutable inputs, usage splits into mix and yield (s01):

\[ \text{Usage} = \underbrace{\sum_i (AQ^{\text{std mix}}_i - AQ_i) SP_i}_{\text{mix}} + \underbrace{\sum_i (SQ_i - AQ^{\text{std mix}}_i) SP_i}_{\text{yield}} \]

where \(AQ^{\text{std mix}}_i\) is the actual total input split in standard proportions.

Practical points:

  • "Actual output" should be good output (after quality rejection) if the standard is set on good output. Otherwise rejection losses disappear from the yield variance.
  • Normal process loss built into \(q^{\text{std}}_i\) is not a variance. Losses beyond normal are adverse usage/yield, and abnormal losses are expensed under Ind AS 2.16.
  • Consumption data comes from production orders or backflushing. With backflushing, actual consumption equals standard by construction unless physical stock counts adjust it. Check for this before reporting "zero usage variance" as good performance.

Illustration

Actual output 1,000 t of soap noodles; standard per tonne 0.8 t palm oil at ₹90,000 and 0.4 t other at ₹30,000.

Input SQ for actual output AQ used SP (₹/t) Usage (₹ lakh)
Palm oil 800 820 90,000 (18.0)
Other 400 440 30,000 (12.0)
Total 1,200 1,260 (30.0)

The split is mix +₹12.0 lakh and yield −₹42.0 lakh. On net revenue of ₹15 crore, usage is −200 bps of GM.

Common error: using budgeted output of 1,100 t would give SQ of 880 t palm and 440 t other. Palm usage would then read +₹54 lakh favourable, which is meaningless and only reflects the output shortfall.

Practice Questions

Q1. Flexed standard quantity [L1]

A biscuit line's standard is 0.62 kg flour per kg of biscuits at ₹33/kg. Budgeted output was 4,00,000 kg; actual good output was 3,70,000 kg; flour consumed was 2,35,000 kg. Compute the usage variance, and show the misleading figure that results from comparing with budgeted output.

Q2. Rejections and backflushing [L2]

A plant backflushes materials at standard on confirmation of production orders. A month-end physical count shows raw material stock 4,800 kg lower than book, at a standard price of ₹140/kg. 2,000 kg of output was also rejected after confirmation, at a standard material cost of ₹95/kg of output. Explain how each item should be reflected in the usage variance and which owner should explain it.

💡 Hint: Consider what the system recorded as "actual consumption" and what physically happened.
