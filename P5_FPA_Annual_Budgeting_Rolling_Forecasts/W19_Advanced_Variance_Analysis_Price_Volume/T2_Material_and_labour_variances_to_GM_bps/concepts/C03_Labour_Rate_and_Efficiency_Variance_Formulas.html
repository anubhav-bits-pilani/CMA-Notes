Key concept

Labour Rate and Efficiency Variance Formulas

Concept: Labour rate variance = (Standard rate − Actual rate) × Actual hours paid; efficiency = (Standard hours for actual output − Actual hours worked) × Standard rate

Statement

\[ \text{Labour rate variance} = (SR - AR) \times H_{\text{paid}} \]

\[ \text{Labour efficiency variance} = (SH - H_{\text{worked}}) \times SR, \qquad SH = Q_{\text{actual output}} \times h^{\text{std}} \]

where \(SR\) is the standard rate per hour, \(AR\) the actual rate per hour (total wages ÷ hours paid), \(H_{\text{paid}}\) the hours paid (including idle), \(H_{\text{worked}}\) the productive hours, \(SH\) the standard hours allowed for actual output, and \(h^{\text{std}}\) the standard hours per unit. Positive = favourable.

Together with the idle-time variance (c04):

\[ SH \times SR - H_{\text{paid}} \times AR = \text{Rate} + \text{Idle} + \text{Efficiency} \]

Explanation

The rate variance uses hours paid because the price of labour (wage rate, overtime premium, contractor rate card) applies to every hour paid, productive or not. The efficiency variance uses hours worked because idle hours are explained separately. Including them would make the line supervisor answer for a power cut or a material shortage.

Standard hours are flexed to actual output, as for materials, so volume differences do not enter the cost variance.

Interpretation depends on workforce type (s02):

  • Contract labour paid per man-day: an efficiency variance changes the bill, so it is a cash effect.
  • Permanent labour on a fixed monthly CTC: an efficiency variance measures capacity used or released. It becomes cash only if overtime, contract man-days or headcount actually change.

Common rate-variance drivers in India: annual increments and long-term wage settlements, variable DA revisions linked to CPI, state minimum-wage notifications for contract labour, overtime premium (statutorily at twice the ordinary rate for factory workers), and the shift-allowance or skill-grade mix. Efficiency drivers: learning curve, line balancing, absenteeism, material quality, change-overs.

Illustration

Standard 2 hours per unit at ₹250/hour; output 10,000 units; 22,000 hours paid at ₹260; 1,000 idle hours; 21,000 hours worked.

Component Working ₹ lakh
Rate (250 − 260) × 22,000 (2.2)
Idle time 1,000 × 250 (2.5)
Efficiency (20,000 − 21,000) × 250 (2.5)
Total 50.0 − 57.2 (7.2)

If efficiency were wrongly computed on hours paid, it would show (20,000 − 22,000) × 250 = −₹5.0 lakh, and the idle time would disappear into it.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Rate and efficiency [L1]

A pump manufacturer's standard is 3.5 hours per pump at ₹320/hour. In September it produced 6,200 pumps. 23,400 hours were paid with total wages of ₹77.22 lakh, and time records show 22,600 hours worked. Compute the rate, idle and efficiency variances and verify the total.

Q2. Overtime in the rate variance [L2]

In October, the same plant pays 20,000 normal hours at ₹320 and 2,500 overtime hours at double rate, to meet a festive-season surge. Compute the actual average rate and the rate variance. Explain how you would separate the "overtime premium" component in the monthly commentary and why it matters for the next month's capacity plan.

💡 Hint: Compute the variance first as if all hours were at the normal rate, then isolate the premium.

Q3. Cash meaning [L3]

A plant with 100% permanent workforce reports a favourable labour efficiency variance of ₹18 lakh for Q2, driven by automation of a packing line. The plant head wants it counted toward the FY cost-saving target. Write the FP&A response, covering what is and is not a saving, and what actions would convert it into cash.
