Key concept

Decomposing GM % Change into Price, Mix and Cost bps

Concept: GM % change = Actual GM% − Budget GM%, decomposed into price, mix and cost effects in bps

Statement

\[ \Delta \text{GM\%} = \text{GM\%}_{\text{act}} - \text{GM\%}_{\text{bud}} = \Delta_{\text{mix}} + \Delta_{\text{price}} + \Delta_{\text{cost}} \;(+\,\Delta_{\text{volume}} = 0) \]

Each effect is computed by sequential recalculation of GM% in a fixed order:

\[ \Delta_{\text{mix}} = \text{GM\%}(AQ, \text{act mix}, BP, BC) - \text{GM\%}_{\text{bud}} \]

\[ \Delta_{\text{price}} = \text{GM\%}(AQ, \text{act mix}, AP, BC) - \text{GM\%}(AQ, \text{act mix}, BP, BC) \]

\[ \Delta_{\text{cost}} = \text{GM\%}_{\text{act}} - \text{GM\%}(AQ, \text{act mix}, AP, BC) = \frac{\sum_i (BC_i - AC_i) \times AQ_i}{R_{\text{act}}} \]

where \(AQ\) = actual quantities, \(BP\)/\(AP\) = budget/actual prices, \(BC\)/\(AC\) = budget/actual unit costs and \(R_{\text{act}}\) = actual net revenue. Multiply each by 10,000 for bps.

Explanation

₹ variances are additive at any order. Margin-% effects are not, because price and mix change the denominator. The sequential method avoids a residual: each step changes one driver, the GM% at the end of each step becomes the start of the next, and the steps therefore sum exactly to the total change.

Properties worth knowing:

  • Volume at constant mix leaves GM% unchanged (0 bps), so it is listed only for completeness.
  • Cost last makes the cost bps equal to the simple ratio cost variance ÷ actual revenue (c05). That is convenient, because cost sub-components (MPV, usage, labour) then convert to bps independently.
  • Price bps equals the naive ratio scaled by one minus the pre-price GM%:

\[ \Delta_{\text{price}} = \frac{PV}{R_{\text{act}}} \times \left(1 - \text{GM\%}_{\text{before price}}\right) \]

where \(PV\) is the ₹ price variance. A price change moves revenue and GM by the same ₹, so only the "non-margin" share of it changes the ratio.

  • Order dependence: a different order (e.g., price before mix) gives a slightly different split of the same total. Fix the order (mix → price → cost is common, matching the ₹ PVM sequence) and document it on the bridge.

Illustration

Core two-SKU data (units '000, ₹ '000): budget revenue 11,000, GM 3,800; actual revenue 11,520, GM 3,510.

Budget 11,000 3,800 34.545%

Check on price: \(-180 / 11{,}520 \times (1 - 0.33846) \times 10{,}000 = -103.4\) bps. Check on cost: \(-270 / 11{,}520 \times 10{,}000 = -234.4\) bps.

Story for the CFO: "Revenue grew 4.7% but gross margin fell 408 bps: 234 bps from input costs, 103 bps from net price (SKU Y price cut), and 70 bps from mix shift towards lower-margin SKU Y. Volume added gross profit but no margin."
