Key concept

Material Price Variance Formula

Concept: Material price variance = (Standard price − Actual price) × Actual qty purchased (or used)

Statement

\[ \text{MPV} = \sum_i (SP_i - AP_i) \times AQ_i \]

where, for each material \(i\):

  • \(SP_i\) = standard price per unit of input (₹/kg, ₹/t) from the standard cost card;
  • \(AP_i\) = actual landed price per unit (invoice + freight + non-creditable duties, net of recoverable GST);
  • \(AQ_i\) = actual quantity, either purchased (PPV at receipt) or used (MPV at consumption).

A positive result is favourable (bought cheaper than standard). A negative result is adverse.

Explanation

The price variance isolates the effect of paying a different price for the quantity actually handled. Multiplying by actual quantity, not standard quantity, is deliberate. It leaves the quantity effect (usage, mix, yield) to be valued at standard price, so the two variances together reconcile exactly to the total material cost variance without a joint "price × quantity" residual. The same logic underlies the revenue price variance at actual volume in Topic 1.

Purchased or used? Under a purchase basis the variance is recognised when the material is received, which suits procurement performance measurement. Under a usage basis it is recognised when the material is consumed. That suits gross margin reporting and aligns with the usage variance, which is always based on quantity used (s05). The ERP setting (e.g., SAP price control S, with price differences at GR/IR) determines the default. Ind AS 2.21 then requires the variance relating to closing stock to be capitalised if standards do not approximate cost.

Decomposition for commercial use. In commodity-heavy businesses, MPV is further split into market inflation (standard vs index price) and buying performance (index vs actual) (s03). After a mid-year standard revision it is split into planning and operational components (s06).

What belongs in actual price: freight inward, customs duty, non-creditable cess and insurance are part of landed cost under Ind AS 2.11. Trade discounts and rebates reduce it. Exchange differences on the creditor after initial recognition do not belong in it (Ind AS 21).

Illustration

Soap-noodles plant, monthly consumption (standard palm oil ₹90,000/t, other inputs ₹30,000/t):

Material AQ used (t) SP (₹/t) AP (₹/t) MPV (₹ lakh)
Palm oil 820 90,000 95,000 (41.0)
Other 440 30,000 29,000 4.4
Total (36.6)

On net revenue of ₹15 crore, that is \(-36.6 / 1{,}500 \times 10{,}000 = -244\) bps of gross margin. If the same month's receipts were 1,000 t of palm oil (not 820 t), the purchase-basis PPV on palm oil would be \(-5{,}000 \times 1{,}000 = -₹50\) lakh. ₹9 lakh of that relates to stock still on hand.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic MPV [L1]

A paint company's standard price for titanium dioxide is ₹285/kg. In August it purchased 62,000 kg at ₹297/kg and consumed 55,000 kg. Compute the MPV on purchase basis and on usage basis, and state where the difference sits at month-end.

Q2. Landed cost elements [L2]

An importer buys 300 t of crude palm oil at USD 1,020/t CIF. The bill-of-entry exchange rate is ₹84.10/USD, basic customs duty and cess total ₹7,600/t, port handling and inland freight ₹1,150/t, and IGST is paid and fully creditable. The standard price is ₹92,000/t. Compute the actual landed price per tonne and the MPV. Then explain how an exchange-rate movement to ₹84.60/USD by the payment date would be accounted for.

💡 Hint: Decide which costs Ind AS 2.11 includes in purchase cost and which items are recoverable or post-recognition.

Q3. Interview question [L2]

"Why is the price variance multiplied by actual quantity rather than standard quantity?" Answer in under 120 words, and include what would happen to the reconciliation if standard quantity were used.
