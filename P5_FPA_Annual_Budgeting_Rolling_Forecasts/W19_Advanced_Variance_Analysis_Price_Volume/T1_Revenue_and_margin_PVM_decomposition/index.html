Roadmap topic: Revenue Price-Volume-Mix (PVM) Decomposition: Isolating how much revenue/gross margin variance came from Selling Price changes, Total Volume growth, and Product/Channel Mix shift

Overview

Price-Volume-Mix (PVM) decomposition is the standard FP&A technique for explaining why revenue and gross margin differ from budget, prior year or forecast. It answers three commercial questions at once: did we realise more or less per unit (price), did we sell more or fewer units in total (volume), and did we sell a richer or poorer basket of products, channels and customers (mix)? For gross margin a fourth effect is added — unit cost — so that the bridge explains both the top line and the margin line. In Indian listed companies the same split surfaces externally as "volume growth" vs "price/mix" in quarterly investor presentations of FMCG, paints, auto and building-materials companies, and as "constant-currency growth" for exporters such as IT services and pharma.

For a CMA-qualified professional the mathematics is already familiar: PVM is the sales price, sales quantity and sales mix variance framework applied to a commercial portfolio, with standard prices replaced by budget net realisations. What is new in an FP&A role is (a) the design choices — level of analysis (SKU, channel, customer), common volume unit, new and discontinued SKU policy, FX separation, hierarchical mix order — and (b) the communication task: turning ₹ variances into a narrative a CEO can act on and into basis points of margin. A PVM that does not reconcile to the P&L, or that hides a residual, destroys trust in the whole management pack.

The learning sequence: start with revenue PVM at a single level (s01) and the three formulas (c01–c03) with the zero-residual check (c04). Extend to gross margin by revaluing volume and mix at budget unit margin and adding the cost variance (s02, c05). Then add the real-world complications: hierarchical channel and product mix (s03), new and discontinued SKUs (s04) and FX for exports (s05, c06). Finish with interpretation — what premiumisation and channel migration to modern trade, e-commerce and quick commerce mean for the margin story (s06). Topics 2 and 4 of this week build on this directly: the cost bar is decomposed into material and labour variances in Topic 2, and all the bars become steps in the EBITDA waterfall in Topic 4.

The single principle running through the topic: price and cost at actual quantity, volume and mix at budget unit economics. Every formula in this topic is a consequence of that rule, and every reconciliation failure is a breach of it.

Related concepts to study

CMA sales variances and material mix/yield analogy

CMA sales variances (sales price, sales quantity, sales mix) and material mix/yield analogy

The CMA syllabus defines sales value variance = sales price variance (actual quantity × (actual − standard price)) + sales volume variance, the latter split into sales mix variance (actual total quantity at actual vs standard mix, at standard price) and sales quantity variance (actual vs budget total quantity at standard average price). These map one-for-one to PVM price, mix and volume. The sales margin variants value mix and quantity at standard margin, which is exactly GM PVM. The material mix and yield variances apply the same algebra to inputs: mix = change in input proportions at standard price, yield = change in total input relative to output at standard average cost. Recognising the analogy lets you reuse your exam intuition — including the "no residual" property — in commercial bridges and in Topic 2.

Week 11 DAX PVM measures with SUMX

→ Week 11: DAX PVM measures with SUMX over SKU tables

In Power BI, PVM is implemented as measures that iterate over the SKU (or SKU × channel) dimension. Price is SUMX(VALUES(Product[SKU]), ([ActPrice]-[BudPrice])*[ActQty]), where the inner measures compute weighted average prices per SKU in the filter context. Volume uses ([ActQty]-[BudQty])*[BudAvgPrice] at the total level, and mix uses SUMX over SKUs of share changes times budget price times total actual quantity, with CALCULATE(..., ALLSELECTED(Product)) to get totals for shares. The key design point from this topic carries over: the iteration level in SUMX is the PVM "item", so iterating over SKU vs SKU × channel changes the split between price and mix. A residual measure on a card visual is the control.

Like-for-like vs new-product contribution analysis

Like-for-like vs new-product contribution analysis

Like-for-like (LFL) analysis compares only items present in both periods — the same SKUs, the same stores (retail "same-store sales growth" or SSSG), the same customers — so that growth from additions and losses from exits are shown separately. Indian retailers report SSSG; FMCG companies discuss innovation contribution (share of revenue from launches in the last few years). PVM Method A in s04 is LFL analysis: run PVM on continuing SKUs and add new and discontinued bars. The judgement lies in definitions — how long a SKU stays "new", whether a relaunch is mapped to its predecessor — and in cannibalisation, which LFL arithmetic does not measure. Consistent definitions across periods matter more than the specific choice.

Phase 2 segment margin analysis and peer comparisons

→ Phase 2: segment margin analysis and peer comparisons

Phase 2 analyses published financials: Ind AS 108 operating segments, segment revenue and results, and peer comparisons of growth and margins. External analysts perform a crude PVM from disclosed data — volume growth from management commentary, realisation per tonne or per vehicle, segment mix — to explain margin trends. Understanding internal PVM helps you read investor presentations critically: "price/mix growth of 6%" combines two effects you now know how to separate, and segment margin changes often reflect mix across segments rather than within them. Conversely, peer benchmarking supplies context for your own bridge: a price variance in line with competitors' realisation trends is market-driven, not a commercial win.

Common pitfalls

Computing volume at actual price

Computing volume at actual price, which double-counts price in volume

The mistake: valuing the change in total quantity at actual average price (or actual SKU prices). People do it because "actual" feels more current, or because they compute volume as actual revenue minus budget-quantity-at-actual-price. When price is also measured on all actual units, the price change on the incremental units is counted twice (once in each bar), and the bridge either fails to reconcile or someone forces it with a balancing figure. Correct treatment: volume at budget weighted average price (or margin); price at actual quantity. Detection: the residual check is non-zero and roughly equals \((\bar{P}^A - \bar{P}^B) \times (Q^A - Q^B)\); or the formula references actual price columns in the volume cell.

Mix that does not sum to zero, or omitted mix

Mix variance that does not sum to zero mix % change, or omitting mix entirely at aggregate level

Two related errors. First, share columns computed on inconsistent populations (continuing SKUs only for actual, all SKUs for budget, or quantities in different units) so that share changes do not sum to zero; the mix variance then contains a hidden volume effect. Second, at aggregate level some packs show only "price" (average price change × units) and "volume", silently burying mix inside price — average realisation falls when cheaper items gain share even if no price changed. Correct treatment: compute shares on the same total that multiplies them, show a check that \(\sum (m^A - m^B) = 0\), and always compute mix at the item level used for pricing. Detection: the share-change check cell, and a price bar that moves when no price list changed.

Ignoring new and discontinued SKUs

Ignoring new/discontinued SKUs, leaving an unexplained residual

When new SKUs have no budget row and discontinued SKUs no actual row, a lookup-based model often drops them, so the PVM covers only part of revenue and the difference to P&L revenue appears as a residual or "Other". Blank budget prices treated as zero produce enormous price variances. People miss it because the SKU master in the model is copied from the budget file. Correct treatment: build the item list as the union of budget and actual SKUs, apply a written policy (LFL plus new and discontinued bars, or proxy budget prices), and maintain a SKU mapping table for relaunches and code changes. Detection: tie PVM total revenue to the P&L for both budget and actual; count SKUs in each source.

Valuing GM mix at price

Valuing gross-margin mix at price instead of budget unit margin

The mistake: reusing the revenue mix variance in the GM bridge, or computing GM mix with budget prices. It happens because the revenue PVM is built first and copied. The result is a GM bridge that reports the revenue consequence of a basket shift, overstating or even reversing the profit effect — in the core example revenue mix is −₹6.75 lakh but GM mix is only −₹3.15 lakh, and in channel shifts GM mix is often far worse than revenue mix. The GM bridge then does not reconcile unless volume is also misvalued. Correct treatment: GM volume at budget average unit margin, GM mix at budget unit margin per item. Detection: the GM mix equals the revenue mix (a red flag in itself) or the GM bridge has a residual.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full revenue and GM PVM for a home-care portfolio [L3]

A home-care company (FY ending 31 March) has the following Q2 data (volume in tonnes, prices and costs in ₹ per kg):

SKU Bud t Bud price Bud cost Act t Act price Act cost
Detergent bar 6,000 60 44 5,500 62 47
Detergent powder 9,000 95 64 9,800 96 68
Liquid detergent 1,500 210 120 2,400 205 118
Dishwash gel 500 240 130 300 250 131

(a) Compute the revenue PVM and the GM PVM (price, cost, volume, mix) in ₹ crore and prove zero residual for both. (b) Compute budget and actual GM%. (c) Write a five-sentence CFO commentary explaining the GM% movement. (d) Identify which SKU contributes most to mix on revenue and on GM, and explain any difference in ranking.

Q2. Channel hierarchy and a new channel [L3]

A snacks company sells one premium SKU (P) and one regular SKU (R) through GT and MT, and in the year launched on quick commerce (QC), which was not in the budget. Budget (lakh packs, ₹/pack): GT-P 30 at ₹18, GT-R 120 at ₹9, MT-P 15 at ₹16.5, MT-R 35 at ₹8.4. Actual: GT-P 28 at ₹18.4, GT-R 110 at ₹9.1, MT-P 18 at ₹16.2, MT-R 34 at ₹8.3, QC-P 12 at ₹15.8, QC-R 8 at ₹7.9. (a) Prepare a Method A bridge treating QC as a new-channel bar, with LFL price, volume, channel mix and product-within-channel mix (channel first) for GT and MT. (b) Recompute treating QC as part of the portfolio with proxy budget prices equal to actual QC prices. (c) Recommend which presentation the sales director and the CFO should each see, with reasons.

💡 Hint: For (b) you need budget channel shares including QC at zero, and a QC budget average price based on the proxy prices.

Q3. Exporter with FX and a discontinued product [L2]

A specialty chemicals exporter budgets Product K at 800 t × USD 4,000/t and Product L at 200 t × USD 6,500/t at a budget rate of ₹83.00/USD. Product L was withdrawn in June after a customer audit; actual sales were K 950 t at USD 3,900/t and L 40 t at USD 6,500/t, at an average rate of ₹85.20/USD. (a) Prepare a bridge in ₹ crore: budget revenue → volume → mix → price (all at budget rate) → FX → actual revenue. (b) Compute reported and constant-currency growth vs budget. (c) Explain whether L should be treated as LFL or discontinued for the full-year bridge and how the answer changes the bars.

Q4. Model review case [L3]

You review a PVM workbook prepared by a junior analyst for the monthly board pack. You observe: (i) volume is computed as (SUM(ActQty)-SUM(BudQty))*ActAvgPrice; (ii) GM mix uses budget prices; (iii) the SKU list is copied from the budget file; (iv) an "Other/rounding" bar equals 4% of the total GM variance; (v) export revenue is stored only in ₹. For each observation, explain the consequence for the bars, the expected sign or size of the error, the correction, and the control you would add to prevent recurrence. Conclude with a short note to the analyst prioritising the fixes.

Q5. Interview case: higher sales, lower margin [L2]

An interviewer gives you: budget revenue ₹500 crore at GM 38%; actual revenue ₹540 crore at GM 35.5%. Volume grew 10% in tonnes, average realisation per kg fell 1.8%, and management says "input costs were in line with budget". (a) Explain, using PVM logic, which combinations of price, mix and cost effects are consistent with these facts. (b) List the three data cuts you would request first. (c) Describe the one-page bridge you would present, including bps labels.
