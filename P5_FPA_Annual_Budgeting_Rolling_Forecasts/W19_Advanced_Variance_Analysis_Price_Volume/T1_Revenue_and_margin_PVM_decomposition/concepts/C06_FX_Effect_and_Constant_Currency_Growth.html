Key concept

FX Effect and Constant-Currency Growth

Concept: FX effect = Actual FC revenue × (Actual rate − Budget rate); constant-currency growth excludes it

Statement

\[ \text{FX effect} = \text{FC}^A \times \left(r^A - r^B\right) \]

\[ \text{CC growth} = \frac{\text{FC}^A \times r^B - R^{\text{comp}}}{R^{\text{comp}}}, \qquad \text{Reported growth} = \frac{\text{FC}^A \times r^A - R^{\text{comp}}}{R^{\text{comp}}} \]

where \(\text{FC}^A\) is actual revenue in foreign currency, \(r^A\) the actual (average or transaction) exchange rate in ₹ per FC unit, \(r^B\) the budget rate (or prior-period rate for year-on-year analysis), and \(R^{\text{comp}}\) the comparison-period revenue in ₹ (budget or PY). For multiple currencies, sum the FX effect currency by currency.

Explanation

The rupee value of export revenue is \(\text{FC} \times r\). Its change against budget can be split into a commercial effect (change in FC revenue at the budget rate — price, volume and mix measured in FC) and a translation effect (actual FC revenue re-priced from budget rate to actual rate). Valuing FX on actual FC revenue is the analogue of valuing price at actual quantity: it leaves no joint term, so the bridge reconciles exactly.

Sign. For an exporter, \(r^A > r^B\) (rupee weaker than budget) gives a positive FX effect on revenue; for an importer's costs the same movement is adverse. Present gross (revenue and cost) and net FX at GM/EBITDA level.

Constant currency removes FX so management sees organic performance. Indian IT services companies report CC growth quarterly; FP&A packs for any exporter should do the same. Conventions to fix and disclose: which comparison rate (budget, PY average, or a fixed "planning rate"), and whether rates are monthly averages or a single annual rate.

Hedges. If cash-flow hedge accounting under Ind AS 109 reclassifies hedge gains/losses into revenue, reported revenue is at an effective hedged rate; the FX bar should then show spot effect and hedge effect separately or explicitly use the realised rate. Without hedge accounting, forward gains/losses sit outside revenue, typically in other income or finance costs; decide whether the EBITDA bridge FX bar includes them.

Ind AS 21. Transactions are recorded at the spot rate on the transaction date (an average rate is permitted if rates do not fluctuate significantly); subsequent exchange differences on receivables are recognised in P&L but outside revenue. The FX effect in PVM is an analytical construct on revenue translation, not the Ind AS 21 exchange difference account.

Illustration

Budget USD 1.8 mn at ₹83.00 = ₹14.94 crore. Actual USD 2.0 mn at ₹84.50 = ₹16.90 crore.

Component Calculation ₹ crore
Commercial (CC) variance \(2.0 \times 83.00 - 14.94\) +1.66
FX effect \(2.0 \times (84.50 - 83.00)\) +0.30
Total \(16.90 - 14.94\) +1.96

Reported growth 13.1%; CC growth 11.1%.

Excel: =ActFC*(ActRate-BudRate) per currency; =SUMPRODUCT(ActFC,ActRate-BudRate) across a currency table.

Practice Questions

Q1. FX bar and CC growth [L1]

PY export revenue: EUR 12 mn at ₹89.40. Current year: EUR 13.5 mn at ₹91.20. Compute reported growth, CC growth at PY rate and the FX effect in ₹ crore.

Q2. Two-currency bridge [L2]

Budget: USD 50 mn at ₹83.00 and GBP 8 mn at ₹105.00. Actual: USD 47 mn at ₹85.30 and GBP 9.2 mn at ₹108.40. Prepare a bridge from budget ₹ revenue to actual ₹ revenue with a CC commercial bar per currency and an FX bar per currency.

Q3. Rupee-appreciation commentary [L2]

An exporter's FX bar is −₹4.2 crore because the rupee appreciated against budget, and CC growth is +9% vs reported growth of +6%. Draft three sentences for the board explaining the result and one question the board should ask treasury.

💡 Hint: Link the answer to the hedge ratio and the forward rates locked in at the start of the year.
