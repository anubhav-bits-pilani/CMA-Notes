Key concept

Gross Margin Cost Variance at Actual Quantity

Concept: GM cost variance = Σ (Budget unit cost_i − Actual unit cost_i) × Actual qty_i

Statement

\[ \text{Cost variance} = \sum_i \left(C_i^B - C_i^A\right) \times Q_i^A \]

where \(C_i^B\) is budget (standard) COGS per unit of item \(i\), \(C_i^A\) actual COGS per unit and \(Q_i^A\) actual quantity sold. The order "budget minus actual" makes a cost saving positive (favourable) and an overrun negative (adverse), consistent with the price variance sign.

Explanation

In GM PVM the cost bar is the mirror of the price bar: both are valued at actual quantity so that the GM bridge closes without an interaction term, while volume and mix are valued at budget unit margin. The full GM identity:

\[ GM^A - GM^B = \text{Volume}_{\bar{M}^B} + \text{Mix}_{M^B} + \text{Price} + \text{Cost} \]

What sits in \(C_i^A\) must be agreed:

  • Absorption basis (full production cost per unit incl. fixed overhead absorbed): volume shortfalls raise \(C^A\) via under-absorption, so the cost bar contains a production-volume effect. Separate it as a "fixed overhead absorption/volume" sub-bar or move to contribution basis.
  • Standard-cost ERPs: if COGS is posted at standard with price differences (PPV, usage, production variances) posted to separate accounts, \(C^A\) must include the variances that relate to goods sold, otherwise the cost bar only reflects standard changes. Variances relating to closing inventory should be excluded (Topic 2, s05; Ind AS 2 treatment).
  • Sold vs produced: unit cost applies to units sold; if production and sales quantities differ, cost of production variances flow through inventory with a lag.

The cost bar is the summary of all material price, usage, mix, yield, labour rate, efficiency, idle time and overhead variances on units sold. Topic 2 breaks it down and converts each piece into margin bps (\(\text{bps} = \text{variance} / \text{actual net revenue} \times 10,000\)).

Sign discipline. Writing the formula as budget − actual is a presentation convention (Topic 5): every bar on a profit bridge should be positive when it helps profit. Writing actual − budget for costs and then remembering to flip in the chart is how cost overruns end up shown as green bars.

Illustration

Core example (units '000, ₹):

SKU \(C^B\) \(C^A\) \(Q^A\) Cost variance ₹ '000
X 30.0 31.0 90 −90
Y 14.0 14.5 360 −180
Total −270

GM bridge: volume +475, mix −315, price −180, cost −270 = −290 = actual GM 3,510 − budget GM 3,800. In bps of actual revenue, the cost bar is \(-270/11,520 \times 10,000 = -234\) bps of GM%.

Excel: =SUMPRODUCT(BudCost-ActCost,ActQty).

Connections

The fourth bar of GM PVM (s02) alongside c01–c03; the check in c04 is extended to include it. It becomes the "COGS inflation/efficiency" step in the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4) and is decomposed into material and labour variances in Topic 2 (c01–c04) and converted to bps in Topic 2 (c05–c06). Links to the refresh of contribution vs absorption profit reconciliation: absorption-basis cost bars contain fixed overhead volume effects.

Practice Questions

Q1. Cost bar by SKU [L1]

Budget unit costs: SKU P ₹420, SKU Q ₹260, SKU R ₹150. Actual unit costs: P ₹445, Q ₹255, R ₹162. Actual quantities sold: P 22,000, Q 41,000, R 63,000. Compute the cost variance by SKU and in total, and express the total in bps of actual net revenue of ₹3.10 crore.

Q2. Absorption leakage in the cost bar [L2]

Budget: 50,000 units, variable cost ₹600/unit, fixed production overhead ₹1.5 crore absorbed on budget volume. Actual: 40,000 units produced and sold, variable cost ₹610/unit, fixed overhead ₹1.52 crore. Compute the absorption-basis cost bar and split it into variable cost spending, fixed overhead spending and fixed overhead volume (under-absorption) components.

💡 Hint: Budget absorbed cost per unit is ₹300 of fixed overhead plus ₹600 variable.

Q3. Standard cost ERP extract [L2]

Your SAP extract shows COGS at standard of ₹48.0 crore for units sold (budget standard costs were unchanged during the year), plus price difference accounts of ₹2.6 crore adverse, of which ₹0.7 crore relates to materials still in closing inventory. Explain what figure you would use as actual COGS in the GM PVM cost bar, and why.
