Key concept

Mix Variance at Budget Item Price or Margin

Concept: Mix variance = Σ (Actual mix %_i − Budget mix %_i) × Actual total qty × Budget price_i (or Budget margin_i for GM)

Statement

\[ \text{Mix variance} = \sum_i \left(m_i^A - m_i^B\right) \times Q^A \times V_i^B, \qquad m_i = \frac{Q_i}{\sum_j Q_j} \]

where \(m_i^A\) and \(m_i^B\) are actual and budget shares of item \(i\) in total quantity, \(Q^A\) is actual total quantity, and \(V_i^B\) is budget price \(P_i^B\) (revenue PVM) or budget unit margin \(M_i^B\) (GM PVM). Because \(\sum_i (m_i^A - m_i^B) = 0\), an equivalent form is

\[ \text{Mix variance} = Q^A \sum_i \left(m_i^A - m_i^B\right)\left(V_i^B - \bar{V}^B\right) \]

with \(\bar{V}^B\) the budget weighted average value.

Explanation

Mix measures what the composition of actual volume did, at budget unit economics. It equals the difference between two hypothetical figures: actual quantities valued at budget item values, \(\sum_i Q_i^A V_i^B\), and actual total quantity at the budget average, \(Q^A \bar{V}^B\).

Key properties:

  • Shares must sum to 100% in both periods, so share changes sum to zero. If your mix % columns do not sum to zero change, the mix variance is wrong (a pitfall of the topic) — typically because new/discontinued items were dropped or shares were computed on different totals.
  • Favourable mix = shift towards above-average items. The deviation form shows that only an item's value relative to the average matters (s06).
  • GM mix must use budget margin, not price, otherwise the GM bridge reports the revenue consequence of the shift (topic pitfall). Revenue mix and GM mix can differ in sign.
  • Level of aggregation drives the size of mix. At total-company level with a single "item", mix is zero by construction; omitting the item breakdown at aggregate level silently pushes mix into price (average price changes). Hierarchical mix (s03) splits mix across levels.

CMA link. Identical to the sales mix variance: (actual quantity at actual mix − actual quantity at budget mix) × standard price/margin; and to the material mix variance in standard costing, where inputs replace products.

Illustration

Core example: \(Q^A = 450\) ('000 units); budget shares X 25%, Y 75%; actual X 20%, Y 80%.

SKU \(m^A - m^B\) \(P^B\) Revenue mix \(M^B\) GM mix
X −5% 50 \(-0.05 \times 450 \times 50 = -1,125\) 20 \(-450\)
Y +5% 20 \(+0.05 \times 450 \times 20 = +450\) 6 \(+135\)
Total 0% −675 −315

Deviation check (revenue): \(450 \times [(-0.05)(50 - 27.5) + (0.05)(20 - 27.5)] = 450 \times (-1.125 - 0.375) = -675\).

Connections

The middle step of the PVM chain between volume (c02) and price (c01); together they reconcile (c04). Interpreted in s06, split hierarchically in s03, and affected by the new/discontinued SKU policy in s04. Material mix and yield variances in Topic 2 use the same structure on inputs. In the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4) the mix bar uses budget unit margin. In DAX, mix is a SUMX over the SKU table using DIVIDE for shares (Week 11).

Practice Questions

Q1. Mix on revenue and GM [L1]

Budget: A 30,000 units at ₹600 (margin ₹240), B 50,000 at ₹350 (margin ₹105), C 20,000 at ₹200 (margin ₹90). Actual: A 27,000, B 58,000, C 25,000. Compute revenue mix and GM mix using both the standard and the deviation-from-average forms.

Q2. Broken mix columns [L2]

A model computes actual mix shares on actual volume of continuing SKUs only (₹ figures and quantities for three continuing SKUs and one new SKU of 4,000 units are given as: continuing actual 36,000 units; total actual 40,000 units), but multiplies by total actual quantity of 40,000. Explain the error, show how the mix share changes would fail to sum to zero, and state the fix.

💡 Hint: Shares and the quantity multiplier must be based on the same population.

Q3. Aggregate-level omission [L2]

A CFO's pack shows only total revenue = average price × total units, with a "price" bar computed as (actual average price − budget average price) × actual units. Using the core example of this week (X and Y), compute this "price" bar and show that it equals the true price variance plus the mix variance.
