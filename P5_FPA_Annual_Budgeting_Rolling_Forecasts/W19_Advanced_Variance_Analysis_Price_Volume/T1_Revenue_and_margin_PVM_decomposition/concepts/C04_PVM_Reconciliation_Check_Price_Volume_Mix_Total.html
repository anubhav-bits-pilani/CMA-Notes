Key concept

PVM Reconciliation Check: Price + Volume + Mix = Total Variance

Concept: Check: Price + Volume + Mix = Actual revenue − Budget revenue (no residual when price uses actual qty)

Statement

\[ \underbrace{\sum_i (P_i^A - P_i^B) Q_i^A}_{\text{Price}} + \underbrace{(Q^A - Q^B)\bar{P}^B}_{\text{Volume}} + \underbrace{\sum_i (m_i^A - m_i^B) Q^A P_i^B}_{\text{Mix}} = \sum_i P_i^A Q_i^A - \sum_i P_i^B Q_i^B \]

with symbols as in c01–c03. The identity is exact — residual zero — whenever price is valued at actual quantity and volume and mix are valued at budget prices.

Explanation

Proof by telescoping. Define three intermediate revenue figures:

  • \(R_1 = Q^A \bar{P}^B\) (actual total quantity, budget mix, budget prices)
  • \(R_2 = \sum_i Q_i^A P_i^B\) (actual quantities, budget prices)
  • \(R_3 = \sum_i Q_i^A P_i^A\) = actual revenue

Volume \(= R_1 - R^B\) because \(R^B = Q^B \bar{P}^B\). Mix \(= R_2 - R_1\) because \(m_i^A Q^A = Q_i^A\) and \(\sum_i m_i^B Q^A P_i^B = Q^A \bar{P}^B\). Price \(= R_3 - R_2\). Summing, all intermediate terms cancel: total \(= R_3 - R^B\).

Why a residual appears in practice:

  1. Price at budget quantity → the price × volume interaction term is unallocated.
  2. Volume at actual price → price change on the incremental units is counted twice.
  3. Mix shares computed on a different population from \(Q^A\) (e.g., excluding new SKUs).
  4. New SKUs with blank budget price (treated as zero → price variance equals full revenue, while mix also counts it).
  5. Rounding in a pasted ERP extract; data at different granularity (budget at SKU-month, actual at invoice line) aggregated inconsistently.
  6. Revenue in the PVM table not reconciling to the P&L revenue line (excluded other operating revenue, export incentives, scrap sales, or a different net-of-discount basis).

Control principle. The residual cell is a test, not a balancing figure. A model should show it explicitly, highlight any non-zero value and never feed it into a chart as "Other". The same principle extends to GM PVM (add the cost bar, c05) and to the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4, c01). A second-level check is that PVM total revenue ties to the reported P&L revenue for both budget and actual.

Illustration

Core example (₹ '000): \(R^B = 11,000\); \(R_1 = 450 \times 27.50 = 12,375\); \(R_2 = 90 \times 50 + 360 \times 20 = 11,700\); \(R_3 = 11,520\).

Step From → To Variance
Volume 11,000 → 12,375 +1,375
Mix 12,375 → 11,700 −675
Price 11,700 → 11,520 −180
Total 11,000 → 11,520 +520
Residual 0

If price were computed at budget quantity (−100), the sum would be +600, leaving a residual of −80 — exactly the interaction term \(\sum (P^A - P^B)(Q^A - Q^B)\).

Excel check cell:

=ROUND((SUMPRODUCT(ActQty,ActPrice)-SUMPRODUCT(BudQty,BudPrice))-(PriceVar+VolVar+MixVar),2)

with conditional formatting red when <>0, and a second check =SUMPRODUCT(ActQty,ActPrice)-PL_Revenue_Actual.

Practice Questions

Q1. Prove the reconciliation [L1]

Budget: SKU M 6,000 units at ₹1,500, SKU N 14,000 units at ₹700. Actual: M 5,400 units at ₹1,560, N 17,600 units at ₹690. Compute the four revenue figures \(R^B, R_1, R_2, R_3\), derive volume, mix and price, and show the residual is zero.

Q2. Diagnose the residual [L2]

A bridge for a different product line — budget SKU E 10,000 units at ₹300 and SKU F 10,000 units at ₹100; actual E 9,000 units at ₹320 and F 13,000 units at ₹95 — shows price +₹1.50 lakh, volume +₹4.00 lakh, mix −₹4.00 lakh and a residual of −₹0.35 lakh. Identify which of the six causes listed in the notes explains the residual, by recomputing each bar under alternative conventions, and state the corrected bars.

💡 Hint: Recompute price at budget quantity and at actual quantity and compare both with the reported price bar.
