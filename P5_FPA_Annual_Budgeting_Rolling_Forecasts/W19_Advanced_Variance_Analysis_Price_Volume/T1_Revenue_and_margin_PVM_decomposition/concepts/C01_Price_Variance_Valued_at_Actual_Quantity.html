Key concept

Price Variance Valued at Actual Quantity

Concept: Price variance = Σ (Actual price_i − Budget price_i) × Actual qty_i

Statement

\[ \text{Price variance} = \sum_i \left(P_i^A - P_i^B\right) \times Q_i^A \]

where \(i\) indexes the items in the analysis (SKU, SKU × channel or customer), \(P_i^A\) is actual net price per unit, \(P_i^B\) budget net price per unit, and \(Q_i^A\) actual quantity sold. A positive value is favourable for revenue and gross margin.

Explanation

The price variance answers: on what we actually sold, how much more (or less) did we realise per unit than planned? Valuing at actual quantity is a deliberate choice with two consequences.

  1. No joint term. Any total variance \(P^A Q^A - P^B Q^B\) can be written as \((P^A - P^B)Q^B + (Q^A - Q^B)P^B + (P^A - P^B)(Q^A - Q^B)\). The last term — the price × volume interaction — has to go somewhere. Valuing price at actual quantity absorbs the interaction into price, leaving volume clean at budget price. This is the CMA sales price variance convention (actual quantity × (actual price − standard price)).
  2. Management meaning. The commercial team is accountable for realisation on the volume they actually sold; measuring price on budget volume would assess them on units that never existed.

"Net" price matters. \(P\) should be net realisation per unit after trade discounts, schemes, rebates and other variable consideration (Ind AS 115 transaction price), and excluding GST. Using list price or MRP shifts all trade-term changes out of price and into an unexplained difference with the revenue line in the P&L.

Level matters. Computing \(P_i\) at SKU level when the SKU sells in several channels at different realisations means a channel shift changes the average SKU price — the price variance then contains mix. Define \(i\) at the level at which prices are set (s01, s03).

Undefined cases. For new SKUs there is no \(P_i^B\); for discontinued SKUs \(Q_i^A = 0\) so the term vanishes. Both need a policy (s04). For export revenue, compute \(P\) in foreign currency and translate at the budget rate so that FX is not inside price (s05).

Illustration

Core example (units '000, ₹):

SKU \(P^B\) \(P^A\) \(Q^A\) Price variance ₹ '000
X 50 52 90 +180
Y 20 19 360 −360
Total −180

Had the price variance been valued at budget quantity instead (X 100, Y 300), it would be \(+200 - 300 = -100\), and the remaining −80 would sit as an interaction term that someone must allocate — usually into an "Other" bar that executives distrust.

Excel: =SUMPRODUCT(ActPrice-BudPrice,ActQty).

Connections

Feeds the price bar in both revenue PVM (s01) and GM PVM (s02) — it is identical in both because price changes flow one-for-one to margin. Paired with c02 (volume at budget average) and c03 (mix at budget item prices), it guarantees the zero-residual check in c04. Same valuation logic as the GM cost variance (c05) and the material price variance in Topic 2. In the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4) the price step is valued at actual volume. In CMA terms it is the sales price variance; in Power BI it is a SUMX over the SKU table (Week 11).

Practice Questions

Q1. Price variance with channel detail [L1]

SKU Z sells in GT at budget ₹420/case and actual ₹432/case on 18,000 actual cases, and in MT at budget ₹395/case and actual ₹388/case on 7,500 actual cases. Compute the price variance by channel and in total.

Q2. Actual vs budget quantity valuation [L2]

Budget: 12,000 units at ₹850; actual: 14,500 units at ₹880. Compute the price variance at actual quantity and at budget quantity, compute the interaction term, and explain which convention leaves the volume variance "clean".

Q3. List vs net price [L2]

A company raised list price by 5% on a SKU budgeted at ₹200 net (₹250 list less 20% trade discount) but simultaneously raised trade discount to 23% to support MT listings. Actual volume was 1,00,000 units. Compute the price variance on a net-price basis and explain what a list-price-based analysis would wrongly show.

💡 Hint: Compute actual net price from the new list price and new discount rate.
