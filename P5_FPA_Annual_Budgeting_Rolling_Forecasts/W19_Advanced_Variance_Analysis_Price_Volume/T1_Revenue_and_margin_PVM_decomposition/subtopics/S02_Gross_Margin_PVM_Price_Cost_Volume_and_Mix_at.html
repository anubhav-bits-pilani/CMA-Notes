Gross Margin PVM: Price, Cost, Volume and Mix at Budget Unit Margin

Scope: Gross margin PVM: price, cost, volume and mix at budget unit margin

Why it matters

Revenue PVM tells you where the top line came from; gross margin (GM) PVM tells you whether that top line made money. The two can point in opposite directions: in the core example of this week revenue beat budget by ₹5.2 lakh while gross margin missed by ₹2.9 lakh. That gap is exactly the question a CFO asks — "why did GM% fall despite higher sales?" — and it is the question set for this week's Saturday case test. GM PVM is also the backbone of the EBITDA bridge (Topic 4): the price, volume, mix and COGS bars on that bridge are the GM PVM components.

For a CMA, GM PVM is the commercial version of the sales margin variances (sales margin price, sales margin volume, sales margin mix) combined with the cost variances that explain the movement in actual unit cost against standard.

Core notes

Four effects instead of three

Let \(C_i\) be unit cost (COGS per unit) and \(M_i = P_i - C_i\) unit gross margin. Gross margin \(GM = \sum_i M_i Q_i\).

Effect Formula Valued at
Price \(\sum_i (P_i^A - P_i^B) \times Q_i^A\) Actual quantity (identical to revenue price variance)
Cost \(\sum_i (C_i^B - C_i^A) \times Q_i^A\) Actual quantity; positive when actual cost is lower
Volume \((Q^A - Q^B) \times \bar{M}^B\), where \(\bar{M}^B = GM^B / Q^B\) Budget weighted average unit margin
Mix \(\sum_i (m_i^A - m_i^B) \times Q^A \times M_i^B\) Budget unit margin of each item

The reconciliation chain now has five GM figures:

\[ GM^B \xrightarrow{\text{Vol}} Q^A \bar{M}^B \xrightarrow{\text{Mix}} \sum_i Q_i^A M_i^B \xrightarrow{\text{Price}} \sum_i Q_i^A (P_i^A - C_i^B) \xrightarrow{\text{Cost}} \sum_i Q_i^A (P_i^A - C_i^A) = GM^A \]

Price and cost both being valued at actual quantity is what guarantees zero residual: no joint "price × volume" term is left over.

Why mix and volume use budget unit margin, not price

The revenue mix variance asks "what did the basket shift do to revenue?". The GM mix variance asks "what did the basket shift do to profit?". Those answers differ whenever the item with the higher price does not also have the higher margin, or when margins differ more than prices. Valuing GM mix at budget price would report the revenue effect of the shift on a profit bridge — a pitfall discussed in the topic overview. Valuing at budget margin isolates the profit consequence of the shift, holding price and cost at budget so that those effects are captured only once, in their own bars.

Worked illustration (core example)

Units '000, ₹ '000.

SKU Bud qty Bud price Bud cost Bud margin Act qty Act price Act cost Act margin
X 100 50 30 20 90 52 31 21
Y 300 20 14 6 360 19 14.5 4.5

Budget GM = \(100\times 20 + 300 \times 6 = 3,800\) on revenue 11,000 (GM% 34.55%). Actual GM = \(90 \times 21 + 360 \times 4.5 = 3,510\) on revenue 11,520 (GM% 30.47%). GM variance = −290.

  • Volume: \(\bar{M}^B = 3,800/400 = 9.50\); \((450-400)\times 9.50 = \) +475.
  • Mix: X \((0.20-0.25)\times 450 \times 20 = -450\); Y \((0.80-0.75) \times 450 \times 6 = +135\); total −315.
  • Price: as in revenue PVM, −180.
  • Cost: X \((30-31)\times 90 = -90\); Y \((14-14.5)\times 360 = -180\); total −270.
  • Check: \(475 - 315 - 180 - 270 = -290\). Zero residual.

Compare with revenue PVM: revenue mix was −675 but GM mix is only −315, because the premium SKU's margin advantage (₹20 vs ₹6, ratio 3.3×) is smaller in absolute terms than its price advantage (₹50 vs ₹20). Volume was +1,375 on revenue but only +475 on GM — each extra unit at budget mix earns ₹9.50 of margin, not ₹27.50 of revenue.

The executive narrative

"Gross margin was ₹2.9 lakh below budget although revenue was ₹5.2 lakh ahead. Volume added ₹4.8 lakh, but almost all the extra units were the mass SKU, so adverse mix cost ₹3.2 lakh. We also cut the mass-pack price by ₹1 (−₹3.6 lakh) only partly offset by a premium price increase (+₹1.8 lakh), and unit costs rose on both SKUs (−₹2.7 lakh), mainly input inflation." Each sentence maps to a bar.

What "cost" includes

The cost bar captures everything that changed actual unit COGS against budget unit COGS: material price, usage and yield, conversion cost (labour rate and efficiency, power, fixed overhead absorption), freight-in and packaging. Topic 2 decomposes this bar further and converts it to basis points. Two definitional decisions must be made and documented:

  1. Absorption vs marginal cost. If unit cost includes absorbed fixed production overhead, a volume shortfall raises actual unit cost through under-absorption, so part of what is economically a volume effect appears in the cost bar. Contribution-margin PVM (variable cost only) avoids this, with fixed costs shown as a separate bar. Many FMCG management packs use contribution-level PVM for this reason.
  2. Standard vs actual cost in "actual". If the ERP books COGS at standard and variances separately (SAP standard price control), "actual unit cost" must include the capitalised and expensed variances, otherwise the cost bar only shows standard-cost revisions.

Excel implementation

Budget avg margin  = SUMPRODUCT(BudQty,BudPrice-BudCost)/SUM(BudQty)
Volume (GM)        = (SUM(ActQty)-SUM(BudQty))*BudAvgMargin
Mix (GM)           = SUMPRODUCT(ActQty/SUM(ActQty)-BudQty/SUM(BudQty),BudPrice-BudCost)*SUM(ActQty)
Price              = SUMPRODUCT(ActPrice-BudPrice,ActQty)
Cost               = SUMPRODUCT(BudCost-ActCost,ActQty)
Residual           = (SUMPRODUCT(ActQty,ActPrice-ActCost)-SUMPRODUCT(BudQty,BudPrice-BudCost))-(Volume+Mix+Price+Cost)

Lay the revenue and GM PVM side by side on one sheet with shared inputs, so that the price bar is visibly identical in both.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Negative-margin items. A loss-making SKU (\(M_i^B < 0\)) gets a favourable mix effect when its share falls. This is correct and often the point of a rationalisation programme; label it clearly.
  • Price changes that are really cost recoveries. If a price increase was taken to pass through commodity inflation, price and cost bars partially offset. Show them separately (do not net them) but comment on pass-through (Topic 2, s03).
  • Trade spend below or above GM. If trade promotions are deducted from revenue (Ind AS 115 variable consideration), they sit in price. If some promotional costs are booked as A&P expense below GM, the GM bridge looks better than economic reality; check classification consistency between budget and actual.
  • Freight-out. Secondary freight is usually opex, primary freight may be in COGS; a shift in policy between years breaks comparability.

Key takeaways

  • GM PVM has four bars: price and cost at actual quantity, volume and mix at budget unit margin.
  • Price is identical in revenue and GM PVM; volume and mix are revalued from budget price to budget margin.
  • Valuing GM mix at budget margin is what makes it a profit statement; at price it would just repeat the revenue story.
  • The cost bar aggregates all unit-cost changes and is the bridge to material and labour variance analysis.
  • Decide and document absorption vs marginal costing, otherwise volume effects leak into the cost bar.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-SKU GM PVM [L1]

Budget: SKU A 40,000 units at ₹500 price and ₹300 cost; SKU B 60,000 units at ₹200 price and ₹150 cost. Actual: A 50,000 units at ₹490 and ₹310; B 55,000 units at ₹210 and ₹155. Compute the price, cost, volume and mix effects on gross margin and the GM% for budget and actual.

Q2. Revenue up, GM down [L2]

Using the data in Q1, prepare both the revenue PVM and the GM PVM. Explain in no more than five sentences why the mix effect has a different sign or size in the two bridges.

💡 Hint: Compare each SKU's budget price and budget margin against the respective budget weighted averages.

Q3. Absorption leakage [L2]

A plant has fixed production overhead of ₹12 crore budgeted on 1,20,000 units (₹1,000 per unit absorbed). Actual production and sales are 1,00,000 units; variable cost per unit is exactly on budget at ₹4,000 and fixed overhead is exactly ₹12 crore. Compute the actual unit cost under absorption costing and the resulting cost bar in a GM PVM. Then explain how a contribution-level PVM would present the same facts.

Q4. Model design review [L3]

You inherit a GM bridge in which mix is computed as \(\sum (m_i^A - m_i^B) \times Q^A \times P_i^B\) and the bridge has a "Other / rounding" bar of ₹1.8 crore on a GM variance of ₹6 crore. List the methodology defects you would look for, the order in which you would test them, and the evidence that would confirm each.

Q5. Board narrative [L3]

For a paint company, GM variance against budget is −₹14 crore: volume +₹22 crore, mix −₹9 crore (shift to economy emulsions), price +₹31 crore, cost −₹58 crore (titanium dioxide and crude-linked solvents). Draft a five-line board commentary and propose two actions management could take, each linked to a specific bar.
