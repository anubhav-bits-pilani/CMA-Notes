Subtopic note

Hierarchical Mix: Channel Mix, Then Product Mix Within Channel

Scope: Hierarchical mix: channel mix then product mix within channel; order of decomposition

Why it matters

A flat SKU × channel PVM gives one mix number. That number mixes two very different management questions: did we sell through the right channels? (a go-to-market question owned by the sales director) and did we sell the right products within each channel? (a portfolio and category question owned by marketing). In Indian consumer businesses the two often move in opposite directions — volume shifts from general trade to quick commerce at a lower net realisation, while within quick commerce the basket skews to premium packs. A single mix bar of near zero can hide two large offsetting effects. Hierarchical mix splits the bar so each owner sees their own lever.

Core notes

The idea

Treat the portfolio as a tree: Total → Channel \(c\) → Product \(p\) within channel. Mix is then decomposed level by level:

  1. Channel mix — shift in the share of total volume going to each channel, valued at each channel's budget average price (which embeds the budget product mix within that channel).
  2. Product mix within channel — shift in product shares inside each channel, given the actual channel volume, valued at the budget price of each product in that channel.

Notation: \(Q_c\) channel volume, \(Q\) total volume, \(s_c = Q_c / Q\) channel share, \(m_{p \mid c} = Q_{pc}/Q_c\) product share within channel, \(\bar{P}_c^B\) budget average price of channel \(c\).

\[ \text{Channel mix} = \sum_c (s_c^A - s_c^B) \times Q^A \times \bar{P}_c^B \]

\[ \text{Product mix within channel} = \sum_c \sum_p (m_{p \mid c}^A - m_{p \mid c}^B) \times Q_c^A \times P_{pc}^B \]

Their sum equals the flat SKU × channel mix exactly, because both are steps in one chain:

\[ Q^A \bar{P}^B \xrightarrow{\text{Channel mix}} \sum_c Q_c^A \bar{P}_c^B \xrightarrow{\text{Product mix}} \sum_c \sum_p Q_{pc}^A P_{pc}^B \]

The middle term — actual channel volumes at budget channel average prices — is the new intermediate column you add to the four-column PVM layout of s01.

Worked illustration

Units '000, ₹ per unit, revenue ₹ '000. GT = general trade, QC = quick commerce; P = premium, R = regular.

Channel–product Bud qty Bud price Bud revenue Act qty Act qty × bud price
GT–P 40 100 4,000 35 3,500
GT–R 60 60 3,600 55 3,300
QC–P 20 90 1,800 40 3,600
QC–R 20 55 1,100 30 1,650
Total 140 75.00 avg 10,500 160 12,050

Budget channel averages: GT \(7,600/100 = 76.00\); QC \(2,900/40 = 72.50\). Budget channel shares GT 71.4% / QC 28.6%; actual GT 56.25% / QC 43.75%.

  • Volume: \((160-140) \times 75 = +1,500\) → intermediate \(160 \times 75 = 12,000\).
  • Flat mix: \(12,050 - 12,000 = +50\).
  • Channel mix: actual channel volumes at budget channel averages \(= 90 \times 76 + 70 \times 72.5 = 6,840 + 5,075 = 11,915\); channel mix \(= 11,915 - 12,000 = \mathbf{-85}\).
  • Product mix within channel: GT \(6,800 - 6,840 = -40\); QC \(5,250 - 5,075 = +175\); total \(\mathbf{+135}\).
  • Check: \(-85 + 135 = +50\).

Narrative: shifting volume towards quick commerce, where budget realisation is ₹3.50 lower per unit, cost ₹0.85 lakh; inside quick commerce, consumers bought proportionally more premium packs (57% vs 50% budget), adding ₹1.75 lakh; GT's product mix was slightly adverse. The flat "+50" would have suggested mix was a non-issue.

Order of decomposition

Hierarchical mix is order-dependent in what it attributes to each level:

  • Channel first, then product within channel (above) values the channel shift at budget channel average prices, which embed budget product mix within each channel. It answers "if we had kept each channel's product mix at budget, what would the channel shift have cost?".
  • Product first, then channel within product values a shift in total product shares at budget product average prices (averaged across channels), then measures channel shifts within each product. It answers "if we had kept each product's channel split at budget, what did the product mix shift do?".

The total mix is identical either way; the split differs. There is no mathematically "right" order. The convention should follow the management hierarchy — the dimension that is decided first or owned more senior goes first. For most Indian FMCG and consumer durables companies, channel strategy (distribution, trade terms) is set first and the category team manages the portfolio within channels, so channel → product is the norm. A B2B chemicals business organised by product business unit might use product → customer segment.

Once chosen, the order must be fixed and documented in the model and kept identical across months, versions (Budget, LE) and comparisons (vs PY), otherwise month-to-month bars move for methodological rather than business reasons.

Deeper hierarchies

The same logic extends to three or more levels: Region → Channel → Product. Each level adds one intermediate column. Practical limit: beyond three levels the bars become small and noisy, and executives stop reading them. Use Power BI drill-down (or a DAX measure with SUMX over the hierarchy) to let users open lower levels on demand instead of printing them all.

Gross margin version

For GM, replace budget prices by budget unit margins everywhere: channel mix at budget channel average margin, product mix at budget margin of each product in each channel. Channel mix on GM is often much larger than on revenue, because channels differ more in margin (after trade terms and channel-specific costs below price) than in price.

Excel implementation

Add a helper column ChannelBudAvg per row, computed with SUMIFS over budget revenue divided by SUMIFS over budget quantity for that row's channel, plus ChannelActQty via SUMIFS. Then:

Channel mix = SUMPRODUCT((ChActQty_unique/SUM(ActQty)-ChBudQty_unique/SUM(BudQty))*ChBudAvg_unique)*SUM(ActQty)
Product mix = SUMPRODUCT(ActQty,BudPrice) - SUMPRODUCT(ChActQty_unique,ChBudAvg_unique)

where the _unique ranges are a small channel summary table. Keep the flat-mix total alongside as a check.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Channel with no budget (e.g., a new quick-commerce partner): there is no budget channel average price; treat as a new-channel bar or use a proxy, as for new SKUs (s04).
  • Same SKU priced differently by channel only because of trade terms: the hierarchy correctly puts the realisation gap into channel mix rather than price.
  • Channel definitions changing mid-year (a distributor converted to a direct key account) require restating the budget into the new structure before computing.
  • Very small channels create large percentage share moves; consider grouping.

Key takeaways

  • Hierarchical mix splits one flat mix number into channel mix and product-mix-within-channel, with the same total.
  • Channel mix uses budget channel average prices (or margins); product mix uses budget prices within each channel.
  • The order changes the split, not the total; follow the management hierarchy and freeze the convention.
  • Add one intermediate column per hierarchy level; keep the flat mix as a check.
  • Large offsetting channel and product mixes are common in Indian consumer channels and are invisible in a flat analysis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-channel hierarchical mix [L2]

Budget: Modern trade (MT) premium 10,000 cases at ₹1,200, MT regular 30,000 at ₹800; GT premium 20,000 at ₹1,300, GT regular 90,000 at ₹850. Actual: MT premium 18,000, MT regular 32,000, GT premium 17,000, GT regular 83,000. Compute volume, flat mix, channel mix and product mix within channel (budget prices throughout) and confirm the reconciliation.

Q2. Reverse the order [L2]

Using Q1 data, recompute the mix split with product first and channel within product. Explain why the two splits differ while the total is unchanged and which order you would recommend for a company whose category managers own pricing and channel managers own trade terms.

💡 Hint: You need budget average price by product across channels, and budget channel shares within each product.

Q3. GM hierarchical mix [L3]

For the Q1 data, budget unit margins are MT premium ₹300, MT regular ₹120, GT premium ₹420, GT regular ₹200. Compute channel mix and product-within-channel mix on gross margin, and comment on whether the channel shift looks more or less damaging on margin than on revenue.

Q4. Three-level design [L3]

A two-wheeler manufacturer wants a revenue bridge by Region (North, South, West, East) → Channel (dealer, institutional, exports) → Model (8 models). Design the column structure of the Excel model, state the number of intermediate columns, the checks you would build, and how you would present the result to the MD without printing all 96 combinations.

Q5. Consistency challenge [L2]

In the Q2 LE pack the product-mix bar for the year is +₹6 crore; in the Q1 LE it was −₹4 crore, although the underlying sales plan barely changed. On investigation, Q1 used channel → product order and Q2 used product → channel. Write a short note to the FP&A head explaining the issue and the control you would introduce.
