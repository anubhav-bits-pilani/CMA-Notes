Subtopic note

Revenue PVM: Price, Volume and Mix at SKU, Channel and Customer Level

Scope: Revenue PVM: price, volume and mix effects at SKU, channel and customer level

Why it matters

"Revenue is up 4.7% against budget" is a headline, not an explanation. A CFO, a board audit committee or an equity analyst on an earnings call wants to know whether the growth came from charging more (price), selling more (volume) or selling a different basket (mix). The three have very different implications. Price-led growth in an inflationary year may be nothing more than cost pass-through. Volume-led growth signals market share gains or distribution expansion. Mix-led growth (or decline) tells you whether the portfolio is moving towards or away from the products and channels where the company earns its money. Indian FMCG, auto and pharma companies routinely split growth into "volume growth" and "price/mix" in their investor presentations, and the internal management pack behind those slides is a Price-Volume-Mix (PVM) bridge.

PVM is the commercial descendant of the CMA sales variance framework (sales price, sales volume, sales mix and sales quantity variances). The arithmetic is the same; what changes in FP&A is the level of granularity (SKU, channel, customer, region), the volume of data (thousands of SKU-customer combinations from the ERP), and the audience (people who will act on the numbers, not examiners).

Core notes

The three effects

Let \(Q_i\) be quantity, \(P_i\) price per unit, and use superscripts \(A\) (actual) and \(B\) (budget) for each item \(i\) in the analysis (SKU, SKU × channel, or customer). Let \(Q^A = \sum_i Q_i^A\), \(Q^B = \sum_i Q_i^B\), and mix shares \(m_i = Q_i / Q\).

Effect Formula Question answered
Price \(\sum_i (P_i^A - P_i^B) \times Q_i^A\) What did realisation changes on what we actually sold add?
Volume \((Q^A - Q^B) \times \bar{P}^B\) where \(\bar{P}^B = \text{Budget revenue} / Q^B\) What would the extra (or fewer) units have earned at budget mix and budget prices?
Mix \(\sum_i (m_i^A - m_i^B) \times Q^A \times P_i^B\) What did selling a different basket, at budget prices, add or take away?

The three always reconcile to the total revenue variance without a residual, because they are a telescoping chain of four revenue figures:

\[ \underbrace{\sum P^B Q^B}_{\text{Budget}} \xrightarrow{\text{Volume}} \underbrace{Q^A \bar{P}^B}_{\text{Actual qty, budget mix}} \xrightarrow{\text{Mix}} \underbrace{\sum P^B Q^A_i}_{\text{Actual qty, actual mix, budget price}} \xrightarrow{\text{Price}} \underbrace{\sum P^A Q^A_i}_{\text{Actual}} \]

Each effect is the difference between adjacent columns. This "four-column" layout is the single most useful way to build PVM in Excel because the check is automatic: the last column minus the first must equal the sum of the three differences.

Worked illustration (core example used throughout this week)

Units in '000, ₹ in '000.

SKU Bud qty Bud price Bud revenue Act qty Act price Act revenue
X (premium) 100 50 5,000 90 52 4,680
Y (mass) 300 20 6,000 360 19 6,840
Total 400 27.50 avg 11,000 450 25.60 avg 11,520

Budget mix is X 25% / Y 75%; actual mix is X 20% / Y 80%. Total variance = +520.

  • Price: X \((52-50)\times 90 = +180\); Y \((19-20)\times 360 = -360\); total −180.
  • Volume: \((450-400)\times 27.50 = \) +1,375.
  • Mix: X \((0.20-0.25)\times 450\times 50 = -1,125\); Y \((0.80-0.75)\times 450 \times 20 = +450\); total −675.
  • Check: \(-180 + 1,375 - 675 = +520\). Zero residual.

Story: we sold 12.5% more units, but the growth came almost entirely from the cheap SKU, and we cut the mass-pack price. Average realisation fell from ₹27.50 to ₹25.60 per unit.

Choosing the level: SKU, channel, customer

The "item" \(i\) is a modelling choice and it changes the size of the mix effect.

  • SKU level answers portfolio questions: are we selling more premium or more entry packs? Mix is computed across SKUs with a single price per SKU.
  • Channel level (general trade, modern trade, e-commerce, quick commerce, institutional/CSD, exports) captures the fact that the same SKU realises different net prices in different channels because of trade margins, listing fees, schemes and logistics terms. If you compute PVM by SKU only, a shift of volume from GT to quick commerce at a lower net price shows up as a price variance (average SKU price fell), which misleads the pricing team.
  • Customer level (key accounts such as a national modern trade chain, a large e-commerce marketplace or a distributor) is used in B2B and in key account management. Here price variance captures negotiated rate changes and mix captures the shift between customers with different price lists.
  • SKU × channel (or SKU × customer) is the most granular flat analysis: each combination is an item. It gives the most accurate price variance (like-for-like realisation on the same SKU in the same channel) but produces a mix number that blends channel and product effects. Separating those requires a hierarchical decomposition (s03).

Rule of thumb: define \(i\) at the level at which a price is actually set. If list price is national but net realisation differs by channel because of trade terms, the item is SKU × channel.

Units of volume

Mix and volume require a common unit. For a homogeneous portfolio, units or cases work. For mixed portfolios (a 200 ml shampoo bottle and a ₹1 sachet), "units" produce absurd mix effects. Common choices:

  • Tonnes / kilolitres (FMCG, chemicals, cement, steel) — the norm in Indian FMCG volume reporting.
  • Standard cases or equivalent units (beverages, pharma "standard units").
  • Revenue-weighted approaches are sometimes used when no physical common unit exists, but then volume is really "budget-priced revenue growth".

Whatever unit is chosen, use it consistently in budget and actual and state it on the bridge.

Excel implementation

With a flat table (one row per item; columns BudQty, BudPrice, ActQty, ActPrice):

Budget avg price  = SUMPRODUCT(BudQty,BudPrice)/SUM(BudQty)
Price variance    = SUMPRODUCT(ActPrice-BudPrice,ActQty)
Volume variance   = (SUM(ActQty)-SUM(BudQty))*BudAvgPrice
Mix variance      = SUMPRODUCT(ActQty/SUM(ActQty)-BudQty/SUM(BudQty),BudPrice)*SUM(ActQty)
Residual check    = SUMPRODUCT(ActQty,ActPrice)-SUMPRODUCT(BudQty,BudPrice)-(Price+Volume+Mix)

Keep a row-level calculation as well, so each item's price and mix contribution can be ranked and commented on. The residual check cell should be formatted to show a red flag if ABS(Residual)>0.5.

Presentation

Present revenue PVM as a short bridge (Budget revenue → Volume → Mix → Price → Actual revenue) with each bar in ₹ crore and as % of budget revenue, followed by a top-five table of items driving price and mix. State the volume unit, the level of analysis and whether figures are net of GST and trade discounts (they should be: revenue per Ind AS 115 is net of variable consideration such as discounts and rebates, and Schedule III revenue from operations excludes GST).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Order of presentation vs order of computation. The bridge can be drawn Price → Volume → Mix for readability, but the formulas above (price at actual quantity, volume and mix at budget prices) fix the values; changing the drawing order does not change the bars.
  • Zero-quantity items. A SKU with budget but no actual (discontinued) or actual but no budget (new launch) has no price variance definable; see s04.
  • Free goods and schemes. "Buy 2 get 1" schemes reduce realisation. If free units are counted in volume, price falls; if excluded, volume understates throughput. Choose and disclose.
  • Returns and expiry claims. Large returns in a month distort both volume and price; many companies analyse gross sales PVM and show returns as a separate bar.
  • Customer-level PVM with very many small customers produces a noisy mix effect; aggregate tail customers into a cluster before computing.

Key takeaways

  • Revenue PVM = price (at actual quantity) + volume (at budget average price) + mix (at budget item prices), and it reconciles with zero residual.
  • Build it as four revenue columns: budget; actual qty at budget mix and price; actual qty and mix at budget price; actual.
  • The level of analysis (SKU, channel, customer, SKU × channel) is a design decision that moves value between price and mix.
  • Define the item where the price is actually set, and use a common physical unit of volume.
  • Always show the residual check cell and a ranked table of item contributions behind the bridge.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-SKU revenue PVM [L1]

A biscuit maker budgets SKU Glucose 2,000 tonnes at ₹90,000/t, SKU Cream 1,200 tonnes at ₹1,50,000/t and SKU Digestive 300 tonnes at ₹2,20,000/t. Actuals are Glucose 2,300 t at ₹92,000/t, Cream 1,100 t at ₹1,48,000/t and Digestive 400 t at ₹2,30,000/t. Compute the price, volume and mix variances in ₹ lakh and prove they sum to the total revenue variance.

Q2. Same data, different level of analysis [L2]

A detergent SKU sells budget 500 t in general trade at ₹1,10,000/t and 100 t on quick commerce at ₹95,000/t. Actual is 450 t in GT at ₹1,10,000/t and 200 t on quick commerce at ₹95,000/t. (a) Compute PVM treating the SKU as a single item with one average price. (b) Recompute treating SKU × channel as two items. Explain why the price variance differs between (a) and (b) and which presentation the pricing team should receive.

💡 Hint: In (a) the "price" is an average realisation, so a channel shift changes it even though no list price moved.

Q3. Excel build with SUMPRODUCT [L2]

Write the Excel formulas (assume budget quantities in C5:C20, budget prices in D5:D20, actual quantities in F5:F20, actual prices in G5:G20) for price, volume and mix variances and for a residual check cell. Then describe a conditional-format rule that highlights the check cell if it is not zero.

Q4. Choosing the volume unit [L2]

A personal-care company sells a ₹2 sachet (8 ml), a ₹120 bottle (200 ml) and a ₹450 bottle (1 litre). Units sold rose 15%, litres sold rose 3%, revenue rose 6%. Explain how the volume and mix variances would differ if volume is measured in units versus litres, and recommend a unit for the board pack, giving reasons.

Q5. Interview: explain PVM to a sales head [L3]

The national sales head says: "My team beat the volume budget by 8%; why does your bridge show a negative mix?" Draft a 150-word explanation, using an illustrative number, that a non-finance audience will accept, and suggest one KPI the sales team could track to manage mix.
