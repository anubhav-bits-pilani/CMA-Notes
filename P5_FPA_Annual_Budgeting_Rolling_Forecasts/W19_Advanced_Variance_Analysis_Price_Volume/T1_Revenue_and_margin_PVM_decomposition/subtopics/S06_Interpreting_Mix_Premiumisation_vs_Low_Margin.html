Interpreting Mix: Premiumisation vs Low-Margin Channel Shift

Scope: Interpreting mix: shift to premium SKUs vs low-margin channels (modern trade, e-commerce, quick commerce)

Why it matters

A mix number is only useful if someone can act on it. In the Indian consumer market two structural forces drive most mix movements: premiumisation (consumers trading up to higher-priced, usually higher-margin variants — premium soaps, liquid detergents, premium two-wheelers, larger-screen TVs) and channel migration (volume moving from general trade (GT) kiranas to modern trade (MT) chains, e-commerce marketplaces and quick-commerce (QC) dark-store platforms such as Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart). Premiumisation usually helps both revenue and margin; channel migration often helps volume but hurts net realisation and margin after trade terms. The FP&A analyst's job is to say which force drove the mix bar, whether it was intended, and what it does to margin — not just to report a number.

Core notes

Reading the sign of the mix bar

The mix variance for item \(i\) is \((m_i^A - m_i^B) \times Q^A \times V_i^B\), where \(V_i^B\) is budget price (revenue) or budget unit margin (GM). Rewrite it relative to the budget average value \(\bar{V}^B\):

\[ \text{Mix} = Q^A \sum_i (m_i^A - m_i^B)\,(V_i^B - \bar{V}^B) \]

This works because \(\sum_i (m_i^A - m_i^B) = 0\). The form makes interpretation immediate: mix is favourable when share moves towards items whose budget value is above the budget average, and adverse when share moves to below-average items. A share gain by an item that is itself "profitable" but below the portfolio average margin is still adverse mix.

Premiumisation: product mix

  • Premium SKUs typically carry higher price per kg and higher gross margin %, so revenue and GM mix are both favourable.
  • Caveat: premium launches often carry heavy A&P (advertising and promotion) and sampling costs below GM; the GM mix looks good while contribution after A&P may not. Look one level down the P&L before celebrating.
  • Caveat: "premium" in price may not be premium in margin — for example, imported-ingredient premium variants or licensed brands paying royalty. Always check GM mix and revenue mix separately; they can have opposite signs.

Channel migration: channel mix

Typical economics (illustrative, varies by category and company):

Channel Net realisation vs GT Typical cost features GM / contribution impact
General trade (via distributors) Base Distributor and retailer margins built into price list; secondary freight; credit to distributors Base
Modern trade (organised retail chains) Lower — higher back-end margins, listing and display fees, promotional funding Direct supply to DCs; longer payment terms Usually lower GM; better working capital visibility but higher receivable days
E-commerce marketplaces Lower — discount funding, platform fees Returns, last-mile packaging, damage Lower contribution; high growth
Quick commerce Often lower — platform margins, visibility spends Small-lot replenishment to dark stores; smaller packs Volatile; high-growth; premium-skewed baskets in metros can partly offset
Institutional / CSD / exports Varies (contract pricing) Bulk packs Lower price, sometimes lower cost-to-serve

A volume shift from GT to MT/e-com/QC is therefore usually adverse revenue mix at the channel level (lower net price per unit) and adverse GM mix if trade terms are deducted from revenue (as Ind AS 115 requires for discounts, rebates and most payments to customers that are not for distinct goods/services). Payments to customers for distinct services (e.g., a genuine fair-value advertising service) can be expensed; check how listing fees and visibility payments are classified, because the classification decides whether the cost hits the price/mix bars or opex.

Separating the two forces

Use hierarchical mix (s03): channel mix isolates migration; product mix within channel isolates premiumisation within each channel. A typical Indian FMCG pattern in recent years: channel mix adverse (shift to QC/e-com), product mix within QC favourable (urban consumers buying premium packs), net mix near zero. Without the split, management would conclude "mix is fine" and miss the margin erosion from channel economics.

Illustration

From s03: channel mix −₹0.85 lakh (shift to QC with ₹3.50 lower budget realisation), product mix within channel +₹1.35 lakh (QC premium share 57% vs 50% budget). Suppose budget unit margins are GT-P ₹40, GT-R ₹18, QC-P ₹28, QC-R ₹10. GM channel averages: GT \((40\times 40 + 60 \times 18)/100 = 26.80\); QC \((20 \times 28 + 20 \times 10)/40 = 19.00\). The ₹7.80 GM gap between channels is far wider in relative terms (29%) than the ₹3.50 price gap (5%). On GM the channel shift is much more damaging than on revenue — exactly the margin story the CFO needs.

From mix to cost-to-serve and contribution

Gross margin does not capture the full channel economics. Channel-specific costs — secondary freight, warehousing for MT DCs, e-commerce return handling, QC dark-store replenishment, key-account team costs — sit in opex. Mature FP&A teams extend PVM to contribution after channel costs, valuing channel mix at budget contribution per unit by channel. This links to Week 21 (contribution margin and pricing decisions).

What to say to executives

A good mix commentary answers four questions:

  1. Which dimension? Channel, product, pack size, region, customer.
  2. Which direction and why? "Volume shifted 15 points to QC as metro consumers moved to 10-minute delivery."
  3. Was it planned? Compare with the AOP channel plan; a strategic shift should have been budgeted.
  4. What is the margin consequence and response? "Each point of channel shift costs ~X bps of GM; we are renegotiating QC trade terms and launching QC-specific pack sizes."

Convert mix to margin bps (Topic 2, s04) for the GM % storyline.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mix vs price confusion within a channel. If a QC platform's deeper discount is applied to the same SKU, that is price, not mix, in a SKU × channel analysis — but it is mix in a SKU-only analysis. Choose the level consciously.
  • Pack-size mix. Small packs (₹5/₹10 price points for rural and QC baskets) have higher price per kg but often lower margin per kg because of packaging costs; revenue mix in ₹/kg and GM mix can diverge.
  • Regional mix. States with higher freight costs or different trade structures (North-East, hill states) create geographic mix effects; include region if material.
  • Intended negative mix. A deliberate entry-pack strategy to gain rural penetration produces adverse mix by design; present it against the strategic KPI (penetration, distribution reach) rather than as a failure.

Key takeaways

  • Mix is favourable when share moves to items whose budget value is above the budget average — not merely to "profitable" items.
  • Premiumisation typically improves both revenue and GM mix; channel migration to MT, e-commerce and quick commerce often hurts GM mix more than revenue mix.
  • Use hierarchical mix to separate channel migration from product premiumisation.
  • Check where trade terms and channel payments are classified (price deduction vs opex) under Ind AS 115 — classification changes the mix story.
  • Executive commentary should state dimension, direction, cause, whether planned, margin consequence and response.

Practice Questions

Q1. Above or below the average [L1]

A portfolio has budget unit margins of ₹120 (SKU A, 20% share), ₹80 (SKU B, 50% share) and ₹60 (SKU C, 30% share). Actual shares are A 22%, B 44%, C 34%, on actual total volume of 5 lakh units. Compute the GM mix variance using the deviation-from-average form and explain why B's share loss is favourable or adverse.

Q2. Channel economics case [L3]

A packaged-foods company sells 60% of volume through GT, 25% through MT and 15% through e-commerce/QC in the budget. Actual shares are 52%, 26% and 22%. Budget net realisation per kg: GT ₹180, MT ₹165, e-com/QC ₹158. Budget GM per kg: GT ₹54, MT ₹38, e-com/QC ₹30. Actual total volume is 40,000 tonnes. Compute the channel mix effect on revenue and on GM, and write a three-sentence CFO commentary.

Q3. Classification of channel payments [L2]

A QC platform charges the company (a) a 6% "platform margin" deducted from invoice value, (b) a fixed ₹40 lakh per quarter for "premium visibility" on the app home screen, and (c) ₹15 lakh for consumer research data with a fair value that can be reasonably estimated. Under Ind AS 115, discuss how each would likely be presented and how each presentation affects the price, mix and opex bars.

💡 Hint: Consider whether the company receives a distinct good or service from its customer in exchange for the payment.

Q4. Premium SKU that hurts margin [L2]

A premium imported-ingredient variant sells at ₹300/kg with GM ₹75/kg; the portfolio budget average is ₹220/kg revenue and ₹82/kg GM. Its share rises by 4 points on actual volume of 10,000 tonnes, taken equally from items at average price and average margin. Compute the revenue and GM mix effect of this shift and explain the apparent contradiction.

Q5. Interview: mix storytelling [L3]

In an FP&A manager interview you are told: "Our revenue mix is +₹12 crore but GM mix is −₹5 crore this quarter." List the hypotheses you would test, the data cuts you would request, and the structure of the one-slide explanation you would prepare for the CEO.
