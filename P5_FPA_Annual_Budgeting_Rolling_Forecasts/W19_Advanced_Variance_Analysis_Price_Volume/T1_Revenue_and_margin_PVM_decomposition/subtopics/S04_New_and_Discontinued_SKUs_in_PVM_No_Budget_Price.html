New and Discontinued SKUs in PVM: No Budget Price or No Actual Volume

Scope: Treatment of new and discontinued SKUs (no budget price or no actual volume)

Why it matters

Real portfolios change during the year. An FMCG company launches a new variant in Q2, delists slow movers, or a pharma company loses a product to price control or patent expiry. The PVM formulas need a budget price for every item that sold (price variance) and treat a missing actual volume as a zero-quantity item. If new and discontinued SKUs are simply left out of the table, the components will not add to the total revenue variance and someone will plug the difference into "Other". If they are included naively, a new SKU with no budget price produces a price variance equal to its whole revenue, or a formula error. Either way the bridge loses credibility. Investors also ask for this split directly: "how much of your growth came from new launches versus the base business?"

Core notes

The two problem cases

Case Budget Actual Problem
New SKU \(Q^B = 0\), no \(P^B\) \(Q^A > 0\), \(P^A\) known Price variance \((P^A - P^B)Q^A\) undefined; mix needs a budget price
Discontinued SKU \(Q^B > 0\), \(P^B\) known \(Q^A = 0\), no \(P^A\) Price variance is zero (actual quantity zero), mix and volume absorb the whole lost revenue

Discontinued SKUs are mechanically harmless — with \(Q^A = 0\) the price term vanishes and the formulas still reconcile — but the lost revenue is spread across volume and mix, which hides it. New SKUs break the formula unless a decision is made.

Method A: separate new and discontinued bars (like-for-like PVM)

Run PVM only on the like-for-like (LFL) set — items with both budget and actual volume. Then add:

  • New products bar = full actual revenue (or GM) of SKUs with no budget.
  • Discontinued products bar = minus the full budget revenue (or GM) of SKUs with no actual.

\[ \text{Total variance} = \text{Price}_{LFL} + \text{Volume}_{LFL} + \text{Mix}_{LFL} + \text{New} - \text{Discontinued} \]

This is the most transparent method and the one investor presentations implicitly use when they quote "LFL growth" or "base business growth".

Method B: include in the portfolio with proxy prices

Keep all SKUs in one table. For each new SKU set the "budget price" proxy equal to its actual price (so price variance is zero and its whole revenue falls into volume and mix). Alternatively use the budget price of the SKU it replaced, or a launch price from the product plan approved after the budget. This treats the new SKU as a mix choice and keeps the bridge to three bars.

Worked illustration

₹ and units in '000.

SKU Bud qty Bud price Bud revenue Act qty Act price Act revenue
L (continuing) 100 50 5,000 110 51 5,610
D (discontinued in April) 30 30 900 0 – 0
N (launched in July) 0 – 0 20 40 800
Total 130 5,900 130 6,410

Total variance = +510.

Method A (LFL + new + discontinued):

  • LFL price \((51-50)\times 110 = +110\); LFL volume \((110 - 100) \times 50 = +500\); LFL mix = 0 (single item).
  • New +800; Discontinued −900.
  • Check: \(110 + 500 + 800 - 900 = 510\).

Method B (proxy budget price for N = ₹40):

  • Budget average price \(= 5,900/130 = 45.38\). Total volume is flat (130 vs 130), so volume = 0.
  • Mix: actual quantities at budget/proxy prices \(= 110 \times 50 + 20 \times 40 = 6,300\); less \(130 \times 45.38 = 5,900\); mix = +400.
  • Price: L +110, N 0 → +110.
  • Check: \(0 + 400 + 110 = 510\).

Same total, two very different stories: Method A says "base business grew volume 10%, and the launch did not quite replace the delisted SKU"; Method B says "no volume growth, positive mix". For a C-suite audience, Method A is usually more useful because it separates innovation performance from base business performance. Method B can be appropriate when the new SKU is a straight replacement (a reformulated variant at a new pack size) and the business thinks of it as the same product.

Choosing and documenting the policy

A PVM policy note should state:

  1. Definition of new: SKU code not in budget; or launched within the last 12 months (for vs PY comparisons, the typical "innovation" definition, e.g., SKUs launched in the last 3 years in many FMCG innovation-rate KPIs).
  2. Replacement mapping: whether a reformulated or resized SKU is mapped to its predecessor (then it is LFL and the change is in price/mix) or treated as new. Maintain a SKU mapping table (old code → new code, conversion factor for pack size).
  3. Discontinued definition: budgeted but zero actual; or partial-year sales (a SKU delisted in September has actual volume — it is LFL for the months it sold, but the planned volume for October–March becomes a discontinued effect only if the budget assumed full-year sales).
  4. Budget-time launches: a launch already in the budget is not "new" for budget comparison — it has a budget price and volume and is LFL; it may still be "new" for the PY comparison.

Vs prior year comparisons

For actual vs PY, a SKU launched this year has no PY price, and one delisted last year has no current volume. The same two methods apply, with "new" defined relative to PY. This is exactly how "LFL" or "same-store" analysis works in retail (stores open in both periods) — see the related concept in the topic overview.

Gross margin and channel analogues

For GM PVM, the new-products bar is the full actual GM of new SKUs (its cost is what it is — there is no budget cost to vary from), and the discontinued bar is minus the full budget GM. A new channel or a lost key account is handled the same way at channel or customer level.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Launches with heavy introductory schemes: the new-SKU bar includes the trade-loading discounts; comment on run-rate realisation separately.
  • Cannibalisation: a new SKU often takes volume from existing ones. Method A shows the new bar as positive and the cannibalised SKU's volume as negative LFL volume; a mature analysis estimates net incremental revenue, but that is an estimate, not a PVM output — keep it in commentary.
  • SKU code changes without a product change (new barcode for a GST-driven MRP relabel, plant code change) must be mapped, not treated as new and discontinued, or both bars inflate artificially.
  • Pack-size changes ("grammage cuts") at the same MRP are a price increase per kg. If volume is in kg and the SKU is mapped, PVM shows a price effect; if it is treated as new, the price increase disappears into a new-SKU bar.

Key takeaways

  • Discontinued SKUs reconcile automatically but bury the lost revenue in volume and mix; new SKUs need a policy because they have no budget price.
  • Method A (LFL PVM plus separate new and discontinued bars) is the most transparent for executives and investors.
  • Method B (proxy budget price) keeps three bars and suits straight replacements.
  • Maintain a SKU mapping table and a written definition of new, discontinued and replacement SKUs.
  • Whatever the method, the bridge must still reconcile with zero residual.

Practice Questions

Q1. LFL bridge with a launch and a delisting [L1]

Budget: SKU P 50,000 units at ₹200; SKU Q 20,000 units at ₹150. Actual: P 54,000 units at ₹205; Q delisted (zero sales); new SKU R 15,000 units at ₹180. Prepare a Method A bridge (LFL price, LFL volume, LFL mix, new, discontinued) and prove it reconciles.

Q2. Method B on the same data [L2]

Using Q1 data, prepare a Method B bridge with R's proxy budget price equal to its actual price. Then repeat with R's proxy budget price equal to Q's budget price (₹150). Explain why the price bar changes between the two proxy choices and which choice you would defend to an auditor of the management pack.

💡 Hint: The proxy determines how much of R's revenue is labelled price versus mix.

Q3. Replacement mapping [L2]

A ₹10 biscuit pack was 75 g in the budget; from August it was relaunched as 70 g at ₹10 under a new SKU code. Budget volume for the year is 9,000 tonnes; actual is 4,000 tonnes of the 75 g pack (April–July) and 4,900 tonnes of the 70 g pack (August–March). Explain how PVM in tonnes differs if the 70 g pack is (a) mapped to the old SKU, (b) treated as new. Quantify the implied price increase per kg.

Q4. Policy note [L3]

Draft a policy note (headings and one or two sentences each) for a listed FMCG company's FP&A team defining new, discontinued and replacement SKUs for both actual vs budget and actual vs PY comparisons, including who maintains the mapping table and how frequently it is reviewed.

Q5. Interview: innovation contribution [L2]

An analyst asks the CFO: "Growth was 11% — how much came from new launches?" The company sells 400 SKUs, 35 of which were launched in the last 12 months. Explain what data you would extract, how you would compute the answer, and two pitfalls that could overstate innovation contribution.
