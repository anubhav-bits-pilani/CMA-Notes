Objective: Bridge CMA standard costing variance formulas into commercial FP&A Price-Volume-Mix (PVM) bridges for C-suite decision-making.

How this week fits

This is the analytical core of Phase 5 (FP&A, Annual Budgeting, Rolling Forecasts & Variance Storytelling). Week 17 built the annual operating plan, flexible budgets and zero-based budgeting packages; Week 18 built the version structure (Budget, 3+9, 6+6, 9+3 LEs) and scenarios. This week asks the question every month-end review asks of those numbers: why are we different from plan? The answer has to be quantified, reconciled to the P&L to the last rupee, and told in the language of executives — ₹ crore, margin basis points and business drivers.

You already know the CMA variance toolkit: sales price, mix and quantity variances; material price, usage, mix and yield; labour rate, efficiency and idle time; overhead spending and volume. This week converts that toolkit into five practitioner skills, one per topic:

  1. Revenue and margin PVM (Topic 1) — price, volume, mix (product, channel, customer), new/discontinued SKUs and FX.
  2. Material and labour variances in GM bps (Topic 2) — turning the cost bar into margin-percentage language, commodity pass-through and PPV timing.
  3. Opex spending vs timing (Topic 3) — separating real savings and overruns from phasing, using the LE.
  4. EBITDA bridge waterfalls (Topic 4) — sequencing, Excel and Power BI construction, labelling and reconciliation.
  5. Sign conventions (Topic 5) — making favourable always mean the same thing across every table, chart and dashboard.

Prerequisites: Week 11/12 Power BI and DAX (SUMX, CALCULATE, waterfall visual), Week 17 flexible budgets and ZBB, Week 18 versions and LE mechanics, and Phase 1 close quality (accruals, prepaids, cut-off). Next: Week 20 develops rolling forecasts and driver-based reforecasting, and Week 21 uses the contribution-margin logic from this week for pricing decisions. In Phase 2 terms, the same bridges let you read investor presentations ("volume growth 7%, price/mix 3%") critically.

Refresh before starting

CMA variances including mix and yield

CMA material, labour, overhead and sales variances, including mix and yield

  • Material: cost variance = price + usage; price = (SP − AP) × AQ; usage = (SQ for actual output − AQ) × SP; usage = mix + yield. Mix = (AQ at standard mix − AQ at actual mix) × SP; yield = (SQ for actual output − actual total input at standard mix) × SP, or (actual output − standard output from actual input) × standard cost per unit of output.
  • Labour: cost = rate + idle time + efficiency; rate = (SR − AR) × hours paid; idle = idle hours × SR (always adverse); efficiency = (SH for actual output − hours worked) × SR. Labour mix and yield apply when grades of labour are substitutable.
  • Variable overhead: spending = (SR × actual hours − actual VOH); efficiency = (SH − AH) × SR.
  • Fixed overhead (absorption): expenditure = budgeted − actual; volume = (actual output × standard rate per unit) − budgeted FOH, split into capacity and efficiency.
  • Sales: price = (AP − SP) × AQ; volume = mix + quantity; mix = (AQ at actual mix − AQ at standard mix) × standard price/margin; quantity = (actual total qty − budget total qty) × budget average price/margin.
  • Signs in CMA are labelled F/A, not by algebraic sign — this week formalises the algebraic convention (positive = favourable).

Contribution vs absorption profit reconciliation

Contribution vs absorption profit reconciliation

  • Absorption profit − marginal (contribution) profit = fixed production overhead in closing inventory − fixed production overhead in opening inventory = (closing units − opening units) × FOH absorption rate per unit (if the rate is constant).
  • When production > sales, absorption profit is higher (overhead carried into inventory); when sales > production, lower.
  • Ind AS 2 requires absorption of fixed production overheads based on normal capacity (Ind AS 2.13); unallocated overheads in low-production periods are expensed — so under-absorption is a P&L cost, not inventory.
  • Implication for PVM: an absorption-cost GM bridge contains under/over-absorption inside the cost bar; a contribution bridge shows fixed costs as a separate bar. Decide which basis your bridge uses and keep budget and actual on the same basis.

Week 17 flexible budgets and Week 18 versions

Week 17 flexible budget and Week 18 version structures

  • Flexible budget = budget unit rates × actual activity. Static budget − flexed budget = volume (activity) variance; flexed budget − actual = spending/price variance. This week's volume-driven opex variance (Topic 3, c03) and spending variance (c04) are the same split.
  • Driver-based budget lines: freight per tonne, commission % of sales, power per unit produced — these are the lines that should be flexed; fixed lines (rent, salaries) should not.
  • Versions: Budget/AOP (frozen), monthly or quarterly LEs (3+9, 6+6, 9+3), Actual. Comparisons: Actual vs Budget, Actual vs PY, LE vs AOP, LE vs prior LE. Every bridge this week must state which comparison it is (Topic 4, s02).
  • Full-year LE = YTD actual + remaining-months forecast — the tool for separating timing from permanent variances (Topic 3, s04).

Excel waterfall and SUMPRODUCT weighted averages

Excel waterfall chart and SUMPRODUCT for weighted averages

  • Weighted average price: =SUMPRODUCT(Qty,Price)/SUM(Qty); weighted margin: =SUMPRODUCT(Qty,Price-Cost)/SUM(Qty). SUMPRODUCT accepts array arithmetic inside (e.g., share changes ActQty/SUM(ActQty)-BudQty/SUM(BudQty)).
  • Excel 2016+ native waterfall: Insert → Charts → Waterfall; right-click the first and last data points → Set as total. Categories are shown in data order.
  • Stacked-column fallback: helper columns for an invisible base and visible up/down bars; base = MIN(cumulative before, cumulative after), bar = ABS(step); this breaks if a bar crosses zero, which needs split positive/negative helper series.
  • Data labels: link to cells with custom number formats such as #,##0.0;(#,##0.0) so adverse bars show in brackets.

Cross-cutting concepts

Every variance reconciles with zero residual

Every variance must reconcile: sum of components = total difference, with no unexplained plug

A variance analysis is an identity, not an estimate. Revenue PVM must satisfy price + volume + mix (+ new/discontinued + FX) = actual revenue − budget revenue; GM PVM adds cost; the EBITDA bridge adds opex and other lines; the opex analysis must satisfy timing + permanent = YTD variance and volume-driven + spending = total variance vs static budget. If the formulas are built correctly — price and cost at actual quantity, volume and mix at budget unit economics, FX on actual foreign-currency revenue — the residual is exactly zero. A non-zero residual is therefore diagnostic: it reveals a methodology error (volume at actual price, mix shares on the wrong population), a data gap (new SKUs missing from the item list, a revenue line excluded) or a scope mismatch (PVM revenue not tying to the P&L). Practitioners build an explicit residual check cell, format it red when non-zero, and add tie-out checks to the reported P&L for both budget and actual. The worst practice is labelling the residual "Other" or "rounding" on the chart: executives cannot challenge a bar they cannot interpret, and auditors and analysts eventually find that the bridge does not survive a change of period. Small genuine "other" items (scrap sales, miscellaneous income) are acceptable as named bars; an unexplained plug is not.

Valuation rules: price at actual quantity, volume and mix at budget economics

Valuation rules: price at actual quantity, volume/mix at budget unit economics

One rule generates every formula this week. Rate effects (selling price, unit cost, material price, labour rate, FX rate) are valued at actual quantity — actual units sold, actual kilograms consumed, actual hours paid, actual foreign-currency revenue. Quantity effects (volume, mix, usage, yield, efficiency) are valued at budget/standard unit economics — budget price, budget unit margin or contribution, standard price per kg, standard rate per hour. This is the CMA convention ("price variances at actual quantity, quantity variances at standard price"), and it is the only standard convention that leaves no price × quantity interaction term. It also gives each bar a clean owner: the commercial team owns price on what they actually sold, procurement owns material price on what was actually bought, operations owns usage and efficiency at standard prices. Breaking the rule causes the classic pitfalls: volume at actual price double-counts price; gross-margin mix at price instead of margin reports a revenue effect on a profit bridge. When a business wants a different convention (for example, price at budget volume with an explicit joint bar), it must be documented, applied identically across months and versions, and the joint bar must be shown and explained.

From ₹ variances to margin bps and business drivers

Translate ₹ variances into margin bps and business drivers for executives

A ₹2.7 crore adverse cost variance means little to a CEO until it is expressed as "234 bps of gross margin, driven by palm oil at ₹97,000/t against a ₹90,000 standard". Executives manage margins and drivers, so every material bar needs three translations. Basis points: bps impact = variance ÷ actual net revenue × 10,000 (1% of margin = 100 bps); a GM% change is decomposed sequentially into mix, price and cost bps that add to the total change. Driver: the operational cause — ₹/kg of an input, net realisation per case, share of quick commerce in volume, hours per unit, headcount and joining dates, campaign timing. Action and outlook: will it persist (permanent), reverse (timing), or does it need a decision (price increase, trade-term renegotiation, hedge, hiring freeze)? Good commentary follows a fixed pattern: what happened (₹ and bps) → why (driver with numbers) → whether it repeats (LE impact) → what we are doing. The same translation applies to opex: an underspend is only good news if the LE shows it is permanent. Avoid reporting percentages of variance (e.g., "cost variance 3.2%") without the margin context; a 3% material overrun can be 150 bps of margin in a material-heavy FMCG business and negligible in IT services.

One sign convention everywhere

Consistent sign convention across tables, bridges and dashboards

Variance tables, bridges, KPI scorecards and Power BI dashboards are read together; if they disagree on signs, readers lose trust immediately. The recommended convention: positive = favourable for every line — revenue and margin variance = actual − budget, cost variance = budget − actual — with adverse amounts shown in brackets via a custom number format such as #,##0;(#,##0);"-". On bridges, a bar that increases profit always rises; cost savings rise, cost overruns fall. ERP extracts must be flipped first: in a trial balance, revenue and other credit-nature accounts carry negative natural signs, so the presentation amount is −1 × TB balance for credit-nature accounts, driven by an account-type attribute in the chart-of-accounts mapping, not by manual edits. Colours follow meaning (green = good, red = bad, IBCS style), never the algebraic sign of the underlying number. Variance % is computed as variance ÷ ABS(budget) and shown as "n.m." when the budget is zero or the sign flips. Document the convention on the cover of the management pack and enforce it in a single calculation layer (one formula pattern in Excel, one set of DAX measures or a calculation group in Power BI), so that every downstream view inherits it.

What the exercise needs

Exercise needs: 4-SKU table (budget/actual units, price, unit cost), revenue PVM, GM PVM with cost variance, EBITDA waterfall with reconciliation table

The hands-on exercise integrates Topics 1, 2, 4 and 5 into one workbook. You need: (1) a 4-SKU input table with budget and actual units, net price per unit and unit cost per unit, plus opex lines with budget, actual and full-year LE; (2) a revenue PVM using the four-column layout (budget → actual quantity at budget mix and price → actual quantity and mix at budget price → actual), with SKU-level rows and a residual check; (3) a GM PVM that reuses the price bar, revalues volume and mix at budget unit margin and adds the cost variance at actual quantity, also reconciled to zero, plus a GM% bps bridge; (4) an EBITDA waterfall from budget EBITDA through price, volume, mix, COGS, opex (split timing/permanent in commentary) and other to actual EBITDA, built with the native Excel waterfall and with the stacked-column method; and (5) a reconciliation table under the chart listing each bar in ₹ crore and bps, its formula reference and a check row proving budget EBITDA + Σ steps = actual EBITDA. Inputs in one sheet, calculations in another, outputs (chart and table) in a third; all signs positive = favourable.

Hands-on exercise brief

Given a 4-SKU product line with Budget vs Actual units, prices, and unit costs, build a complete PVM Revenue & Gross Margin bridge and an EBITDA Waterfall chart.

Business context

Sundaram Home Care Ltd (NSE-listed, FY ending 31 March) sells a liquid hand-wash range. You are preparing the 9-month (April–December) management pack for the CFO. All prices are net of GST and trade discounts.

Inputs

SKU Bud units (lakh) Bud price ₹/unit Bud unit cost ₹ Act units (lakh) Act price ₹/unit Act unit cost ₹
A Premium 500 ml 12.0 240 132 13.5 248 140
B Regular 200 ml 30.0 110 70 28.0 108 72
C Value refill 1 L 8.0 320 230 10.5 312 241
D Mini 80 ml 45.0 40 26 52.0 40 27.5

Unit cost breakdown for SKU C (to convert cost variances into bps): budget material ₹190 (palm-based surfactant 0.40 kg at ₹250/kg = ₹100, other materials ₹90), conversion ₹40; actual surfactant price ₹270/kg, actual surfactant usage 0.41 kg per unit, other materials ₹90.30 per unit, conversion on budget.

Opex (₹ crore, April–December):

Line YTD budget YTD actual FY budget FY LE (9+3) Notes
Advertising & promotion 9.00 7.60 12.00 11.80 Festive campaign partly moved to February
Employee costs 7.50 7.05 10.00 9.60 Two sales manager hires deferred to Q4
Secondary freight 3.80 4.47 5.10 5.95 Budget ₹4.00 per unit sold; actual ₹4.30 per unit
Other overheads 5.50 5.95 7.40 7.90 Includes ₹0.30 crore one-off legal fee

Required tabs

  1. Inputs — the tables above, with named ranges; no hard-coded numbers elsewhere.
  2. Rev_PVM — SKU-level rows: budget revenue, actual qty at budget mix and price, actual qty at budget price, actual revenue; price, volume and mix by SKU and total; residual check.
  3. GM_PVM — budget and actual unit margins; volume at budget average margin; mix at budget unit margin; price; cost; residual check; budget and actual GM%; GM% bps bridge (mix, price, cost — sequential) that sums to the total GM% change.
  4. Cost_Detail — SKU C surfactant price and usage variances, converted to ₹ lakh and bps of actual net revenue; reconcile to SKU C's cost variance.
  5. Opex — YTD variance, permanent (FY budget − FY LE), timing (YTD variance − permanent); freight split into volume-driven (at ₹4.00 per unit) and spending; one-off flag; materiality flag (absolute variance > ₹0.25 crore and > 5% of budget).
  6. EBITDA_Bridge — sequence: Budget EBITDA → Price → Volume → Mix → COGS → A&P → Employee → Freight (volume) → Freight (spending) → Other overheads → Actual EBITDA. Build (a) Excel native waterfall with start and end bars set as totals, (b) stacked-column version with invisible base and up/down series.
  7. Recon — table beneath the chart: each bar in ₹ crore, bps of actual revenue, source cell reference; check row Budget EBITDA + Σ bars − Actual EBITDA = 0.

Required checks

  • Revenue PVM residual = 0; GM PVM residual = 0; GM% bps components sum to the total change.
  • PVM budget and actual revenue tie to the Inputs SUMPRODUCT totals.
  • Mix share changes sum to 0%.
  • Opex timing + permanent = YTD variance for every line.
  • EBITDA bridge check row = 0; all bars use positive = favourable.

Deliverables

  • One workbook (W19_PVM_<name>.xlsx) with the seven tabs and a check summary panel on the Recon tab.
  • A one-page CFO memo (max 300 words): why GM% moved, which opex variances are timing vs permanent, and three actions.
  • Optional extension: load the Inputs table into Power BI and reproduce the EBITDA waterfall with a DAX bridge measure and a sorted category table.
