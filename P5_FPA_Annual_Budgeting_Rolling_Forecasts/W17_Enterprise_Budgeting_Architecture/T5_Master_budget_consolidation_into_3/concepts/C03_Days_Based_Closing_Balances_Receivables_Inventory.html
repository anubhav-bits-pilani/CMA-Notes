Key concept

Days-Based Closing Balances: Receivables, Inventory, Payables

Concept: Closing receivables = Monthly credit sales × DSO ÷ Days in month (or rolling last N months' sales); similarly inventory on COGS, payables on purchases

Statement

Single-month base:

\[ \text{AR}_t = S_t \times \frac{\text{DSO}}{d_t}, \qquad \text{Inv}_t = \text{COGS}_t \times \frac{\text{DIO}}{d_t}, \qquad \text{AP}_t = \text{Pur}_t \times \frac{\text{DPO}}{d_t} \]

Rolling \(N\)-month base:

\[ \text{AR}_t = \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} S_{t-k}}{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} d_{t-k}} \times \text{DSO} \]

where \(S_t\) is gross credit sales including GST, \(\text{COGS}_t\) cost of goods sold (or RM consumption / production cost for component inventories), \(\text{Pur}_t\) gross credit purchases including GST, \(d_t\) days in month \(t\), and DSO, DIO, DPO the days assumptions. When days exceed the month length, the count-back method (s03) layers whole months plus a fraction of the earlier month.

Explanation

Days assumptions make working capital scale with activity. The base must match the balance: receivables include GST, so the DSO base is gross sales; payables include GST, so the DPO base is gross purchases (not COGS, which includes labour, overhead and depreciation). Inventory is at cost, so the DIO base is COGS (or forward-looking: next month's COGS, which better matches how inventory is planned).

Choice of method:

  • Single-month is simple and fine when days are less than a month and activity is smooth.
  • Rolling \(N\)-month smooths seasonality and approximates how receivables age when DSO > 30 days, but lags turning points.
  • Count-back most closely mimics invoice ageing for DSO > 30 days.
  • Forward-looking inventory (cover on next month) is consistent with production planning (c01).

Once balances are set, collections and payments follow from roll-forwards (Topic 4 c02, c03), and the change in working capital flows into the indirect cash flow (c05). Days assumptions should be set by channel or supplier class (modern trade vs general trade; MSME vs large suppliers) and checked against history computed on the same basis.

Illustration

Gross credit sales (₹ crore): Oct 120, Nov 150, Dec 90; DSO 30 days; days 31, 30, 31.

Method AR at 31 Dec
Single-month: \(90 \times 30 \div 31\) 87.1
Rolling 3-month: \((120 + 150 + 90) \div 92 \times 30\) 117.4
Count-back (30 days ≤ 31): \(90 \times 30 \div 31\) 87.1

The rolling method gives ₹30 crore more receivables because it still "remembers" the festive months — which is wrong if customers actually pay in 30 days, but right if a material share of November invoices is still outstanding. Choose the method that matches observed ageing.

Payables: purchases incl. GST in December ₹70 crore, DPO 45 days with count-back: \(70 + \text{Nov purchases} \times 14 \div 30\). If November purchases were ₹96 crore, AP = 70 + 44.8 = ₹114.8 crore.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three methods compared [L1]

Gross credit sales (₹ crore): Jan 40, Feb 44, Mar 70; days 31, 28, 31; DSO 35 days. Compute receivables at 31 March using the single-month, rolling 3-month and count-back methods.

Q2. Inventory on forward COGS [L2]

COGS (₹ crore): Mar 50, Apr 62, May 80, Jun 75. DIO policy: 25 days of next month's COGS. Compute inventory at the end of March, April and May, and the cash impact of the inventory movement in each month.

💡 Hint: Use next month's days count when converting days of cover into a balance.

Q3. Base mismatch [L2]

A model computes DPO as trade payables (incl. GST) ÷ COGS (ex-GST) × 365, giving 62 days historically, and then budgets payables as budgeted COGS × 62 ÷ 365. COGS includes 30% non-purchase costs and GST on purchases averages 18%. Explain whether the budgeted payables will be right, and when this inconsistent-but-consistent approach breaks down.
