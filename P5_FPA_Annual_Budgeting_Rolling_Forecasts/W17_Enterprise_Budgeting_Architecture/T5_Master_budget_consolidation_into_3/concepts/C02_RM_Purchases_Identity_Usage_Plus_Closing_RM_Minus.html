Key concept

RM Purchases Identity: Usage Plus Closing RM Minus Opening RM

Concept: RM purchases (qty) = RM for production + Desired closing RM − Opening RM

Statement

\[ Q^{\text{pur}}_t = U_t + \text{RM}^{c}_t - \text{RM}^{o}_t, \qquad U_t = P_t \times q \times (1 + w) \]

\[ V^{\text{pur}}_t = Q^{\text{pur}}_t \times c_t \]

where \(Q^{\text{pur}}_t\) is quantity of raw material purchased, \(U_t\) usage for production, \(P_t\) production units (c01), \(q\) standard quantity per unit (BOM), \(w\) the normal wastage/yield loss rate, \(\text{RM}^{c}_t\) desired closing RM stock (policy: cover on next period's usage, or reorder-level based), \(\text{RM}^{o}_t\) opening RM stock, \(V^{\text{pur}}_t\) purchase value and \(c_t\) budgeted landed cost per unit (price plus freight, insurance and non-creditable duties; excluding creditable GST).

Explanation

The identity converts the production plan into a procurement plan, and procurement value into payables and cash (via Topic 4 c03). It is applied separately for each material — commodity raw materials (palm oil, LAB, milk powder, sugar), packing materials (laminates, PET preforms, cartons) and consumables. Key mechanics:

  • Multi-material BOMs. Usage for material \(j\) is \(\sum_i P_i \times q_{ij}\) across products \(i\) — a matrix multiplication (MMULT or SUMPRODUCT on a BOM table).
  • Closing stock policy reflects lead time and supply risk: imported materials (shipping 30–45 days) need higher cover than local packaging; MOQs can force lumpy purchases.
  • Price: commodity budgets should state a price deck (e.g. palm oil ₹/kg by quarter) and FX for imports; use sensitivity tables for ±10% movements.
  • Purchase value vs consumption value: purchases feed payables and cash; consumption (usage × cost under the inventory method) feeds COGS. The difference is the change in RM inventory value.
  • Normal vs abnormal wastage: normal wastage is part of cost (Ind AS 2.10 costs of purchase/conversion); abnormal wastage is expensed (Ind AS 2.16(a)). The budget includes only normal wastage in \(w\).

Illustration

A detergent SKU uses 0.95 kg of base powder per kg of finished product, wastage 2%. Production (tonnes): Oct 1,400, Nov 1,250, Dec 850. Closing RM = 30% of next month's usage; opening RM on 1 October 420 tonnes. Landed cost ₹68,000 per tonne.

Tonnes Oct Nov
Usage \(= P \times 0.95 \times 1.02\) 1,356.6 1,211.3
Closing RM (30% of next month usage) 363.4 247.1
Opening RM 420.0 363.4
Purchases 1,300.0 1,095.0
Purchase value (₹ crore) 8.84 7.45

(Dec usage = 850 × 0.969 = 823.7 tonnes; Nov closing = 30% × 823.7 = 247.1.)

October purchase value ₹8.84 crore, plus 18% GST, is the invoice amount entering payables; at 45-day MSME terms for local suppliers, roughly half is paid in November and the rest in December.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Two-material purchase budget [L1]

Production of product A: Apr 20,000 units, May 25,000, Jun 30,000. Each unit uses 2 kg of M1 and 0.5 kg of M2 (no wastage). Closing stock policy: M1 = 40% of next month's usage; M2 = 25%. Opening stock on 1 April: M1 18,000 kg, M2 2,500 kg. Prepare April and May purchase quantities for both materials.

Q2. BOM matrix in Excel [L2]

Three products (P1, P2, P3) with monthly production in rows and four materials with BOM quantities in a 3×4 table. Describe the Excel layout and write an MMULT- or SUMPRODUCT-based formula to compute monthly usage for each material, then extend it to purchases with a closing stock policy.

💡 Hint: Keep the BOM as a structured table with Product and Material keys if you prefer SUMIFS over matrix functions.

Q3. Commodity price sensitivity [L2]

Annual palm-oil usage is 18,000 tonnes, budgeted at ₹1,05,000 per tonne. A 10% price increase is expected from Q3 (half the year's volume). Compute the incremental purchase cost and explain how you would show it in the AOP (base vs risk scenario) and which balance sheet and cash lines change.
