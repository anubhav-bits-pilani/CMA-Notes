Key concept

Indirect Cash Flow Bridge from PAT to Change in Cash

Concept: Indirect CF: PAT + D&A ± ΔWorking capital − Capex ± Debt/Equity flows = ΔCash

Statement

\[ \Delta \text{Cash} = \underbrace{\text{PAT} + \text{D\&A} + \text{Non-cash items} - \Delta \text{NWC}}_{\text{CFO}} \; \underbrace{- \text{Cash capex} + \text{Disposal proceeds} \pm \text{Investments}}_{\text{CFI}} \; \underbrace{\pm \Delta \text{Debt} \pm \text{Equity} - \text{Dividends paid}}_{\text{CFF}} \]

\[ \Delta \text{NWC} = \Delta \text{AR} + \Delta \text{Inventory} + \Delta \text{Other operating assets} - \Delta \text{AP} - \Delta \text{Other operating liabilities} \]

where D&A is depreciation and amortisation (including ROU depreciation), non-cash items include provisions, unrealised FX, deferred tax and share-based payment expense, cash capex is from Topic 4 c06, \(\Delta \text{Debt}\) is net borrowings (term loans, revolver, lease principal), and equity covers share issues/buybacks. An increase in an operating asset is a use of cash (negative); an increase in an operating liability is a source (positive).

Explanation

The bridge reverses accrual accounting: start from profit, add back items that reduced profit without using cash, and adjust for balance sheet changes that used or released cash. Ind AS 7 presentation details:

  • Ind AS 7.20 (indirect method) starts from profit or loss; Indian practice, consistent with Schedule III-style formats, usually starts from profit before tax, adds back finance cost and deducts interest income (reclassified to financing/investing), then deducts income taxes paid (not tax expense) as a separate operating line. Starting from PAT is equivalent if deferred tax and the tax payable movement are included as adjustments.
  • Interest paid and dividends paid: financing (common for non-financial companies) — Ind AS 7.33–34 permit choices; be consistent.
  • Lease payments: principal in financing, interest per interest-paid policy (Ind AS 116 and Ind AS 7.17(e)).
  • Non-cash investing/financing (ROU additions, conversion of debt) excluded and disclosed (Ind AS 7.43).

In the budget, the indirect CFS is generated from P&L and balance sheet movements, and must equal the change in balance sheet cash (s05). It should also agree with the direct cash budget built from receipts and payments.

Opening cash ₹9.0 crore → closing ₹8.0 crore, which must equal balance sheet cash. If the tax payable increased by ₹2 crore (tax expense > tax paid), that ₹2 crore must appear in CFO (or in the PBT-based format, tax paid shown separately) — omitting it is a common reason the cash tie fails.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the bridge [L1]

PBT ₹50 crore; finance cost ₹6 crore (all paid); depreciation ₹12 crore; tax expense ₹12.6 crore, tax paid ₹11 crore; receivables +₹5 crore; inventory −₹2 crore; payables +₹3 crore; cash capex ₹20 crore; loan repaid ₹8 crore; dividend paid ₹7 crore. Prepare the budgeted cash flow statement in Ind AS 7 format starting from PBT, with interest paid in financing.

Q2. PAT-based vs PBT-based formats [L2]

Using Q1's data, rebuild the bridge starting from PAT and show that both formats give the same net change in cash. Identify every adjustment that differs between the two formats.

💡 Hint: The difference lies in how tax and interest are handled between the operating and financing sections.

Q3. Cash tie failure [L2]

The budgeted indirect CFS shows closing cash ₹14.6 crore; the balance sheet shows ₹11.2 crore. Movements omitted could include: capex creditors, dividend payable, interest accrued, GST payable, advance tax. Explain how each would cause a difference and the direction of each.
