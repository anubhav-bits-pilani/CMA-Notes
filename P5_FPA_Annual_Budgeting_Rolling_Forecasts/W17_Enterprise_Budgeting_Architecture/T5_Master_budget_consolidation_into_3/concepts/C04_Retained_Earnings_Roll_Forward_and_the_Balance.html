Key concept

Retained Earnings Roll-Forward and the Balance Check

Concept: Retained earnings_close = Opening + PAT − Dividends; Balance check = Total assets − (Equity + Liabilities) = 0

Statement

\[ \text{RE}^{c}_t = \text{RE}^{o}_t + \text{PAT}_t - \text{Div}_t \pm \text{Other}_t, \qquad \text{RE}^{o}_t = \text{RE}^{c}_{t-1} \]

\[ \text{Check}_t = \text{TA}_t - (\text{TE}_t + \text{TL}_t) = 0 \]

where \(\text{RE}\) is retained earnings (within "Other equity" under Schedule III), \(\text{PAT}_t\) profit after tax attributable to owners for the period, \(\text{Div}_t\) dividends declared (recognised as a liability when approved — final dividends at the AGM, interim when declared by the Board; Ind AS 10.12–13), \(\text{Other}_t\) transfers to/from reserves and items of OCI recognised directly in retained earnings (e.g. remeasurements of defined benefit plans, by policy), \(\text{TA}\) total assets, \(\text{TE}\) total equity and \(\text{TL}\) total liabilities.

Explanation

The RE roll-forward is the link from the P&L to the balance sheet: if PAT does not reach equity, nothing else will balance. Dividends reduce RE when declared, not when paid; between declaration and payment they sit as "dividend payable" (other financial liability). Under Ind AS 10, dividends proposed after the reporting date are not a liability at that date — so the FY 2025-26 final dividend, declared at the AGM in August 2026, reduces RE in the FY 2026-27 budget in August.

The balance check is the model's master test. It holds only if every transaction has been recorded with equal debits and credits across the three statements: each P&L item reaches RE, each cash movement reaches cash, and each non-cash item has two balance sheet legs. Practical rules:

  • Compute the check every month including the opening balance sheet.
  • Use a tolerance for rounding and display a master flag on every output.
  • Never use cash, "other current assets" or "suspense" as a plug — the check must be independent.
  • For consolidated budgets, include non-controlling interest (NCI) — PAT attributable to NCI goes to NCI, not RE — and FCTR on foreign subsidiaries.

Illustration

FY 2026-27 budget, ₹ crore: opening RE 412.0; PAT 96.5; final dividend for FY 2025-26 of ₹8 per share on 3 crore shares declared August 2026, paid September 2026; interim dividend ₹5 per share declared February 2027, paid March 2027; actuarial loss on gratuity (OCI, net of tax) 1.2 recognised in RE by policy.

\[ \text{RE}^{c} = 412.0 + 96.5 - 24.0 - 15.0 - 1.2 = ₹468.3 \text{ crore} \]

At 31 August 2026 the balance sheet carries a dividend payable of ₹24 crore; if the model reduces cash in August instead (payment month wrong), the check still balances but August cash is understated by ₹24 crore — the balance check does not catch timing errors, only one-sided entries. A separate dividend-payable corkscrew catches them.

RE close  =RE_Open + PAT - DivDeclared + OCI_to_RE
Check     =ROUND(TotalAssets - TotalEquity - TotalLiabilities, 3)
Master    =IF(MAX(ABS(CheckRow))<0.001, "OK", "ERROR")

Practice Questions

Q1. RE roll-forward [L1]

Opening RE ₹180 crore; budgeted PAT ₹42 crore; final dividend of ₹3 per share on 5 crore shares declared at the AGM in September; transfer of ₹5 crore to general reserve. Compute closing RE and state the balance sheet lines affected by the dividend in September and October if it is paid on 10 October.

Q2. Using the check to find errors [L2]

A model's balance check is zero every month, but the CFO notes that equity rises by ₹2 crore more than PAT less dividends. List the possible causes and the checks you would add to detect each.

💡 Hint: A balanced balance sheet can still contain two offsetting errors; think about other equity components.

Q3. Consolidated RE with NCI [L2]

A parent owns 80% of a subsidiary. Budgeted group PAT is ₹60 crore, of which the subsidiary contributes ₹15 crore. The subsidiary pays a dividend of ₹5 crore. Compute the movement in group RE and NCI, and explain how the intra-group portion of the dividend is treated.
