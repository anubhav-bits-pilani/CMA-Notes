Key concept

Circuit Breaker Switch for Interest Circularity

Concept: Circularity break: =IF(CircSwitch=1, Interest_calc, 0) with iterative calculation enabled

Statement

Interest_t = IF(CircSwitch = 1, Rate/12 * AVERAGE(Debt_open_t, Debt_close_t), 0)

Mathematically, with switch value \(s \in \{0, 1\}\):

\[ I_t = s \times r_m \times \frac{B_{t-1} + B_t}{2} \]

where \(I_t\) is interest for month \(t\), \(r_m\) the monthly rate, \(B_{t-1}\) and \(B_t\) the opening and closing balances (closing depends on cash, which depends on \(I_t\)), and \(s\) the named switch CircSwitch. Excel's iterative calculation (File → Options → Formulas → Enable iterative calculation; maximum iterations e.g. 100; maximum change e.g. 0.001) must be enabled for \(s = 1\) to resolve. A safer variant falls back to opening-balance interest when off: \(I_t = r_m \left[s \frac{B_{t-1}+B_t}{2} + (1-s) B_{t-1}\right]\).

Explanation

Circularity is intentional when interest depends on balances that depend on cash. Iteration solves it by repeated recalculation until changes fall below the tolerance. The circuit breaker exists because iterative loops are fragile: if any cell in the loop evaluates to an error (#DIV/0!, #VALUE!), the error feeds back into itself and persists even after the original cause is fixed. Setting CircSwitch to 0 removes interest from the loop (or replaces it with non-circular opening-balance interest), the error clears, and the switch can be turned back to 1.

Best practice:

  • One named switch on the assumptions sheet, used in every circular formula — not scattered hard-coded 1s.
  • Fallback to opening-balance interest rather than zero, so the model gives a sensible answer with the switch off (zero interest overstates PAT and cash).
  • Keep the loop small: only revolver interest and interest income; tax follows automatically.
  • Status indicator on outputs: "Circularity ON/OFF".
  • Check for unintended circularities: with iteration enabled Excel no longer warns; periodically switch iteration off and inspect the circular reference warnings, or use Formulas → Error Checking → Circular References.
  • Model governance: document the switch in the model map; in FAST-style standards circularity is discouraged, and copy-paste macros or opening-balance interest may be preferred.

Illustration

A monthly budget model's revolver interest in row 45, opening balance row 40, closing balance row 44:

=IF(CircSwitch=1, RevRate/12*AVERAGE(F40,F44), RevRate/12*F40)

During a review, an input error sets days in month to zero, producing #DIV/0! in the working capital block, which flows into cash, the revolver draw and interest. After correcting the input, the errors remain. Setting CircSwitch = 0 breaks the loop: interest becomes opening-balance based, errors clear on recalculation; resetting to 1 restores the iterated result. With an opening revolver of ₹30 crore, a month's draw of ₹18 crore and 10.8% p.a., interest is ₹0.270 crore with the switch off and ≈ ₹0.351 crore with it on — the difference quantifies the precision gained.

Practice Questions

Q1. Writing the switch formulas [L1]

Write Excel formulas for revolver interest and interest income on cash that use a named CircSwitch, average balances when on and opening balances when off. State the iterative calculation settings you would use.

Q2. Quantifying the switch [L2]

Revolver opening ₹50 crore, pre-interest deficit for the month ₹25 crore, 12% p.a., interest paid monthly, ignore tax. Compute interest with the switch off (opening balance) and on (average balance, solved algebraically or by iteration), and the closing revolver in each case.

💡 Hint: With the switch on, the draw must also fund its own interest.

Q3. Review finding [L2]

A model uses =IF(CircSwitch=1, Interest_calc, 0) and the CFO's pack was produced with the switch accidentally at 0. Explain the impact on PBT, tax, cash, revolver and covenant ratios, and propose two model controls to prevent this.
