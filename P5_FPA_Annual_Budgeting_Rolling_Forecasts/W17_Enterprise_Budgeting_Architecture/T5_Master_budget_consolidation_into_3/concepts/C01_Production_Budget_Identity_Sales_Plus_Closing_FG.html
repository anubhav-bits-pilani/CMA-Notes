Key concept

Production Budget Identity: Sales Plus Closing FG Minus Opening FG

Concept: Production units = Budgeted sales + Desired closing FG − Opening FG

Statement

\[ P_t = S_t + \text{FG}^{c}_t - \text{FG}^{o}_t, \qquad \text{FG}^{o}_t = \text{FG}^{c}_{t-1} \]

where \(P_t\) is units to be produced in period \(t\), \(S_t\) budgeted sales units, \(\text{FG}^{c}_t\) desired closing finished goods inventory (units), and \(\text{FG}^{o}_t\) opening finished goods inventory. Desired closing FG is normally a policy: \(\text{FG}^{c}_t = k \times S_{t+1}\) (forward cover as a fraction \(k\) of next period's sales) or days of cover \(\times\) next period's daily sales. Where goods require further processing, add the change in WIP equivalent units: \(P_t = S_t + \Delta\text{FG}_t + \Delta\text{WIP}^{\text{eq}}_t\).

Explanation

The identity is a units corkscrew: what you sell plus what you want to hold must come from what you had plus what you make. It converts a demand plan into a supply plan. Three conditions make it operational:

  • Policy-driven closing stock. Forward cover links inventory to future demand, so the production plan automatically pre-builds ahead of a seasonal peak.
  • Capacity check. \(P_t \le \text{Capacity}_t\). If violated, either pre-build in earlier months (raising earlier closing FG beyond policy), outsource, or reduce sales — the principal budget factor becomes capacity.
  • Shelf-life and storage limits. Pre-building is limited by expiry dates and warehouse capacity, common in food and personal care.

Units first, values second: valuing production at standard cost gives the cost of production; COGS = opening FG value + cost of production − closing FG value. Under Ind AS 2.13, fixed overhead absorption is based on normal capacity — so the production quantity also determines under-/over-absorption in the P&L.

Excel, with sales in row 10, cover k in a named cell and opening FG in row 12:

=F10 + G10*FGCover - F12

with F13 = G10*FGCover as closing FG and G12 = F13 linking next month's opening.

Illustration

Sales (lakh units): Jul 8, Aug 10, Sep 14, Oct 18, Nov 9. Cover = 50% of next month's sales; opening FG on 1 July = 4 lakh units; capacity 13 lakh units per month.

Lakh units Jul Aug Sep Oct
Sales 8 10 14 18
Closing FG (50% of next) 5 7 9 4.5
Opening FG 4 5 7 9
Production 9 12 16 13.5

September (16) and October (13.5) exceed capacity of 13. A levelled plan produces 13 in July–October (52 total vs 50.5 required), building closing FG higher in July and August: e.g. Jul closing 9, Aug 12, Sep 11, Oct 6 — within warehouse limits if those allow 12 lakh units. The cash budget then sees RM purchases and inventory funding earlier.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic production budget [L1]

Sales (units): Q1 1,20,000, Q2 1,50,000, Q3 2,10,000, Q4 1,40,000, next Q1 1,30,000. Closing FG = 20% of next quarter's sales; opening FG for Q1 = 22,000 units. Prepare the quarterly production budget.

Q2. Levelling under capacity [L2]

Using the illustration's sales, cover policy and a capacity of 12.5 lakh units per month, with a maximum FG storage of 10 lakh units, prepare a feasible July–October production plan and state any unmet demand.

💡 Hint: Work backwards from the peak month to determine the latest pre-build needed.

Q3. WIP equivalent units [L2]

A company sells 50,000 units in a month, wants closing FG of 8,000 (opening 6,000), and holds WIP of 4,000 units 50% complete at the start and 6,000 units 40% complete at the end. Compute equivalent units of production for the month and explain how this changes the conversion cost budget.
