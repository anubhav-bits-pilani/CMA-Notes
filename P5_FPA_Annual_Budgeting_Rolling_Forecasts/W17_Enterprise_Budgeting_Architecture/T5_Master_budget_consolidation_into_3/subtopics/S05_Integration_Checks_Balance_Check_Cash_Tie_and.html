Subtopic note

Integration Checks: Balance Check, Cash Tie and Retained Earnings

Scope: Integration checks: balance sheet balances, cash ties between CF and BS, retained earnings roll-forward

Why it matters

A budget model that does not balance cannot be trusted for anything — not the cash peak, not the covenant test, not the EPS guidance. The integration checks are the proof that the P&L, balance sheet and cash flow have been built from the same transactions. In model reviews (internal, auditors of lender models, PE due diligence), the first thing a reviewer looks at is the check sheet. In interviews, candidates are often asked "your balance sheet doesn't balance by ₹3 crore — how do you find it?" A systematic answer, grounded in how the checks are designed, is expected.

Core notes

Check 1: Balance sheet balances

\[ \text{Check}_t = \text{Total assets}_t - (\text{Total equity}_t + \text{Total liabilities}_t) = 0 \]

Computed every month, including the opening balance sheet. Use a tolerance for rounding (e.g. ABS(x) < 0.001 in ₹ crore). Display a single master flag: =IF(MAX(ABS(CheckRow))<Tol, "OK", "ERROR").

Check 2: Cash ties between CF and BS

\[ \text{Closing cash}^{\text{CFS}}_t = \text{Opening cash}_t + \text{CFO}_t + \text{CFI}_t + \text{CFF}_t = \text{Cash}^{\text{BS}}_t \]

If the balance sheet cash line is linked directly from the cash flow statement, this check is trivially true and the real test moves to Check 1. A better design keeps three independent cash computations and ties them:

  • cash per direct cash budget (receipts − payments);
  • cash per indirect cash flow statement (PAT + adjustments ± working capital − capex ± financing);
  • cash on the balance sheet.

Direct and indirect must agree each month. Also check definitional consistency: if CC/OD is included in cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7.8), the CFS cash and the BS cash line must use the same definition — reconcile in a note.

Check 3: Retained earnings roll-forward

\[ \text{RE}_t = \text{RE}_{t-1} + \text{PAT}_t - \text{Dividends}_t \pm \text{Transfers}_t \pm \text{OCI reclassified}_t \]

Transfers include amounts to/from other reserves (e.g. debenture redemption reserve where still applicable, general reserve) and items such as remeasurement of defined benefit plans if the company presents them within retained earnings via OCI. The check confirms that P&L profit actually reaches the balance sheet and that dividends are not double-counted.

Supporting corkscrew checks

Check Rule
PPE roll-forward Opening + capitalised − depreciation − disposals (NBV) = closing
CWIP roll-forward Opening + additions − capitalised = closing
Debt roll-forward Opening + draws − repayments ± FX = closing; ties to Ind AS 7.44A note
Tax payable Opening + current tax − tax paid = closing
Deferred tax Opening + DT expense ± OCI DT = closing
Working capital ΔAR, ΔInv, ΔAP in CFS = BS movements
Revolver Balance ≤ min(limit, DP); cash ≥ minimum
Totals Sum of departmental loads = consolidated P&L lines
Signs Revenue positive, costs negative (or by convention); no negative inventory or receivables

Designing the check sheet

  • One Checks tab listing every check, its formula, the monthly result row, and a master flag.
  • A master check cell displayed on every output tab (e.g. top-right "Model check: OK").
  • Checks must be independent — computed from source lines, not from the same subtotal they test.
  • Never "fix" a check by hard-coding an adjustment; a plug defeats the purpose.

Finding an imbalance — systematic approach

  1. When does it start? Find the first month the check is non-zero. If it is the opening month, the opening balance sheet is wrong (often an LE that itself doesn't balance or an unmapped TB line).
  2. Is it constant or growing? A constant difference from month 1 suggests an opening or one-off item; a growing difference suggests a flow missing from one statement every month.
  3. Is the difference equal to a known number? Compare the imbalance with candidate lines: depreciation, interest, tax, dividend, capex creditors movement, GST balance. Half or double a line suggests a sign error.
  4. Run the cash tie. If BS cash ≠ CFS cash, the error is in the CFS (a BS movement not captured). If they tie but the BS doesn't balance, a P&L item is missing from RE or a BS item has no cash or P&L counterpart.
  5. Test every corkscrew individually.
  6. Check signs of working capital movements (increase in asset = cash outflow).

Illustration

The model balances until September. The balance check (assets − equity − liabilities) is −₹0.70 crore in October (month 7) and grows by ₹0.70 crore every month to −₹4.20 crore in March. The cash tie (CFS vs BS) holds. A new ₹90 crore term loan was drawn on 1 October at 9.33%, so interest is ₹0.70 crore per month.

Reasoning through the pattern:

Observation Inference
Starts in October Linked to something new in October — the term loan
Grows by a constant ₹0.70 crore A monthly flow is missing from one statement — equal to monthly interest
Negative sign (assets too low) Cash has fallen without a matching fall in equity or rise in liabilities
Cash tie holds The CFS includes the outflow, so the gap is on the P&L/RE side

Diagnosis: the debt schedule's interest is deducted as "interest paid" in the financing section of the CFS (reducing cash) but was never linked into the P&L finance cost line, so PAT and retained earnings are overstated by ₹0.70 crore each month. Fix: link the debt schedule interest into finance cost; PAT, tax and RE then fall, and the check returns to zero (the tax effect also flows through the tax payable corkscrew).

The lesson: start month, a monthly increment equal to a known line, the sign, and the cash-tie result together point directly to the missing link.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Rounding: summing rounded figures in output tabs creates small differences; compute in full precision and round only for display.
  • Circularity: iterative calculation can leave tiny residuals; set tolerance accordingly and ensure the circularity switch (s06) doesn't break checks when off.
  • FX translation: for foreign subsidiaries, translation differences go to OCI (FCTR); omit them and the consolidated BS will not balance.
  • Non-cash transactions (ROU additions, share-based payments, conversion of debt to equity) need both BS legs and exclusion from cash flows.
  • Plugs disguised as "other current assets" — a line that moves by exactly the imbalance amount is a plug.
  • Opening balance sheet from LE: if the LE itself does not balance (e.g. P&L forecast without BS forecast), the budget inherits the error — insist on a full LE balance sheet.

Key takeaways

  • Three core checks: balance sheet balances, cash ties between CFS and BS (and direct vs indirect), retained earnings rolls forward.
  • Support them with corkscrew checks for every BS line and a master flag on every output.
  • Checks must be independent of the lines they test; never plug.
  • Diagnose imbalances by start month, pattern, matching amount, cash tie result and corkscrew tests.
  • The opening balance sheet must itself balance and come from the latest estimate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Diagnosing an imbalance [L2]

A monthly budget model balances in April–August. From September, assets exceed equity and liabilities by ₹1.5 crore, rising to ₹3.0 crore in October and staying at ₹3.0 crore thereafter. The cash tie holds. In September and October, two dividends of ₹1.5 crore each were paid. Identify the likely error, explain your reasoning, and state the fix.

💡 Hint: Consider where dividends must appear: cash flow, retained earnings, or both.

Q2. Building a check sheet [L2]

List the checks you would build into a 12-month budget model with PPE, CWIP, term loans, a revolver, deferred tax and working capital. For each, write the Excel test formula in words and the tolerance.

Q3. Direct vs indirect reconciliation [L2]

The direct cash budget shows operating cash flow of ₹62 crore for the year; the indirect CFS shows ₹58.5 crore. Opening and closing cash agree in both. List five possible reasons, and describe how you would locate the difference month by month.

Q4. Interview: "Just plug cash" [L3]

A senior manager suggests making the balance sheet balance by letting cash be the balancing figure "because cash is a residual anyway". Explain why this is wrong, what it hides, and how the correct model structure makes cash a computed result rather than a plug.
