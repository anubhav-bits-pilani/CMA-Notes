Subtopic note

Budgeting Current and Deferred Tax at 25.17% under Sec 115BAA

Scope: Tax provisioning (current tax on budgeted PBT at 25.17% under Sec 115BAA, deferred tax) in the budget

Why it matters

Tax is often the largest single line between PBT and PAT, and the budgeted PAT drives EPS guidance, dividend capacity and retained earnings. A budget that applies a flat rate to PBT will be wrong whenever there are disallowances, timing differences, MAT credits from the old regime or losses. Tax also has its own cash calendar (advance tax, Topic 4 c04) and balance sheet lines (current tax liability/asset, deferred tax). FP&A must budget the tax expense, the tax cash and the tax balances consistently — and know which regime the company is in.

Core notes

The Sec 115BAA regime

Sec 115BAA (Income-tax Act 1961, from AY 2020-21) allows a domestic company to pay tax at 22% plus surcharge of 10% and health and education cess of 4%, regardless of income level:

\[ 22\% \times 1.10 \times 1.04 = 25.168\% \approx 25.17\% \]

Conditions: the company forgoes specified deductions and incentives (e.g. Sec 10AA SEZ deduction, additional depreciation under Sec 32(1)(iia), Sec 35(2AB) weighted R&D deduction, Chapter VI-A deductions other than Sec 80JJAA and 80M), cannot set off losses/unabsorbed depreciation attributable to those deductions, and MAT under Sec 115JB does not apply. Unutilised MAT credit is lost on opting in. The option, once exercised, cannot be withdrawn. Companies with large MAT credit or SEZ benefits sometimes stayed in the old regime (base rate 30% or 25% for smaller companies, plus surcharge 7%/12% above ₹1 crore/₹10 crore of income, and cess) until credits were used.

The Income-tax Act 2025, effective 1 April 2026, replaces the 1961 Act with a "tax year" concept and renumbered provisions; the concessional regime and its rate continue in substance. Reference the current section numbers in the AOP tax memo.

Current tax computation in the budget

\[ \text{Current tax} = \left(\text{PBT} + \text{Permanent disallowances} - \text{Exempt income} \pm \text{Timing differences}\right) \times 25.168\% \]

Typical adjustments for an Indian manufacturing/FMCG company:

Item Type Direction (taxable vs book)
CSR expenditure (Sec 135 Companies Act) Permanent (Explanation 2 to Sec 37(1)) Add back
Donations (Sec 80G deduction not available under 115BAA) Permanent Add back
Interest/penalties on tax, Sec 40(a) disallowances Permanent Add back
Book depreciation vs tax depreciation (block WDV) Timing Add book, deduct tax
Gratuity and leave encashment provisions (Sec 43B — allowed on payment) Timing Add provision, deduct payments
Bonus (Sec 43B, allowed if paid by return due date) Timing Usually nil if paid
MSME dues beyond 45 days (Sec 43B(h)) Timing Add back, deduct when paid
ECL/doubtful debt provisions (allowed on write-off) Timing Add provision, deduct write-offs
Ind AS 116 (lease depreciation + interest vs rent paid) Timing Add book charges, deduct rent
Fair value gains on liquid funds (taxed on redemption) Timing Deduct unrealised gains
Dividend income from Indian companies Taxable, with Sec 80M deduction for onward distribution —

Deferred tax

Under Ind AS 12, deferred tax is recognised on temporary differences between carrying amounts and tax bases of assets and liabilities (balance sheet approach):

\[ \text{DTL (DTA)} = (\text{Carrying amount} - \text{Tax base}) \times \text{Enacted/substantively enacted rate} \]

For the budget:

  • PPE: project book NBV (from the capex schedule) and tax WDV (block-wise, 15% for plant and machinery, 10% for buildings, 40% for computers, half rate for assets used < 180 days in the year). DTL = (Book NBV − Tax WDV) × 25.168%.
  • Provisions for employee benefits, ECL, MSME disallowance: DTA = carrying amount of provision × rate (to the extent future deductible).
  • Lease liabilities and ROU assets: recognise DTA and DTL on lease liability and ROU asset separately (following the 2023 amendment to Ind AS 12 on deferred tax related to assets and liabilities arising from a single transaction, applicable in India from 1 April 2023).
  • Deferred tax expense = closing net DTL − opening net DTL (excluding OCI items such as actuarial gains, which go to OCI).

Ind AS 12.47 uses rates expected to apply when the asset is realised, based on enacted or substantively enacted rates. A change in regime (e.g. opting into 115BAA) requires remeasurement of deferred tax — budget the one-off.

Total tax expense and effective tax rate

\[ \text{Tax expense} = \text{Current tax} + \text{Deferred tax}, \qquad \text{ETR} = \frac{\text{Tax expense}}{\text{PBT}} \]

Under 115BAA, ETR ≈ 25.17% plus the effect of permanent differences only; timing differences change the split between current and deferred, not the total. A budget ETR materially different from 25.17% must be explained in the tax reconciliation (Ind AS 12.81(c)).

Balance sheet and cash

  • Current tax liability = opening + current tax − advance tax/TDS − self-assessment tax paid (net presentation under Ind AS 12.71 where conditions met).
  • Cash: advance tax instalments (15 Jun/Sep/Dec/Mar), self-assessment tax for the previous year paid before filing the return (due date generally 31 October for companies, 30 November where transfer pricing reports apply), and refunds of earlier years.

Illustration

FY 2026-27 budget (₹ crore): PBT 150.0; CSR 2.8; book depreciation 24.0; tax depreciation 31.0; gratuity provision 3.0, payments 1.2; ECL provision 1.5, write-offs 0.5.

Computation ₹ crore
PBT 150.0
Add: CSR (permanent) 2.8
Add: book depreciation 24.0
Less: tax depreciation (31.0)
Add: gratuity provision − payments 1.8
Add: ECL provision − write-offs 1.0
Taxable income 148.6
Current tax @ 25.168% 37.40
Deferred tax: DTL increase on PPE (7.0 × 25.168%) 1.76
Deferred tax: DTA increase on provisions (2.8 × 25.168%) (0.70)
Total tax expense 38.46
ETR 25.64%

ETR exceeds 25.17% by the CSR effect (2.8 × 25.168% = 0.70 ÷ 150 = 0.47%). Monthly: accrue current and deferred tax on monthly PBT at the annual ETR (Ind AS 34 approach for interim periods), true-up at quarter-ends.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Old regime companies with MAT: tax = higher of normal tax and MAT (15% of book profit plus surcharge and cess); MAT credit recognised as an asset only if convincing evidence of utilisation.
  • Losses: DTA on unused losses only to the extent probable future taxable profit (Ind AS 12.34–36); budgets showing a return to profit may support recognition, but auditors need more than the budget.
  • Group budgets: each Indian entity is taxed separately; no group relief. Foreign subsidiaries at local rates; consider undistributed profits DTL (Ind AS 12.39 exception if parent controls timing).
  • Uncertain tax positions (Appendix C to Ind AS 12) — budget provisions for disputed items where probable that the authority will not accept the treatment.
  • Interest under 234B/234C is finance cost or tax-related expense per policy — not part of income tax expense in many policies.
  • Self-assessment tax timing: for companies the return due date is generally 31 October (30 November for transfer pricing cases); check the current notified dates under the new Act.

Key takeaways

  • 115BAA effective rate: 22% × 1.10 × 1.04 = 25.168%; no MAT; specified deductions forgone.
  • Current tax = (PBT ± permanent ± timing differences) × rate; never just PBT × 25.17%.
  • Deferred tax via balance sheet temporary differences — PPE (book NBV vs tax WDV), provisions, leases.
  • Timing differences shift current vs deferred; permanent differences move ETR.
  • Budget tax expense monthly at the annual ETR, tax cash per advance tax calendar, tax balances via corkscrew.

Practice Questions

Q1. Current tax and ETR [L1]

Budgeted PBT ₹90 crore; CSR ₹1.6 crore; penalty for regulatory non-compliance ₹0.4 crore; book depreciation ₹15 crore; tax depreciation ₹18 crore; leave encashment provision ₹2.0 crore, payments ₹1.4 crore. Compute taxable income, current tax at 25.168%, deferred tax and ETR.

Q2. Deferred tax on PPE in the budget [L2]

Opening: book NBV of plant ₹120 crore, tax WDV ₹92 crore. FY 2026-27 additions ₹30 crore, put to use in December (book: 15 years, 5% residual, from December; tax: 15% WDV block, half rate for < 180 days). Book depreciation on opening assets ₹9 crore. Compute closing book NBV, closing tax WDV, closing DTL and the deferred tax charge for the year.

💡 Hint: Apply the tax half-rate rule to the addition only; the opening block takes the full rate.

Q3. Regime choice [L3]

A company in the old regime has MAT credit of ₹22 crore, expected taxable income of ₹80 crore per year for the next 4 years, and book profit of ₹85 crore per year. Compare the tax cash outflow over 4 years under staying in the old regime (30% base rate plus 12% surcharge and 4% cess, MAT credit utilisation permitted up to the excess of normal tax over MAT) versus opting for 115BAA now, and set out the qualitative factors.

Q4. Monthly tax accrual and cash [L2]

Budgeted PBT by quarter is ₹20, ₹25, ₹45 and ₹30 crore; ETR 25.6%; current tax is 92% of total tax expense. Prepare quarterly tax expense, advance tax payments (15/45/75/100% of current tax), and closing current tax liability/(asset) at each quarter-end, assuming no TDS and opening balance nil.
