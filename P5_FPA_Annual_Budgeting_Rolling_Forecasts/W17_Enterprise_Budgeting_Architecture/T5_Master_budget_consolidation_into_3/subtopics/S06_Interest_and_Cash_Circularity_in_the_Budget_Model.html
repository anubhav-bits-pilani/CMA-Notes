Interest and Cash Circularity in the Budget Model

Scope: Interest and cash circularity handling (average balance with iteration toggle vs opening-balance interest)

Why it matters

In an integrated budget, interest depends on the debt balance; the debt balance (via the revolver) depends on cash; cash depends on interest paid and on tax, which depends on interest. This loop is a circular reference. Handled badly, it produces models that crash, give different answers on each recalculation, or propagate #REF!/#NUM! errors that cannot be cleared. Handled well, it is a controlled, switchable feature. Every FP&A and modelling interview that touches 3-statement work asks about circularity, and model reviewers judge a modeller partly on how it is handled.

Core notes

Where the circularity comes from

\[ \text{Interest}_t = r \times \overline{B}_t, \quad \overline{B}_t = \frac{B_{t-1} + B_t}{2}, \quad B_t = B_{t-1} + D_t(\text{Cash}_t), \quad \text{Cash}_t = f(\text{Interest}_t, \text{Tax}_t(\text{Interest}_t)) \]

Using the closing or average balance creates the loop because the closing balance depends on this period's draw, which depends on this period's cash, which depends on this period's interest. The same applies to interest income on average cash balances.

Option 1: Opening-balance interest (no circularity)

\[ \text{Interest}_t = r \times B_{t-1} \]

Interest depends only on last period's closing balance, which is already known — no loop. Accuracy loss is small in monthly models because the balance changes little within a month; it is larger in annual models with large draws or repayments. Many corporate budget models use this approach for simplicity and robustness, sometimes with a refinement for known scheduled movements (e.g. a term loan repaid mid-month: \(r \times (B_{t-1} - \text{Repayment}_t \times \text{fraction of month after repayment})\)) — still non-circular because scheduled flows don't depend on cash.

Option 2: Average-balance interest with iteration and a circuit breaker

Enable iterative calculation (Excel: File → Options → Formulas → Enable iterative calculation; e.g. maximum iterations 100, maximum change 0.001) and include a circularity switch (circuit breaker):

=IF(CircSwitch=1, InterestRate/12 * AVERAGE(F20, E20), 0)

or, preferably, fall back to opening-balance interest when off so the model remains meaningful:

=IF(CircSwitch=1, InterestRate/12*AVERAGE(E20,F20), InterestRate/12*E20)

where row 20 holds the revolver balance (E = prior month closing, F = current month closing). Why the switch matters: if an error (e.g. #DIV/0!) enters the loop, iteration propagates it around the loop permanently; setting the switch to 0 breaks the loop, lets the error clear, and the switch can be set back to 1.

Practices:

  • One switch, a named cell (CircSwitch) on the assumptions sheet, clearly labelled.
  • Keep the loop small — only interest on the revolver/cash should be circular; avoid looping through fees, covenants or dividends.
  • Add a check that model results are stable (e.g. interest with switch on vs a copy-paste-value iteration differs by < tolerance).
  • Document the circularity in the model map.

Option 3: Copy-paste macro or algebraic solution

  • Copy-paste macro: compute interest from a pasted "prior iteration" value; a VBA loop repeats paste until convergence. No iterative calculation setting needed; used in banking models, but requires macros and discipline.
  • Algebraic closed form: for simple structures, solve the loop analytically. If the revolver must cover a pre-interest deficit \(X\) with interest on the average balance and the opening revolver \(B_0\):

\[ D = X + r_m \frac{B_0 + B_0 + D}{2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad D = \frac{X + r_m B_0}{1 - r_m/2} \]

where \(r_m\) is the monthly rate (ignoring tax effects). Precise but brittle when structure changes.

Choosing for a budget model

Criterion Opening-balance Average + iteration Macro / algebraic
Accuracy (monthly) Good Best Best
Robustness Highest Medium (needs breaker) Medium
Transparency for business users High Medium Low
Typical use Corporate AOP models, EPM Transaction / lender models, FP&A where precision matters Banking, project finance

For a monthly AOP, opening-balance interest is usually adequate; for a large, volatile revolver (festive working capital peaks) average-balance interest improves accuracy of finance cost by a meaningful amount — test both and decide.

Illustration

Revolver opening ₹40 crore; pre-interest cash deficit in the month ₹20 crore; rate 9.6% p.a. (0.8% per month); interest paid monthly; ignore tax.

  • Opening-balance interest: \(40 \times 0.8\% = ₹0.320\) crore; draw = 20 + 0.32 = ₹20.32 crore; closing ₹60.32 crore.
  • Average-balance (iterated): \(D = \frac{20 + 0.008 \times 40}{1 - 0.004} = \frac{20.32}{0.996} = ₹20.402\) crore; closing ₹60.402 crore; interest = 0.008 × (40 + 60.402)/2 = ₹0.402 crore.

Difference ₹0.08 crore for the month. Across a festive build-up (August–November) with large draws the cumulative difference may be ₹0.3–0.5 crore — material for a tight interest coverage covenant, immaterial for a ₹1,000 crore P&L.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Interest income on cash: also circular if computed on average cash; usually use opening cash.
  • Tax shield: interest affects tax, which affects cash — the loop includes tax. Keep tax calculation in the loop small and monthly.
  • Commitment fees on undrawn limits depend on the drawn balance; same treatment as interest.
  • Ind AS 23 capitalised interest depends on qualifying expenditure, which may itself depend on cash timing — avoid linking it to the revolver loop; compute on scheduled project spend.
  • Model performance: iterative calculation slows large models and can mask unintentional circular references elsewhere (Excel stops warning). Use the Error Checking → Circular References tool regularly to confirm only the intended loop exists.
  • EPM tools (Anaplan, Oracle EPBCS) generally do not allow circular references; budget interest there on opening balances or via sequenced calculations.

Key takeaways

  • Average- or closing-balance interest on a cash-driven revolver creates circularity through cash and tax.
  • Opening-balance interest removes it with minor loss of accuracy in monthly models.
  • If iterating, use a named circuit-breaker switch with a sensible fallback, keep the loop small and document it.
  • Algebraic and macro solutions exist but trade transparency for precision.
  • Choose by materiality: test the finance cost difference between methods on the budget's peak months.

Practice Questions

Q1. Opening vs average interest [L1]

Revolver opening ₹25 crore; this month's pre-interest cash deficit ₹15 crore; rate 12% p.a.; interest paid in the month; ignore tax. Compute the draw, closing balance and interest under opening-balance interest and under average-balance interest (closed form).

Q2. Circuit breaker design [L2]

Write the Excel formulas for revolver draw, revolver closing balance and revolver interest in a monthly model with a named CircSwitch, minimum cash MinCash, limit Limit, and annual rate RevRate, such that switching off circularity falls back to opening-balance interest. Explain what happens to the model if a #DIV/0! enters the loop with the switch on, and how to recover.

💡 Hint: Interest feeds pre-financing cash, which feeds the draw; place the switch inside the interest formula only.

Q3. Materiality test [L2]

A company's revolver balances (₹ crore, month-end) are: Jul 30, Aug 55, Sep 85, Oct 120, Nov 90, Dec 50, at 10.2% p.a. Estimate annualised interest for these months using opening and average balances and comment on whether iteration is warranted given an interest coverage covenant of 3.0× with budgeted EBITDA ₹48 crore and other finance cost ₹11 crore.

Q4. Interview: unintended circularity [L3]

You inherit a budget model with iterative calculation switched on and no switch. Results change slightly every time you press F9. Describe step by step how you would find all circular references, decide which (if any) to keep, and restructure the model.
