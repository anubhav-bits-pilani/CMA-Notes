Subtopic note

The Financial Budget Chain: Capex, Cash, P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow

Scope: Financial budget chain: capex → cash budget → budgeted P&L → budgeted balance sheet → budgeted cash flow

Why it matters

The operating budget chain (s01) produces revenue, costs and volumes. The financial budget chain turns those into the three statements the Board actually approves and lenders analyse: a budgeted P&L, a budgeted balance sheet at each month- or quarter-end, and a budgeted cash flow statement. Each statement must be derived from the same underlying schedules — capex, working capital, debt, tax, equity — so that they reconcile automatically. In CMA exams the financial chain is often presented as "prepare the cash budget, then the budgeted P&L, then the budgeted balance sheet". In practice the order of calculation and the order of presentation differ, and the model must be built so that the balance sheet balances without any plug.

Order of calculation vs presentation

The textbook sequence — capex → cash budget → P&L → BS → CFS — describes dependency of thought. In a model, the calculation order is:

  1. Opening balance sheet — from the latest estimate (LE) of the current year's closing position (e.g. 9+3 forecast at 31 March 2026), not last year's audited numbers.
  2. Operating schedules — revenue, COGS, opex (s01).
  3. Capex and depreciation (Topic 4 s04–s05).
  4. Working capital closing balances and cash conversions (s03).
  5. Debt schedule with interest (circularity managed per s06).
  6. Tax on PBT (s07).
  7. P&L assembled.
  8. Cash flow — operating (indirect or direct), investing, financing — and revolver.
  9. Balance sheet — every line is a closing balance from a schedule (corkscrew), with cash from the cash flow statement.
  10. Checks — balance check, cash tie, retained earnings roll-forward (s05).

Cash is computed from flows, never as the difference that makes the balance sheet balance.

The corkscrew principle

Every balance sheet line is the closing balance of a roll-forward:

\[ \text{Closing}_t = \text{Opening}_t + \text{Increases}_t - \text{Decreases}_t, \qquad \text{Opening}_t = \text{Closing}_{t-1} \]

Examples: PPE (opening + additions − depreciation − disposals), CWIP (opening + spend − capitalised), debt (opening + draws − repayments), retained earnings (opening + PAT − dividends), provision for tax (opening + current tax − payments). Each flow in a corkscrew is either a P&L item or a cash item (or a non-cash reclass), which is what makes the three statements link.

Budgeted cash flow statement

Present in Ind AS 7 format:

  • Operating activities (indirect): PBT → adjustments (depreciation, finance cost, interest income, unrealised FX, provisions) → working capital changes → cash generated from operations → income taxes paid.
  • Investing: purchase of PPE (cash capex, Topic 4 c06), proceeds from disposals, investments in liquid funds, interest received.
  • Financing: borrowings drawn/repaid (term and revolver), interest paid, dividends paid, lease payments (principal and interest under Ind AS 116).

Ind AS 7.44A requires a reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities — build the debt corkscrew so this falls out directly.

Monthly vs annual statements

The Board approves annual numbers, but lenders and treasury need monthly balance sheets for drawing power and covenant tests; the CFO needs quarterly views for guidance and advance tax. Build monthly; aggregate to quarter and year with SUMIFS on flows and end-of-period lookups on balances (balances are not summed).

=XLOOKUP(EOMONTH(Q_End,0), Timeline, BS_Row)

Illustration: mini three-statement link (₹ crore, one year)

Opening: cash 10, PPE 100, debt 40, equity 70. During the year: PAT 18 (after depreciation 12, interest 4, tax 6), capex 30 (all paid), debt repaid 8, dividend 5, no working capital change.

  • Cash flow: CFO = 18 + 12 = 30; CFI = −30; CFF = −8 − 5 = −13; net change = −13 → closing cash = −3.

Negative cash means the plan needs funding: with a ₹5 crore minimum, the revolver draws 8, CFF becomes −5, closing cash 5. Closing BS: cash 5 + PPE 118 = 123; debt 40 − 8 + 8 = 40; equity 70 + 18 − 5 = 83; total 123. Balanced without a plug.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Interest classification (operating vs financing) and dividends paid (financing, or operating permitted for some under Ind AS 7.34) must follow the statutory policy so the budget and actual CFS are comparable.
  • Lease accounting: Ind AS 116 moves rent below EBITDA (depreciation + interest) and splits lease payments in financing; management P&Ls sometimes keep rent in opex — map both.
  • OCI items (actuarial gains on gratuity, FVOCI equity, cash flow hedge reserve) are usually budgeted at nil but must have a line in equity.
  • Dividend timing: final dividend for FY 2025-26 is declared at the AGM (by September) and paid within 30 days (Sec 127); interim dividends per Board. Dividend distribution tax no longer exists; TDS under Sec 194 is deducted from shareholders.
  • Group budgets require intercompany eliminations in all three statements, not just revenue.
  • Annual-only models hide intra-year funding peaks; always build monthly cash at minimum.

Key takeaways

  • The financial chain converts operating budgets into P&L, balance sheet and cash flow through capex, working capital, debt, tax and equity schedules.
  • Start from the latest-estimate opening balance sheet.
  • Every BS line is a corkscrew; cash comes from the cash flow statement, never a plug.
  • Present the CFS in Ind AS 7 format with policy-consistent classification and a 7.44A debt reconciliation.
  • Build monthly, report quarterly and annually; sum flows, look up balances.

Practice Questions

Q1. Linking transactions [L1]

For each event, state the effect on the P&L, balance sheet and cash flow statement: (a) ₹5 crore capex on credit; (b) payment of ₹3 crore of that creditor; (c) ₹1.2 crore interest accrued, ₹0.9 crore paid; (d) final dividend of ₹4 crore declared at the AGM in August and paid in September; (e) advance tax ₹2.5 crore paid.

Q2. Mini three-statement budget [L2]

Opening balance sheet (₹ crore): cash 8, receivables 30, inventory 25, PPE 120, payables 20, term loan 50, equity 113. Budget year: revenue 300, EBITDA margin 15%, depreciation 14, interest 10% on opening loan, tax 25.168% of PBT (paid fully in year), receivables +6, inventory +4, payables +3, capex 35, loan repayment 10, dividend 8, minimum cash 5 with a revolver (no interest on revolver for simplicity). Prepare the budgeted P&L, cash flow statement and closing balance sheet, and confirm the balance check.

💡 Hint: Compute PAT first, then CFO via the indirect method, then decide the revolver draw.

Q3. Monthly to annual aggregation [L2]

Explain how you would aggregate a monthly budget model into quarterly and annual statements in Excel, distinguishing flow lines from balance lines, and write the formulas for one of each.

Q4. Presentation choices [L3]

The management P&L of a listed FMCG company shows rent of ₹18 crore within opex, while the statutory P&L applies Ind AS 116. Design the budget so both views are produced from the same lease inputs, and describe the effect on EBITDA, finance cost, CFO and CFF.
