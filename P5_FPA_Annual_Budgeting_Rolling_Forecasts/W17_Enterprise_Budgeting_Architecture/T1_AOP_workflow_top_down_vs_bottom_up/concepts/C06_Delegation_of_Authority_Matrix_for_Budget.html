Key concept

Delegation of Authority Matrix for Budget Approvals

Concept: Delegation of Authority (DoA) matrix defines who approves which budget line and threshold

Statement

A Delegation of Authority (DoA) matrix is a Board-approved table that specifies, for each type of decision (approving a budget, a budget transfer, a new position, a capex project, a contract, a write-off), the role authorised to approve it and the monetary or other threshold up to which that role may approve:

\[ \text{Approver}(d, x) = \text{lowest role } r \text{ such that } x \le L_{r,d} \]

where \(d\) is the decision type, \(x\) its value and \(L_{r,d}\) the limit for role \(r\) on decision type \(d\). Decisions above the highest management limit go to the Board or a Board committee.

Explanation

The DoA is the backbone of internal financial controls (Sec 134(5)(e) of the Companies Act, 2013 requires directors of listed companies to state that internal financial controls are adequate and operating effectively; auditors report on IFC under Sec 143(3)(i)). In budgeting, it answers three questions: who can approve the AOP for each unit, who can approve deviations, and who can approve spend that was not budgeted.

Typical budget-related decision types:

  • AOP approval: department budget by department head; BU budget by BU head and CFO; consolidated AOP by CEO, then Board.
  • Budget transfers between lines (within a cost centre, across cost centres, across BUs), with tighter rules for transfers out of payroll or capex.
  • Unbudgeted spend: requires approval one level higher than budgeted spend of the same value.
  • New positions: budgeted positions by HR head; unbudgeted positions by CHRO + CFO.
  • Capex: appropriation requests by value band (Topic 4); unbudgeted capex always to the capex committee.
  • Commitments: contracts and purchase orders beyond the budget year.

Design principles: separation of duties (preparer ≠ approver), two-signature rules above a threshold, approval limits linked to role not person, explicit treatment of budgeted vs unbudgeted, and annual Board review. In EPM tools, the DoA is mapped to workflow (approval units and promotional paths) so the system enforces it.

Statutory overlays in India: Sec 179(3) matters reserved for the Board (e.g., borrowing, investing funds, granting loans), Sec 180 shareholder approvals for borrowing beyond limits, Sec 186 loan and investment limits, and related party transactions under Sec 188 and SEBI LODR Reg 23 (Audit Committee approval). A DoA cannot delegate what the law reserves.

A ₹70 lakh unbudgeted digital campaign request from a BU therefore requires CFO approval; a ₹70 lakh transfer from travel to training within one cost centre requires CFO approval only because it exceeds the BU head's ₹50 lakh limit.

Connections

The DoA governs the sign-off workflow in s04, the authority to close gaps in catchball (c02, s05), the lock and transfers in s07, and incentive plan approvals in s08. It is central to capex approvals in Topic 4 and to the governance cross-cutting concept of this week. Internal audit (your background) tests DoA compliance by sampling approvals against the matrix.

Practice Questions

Q1. Applying the DoA [L1]

Using the illustration matrix, identify the approver for: (a) ₹40 lakh transfer from consulting to IT within one cost centre; (b) ₹3 crore of unbudgeted capex for an effluent treatment upgrade; (c) ₹6 crore transfer of A&P from Beverages to Snacks; (d) ₹18 crore budgeted capex project.

💡 Hint: Check whether each item is budgeted or unbudgeted before reading the limit.

Q2. Designing a DoA for a mid-sized company [L2]

Design a budget DoA for a ₹800 crore revenue unlisted company with three BUs and a CFO, covering AOP approval, transfers, unbudgeted opex, new positions and capex. Explain your thresholds with reference to materiality (e.g., % of EBITDA).

Q3. Audit test of DoA compliance [L2]

As an internal auditor, design a test of DoA compliance for budget transfers: population, sample size rationale, attributes tested, and how you would report exceptions.
