Key concept

Planning Gap: Target Minus Bottom-Up Submission

Concept: Planning gap = Top-down target − Bottom-up submission; closed via initiatives, stretch, or target revision

Statement

\[ G = T - B \]

where \(G\) is the planning gap for a metric (revenue, EBITDA, cash), \(T\) is the top-down target from the target letter and \(B\) is the consolidated bottom-up submission after corrections. The gap is closed so that

\[ T' = B + I + S, \qquad T' \le T \]

where \(I\) is the value of validated initiatives (new actions with owners and drivers), \(S\) is accepted stretch (management challenge without a specific action yet) and \(T'\) is the final approved budget; if \(B + I + S < T\), the remaining \(T - T'\) is a target revision. For cost lines the sign is reversed (gap = submission − cap).

Explanation

The gap is a measurement, not a verdict. A positive revenue or EBITDA gap may mean BUs are sandbagging, or that the target was unrealistic, or both. The concept forces the question "what will we do differently to close it?" and classifies the answer into three levers:

  • Initiatives — specific, driver-based actions: a price increase with elasticity assumptions, procurement savings with signed term sheets, new outlets with rate-of-sale assumptions. These are the most credible closure and should be tracked as separate budget lines or tags so that in-year delivery can be monitored.
  • Stretch — a management challenge accepted by a BU head without a full action plan ("we will find ₹10 crore of overhead savings"). It is legitimate in moderation but carries delivery risk; good practice is to cap stretch (e.g., ≤ 2% of the cost base) or hold it centrally.
  • Target revision — the residual accepted as not achievable. This must be an explicit decision of the CEO/CFO, reported to the Board with the LRP impact, not a silent fudge.

Measure the gap after corrections (errors, missing accruals, wrong FX rates). Counting the fixing of errors as gap closure overstates the quality of the plan. Also compute the gap at the right level — EBITDA gaps are often closed by revenue initiatives whose contribution (not revenue) closes EBITDA.

Illustration

₹ crore Revenue EBITDA
Target \(T\) 2,760 428
Bottom-up after corrections \(B\) 2,640 372
Gap \(G\) 120 56
Initiatives \(I\): price +2% on ₹900 crore annual revenue from 1 July (elasticity −0.5, contribution margin 45%) +6.6 +10.3
Initiatives \(I\): rural distribution 25,000 outlets × ₹1,500/month × 6 months (contribution 40%) +22.5 +9.0
Initiatives \(I\): packaging savings — +8.0
Stretch \(S\): indirect spend — +12.0
Approved budget \(T'\) 2,669.1 411.3
Target revision \(T - T'\) 90.9 16.7

The price line: revenue in scope is ₹900 crore × 9/12 = ₹675 crore. Revenue effect = ₹675 crore × (1.02 × 0.99 − 1) ≈ ₹6.6 crore. EBITDA effect = price gain on retained volume (₹675 crore × 0.99 × 2% ≈ ₹13.4 crore) less contribution lost on the 1% volume drop (₹675 crore × 1% × 45% ≈ ₹3.0 crore) ≈ ₹10.3 crore. Note how a price initiative closes more EBITDA gap than revenue gap. The Board paper would show the ₹16.7 crore EBITDA revision with an explanation.

Practice Questions

Q1. Computing and closing the gap [L2]

Target EBITDA ₹250 crore; V0 submission ₹221 crore, which includes a ₹3 crore error (double-counted rebate reversal). Initiatives: 3% price increase from 1 October on ₹600 crore of annual revenue with elasticity −0.8 and contribution margin 40%; logistics savings ₹4 crore. Stretch capped at 1.5% of ₹420 crore overhead. Compute the gap after correction, the value of each lever and the target revision required.

💡 Hint: Revenue initiatives close an EBITDA gap only through their contribution, net of the volume lost.

Q2. Stretch or revision? [L3]

A BU head accepts ₹15 crore of "stretch" to close his gap but refuses to name actions. Explain the risks for the Board, the in-year reporting consequences and how you would structure the stretch to keep it accountable.

Q3. Interview: what is a planning gap? [L1]

Explain the planning gap in under 90 seconds to a non-finance interviewer, including the three ways to close it and one control you would apply.
