Key concept

Relative vs Fixed Targets and Jensen's "Paying People to Lie"

Concept: Relative targets (vs peers/PY) vs fixed targets; Jensen's critique "Paying people to lie" on target-linked bonuses

Statement

  • A fixed target is an absolute number agreed in advance (e.g., AOP EBITDA ₹428 crore); payout depends on actual \(A\) vs that number \(T\).
  • A relative target measures performance against a benchmark observed after the fact: peers (margin vs median of listed peers), prior year (growth vs PY) or market (share gain).

Jensen's critique (Michael C. Jensen, "Corporate Budgeting Is Broken — Let's Fix It", Harvard Business Review, 2001; and "Paying People to Lie: The Truth About the Budgeting Process", European Financial Management, 2003): when pay depends non-linearly on performance relative to a budget target, managers are paid to lie — to understate targets when they are set and to manipulate results once performance is realised. His remedy is to sever the link between budget targets and compensation, using a linear pay-for-performance schedule:

\[ \text{Bonus} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times A \]

where \(A\) is the actual performance measure, \(\beta_0\) a base amount and \(\beta_1\) a constant slope, independent of the budget target \(T\).

Explanation

The typical bonus curve has a threshold (no payout below 90% of target), a linear range and a cap (no further payout above 110–120%). Jensen shows that each kink creates a perverse incentive:

  • During target setting: because payout depends on \(A/T\), lowering \(T\) raises the expected payout — so managers sandbag.
  • Below threshold: once a manager knows he will miss the threshold, he has an incentive to make results worse this year (taking provisions, pushing sales into next year) to make next year easier.
  • Above cap: once the cap is reached, he defers sales and accelerates costs to protect next year.
  • Near threshold: he may pull sales forward, channel-stuff distributors or delay necessary maintenance to just clear the threshold.

Under a linear schedule with no target in the formula, there is no gain from lying about \(T\) and no incentive to shift results between years (beyond the time value). The budget then becomes an honest coordination tool.

Relative targets address a different weakness: fixed targets reward or punish managers for conditions they do not control (market growth, commodity prices, a competitor's stock-out). Benchmarking against peers or the market filters out common shocks. Their limitations: peer data arrives late (listed peers report quarterly with a lag), peer sets must be comparable, and relative measures can reward a BU for losing less than peers in a collapse. Many companies combine absolute (EBITDA vs AOP, linear) and relative (margin vs peer median, share gain) components.

Illustration

Plan A (fixed, kinked): threshold 90% of AOP EBITDA ₹200 crore → 50% payout; target 100% → 100%; cap 110% → 150%. Plan B (linear): bonus pool = 2% of actual EBITDA, no target reference.

Actual EBITDA (₹ crore) Plan A payout % of target bonus Plan B pool (₹ crore)
175 0% 3.50
182 55% 3.64
200 100% 4.00
220 150% 4.40
235 150% (capped) 4.70

Under Plan A, a manager forecasting ₹235 crore in February gains nothing from booking the last ₹15 crore this year and has an incentive to defer it. A manager forecasting ₹175 crore gains by pushing more losses into this year. Under Plan B, each additional ₹1 crore of EBITDA is worth ₹2 lakh to the pool whenever it is earned, so there is no reason to shift. A relative overlay could adjust the pool by ±20% depending on margin rank versus five listed peers.

Practice Questions

Q1. Kinks and timing incentives [L2]

A BU head's plan pays 0% below 92% of AOP revenue (₹800 crore), 60% at 92%, 100% at 100%, rising linearly to 140% at 108% and capped thereafter. In February his forecast is ₹880 crore. Compute his payout at ₹880 crore and at ₹864 crore, and explain what he is likely to do with ₹16 crore of March orders.

💡 Hint: Find the cap point first.

Q2. Designing a relative component [L3]

Design a relative-performance component for an FMCG BU's bonus using listed Indian peers: specify the metric, the peer set criteria, the payout scale and how you would handle peers' reporting lags and accounting differences.

Q3. Interview: why might a bonus tied to the budget be harmful? [L1]

Answer in under two minutes, citing Jensen and giving one example each of gaming at target-setting and at year-end.
