Key concept

Budget Gaming: Sandbagging, Padding and Use-It-or-Lose-It

Concept: Budget gaming: sandbagging (understating revenue/overstating cost) vs padding (budgetary slack) vs "use-it-or-lose-it" spending

Statement

Budget gaming is any deliberate distortion of budget inputs or in-year behaviour to improve a manager's measured performance against the budget without improving actual performance. Three forms:

  • Sandbagging — understating revenue or overstating cost in the submission so the target is easy to beat. For revenue: submitted \(R_B < \hat{R}\), the manager's own best estimate.
  • Padding (budgetary slack) — building excess resources into cost budgets: \(C_B > \hat{C}\). Budgetary slack is the difference \(C_B - \hat{C}\) (or \(\hat{R} - R_B\) for revenue).
  • Use-it-or-lose-it spending — accelerating discretionary spend near year-end to consume remaining budget, because unspent budget is lost and next year's budget is based on this year's spend.

Explanation

Gaming arises when three conditions coexist: the budget is used to evaluate and reward managers (s08), managers know more than the centre (information asymmetry), and next year's budget is set incrementally from this year. Under these conditions, rational managers protect themselves.

Sandbagging is most common on revenue and in BUs with volatile markets, where a low number is easy to justify. Padding is common in cost centres — contingency hidden in travel, consulting or headcount (budgeting positions that will never be filled). Use-it-or-lose-it shows up as a spike in February–March discretionary spend (training, events, consulting, IT purchases), often of low value, and in prepayments booked as expense.

Detection techniques for FP&A:

  • Compare submissions with the manager's own pipeline, CRM or operational forecasts; with market growth and peer performance; and with last year's actual-vs-budget pattern (a BU that beat budget by 8–12% three years running is sandbagging).
  • Ratio tests on costs: cost per FTE, cost per unit, vacancy assumptions vs actual time-to-hire.
  • Monthly spend profile: March spend as a multiple of the April–February average; spikes in specific GLs.
  • Unfilled-position analysis: positions budgeted for 12 months but historically filled late.

Structural remedies:

  • Zero-based or activity-based budgeting for discretionary spend (Topic 3).
  • Relative or linear incentives rather than fixed-target bonuses with cliffs (c05).
  • Separating forecasts from evaluation (Beyond Budgeting, c04).
  • Carry-forward rules allowing a portion of genuine savings to be retained (e.g., 50% of underspend reallocated next year), removing the reason for year-end splurges.
  • Rolling, event-driven resource allocation: approve discretionary spend in quarterly tranches.

Illustration

A regional sales head's CRM shows a weighted pipeline implying ₹420 crore of revenue next year; market growth is 10% on this year's ₹380 crore. He submits ₹395 crore (4% growth). Slack ≈ ₹420 − ₹395 = ₹25 crore. In his cost centre, discretionary spend averaged ₹45 lakh/month April–January and then ran at ₹1.1 crore in each of February and March — about 2.4× the run-rate — with ₹60 lakh of conferences and ₹35 lakh of consulting. Both patterns point to gaming; the remedy is a CRM-based challenge in catchball, a linear incentive curve, and a quarterly discretionary-spend release with a 50% carry-forward of underspend.

Connections

Gaming is the behavioural problem that catchball (c02) and the gap bridge (s05) must handle and that the target letter (s03) and templates (s04) try to prevent through evidence standards. It is the core argument of Beyond Budgeting (c04) and Jensen's critique (c05). ZBB (Topic 3, s06–s07) attacks padding and use-it-or-lose-it; quota over-assignment (Topic 2, s05) acknowledges sandbagging in revenue plans.

Practice Questions

Q1. Quantifying slack from data [L2]

A cost centre's travel budget submissions and actuals for three years were: FY25 budget ₹1.20 crore, actual ₹0.96 crore; FY26 budget ₹1.30 crore, actual ₹1.02 crore; FY27 budget ₹1.40 crore, LE ₹1.12 crore with ₹0.31 crore spent in March alone. The FY28 submission is ₹1.55 crore. Quantify historical slack, the March effect and propose a challenged budget with reasoning.

💡 Hint: Normalise March spend to the April–February run-rate before measuring slack.

Q2. Sandbagging vs genuine caution [L3]

A BU facing a new competitor submits revenue 6% below target. How would you distinguish sandbagging from genuine caution? List the evidence you would request and the analytics you would run.

Q3. Designing anti-gaming rules [L2]

Draft four process rules for the next AOP cycle that address sandbagging, padding and use-it-or-lose-it, and explain the behaviour each rule targets.
