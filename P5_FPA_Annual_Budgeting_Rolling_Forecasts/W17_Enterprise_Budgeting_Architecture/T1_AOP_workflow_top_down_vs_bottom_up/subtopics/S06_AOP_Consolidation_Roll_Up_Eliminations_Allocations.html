Subtopic note

AOP Consolidation: Roll-Up, Eliminations, Allocations, FX Rates

Scope: Consolidation mechanics: entity/BU roll-up, intercompany eliminations, corporate allocations, FX budget rates

Why it matters

Once BUs and entities submit, FP&A must turn dozens of templates into one consolidated AOP that is comparable to the statutory consolidated financial statements the Board sees each quarter. Consolidation errors are the most embarrassing AOP failures: double-counted intercompany revenue inflates growth, unallocated corporate costs overstate BU margins, and inconsistent FX rates make the plan unreconcilable with actuals. The mechanics mirror Ind AS 110 consolidation and Ind AS 21 translation, simplified for planning.

Core notes

Entity and BU roll-up

Budgets are usually built along two hierarchies:

  • Legal entity (Holding Co, Indian subsidiaries, overseas subsidiaries) — needed for statutory, tax, funding and covenant views.
  • Management/BU (Snacks, Beverages, Exports; regions; channels) — needed for performance management.

The cube intersection is entity × BU × cost centre × account × month. Design the roll-up so both hierarchies aggregate the same base data — never build separate entity and BU budgets. In Excel, a single fact table with entity, BU, cost centre, account and month columns, summarised with SUMIFS or a pivot/Power Pivot model, achieves this; in EPM it is native.

=SUMIFS(tblFact[Amount],tblFact[Entity],$B5,tblFact[Account],$C5,tblFact[Month],F$3)

Intercompany eliminations

Intercompany (IC) transactions inside the group must be eliminated so the consolidated plan shows only external transactions.

Step 1 — IC matrix. Before submissions, FP&A agrees with counterparty pairs the budgeted IC volumes and prices: Manufacturing Co sells ₹180 crore to Distribution Co; Holding Co charges ₹24 crore of management fees and ₹6 crore of brand royalty; Holding Co lends ₹50 crore at 9%. Both sides must submit the same amounts.

Step 2 — Eliminate revenue and cost. Eliminate IC sales against IC purchases/COGS; management fees against fee expense; interest income against interest expense; IC receivables against payables; IC loans against investments.

Step 3 — Eliminate unrealised profit in inventory (URPI). If the buyer holds IC inventory at period-end, the seller's margin in that inventory is unrealised to the group and is eliminated (Ind AS 110 B86(c)). Budget URPI on the change between opening and closing IC inventory.

Illustration. Manufacturing Co sells to Distribution Co at cost plus 20%. Budgeted IC sales ₹180 crore; Distribution Co's opening IC inventory ₹18 crore, closing ₹24 crore (at transfer price).

₹ crore Calculation Effect

Under Ind AS 12, the URPI elimination also gives rise to a deferred tax asset at the buyer's tax rate; in many AOPs this is ignored as immaterial, but note it if margins and IC stocks are large.

Corporate allocations

Shared-service costs (finance, HR, IT, legal, corporate office) are budgeted in corporate cost centres and allocated to BUs so that BU EBITDA is fully loaded. Steps:

  1. Budget corporate costs in their own templates (owners: function heads).
  2. Choose allocation keys per service: IT — users or tickets; HR — headcount; finance AP — invoice count; legal — matters or revenue; corporate office — revenue or a blended key.
  3. Fix key volumes at AOP so in-year allocations do not change because of other BUs' activity.
  4. Show allocated costs below BU controllable EBITDA ("BU EBITDA before allocations" and "BU EBITDA after allocations").
  5. Ensure allocations net to zero at consolidated level (corporate cost centre credit = BU debits).

When allocations cross legal entities, they become IC charges requiring GST invoices (or ISD distribution for common input services — mandatory from 1 April 2025) and transfer-pricing support; budget the GST and TDS cash effects accordingly.

FX budget rates

Ind AS 21 translates foreign operations' income and expenses at transaction-date rates (average rates as an approximation) and assets and liabilities at closing rates. For budgeting, companies fix FX budget rates for the year on the central assumptions sheet:

  • P&L budget rate (e.g., ₹86.00/USD, ₹93.50/EUR) used for all foreign-currency revenue, costs and translation of foreign subsidiaries' P&L.
  • Balance sheet rate for closing balances — often the same rate for simplicity.
  • Derived from forward rates, consensus forecasts or the treasury's hedge rate; documented with source and date.

Benefits: every BU uses identical rates; in-year variance analysis can split results into constant-currency performance and FX impact (actual at budget rate vs actual at actual rate). Hedged exposures can be budgeted at the hedged rate, unhedged at the budget rate, if treasury policy requires.

Consolidation sequence and checks

  1. Load all templates (V-numbered) into the fact table.
  2. Translate foreign entities at budget rates.
  3. Apply IC eliminations from the matrix; check that IC pairs match (unmatched IC is a red flag).
  4. Apply allocations; check allocated total = corporate cost pool.
  5. Add consolidation-level items (goodwill-related amortisation of acquired intangibles, minority interest share of profit, group tax adjustments).
  6. Produce consolidated P&L, balance sheet and cash flow (Topic 5) and the BU view.
  7. Reconcile: Σ entity = Σ BU = consolidated before eliminations; eliminations listed separately.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • IC mismatch at submission: do not "pick one side"; resolve with both counterparties and record the agreed figure.
  • Allocation disputes: BUs will challenge keys that rise; agree keys before submissions and hold them for the year.
  • Revenue-based allocation penalises fast-growing BUs; consider activity-based keys (see related item) or fixed allocations.
  • Non-controlling interest: partly-owned subsidiaries' budgets roll up 100%, with NCI share of profit shown separately; incentives may be on attributable profit.
  • Hyperinflationary or volatile currencies: set a rate review trigger (e.g., > 5% movement) for reforecasts rather than changing the AOP.

Key takeaways

  • Roll up from one fact table that serves both legal-entity and BU hierarchies.
  • Agree an IC matrix before submissions; eliminate IC revenue/COGS, fees, interest, balances and URPI.
  • Allocate shared services with stated keys, fixed at AOP, shown below controllable EBITDA and netting to zero.
  • Use central FX budget rates so variances split into constant-currency and FX effects.
  • Cross-entity allocations bring GST/ISD and transfer-pricing consequences.
  • Reconcile entity, BU and consolidated totals, with eliminations shown separately.

Practice Questions

Q1. IC elimination with URPI [L2]

Plant Co sells finished goods to Sales Co at cost plus 25%. Budgeted IC sales for FY 2027-28 are ₹240 crore. Sales Co's IC inventory is ₹30 crore at 1 April 2027 and ₹40 crore at 31 March 2028 (at transfer price). Sales Co sells externally for ₹330 crore. Prepare the consolidation journal and the consolidated revenue and gross profit contribution from this flow.

💡 Hint: Margin on transfer price is 25/125 of the inventory value.

Q2. Allocating shared services under two keys [L2]

Corporate shared services cost ₹30 crore. BU data: revenue Snacks ₹1,400 crore, Beverages ₹800 crore, Exports ₹500 crore; transactions processed 40%, 25% and 35%; headcount 1,800, 900 and 300. Allocate the cost under revenue-based and activity-based (transactions) keys, compare BU EBITDA impacts and recommend a key, giving reasons.

Q3. FX budget rate and constant currency [L2]

Exports BU budgets USD 55 million revenue at a budget rate of ₹86/USD. Actual H1 revenue was USD 26 million at an average rate of ₹88.4/USD against an H1 budget of USD 27.5 million. Compute the H1 revenue variance split into volume/constant-currency and FX effects.

Q4. Consolidation review checklist [L3]

You are the FP&A lead reviewing the first consolidation of a group with eight entities (two overseas) and three BUs. Draft a review checklist of at least ten checks covering roll-up, IC, allocations, FX and output statements, and state for each the evidence you would expect.
