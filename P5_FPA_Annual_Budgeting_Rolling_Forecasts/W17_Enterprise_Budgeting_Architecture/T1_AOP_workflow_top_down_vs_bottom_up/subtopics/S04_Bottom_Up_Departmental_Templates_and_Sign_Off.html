Bottom-Up Departmental Templates and Sign-Off Workflow

Scope: Bottom-up departmental templates: input owners, submission cut-off, mandatory driver fields, sign-off workflow

Why it matters

Bottom-up templates are how the people closest to the business — regional sales managers, plant heads, HR business partners, the marketing team — translate their knowledge into numbers. Template design determines the quality of everything downstream: a template with free-form cells and no drivers produces totals that cannot be challenged, consolidated or flexed; a template with locked structure, mandatory drivers and a clear sign-off produces a budget that FP&A can roll up in hours rather than weeks. In interviews and on the job, "how did you design your budget templates and control submissions?" is a standard question.

Core notes

Template architecture

Each template has the same skeleton regardless of department:

Block Content Who edits
Cover Department, cost centre, owner, version, submission date, sign-off status Owner (status), FP&A (version)
Central assumptions (read-only) FX, inflation, increment %, GST rates, statutory ceilings — linked from the master assumptions sheet Nobody in the department
Driver inputs Volumes, rates, headcount, projects — in blue, data-validated cells Input owner
Calculations Driver × rate by month; locked formulas Nobody (protected)
Output Monthly totals by account code on the standard chart of accounts Nobody (protected)
Commentary Key assumptions, risks and opportunities, changes vs prior version Owner
Checks Completeness, driver sanity, totals vs envelope Automatic

The output block always uses the same account list and timeline across templates so that Power Query or the EPM tool can append them into one fact table (Week 10).

Input owners

Each input block is owned by a named individual, not a department. The sales template's volume rows are owned by regional sales managers; price and GTN rates by revenue management; the payroll roster by HR business partners (positions, CTC) with the department head confirming vacancies; rent by administration based on lease contracts. Ownership must be recorded in the template (owner field per block) and in a RACI on the AOP calendar: Responsible (fills the input), Accountable (signs off — the department head), Consulted (FP&A business partner, procurement), Informed (CFO).

Submission cut-off

Cut-offs give the process its rhythm. Good practice:

  • A fixed date and time per version (V0: 15 December 18:00) published in the target letter.
  • Template locking after cut-off (worksheet protection or EPM workflow status change), so late edits go through FP&A with a change log entry.
  • A submission tracker showing status by owner, escalated daily to the CFO in the last week.
  • A rule for late or missing submissions: FP&A loads a default (current-year LE run-rate plus inflation) and flags it, which creates a strong incentive to submit on time.

Mandatory driver fields

A driver field is mandatory when the line is material or variable. Examples:

Template Mandatory drivers
Sales Volume by SKU × channel × month, list price, GTN rates, number of outlets/distributors
Manufacturing Production volume, BOM quantities, RM price, yield %, conversion cost per unit, shifts
Payroll Position ID, grade, status, start month, exit month, fixed gross, basic %, increment eligibility
Marketing Decision package, objective, cost, expected incremental NSV, timing
Travel Trips per person per quarter, cost per trip by type
IT Users, licence cost per user, contract dates
Capex Project ID, category, amount, milestone dates, capitalisation date, useful life

Implementation in Excel: data validation lists for codes and months; conditional formatting to highlight blanks; a completeness check counting missing mandatory fields; and a rule that the template's output cannot be "Submitted" while the check is non-zero.

=IF(COUNTIFS(tblDrivers[Mandatory],"Y",tblDrivers[Value],"")>0,"INCOMPLETE","OK")

Sign-off workflow

  1. Preparer completes inputs and commentary, sets status to "Prepared".
  2. FP&A business partner reviews drivers and checks, raises queries in the commentary log, sets "Reviewed".
  3. Department/BU head approves and sets "Approved — V0" (in Excel, a sign-off block with name and date; in EPM, a workflow promotion).
  4. FP&A consolidates; challenge-round changes generate V1, V2 with the same sign-off sequence.
  5. The final version is approved per the DoA (c06) and locked.

Keep the evidence: emails or EPM audit trails showing who approved what and when. Internal auditors testing the budgeting process will sample them.

Template design rules

  • One template per cost centre or per BU, never a single shared workbook edited by fifty people.
  • No hard-coded numbers in calculation or output blocks; no external links to personal files.
  • Consistent monthly timeline (April–March) with the same column positions in every template.
  • Explicit separation of one-off items (a ₹2 crore litigation settlement) from run-rate lines.
  • A "variance to envelope" and "variance to LE" summary on the cover so owners see the impact of their inputs immediately.
  • Version-controlled distribution: templates are issued by FP&A with a version number; owners cannot insert rows in protected areas (use pre-built spare rows or a structured table).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Very small cost centres (₹20 lakh annual spend) do not justify a full template; let FP&A budget them centrally on run-rate with owner confirmation.
  • Shared costs across departments (office rent, IT licences) should sit in one owner's template with allocation, not split by each department, otherwise totals double-count or miss.
  • Owner cannot supply drivers (e.g., a new function): accept a top-down allocation for one cycle with a commitment to build drivers by the first reforecast.
  • Late template changes by FP&A: when FP&A adjusts a submission after cut-off, the original and adjusted values must both be visible; silent overwrites destroy ownership.
  • Excel vs EPM: Excel templates with Power Query consolidation work for mid-sized companies; above a few hundred templates or with frequent reforecasts, workflow, audit trail and concurrency needs usually justify an EPM tool.

Key takeaways

  • Every template has the same skeleton: cover, read-only assumptions, driver inputs, locked calculations, standard account-coded outputs, commentary and checks.
  • Ownership is personal and recorded, with a RACI and sign-off evidence.
  • Cut-offs, locking and default-loading for late submissions keep the calendar on track.
  • Mandatory driver fields are enforced by validation and completeness checks, not by requests.
  • The sign-off chain runs preparer → FP&A review → department head → DoA approval, repeated for every version.
  • Consistent timeline and account mapping make roll-up mechanical.

Practice Questions

Q1. Designing a travel template [L2]

Design the driver inputs, calculation logic and checks for a sales function travel template covering 120 field staff, four trip types (local, intra-state, inter-state, air), a trips-per-quarter assumption and cost per trip by type. Include the Excel formula that computes monthly travel cost from a structured table and the check that flags any staff member with no trips assigned.

💡 Hint: Use one row per staff × trip type and a SUMIFS on month and cost centre for the output block.

Q2. Completeness and sign-off controls [L2]

You receive 64 departmental templates at V0 cut-off. Twelve have blank start months for new positions, five have hard-coded totals in the output block, and three were not submitted. Describe the actions you take on each group within the next two working days, and the evidence you retain.

Q3. RACI for a manufacturing template [L1]

Prepare a RACI table for the manufacturing template's blocks: production volume, BOM and yield, RM price, conversion cost, plant headcount and maintenance capex.

Q4. Interview: how do you get budget owners to own their numbers? [L3]

An interviewer asks how you would increase ownership among plant heads who see the AOP as "finance's numbers". Give a structured answer covering template design, process, incentives and review behaviour, with one example of a control that changed behaviour.
