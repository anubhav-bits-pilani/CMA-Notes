Subtopic note

The Top-Down Target Letter: Growth, Margin, Capex, Headcount, Cash

Scope: Top-down target letter: revenue growth, EBITDA margin, capex envelope, headcount caps, cash conversion targets

Why it matters

The target letter (also called the guidance letter, planning letter or AOP kick-off memo) is the document that launches the bottom-up cycle. Issued by the CEO/CFO, usually in November in Indian companies, it tells every BU and function what the company needs from next year and within what constraints they must plan. A well-written letter reduces the number of challenge rounds because submissions start close to the target; a vague one ("plan for aggressive growth") produces submissions that miss by miles and a February scramble. FP&A drafts the letter, so you must know what it contains and how each target is derived.

Core notes

Structure of a target letter

  1. Context: LRP ambition, current-year LE, market outlook, strategic priorities (e.g., "premiumisation and rural reach").
  2. Financial targets by BU: revenue growth, EBITDA margin, capex envelope, headcount caps, cash conversion targets — each with its definition.
  3. Macro and central assumptions: inflation, FX budget rates, commodity prices, GST rates, payroll increment guideline, interest rate, tax rate — all on the central assumptions sheet.
  4. Strategic initiatives to be included, with owners.
  5. Process: calendar, templates, cut-offs, mandatory driver fields, review forums, DoA.
  6. Principles: no padding, no unexplained hard codes, one-off items separately identified, and the consequences of late or incomplete submissions.

Revenue growth

Expressed as a percentage over the current-year LE (or as an absolute floor), often split into volume, price and mix so BUs cannot meet the target only through price: e.g., "Snacks: 13% revenue growth, of which at least 8% volume". Revenue is defined as net sales value after GTN (Topic 2) and excluding GST. The target may also specify channel aspirations (e-commerce to 15% of sales) or new-product share (10% of revenue from launches in the last three years).

EBITDA margin

The margin target forces cost discipline alongside growth. Define it precisely: EBITDA before or after other income, before or after corporate allocations, under Ind AS 116 (rent excluded from EBITDA) or pre-Ind AS 116 management view. A BU-level target is often set on contribution after direct A&P or BU EBITDA before corporate allocations, because BUs cannot control allocations. Express the target as both percentage and absolute rupees; a percentage alone lets a BU shrink revenue to hit margin.

Capex envelope

A ceiling on total capital expenditure, split into maintenance (sustain existing capacity, typically a multiple of depreciation), growth (capacity, new products, distribution assets such as coolers or display units) and regulatory/ESG (effluent treatment, EPR-related, safety). Specify whether it is commitment (purchase orders placed) or cash or capitalisation basis — the three differ by timing (Topic 4). Projects above a threshold (e.g., ₹5 crore) require individual appropriation requests irrespective of the envelope.

Headcount caps

Expressed as closing headcount, average FTE or employee cost, by function. Common rules: "flat headcount except for sales in new territories", "no replacement of exits in corporate functions without CHRO and CFO approval", "employee cost not to exceed 8.5% of revenue". The letter also gives the increment guideline (e.g., merit pool 8.5%, effective 1 July; promotion pool 1%) so every department uses the same rates.

Cash conversion targets

Cash targets prevent profit growth funded by working capital. Typical forms: operating cash flow ÷ EBITDA ≥ 85%; free cash flow ÷ PAT ≥ 90%; days targets — DSO ≤ 22, DIO ≤ 45, DPO ≥ 55; cash conversion cycle reduction of 5 days. The letter should specify how days are measured (on gross or net sales, closing or average balances) to avoid arguments later.

How targets are derived

  • From the LRP: Year-1 numbers adjusted for the LE base (s02).
  • From external commitments: margin guidance to investors, covenant headroom (net debt/EBITDA below 2.5×), credit rating thresholds.
  • From benchmarking: peer margins and A&P ratios (Weeks 7–8).
  • From capacity: maximum volume deliverable given plant capacity and new capex timing.
  • From value: a DCF-based hurdle — e.g., ROCE ≥ WACC + 3% for any new capex.

What the letter should not do

It should not prescribe every line (that defeats bottom-up ownership), and it should not issue different macro assumptions to different BUs. It should also avoid targets without definitions. A frequent design error is setting a revenue target and an EBITDA target that are mutually inconsistent with the A&P and capex envelopes — FP&A should test the letter's internal consistency in a quick top-down model before issuing it.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Over-specified letters that set line-item caps (travel −10%, consulting −15%) are effectively top-down budgets; they are appropriate in a cost crisis but reduce ownership. Prefer category-owner targets (Topic 3, s07).
  • New BUs or start-ups inside the group should get investment envelopes (cash burn cap, milestones) rather than margin targets.
  • Headcount caps vs cost caps: a headcount cap encourages hiring expensive senior people or contractors; a cost cap is neutral but harder to police. Many companies use both, with contract staff tracked separately.
  • Cash targets for seasonal BUs: a festive-season business will breach DIO in September by design; set the target on average or March-closing days and say so.
  • Public guidance: where the target letter is stricter than market guidance, communication must be careful — the letter is price-sensitive information internally; restrict circulation appropriately under SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations.

Key takeaways

  • The target letter launches the bottom-up cycle with BU-level targets, central assumptions, initiatives and process rules.
  • Each target — revenue growth, EBITDA margin, capex, headcount, cash conversion — needs a precise definition and measurement basis.
  • Split revenue targets into volume and price; express margins in both % and ₹.
  • Capex envelopes must state commitment, cash or capitalisation basis.
  • Test the internal consistency of targets in a quick top-down model before issuing the letter.
  • Treat the letter as confidential, especially in listed companies.

Practice Questions

Q1. Testing target-letter consistency [L2]

A draft letter sets FY 2027-28 targets: revenue growth 15% on LE revenue of ₹1,200 crore, gross margin flat at 48%, A&P 9% of revenue (LE 8%), employee cost growth capped at 8% on LE ₹110 crore, other overheads flat at ₹180 crore, EBITDA margin target 18% (LE 16.5%). Test whether the targets are internally consistent and propose a correction.

💡 Hint: Build the top-down P&L from the stated targets and compare the resulting margin with the target margin.

Q2. Defining the cash conversion target [L2]

A BU with annual NSV of ₹730 crore (GST 18% on all sales), COGS ₹438 crore and purchases ₹420 crore has closing receivables ₹62 crore (gross of GST), inventory ₹66 crore and payables ₹58 crore. Compute DSO, DIO and DPO on two different definitions each (e.g., gross vs net sales) and explain which you would specify in the target letter and why.

Q3. Drafting a headcount clause [L3]

Draft the headcount section of a target letter for a company with 3,000 permanent employees, 600 contract staff, attrition of 16%, a planned new region requiring 120 sales staff, and a CFO instruction to hold employee cost growth to 9%. Include rules for vacancies, backfills, contractors and the increment guideline, and explain how you would prevent cap-gaming.

Q4. Capex envelope basis [L2]

A ₹40 crore project will have purchase orders placed in June 2027, payments of 20% in June, 50% in October, 20% in January 2028 and 10% retention in FY 2028-29, and capitalisation in February 2028. Show the FY 2027-28 capex amount on commitment, cash and capitalisation bases, and recommend which basis the envelope should use for capex committee control.
