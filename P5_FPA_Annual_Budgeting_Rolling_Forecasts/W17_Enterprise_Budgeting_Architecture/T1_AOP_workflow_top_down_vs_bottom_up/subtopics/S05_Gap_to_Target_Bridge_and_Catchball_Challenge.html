Gap-to-Target Bridge and Catchball Challenge Rounds

Scope: Gap-to-target bridge and iterative challenge rounds ("catchball") between BU heads, FP&A and CFO

Why it matters

The first consolidation of bottom-up submissions almost never meets the target letter. The difference — the planning gap — must be explained, challenged and closed before the Board sees the AOP. The gap-to-target bridge is the analytical artefact (a waterfall from submission to target), and catchball is the process (iterative rounds between BU heads, FP&A and the CFO). This is where FP&A adds the most value and where most of the political energy of the AOP is spent. You will build the bridge, run the analytics behind each challenge and document the outcomes.

Core notes

Building the gap-to-target bridge

Start from the consolidated V0 submission and walk to the target in clearly labelled steps. Every bar must have an owner, a driver-level explanation and a status (identified, validated, included in V1).

Structure the bridge in three layers: corrections (pure errors — not negotiation), initiatives (new actions with owners and driver logic), stretch (management challenge without a specific action yet), and the residual that leads to a target-revision decision. This mirrors the planning-gap concept (c01).

Also build the bridge from the current-year LE to the AOP (price, volume, mix, inflation, savings, investments) — the CFO and Board want to see both "how far from target" and "why next year differs from this year".

Catchball rounds

Catchball (c02) originates in Japanese hoshin kanri: targets are "thrown" down, plans are "thrown" back up with evidence, and the ball travels until both sides converge.

Round 1 (BU head × FP&A × CFO, January)

  • Pre-read: BU submission vs target vs LE, driver analytics, gap bridge, peer benchmarks.
  • Agenda: challenge the drivers (volume growth vs market growth and distribution gains; price increases vs competitor moves; cost per unit vs last year; headcount additions vs productivity), identify initiatives, agree actions.
  • Output: an action list with owners and due dates; V1 submissions within a week.

Round 2 (CEO + CFO, February)

  • Focus on the residual gap, risk and opportunity list, and trade-offs across BUs (e.g., shifting capex from Beverages to Snacks).
  • Output: final targets per BU, agreed stretch, and a decision on any target revision.

Optional round 3 (Board or Audit Committee pre-read) for listed companies where the Board challenges the consolidated plan.

Challenge analytics FP&A prepares

  • Growth decomposition: revenue growth split into market growth, share gain, price, mix and distribution; anything above market growth needs a named initiative.
  • Unit-cost trends: RM cost per tonne, conversion cost per unit, freight per case, cost per FTE — vs LE and two prior years.
  • Ratio tests: A&P/NSV, employee cost/NSV, revenue per FTE, opex per outlet.
  • Seasonality sanity: monthly phasing vs last three years (Topic 2).
  • Pipeline and evidence: for B2B, stage-weighted pipeline coverage; for capex, approved ARs.
  • Risk/opportunity register: items not in the base with probability and impact — kept off the base numbers to avoid double counting.

Documenting the rounds

Maintain a challenge log: date, forum, BU, line, original value, challenged value, agreed value, rationale, owner, approver, version. This is the antidote to the pitfall of undocumented history. In an EPM tool, store each round as a separate version (Budget V0, V1, V2) so changes can be reported by line.

Converging without destroying ownership

Two failure modes are common. If the CFO simply imposes the target ("plug the gap into your sales line"), BUs disown the budget and will explain every variance as "finance's number". If FP&A accepts submissions without challenge, the plan is padded. The middle path: impose evidence standards (every increment has a driver) rather than numbers, give BUs the choice of how to close their share of the gap, and make the residual stretch explicit and visible — sometimes held centrally as a "management challenge" line that is not loaded into BU budgets.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Central stretch vs BU allocation: holding unallocated stretch centrally keeps BU budgets credible but means the consolidated budget has a line no one owns; set a deadline (first reforecast) to allocate or release it.
  • Initiatives with uncertain timing: phase their impact conservatively (H2) and keep the upside in the opportunity register.
  • Cross-BU trade-offs: shifting capex or A&P between BUs should be decided on value (incremental margin or NPV), not on who argues hardest.
  • Target revision: if the residual gap is large and evidence-based, revising the target is better than an unachievable budget; document the reason and the LRP impact (s02).
  • Errors found late: corrections in the bridge are not negotiation wins; keep them separate so BU heads are not credited with "closing the gap" by fixing their own errors.

Key takeaways

  • The bridge walks from V0 submission to target in layers: corrections, initiatives, stretch and residual.
  • Every bar needs an owner, driver logic and status; a second bridge from LE to AOP explains year-on-year change.
  • Catchball typically runs two rounds: BU × FP&A × CFO, then CEO + CFO on the residual and trade-offs.
  • FP&A's value is the challenge analytics — growth decomposition, unit costs, ratios, phasing and evidence.
  • Keep a challenge log and store each round as a version.
  • Impose evidence standards rather than numbers to preserve ownership.

Practice Questions

Q1. Building the gap bridge [L2]

V0 consolidated revenue is ₹2,610 crore and EBITDA ₹371 crore against targets of ₹2,760 crore and ₹428 crore. FP&A identifies: Beverages applied ₹88/USD instead of the central ₹86/USD on USD 40 million of export revenue (EBITDA margin on exports 12%); an omitted ₹4 crore bonus provision in Snacks; a price increase of 2.5% on ₹800 crore of hair-care revenue from 1 October with elasticity −0.5 and contribution margin 45%; packaging savings of ₹7 crore; and an opex ZBB stretch of ₹10 crore. Prepare the revenue and EBITDA bridges and compute the residual gap.

💡 Hint: Handle the FX correction and bonus omission as corrections before any initiative.

Q2. Challenge pack for round 1 [L2]

The Beverages BU submits 22% volume growth with market growth of 9%, flat outlet count, no new launches and A&P up 30%. Prepare five specific challenge questions with the analytics you would bring to each.

Q3. Central stretch decision [L3]

After round 2, a residual gap of ₹18 crore EBITDA remains. The CEO wants it allocated to BUs pro rata to EBITDA; the Snacks head argues his submission already includes ₹25 crore of initiatives. Evaluate three options — pro rata allocation, central management challenge line, target revision — and recommend one with the governance needed.

Q4. Challenge log design [L1]

Design the columns of a challenge log and populate three example rows from a round-1 meeting covering a volume change, a headcount deferral and a marketing package reduction.
