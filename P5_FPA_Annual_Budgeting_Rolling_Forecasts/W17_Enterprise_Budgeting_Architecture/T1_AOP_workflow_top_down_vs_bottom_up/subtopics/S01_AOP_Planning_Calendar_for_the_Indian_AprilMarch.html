Subtopic note

AOP Planning Calendar for the Indian April–March Financial Year

Scope: Planning calendar for Indian FY (Apr–Mar): LRP refresh Sep–Oct, guidance letter Nov, submissions Dec–Jan, Board approval Feb–Mar

Why it matters

The AOP calendar is the project plan for the most resource-intensive finance process of the year. If it slips, the Board approves a budget in April or May, the ERP "Budget" version is not loaded for April reporting, sales quotas and variable pay plans are announced late, and the first quarter is managed without a baseline. FP&A analysts are judged on whether the calendar holds: interviewers ask "when does your AOP start and what happens in each month?" and expect specific dates, documents and owners. The Indian context adds its own fixed points — Q2 and Q3 results under SEBI LODR, the Union Budget on 1 February, the festive season that distorts H1/H2 trading, and the April increment cycle.

Core notes

Why September to March

An April–March year means the AOP must be approved by the end of March. Working back: the Board meeting that approves Q3 results (late January to mid-February, within 45 days of quarter-end under SEBI LODR Reg 33) or a dedicated March meeting is the natural approval forum. Before that you need consolidated numbers (two to three weeks), at least two challenge rounds (four to six weeks), bottom-up submissions (four to six weeks), and a guidance letter whose envelopes are derived from a refreshed LRP. That pushes the LRP refresh into September–October, after Q1 results are known and while H1 trading is visible.

The four phases

Bottom-up submissions December–January BU and department heads build driver-based plans; FP&A supports and reviews; round 1 and round 2 challenge sessions Completed templates, gap bridge, challenge log BU heads, cost centre owners, FP&A business partners
Consolidation and approval February–March Consolidate, eliminate, allocate; CEO/CFO final review; Audit Committee/Board approval; phasing and ERP/EPM load Consolidated AOP book, Board paper, approved AOP, load file CFO, CEO, Board

Not every company follows this exactly. Some FMCG multinationals' Indian subsidiaries run a January–December group calendar alongside a statutory April–March year; GCCs follow the parent's calendar (often a US fiscal year). Smaller companies compress the cycle into January–March. The principle — LRP before targets, targets before submissions, at least two challenge rounds, approval before the year starts — is universal.

Fixed points specific to India

  • SEBI LODR results deadlines: Q2 results by mid-November and Q3 by mid-February (45 days after quarter-end) consume the same finance team. Schedule submissions so that the cut-off does not coincide with results close.
  • Union Budget (1 February): changes to corporate tax, customs duty (critical for import-dependent capex and RM), TDS/TCS rates and GST Council decisions announced around then can shift the AOP. Build a "Budget-day re-run" slot.
  • Festive season: Diwali falls in October or November, so H1 LE is uncertain when the LRP is refreshed. Consumer goods companies often wait for the festive read before finalising volume submissions.
  • Increment cycle: if increments are effective 1 April, HR must finalise the merit matrix before the AOP lock; if effective 1 July, the payroll budget carries three months at old rates (Topic 3).
  • Statutory board approvals: borrowing limits under Sec 180(1)(c) and investment/loan limits under Sec 186 of the Companies Act, 2013 may need shareholder resolutions if the AOP's funding plan breaches existing limits — identify this by January so the notice can be issued.

Calendar as a control

Publish the calendar in the target letter with named owners and cut-off times (e.g., "V0 templates locked at 18:00 on 15 December; late submissions escalated to the CFO"). Track progress on a submission tracker (department, owner, status: not started/in progress/submitted/reviewed/approved, version, date). In an EPM tool this is the workflow status of each approval unit. The tracker is also evidence for internal audit that the process operated as designed.

Rolling forecasts and the calendar

Companies that run quarterly rolling forecasts (Week 18) often shorten the AOP cycle because the Q2 forecast already provides an updated base. The AOP then becomes "the first year of the rolling forecast, with targets attached", and the calendar can be compressed into November–February.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Late Board date: if the Board can only meet on 10 April, seek interim approval of a "provisional AOP" or first-quarter spending authority so April operations and payroll are not blocked; load the provisional version and replace it after approval.
  • Mid-cycle shock: a commodity spike (palm oil, crude derivatives) in January can invalidate submissions. Decide whether to re-open all templates or apply a central adjustment layer owned by FP&A; the latter is faster and keeps the audit trail clean.
  • Acquisitions during the cycle: an entity acquired in December may not have templates; budget it as a separate block at the deal model's numbers and integrate in the first reforecast.
  • Group vs statutory year: Indian subsidiaries of December year-end groups must produce two views; maintain one monthly model and derive both by period aggregation, never two separate budgets.
  • LE quality: the opening balance sheet comes from the LE for 31 March; a poor LE (e.g., ignoring a year-end inventory build) flows straight into the budgeted cash flow.

Key takeaways

  • The calendar runs backwards from Board approval before 1 April: LRP Sep–Oct, target letter Nov, submissions Dec–Jan, consolidation and approval Feb–Mar.
  • Indian fixed points — LODR results deadlines, Union Budget on 1 February, the festive season and the increment cycle — must be built into the plan.
  • Each phase has a defined document set and owner; the submission tracker and cut-offs are controls, not admin.
  • Plan for at least two challenge rounds and a Budget-day re-run slot.
  • Approval also covers capex and borrowing limits; check Companies Act Sec 179/180/186 implications early.
  • The 31 March LE is the AOP's opening position, so LE quality directly affects the budget.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build an AOP calendar backwards from the Board date [L2]

Konkan Paints Ltd's Board will approve the FY 2027-28 AOP on 18 March 2027. Q3 results will be approved on 6 February 2027. The finance team needs 10 working days to consolidate and 5 working days to prepare the Board paper; each challenge round needs two weeks plus one week for resubmission; BUs need four weeks to complete templates after the target letter. Build the calendar with dates for LRP refresh, target letter, V0 cut-off, rounds 1 and 2, consolidation and Board pre-read, and identify the clash points with the results close.

💡 Hint: Lay out the dates backwards first, then check whether two rounds fit before the Union Budget re-run.

Q2. Union Budget impact on a submitted AOP [L2]

Submissions are complete when the 1 February Union Budget raises basic customs duty on an imported resin from 7.5% to 10% (assume social welfare surcharge at 10% of BCD and IGST credit available). The AOP assumes imports of ₹120 crore assessable value. Quantify the cost impact for the year and describe the process for incorporating the change without re-opening every template.

Q3. Provisional AOP when the Board meeting slips [L3]

The Board meeting is postponed from 25 March to 22 April because two independent directors are unavailable. Payroll increments are effective 1 April and the capex committee wants to place a ₹14 crore order in April. Explain what interim approvals you would seek, how you would load the ERP, and what risks this creates for Q1 variance reporting.

Q4. Interview: walk me through your budgeting cycle [L1]

You are interviewing for an FP&A manager role at a Gurugram-based consumer durables company. Prepare a two-minute structured answer covering phases, timing, documents, owners and controls for an April–March company, including one India-specific complication and how you managed it.
