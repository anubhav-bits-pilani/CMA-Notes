Subtopic note

AOP Lock, Monthly Phasing and Loading the Budget Version

Scope: Final AOP lock, phasing into months, and loading into ERP/EPM as the "Budget" version

Why it matters

Board approval is not the end of the AOP; it is the start of its use. From 1 April, every management report compares actuals with the budget by month, cost centre and account. That comparison only works if the approved plan is locked (no further edits), phased into months in a way that reflects how revenue and costs actually occur, and loaded into the ERP (SAP, Oracle, Tally-based MIS) or EPM tool as a distinct "Budget" version mapped to the chart of accounts. Poor phasing creates false variances every month; poor mapping creates reconciliation work every month-end. Both erode trust in FP&A.

Core notes

The lock

After Board approval, FP&A:

  1. Freezes the final version (e.g., "AOP FY28 Final V3, approved 25 March 2027"), saving the workbook/EPM version as read-only.
  2. Records the approval reference (Board resolution date, minutes extract) in the version log.
  3. Removes write access in EPM (approval units promoted to "Approved"; Anaplan version marked as locked or "actual"-style read-only; Adaptive version locked).
  4. Publishes the approved numbers by BU and cost centre to owners, with their phased monthly budgets.

From this point, the AOP does not change for routine reasons. Forecasts change (Week 18); the budget remains the commitment. Genuine structural changes (reorganisations, acquisitions, discontinued operations) are handled through a controlled budget transfer or re-baselining process approved per the DoA, preserving the original total and documenting the change.

Phasing into months

If submissions were already monthly and driver-based, phasing is inherent. If the Board approved annual figures, or some lines were submitted annually, apply a phasing method per line:

Line Phasing method Example
Revenue Seasonality indices by category and channel (Topic 2) and price increase dates Hair oil Oct–Jan peak; detergent price +4% from July
COGS Follows volume phasing × unit cost, with RM price changes by month Palm oil price curve
Payroll Position-level roster: start/exit months, increment month, bonus/variable pay months Increment from July; variable pay in June
Rent, leases Contract terms; escalation dates 5% escalation from 1 October
Marketing Decision package timing (campaign months) Diwali campaign Sep–Nov
Depreciation Capitalisation dates of assets New line from December
Professional fees Contract timing Statutory audit fee in Q1 and Q4
Insurance Policy start and premium payment; expense evenly over cover period Annual premium paid April, expensed monthly
Utilities Production volume × rate plus fixed demand charges; seasonal Higher summer power for cooling
Interest Debt balance by month from the cash budget Revolver peaks in October

Flat 1/12 phasing is acceptable only for genuinely time-based costs (straight-line rent, software subscriptions expensed evenly). Using it for seasonal items guarantees monthly variances that are pure timing.

Also phase the balance sheet and cash monthly (Topic 5): budgeted receivables, inventory, payables, capex creditors and debt by month. Treasury needs the monthly cash and revolver profile, not just the annual.

Mapping to the chart of accounts

AOP templates use management lines (40–80 lines); the ERP uses GL accounts (several hundred) and cost centres. Two approaches:

  • Load at GL level: map each management line to one "budget GL" per line (e.g., all travel to GL 520100 "Travel — domestic") or split by historical mix. Simple to report in ERP; split assumptions must be documented.
  • Load at management-line level: maintain a mapping table GL → management line in the reporting layer (Power BI or EPM), and load budget at management-line level. Actuals are mapped up; budgets stay at the level they were built.

Most mid-sized Indian companies use the second approach for MIS and the first only where ERP budget control (e.g., SAP funds management or PO budget checks) requires GL-level budgets.

Mapping table example:

GL code GL name Management line P&L section Cash flow class
410100 Sales — domestic Gross sales Revenue Operating
410900 Trade schemes GTN — schemes Revenue deductions Operating
610200 Employer PF contribution Payroll — statutory Employee benefits expense Operating
640300 Travel — international Travel Other expenses Operating

The load file and reconciliation

The load file is a flat table: Version, Entity, Cost centre, Account, Month, Amount, Currency, (optional) Product/Channel. Before and after loading:

  • Total of load file = total of approved AOP by entity and account section.
  • Count of cost centres in load = count of active cost centres in ERP (no orphan cost centres).
  • Sign conventions match the ERP (revenue negative/credit in many ERPs).
  • After load, run the ERP budget report and reconcile to the AOP by BU and month; sign off the reconciliation.
=SUMIFS(tblLoad[Amount],tblLoad[Entity],"NHCL",tblLoad[Month],"2027-04")-SUMIFS(tblAOP[Amount],tblAOP[Entity],"NHCL",tblAOP[Month],"2027-04")

The difference must be zero for every entity-month.

Version naming

Keep clean, permanent version names in ERP/EPM: Actual, Budget (AOP FY28), Forecast Q1/Q2/Q3 (or 3+9, 6+6, 9+3), LE, LRP. Avoid "Budget revised" or "Budget 2" in the reporting version; changes to the budget are handled as transfers inside the Budget version with audit trail, or as a separate "Revised budget" version only if the Board formally approves one.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Re-organisations: if two cost centres move from Beverages to Snacks in June, transfer their remaining budget (July–March) with the cost centres, keep April–June in Beverages, and keep the consolidated total unchanged.
  • Budget transfers between lines: allow within a department with DoA approval (e.g., travel to training) but disallow transfers out of payroll into discretionary spend without CFO approval.
  • Re-phasing requests: owners often ask to re-phase (move budget to later months) after underspending; allow only with evidence and FP&A approval, otherwise underspend is reported as a variance and the forecast carries the timing.
  • New GL codes created mid-year: map them immediately to a management line so actuals do not drop out of budget comparisons.
  • Statutory vs management: management P&L may exclude certain Ind AS adjustments (e.g., Ind AS 116 or 109 fair value effects); keep a reconciliation line so the budget can still be compared with the statutory quarterly P&L presented to the Board.

Key takeaways

  • Lock the approved version, record the Board approval and remove write access.
  • Phase each line with a method that reflects its true timing; flat 1/12 is only for time-based costs.
  • Phase the balance sheet and cash monthly as well as the P&L.
  • Map management lines to the chart of accounts through a documented mapping table and reconcile the load to zero by entity and month.
  • Use clean version names and control in-year changes through transfers with audit trail.
  • Handle re-organisations by moving remaining budget with cost centres while preserving totals.

Practice Questions

Q1. Phasing an annual approved opex budget [L2]

The approved FY 2027-28 opex for a sales region is: payroll ₹12.0 crore (increment of 9% from July on a pre-increment base; variable pay ₹0.9 crore paid in June), rent ₹2.4 crore (lease escalation of 5% from 1 October on a monthly rent of ₹19.5 lakh), marketing ₹6.0 crore (40% Sep–Nov, rest evenly), and travel ₹1.2 crore. Phase each line into months and explain the method chosen for each.

💡 Hint: Back-solve the pre-increment monthly payroll from the annual total before phasing.

Q2. Load reconciliation failure [L2]

After loading the AOP into SAP, the April budget report for Entity A shows ₹3.2 crore less opex than the approved AOP. Investigations reveal two cost centres not active in SAP and a sign error on one GL. Describe the reconciliation steps and controls that would have caught the issues before go-live.

Q3. Mid-year re-organisation [L3]

On 1 August, the e-commerce team (annual budget ₹8.4 crore opex, evenly phased; ₹2.8 crore spent April–July against a budget of ₹2.8 crore) moves from the Beverages BU to a new Digital BU. Explain the budget transfer, the effect on each BU's reported variance, and the approvals required.

Q4. Mapping design choice [L1]

Compare loading the budget at GL level versus management-line level for a company using SAP for actuals and Power BI for MIS, with two advantages and one disadvantage of each.
