Key concept

Cash Capex from Additions, Capex Creditors and Capital Advances

Concept: Cash capex = Additions incl. CWIP + Opening capex creditors − Closing capex creditors + Closing capital advances − Opening capital advances

Statement

\[ \text{Cash capex}_t = A_t + \text{CC}_{t-1} - \text{CC}_t + \text{CA}_t - \text{CA}_{t-1} \]

where \(A_t\) is additions to PPE, CWIP and intangibles (including intangibles under development) during period \(t\) on an accrual basis, \(\text{CC}_{t-1}\) and \(\text{CC}_t\) opening and closing capex creditors (including retention money payable), and \(\text{CA}_{t-1}\), \(\text{CA}_t\) opening and closing capital advances. Non-cash additions are excluded from \(A_t\): ROU assets under Ind AS 116, capitalised borrowing costs that are not yet paid (they sit in interest accrued), decommissioning provisions (Ind AS 16.16(c)), assets acquired by share issue or exchange, and capitalised exchange differences under the Ind AS 101 D13AA option.

Explanation

The formula reconciles the accrual capex budget (what goes into the fixed asset and CWIP schedules) to the cash budget and to "Purchase of property, plant and equipment" in the investing section of the Ind AS 7 cash flow statement. Each adjustment has a simple meaning:

  • Opening capex creditors are paid this period — add.
  • Closing capex creditors are additions not yet paid — subtract.
  • Closing capital advances are cash paid for assets not yet received — add.
  • Opening capital advances were paid last period and are now consumed into additions — subtract.

Two items need care:

  • GST on capex. If capex creditors are recorded gross of GST while additions are net of creditable ITC, the formula must add the ITC portion of the period's capex invoices: cash capex (gross) = additions (net) + ITC on capex invoices + creditor movement + advance movement. The ITC then reduces the GST cash payable line. Either present capex cash gross with GST recovery in the GST line, or net — but be consistent with how the receivables/payables are measured.
  • Capitalised interest. Interest capitalised under Ind AS 23 is an addition to CWIP but its cash payment is classified per the entity's Ind AS 7 policy (Ind AS 7.33: interest paid can be operating or financing; Ind AS 7.32 notes that capitalised interest is disclosed in total interest paid). Exclude it from cash capex if interest paid is shown in financing activities.

In the cash flow statement, cash capex is presented under investing activities; Ind AS 7.43 requires non-cash investing transactions to be excluded and disclosed elsewhere.

Illustration

FY 2026-27 budget, ₹ crore:

Item Amount
Additions to PPE and CWIP (net of creditable GST) 74.0
of which: capitalised borrowing costs (interest shown in financing) 3.0
of which: ROU asset additions 6.0
ITC on capex invoices booked during the year 9.2
Opening capex creditors (incl. GST, incl. retention 2.0) 11.0
Closing capex creditors (incl. GST, incl. retention 4.5) 16.5
Opening capital advances 3.0
Closing capital advances 8.0

Cash additions basis = 74.0 − 3.0 − 6.0 = 65.0. Gross of GST = 65.0 + 9.2 = 74.2.

\[ \text{Cash capex} = 74.2 + 11.0 - 16.5 + 8.0 - 3.0 = ₹73.7 \text{ crore} \]

The ₹9.2 crore ITC is recovered through lower GST cash payments in the operating section (or presented net, by policy). The budgeted CF statement then shows purchase of PPE ₹73.7 crore, while the fixed asset schedule shows ₹74.0 crore of additions — the CFO's question "why don't they match?" is answered line by line.

=Additions - CapInt - ROU + CapexITC + CapexCred_Open - CapexCred_Close + CapAdv_Close - CapAdv_Open

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic reconciliation [L1]

Additions to PPE and CWIP ₹30 crore (all cash-type, ex-GST, no ITC complication); opening capex creditors ₹4 crore, closing ₹7 crore; opening capital advances ₹1 crore, closing ₹2.5 crore. Compute cash capex.

Q2. Non-cash items and GST [L2]

Additions ₹52 crore include ₹4 crore decommissioning provision, ₹7 crore ROU assets and ₹2.2 crore capitalised interest (interest paid shown in financing). ITC on capex invoices ₹6.3 crore; capex creditors (gross) opening ₹9 crore, closing ₹5 crore; capital advances opening ₹6 crore, closing ₹2 crore. Compute cash capex for the cash flow statement and list the non-cash disclosures.

💡 Hint: Strip non-cash additions first, then gross-up for GST before applying the creditor and advance movements.

Q3. Monthly model check [L2]

Describe a monthly check row you would add to the budget model to prove that capex cash in the cash budget equals the formula above, and explain what a non-zero difference most likely indicates.
