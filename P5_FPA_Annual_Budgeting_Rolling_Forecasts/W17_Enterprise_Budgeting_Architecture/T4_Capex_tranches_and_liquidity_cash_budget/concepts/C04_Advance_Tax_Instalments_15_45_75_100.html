Key concept

Advance Tax Instalments: 15%, 45%, 75%, 100%

Concept: Advance tax cumulative instalments: 15% (15 Jun), 45% (15 Sep), 75% (15 Dec), 100% (15 Mar)

Statement

For a company, advance tax payable by each due date \(d\) is:

\[ \text{Instalment}_d = \max\left(0,\; c_d \times (\hat{T}_d - \text{TDS}_d) - \text{Paid}_{<d}\right) \]

where \(\hat{T}_d\) is the estimated total tax liability for the year as re-estimated at date \(d\), \(\text{TDS}_d\) the expected TDS/TCS credits for the year, \(c_d\) the cumulative percentage (15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December, 100% by 15 March), and \(\text{Paid}_{<d}\) advance tax already paid. Advance tax applies when the estimated liability is ₹10,000 or more (Sec 208). Provisions referenced are under the Income-tax Act 1961 (Sec 207–211, 234B, 234C); from 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 carries equivalent provisions with new numbering.

Explanation

The schedule front-loads the year: by mid-December three-quarters of the year's tax must have been paid even though only nine months of profit have been earned. For a seasonal FMCG business with a strong Q3, the December instalment is the largest and coincides with the post-Diwali receivables peak.

Interest consequences:

  • Sec 234C (deferment): 1% per month (simple) on the shortfall against each instalment — for 3 months for the June, September and December instalments and 1 month for March. No 234C interest arises on the June and September instalments if cumulative payment is at least 12% and 36% respectively. Shortfalls arising only because of capital gains or unexpected incomes after an instalment date are relieved if paid in the remaining instalments.
  • Sec 234B (default): if advance tax paid is below 90% of assessed tax, 1% per month on the shortfall from 1 April of the assessment year until payment.

Budget treatment: advance tax is a cash outflow and, in the balance sheet, is netted against the current tax provision (Ind AS 12.71 permits offset when there is a legally enforceable right and intention to settle net — typically same tax authority and same assessment year). The current tax provision accrues monthly with PBT; advance tax payments are lumpy — the difference sits as a current tax asset/liability through the year.

Forecast discipline: re-estimate \(\hat{T}\) before each instalment using the latest forecast, including one-offs (asset sales, write-backs), disallowances (Sec 43B(h), CSR expenditure under Sec 37 explanation 2), and deferred tax timing differences — advance tax follows taxable income, not book PBT.

Illustration

Budgeted PBT ₹200 crore; permanent disallowances (CSR ₹4 crore) and timing differences (book depreciation over tax depreciation ₹10 crore) → taxable income ₹214 crore. Tax at 25.168% = ₹53.86 crore. Expected TDS credits ₹2.0 crore → net ₹51.86 crore.

Date Cumulative % Cumulative due (₹ crore) Instalment
15 Jun 15% 7.78 7.78
15 Sep 45% 23.34 15.56
15 Dec 75% 38.90 15.56

If the September forecast raises taxable income to ₹240 crore (net tax ₹58.40 crore), the September instalment becomes 45% × 58.40 − 7.78 = ₹18.50 crore, and later instalments are recomputed accordingly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Instalment schedule [L1]

Estimated taxable income for FY 2026-27 is ₹80 crore under Sec 115BAA (25.168%); expected TDS credit ₹0.5 crore. Compute each advance tax instalment and the dates.

Q2. Re-estimation and 234C exposure [L2]

A company paid ₹6 crore on 15 June based on an estimate of ₹40 crore net tax. In September its forecast rises to ₹52 crore, but it pays only ₹12 crore on 15 September. Compute the shortfall against the September cumulative requirement, the Sec 234C interest on it, and the December instalment needed to be fully compliant at 75%.

💡 Hint: Check the 36% relief threshold for the September instalment before computing interest.

Q3. Book vs taxable income [L2]

Explain why advance tax computed on budgeted book PBT × 25.168% can be materially wrong. List at least four adjustments typical for an Indian manufacturing company and their direction.
