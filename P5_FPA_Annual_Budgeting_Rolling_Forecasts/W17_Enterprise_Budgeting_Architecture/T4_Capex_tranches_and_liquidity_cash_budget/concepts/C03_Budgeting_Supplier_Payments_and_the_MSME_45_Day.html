Key concept

Budgeting Supplier Payments and the MSME 45-Day Rule (Sec 43B(h))

Concept: Supplier payments = Opening payables + Purchases (incl. GST) − Closing payables; MSME dues > 45 days disallowed under Sec 43B(h)

Statement

\[ \text{Pay}_t = \text{AP}_{t-1} + \text{Pur}_t \times (1 + g) - \text{AP}_t \]

where \(\text{Pay}_t\) is cash paid to trade suppliers in month \(t\), \(\text{AP}_{t-1}\) and \(\text{AP}_t\) opening and closing trade payables (gross of GST), \(\text{Pur}_t\) purchases on credit ex-GST (materials, packing, services charged to trade payables), and \(g\) the weighted GST rate on purchases. Equivalently, a payment profile: \(\text{Pay}_t = \sum_k q_k \times \text{Pur}_{t-k}(1+g)\).

Sec 43B(h) (Income-tax Act 1961, inserted by Finance Act 2023, applicable from FY 2023-24): any sum payable to a micro or small enterprise beyond the time limit in Sec 15 of the MSMED Act 2006 — the agreed date, but not more than 45 days from acceptance; 15 days if there is no written agreement — is deductible only in the year of actual payment.

Explanation

The roll-forward mirrors collections (c02). Payables are gross of GST because the supplier invoices GST; the ITC is recovered when it is set off against output tax in GSTR-3B (s07), which is a separate cash line.

The MSME constraint turns a credit policy into a tax issue:

  • Scope: suppliers registered under Udyam as micro or small (not medium) enterprises, in manufacturing or services. Traders registered only for priority sector lending purposes are generally regarded as outside the Sec 15 delayed-payment provisions — verify each supplier's Udyam classification.
  • Consequence: if an invoice dated 10 February remains unpaid at 31 March beyond the 45-day limit, the expense is disallowed for that year and allowed in the year paid — a timing difference that raises current tax and creates a deferred tax asset.
  • MSMED Act interest: Sec 16 imposes compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate on delayed payments, and Sec 23 makes that interest non-deductible. Schedule III requires disclosure of MSME dues and interest; companies also file half-yearly MSME Form 1 with the MCA for dues beyond 45 days.
  • Budget implication: the payment profile for MSME suppliers must be ≤ 45 days. A blanket DPO assumption of 60–75 days that includes MSME suppliers produces an unrealistic cash budget and a tax leakage.
  • Under the Income-tax Act 2025 (from 1 April 2026), the equivalent provision continues with renumbered sections; confirm the reference.

Illustration

Budgeted April purchases ₹40 crore ex-GST at 18%: 30% from micro/small suppliers (terms 45 days), 70% from large suppliers (terms 75 days). Opening payables ₹68 crore (incl. GST).

₹ crore MSME Non-MSME Total
April purchases incl. GST 14.16 33.04 47.20
Closing payables (incl. GST, days ÷ 30 × month purchases) 21.24 82.60 103.84 (approx., steady-state)

In steady state, payables equal 45/30 × 14.16 = 21.24 for MSME and 75/30 × 33.04 = 82.60 for others; blended DPO ≈ 66 days. If the procurement team proposes extending all suppliers to 75 days, MSME payables would rise to 35.40, of which roughly ₹14 crore at year-end would be beyond 45 days and disallowed — tax cost ≈ ₹14 crore × 25.168% ≈ ₹3.5 crore of extra current tax in that year, plus MSMED interest exposure.

Connections

Feeds s06 (payments line), c01 (cash roll-forward), s07 (GST ITC set-off as a separate cash line) and Topic 5 s03/c03 (DPO-based payables, RM purchase budget via c02 of Topic 5) and s07 (tax provision and deferred tax). Supply chain finance and TReDS (s08) are the compliant way to give MSMEs early cash while extending effective DPO.

Practice Questions

Q1. Supplier payments roll-forward [L1]

Opening trade payables (incl. GST) ₹55 crore; May purchases ex-GST ₹48 crore at 18% GST; closing payables target DPO 50 days on May gross purchases (31-day month). Compute closing payables and May payments.

Q2. MSME split in the payment profile [L2]

Monthly purchases ex-GST are ₹30 crore, GST 12%, of which 40% from micro/small enterprises. Current practice pays all suppliers at 60 days. Compute the year-end MSME payables beyond 45 days, the Sec 43B(h) disallowance, the tax effect at 25.168%, and the change in the cash budget if MSME suppliers are moved to 45 days.

💡 Hint: At steady state, invoices older than 45 days at year-end are roughly 15 days of MSME purchases.

Q3. Policy recommendation [L3]

Procurement wants to extend payment terms from 45 to 90 days for all packaging suppliers (half of whom are small enterprises) to improve cash conversion by ₹20 crore. Evaluate the proposal across tax, MSMED Act, supplier relationship and disclosure angles, and suggest an alternative structure.
