Key concept

Cash Roll-Forward with Minimum Balance and Revolver Logic

Concept: Closing cash = Opening cash + Receipts − Payments; Shortfall vs minimum balance → revolver draw; surplus → repay/invest

Statement

\[ \text{PreFin}_t = \text{Cash}_{t-1} + R_t - P_t \]

\[ D_t = \min\left(\max\left(0,\; M - \text{PreFin}_t\right),\; A_t\right), \qquad Y_t = \min\left(\max\left(0,\; \text{PreFin}_t - M\right),\; B_{t-1}\right) \]

\[ \text{Cash}_t = \text{PreFin}_t + D_t - Y_t, \qquad B_t = B_{t-1} + D_t - Y_t \]

where \(\text{Cash}_{t-1}\) is opening cash, \(R_t\) receipts, \(P_t\) payments (operating, capex, tax, interest, scheduled debt service, dividends), \(\text{PreFin}_t\) cash before revolver activity, \(M\) the minimum cash balance, \(A_t\) available revolver capacity (\(\min(\text{limit}, \text{drawing power}) - B_{t-1}\)), \(D_t\) the draw, \(Y_t\) the repayment, and \(B_t\) the revolver balance. Any surplus remaining after full repayment (\(\text{Cash}_t > M\) with \(B_t = 0\)) is available to invest.

Explanation

The identity is the corkscrew of the cash budget. The policy layer on top says: never let cash fall below \(M\); fund shortfalls first from the revolver (CC/OD/WCDL), and use surpluses first to repay the revolver (the most expensive, most flexible debt), then invest (liquid funds, sweep FDs) or prepay term debt per the treasury policy.

Key conditions:

  • Ordering. Scheduled term-loan repayments and interest are included in \(P_t\) before the revolver calculation; the revolver is the balancing facility.
  • Availability cap. If \(M - \text{PreFin}_t > A_t\), the model must show a funding gap (an explicit check line), not quietly breach the limit or let cash go negative.
  • Surplus deployment. A policy may keep a target cash \(M^* > M\) before investing; investments then become another corkscrew (opening + purchase − redemption).
  • Interest. Revolver interest on the average balance creates circularity (Topic 5); interest on the opening balance avoids it.
  • Sign discipline. Present payments as negative numbers or as positives subtracted — never mix within one schedule.

Illustration

Minimum cash ₹5 crore; opening cash ₹6 crore; opening CC ₹10 crore; CC limit ₹30 crore (DP not binding).

₹ crore Apr May Jun
Opening cash 6.0 5.0 5.0
Receipts 40.0 38.0 52.0
Payments (45.0) (41.0) (39.0)
Pre-financing cash 1.0 2.0 18.0
CC draw / (repay) 4.0 3.0 (13.0)
Closing cash 5.0 5.0 5.0
Closing CC 14.0 17.0 4.0

In June the surplus of ₹13 crore over minimum is less than the CC balance of ₹17 crore, so all ₹13 crore repays CC and closing cash stays at ₹5 crore; nothing is invested.

Excel (row 8 = pre-financing cash, row 10 = opening CC, MinCash and Limit named inputs):

Draw  =MIN(MAX(0, MinCash - F8), Limit - F10)
Repay =MIN(MAX(0, F8 - MinCash), F10)
Gap   =MAX(0, MinCash - F8 - (Limit - F10))

Practice Questions

Q1. Six-month revolver schedule [L2]

Opening cash ₹4 crore, minimum cash ₹4 crore, opening revolver ₹0, limit ₹20 crore. Receipts (₹ crore): 30, 28, 26, 35, 42, 45. Payments: 33, 34, 31, 30, 33, 36. Build the cash and revolver corkscrew, and state the closing investible surplus in month 6.

Q2. Funding gap check [L2]

Extend Q1 with a capex payment of ₹14 crore in month 3 and a revolver limit of ₹15 crore. Identify the month and size of any funding gap and propose three ways the budget could close it.

💡 Hint: The gap is the shortfall after the draw is capped at available capacity.

Q3. Policy design [L3]

A treasury policy says: keep ₹10 crore minimum, repay CC before investing, prepay term loan only if surplus exceeds ₹25 crore for three consecutive months. Describe how you would implement this policy in a monthly Excel cash budget, including the checks and named inputs.
