Key concept

Drawing Power on Stock and Book Debts

Concept: Drawing power = (Eligible stock × (1 − margin %)) + (Eligible debtors < 90 days × (1 − margin %)) − Creditors (per bank terms)

Statement

\[ \text{DP}_t = S^{e}_t \times (1 - m_s) + D^{e}_t \times (1 - m_d) - \text{Cr}_t \]

\[ \text{Available}_t = \min(\text{Sanctioned limit},\; \text{DP}_t) - \text{Outstanding}_t \]

where \(S^{e}_t\) is eligible stock (raw materials, WIP, finished goods per sanction terms, at lower of cost and net realisable value, excluding obsolete, slow-moving, in-transit uninsured or third-party stock), \(m_s\) the stock margin, \(D^{e}_t\) eligible book debts (typically aged under 90 days from invoice, excluding related parties, disputed or already discounted receivables), \(m_d\) the debtor margin, and \(\text{Cr}_t\) trade creditors for goods where the sanction requires deduction ("paid stock"). All values are taken from the stock and book-debt statement for the relevant month.

Explanation

The margin is the borrower's own stake in current assets; the bank finances only the remainder. Creditors are deducted because unpaid stock has effectively been financed by suppliers — the bank will not finance it twice. Some sanctions apply the margin after deducting creditors from stock: \((S^e - \text{Cr}) \times (1 - m_s)\), which yields a higher DP than deducting creditors after the margin. Always model exactly what the sanction letter says.

Practical points for the budget:

  • Lag. DP for month \(t\) is usually based on the statement for month \(t-1\), submitted by a set date. A budget that uses same-month balances overstates availability in a build-up month.
  • Ageing cliff. Debtors crossing 90 days drop out entirely; a slow-paying modern-trade customer can cut DP sharply after the festive season.
  • Seasonality. DP rises as inventory builds for Diwali — but so do creditors, partly offsetting it.
  • Book consistency. Schedule III and CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) require that quarterly statements filed with banks agree with books, or that discrepancies are disclosed and explained. Budget DP should be computed from the same ledgers and valuation as the balance sheet.
  • Insurance and audit. Banks require stock insurance and periodic stock audits; uninsured stock is ineligible.

DP caps the revolver in c01. The binding constraint can switch between the sanctioned limit (when current assets are high) and DP (in lean months).

Illustration

Sanction terms: stock margin 25%, debtor margin 40%, debtors < 90 days, creditors deducted after margin. September-end statement (used for October DP), ₹ crore:

Item Book value Ineligible Eligible Margin Net
RM and packing 38 2 (obsolete) 36 25% 27.0
FG 54 4 (near-expiry) 50 25% 37.5
Debtors 70 12 (> 90 days) + 3 (group) 55 40% 33.0
Less creditors for goods (29.0)
Drawing power 68.5

Sanctioned CC ₹80 crore; outstanding ₹61 crore → available = min(80, 68.5) − 61 = ₹7.5 crore. If October's pre-financing deficit is ₹12 crore, there is a ₹4.5 crore funding gap despite ₹19 crore of apparent undrawn sanction.

Practice Questions

Q1. Basic DP [L1]

Eligible stock ₹45 crore, eligible debtors ₹30 crore, creditors for goods ₹12 crore; stock margin 25%, debtor margin 30%; sanctioned limit ₹50 crore; outstanding ₹38 crore. Compute DP and available limit under (a) creditors deducted after margin, and (b) creditors deducted from stock before margin.

Q2. Monthly DP with lag [L2]

Month-end balances (₹ crore) for Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov: stock 60, 82, 95, 58; debtors < 90 days 40, 48, 66, 72; creditors 22, 35, 41, 20. Margins 25% (stock) and 35% (debtors); DP uses previous month-end statement. Sanctioned limit ₹75 crore. Compute DP available in Sep, Oct, Nov and Dec, and identify the month where the limit, not DP, binds.

💡 Hint: Shift the statement balances by one month before applying the formula.

Q3. Ageing shock [L3]

A key modern-trade account owes ₹18 crore, of which ₹10 crore will cross 90 days by December under current trends. Quantify the DP impact at a 40% debtor margin, and propose operational and financing responses in the budget.
