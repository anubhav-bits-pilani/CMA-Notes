Key concept

Budgeting Customer Collections: Roll-Forward and Ageing Profiles

Concept: Collections = Opening receivables + Credit sales (incl. GST) − Closing receivables; or ageing-based: Sales_t × % collected in t, t+1, t+2

Statement

Balance roll-forward method:

\[ C_t = \text{AR}_{t-1} + S^{\text{cr}}_t \times (1 + g) - \text{AR}_t \]

Ageing (collection profile) method:

\[ C_t = \sum_{k=0}^{K} p_k \times S^{\text{cr}}_{t-k} \times (1 + g), \qquad \sum_{k=0}^{K} p_k + b = 1 \]

where \(C_t\) is cash collected in month \(t\), \(\text{AR}_{t-1}\) and \(\text{AR}_t\) opening and closing trade receivables (gross of GST, as invoiced), \(S^{\text{cr}}_t\) credit sales ex-GST, \(g\) the weighted GST rate on sales, \(p_k\) the proportion of month \(t-k\) invoices collected \(k\) months later, \(K\) the longest collection lag, and \(b\) the bad debt / write-off rate. Cash sales are added separately.

Explanation

Receivables are recorded at invoice value including GST, so collections must also include GST — the government's share is collected from the customer and paid out on the 20th of the next month (s07). Budgeting collections ex-GST understates gross receipts and, combined with omission of the GST payment line, misstates cash timing.

The roll-forward method is consistent with a days-based balance sheet: set closing AR from DSO (e.g. \(\text{AR}_t = S_t (1+g) \times \text{DSO} \div \text{days}_t\)) and back out collections. It guarantees that the P&L, balance sheet and cash budget tie.

The ageing method models behaviour directly — "40% in month of sale, 50% next month, 8% in month 2, 2% bad" — and is better for seasonal businesses where a flat DSO misstates collections after a festive spike. The closing AR implied is the uncollected tail of recent months' sales.

Both should be used together: build collections by profile, derive closing AR, and check implied DSO against history. Deductions matter too: schemes and claims settled by credit notes, TDS deducted by customers (Sec 194Q/194C/194J) and TCS reduce cash received versus invoice value. TDS deducted by customers is a tax asset, not a collection.

Expected credit loss under Ind AS 109 (simplified approach, provision matrix) reduces the AR carrying amount but not the gross collection profile; model write-offs explicitly.

Illustration

Credit sales ex-GST (₹ crore): Oct 50, Nov 80 (Diwali), Dec 45. GST 18%. Profile: 30% same month, 60% next, 9% month after, 1% bad debt.

₹ crore (incl. GST) Oct Nov Dec
Invoiced 59.0 94.4 53.1

From Dec sales — — 15.9
Collections from these sales 17.7 63.7 77.8

December collections (plus any tail from September) peak a month after the festive spike. A flat 45-day DSO roll-forward would show a smoother pattern and misstate the December cash position.

In Excel, with invoiced sales incl. GST in row 6 and the profile percentages in $C$10:$C$12 (lag 0, 1, 2), collections in column H are:

=H$6*$C$10 + G$6*$C$11 + F$6*$C$12

Keep one row per lag (rather than a single array formula) so reviewers can trace each component, and add a check that the profile plus bad-debt rate sums to 100%.

Practice Questions

Q1. Roll-forward collections [L1]

Opening receivables (incl. GST) ₹42 crore; budgeted credit sales for April ex-GST ₹60 crore at 12% GST; target DSO 40 days on April's gross sales (30-day month). Compute closing receivables and April collections.

Q2. Ageing profile vs DSO [L2]

Monthly credit sales ex-GST (₹ crore) for July–December are 30, 32, 38, 55, 70, 40; GST 18%. Collection profile: 20% in month, 65% next month, 14% month after, 1% bad. Compute collections for September to December, the implied closing AR each month and implied DSO, and comment on why a constant DSO assumption would mislead.

💡 Hint: Closing AR = invoiced amounts not yet collected or written off from the last two months.

Q3. Deductions in collections [L2]

A modern-trade customer is invoiced ₹10 crore (incl. GST) and deducts TDS under Sec 194Q at 0.1% on the value excluding GST (₹8.47 crore), a ₹0.25 crore display-scheme claim and ₹0.05 crore shortage claim. Compute cash received and explain how each deduction is treated in the budget and books.
