Subtopic note

Funding the Budget: CC/OD Limits, Drawing Power, Cash Buffer, Covenants

Scope: Funding: CC/OD limits, drawing power from stock/debtor statements, minimum cash buffer, covenants

Why it matters

A cash budget that shows a ₹30 crore peak deficit in October is useful only if it also answers: "Can we actually borrow ₹30 crore in October?" In Indian mid-corporates the answer depends less on the sanctioned limit and more on drawing power — the amount the bank allows against current assets declared in monthly stock and book-debt statements. Covenant tests on the budgeted financials determine whether limits will be renewed. FP&A therefore needs to model working capital funding the way the bank does: sanctioned limit, drawing power, margin, minimum cash and covenant headroom, month by month.

Core notes

Working capital facilities

Facility Nature Budget treatment
Cash credit (CC) Running account secured by hypothecation of stock and book debts; drawable up to the lower of sanctioned limit and drawing power Revolver in the cash budget; interest on daily balance (MCLR/EBLR/T-bill linked + spread)
Overdraft (OD) Running account, often secured by FDs, property or unsecured for strong credits Same mechanics; limit not usually DP-linked unless secured by current assets
Working capital demand loan (WCDL) Short tenor (7–180 days) loan carved out of the fund-based limit, usually cheaper than CC Fixed draws with maturity dates; rollover assumption
Non-fund based: LC, bank guarantee Contingent; consumes limit and margin Margin money as restricted cash; commission as finance cost
Commercial paper (CP) Unsecured money-market instrument (7 days–1 year) for highly rated issuers; usually requires backstop lines Alternative to CC for A1+ issuers
Supply chain finance / TReDS Discounting of receivables (e.g. MSME invoices on TReDS) or reverse factoring Affects DSO/DPO and may be debt-like — disclose policy

For large borrowers, RBI's guidelines on the Loan System for Delivery of Bank Credit require a minimum "loan component" (WCDL) for borrowers with aggregate fund-based working capital limits of ₹150 crore and above, limiting how much can sit in the cash credit component — confirm current thresholds with the bank.

Assessed limit vs drawing power

Banks assess working capital requirement (Nayak Committee turnover method for smaller borrowers — 25% of projected turnover, with 5% margin from the borrower; MPBF/Tandon method II for larger: 75% of (current assets − current liabilities other than bank borrowings)). The sanctioned limit is the ceiling. Drawing power is computed monthly from the stock and book debt statement:

\[ \text{DP} = \text{Eligible stock} \times (1 - m_s) + \text{Eligible debtors} \times (1 - m_d) - \text{Creditors (for goods, per sanction terms)} \]

with \(m_s\), \(m_d\) the stock and debtor margins (typically 20–30% and 25–40%). Eligibility rules are sanction-specific: debtors under 90 days (sometimes 120), excluding related-party and export-bill-discounted debtors; stock excluding obsolete, slow-moving, goods in transit not insured, consigned stock. Some banks deduct creditors from stock before applying margin (paid stock method). Usable limit:

\[ \text{Available} = \min(\text{Sanctioned limit}, \text{DP}) - \text{Outstanding} \]

Stock statements and Schedule III

Stock statements are filed monthly (often by the 10th–20th of the following month); quarterly financial information returns (QIS/FFR) are also filed. Schedule III (2021 amendment) requires disclosure, where borrowings from banks/FIs are on the basis of security of current assets, of whether quarterly returns or statements filed agree with the books, and a reconciliation with reasons for material discrepancies. Auditors report on this under CARO 2020 clause 3(ii)(b) for sanctioned working capital limits exceeding ₹5 crore. Budgeted DP must therefore be computed from the same inventory and receivable definitions as the books.

Minimum cash buffer

The minimum operating cash is the floor the treasury will not breach. Sizing approaches:

  • Days of disbursements: e.g. 5–10 days of average payments.
  • Peak intra-month trough: from the 13-week forecast, the worst week's net outflow plus a margin.
  • Statutory coverage: enough to meet the next statutory payment (GST/advance tax) without drawing.
  • Covenant or lender requirement: some facilities specify a minimum liquidity or DSRA.

A buffer held in liquid funds or sweep FDs earns some return; a buffer held as undrawn committed limits is cheaper but depends on DP.

Covenants

Typical financial covenants for Indian term loans and working capital facilities:

Covenant Definition Typical threshold
Net debt / EBITDA (Borrowings − cash & liquid investments) ÷ trailing 12-month EBITDA ≤ 2.5–3.5×
DSCR (PAT + depreciation + interest on term debt) ÷ (term debt principal + interest due) ≥ 1.2–1.5×
TOL / TNW Total outside liabilities ÷ tangible net worth ≤ 2.5–3.0×
Current ratio Current assets ÷ current liabilities (incl. bank borrowings) ≥ 1.25–1.33× (MPBF logic)
Interest coverage EBITDA ÷ finance cost ≥ 3×
FACR Net fixed assets ÷ term debt ≥ 1.25×

Definitions are contract-specific (treatment of lease liabilities under Ind AS 116, one-offs, CWIP). The budget model should compute each covenant on the budgeted quarterly financials exactly as defined in the facility agreement, show headroom, and flag breaches. A covenant breach at the reporting date that makes a long-term loan repayable on demand requires current classification under Ind AS 1.74 unless a waiver/grace period exists by the reporting date.

Revolver logic in the budget

\[ \text{Draw}_t = \min\left(\max\left(0,\; \text{Min cash} - \text{Pre-financing cash}_t\right),\; \text{Available}_t\right) \]

\[ \text{Repay}_t = \min\left(\max\left(0,\; \text{Pre-financing cash}_t - \text{Min cash}\right),\; \text{Opening CC}_t\right) \]

If the required draw exceeds availability, the model must flag a funding gap rather than show negative cash. In Excel:

=MIN(MAX(0, MinCash - PreFinCash), MIN(Sanctioned, DP) - OpeningCC)

Illustration

Budget for October (pre-Diwali build), ₹ crore:

Item Amount
Stock (eligible) 120
Debtors < 90 days (eligible) 80
Trade creditors for goods 45
Stock margin 25%, debtor margin 30% —
DP = \(120 \times 0.75 + 80 \times 0.70 - 45\) 101
Sanctioned CC 110
Opening CC drawn 72
Pre-financing cash (18)
Minimum cash 10

Required draw = 10 − (−18) = ₹28 crore; available = min(110, 101) − 72 = ₹29 crore. The draw fits with ₹1 crore headroom — too thin. If eligible debtors fall because a modern-trade account goes beyond 90 days, DP drops and the plan fails. Actions: negotiate an ad hoc limit, arrange a WCDL, defer capex payments or increase supplier credit before the peak.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • CC in cash and cash equivalents? Ind AS 7.8 allows bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form an integral part of cash management to be included in cash and cash equivalents. Many Indian companies present CC as borrowings; decide policy consistently between the budget and statutory accounts.
  • DP timing lag. DP for October is based on the September-end statement filed in October; model the lag.
  • Inventory valuation for DP is usually at lower of cost and market per the books; inflated valuations in stock statements are an audit and fraud red flag.
  • Seasonal limits. Banks can sanction peak/non-peak limits for seasonal businesses (e.g. festive or harvest season).
  • Interest on overdraft depends on daily balance; average-balance interest in the budget introduces circularity (see Topic 5).
  • Covenant definitions under Ind AS 116 can include lease liabilities in debt — check "frozen GAAP" clauses.
  • Cross-default clauses mean a breach on one facility can accelerate others.

Key takeaways

  • Borrowable amount = min(sanctioned limit, drawing power) − outstanding; DP depends on eligible stock and debtors net of margins and creditors.
  • Budget DP from the same definitions as the books; Schedule III requires reconciliation of bank statements with books.
  • Size the minimum cash buffer from intra-month troughs and statutory peaks.
  • Compute covenants on budgeted quarterly numbers using the contract's definitions; flag headroom.
  • Revolver logic must cap draws at availability and flag funding gaps rather than show negative cash.

Practice Questions

Q1. Drawing power computation [L1]

Stock ₹64 crore (including ₹4 crore obsolete), debtors ₹52 crore (of which ₹9 crore > 90 days and ₹3 crore from a group company), creditors for goods ₹18 crore. Margins: stock 25%, debtors 40%. Sanctioned CC ₹60 crore; outstanding ₹38 crore. Compute DP and available limit.

Q2. Monthly revolver with DP constraint [L2]

For April–September, pre-financing cash flows are (₹ crore): +4, −6, −9, −12, +3, +8. Opening cash ₹5 crore, minimum cash ₹5 crore, opening CC ₹20 crore, sanctioned ₹50 crore, DP by month 42, 40, 38, 36, 40, 45. Ignore interest. Build the revolver schedule and identify any funding gap.

💡 Hint: Track cumulative pre-financing cash against the CC balance month by month; the binding constraint can switch between sanctioned limit and DP.

Q3. Covenant test on the budget [L2]

Budgeted FY 2026-27: EBITDA ₹96 crore, depreciation ₹28 crore, finance cost ₹14 crore (₹9 crore on term debt), PAT ₹40 crore, term loan principal due ₹30 crore, closing borrowings ₹230 crore, cash and liquid funds ₹20 crore, lease liabilities ₹18 crore. Covenants: Net debt/EBITDA ≤ 2.5× (debt includes leases), DSCR ≥ 1.3×. Test both and propose two budget actions if either fails.

Q4. Minimum cash policy [L3]

A company's 13-week forecasts show intra-month troughs of ₹8–14 crore around the 20th (GST) and month-end payroll; average monthly disbursements are ₹120 crore; undrawn committed limits average ₹25 crore but fall to ₹6 crore in the festive peak. Propose a minimum cash policy (amount and form — cash, liquid funds, undrawn lines) and justify it.

Q5. Stock statement mismatch [L3]

At year-end, the September quarterly stock statement filed with the bank showed inventory of ₹88 crore while the books showed ₹79 crore. Explain the disclosure implications under Schedule III and CARO 2020, the likely causes, and how you would prevent the mismatch through the budget/forecast process.
