Subtopic note

Capex Classification: Maintenance, Growth, Regulatory/ESG, Project vs Blanket

Scope: Capex classification: maintenance vs growth vs regulatory/ESG; project vs blanket capex

Why it matters

The capex budget is usually the single largest discretionary cash outflow in a manufacturing or consumer goods AOP. How each rupee is classified decides three things: which approval gate it must pass, whether it is protected or cut when the cash envelope is tight, and how analysts and lenders read the company's free cash flow. Equity research on NSE-listed FMCG and auto names routinely splits capex into "maintenance" and "growth" to estimate sustainable free cash flow, and rating agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE) do the same when they test debt-funded expansion. If the budget does not carry a classification tag per line, FP&A cannot answer the CFO's first question in a cash crunch: "What is the minimum capex to keep the plants running and compliant?"

Core notes

Maintenance capex

Maintenance (sustaining, "stay-in-business") capex keeps existing capacity, quality and safety at their current levels. Examples: replacing a worn-out filling line motor, re-roofing a warehouse, overhauling a boiler, refreshing end-of-life laptops. It does not add volume or margin; it prevents decline.

Budget-setting approaches:

  • Engineering-led asset register review — each plant lists assets reaching end of life, overhaul cycles and condition-based replacements. Most defensible method.
  • Depreciation proxy — maintenance capex ≈ 80–120% of annual depreciation. Crude, because historical cost depreciation understates replacement cost in an inflationary environment, and freshly built plants need far less than depreciation.
  • % of gross block — e.g. 2–3% of gross block of plant and machinery. Useful as a sanity check at LRP level.

Maintenance capex is treated as quasi-committed: if it is cut, the cost reappears later as breakdowns, quality failures or safety incidents.

Growth capex

Growth (expansion) capex increases capacity, enters new geographies or products, or improves unit economics (automation, debottlenecking, energy efficiency with a payback). It must be justified on incremental cash flows — NPV, IRR and payback against a hurdle rate (covered in Week 21). Growth capex is discretionary and is where capital rationing happens: projects are ranked and funded down to the capex envelope from the top-down target letter.

A useful sub-split for FMCG firms:

Sub-type Example Typical gate
Capacity expansion New ₹120 crore detergent plant in Gujarat Full AR, Board approval, IRR > hurdle
Debottlenecking Additional ₹4 crore packing machine removing a bottleneck AR, payback < 3 years
Cost-reduction / productivity Solar rooftop, automation of palletising Payback / IRR, often lower risk premium
Commercial Visi-coolers, display units at retailers Return per asset deployed, ROI tracked per outlet
Strategic / digital ERP upgrade, DMS rollout to distributors Strategic case plus qualitative benefits

Regulatory, safety and ESG capex

This is spend required by law, licence conditions or binding commitments — Pollution Control Board consent conditions (effluent treatment plant, zero liquid discharge), Factories Act safety requirements, fire NOC upgrades, BIS/FSSAI compliance, Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) obligations under the Plastic Waste Management Rules, and Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) commitments for the top 1,000 listed entities under SEBI LODR Reg 34(2)(f). ESG capex also includes voluntary but Board-committed decarbonisation (renewable power purchase, electric forklifts).

Regulatory capex typically fails a pure NPV test because the "benefit" is avoiding closure, penalties or licence loss. Treat it as non-discretionary with a lighter financial gate but a strict cost-efficiency review: the question is not "should we" but "what is the cheapest compliant option". Voluntary ESG capex should still carry an internal carbon price or a stated qualitative rationale so it can be ranked.

Project vs blanket capex

The second axis is how the money is approved and tracked, independent of purpose.

  • Project capex — a discrete, identifiable investment with its own appropriation request (AR), scope, budget, timeline, milestones and post-investment review. Tracked as a WBS element or internal order in SAP (project systems module) and accumulated in CWIP until capitalised.
  • Blanket capex — a pooled annual allocation for many small, recurring items below the individual AR threshold (e.g. each item < ₹10 lakh): IT peripherals, small tools, minor civil works, furniture. Approved once as a pool during the AOP; individual items are released by the plant head or function head within DoA limits. Tracked against the pool, not per item.

Blanket pools are efficient but leak: managers split a ₹40 lakh purchase into four ₹10 lakh POs to stay below the AR threshold ("PO splitting"). Controls include cumulative vendor/item reporting, a rule that related items are aggregated, and internal audit sampling.

Building the classification into the budget model

Each capex line in the AOP template should carry at least these tags:

Field Values
Purpose class Maintenance / Growth / Regulatory-ESG
Approval type Project (AR no.) / Blanket pool
Entity, plant, cost centre From master data
Asset class Schedule II category (drives useful life and depreciation)
Priority Must-do / Should-do / Could-do
Cash phasing profile Link to milestone payment profile (see s04)
Expected capitalisation month Drives depreciation start (see s05)

With a structured table, summarising is a SUMIFS on the class column:

=SUMIFS(tblCapex[FY27_Budget], tblCapex[Class], "Maintenance", tblCapex[Plant], $B$3)

Illustration

A ₹500 crore turnover consumer goods company drafts ₹62 crore of capex for FY 2026-27:

Item ₹ crore Class Type
Line 3 filler replacement 6.0 Maintenance Project
Plant blanket (tools, minor civil) 4.5 Maintenance Blanket
IT refresh pool 1.5 Maintenance Blanket
ETP upgrade per PCB consent 8.0 Regulatory Project
New 30,000 TPA plant (Phase 1) 35.0 Growth Project
Visi-coolers for 2,000 outlets 7.0 Growth Blanket (per-outlet ROI rule)
Total 62.0

Non-discretionary capex = 6.0 + 4.5 + 1.5 + 8.0 = ₹20 crore. If the CFO's envelope is ₹50 crore, the ₹12 crore cut falls on growth items — Phase 1 rephasing or fewer coolers — not on the filler or ETP. Without the tags, the instinct is a proportional 19% cut across all lines, which damages compliance and reliability.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mixed-purpose projects. Replacing an old line with a faster one is part maintenance (like-for-like) and part growth (extra capacity). Split the cost: like-for-like replacement value as maintenance, the increment as growth, and evaluate only the increment on IRR.
  • Lease vs capex. Under Ind AS 116, a leased warehouse creates a right-of-use asset, not capex in the cash sense. Budgets often show ROU additions separately so the capex envelope is not gamed by switching to leases. Decide the policy upfront.
  • Capex vs revenue expenditure. Repairs that only restore original performance are expensed (Ind AS 16.12); replacement of a significant component is capitalised (Ind AS 16.13) with the old component derecognised. A "maintenance capex" line may actually be opex — misclassification inflates EBITDA.
  • Software and SaaS. SaaS subscriptions are generally service contracts (opex); configuration/customisation costs are often expensed per the IFRIC agenda decision on cloud computing arrangements. ERP "capex" budgets frequently overstate what can be capitalised.
  • Commercial assets at customer sites. Visi-coolers remain the company's PPE; ensure physical verification and tagging, or they become write-offs.
  • Blanket threshold calibration. Too low a threshold floods the capex committee; too high lets significant spend escape scrutiny. Review the threshold annually against the distribution of item sizes.

Key takeaways

  • Tag every capex line by purpose (maintenance / growth / regulatory-ESG) and approval type (project / blanket).
  • Maintenance and regulatory capex are quasi-committed; growth capex is where capital rationing happens.
  • Depreciation is a weak proxy for maintenance capex; use an asset-register-based estimate.
  • Regulatory capex is tested for cost-efficiency, not NPV.
  • Blanket pools need anti-splitting controls and periodic threshold review.
  • Classification supports free-cash-flow analysis, envelope cuts and investor communication.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classifying a capex list [L1]

A dairy company's draft FY 2026-27 capex contains: (a) ₹3.2 crore replacement of a pasteuriser of the same capacity; (b) ₹9 crore new UHT line adding 1.5 lakh litres/day; (c) ₹2.1 crore zero liquid discharge system mandated by the State Pollution Control Board; (d) ₹0.8 crore pool for laptops and printers, no item above ₹1 lakh; (e) ₹1.5 crore rooftop solar with a 4-year payback. Classify each by purpose and by approval type, and state which you would protect first if the envelope were cut by 25%.

Q2. Mixed-purpose replacement [L2]

An existing filling line (capacity 120 bottles/minute, replacement cost of an identical line ₹4 crore) is to be replaced by a 200 bottles/minute line costing ₹6.5 crore. The extra capacity is expected to generate incremental contribution of ₹1.1 crore per year for 8 years. The company's hurdle rate is 13%. Explain how you would split the ₹6.5 crore between maintenance and growth, and set out which cash flows should be used to evaluate the growth portion.

💡 Hint: Only the increment over like-for-like replacement should be tested against the hurdle rate.

Q3. Depreciation as a maintenance proxy [L2]

A company built a new plant in FY 2024-25 at ₹200 crore with Schedule II life of 15 years, and also runs a 25-year-old plant with gross block ₹80 crore that is almost fully depreciated. Total depreciation is about ₹14 crore. A board member proposes maintenance capex equal to depreciation. Critique the proposal and describe a better method for each plant.

Q4. Blanket capex control design [L3]

Internal audit finds that in a plant with a blanket capex threshold of ₹10 lakh per item, a single vendor received 11 POs of ₹9.2–9.8 lakh each for "conveyor sections" within six weeks. Design the controls (system, policy and reporting) that would prevent or detect this, and explain how you would adjust the AOP template to support them.

Q5. Lease vs capex envelope [L3]

The CFO has set a capex envelope of ₹50 crore. The supply chain head proposes leasing a ₹30 crore warehouse (10-year lease, ₹4.2 crore annual rent) instead of building one, saying it "frees up the capex envelope". Explain the Ind AS 116 balance sheet and cash flow effects, and recommend how the AOP governance should treat ROU additions relative to the capex envelope.
