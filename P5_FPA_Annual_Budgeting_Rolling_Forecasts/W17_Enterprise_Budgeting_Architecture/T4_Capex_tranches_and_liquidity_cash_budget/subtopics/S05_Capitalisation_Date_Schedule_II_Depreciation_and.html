Subtopic note

Capitalisation Date, Schedule II Depreciation and Ind AS 23 in the Budget

Scope: Capitalisation date driving depreciation start (Companies Act Schedule II useful lives) and Ind AS 23 borrowing cost capitalisation

Why it matters

The capex budget feeds the P&L through two lines: depreciation and finance cost. Both hinge on one date — when the asset is available for use. A project slipping from June to November moves five months of depreciation out of the year and keeps interest on project borrowings in CWIP instead of the P&L. For a ₹200 crore project, that can move EBITDA-to-PBT bridges by several crore and change the budgeted EPS. FP&A must therefore budget capitalisation dates explicitly, by asset class, and apply Schedule II useful lives and Ind AS 23 consistently with how the statutory accounts will be prepared at year-end.

Core notes

Capitalisation date

Under Ind AS 16.55, depreciation begins when the asset is available for use — in the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of operating in the manner intended by management. It does not wait for commercial production or full capacity. Practical evidence: commissioning certificate, successful trial run / PG test, handover from projects to operations.

Costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to that condition are capitalised (Ind AS 16.16–17): purchase price including non-refundable duties, site preparation, installation, professional fees, testing costs. Since the 2022 amendment to Ind AS 16 (effective FY 2022-23), proceeds from selling items produced during testing are recognised in profit or loss, not deducted from cost. Costs after the asset is available for use — start-up losses, initial operating losses while demand builds, relocation and reorganisation — are not capitalised (Ind AS 16.19–20).

Schedule II useful lives

Schedule II to the Companies Act 2013 prescribes indicative useful lives. A company may adopt a different life or residual value if justified by technical evaluation, and must disclose the justification (Schedule II Part A). Residual value is generally not more than 5% of original cost unless justified. Selected lives (Part C):

Asset class (Schedule II) Useful life (years)
Factory buildings 30
Buildings other than factory buildings (RCC frame) 60
Plant and machinery — general (not otherwise specified, single shift) 15
Furniture and fittings (general) 10
Motor cars (other than used in business of running them on hire) 8
Office equipment 5
Computers — end-user devices (desktops, laptops) 3
Computers — servers and networks 6
Electrical installations and equipment 10

Schedule II also requires:

  • component accounting where a part of an asset is significant relative to the total and has a different useful life (aligned with Ind AS 16.43);
  • pro rata depreciation for additions/disposals from/to the date of addition/disposal;
  • extra shift: for plant and machinery (other than items marked NESD — no extra shift depreciation), depreciation for the period of double-shift use increases by 50% and for triple-shift use by 100% (Schedule II Part C notes) — a multi-shift FMCG plant must budget shift patterns, not just capex;
  • intangibles follow Ind AS 38, not Schedule II.

Under Ind AS, the method (SLM or WDV), useful life and residual value are reviewed at least each financial year-end (Ind AS 16.51, 16.61) and changes are a change in estimate.

Budget depreciation mechanics

For each capitalisation tranche:

\[ \text{Monthly depreciation} = \frac{\text{Capitalised cost} - \text{Residual value}}{\text{Useful life (years)} \times 12} \]

starting from the capitalisation month (pro rata days if the policy is daily; most budgets use the month of capitalisation in full or from the following month — state the convention). Excel, with capitalisation month in $D7, monthly timeline header in row 5:

=IF(AND(F$5>=$D7, F$5<=EDATE($D7, $E7*12)-1), ($C7*(1-$G7))/($E7*12), 0)

where $C7 = cost, $E7 = life in years, $G7 = residual %. Use a "depreciation waterfall" (capitalisation month in rows, budget months in columns) when there are many tranches.

Existing assets: depreciation on the opening gross block is taken from the fixed asset register (FAR), projected forward with known disposals and fully depreciated assets dropping out. A common budget error is applying an average rate to the opening gross block, including fully depreciated assets.

Ind AS 23 — borrowing costs

Borrowing costs directly attributable to the acquisition, construction or production of a qualifying asset — one that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use (commonly interpreted as 12 months or more, though not specified in the standard) — are capitalised as part of the asset's cost (Ind AS 23.8). Others are expensed.

Rules:

  • Commencement (Ind AS 23.17): when expenditure on the asset is incurred, borrowing costs are incurred, and activities necessary to prepare the asset are undertaken (including technical and administrative work before physical construction).
  • Suspension (Ind AS 23.20): during extended periods in which active development is suspended (not for temporary delays that are a necessary part of the process).
  • Cessation (Ind AS 23.22): when substantially all activities necessary to prepare the asset for intended use are complete. For assets completed in parts, cease for each part usable while construction continues on others (Ind AS 23.24).
  • Specific borrowings (Ind AS 23.12): actual borrowing costs incurred less investment income on temporary investment of those borrowings.
  • General borrowings (Ind AS 23.14): apply a capitalisation rate — the weighted average of borrowing costs on general borrowings outstanding — to the expenditure on the asset; the amount capitalised cannot exceed borrowing costs incurred. Following the 2019 amendment, specific borrowings remaining outstanding after the asset is ready become part of general borrowings.
  • Borrowing costs include interest under the effective interest method, finance charges on lease liabilities, and exchange differences on foreign currency borrowings to the extent regarded as an adjustment to interest cost (Ind AS 23.6).

Illustration

A ₹90 crore plant: construction from April 2026, capitalisation planned 1 October 2027. Specific term loan ₹60 crore drawn on 1 April 2026 at 9.5%; temporary surplus parked in liquid funds earned ₹0.40 crore in FY 2026-27. Expenditure beyond the term loan (₹20 crore spent evenly during FY 2026-27, average ₹10 crore) is funded from general borrowings with a capitalisation rate of 10%.

Component (FY 2026-27) ₹ crore
Specific: \(60 \times 9.5\%\) 5.70
Less investment income (0.40)
General: \(10 \times 10\%\) 1.00
Borrowing cost capitalised in CWIP 6.30

P&L finance cost for FY 2026-27 excludes ₹6.30 crore. If capitalisation slips to March 2028, FY 2027-28 capitalised interest rises and depreciation start moves out — both improve FY 2027-28 PBT, which is why auditors challenge whether the asset was really not "available for use" earlier.

Depreciation for FY 2027-28 (plant and machinery, 15 years, 5% residual, capitalised cost say ₹100 crore including ₹9 crore borrowing costs, capitalised 1 October 2027):

\[ \frac{100 \times 0.95}{15} \times \frac{6}{12} = ₹3.17 \text{ crore} \]

Tax view (deferred tax)

Under the Income-tax Act, depreciation is block-based WDV (plant and machinery 15%), with half-rate if the asset is put to use for less than 180 days in the year; interest on borrowings for acquiring an asset, for the period till the asset is first put to use, is capitalised under the proviso to Sec 36(1)(iii). Differences between book and tax WDV create deferred tax (Ind AS 12) — the budget's tax schedule in Topic 5 needs both depreciation streams.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • "Ready but not used." Depreciation starts when available for use even if idle due to lack of demand; idle assets still depreciate (Ind AS 16.55).
  • Trial-run output. Post-2022, revenue from trial production goes to P&L along with the cost of producing it (Ind AS 2); do not net it against CWIP in the budget.
  • Phased commissioning. If Line 1 is ready in July and Line 2 in December, capitalise and depreciate separately and stop borrowing-cost capitalisation for Line 1 (Ind AS 23.24).
  • Short projects. A 4-month installation is usually not a qualifying asset — interest is expensed.
  • Extended suspension. A 7-month halt for funding issues or litigation suspends capitalisation; budget the interest in P&L for that period.
  • CWIP ageing. Schedule III (2021) requires CWIP ageing (< 1 year, 1–2, 2–3, > 3 years) and completion schedules for overdue/over-budget projects — budgeting realistic capitalisation dates keeps these disclosures credible.

Key takeaways

  • Depreciation starts when the asset is available for use (Ind AS 16.55), not when production stabilises.
  • Use Schedule II lives (or technically justified alternatives), component accounting and pro rata depreciation by capitalisation month.
  • Budget depreciation on existing assets from the FAR, excluding fully depreciated items.
  • Ind AS 23 capitalises borrowing costs on qualifying assets between commencement and cessation; specific vs general borrowings follow different rules.
  • Capitalisation slippage shifts both depreciation and interest — model capitalisation dates as explicit inputs.
  • Keep book and tax depreciation separately for deferred tax.

Practice Questions

Q1. Depreciation waterfall [L1]

Budget capitalisations for FY 2026-27: ₹6 crore plant and machinery in July (15 years), ₹1.8 crore laptops in May (3 years), ₹12 crore factory building in December (30 years). Residual value 5% for all except laptops (nil). Using the month-of-capitalisation-in-full convention, compute FY 2026-27 depreciation for each.

Q2. Borrowing cost capitalisation [L2]

A company starts a ₹150 crore project on 1 June 2026, expected to be ready on 30 September 2027. It draws a specific loan of ₹80 crore at 9% on 1 June 2026 and earns ₹0.6 crore on temporary investment in FY 2026-27. Expenditure in FY 2026-27 is ₹110 crore, incurred evenly June–March. General borrowings: ₹200 crore at 10% and ₹100 crore at 8.5% outstanding all year. Work activity was halted for litigation from 1 November 2026 to 31 January 2027. Compute borrowing costs to be capitalised in FY 2026-27.

💡 Hint: Determine the suspension period first; compute the general borrowing capitalisation rate as a weighted average.

Q3. Slippage impact on budgeted P&L [L2]

For the Q2 project, compute the FY 2027-28 P&L impact (depreciation and finance cost) if capitalisation slips from 30 September 2027 to 31 January 2028. Assume capitalised cost of ₹165 crore, 15-year life, 5% residual, and total project-related interest of ₹1.3 crore per month after FY 2026-27.

Q4. Audit challenge on available-for-use date [L3]

A plant completed PG tests in August 2026, but management proposes a capitalisation date of January 2027 because "commercial production at 60% utilisation began only then". Interest of ₹2 crore per month is being capitalised. Discuss the Ind AS 16 and Ind AS 23 position, the P&L effect of each date, and the evidence you would ask for.

Q5. Component accounting in the budget [L2]

A ₹40 crore furnace includes a refractory lining costing ₹6 crore that must be replaced every 4 years; the shell lasts 20 years. Explain how you would budget depreciation and future replacement capex, and how the replacement in year 4 would be accounted for.
