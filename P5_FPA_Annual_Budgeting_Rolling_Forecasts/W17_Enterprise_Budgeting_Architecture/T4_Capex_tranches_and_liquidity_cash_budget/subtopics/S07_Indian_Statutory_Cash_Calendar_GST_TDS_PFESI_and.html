Subtopic note

Indian Statutory Cash Calendar: GST, TDS, PF/ESI and Advance Tax

Scope: Indian statutory cash calendar: GST (GSTR-3B by 20th), TDS (7th), PF/ESI (15th), advance tax (15 Jun/Sep/Dec/Mar)

Why it matters

Statutory payments are the least flexible outflows in the cash budget: missing them triggers interest, penalties, disallowances and, for TDS and PF, potential prosecution of officers in default. They also arrive in predictable spikes — the 7th, 15th and 20th of every month, plus the four advance-tax dates — which create intra-month troughs that a monthly budget hides. For a ₹1,000 crore consumer goods company, net GST alone can be ₹15–25 crore a month and advance tax instalments ₹10–20 crore each. Building a statutory calendar into both the monthly cash budget and the 13-week forecast is a core FP&A and treasury skill, and an area where the learner's compliance background is a direct advantage.

Core notes

Master calendar (monthly obligations)

Obligation Due date Base Budget driver
TDS/TCS deposit (Income-tax) 7th of following month; for March, 30 April TDS deducted on salary (Sec 192), contractors (194C), professionals (194J), rent (194I), purchase of goods (194Q), etc. Payroll TDS schedule; vendor payments × applicable rates
PF (EPF, EPS, EDLI, admin) 15th of following month (ECR filing) Employee 12% + employer 12% of PF wages, plus EDLI 0.5% and admin 0.5% Payroll model (Topic 3)
ESI 15th of following month Employer 3.25% + employee 0.75% of gross wages for employees earning ≤ ₹21,000/month Payroll model
GST (net cash after ITC) via GSTR-3B 20th of following month for monthly filers (QRMP taxpayers: 22nd/24th by state, with monthly PMT-06 payments by 25th) Output tax − eligible ITC; reverse-charge liability always paid in cash GST schedule from sales and purchases
GSTR-1 (outward supplies return, no payment) 11th of following month — Not a cash event, but drives customers' ITC
Professional tax State-specific (e.g. Maharashtra: by end of month for employers with larger liability; Karnataka: 20th) Slab per employee Headcount by state
Labour welfare fund State-specific, often half-yearly Per employee Headcount by state

Under the Labour Codes (in force from November 2025), the definition of "wages" for PF, ESI and gratuity is aligned (wages must be at least 50% of total remuneration), and the Code on Social Security consolidates EPF and ESI contribution provisions — due dates in practice continue to be the 15th pending any change in the rules; verify each budget cycle.

Advance tax

Companies pay advance tax on estimated current-year income (Sec 207–211 of the Income-tax Act 1961; carried into the Income-tax Act 2025 effective 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections). Cumulative minimum instalments:

Due date Cumulative % of estimated tax liability
15 June 15%
15 September 45%
15 December 75%
15 March 100%

Shortfalls attract interest under Sec 234C (1% per month on the instalment shortfall, generally for 3 months per instalment, 1 month for the last); if advance tax paid is below 90% of assessed tax, Sec 234B interest at 1% per month applies from 1 April of the assessment year. Tax deducted at source on the company's receipts (e.g. TDS on interest, rent, or under Sec 194Q/194C by customers) reduces the advance tax payable. The balance is paid as self-assessment tax before filing the return.

Budget mechanics: estimated tax liability = budgeted PBT adjusted for permanent and timing differences × tax rate (25.168% under Sec 115BAA), less TDS credits expected. Re-estimate at each instalment date using the latest forecast — a strong H1 should increase the September instalment.

Building the GST cash line

Net GST cash for month \(m\) is paid on the 20th of month \(m+1\):

\[ \text{GST cash}_{m+1} = \text{Output GST}_m - \text{Eligible ITC}_m + \text{RCM liability}_m \]

ITC utilisation order matters: IGST credit is used first against IGST, then CGST and SGST (in any order/proportion, per Sec 49A/49B and rules); CGST credit cannot be used for SGST and vice versa. In a multi-state company, one state may pay cash while another accumulates credit — GST is registration-wise (state-wise GSTIN), so budget the GST cash by GSTIN, not in total.

Because gross receipts from customers include GST and payments to suppliers include GST, the cash budget must:

  1. include GST in customer collections and supplier payments, and
  2. show net GST paid to the government as a separate line in the following month.

Budgeting receipts and payments ex-GST and omitting the GST line understates both gross flows and misses the 20th-of-month spike — a named pitfall.

Excel implementation

Use a statutory calendar table with columns Obligation, AccrualMonth, PaymentDate, Lag, Rule and compute payment month from accrual month:

=EDATE(EOMONTH([@AccrualMonth],-1)+1, 1) + [@DueDay] - 1

then aggregate accrued liabilities into the payment month with SUMIFS. For the weekly forecast, PaymentDate falls into a week bucket via the calendar table. Advance tax uses a separate table of the four dates and cumulative percentages:

=MAX(0, EstTax * XLOOKUP(F$5, tblAdvTax[Date], tblAdvTax[CumPct], 0) - CumPaidBefore)

A company's March-quarter data (₹ crore):

Item Jan Feb Mar
Output GST 18.0 16.5 21.0
Eligible ITC (incl. capex IGST) 12.0 13.5 11.0
TDS deducted (salary + vendors) 2.2 2.1 3.4

Cash outflows: GST 6.0 on 20 Feb, 3.0 on 20 Mar, 10.0 on 20 Apr; TDS 2.2 on 7 Feb, 2.1 on 7 Mar, 3.4 by 30 Apr; PF/ESI 3.0 on 15 Feb, 3.0 on 15 Mar, 3.1 on 15 Apr. If estimated tax for the year is ₹48 crore and ₹36 crore (75%) has been paid by December, ₹12 crore is due on 15 March. March's outflow thus includes ₹12 crore advance tax plus ₹8.1 crore of other statutory payments — the March peak is mostly statutory.

Consequences of delay (for prioritisation)

Obligation Consequence of late payment
TDS Interest 1.5% per month from deduction to payment (Sec 201(1A)); disallowance of expense under Sec 40(a)(ia)/(i) if not deducted/paid; prosecution risk
GST Interest 18% p.a. on net cash liability (Sec 50 CGST Act); late fee on returns; customers' ITC unaffected by payment but affected by non-filing of GSTR-1
PF Interest under Sec 7Q (12% p.a.) and damages under Sec 14B of the EPF Act; employee contribution not deposited by due date disallowed under Sec 36(1)(va) (per Supreme Court in Checkmate Services, 2022)
ESI Interest and damages under the ESI Act; same Sec 36(1)(va) disallowance for employee share
Advance tax Sec 234B/234C interest

Employer contributions are deductible under Sec 43B if paid before the return due date, but employee contributions must be deposited by the statutory due date to be deductible — a sharp budgeting point for stressed companies tempted to delay PF.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Due-date extensions. CBIC and CBDT periodically extend dates (e.g. portal issues); budget on standard dates and treat extensions as upside timing.
  • QRMP vs monthly filers. Smaller entities (turnover up to ₹5 crore) can file quarterly with monthly payments; group companies may differ.
  • Exporters and inverted duty structures accumulate ITC and file refund claims; refund timing (often 1–3 months, longer if scrutinised) is a receipt line to budget explicitly.
  • Electronic cash vs credit ledger. Excess cash deposited in the electronic cash ledger is not lost but is not liquid until refunded — avoid over-depositing.
  • March TDS is due 30 April, which shifts a significant outflow out of the financial year — this affects year-end cash and creditors.
  • Advance tax on one-off gains (e.g. sale of land) must be paid in the instalment following the gain; failure leads to 234C interest.
  • Rules change: Labour Code rules and Income-tax Act 2025 transition may alter section references and some procedural dates; refresh the calendar every AOP.

Key takeaways

  • Monthly spikes: TDS on the 7th, PF/ESI on the 15th, GST on the 20th; plus advance tax on 15 June, September, December and March at 15/45/75/100% cumulative.
  • Budget GST gross in receipts and payments, then net GST cash as a separate line by GSTIN.
  • Re-estimate advance tax at each instalment from the latest forecast; reduce for TDS credits.
  • March TDS is due 30 April — a year-end timing nuance.
  • Employee PF/ESI must be paid by the due date to be deductible; never treat it as a funding source.
  • Keep a statutory calendar table and map payment dates into both monthly and weekly forecasts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Statutory payments by date [L1]

For August 2026 accruals — output GST ₹9.4 crore, eligible ITC ₹6.1 crore, RCM liability ₹0.3 crore, TDS deducted ₹1.25 crore, PF (all components) ₹1.8 crore, ESI ₹0.22 crore — list each payment, its due date and amount in September 2026.

Q2. Advance tax re-estimation [L2]

Original budgeted PBT for FY 2026-27 is ₹160 crore (115BAA, effective rate 25.168%; permanent disallowances ₹4 crore; book depreciation exceeds tax depreciation by ₹6 crore). Expected TDS credits ₹3 crore. After H1, the forecast PBT rises to ₹185 crore. Compute instalments due on 15 June and 15 September (original estimate for June; revised for September), and those for December and March on the revised estimate.

💡 Hint: Instalments are cumulative percentages of estimated tax net of TDS, less amounts already paid.

Q3. Multi-state GST cash [L2]

A company has GSTINs in Maharashtra and Uttarakhand. In October, Maharashtra: output tax ₹6 crore, ITC ₹2 crore; Uttarakhand (manufacturing): output tax ₹3 crore, ITC ₹5 crore. Explain the November GST cash outflow, why credit cannot be transferred across GSTINs, and how you would model this in the budget.

Q4. Payment prioritisation under stress [L3]

In a month where cash is ₹6 crore short of commitments, the plant controller proposes delaying PF (₹2.4 crore, of which ₹1.2 crore employee share), GST (₹3.1 crore) and a supplier payment to an MSME (₹1.5 crore, 50 days old). Evaluate each option's cost and legal consequences and recommend a payment priority order.

Q5. Building the statutory calendar table [L2]

Design an Excel statutory calendar table and the formulas needed to move monthly accrued liabilities (GST, TDS, PF, ESI, PT for two states, advance tax) into payment months and payment weeks. Include the special case for March TDS.
