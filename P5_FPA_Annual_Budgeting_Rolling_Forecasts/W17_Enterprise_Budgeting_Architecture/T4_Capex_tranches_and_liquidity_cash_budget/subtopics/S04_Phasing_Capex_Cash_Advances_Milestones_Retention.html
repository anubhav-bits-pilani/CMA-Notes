Subtopic note

Phasing Capex Cash: Advances, Milestones, Retention, LC and Duty

Scope: Phasing cash outflows: advances, milestone payments, retention money, import LC/duty timing vs accrual/CWIP

Why it matters

The capex line in the budgeted balance sheet (additions to CWIP and PPE) and the capex line in the cash budget are different numbers in almost every month. Advances leave cash before any asset is recognised; retention money stays in the bank long after the asset is capitalised; import payments under usance letters of credit can fall 90–180 days after shipment; customs duty and IGST are paid at the port on a date set by the bill of entry. A cash budget that phases capex cash equal to accrual will be wrong in both timing and amount, and treasury will either over-borrow or face a shortfall when the advance is due. This is one of the topic's named pitfalls.

Core notes

Accrual view vs cash view

Schedule III requires capital advances to be presented separately under "Other non-current assets", not within CWIP, and capex creditors are normally presented within "Other financial liabilities" (not trade payables). The budget balance sheet should carry both lines explicitly.

Advances

Domestic equipment suppliers typically demand 10–30% advance with the PO, often backed by an advance bank guarantee (ABG) from the vendor. Civil contractors receive a mobilisation advance (5–10%) recovered pro rata from running account (RA) bills. For budgeting:

  • advance cash month = PO month (or contractual days after PO);
  • advance recovery reduces later milestone cash, not later accrual;
  • the advance balance at month-end = cumulative advances paid − cumulative recovered.

Milestone payments

Equipment contracts commonly pay on milestones such as: 20% advance, 60% against proforma/despatch documents, 10% on erection, 10% on PG test. Civil works are paid on monthly RA bills certified by the project engineer, net of advance recovery, retention and TDS. Each contract's payment terms should be captured as a percentage profile linked to milestone dates.

Retention money

Retention (typically 5–10% of each RA bill or the final payment) is withheld to secure performance and defect rectification, and released after the defect liability period (12–24 months after commissioning) or earlier against a performance bank guarantee. Retention is a liability from the date the work is billed; the cash leaves at release. In the budget it is a delayed outflow, which matters when the release falls in the budget year from projects commissioned last year.

TDS on contractor and professional payments

Payments to civil contractors attract TDS under Sec 194C (1% for individual/HUF, 2% for others); fees for technical services/consultants under Sec 194J (2% for technical services, 10% for professional services). TDS reduces the cash paid to the vendor in the payment month but is deposited with the government by the 7th of the following month (see s07). Total cash outflow is unchanged; the timing splits. Under the Income-tax Act 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), these provisions continue with renumbered sections — check the current section references.

Import capex: LC, customs duty and IGST

For imported machinery:

  1. LC opening — the importer's bank issues a letter of credit; LC margin money (often 10–25% of LC value, lower for strong credits) may be blocked as a fixed deposit. Margin money is restricted cash, not available for operations.
  2. Shipment — under a sight LC, payment is made on presentation of documents (roughly at shipment + transit of documents); under a usance LC, payment is due 90/180 days after bill of lading, often with buyer's credit.
  3. Bill of entry / customs clearance — Basic Customs Duty (BCD), Social Welfare Surcharge (10% of BCD) and IGST on the assessable value plus BCD and SWS are paid before clearance. Project imports can be classified under Customs Tariff heading 9801 (project imports) with a concessional rate, subject to registration of the contract.
  4. Freight, insurance, clearing and forwarding — paid around arrival.

BCD and SWS are capitalised; IGST is creditable as input tax credit (subject to CGST Act conditions; note Sec 16(3) — ITC is not available if depreciation is claimed on the tax component under the Income-tax Act). IGST is therefore an outflow at the port and a reduction of GST cash payable when it is set off in the GSTR-3B for that month.

FX: budget the foreign currency amount at the budget FX rate; if the payment is hedged with a forward contract, use the forward rate for the cash budget. Exchange differences on settlement of the creditor are not capitalised under Ind AS 21 (except to the extent they qualify as borrowing cost adjustments under Ind AS 23.6(e)), unless the company had elected the Ind AS 101 D13AA option to continue the previous Indian GAAP policy for long-term foreign currency monetary items existing at transition.

CWIP accrual peaks in September when goods arrive (17.20 + 1.42 + clearing), but most cash leaves in February. A budget that put ₹18.6 crore of capex cash in September would show a false liquidity squeeze in Q2 and miss the Q4 outflow.

Building the schedule

For each project row: contract value, payment profile (advance %, milestone %s, retention %), milestone dates, retention release lag, GST rate and creditability, import flag, FX currency, LC tenor. The monthly cash formula aggregates milestone percentages by date (as in s03) and adds tax cash lines separately. Reconcile monthly using the cash capex identity (c06):

\[ \text{Cash capex} = \text{Additions} + \text{Opening capex creditors} - \text{Closing capex creditors} + \text{Closing capital advances} - \text{Opening capital advances} \]

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Buyer's credit converts a sight payment into a short-term foreign currency loan; the supplier is paid on time but the company's cash leaves at buyer's credit maturity. Model it as a financing inflow/outflow, not as delayed capex.
  • Capital advances for land often sit for years pending conversion/registration; stamp duty and registration charges are capitalised and paid at registration.
  • GST on works contracts for immovable property: ITC is blocked under Sec 17(5)(c)/(d) of the CGST Act (except plant and machinery), so GST on factory building construction is a cost — capitalised and not creditable.
  • Vendor-funded deferred payment may contain a significant financing component; the asset is recognised at the cash price equivalent (Ind AS 16.23) and the difference is interest.
  • Retention released against bank guarantee accelerates cash; budget based on contract clause, not habit.
  • Restricted cash: LC margin money and earmarked balances must be excluded from available cash in the liquidity budget.

Key takeaways

  • Capex accrual (CWIP additions) and capex cash differ in timing every month; model them separately.
  • Advances leave first, retention leaves last; capital advances and capex creditors are separate balance sheet lines.
  • Import capex cash splits into LC margin, supplier payment at LC maturity, and duty/IGST at clearance.
  • BCD/SWS are capitalised; IGST on plant and machinery is generally creditable — cash at port, recovery via GSTR-3B.
  • TDS on contractor bills shifts part of the cash to the following month's 7th.
  • Reconcile accrual to cash monthly using the capex creditors and advances roll-forward.

Practice Questions

Q1. Monthly cash vs CWIP schedule [L2]

A ₹12 crore (excluding GST at 18%, fully creditable) domestic boiler is ordered in May 2026 with terms: 20% advance with PO, 70% on delivery (October), 10% retention released 12 months after commissioning (commissioned December 2026). Advance is adjusted against the delivery invoice. Prepare month-by-month CWIP additions, capital advance balance, capex creditor balance and cash outflow (including GST) for April 2026 to March 2027.

💡 Hint: GST is charged on each invoice; the advance itself may attract GST depending on whether it is for goods or services — state your assumption.

Q2. Import capex timing [L2]

Machinery worth EUR 1.5 million is shipped on 10 July 2026 under a 120-day usance LC. Budget rate ₹96/EUR; forward cover booked at ₹98.4/EUR for the maturity date. BCD 7.5%, SWS 10% of BCD, IGST 18%, clearance on 5 August 2026. LC margin 10% blocked from LC opening in May until maturity. Prepare the cash flow timeline, identify which amounts are capitalised, and state the rate you would use in the cash budget.

Q3. Journal entries for a contractor bill [L1]

A civil contractor (company) submits an RA bill of ₹50 lakh plus 18% GST for factory building works. Terms: 10% mobilisation advance recovery, 5% retention, TDS under Sec 194C at 2% on the bill value excluding GST. Pass the journal entries for booking and paying the bill, and comment on the GST credit position.

Q4. Reconciling capex accrual and cash [L2]

Budgeted CWIP/PPE additions for FY 2026-27 are ₹48 crore. Opening capex creditors ₹6 crore, closing ₹9.5 crore (including ₹3 crore retention). Opening capital advances ₹2 crore, closing ₹5 crore. Compute cash capex and explain each reconciling item to the CFO in one sentence each.

Q5. Review of a draft cash budget [L3]

You are reviewing a plant's cash budget where capex cash equals the CWIP additions column month by month and GST on capex is excluded. List the errors this creates, how you would detect them from the budgeted balance sheet, and the redesign you would ask for.
