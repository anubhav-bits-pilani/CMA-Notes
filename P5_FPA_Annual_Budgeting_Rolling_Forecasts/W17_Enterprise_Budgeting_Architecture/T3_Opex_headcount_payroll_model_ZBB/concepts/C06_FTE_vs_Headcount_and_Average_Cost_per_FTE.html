Key concept

FTE vs Headcount and Average Cost per FTE

Concept: FTE vs headcount: FTE = Σ (months employed ÷ 12) per role; Average cost per FTE = Payroll ÷ Average FTE

Statement

\[ \text{FTE}_{\text{year}} = \sum_{p} \frac{M_p}{12} \times f_p \]

where \(M_p\) is the number of months position \(p\) is filled in the year and \(f_p\) its working-time fraction (1 for full-time, 0.5 for half-time). Average FTE equals the average of monthly FTE:

\[ \overline{\text{FTE}} = \frac{1}{12} \sum_{m=1}^{12} \sum_p a_{p,m} f_p \]

with \(a_{p,m} = 1\) if the position is filled in month \(m\). Headcount is a point-in-time count of people (e.g., closing headcount at 31 March). Unit cost:

\[ \text{Average cost per FTE} = \frac{\text{Payroll cost for the year}}{\overline{\text{FTE}}} \]

Explanation

Headcount answers "how many people do we have on a date?"; FTE answers "how much paid working capacity did we have over the period?" They differ whenever people join or leave during the year, or work part-time.

Why the distinction matters:

  • Unit cost: dividing annual payroll by closing headcount understates cost per person in a growing team (new hires inflate the denominator but contribute only part-year cost) and overstates it in a shrinking one. Use average FTE.
  • Budget review: if budgeted average FTE is close to closing headcount despite many new hires, start months are probably missing (a key pitfall of this topic).
  • Productivity metrics: revenue per FTE, cost per FTE and span of control should use FTE for flows (cost, revenue) and headcount for structure (spans, org charts).
  • Headcount caps (Topic 1, target letter) are usually set on closing headcount; cost caps on payroll. Both are needed: a team can respect a closing headcount cap while front-loading hires and overspending, or vice versa.
  • Contractors and part-timers: report them as FTE separately so workforce capacity is complete.

Illustration

Analytics team, FY 2027-28:

Position Months filled FTE Annual cost (₹ lakh)
Head (full year) 12 1.00 52.0
Senior analyst (full year) 12 1.00 19.6
Analyst (exits 30 September) 6 0.50 5.2
Analyst backfill (from 1 December) 4 0.33 3.6
Data engineer (from 1 August) 8 0.67 15.6
Part-time statistician (50%, full year) 12 0.50 9.0
Total 4.00 105.0

Opening headcount 4 (head, senior analyst, analyst, part-timer); closing headcount 5 (head, senior analyst, backfill, data engineer, part-timer). Average cost per FTE = ₹105.0 lakh ÷ 4.00 = ₹26.25 lakh. Dividing by closing headcount gives ₹21.0 lakh — understating unit cost by 20% and making next year's per-head budget look too low.

Practice Questions

Q1. FTE and unit cost [L1]

A team has 10 people on 1 April; 3 join on 1 July, 2 on 1 October; 2 leave on 31 December; one employee moves to 60% part-time from 1 January. Annual payroll is ₹3.6 crore. Compute average FTE, closing headcount, average cost per FTE and cost per closing head.

💡 Hint: Count FTE-months for each group and divide by 12.

Q2. Using FTE in catchball [L2]

A BU's budget shows closing headcount rising from 420 to 470 and payroll rising 9% with a 9% July increment. Explain why this combination is suspicious, what FTE analysis you would run, and what the corrected payroll might look like if the 50 hires are spread evenly across the year.

Q3. Monthly FTE in Excel [L2]

Write a SUMPRODUCT formula that computes monthly FTE from tblRoster using start month, exit month and a working-time fraction column, and a formula for average FTE across the year.
