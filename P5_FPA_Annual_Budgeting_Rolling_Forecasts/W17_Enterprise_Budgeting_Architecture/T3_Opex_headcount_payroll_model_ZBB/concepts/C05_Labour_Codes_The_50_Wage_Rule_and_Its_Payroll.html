Key concept

Labour Codes: The 50% Wage Rule and Its Payroll Budget Impact

Concept: Labour Codes (in force from Nov 2025): "wages" must be ≥ 50% of total remuneration, raising PF/gratuity base for many CTC structures

Statement

The Code on Wages, 2019 (Sec 2(y)), and the Code on Social Security, 2020, use a uniform definition of wages: all remuneration expressed in money, including basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, but excluding specified items — among them employer contributions to pension/PF, HRA, conveyance allowance or travel concession, overtime allowance, commission, bonus not forming part of remuneration, gratuity and retrenchment compensation, value of house accommodation and certain amenities. The proviso: if the excluded items (other than certain statutory items) exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages:

\[ W = W_{\text{incl}} + \max\left(0, \; E - 0.5 \times R\right) \]

where \(W\) is wages for statutory purposes, \(W_{\text{incl}}\) the included components (basic + DA + retaining allowance, plus allowances not listed as exclusions), \(E\) the excluded components, and \(R\) total remuneration \(= W_{\text{incl}} + E\). Equivalently, \(W \ge 0.5 \times R\). The four Labour Codes were brought into force from 21 November 2025, with rules and transitional provisions being notified in stages.

Explanation

Indian CTC structures historically kept basic at 30–40% of pay and paid the rest as HRA, special allowance and flexible benefits, minimising PF, gratuity and leave encashment bases. The Codes attack this in two ways:

  1. The 50% floor on wages — if exclusions are more than half of remuneration, the excess becomes wages.
  2. The inclusive definition — allowances not specifically excluded (notably a general "special allowance") are part of wages, consistent with the Supreme Court's 2019 ruling in Vivekananda Vidyamandir on universally paid allowances for PF.

Budget impacts:

  • Gratuity is computed on wages; if wages rise above basic, the gratuity base rises for all past service — an Ind AS 19 past service cost recognised immediately in P&L when the change takes effect. Several large listed Indian companies recognised one-time charges in their December 2025 quarter results; for budgets, the ongoing current service cost is higher.
  • PF: for employers contributing on actual wages, the base rises; for those capped at ₹15,000, the effect is limited to employees whose basic was below ₹15,000.
  • Leave encashment valued on wages rises.
  • Bonus and ESI use their own definitions and thresholds; check each.
  • Take-home pay: if CTC is held constant, higher employee PF reduces net pay; companies may restructure CTC (e.g., raise basic, reduce special allowance) — cost-neutral on CTC but not on gratuity.

Because interpretation of individual allowances and transitional rules is still evolving, the budget should flag the assumption explicitly and include a sensitivity.

Illustration

Monthly total remuneration ₹1,00,000: basic ₹30,000, special allowance ₹15,000, HRA ₹30,000, conveyance ₹10,000, LTA ₹10,000, employer NPS ₹5,000.

  • Included: basic ₹30,000 + special allowance ₹15,000 = ₹45,000.
  • Excluded: HRA, conveyance, LTA, employer NPS = ₹55,000, which exceeds 50% of ₹1,00,000 by ₹5,000.
  • Wages = ₹45,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹50,000.
Cost (per employee per year) Old base (basic ₹30,000) New base (wages ₹50,000) Increase
Gratuity accrual at 4.81% 17,316 28,860 11,544
Employer PF at 12% on actual 43,200 72,000 28,800
Leave encashment (18 days ÷ 30) 18,000 30,000 12,000

For 800 such employees, the annual increase is about ₹4.2 crore (≈ ₹1.88 crore excluding PF if PF is capped), plus a one-time past-service gratuity cost on the increased base.

Practice Questions

Q1. Applying the 50% test [L1]

Compute statutory wages for: (a) basic ₹25,000, HRA ₹12,500, special allowance ₹12,500 (total ₹50,000); (b) basic ₹20,000, HRA ₹16,000, conveyance ₹4,000, LTA ₹6,000, food coupons ₹2,000 (treated as excluded), special allowance ₹12,000 (total ₹60,000); (c) basic ₹40,000, HRA ₹40,000, LTA ₹10,000, special allowance ₹10,000.

💡 Hint: Classify each component as included or excluded, then test the excluded total against half of remuneration.

Q2. Budget sensitivity [L2]

A GCC with 2,500 employees has average monthly remuneration of ₹1,20,000 with basic at 35%, HRA 17.5%, special allowance 40%, other exclusions 7.5%. PF is capped at ₹15,000 for all. Compute the annual increase in gratuity and leave encashment accrual (20 days on wages ÷ 30) if special allowance is treated as wages, and present a sensitivity where it is not.

Q3. CTC restructuring advice [L3]

Management proposes restructuring CTCs to keep statutory wages exactly at 50% to minimise the impact. Analyse the effects on employer cost, employee take-home pay, employee relations and compliance risk, and recommend a budget assumption for FY 2027-28.
