Key concept

ZBB Principle and the Contents of a Decision Package

Concept: ZBB: every rupee justified from zero each cycle; decision package = purpose, cost, benefits, alternatives, consequences of not funding

Statement

Zero-Based Budgeting requires that each activity's budget for the next cycle be built and justified from a zero base rather than from the prior year's spend:

\[ B_{t+1} = \sum_{k \in F} C_k, \qquad F = \{k : \text{package } k \text{ ranks above the funding cut-off}\} \]

where \(C_k\) is the cost of decision package \(k\) and \(F\) the set of funded packages. A decision package is the document that justifies an activity at a given level of effort and contains, at minimum:

  1. Purpose — the objective and the need it serves.
  2. Cost — opex, capex and headcount by month.
  3. Benefits — quantified outcomes (incremental NSV, savings, risk avoided, compliance met) and KPIs.
  4. Alternatives — other ways of achieving the purpose (different method, vendor, scale, in-house vs outsourced) and why the proposed one is preferred.
  5. Consequences of not funding — what happens to the business if the package is rejected.

Explanation

The zero base does not mean every activity is assumed to be worthless; it means no spending is presumed. Incremental budgeting asks "what more do you need?"; ZBB asks "why should we spend anything, and how much is enough?". The five elements force that reasoning:

  • Purpose ties spend to strategy (and to Kaplan–Norton strategy-map objectives).
  • Cost must be driver-based and phased, so it can be loaded and tracked.
  • Benefits allow ranking by return; for support activities, use service-level or risk metrics where financial return cannot be measured.
  • Alternatives are the heart of ZBB: they often reveal cheaper ways to achieve the same outcome (digital instead of TV for a regional campaign; shared services instead of BU-level teams).
  • Consequences of not funding distinguish essential packages (statutory compliance, safety) from desirable ones and give decision-makers an honest view of risk.

Each package is prepared at a specific level of effort (minimum, current, enhanced — s06), and packages are ranked and funded down to the envelope. A good package is short (one to two pages), quantified and verifiable — long narrative packages are the main reason ZBB acquired its paperwork reputation.

Illustration

Decision package — "Detergent rural sampling programme (enhanced level)":

Element Content
Decision unit Brand marketing — Home care
Purpose Increase trial of 1 kg detergent in 6,000 villages in UP, Bihar and MP where penetration is below 30%, supporting the rural distribution initiative
Cost ₹5.5 crore: sampling packs ₹3.1 crore, promoter agency ₹1.8 crore, logistics ₹0.6 crore; phased July–November
Benefits 18 lakh households sampled; expected conversion 12% to regular users; incremental NSV ₹12 crore in FY 2027-28 and ₹20 crore in FY 2028-29; KPI: repeat purchase rate in sampled villages
Alternatives (a) Retailer-led trial packs at ₹5 (cost ₹2.8 crore, expected NSV ₹7 crore); (b) haat (village market) activation only (cost ₹1.9 crore, expected NSV ₹4.5 crore); proposed option preferred because it reaches non-buying households directly
Consequences of not funding Rural distribution expansion (12,000 new outlets) will run with low consumer pull; expected rate of sale in new outlets 25% lower; risk of distributor dissatisfaction
Level of effort Enhanced (minimum level = alternative (b))

At a 42% contribution margin, first-year ROI = (12 × 0.42 − 5.5) ÷ 5.5 = −8%, but two-year ROI = ((12 + 20) × 0.42 − 5.5) ÷ 5.5 = +144%. The ranking committee must decide whether multi-year benefits are admissible — a policy that should be set before packages are submitted.

Practice Questions

Q1. Writing a decision package [L2]

Prepare a decision package at the "current" level for a ₹1.8 crore internal audit co-sourcing contract, including purpose, cost, benefits, two alternatives and consequences of not funding.

💡 Hint: Consider statutory requirements for internal audit under the Companies Act when describing consequences.

Q2. Evaluating alternatives [L2]

An IT decision unit proposes renewing on-premise CRM licences (₹2.4 crore per year including AMC) versus moving to SaaS (₹2,300 per user per month for 750 users plus ₹1.2 crore one-time migration). Build the alternatives section with a three-year cost comparison and non-financial factors.

Q3. Single-year vs multi-year ROI policy [L3]

Draft the ranking policy for a ZBB process covering marketing, training and IT, specifying when multi-year benefits may be counted, how they are discounted, and how claimed benefits are validated and later audited.
