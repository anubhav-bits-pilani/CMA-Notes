Key concept

Gratuity: Formula, ₹20 Lakh Ceiling and the 4.81% Budget Proxy

Concept: Gratuity = 15/26 × last drawn (Basic + DA) × completed years; ceiling ₹20 lakh; budget proxy ≈ 4.81% of basic (15/26 ÷ 12)

Statement

Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 (now subsumed in the Code on Social Security, 2020):

\[ G = \min\left(\frac{15}{26} \times W_{\text{last}} \times n, \; \text{₹}20{,}00{,}000\right) \]

where \(W_{\text{last}}\) is the last drawn monthly wages (basic + DA under the 1972 Act; "wages" as defined in the Code from 21 November 2025), \(n\) the number of completed years of service (a fraction above six months counts as a full year), 15 the days of wages per year and 26 the working days in a month. Eligibility: five years of continuous service (not required on death or disablement; one year for fixed-term employees under the Code). Seasonal establishments use 7 days instead of 15.

Budget proxy: each year of service adds \(\frac{15}{26} \times W\) to the entitlement, i.e., as a percentage of annual wages

\[ \frac{15/26}{12} = \frac{15}{312} \approx 4.81\% \]

so the annual accrual ≈ 4.81% × annual basic (or Code wages).

Explanation

Gratuity is a defined benefit: the company pays based on final salary and total service, not on contributions. In Ind AS 19 accounts, it is measured by an actuary using the Projected Unit Credit method with assumptions for discount rate, salary escalation, attrition and mortality; the P&L charge is current service cost plus net interest, with remeasurements in OCI.

The 4.81% proxy is a budget convenience. It approximates the current-year service accrual at current pay but ignores:

  • Past-service step-up: an increment raises the liability for all past years (final-salary design), so the true charge exceeds 4.81% in high-increment, long-tenure workforces.
  • Vesting and attrition: employees who leave before five years get nothing, so in a young, high-attrition workforce the true charge is lower.
  • Discounting and interest: actuarial service cost is discounted; net interest adds a further charge.
  • The ₹20 lakh ceiling: for senior long-tenure employees, the cap limits the liability (the employer may pay more under its own policy; amounts above ₹20 lakh are taxable under Sec 10(10)).

Good practice: use the actuary's projected next-year service cost and interest (disclosed in Ind AS 19 reports) as the budget for the total, and use 4.81% only to allocate it across departments and months. Cash: funded plans (LIC/insurer group gratuity schemes) require annual contributions; unfunded plans pay on exit. Tax: provision is deductible only for contributions to an approved gratuity fund (Sec 40A(7)) or on actual payment.

Illustration

An employee with last drawn basic + DA ₹50,000 per month resigns after 12 years and 7 months.

  • Completed years: 12 years 7 months → 13 (more than six months).
  • Gratuity = 15/26 × ₹50,000 × 13 = ₹3,75,000 (below the ₹20 lakh ceiling).

Budget accrual for the same employee in a year: 4.81% × ₹6,00,000 = ₹28,860. If her basic rises 10% next year, her entitlement on 13 years rises by 15/26 × ₹5,000 × 13 = ₹37,500 — more than the annual proxy — which is why actuarial charges exceed the proxy for long-tenure staff.

Ceiling case: basic ₹2,40,000 per month and 15 years: 15/26 × 2,40,000 × 15 = ₹20,76,923 → capped at ₹20,00,000.

Practice Questions

Q1. Computing gratuity on exit [L1]

Compute gratuity for: (a) basic + DA ₹36,000, service 7 years 5 months; (b) basic + DA ₹36,000, service 4 years 11 months on resignation; (c) basic + DA ₹1,80,000, service 22 years; (d) a fixed-term employee with wages ₹30,000 completing 2 years 2 months under the Code.

💡 Hint: Check eligibility and the six-month rounding rule before applying the formula.

Q2. Proxy vs actuarial [L2]

A company's payroll basic is ₹80 crore. The actuary's report projects next-year current service cost of ₹4.9 crore and net interest of ₹1.1 crore. Compare with the 4.81% proxy and explain which workforce characteristics might explain the difference. How would you budget and allocate it?

Q3. Labour Code base change [L2]

An employee's basic is ₹30,000 and special allowance ₹25,000 (treated as wages under the Code), with 9 years of service at 21 November 2025. Compute the gratuity entitlement on the old and new bases and the increase in the liability, and explain how Ind AS 19 treats such an increase.
