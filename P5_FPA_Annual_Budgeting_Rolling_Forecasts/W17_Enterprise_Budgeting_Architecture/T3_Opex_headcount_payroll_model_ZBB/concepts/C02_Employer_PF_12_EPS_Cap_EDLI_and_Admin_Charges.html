Key concept

Employer PF: 12%, EPS Cap, EDLI and Admin Charges

Concept: Employer PF = 12% of PF wages (EPS 8.33% capped at ₹15,000 wage = ₹1,250/month); plus EDLI 0.5% and admin 0.5%

Statement

For an employee with monthly PF wages \(W\) (basic + DA + retaining allowance under the EPF Act; "wages" under the Code on Social Security from 21 November 2025), and the employer's chosen contribution base \(W_c\) (either \(W\) or \(\min(W, 15{,}000)\)):

\[ \text{Employer PF} = 12\% \times W_c \]

split as

\[ \text{EPS} = 8.33\% \times \min(W_c, 15{,}000) \le \text{₹}1{,}250, \qquad \text{EPF (employer share)} = 12\% \times W_c - \text{EPS} \]

plus employer-only charges:

\[ \text{EDLI} = 0.5\% \times \min(W_c, 15{,}000) \le \text{₹}75, \qquad \text{Admin} = 0.5\% \times W_c \]

(admin subject to a minimum of ₹500 per month per establishment). Total employer cost = \(12\% \times W_c + \text{EDLI} + \text{Admin}\). The employee contributes a further 12% (a deduction from pay, not an employer cost).

Explanation

The EPF scheme is mandatory for employees whose PF wages are up to ₹15,000 per month at joining; above that, coverage is voluntary for new joiners (but once a member, always a member). Employers may restrict both contributions to ₹15,000 or contribute on actual wages — a CTC design choice that materially affects cost for higher earners.

The split between EPS and EPF does not change the employer's total 12% — it only determines where the money goes. Employees who joined after 1 September 2014 with wages above ₹15,000 are not eligible for EPS membership, so their entire employer share goes to EPF. Employees who opted for higher pension following the Supreme Court's November 2022 judgment have EPS on actual wages, again changing the split but not the 12% total.

Budget implications:

  • Model PF on the correct base: under the Labour Codes, the wage base may include allowances previously outside basic (c05) — for employers contributing on actual wages, this increases cost.
  • Caps make PF a step-like cost: an increment for a capped employee does not change PF.
  • EDLI and admin are often forgotten because they are outside CTC.
  • Cash: employer and employee contributions are due by the 15th of the following month via the ECR; late payment attracts interest (Sec 7Q, 12% p.a.) and damages (Sec 14B), and late deposit of the employee share is disallowed under Sec 36(1)(va) of the Income-tax Act.
  • Exempted establishments running their own PF trusts must budget any interest-rate shortfall versus the EPFO declared rate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Computing PF for a mixed population [L1]

Compute monthly employer PF, EDLI and admin for: (a) basic ₹9,500; (b) basic ₹15,000; (c) basic ₹28,000 with PF capped; (d) basic ₹28,000 with PF on actual basic. Show the EPS/EPF split for each.

💡 Hint: EPS is always on wages capped at ₹15,000, whatever the employer's base.

Q2. Policy change cost [L2]

A company with 1,200 employees (average basic ₹32,000; 900 currently on capped PF, 300 on actual) proposes moving all to actual-basic PF as a retention measure from 1 October 2027. Compute the additional FY 2027-28 employer cost including admin charges.

Q3. Late deposit consequences [L2]

The March 2028 PF (employee share ₹42 lakh, employer share ₹44 lakh) is deposited on 10 May 2028 because of a cash crunch. Explain the interest, damages and income-tax consequences and how the cash budget and covenant headroom analysis should prevent this.
