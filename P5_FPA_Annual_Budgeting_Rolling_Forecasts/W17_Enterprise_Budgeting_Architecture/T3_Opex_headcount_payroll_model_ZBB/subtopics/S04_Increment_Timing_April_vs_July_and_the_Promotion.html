Increment Timing (April vs July) and the Promotion Budget Pool

Scope: Annual appraisal/increment timing (April vs July effective) and promotion budget pool

Why it matters

The annual increment is the single largest driver of year-on-year payroll growth. In India, merit increments typically range from 7% to 10% (higher in tech and for top performers), and the effective date — 1 April for many Indian companies, 1 July (or later) for others, 1 January for some MNC subsidiaries — determines how much of the increment hits the budget year. A three-month difference in the effective date on a ₹100 crore payroll at 9% is worth more than ₹2 crore. Promotions add a separate pool with its own timing. FP&A must model both precisely and consistently with HR's appraisal calendar, and understand the knock-on effects on statutory costs and the cash budget.

Core notes

The appraisal calendar

Step April-effective company July-effective company
Performance year April–March April–March
Ratings calibrated April–May April–May
Increment letters May–June June–July
Effective date 1 April (retrospective) 1 July
Arrears April–May (or June) arrears paid in June/July payroll None
Budget months at new pay 12 9

In April-effective companies the increment is decided after the effective date, so arrears are paid in the first payroll after letters are issued. For the budget, the P&L cost is from April (accrued) even though cash catches up later — an accrual vs cash phasing difference the cash budget must capture.

Cost formula

For a position with pre-increment annual fixed gross \(S_0\), increment \(g\), effective month \(m^{*}\) (April = 1) and active months between start and exit:

\[ \text{Annual cost} = \sum_{m = \text{start}}^{\text{exit}} \frac{S_0}{12} \times \left(1 + g \times [m \ge m^{*}]\right) \]

where \([m \ge m^{*}]\) is 1 from the effective month onward (c01). For a full-year employee:

\[ \text{Increment cost in year} = S_0 \times g \times \frac{13 - m^{*}}{12} \]

Illustration: pre-increment payroll run-rate ₹60 crore, increment 9%.

Effective date Months at new pay Increment cost in FY Total fixed payroll
1 April 12 ₹5.40 crore ₹65.40 crore
1 July 9 ₹4.05 crore ₹64.05 crore
1 October 6 ₹2.70 crore ₹62.70 crore

The carry-over (annualisation) effect matters for next year: with a July date, ₹1.35 crore of this year's increment falls into next year's base even before next year's increment.

Merit matrix and pool

HR allocates increments by rating and position in the salary band (compa-ratio = current salary ÷ band midpoint). Example merit matrix:

Rating \ Compa-ratio < 0.90 0.90–1.10 > 1.10
A (top 15%) 14% 12% 9%
B (60%) 10% 8.5% 6.5%
C (20%) 6% 5% 3%
D (5%) 0% 0% 0%

The weighted average must equal the merit pool approved in the AOP (e.g., 8.5% of eligible payroll). In the budget, either apply the pool % uniformly (fast, accurate in total) or apply the matrix by employee using prior ratings (more precise by department, but ratings are not known at budget time — use last year's distribution). Exclude ineligible employees (joined after the cut-off date, serving notice, on PIP) and apply pro-rata increments where policy requires (e.g., 50% for those who joined in Q3).

Promotion budget pool

Promotions are budgeted as a pool, typically 1–2% of payroll, because individual promotions are not known at AOP time:

\[ \text{Promotion cost} = \text{Eligible payroll} \times p \times u \times \frac{13 - m_p}{12} \]

where \(p\) is the proportion of employees promoted, \(u\) the average promotion uplift (e.g., 10–15% over the merit increment) and \(m_p\) the promotion effective month. Example: eligible payroll ₹65 crore, 8% promoted, uplift 12%, effective 1 October → ₹65 crore × 0.08 × 0.12 × 6/12 = ₹0.31 crore. Hold the pool in a central HR line or allocate pro rata to departments.

Knock-on effects

  • Statutory costs: PF on actual basic and gratuity accrual rise with basic; ESI and bonus eligibility may end if the increment pushes wages above ₹21,000 (from the next contribution period for ESI). For capped-PF employees, PF does not change.
  • Variable pay: target bonus is usually a % of fixed pay — check whether the base is pre- or post-increment.
  • Gratuity liability: gratuity is computed on last drawn wages × all completed years, so an increment increases the liability for all past service. The 4.81% budget proxy captures only current service; the actuarial charge (Ind AS 19 service cost and interest) reflects the salary escalation assumption — reconcile with the actuary's projection.
  • Leave encashment liability similarly rises on all accumulated leave.
  • Minimum wage revisions: for plant and contract workers, state governments revise variable DA typically on 1 April and 1 October; budget these as mandatory increments separate from merit.
  • Long-term settlements with unions in manufacturing (three-to-four year wage agreements) fix increments contractually — use the settlement terms.

Excel implementation

=([@Fixed_Gross]/12)*(1+[@Merit_Pct]*(F$3>=Incr_Month)*[@Incr_Eligible])*(F$3>=[@Start_Month])*(F$3<=[@Exit_Month])

Incr_Month is a named cell on the assumptions sheet; changing it from 4 (July) to 1 (April) re-phases the entire payroll.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Retrospective increments decided late: if letters are issued in August effective 1 April, accrue from April in actuals (there is a constructive obligation once the practice is established); in the budget, the P&L follows the effective date and cash shows arrears in August.
  • Increment freeze scenarios: keep the merit % as an input so a freeze can be modelled by setting it to zero for some grades.
  • Market corrections for retention (special increments for critical skills) — budget as a separate pool, not inside merit.
  • Different effective dates by population (management in July, union staff per settlement, minimum wage staff in April/October) — use effective month per row, not one global month.
  • New hires joining just before cut-off often negotiate an increment — check policy (commonly no increment if joined after 1 January).

Key takeaways

  • The effective date determines how many months of the increment fall in the budget year: April = 12, July = 9, October = 6.
  • April-effective increments decided in June create arrears — P&L from April, cash later.
  • The merit matrix (rating × compa-ratio) must average to the approved merit pool on eligible payroll.
  • Budget promotions as a pool: payroll × % promoted × uplift × months remaining ÷ 12.
  • Increments flow into PF, gratuity, leave, variable pay and ESI/bonus eligibility — model at component level.
  • Use per-row effective months for different populations and a named cell for the main increment month.

Practice Questions

Q1. Effective date sensitivity [L1]

A department's pre-increment annual fixed payroll is ₹14.4 crore. Compute FY 2027-28 fixed payroll for a 9.5% increment effective 1 April, 1 July and 1 September, and the carry-over into FY 2028-29 for each.

💡 Hint: Months at new pay = 13 − effective month index (April = 1).

Q2. Merit matrix calibration [L2]

Eligible payroll is ₹50 crore distributed: A 15% (half below compa-ratio 0.90, half within band), B 60% (all within band), C 20% (within band), D 5%. Using the merit matrix in these notes, compute the weighted average increment and the adjustment needed to the B-rating cell to land at a pool of 8.5%.

Q3. Arrears and cash [L2]

Increments of 8% on a ₹30 crore annual payroll are effective 1 April 2027 but letters are issued in late June and arrears paid with July salaries. Show the monthly P&L payroll cost and the monthly cash salary payments for April–August 2027, ignoring statutory costs.

Q4. Promotion pool and statutory knock-on [L3]

A company has eligible payroll of ₹120 crore (basic 40%, all PF on actual basic). Merit 9% from 1 July; promotions for 7% of employees with 15% uplift effective 1 October. Compute the increment and promotion cost for FY 2027-28 including employer PF (12%) and gratuity accrual (4.81% of basic), and explain why the actuarial gratuity charge may differ materially from your figure.
