Subtopic note

Zero-Based Budgeting: Decision Units, Packages, Ranking and Cut-Off

Scope: Zero-Based Budgeting: decision units, decision packages, levels of effort, ranking and funding cut-off

Why it matters

Incremental budgeting — last year's actual plus inflation plus new requests — embeds every past inefficiency in the base. Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB) reverses the burden of proof: each activity must justify its existence and its level of funding from zero in every cycle. Developed by Peter Pyhrr at Texas Instruments around 1969–70 and adopted by the State of Georgia under Jimmy Carter and later the US federal government, ZBB fell out of favour because of its paperwork burden, then returned in the 2010s in FMCG and consumer companies as a cost-discipline system. For FP&A, ZBB is the tool of choice for discretionary spend — marketing, consulting, travel, events, IT projects — and this week's exercise requires a Marketing ZBB.

Core notes

Decision units

A decision unit is the lowest organisational or activity level for which a manager can meaningfully define objectives and costs — a cost centre, a programme, a function (e.g., "Brand marketing — hair oil", "Internal audit", "Corporate travel"). Each decision unit has a manager who prepares its decision packages. Choose decision units small enough to be understood but large enough to avoid thousands of packages.

Decision packages

A decision package describes a discrete activity or service level that can be funded or not (c07). Each contains:

  • Purpose/objective of the activity.
  • Description of actions and resources (people, spend).
  • Cost (opex, capex, headcount) by month.
  • Benefits — quantified where possible (incremental NSV, savings, risk reduction, compliance).
  • Alternatives — different ways of performing the activity (in-house vs outsource, digital vs TV).
  • Consequences of not funding — what happens if the package is rejected.
  • Performance measures to track if funded.

Levels of effort

For each decision unit, the manager prepares incremental packages at different levels of effort:

Level Meaning Example: hair oil brand marketing
Minimum (base) Lowest level below which the activity is not viable; often below current spend (e.g., 70–80%) Maintain digital always-on presence and POS materials: ₹4.0 crore
Current Incremental package to reach current service level Add Diwali TV burst at current weight: +₹5.0 crore
Enhanced Incremental packages above current, each separately justifiable Add regional influencer programme: +₹2.0 crore; add new variant launch support: +₹3.5 crore

Packages are incremental and cumulative: the "current" package can only be funded if the minimum package is funded. Alternatives (e.g., TV vs digital for the Diwali burst) are evaluated within the decision unit before the preferred one is submitted.

Ranking

Managers rank their own packages; then higher levels consolidate and re-rank across decision units. Ranking criteria:

  • Mandatory first: statutory and contractual commitments (statutory audit, regulatory compliance, contracted media).
  • Return: incremental contribution ÷ cost (ROI), payback, or a scoring model for non-financial benefits.
  • Strategic fit: alignment with strategy-map objectives (Topic 1).
  • Risk: consequences of not funding.

For marketing, a common ranking metric is incremental gross margin (or contribution) ÷ package cost:

\[ \text{ROI}_k = \frac{\Delta \text{NSV}_k \times \text{Contribution margin} - \text{Cost}_k}{\text{Cost}_k} \]

Funding cut-off

The total budget envelope (from the target letter) is applied to the ranked list: packages above the cut-off line are funded; those below are not. Packages near the line are debated; packages below may be placed in a "reserve" list for funding if in-year savings or upside arise.

Illustration — Marketing ZBB

Envelope ₹30 crore; contribution margin 45%.

Rank Package Level Cost (₹ crore) Incremental NSV (₹ crore) ROI Cumulative cost Funded?
1 Brand health research Mandatory 1.0 — n/a 1.0 Yes
2 Hair oil minimum (digital + POS) Minimum 4.0 16 0.80 5.0 Yes
3 E-commerce performance marketing Current 6.0 21 0.58 11.0 Yes
4 Hair oil Diwali TV Current 5.0 17 0.53 16.0 Yes
5 Soap summer campaign Current 7.5 22 0.32 23.5 Yes
6 Detergent rural sampling Enhanced 5.5 12 −0.02 29.0 Debate
7 Influencer programme Enhanced 2.0 4 −0.10 31.0 No (below line)
8 Cricket sponsorship Enhanced 6.5 9 −0.38 37.5 No

Packages 6–8 have negative first-year ROI; package 6 may still be funded if it builds long-term rural penetration (strategic fit), and the decision should be documented. ₹1.0 crore of headroom remains if package 6 is funded; it could go to a reserve.

ZBB process steps in the AOP

  1. Define decision units and the package template (with mandatory fields).
  2. Train managers; issue the envelope and ranking criteria.
  3. Managers build packages at levels of effort, with alternatives evaluated.
  4. Rank within units, then consolidate and re-rank across units (marketing committee, CFO).
  5. Apply the envelope cut-off; record funded and unfunded packages.
  6. Load funded packages into the budget by month and account; track spend and KPIs by package.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths Weaknesses
Removes base-year inertia; exposes low-value spend Time-consuming; heavy documentation
Forces alternatives and consequences to be considered Benefits of support functions hard to quantify
Links spend to outcomes and ROI Managers may game rankings or inflate benefits
Good fit for discretionary spend Poor fit for committed/fixed costs and payroll in the short term

The practical answer is targeted ZBB: apply it to discretionary categories every year and to other areas on a rotating cycle (every 2–3 years), and use ABB for transactional functions.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Long-term brand building shows low first-year ROI; use multi-year or brand-equity measures for some packages so ZBB does not starve brands.
  • Interdependent packages (e.g., a launch package requires research and POS packages) — bundle them or rank them together.
  • Committed costs (signed media contracts) are mandatory for this cycle; ZBB applies at renewal.
  • Benefit inflation: require benefits to be validated by RGM or finance; track actual vs claimed ROI to calibrate future submissions.
  • Reserve list: releasing below-the-line packages in-year should go through the DoA (Topic 1, c06), not automatically on underspend.

Key takeaways

  • ZBB justifies spending from zero each cycle through decision units and decision packages.
  • Packages are prepared at levels of effort — minimum, current, enhanced — and are incremental and cumulative.
  • Rank packages by mandatory status, ROI, strategic fit and risk, first within units then across units.
  • Apply the envelope as a funding cut-off; document debated and unfunded packages.
  • Best applied to discretionary spend annually and to other areas on a rotating basis.
  • Guard against benefit inflation and short-termism with validation and multi-year measures.

Practice Questions

Q1. Ranking and cut-off [L2]

Using the week's exercise package list (M1–M10) with a contribution margin of 42% and an envelope of ₹46 crore, compute ROI for each package, rank them (mandatory first), apply the cut-off and identify the packages to debate.

💡 Hint: Remember that M8 is mandatory with no NSV benefit.

Q2. Designing levels of effort [L2]

For the decision unit "Corporate training" (current spend ₹2.4 crore: leadership programme ₹0.9 crore, compliance training ₹0.3 crore, technical certifications ₹0.7 crore, e-learning licences ₹0.5 crore), design minimum, current and enhanced packages with costs, benefits and consequences of not funding.

Q3. Alternatives within a package [L2]

A brand team proposes a ₹6 crore Diwali TV campaign expected to add ₹18 crore NSV. An alternative digital-plus-regional-TV plan costs ₹4.2 crore with expected NSV of ₹14 crore. At a 45% contribution margin, compare the alternatives on ROI and incremental contribution and recommend which to submit, noting non-financial considerations.

Q4. ZBB critique [L3]

A CFO says ZBB "worked brilliantly in year one and then everyone went back to old habits". Explain why this happens and design three process changes to make ZBB sustainable, linking to category ownership.
