Subtopic note

Non-Payroll Opex: Drivers and Fixed, Variable and Step Behaviour

Scope: Non-payroll opex: rent, IT/SaaS licences, travel, professional fees, marketing; fixed vs variable vs step behaviour

Why it matters

Non-payroll operating expenses — rent and facilities, IT and SaaS licences, travel, professional fees, marketing and dozens of smaller lines — are where budget padding and "last year + inflation" habits are most common. They are also where cost behaviour is most varied: rent is contractually fixed, travel varies with the sales force, licences step up with users, marketing is discretionary. Classifying each line by behaviour and driver lets FP&A build it correctly, flex it later (Topic 6) and challenge it in catchball. It also determines which lines go through ZBB (s06–s07).

Core notes

Cost behaviour categories

Behaviour Definition Budget method Examples
Fixed (committed) Unchanged within the relevant range for the year; set by contract Contract schedule with escalation dates Office rent, annual maintenance contracts, statutory audit fee, insurance
Variable Proportional to an activity driver Driver volume × rate Freight per tonne, sales commission, payment gateway fees, per-transaction BPO charges
Step-fixed Fixed within a band of activity; jumps at thresholds Bands × cost per step Warehouse per 2,000 tonnes of throughput, SaaS tier pricing, supervisor per shift
Semi-variable (mixed) Fixed base plus variable element Fixed + rate × driver (split by high-low or regression) Electricity (demand charge + units), telecom plans, vehicle cost
Discretionary Set by management decision, not by activity Decision packages (ZBB) Marketing, training, consulting, CSR beyond mandatory

Note that "fixed" here is about cost behaviour, not about controllability — a discretionary cost is fixed once committed but can be changed at the next decision point.

Rent and facilities

Under Ind AS 116, most leases are on the balance sheet: the P&L shows depreciation of the right-of-use asset and interest on the lease liability, not rent. Short-term leases (≤ 12 months) and low-value assets may be expensed. The management budget usually shows both:

  • Cash rent (for the cash budget and for "pre-Ind AS 116" management EBITDA many companies still track), with escalation clauses (commonly 5% annually or 15% every three years) and security deposits.
  • Ind AS 116 effects (ROU depreciation and interest) for the statutory budgeted P&L, typically computed from a lease schedule.

Also budget CAM charges, property tax, electricity, housekeeping and security (contract labour — note minimum wage revisions), repairs.

IT and SaaS licences

Drivers: number of users or seats, modules, tiers, and contract dates. Typical structure: per-user-per-month subscription (CRM, collaboration, HRMS), annual enterprise agreements (ERP, database), cloud consumption (variable with usage), AMC on on-premise hardware.

\[ \text{SaaS cost}_m = \text{Users}_m \times \text{Price per user} \times \text{FX rate (if USD-billed)} \]

Budget points: prepayment of annual licences (cash in month of invoice, expense spread over the subscription period — a prepaid asset in the balance sheet); price escalations at renewal (often 5–10%); FX exposure on USD-priced SaaS; withholding tax/equalisation levy implications on foreign SaaS payments (equalisation levy on e-commerce supply was abolished from 1 August 2024, but TDS under Sec 195 may still apply depending on characterisation — check with tax); GST under reverse charge on imported services (input credit available). Tier pricing creates step behaviour: a CRM at ₹2,100 per user for up to 200 users may move to a different tier above.

Travel

Driver-based: trips × cost per trip by type (local conveyance, intra-state, domestic air, international), or ₹ per FTE per month by role (field sales vs corporate). Include hotels, per diem and visa costs. Travel is semi-discretionary: a sales force needs field travel (variable with reps and outlets covered), while conferences and internal meetings are discretionary — split them. Travel policy (class of travel, hotel caps, advance booking) is the main price lever and is owned by a category owner in ZBB variants (s07).

Professional fees

Mostly fixed by engagement letter: statutory audit, tax audit (Sec 44AB), internal audit, secretarial audit (Sec 204 for listed and large companies), cost audit (Sec 148 where applicable), legal retainers. Variable or project-based: consulting, litigation, transaction advisory, recruitment agencies (driven by hires, s03). Budget TDS (Sec 194J at 10%) and GST (18%, input credit) separately for cash.

Marketing

Marketing (A&P — advertising and promotion) is discretionary and usually the largest non-payroll line in FMCG (8–12% of NSV). Split into ATL (TV, digital, print), BTL (sampling, activation, POS material), research and agency fees. Trade spend that is variable consideration belongs in GTN (Topic 2), not marketing. Marketing is the classic ZBB area: build as decision packages with expected incremental NSV and ROI (s06). Phase by campaign calendar (festive, summer).

Inflation and price assumptions

Apply specific inflation rates by category from the central assumptions sheet (rent per contract; IT per vendor; travel at CPI or airfare outlook; contract labour per minimum wage revisions), rather than one blanket inflation rate.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Prepaid and annual contracts: the P&L is spread over the service period, cash on invoice — get both right for the cash budget and the prepaid balance.
  • Ind AS 116 lease modifications (renewals, area changes) mid-year change ROU and liability — budget them if known.
  • Capex vs opex: SaaS implementation costs are generally expensed unless they create a controlled intangible asset (IFRIC agenda decision on configuration/customisation in cloud computing arrangements); align the budget with accounting policy.
  • Allocated vs direct: shared facilities and IT should sit with one owner and be allocated (Topic 1, s06) rather than budgeted piecemeal.
  • Discretionary items disguised as fixed: "annual" conferences or retainers that are not contractually committed should be treated as discretionary and go through ZBB.

Key takeaways

  • Classify every opex line as fixed, variable, step, semi-variable or discretionary, and budget it with the matching method.
  • Rent is fixed with escalations; under Ind AS 116 the statutory P&L shows ROU depreciation and interest, while cash rent drives the cash budget.
  • IT/SaaS is user- and tier-driven, often prepaid and sometimes USD-priced; model steps, prepayments, FX and tax.
  • Split travel into activity-driven field travel and discretionary travel; professional fees into contractual and project-based.
  • Marketing is discretionary and belongs in ZBB; trade spend belongs in GTN.
  • Use category-specific inflation from the central assumptions sheet.

Practice Questions

Q1. Building a mixed opex budget [L2]

Compute FY 2027-28 budgets for: (a) a plant office lease at ₹8 lakh/month with 15% escalation from 1 January 2028; (b) a collaboration SaaS at USD 12 per user per month for 900 users rising to 1,050 from 1 October, budget rate ₹86/USD, billed annually in advance in April for the base users with a true-up in October; (c) field travel for 140 reps at ₹9,500 per rep per month, with 20 new reps from 1 August; (d) consulting of ₹1.2 crore discretionary. Show P&L and cash phasing for (b).

💡 Hint: For the SaaS, separate the expense recognition (monthly) from the cash (annual and true-up).

Q2. Step cost thresholds [L2]

A distribution centre can handle 2,500 tonnes per month per shift. Each additional shift costs ₹6.5 lakh per month (supervisors, security, power). Monthly throughput budget (tonnes, Apr–Mar): 2,200, 2,300, 2,400, 2,600, 2,700, 3,100, 4,200, 4,800, 3,600, 2,900, 2,500, 2,400. Compute the shift cost by month and the annual total, and comment on smoothing options.

Q3. Ind AS 116 vs cash rent in the budget [L3]

A new 9-year office lease starts 1 April 2027 at ₹20 lakh per month, escalating 15% every three years, with a 3-year lock-in; incremental borrowing rate 9%. Explain how the budgeted P&L, EBITDA, balance sheet and cash flow differ between management (cash rent) and Ind AS 116 views in FY 2027-28, and which you would use for BU opex targets.

Q4. Classifying opex lines [L1]

Classify each as fixed, variable, step, semi-variable or discretionary and name the driver: payment gateway charges, secretarial audit fee, data centre co-location racks, sales conference, mobile phone plans with data overage, canteen subsidy per meal, CSR spend under Sec 135.
