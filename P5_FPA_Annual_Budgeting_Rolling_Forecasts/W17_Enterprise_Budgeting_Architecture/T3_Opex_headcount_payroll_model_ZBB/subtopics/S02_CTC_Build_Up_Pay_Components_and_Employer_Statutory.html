Subtopic note

CTC Build-Up: Pay Components and Employer Statutory Costs

Scope: CTC build-up: basic, HRA, special allowance, variable pay, employer PF, gratuity, bonus, ESI, insurance, leave encashment

Why it matters

"Cost to Company" (CTC) is how Indian employers quote pay, but CTC is a contractual convention, not an accounting number. Two employees with the same CTC can cost the company differently depending on how basic pay is set, whether PF is capped, whether ESI and statutory bonus apply, and what CTC includes. For the budget, FP&A must decompose CTC into components, compute each employer cost with its own base, cap and timing, and avoid both double counting (employer PF already inside CTC) and omissions (EDLI, admin charges, leave encashment, insurance outside CTC). The Labour Codes have made the structure matter even more (c05).

Core notes

Components and their budget treatment

Component Typical design Base for Budget treatment
Basic (+ DA where applicable) 35–50% of fixed gross; DA common in manufacturing and PSUs PF, gratuity, leave encashment, bonus eligibility (Bonus Act) Monthly, from start to exit, increased from increment month
HRA 40–50% of basic (metro/non-metro) Excluded from "wages" under the Codes (subject to the 50% rule); included in ESI wages Monthly
Special allowance Balancing figure of fixed gross Part of "wages" under the Codes unless a listed exclusion; Supreme Court in Vivekananda Vidyamandir (2019) held universally paid allowances form basic wages for PF Monthly
Other allowances (conveyance, LTA, meal cards, telephone) Fixed amounts Mostly exclusions from wages Monthly or on claim
Variable pay / performance bonus % of fixed gross at target; paid annually or quarterly Not part of wages Accrue monthly at expected payout; cash on payout date
Employer PF 12% of PF wages (actual or capped at ₹15,000) — Monthly; cash by 15th of next month
EDLI and PF admin 0.5% EDLI (on wages capped at ₹15,000), 0.5% admin (on PF wages) — Monthly; often outside CTC
Gratuity 15/26 × wages × years; budget at 4.81% of basic — Monthly accrual (actuarial in actuals); cash on exit or fund contribution
Statutory bonus 8.33–20% of capped salary for eligible employees — Monthly accrual; payment within 8 months of year-end
ESI (employer) 3.25% of gross wages if ≤ ₹21,000/month — Monthly; cash by 15th
Insurance Group medical (GMC), group term life (GTL), group personal accident (GPA) premiums — Premium usually paid annually in advance; expense over cover period
Leave encashment Earned leave accrual (e.g., 15–30 days/year) valued at basic or gross per policy — Monthly accrual; cash on encashment or exit

Other employer costs to remember: Labour Welfare Fund (state-specific, small), professional tax (employee deduction, not employer cost, in most states), NPS employer contribution (if offered, up to 14% of basic + DA tax-efficient under the new regime), canteen and transport subsidies, and employee stock option charges (Ind AS 102, usually budgeted centrally).

Is employer PF inside CTC?

Most Indian offer letters show CTC = fixed gross + employer PF + gratuity + target variable pay (+ sometimes insurance). Other companies quote CTC excluding gratuity or insurance. The budget must start from a clear definition. Recommended practice: hold fixed gross (sum of basic, HRA, allowances) in the roster and compute every employer cost on top, then reconcile the total to the contractual CTC to confirm no double counting.

Fixed gross ₹10,00,000 per annum: basic 40% = ₹4,00,000 (₹33,333/month), HRA 50% of basic = ₹2,00,000, special allowance ₹4,00,000. PF on actual basic; variable pay 10% of fixed gross; GMC ₹15,000; leave accrual 15 days at basic ÷ 30.

Monthly gross ₹19,500: basic + DA ₹11,000, HRA ₹3,000, special allowance ₹5,500. Wages under the Codes = ₹16,500 (exclusions ₹3,000 < 50%). PF on wages capped at ₹15,000; minimum wage (bonus ceiling) ₹12,000.

On-costs add about 26% to gross — compared with about 20% for the manager — because ESI and bonus apply below the thresholds. A 9% increment would lift gross to ₹21,255, above the ESI ceiling; ESI would stop from the next contribution period (April–September or October–March), while bonus eligibility depends on salary/wage as defined (basic + DA) and the notified ceiling.

Timing for the cash budget

Gross pay is paid monthly (often on the last working day or the 1st–7th of next month); TDS on salary (Sec 192) is deposited by the 7th; PF and ESI by the 15th of the following month; statutory bonus within eight months of year-end (commonly October–November around Diwali); variable pay on the plan date; GMC premium at policy renewal; gratuity on exits or as contributions to an LIC/insurer-managed gratuity fund. Employer contributions to PF/ESI and the employees' share must be deposited by the statutory due dates to be deductible (Sec 36(1)(va) and 43B; the Supreme Court in Checkmate Services (2022) confirmed no grace for late deposit of employees' contributions).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Voluntary higher PF: some employers contribute 12% on actual basic above ₹15,000; EPS remains capped (₹1,250), the balance goes to EPF. Employer contributions to PF, NPS and superannuation above ₹7.5 lakh per year are taxable perquisites for the employee — relevant to CTC design, not to company cost.
  • Flexible benefit plans convert part of special allowance into reimbursements; total cost is unchanged but the wage base may change.
  • Notice pay recovery and joining bonuses (with clawback) should be separate lines.
  • Gratuity for fixed-term employees under the Code on Social Security is payable pro rata after one year — budget it for fixed-term contracts.
  • Leave encashment policy (basic vs gross, cap on accumulation) dramatically changes the liability; follow the actual policy and the actuarial valuation basis.

Key takeaways

  • CTC is a convention; budget from fixed gross and compute each employer cost with its own base, cap and timing.
  • Basic (+ DA) drives PF, gratuity and leave encashment; gross drives ESI; Code "wages" drive PF/gratuity bases after the Labour Codes.
  • Remember costs outside CTC: PF admin, EDLI, leave encashment, insurance and statutory bonus for eligible staff.
  • Low-wage employees carry higher on-cost percentages because ESI and bonus apply.
  • Increments can push employees across ESI and bonus thresholds; model at component level.
  • Timing of statutory payments matters for the cash budget and for tax deductibility under Sec 36(1)(va)/43B.

Practice Questions

Q1. CTC to employer cost [L2]

An employee's offer letter states CTC ₹18,00,000 comprising basic ₹6,60,000, HRA ₹3,30,000, special allowance ₹5,73,000, employer PF ₹79,200 (12% of actual basic), gratuity ₹31,746 and target variable pay ₹1,26,054. GMC of ₹16,500 is outside CTC. Compute the total annual budgeted employer cost including PF admin (0.5% of PF wages), EDLI (0.5% of capped wages) and leave encashment accrual of 18 days on basic ÷ 30.

💡 Hint: Start from fixed gross; do not add employer PF and gratuity twice.

Q2. Threshold crossing after increment [L2]

A packer earns monthly gross ₹20,200 (basic + DA ₹10,500, HRA ₹2,700, special allowance ₹7,000). A 6% increment applies from 1 July 2027. Compute monthly employer ESI from April 2027 to March 2028, explaining when ESI stops, and compute employer PF on capped wages before and after the increment.

Q3. Component-level payroll template [L3]

Design the column structure of a payroll calculation sheet that computes, for each position and month, basic, HRA, special allowance, employer PF (actual or capped), EDLI, admin, ESI, bonus, gratuity, leave accrual, variable pay accrual and insurance. Include the formulas for PF and ESI with caps and thresholds, and the checks you would add.

Q4. Cash timing of payroll costs [L1]

For a March 2028 payroll of ₹4.2 crore gross (TDS ₹38 lakh, employee PF ₹21 lakh, employee ESI ₹0.9 lakh, employer PF ₹22 lakh, employer ESI ₹3.9 lakh), list each cash payment with its due date and the month it falls into in the cash budget.
