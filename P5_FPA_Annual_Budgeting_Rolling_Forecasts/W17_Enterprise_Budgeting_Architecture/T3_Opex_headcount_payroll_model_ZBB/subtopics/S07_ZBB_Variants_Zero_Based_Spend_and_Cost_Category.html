ZBB Variants: Zero-Based Spend and Cost-Category Ownership

Scope: ZBB variants: Zero-Based Spend / cost-category ownership (category owner vs budget owner matrix), as used by FMCG majors

Why it matters

Classic ZBB (s06) is organised by decision unit — each department justifies its activities. Modern ZBB, popularised by 3G Capital at AB InBev and Kraft Heinz and adopted by Unilever (including Hindustan Unilever in India), Mondelez and others from around 2015, reorganises cost management around cost categories across the whole company — travel, IT, professional services, facilities, marketing production, fleet — each owned by a category owner who sets policy and price, while budget owners in departments control consumption. This matrix makes cost discipline a continuous management system rather than an annual paperwork exercise. Interviewers for FP&A roles in FMCG often ask about it directly.

Core notes

From decision units to cost categories

The category owner × budget owner matrix

Each cost category is split into price and volume (consumption) levers:

\[ \text{Spend} = \text{Price per unit} \times \text{Units consumed} \]

  • Category owner (typically a senior functional expert or procurement-led role, e.g., head of travel for all travel spend) owns the price lever and policy: negotiates contracts, sets policy (economy class below a grade, hotel caps, preferred vendors), defines benchmarks and KPIs, and builds the category baseline from zero.
  • Budget owners (department and BU heads) own volume: how many trips, how many consultants, how many print runs — within policy.

The matrix has categories in rows and budget owners in columns, with each cell the budget for that category in that department:

This creates a dual accountability: category owners are measured on the category total and KPIs across the company; budget owners on their column totals. Each is a check on the other — a category owner cannot achieve savings without budget owners reducing consumption, and budget owners cannot overspend without the category owner seeing it.

Building a category baseline from zero

  1. Spend cube: extract 12–24 months of spend by GL, vendor, cost centre and description; reclassify into categories and sub-categories (travel → air, hotel, local conveyance, visa; professional services → legal, consulting, audit, tax).
  2. Benchmark: external (peer cost per FTE, cost as % of NSV) and internal (best-performing BU or region).
  3. Policy levers: what the policy should be (e.g., virtual meetings by default for internal reviews; economy class below VP; consultants only via competitive bid above ₹25 lakh).
  4. Price levers: vendor consolidation, renegotiation, rate cards, e-auctions.
  5. Volume levers: demand management by budget owners — fewer trips, lower print runs, licence rationalisation.
  6. Zero-based target: category budget = required volume at policy × target price, not last year + inflation.
  7. Savings pipeline: initiatives with owners, phasing and status, tracked monthly.

Illustration — travel category

Baseline: 1,800 FTE; FY 2026-27 travel ₹12.0 crore (₹6,667 per FTE). Zero-based build: field sales trips required 9,000 at ₹6,800 (policy rate after hotel cap) = ₹6.12 crore; corporate essential trips 1,200 at ₹18,000 = ₹2.16 crore; conferences (discretionary package) ₹0.9 crore; visa and other ₹0.32 crore → ₹9.50 crore. Savings ₹2.5 crore (21%) split into price (hotel and airfare contracts: ₹1.1 crore — category owner) and volume (fewer internal-meeting trips: ₹1.4 crore — budget owners).

Making it repeatable

  • Monthly governance: category reviews (spend vs target, KPIs, policy compliance) and budget owner reviews.
  • Embedded policies in procurement and expense systems (travel booking tools enforce class and hotel caps; PO approval checks category budgets).
  • Incentive linkage: category and budget owners have ZBB targets in KRA scorecards (Topic 1, s08).
  • Reinvestment: a share of savings is visibly reinvested into growth (e.g., A&P), which builds organisational support — Unilever explicitly framed ZBB savings as fuel for brand investment.
  • Annual reset: each AOP, category baselines are rebuilt from zero with updated benchmarks rather than rolled forward.

Common FMCG categories under ZBB

Indirect categories (travel, professional services, IT, facilities, fleet, telecom, events, marketing production and agency fees, samples and POS material, logistics overheads). Direct materials are usually managed through procurement and value engineering; trade spend through RGM; payroll through organisation design — although the same zero-based thinking can apply.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Over-centralisation: category owners who refuse legitimate local needs create friction; allow documented policy exceptions approved under the DoA.
  • Cutting investment spend (R&D, brand building) under the ZBB label can damage growth; ring-fence strategic categories with separate rules.
  • Allocation vs ownership: shared costs (IT licences) should be charged to budget owners on consumption, not allocated on revenue, otherwise budget owners have no volume lever.
  • Data quality: a spend cube built on poor GL coding misclassifies categories — invest in GL descriptions and vendor master cleanup first.
  • Cultural fit: 3G-style ZBB at Kraft Heinz was criticised for underinvesting in brands; adapt the intensity to strategy.

Key takeaways

  • Category ZBB organises spend by cost category across the company, not by department.
  • Category owners own price and policy; budget owners own volume — a matrix of dual accountability.
  • Baselines are rebuilt from zero using spend cubes, benchmarks, policies, price and volume levers.
  • KPIs (₹ per FTE, ₹ per user, % compliant bookings) and monthly reviews make it a system, not a one-off cut.
  • Reinvesting savings and embedding policy in systems sustain the process.
  • Protect strategic investment categories and allow governed exceptions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Building a category matrix [L2]

A ₹1,500 crore personal care company has indirect spend of ₹90 crore across five departments. Design the category owner × budget owner matrix for four categories of your choice, assign owners, define one price KPI and one volume KPI per category, and explain how monthly reviews would work.

💡 Hint: Separate the price lever from the volume lever for every category.

Q2. Zero-based travel baseline [L2]

FY 2026-27 travel spend is ₹8.4 crore for 1,200 FTE: 70% field sales, 20% internal meetings, 10% conferences. A new policy requires internal meetings to be virtual by default (expect 60% fewer trips), hotel caps reduce average trip cost by 8%, and conferences become a ZBB decision package with ₹0.5 crore funded. Build the FY 2027-28 zero-based travel budget and split the saving between price and volume levers.

Q3. Interview: ZBB at an FMCG major [L1]

Explain in under two minutes how Zero-Based Spend with category ownership differs from classic ZBB, using one category example and one KPI.

Q4. Sustainability review [L3]

Two years after a ZBB programme delivered ₹14 crore of savings, indirect spend has risen back by ₹9 crore. Diagnose likely causes using the category matrix framework and propose a remediation plan with governance, systems and incentive changes.
