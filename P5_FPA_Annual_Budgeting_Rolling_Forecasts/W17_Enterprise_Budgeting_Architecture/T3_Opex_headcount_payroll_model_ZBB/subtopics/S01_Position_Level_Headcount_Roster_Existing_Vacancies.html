Subtopic note

Position-Level Headcount Roster: Existing, Vacancies, Hires, Exits

Scope: Position-level headcount roster: existing employees, approved vacancies, new hires, attrition backfills, exits

Why it matters

A position-level roster is the foundation of a credible payroll budget. Instead of budgeting "Sales department: ₹18 crore", the roster lists every position — filled or not — with who holds it, what it costs and when it is active. That level of detail lets FP&A compute monthly cost precisely, reconcile headcount to HR records, control hiring against approved positions, model vacancies and attrition, and answer the CFO's favourite questions: "How many people are we adding, where, and when?" and "Why is payroll 4% under budget?" It is also the format used by EPM workforce modules.

Core notes

Positions vs employees

A position is an approved seat in the organisation (a role in a cost centre at a grade). An employee is the person filling it. Budgeting at position level separates two controls: the number of approved seats (headcount control) and the cost of the people in them (payroll cost). A position can be filled, vacant (approved but not filled), or planned (new, approved in the AOP).

The five row types

Row type Definition Start month Exit month Cost basis
Existing employee Filled position at 1 April April (or earlier) March, unless planned exit Current CTC from payroll extract
Approved vacancy Position approved and currently vacant Expected fill month (today + time-to-hire) March Budgeted CTC for grade (midpoint of band)
New hire (new position) Incremental position approved in AOP Planned start month + time-to-hire lag March Grade midpoint or offered CTC
Attrition backfill Replacement for an expected or known exit Exit month + backfill lag + 1 March Grade midpoint (often higher than leaver's pay)
Exit Known or planned departure (retirement, resignation served, restructuring) — Last working month Leaver's CTC until exit; plus exit costs (leave encashment, gratuity, notice pay)

Known exits include retirements (age 58/60 per company policy), employees serving notice, fixed-term contract ends and announced restructuring. Unknown attrition is modelled statistically (s03) rather than by naming individuals.

Building the roster

  1. Extract the active employee master from the HRMS/payroll system (e.g., as of 31 October) with CTC components.
  2. Roll forward to 31 March using known changes up to year-end (joiners, leavers, current-year increments).
  3. Add approved vacancies from the HR open-requisition list.
  4. Add AOP new positions submitted by department heads (with business justification and grade).
  5. Add known exits and their backfills (backfill only if the position remains approved).
  6. Apply statistical attrition separately (vacancy factor or modelled backfill lag) for unknown exits.
  7. Validate with department heads and HR: position count by cost centre, CTC totals vs payroll register, grade consistency.

Headcount roll-forward check

\[ \text{Closing HC}_m = \text{Opening HC}_m + \text{Joiners}_m - \text{Leavers}_m \]

for every month and department. Example (Sales department):

Month Opening Joiners (new + backfill) Leavers Closing
April 120 0 0 120
July 120 4 2 122
September 122 2 (backfills) 0 124
March (year total) 120 14 6 128

The same roster drives monthly payroll cost (c01), average FTE (c06) and sales capacity (Topic 2, c04).

Excel mechanics

Store months as index numbers (April = 1 … March = 12) in the roster; exits beyond the year get 12 (or 99), joiners before the year get 1 (or 0). Active flag per position per month:

=(F$3>=[@Start_Month])*(F$3<=[@Exit_Month])
=SUMPRODUCT((tblRoster[Dept]="Sales")*(tblRoster[Start_Month]<=F$3)*(tblRoster[Exit_Month]>=F$3))

The second formula gives monthly headcount by department without a helper grid.

Data privacy

Salary data is personal data under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Limit roster access to HR and FP&A, mask names, share only aggregated outputs with budget owners and store files in access-controlled locations.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Contract and outsourced staff (housekeeping, security, sales promoters through agencies, fixed-term contractors) are often in opex, not payroll; keep a separate roster or a headcount memo line so total workforce is visible and caps cannot be gamed by converting roles to contract.
  • Interns, trainees and apprentices (Apprentices Act, 1961 stipend) — budget as a separate category with stipend and no PF (unless covered).
  • Employees on long leave (maternity under the Maternity Benefit Act — 26 weeks paid) — the position is filled but may require a temporary replacement; budget both if a replacement is planned.
  • Transfers between cost centres or entities mid-year: move the position with an effective month rather than creating an exit and a new hire, so headcount roll-forward stays clean.
  • Backfills at a different grade: record the backfill's grade and CTC explicitly; replacement costs are often 10–20% higher than the leaver's pay in tight talent markets.

Key takeaways

  • Budget positions, not just people: one row per approved seat, with status, start and exit months.
  • The five row types — existing, approved vacancy, new hire, attrition backfill, exit — each have different timing and cost bases.
  • A complete field set (grade, location, basic %, PF basis, eligibility flags, rating, backfill link) enables component-level statutory costing.
  • Reconcile closing headcount = opening + joiners − leavers each month and department.
  • Use month-index flags and SUMPRODUCT/SUMIFS on a structured table.
  • Keep contract staff visible, protect salary data, and move transfers rather than recreating positions.

Practice Questions

Q1. Building the roster from HR data [L2]

HR provides: 46 active employees on 31 October 2026; 3 joiners and 2 leavers expected by 31 March 2027; 4 open requisitions (two to be filled by June 2027, two by August); department heads request 5 new positions for FY 2027-28 (starts July, September, October, December, January); two retirements in FY 2027-28 (August and February) with backfills after a two-month lag; one fixed-term contract ending in September with no backfill. Construct the headcount roll-forward by month for FY 2027-28.

💡 Hint: Establish the 1 April 2027 opening headcount first.

Q2. Roster field design [L1]

List the minimum roster fields needed to compute employer PF, ESI, statutory bonus, gratuity and an increment from July, and explain which field each calculation uses.

Q3. SUMPRODUCT headcount by month [L2]

Write the Excel formulas to compute, from tblRoster, (a) headcount in Marketing in month 7, (b) total annual fixed gross of positions active in month 12 in grade M3, and (c) number of new hires starting in H2. Explain how you would test them.

Q4. Contractors vs headcount cap [L3]

A BU facing a headcount cap proposes converting 25 permanent sales promoter roles (average monthly gross ₹18,500) into agency staff at an agency fee of 9% on gross plus statutory costs passed through. Compare the cost and headcount reporting effects and explain the governance response you would recommend.
