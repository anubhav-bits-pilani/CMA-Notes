Static vs Flexible Budgets for Performance Evaluation

Scope: Static vs flexible budget and when each is appropriate for performance evaluation

Why it matters

A plant that produced 20% more than budget will almost certainly overspend its static cost budget — and that tells you nothing about whether it was efficient. A sales team that sold 15% less but cut variable costs proportionally will show a favourable cost variance that hides a revenue failure. Comparing actuals with a budget set at a different activity level mixes two separate questions: "Did we sell the planned volume?" and "Given the volume we actually achieved, did we earn and spend what we should have?" Flexible budgeting separates them. It is the foundation of monthly variance commentary, of the PVM/EBITDA bridges in Week 19, and of fair performance evaluation of managers who control costs but not volumes.

Core notes

Definitions

  • Static (fixed) budget: the original budget prepared for one planned level of activity (e.g. 1,20,000 cases per month), not adjusted after the fact. It is the AOP number approved by the Board.
  • Flexible (flexed) budget: the budget recomputed for the actual level of activity, using the budgeted selling prices, budgeted variable costs per unit of the relevant driver and budgeted fixed costs (with step costs re-evaluated at the actual activity level).

\[ \text{Flexed cost} = \text{Budgeted fixed cost} + \text{Budgeted variable cost per unit of driver} \times \text{Actual driver volume} \]

A flexible budget can be prepared ex ante — as a schedule of allowances at several activity levels (e.g. 80%, 90%, 100%, 110% of capacity) — or ex post, at the single actual level once known. ICMAI terminology describes the flexible budget as one designed to change in accordance with the level of activity attained.

  • Static − Flexed = sales volume (activity) variance — the effect of selling a different quantity.
  • Flexed − Actual = flexible budget variance — price, spending and efficiency differences at the actual volume.

When the static budget is appropriate

  • Strategic and Board-level accountability — did the business deliver the committed plan (revenue, EBITDA, cash)? The Board and investors care about the absolute shortfall.
  • Sales and commercial managers who control volume — the sales volume variance is theirs.
  • Discretionary and committed fixed costs — marketing campaigns, corporate overheads, R&D, rent. These do not change with volume in the short run; the static budget is the correct allowance.
  • Cash and funding plans — the bank cares about actual cash vs plan, not a flexed number.
  • Incentive plans tied to absolute targets (Topic 1 s08).

When the flexible budget is appropriate

  • Cost-centre managers (plant heads, logistics, contact centres) whose costs are driven by volumes they do not control — evaluate them on flexed allowances.
  • Variable and semi-variable costs — materials, power, freight, packing, contract labour, commissions.
  • Monthly variance analysis — flexing first isolates the volume story from the efficiency story, so commentary is causal.
  • Standard costing environments — the flexible budget at actual output is the "standard cost of actual output".
  • Rolling forecasts and scenario planning — "what would the P&L look like at 90% volume?" is a flexed budget prepared ex ante.

Controllability principle

Managers should be evaluated on what they can control. Flexing removes the volume effect from cost-centre performance, but the choice of driver matters: a plant controls efficiency per case, not the number of cases ordered; a logistics manager controls cost per trip, not the number of distributor orders. Some costs are controllable only partly — e.g. power tariffs are set by the state electricity board; usage per unit is controllable. Variance reports should be split accordingly.

Illustration

Budget for October: 1,00,000 cases at ₹500 net price; variable cost ₹320 per case; fixed overheads ₹1.20 crore. Actual: 1,12,000 cases at ₹495; variable cost ₹3.66 crore; fixed overheads ₹1.25 crore.

₹ lakh Static Flexed (1,12,000) Actual Flexible budget variance Sales volume variance
Revenue 500.0 560.0 554.4 5.6 A 60.0 F
Variable costs 320.0 358.4 366.0 7.6 A 38.4 A
Contribution 180.0 201.6 188.4 13.2 A 21.6 F
Fixed overheads 120.0 120.0 125.0 5.0 A —
Profit 60.0 81.6 63.4 18.2 A 21.6 F

Static variance: 63.4 − 60.0 = ₹3.4 lakh F. The static comparison suggests a good month; the flexible budget shows that volume added ₹21.6 lakh but price, cost efficiency and fixed overspend lost ₹18.2 lakh. Comparing actual variable costs (₹366 lakh) with the static ₹320 lakh would have produced a misleading ₹46 lakh adverse cost variance, mostly caused by higher volume.

Excel implementation

Keep a driver-rate table (variable cost per case by line item, fixed cost by line item, step thresholds) and compute the flexed column from actual drivers:

=XLOOKUP([@Line], tblRates[Line], tblRates[Fixed]) + XLOOKUP([@Line], tblRates[Line], tblRates[VarPerUnit]) * XLOOKUP([@Driver], tblActDrivers[Driver], tblActDrivers[Actual])

In Power BI, a "Flexed Budget" measure multiplies budget rates by actual driver volumes at the row context of the account and month.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Which volume? Sales volume flexes revenue and selling costs; production volume flexes manufacturing costs. When inventory changes, the two differ — flex each cost on its own driver.
  • Mix: in a multi-product business, flexing on total units ignores mix; flex by product or use a standard mix (sales mix variance, Week 19).
  • Fixed costs that are actually step costs must be re-evaluated at actual volume (s03).
  • Gaming: if managers know they will be judged on flexed budgets only, they may ignore volume shortfalls. Report both views.
  • Absorption vs marginal: under absorption costing, fixed overhead volume variances appear; ensure the flexed profit is computed on the same basis as the static budget (s05).
  • Ind AS reporting uses actuals only; flexible budgeting is a management accounting tool — no statutory implication, but explain management vs reported numbers consistently.

Key takeaways

  • Static budget = plan at planned volume; flexible budget = budgeted prices and rates at actual volume.
  • Static − flexed isolates the volume effect; flexed − actual isolates price, spending and efficiency.
  • Use static for strategic, commercial, discretionary-cost and cash accountability; flexible for cost-centre and variable-cost evaluation.
  • Apply the controllability principle when choosing drivers and assigning variances.
  • Present both views to avoid blaming managers for volume they do not control — or excusing volume failures.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three-column report [L1]

Budget: 40,000 units at ₹250; variable cost ₹140 per unit; fixed costs ₹22 lakh. Actual: 36,500 units at ₹258; variable costs ₹52.2 lakh; fixed costs ₹21.4 lakh. Prepare the static, flexed and actual columns and compute the sales volume and flexible budget variances for revenue, variable cost, contribution, fixed cost and profit.

Q2. Choosing the evaluation basis [L2]

For each manager, state whether you would evaluate them primarily against a static or flexible budget and why: (a) national sales head; (b) plant head of a contract-manufacturing-heavy FMCG company; (c) head of brand marketing; (d) head of logistics; (e) CFO on finance cost.

💡 Hint: Ask what the manager controls — volume, rate, efficiency, or none of these.

Q3. Misleading static report [L2]

A plant's monthly report shows power cost ₹1.38 crore vs budget ₹1.20 crore (15% adverse). Budgeted production was 9,000 tonnes with power of ₹1,100 per tonne variable plus ₹21 lakh fixed; actual production was 10,800 tonnes. Prepare the flexed allowance and restate the variance, and draft a two-sentence commentary.

Q4. Interview: "Why not just flex everything?" [L3]

An interviewer argues that a fully flexed P&L is always fairer. Present a structured counter-argument covering accountability for volume, discretionary costs, cash and investor guidance, and suggest how a management pack should present both views.
