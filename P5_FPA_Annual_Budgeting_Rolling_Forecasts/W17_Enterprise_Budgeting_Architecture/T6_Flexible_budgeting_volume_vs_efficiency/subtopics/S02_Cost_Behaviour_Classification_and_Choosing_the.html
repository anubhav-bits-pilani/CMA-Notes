Cost Behaviour Classification and Choosing the Activity Driver

Scope: Cost behaviour classification: variable, fixed, semi-variable, step-fixed; choosing the activity driver

Why it matters

A flexible budget is only as good as its cost behaviour assumptions. If a cost is classified as variable when it is actually fixed, the flexed allowance moves with volume and managers get credit (or blame) that is not real. If the driver is wrong — flexing freight on revenue rather than on tonne-kilometres — price increases or mix shifts make the allowance meaningless. Classification is also the basis of CVP analysis, break-even, operating leverage (Week 21) and driver-based forecasting (Week 18). In FP&A practice it is a judgement exercise done with operations, supported by data (high-low or regression, c06).

Core notes

The four behaviour types

Type Definition Total cost as activity rises Cost per unit FMCG examples
Variable Changes in direct proportion to activity Rises linearly Constant Raw and packing materials, freight-out per case, sales commission, royalty per unit
Fixed Unchanged within the relevant range and period Constant Falls Plant management salaries, insurance, rent, depreciation (time-based), software subscriptions
Semi-variable (mixed) Fixed component plus variable component Rises from a positive intercept Falls towards the variable rate Power (demand charge + energy charge), telephone, maintenance (contract + parts), vehicle running costs
Step-fixed Fixed over a band of activity, then jumps Staircase Saw-tooth Supervisors (one per 25 operators), shifts, warehouse leases, additional production lines

Relevant range and time horizon

Classifications hold only within a relevant range of activity and a time period. Over a year, a third shift can be added (step); over five years, almost every cost is variable. The flexible budget for a month should classify costs as they behave in a month at volumes within ±20–30% of budget; outside that range (a plant shutdown, a doubling of demand) re-plan rather than flex.

Accounting structure also matters: depreciation by straight line is fixed; units-of-production depreciation (permitted under Ind AS 16.62) is variable. Permanent workforce wages in Indian plants are effectively fixed in the short run (Industrial Relations Code restrictions on retrenchment for establishments above the threshold, standing orders), while contract labour under the Contract Labour arrangements is closer to variable or step.

Semi-variable costs

Split into fixed and variable elements:

\[ Y = a + bX \]

where \(Y\) is total cost, \(a\) the fixed element, \(b\) the variable rate per unit of driver and \(X\) activity. Methods: account analysis (engineering or contract terms — e.g. electricity tariff demand charge vs per kWh energy charge), high-low (c06), scatter graph, least-squares regression (SLOPE, INTERCEPT, LINEST).

Example: industrial power tariff in many states has a fixed demand charge (₹/kVA of contract demand per month) plus an energy charge (₹/kWh) plus electricity duty and fuel adjustment. The fixed element is the demand charge; the variable element is energy × usage per tonne.

Step-fixed costs

Model with a threshold function:

\[ \text{Step cost} = c \times \left\lceil \frac{X}{k} \right\rceil \]

where \(c\) is cost per step, \(k\) the capacity per step and \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) the ceiling function. In Excel:

=CostPerStep * ROUNDUP(ActualVolume / CapacityPerStep, 0)

If steps are wide relative to monthly variation (e.g. a warehouse), treat as fixed; if narrow (supervisors per 25 operators), treat as quasi-variable but flex at thresholds.

Choosing the activity driver

A good driver:

  1. Causes the cost (cause-and-effect), not merely correlates.
  2. Is measurable monthly from systems (SAP production confirmations, WMS, TMS).
  3. Is not controlled by the manager in a way that distorts evaluation.
  4. Is stable — the rate per unit doesn't swing with price or mix.
Cost Poor driver Better driver
Freight-out Revenue Tonne-km or cases × lane rate
Power Production value kWh per tonne × tonnes; machine hours
Packing material Revenue Units produced by SKU (BOM)
Warehouse handling Revenue Cases handled / pallets moved
Quality control Units Number of batches
Customer service Revenue Number of orders / tickets
Sales commission Units Net revenue (per scheme rules)

Revenue is a poor driver for most operating costs because price increases inflate the allowance without any increase in work done — a named topic pitfall.

Illustration

A plant's maintenance cost over six months (₹ lakh) vs machine hours: Apr 18.4 (5,200 h), May 20.1 (6,100 h), Jun 16.9 (4,400 h), Jul 21.0 (6,600 h), Aug 19.2 (5,700 h), Sep 17.6 (4,800 h). Highest activity 6,600 h at ₹21.0 lakh, lowest 4,400 h at ₹16.9 lakh:

\[ b = \frac{21.0 - 16.9}{6{,}600 - 4{,}400} = ₹186 \text{ per hour (approx.)}, \qquad a = 21.0 - 0.001864 \times 6{,}600 = ₹8.70 \text{ lakh} \]

(in ₹ lakh, \(b = 0.001864\) lakh per hour). The flexed maintenance allowance for 5,900 hours = 8.70 + 0.001864 × 5,900 = ₹19.70 lakh. Regression on all six points would use more information and is preferred where data quality allows.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Discretionary fixed costs (marketing, training) are fixed by decision, not by nature — they don't flex, but they can be cut; evaluate against static budget.
  • Committed fixed costs (lease, depreciation) cannot be changed in the short run — exclude from manager-level controllable variance.
  • Mixed drivers: a cost may depend on two drivers (e.g. freight on cases and number of drops); use both, or an activity-based approach (s06).
  • Learning curves make labour per unit fall as volume accumulates — a constant variable rate overstates allowances for new lines.
  • Contracts with minimum guarantees (e.g. 3PL warehouse minimum billing) are fixed up to the minimum, variable above — a semi-variable with a floor, not an intercept.
  • Inflation and tariff changes: separate rate changes from behaviour; flex on budget rates and report price variances separately.

Key takeaways

  • Classify every cost as variable, fixed, semi-variable or step-fixed within a relevant range and period.
  • Split semi-variable costs into \(a + bX\) using contract terms, high-low or regression.
  • Model step costs with thresholds (ROUNDUP) and flex them only at breaks.
  • Choose drivers that cause the cost, are measurable and stable — rarely revenue.
  • Separate discretionary and committed fixed costs from controllable variable costs in reporting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classification [L1]

Classify each cost for a monthly flexible budget of a biscuit plant and propose a driver: wheat flour; carton boxes; plant head salary; supervisors (one per 30 operators); power with demand charge; boiler fuel; factory insurance; QC lab consumables; contract loaders paid per tonne; machine maintenance under an AMC with a per-call charge.

Q2. Step cost flexing [L2]

Packing operations need one supervisor (₹65,000 per month CTC) for every 25 operators; each operator packs 4,000 cases per month. Budgeted volume 2,40,000 cases; actual 2,82,000 cases. Compute budgeted and flexed supervisor cost and the operator headcount at each level, and explain what happens at 3,00,000 cases.

💡 Hint: Compute operators first, then supervisors using a ceiling function.

Q3. Driver selection debate [L2]

Logistics costs have been budgeted at 4.2% of net revenue. This year a 9% price increase is taken with flat volumes, and the logistics head reports a favourable variance. Explain why, propose a better driver, and recompute the allowance given budgeted volume of 60 lakh cases, average lead distance 380 km and budgeted rate ₹0.0045 per case-km, with actual volume 60 lakh cases and actual average distance 410 km (because a new depot opened late). Budgeted net revenue was ₹1,500 crore.

Q4. Regression vs high-low [L3]

Using the six months of maintenance data in the illustration, describe how you would run a regression in Excel (LINEST with statistics), interpret \(R^2\) and the standard error, and decide whether to use high-low, regression or account analysis for the budget. Identify one data issue (e.g. an overhaul month) that would distort both methods.
