Key concept

Static Budget Variance = Sales Volume Variance + Flexible Budget Variance

Concept: Static budget variance = Sales volume variance + Flexible budget variance

Statement

\[ \underbrace{A - S}_{\text{Static budget variance}} = \underbrace{(F - S)}_{\text{Sales volume variance}} + \underbrace{(A - F)}_{\text{Flexible budget variance}} \]

where \(A\) is the actual result (profit, revenue or cost line), \(S\) the static budget amount at budgeted activity, and \(F\) the flexed budget amount at actual activity with budgeted prices, rates and efficiency. For profit lines, a positive value is favourable; for cost lines, sign conventions must be stated (commonly, adverse when actual cost exceeds the comparator).

Explanation

The identity is arithmetic — adding and subtracting \(F\) — but its value is analytical. It splits the total gap into:

  • Sales volume variance (\(F - S\)): attributable to activity differences — selling more or fewer units than planned. At profit level it equals \(\Delta Q \times\) budgeted contribution per unit (c03). Owned by sales/marketing, or by the market.
  • Flexible budget variance (\(A - F\)): attributable to everything else at the actual activity level — selling prices, input prices, usage efficiency, fixed cost spending (c04). Owned by commercial, procurement and operations.

Conditions for a meaningful split:

  • \(F\) must be built correctly (fixed costs not flexed, step costs re-evaluated, each cost on its own driver).
  • The same costing basis (marginal or absorption) must be used for \(S\), \(F\) and \(A\).
  • For multi-product firms, \(F\) should be built at product level (actual mix), so the volume variance includes mix; split mix out explicitly if needed.

The split applies line by line (revenue, each cost, contribution, profit), and the line-level components sum to the profit-level components.

Illustration

Budget: 60,000 units; price ₹450; variable cost ₹270; fixed ₹70 lakh → budget profit ₹38 lakh. Actual: 54,000 units; price ₹462; variable cost ₹1.485 crore; fixed ₹71.5 lakh → actual profit = 249.48 − 148.5 − 71.5 = ₹29.48 lakh.

Flexed at 54,000: revenue 243.0; variable cost 145.8; fixed 70.0; profit 27.2.

₹ lakh Static Flexed Actual SVV (F − S) FBV (A − F)
Profit 38.0 27.2 29.48 10.8 A 2.28 F

Static budget variance = 29.48 − 38.0 = ₹8.52 lakh A = −10.8 + 2.28. The volume shortfall (10%) explains more than the total; price and cost performance were net favourable.

Connections

Built from c01 (flexed cost) and refined by c03 (valuing volume at contribution) and c04 (content of the flexible budget variance). Detailed in s04; s05 adds the overhead split. Week 19's price-volume-mix and EBITDA bridges start from this split. The topic pitfall of presenting only static variances is avoided by always showing both components.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the identity [L1]

Budget: 15,000 units at ₹2,000, variable cost ₹1,300, fixed ₹62 lakh. Actual: 16,200 units at ₹1,960, variable cost ₹2.14 crore, fixed ₹63 lakh. Compute the static budget variance and split it into the sales volume and flexible budget variances at revenue, variable cost, contribution and profit level.

Q2. Commentary [L2]

A regional P&L shows static budget variance of ₹1.2 crore favourable, consisting of SVV ₹2.9 crore favourable and FBV ₹1.7 crore adverse. Draft a four-sentence commentary for the regional head and identify the follow-up questions you would ask about the FBV.

💡 Hint: A favourable total can hide a deteriorating unit economics story.

Q3. Absorption vs marginal [L2]

Explain how the split differs if the static and flexed budgets are prepared under absorption costing with fixed production overhead absorbed per unit and inventory levels changing. Where does the fixed overhead volume variance appear?
