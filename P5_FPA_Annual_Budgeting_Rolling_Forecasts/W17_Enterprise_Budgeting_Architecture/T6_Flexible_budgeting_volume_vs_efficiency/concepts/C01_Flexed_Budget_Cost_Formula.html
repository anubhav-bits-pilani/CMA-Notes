Key concept

Flexed Budget Cost Formula

Concept: Flexed budget cost = Budgeted fixed cost + Budgeted variable cost per unit × Actual units

Statement

\[ C^{\text{flex}} = F^{\text{bud}} + v^{\text{bud}} \times Q^{\text{act}} \]

where \(C^{\text{flex}}\) is the flexed (allowed) cost at the actual level of activity, \(F^{\text{bud}}\) the budgeted fixed cost for the period (including the fixed element of semi-variable costs and step costs evaluated at the actual activity level), \(v^{\text{bud}}\) the budgeted variable cost per unit of the activity driver, and \(Q^{\text{act}}\) the actual quantity of that driver (units produced, machine hours, cases shipped). For multiple cost lines and drivers:

\[ C^{\text{flex}} = \sum_j \left(F^{\text{bud}}_j + v^{\text{bud}}_j \times Q^{\text{act}}_j\right) \]

Explanation

The formula answers: "What should costs have been, given what we actually did, if we had spent at budgeted rates and efficiency?" It holds price and efficiency at budget and lets only activity change. Conditions:

  • Relevant range: \(F\) and \(v\) are assumed constant within the range of activity; outside it (a new shift, a shut line) re-evaluate step costs or re-plan.
  • Correct driver: \(Q\) must be the activity that causes the cost — production units for manufacturing costs, sales units for selling costs, machine hours for running overheads.
  • Budget rates, not actual: using actual rates would hide price variances inside the flexed allowance.
  • Standard efficiency: for costs where efficiency matters (labour, variable overhead), \(Q^{\text{act}}\) should be standard inputs allowed for actual output (e.g. standard hours), not actual inputs — otherwise efficiency variances disappear into the allowance.

The formula is the cost-side engine of the flexible budget (s01, s03); the revenue side is actual volume × budgeted price.

Illustration

A packing department's budget: fixed ₹14 lakh per month; variable ₹22 per case (packing labour on piece rate ₹9, consumables ₹8, power ₹5). Budget 80,000 cases; actual 92,000 cases; actual cost ₹35.3 lakh.

\[ C^{\text{flex}} = 14.0 + 0.00022 \times 92{,}000 = 14.0 + 20.24 = ₹34.24 \text{ lakh} \]

Static budget = 14.0 + 17.6 = ₹31.6 lakh. Static comparison: ₹3.7 lakh adverse. Flexed comparison: 35.3 − 34.24 = ₹1.06 lakh adverse — the real overspend; ₹2.64 lakh of the static variance is simply the extra 12,000 cases.

Practice Questions

Q1. Flexed allowance [L1]

A warehouse has fixed costs of ₹9.5 lakh per month and variable costs of ₹6.40 per pallet moved. Budget: 1,10,000 pallets; actual: 96,000 pallets; actual cost ₹16.2 lakh. Compute static and flexed allowances and both variances.

Q2. Multi-driver flexing [L2]

A plant's costs: materials ₹210 per unit produced; power ₹3.5 lakh fixed + ₹180 per machine hour; supervision ₹4 lakh fixed; freight-out ₹28 per unit sold. Budget: 20,000 units produced and sold, 2,500 machine hours. Actual: 22,000 units produced, 21,000 sold, 2,640 machine hours (standard 0.125 hours per unit). Compute the flexed cost for each line, stating the driver and whether you used actual or standard hours.

💡 Hint: Use standard hours for actual output when you want efficiency to show up as a variance.

Q3. Relevant range [L2]

The packing department in the illustration needs a second supervisor (₹0.8 lakh per month) above 90,000 cases. Recompute the flexed budget at 92,000 cases and explain how you would present the change to the department head.
