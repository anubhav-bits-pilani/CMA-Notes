Key concept

Sales Volume Variance Valued at Budgeted Contribution

Concept: Sales volume variance (profit) = (Actual units − Budget units) × Budgeted contribution per unit

Statement

\[ \text{SVV} = (Q^{\text{act}} - Q^{\text{bud}}) \times (p^{\text{bud}} - v^{\text{bud}}) \]

where \(Q^{\text{act}}\) and \(Q^{\text{bud}}\) are actual and budgeted sales units, \(p^{\text{bud}}\) the budgeted net selling price per unit and \(v^{\text{bud}}\) the budgeted variable cost per unit, so \((p^{\text{bud}} - v^{\text{bud}})\) is budgeted contribution per unit. Under absorption costing the valuation is budgeted (standard) profit per unit, \(p^{\text{bud}} - \text{full standard cost per unit}\). For multiple products, sum by product: \(\text{SVV} = \sum_i (Q^{\text{act}}_i - Q^{\text{bud}}_i) \times c^{\text{bud}}_i\).

Explanation

When one more unit is sold at budgeted price and cost, profit rises by the contribution, not the price — the variable costs of making and delivering it rise too, while fixed costs are unchanged. Valuing the volume variance at selling price overstates the profit effect by \(\Delta Q \times v^{\text{bud}}\) and leaves an offsetting fictitious "cost variance" elsewhere — a named topic pitfall.

Choice of valuation:

  • Marginal costing (contribution): preferred for management decision-making and FP&A bridges; fixed costs are period costs and are analysed only for spending.
  • Absorption costing (standard profit): consistent with statutory-style inventory valuation; the fixed overhead per unit not covered by the volume variance shows up in the fixed overhead volume variance (s05). The two approaches give the same total profit variance only if production equals sales.

Decomposition for richer analysis:

  • Mix vs quantity (multi-product) — mix measures the effect of selling a richer or poorer product mix at budgeted unit contributions; quantity measures the effect of total volume at budget mix.
  • Market size vs market share — quantity can be split using market data, separating category growth (external) from share gain (sales performance).

Illustration

Two SKUs:

SKU Budget units Actual units Budget price Budget VC Budget contribution
Premium 500 ml 40,000 36,000 ₹240 ₹130 ₹110
Value 200 ml 60,000 72,000 ₹90 ₹62 ₹28

SVV = (36,000 − 40,000) × 110 + (72,000 − 60,000) × 28 = −4.40 lakh + 3.36 lakh = ₹1.04 lakh A — even though total units rose 8%.

Valued at selling price instead: (−4,000 × 240) + (12,000 × 90) = −9.6 + 10.8 = ₹1.2 lakh F — the wrong sign. The contribution view exposes the adverse mix shift from premium to value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Single-product SVV [L1]

Budget 1,25,000 cases at ₹380 net price and ₹236 variable cost; actual 1,18,000 cases. Compute the sales volume variance at contribution and at selling price, and explain the difference.

Q2. Mix and quantity split [L2]

Using the two-SKU illustration, split the ₹1.04 lakh adverse SVV into mix and quantity variances.

💡 Hint: Total actual units 1,08,000 at budgeted mix (40:60) gives the standard-mix quantities.

Q3. Absorption valuation [L2]

For Q1, the budgeted fixed production overhead is ₹45 lakh at 1,25,000 cases, absorbed per case. Compute the SVV at standard profit, and show how the fixed overhead volume variance reconciles it to the marginal costing SVV if production equals sales.
