Key concept

Flexible Budget Variance: Actual Versus Flexed Budget

Concept: Flexible budget variance = Actual result − Flexed budget (captures price and efficiency/spending)

Statement

\[ \text{FBV} = A - F \]

where \(A\) is the actual result and \(F\) the flexed budget at actual activity (budgeted prices, rates, efficiency and fixed costs). For profit:

\[ \text{FBV}_{\text{profit}} = \underbrace{(p^{\text{act}} - p^{\text{bud}}) Q^{\text{act}}}_{\text{Sales price}} - \sum_k \underbrace{\left(C^{\text{act}}_k - C^{\text{flex}}_k\right)}_{\text{Cost line } k} - \underbrace{(F^{\text{act}} - F^{\text{bud}})}_{\text{Fixed spending}} \]

Each variable cost line's \(C^{\text{act}}_k - C^{\text{flex}}_k\) splits into a price (rate) component and a usage (efficiency) component:

\[ \text{Price} = (r^{\text{std}} - r^{\text{act}}) \times U^{\text{act}}, \qquad \text{Usage} = (U^{\text{std}} - U^{\text{act}}) \times r^{\text{std}} \]

where \(r\) is the price per unit of input (₹/kg, ₹/hour), \(U^{\text{act}}\) actual input quantity and \(U^{\text{std}}\) standard input allowed for actual output (sign: positive = favourable).

Explanation

Once volume has been isolated, the flexible budget variance is what managers can be held accountable for: did we realise the budgeted price, buy inputs at budgeted rates, use them at standard efficiency, and spend fixed budgets as planned? Its components map to owners:

Component Typical owner
Sales price / GTN realisation Sales and revenue management
Material price Procurement (and commodity markets)
Material usage / yield Plant operations
Labour rate HR / wage settlements
Labour efficiency Plant operations
Variable overhead spending and efficiency (c05) Plant / utilities
Fixed overhead spending Cost-centre heads

Conditions: standards must be current and attainable; otherwise the FBV reflects stale standards rather than performance. Separate planning variances (standard was wrong — e.g. a commodity price spike unforeseeable at budget time) from operational variances (performance against a revised, ex-post standard) when the environment changed materially.

Illustration

Flexed at actual 20,000 units: material standard 2.5 kg/unit at ₹80/kg → flexed ₹40.0 lakh. Actual: 51,500 kg at ₹78.40/kg = ₹40.376 lakh.

  • Material price = (80 − 78.40) × 51,500 = ₹0.824 lakh F.
  • Material usage = (50,000 − 51,500) × 80 = ₹1.200 lakh A.
  • Total material FBV = 40.0 − 40.376 = ₹0.376 lakh A (= 0.824 F − 1.200 A).

Procurement beat the price; the plant used 3% more material than standard. Without the split, the ₹0.38 lakh adverse total looks immaterial and the yield problem goes unnoticed.

Practice Questions

Q1. Full FBV breakdown [L2]

Actual output 12,000 units sold at ₹1,050 (budget ₹1,000). Standards per unit: 3 kg material at ₹120/kg; 1.5 labour hours at ₹300/h. Actual: 37,200 kg at ₹116/kg; 17,400 hours at ₹310/h; fixed overhead ₹22 lakh vs budget ₹20 lakh. Compute the sales price variance, material price and usage, labour rate and efficiency, and fixed overhead spending variances, and the total FBV.

Q2. Planning vs operational [L3]

The budget assumed palm oil at ₹95/kg; a global supply shock moved the market price to ₹118/kg for the whole quarter; procurement bought at ₹114/kg. Usage was 2,40,000 kg against a standard of 2,30,000 kg for actual output. Split the material variance into planning, operational price and usage variances, and explain how this changes the performance discussion with procurement.

💡 Hint: Use the ex-post market price as the revised standard for operational variances.

Q3. Owner mapping [L1]

For a monthly FMCG management pack, list each component of the flexible budget variance, the system source of its actual data, and the accountable owner.
