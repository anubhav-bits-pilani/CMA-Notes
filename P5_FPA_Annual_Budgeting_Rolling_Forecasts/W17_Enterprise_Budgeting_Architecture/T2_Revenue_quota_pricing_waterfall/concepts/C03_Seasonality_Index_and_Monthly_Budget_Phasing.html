Key concept

Seasonality Index and Monthly Budget Phasing

Concept: Seasonality index_m = Average sales of month m ÷ Average monthly sales; indices sum to 12; Monthly budget = Annual × index_m ÷ 12

Statement

For month \(m = 1, \dots, 12\) and \(n\) years of history with sales \(S_{y,m}\):

\[ I_m = \frac{\bar{S}_m}{\bar{S}}, \qquad \bar{S}_m = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{y=1}^{n} S_{y,m}, \qquad \bar{S} = \frac{1}{12n} \sum_{y=1}^{n} \sum_{m=1}^{12} S_{y,m} \]

By construction:

\[ \sum_{m=1}^{12} I_m = 12 \]

and the monthly budget is

\[ B_m = B_{\text{annual}} \times \frac{I_m}{12} \]

so that \(\sum_m B_m = B_{\text{annual}}\).

Explanation

An index of 1.20 means the month is typically 20% above an average month; 0.85 means 15% below. Because the indices average to 1, the phasing preserves the annual total.

Practical refinements:

  • De-trending: in a growing business, later months in each year are larger simply because of growth. Compute ratios within each year (\(S_{y,m} / \bar{S}_y\)) and average those across years, then normalise to sum to 12. This is the ratio-to-moving-average idea in classical time-series decomposition.
  • Volume vs value: build indices on volume and apply price changes separately by month, otherwise last year's price increase month distorts the curve.
  • Event alignment: shift festive weight between October and November depending on the Diwali date.
  • Exclusions: remove one-offs (launch pipeline fills, stock-outs, institutional bulk orders, a COVID year).
  • Granularity: separate indices by category and channel (MT seasonality differs from GT; e-commerce has sale events such as festive "big billion days").
  • Normalisation after manual adjustments: \(I'_m = 12 \times I^{\text{adj}}_m / \sum_k I^{\text{adj}}_k\).

The same indices can phase related lines (schemes, sales incentives, freight), while costs with other drivers (payroll, rent) follow their own timing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Index calculation and phasing [L1]

Average monthly sales of a beverage over three years (₹ crore, April–March): 18, 22, 20, 12, 10, 11, 12, 11, 9, 9, 11, 15. Compute the 12 indices, verify they sum to 12, and phase an annual budget of ₹198 crore.

💡 Hint: Compute the average monthly sales across all 12 months first.

Q2. De-trended vs simple indices [L2]

Explain, with a two-year numerical example of your own (growth of 20% between years), why simple averaging overstates the indices of later months in a growing business, and show the de-trended calculation.

Q3. Adjusting and re-normalising [L2]

A category's indices are Apr–Mar: 0.90, 0.90, 0.95, 1.00, 1.00, 1.10, 1.30, 1.05, 0.95, 0.95, 0.95, 0.95. Management wants to move 0.10 from October to November for a Diwali shift and add a 0.15 uplift in March for a planned consumer promotion. Compute the adjusted, re-normalised indices.
