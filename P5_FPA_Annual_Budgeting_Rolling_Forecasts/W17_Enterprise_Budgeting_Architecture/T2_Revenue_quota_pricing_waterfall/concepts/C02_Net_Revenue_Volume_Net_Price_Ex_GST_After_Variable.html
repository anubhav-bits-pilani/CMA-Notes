Key concept

Net Revenue: Volume × Net Price, Ex-GST, After Variable Consideration

Concept: Net revenue = Σ (Volume_i × Net price_i); GST excluded from revenue; Ind AS 115 treats rebates/schemes as variable consideration reducing revenue

Statement

\[ R_{\text{net}} = \sum_{i} V_i \times P^{\text{net}}_i \]

where \(i\) indexes the planning cells (SKU × channel × customer class × territory × month), \(V_i\) is primary volume and \(P^{\text{net}}_i\) the net price per unit after all revenue deductions, excluding GST:

\[ P^{\text{net}}_i = P^{\text{list}}_i \times \left(1 - \sum_k d_{k,i}\right) - \frac{F_i}{V_i} \]

with \(d_{k,i}\) the percentage deductions (trade discounts, schemes, cash discount, rebates, returns) and \(F_i\) fixed customer payments (listing, visibility) that reduce revenue.

Explanation

GST excluded. Ind AS 115.47 defines the transaction price as the consideration the entity expects to be entitled to, excluding amounts collected on behalf of third parties. GST charged on invoices is collected for the government, so revenue is ex-GST. GST still matters in the budget for receivables, collections and the GST payable cash outflow (Topic 4), so build revenue ex-GST and compute GST separately by rate.

Variable consideration. Discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, performance bonuses and similar items make consideration variable (Ind AS 115.51). The entity estimates the amount using the expected value or most likely amount (para 53) and includes it only to the extent it is highly probable that a significant reversal will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved (the constraint, para 56). Consequences for budgeting:

  • Trade schemes, QPS/target rebates, cash discounts and expected returns are deducted from revenue, not shown as selling expenses.
  • Rebates with thresholds (pay 2% if annual volume exceeds 1,00,000 cases) are accrued at the expected rate consistent with the budgeted volume.
  • Expected returns create a refund liability and an asset for the right to recover goods.

Consideration payable to a customer (para 70) — listing fees, visibility payments, slotting allowances to MT chains or e-commerce platforms — reduces revenue unless it is for a distinct good or service received at fair value.

What is not a revenue deduction: freight paid by the company (fulfilment cost, in COGS or distribution expense), consumer advertising (A&P), and distributor margins already embedded in the price structure (they never form part of the company's consideration).

Granularity. Summing at the cell level (rather than total volume × average net price) preserves mix: if premium SKUs grow faster, average net price rises — the mix effect in the PVM bridge.

Connections

The output of s01 (volume), s02 (price) and s03 (GTN), and the definition used in c01 (pocket price) and c03 (phasing). It links to the Ind AS 115 related item (Phase 1), to receivables and GST cash flows in Topic 4, and to the PVM bridge in Week 19, which requires this cell-level structure.

Practice Questions

Q1. Net revenue across channels [L2]

Compute FY 2027-28 net revenue and the average net price for detergent: GT 210 lakh units, MT 60 lakh, e-commerce 30 lakh; list price ₹92; deductions GT 12.5%, MT 15.2%, e-commerce 17.5%; fixed MT listing fees ₹2.4 crore and e-commerce visibility ₹1.5 crore. Also compute the GST (18%) on invoice value for GT if on-invoice discounts are 4% of list.

💡 Hint: GST is computed on invoice value after on-invoice discounts, not on net revenue.

Q2. Classification under Ind AS 115 [L2]

For each item, state whether it reduces revenue, is an expense, or is neither, and why: (a) GST on sales; (b) annual growth rebate to a distributor; (c) freight to customer depots; (d) TV advertising; (e) display fee to a supermarket chain with no evidence of fair value; (f) expected expiry returns.

Q3. Constraint on a threshold rebate [L3]

A key account earns a 3% retrospective rebate on all purchases if annual purchases exceed ₹40 crore. The budget projects ₹42 crore, but last year purchases were ₹36 crore and the account is volatile. Explain how you would budget the rebate accrual monthly, how the Ind AS 115 constraint applies in actual reporting, and what happens to Q4 revenue if the threshold is missed.
