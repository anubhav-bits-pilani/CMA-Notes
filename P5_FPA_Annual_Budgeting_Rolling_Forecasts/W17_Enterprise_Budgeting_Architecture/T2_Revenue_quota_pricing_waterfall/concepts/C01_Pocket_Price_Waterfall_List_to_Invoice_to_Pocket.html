Key concept

Pocket Price Waterfall: List to Invoice to Pocket Price

Concept: Pocket price waterfall: List price − on-invoice discounts = Invoice price − off-invoice items (rebates, schemes, cash discount, freight) = Pocket price

Statement

\[ P_{\text{invoice}} = P_{\text{list}} - \sum_k D^{\text{on}}_k \]

\[ P_{\text{pocket}} = P_{\text{invoice}} - \sum_j D^{\text{off}}_j \]

where \(P_{\text{list}}\) is the list price to the customer (ex-GST), \(D^{\text{on}}_k\) are on-invoice deductions (trade discounts, distributor or channel discounts, order-size discounts) shown on the invoice, and \(D^{\text{off}}_j\) are off-invoice items given after or outside the invoice (rebates, schemes, cash discounts, freight paid by the seller, co-marketing, listing and visibility fees, payment-term costs). The pocket price is what the company actually "puts in its pocket" per unit from that customer.

Explanation

The concept comes from Marn and Rosiello's McKinsey work on pricing ("Managing Price, Gaining Profit", Harvard Business Review, 1992). Their key observation: companies manage list and invoice price closely, but off-invoice leakages are spread across many budgets and owners, so the pocket price is often 15–30% below list and varies widely across customers — the pocket price band. Two customers with the same invoice price can have very different pocket prices because one pays early (cash discount), takes rebates and demands free delivery.

Relationship with the GTN waterfall and Ind AS 115:

  • Most on- and off-invoice items (discounts, rebates, schemes, cash discounts, customer payments) reduce revenue under Ind AS 115 — the NSV is roughly the pocket price before cost items.
  • Freight paid by the seller and cost of extended payment terms are not revenue deductions in the financial statements (freight is a fulfilment cost; financing is a separate component only if significant), but they are included in the pocket price because they reduce what the customer relationship is worth. So pocket price ≤ NSV per unit.

Uses in budgeting and FP&A:

  • Set channel and customer-class GTN rates in the budget with a view of true profitability.
  • Identify customers or distributors with pocket prices far below average (outliers in the band) for renegotiation.
  • Track "price realisation" in the PVM bridge (Week 19) at pocket level, not invoice level.

Customer B's invoice price is only 2% lower than A's, but its pocket price is 8.6% lower. The budget for Customer B should reflect the full leakage; the RGM team should target the visibility fees and freight terms.

Connections

Directly underlies the GTN waterfall (s03) and price build (s02), and the net revenue definition in c02. It informs channel profitability and cost-to-serve analysis (Topic 3 related item) and the price element of PVM bridges (Week 19). In Revenue Growth Management, the pocket price band is the starting point for trade-terms redesign.

Practice Questions

Q1. Computing pocket price [L1]

A distributor buys hair oil at a list price of ₹112 ex-GST with a 4% on-invoice discount. Off-invoice items: scheme 5% of list, QPS rebate 1.5% of list, cash discount 2% of invoice price (taken), and company-paid freight of ₹1.60 per unit. Compute invoice and pocket price and pocket price as a % of list.

💡 Hint: Watch the base of each percentage — list or invoice.

Q2. Pocket price band analysis [L2]

For 40 distributors you have list price, invoice price and off-invoice items per unit. Describe how you would build a pocket price band chart in Excel or Power BI, the outliers you would look for, and the actions that might follow in the next AOP.

Q3. Pocket price vs NSV [L2]

Explain why freight paid by the seller is included in the pocket price but not deducted from revenue under Ind AS 115, and how you would present both measures in a channel profitability report.
