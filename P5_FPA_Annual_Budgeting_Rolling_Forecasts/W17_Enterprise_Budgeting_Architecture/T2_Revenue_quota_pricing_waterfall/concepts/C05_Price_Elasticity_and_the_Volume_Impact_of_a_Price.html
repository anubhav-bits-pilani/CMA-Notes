Key concept

Price Elasticity and the Volume Impact of a Price Increase

Concept: Price elasticity = %ΔQuantity ÷ %ΔPrice; volume impact of price increase = Base volume × elasticity × %ΔPrice

Statement

\[ \varepsilon = \frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P} = \frac{(Q_1 - Q_0)/Q_0}{(P_1 - P_0)/P_0} \]

\[ \Delta Q = Q_0 \times \varepsilon \times \%\Delta P \]

where \(Q_0\) is base volume, \(P_0\) base price, \(\%\Delta P\) the price change (e.g., +4%) and \(\varepsilon\) the (usually negative) own-price elasticity. Revenue after the change:

\[ R_1 = Q_0 (1 + \varepsilon \, \%\Delta P) \times P_0 (1 + \%\Delta P) \]

Contribution is unchanged when the volume loss exactly offsets the higher unit contribution; the break-even volume change for a price increase of \(\%\Delta P\) with contribution margin \(c\) (as a fraction of price) is

\[ \%\Delta Q^{*} = -\frac{\%\Delta P}{c + \%\Delta P} \]

Explanation

Elasticity measures how sensitive demand is to price. \(\lvert \varepsilon \rvert < 1\) (inelastic): revenue rises with price; \(\lvert \varepsilon \rvert > 1\) (elastic): revenue falls. For profit, what matters is the comparison with the break-even volume change, which depends on the contribution margin — high-margin products can lose more volume and still gain profit.

Estimating elasticity:

  • Historical regression of log volume on log relative price (own price ÷ competitor price), controlling for promotions, distribution and seasonality; the coefficient is the elasticity. In Excel, LINEST on log-transformed data.
  • Price tests in selected markets or e-commerce A/B tests.
  • Conjoint or survey research for new price points.
  • Analogues from past increases in similar categories.

Cautions: the formula is a linear approximation, reasonable for small price changes (up to about 5–10%); for large changes use the constant-elasticity form \(Q_1 = Q_0 (P_1/P_0)^{\varepsilon}\). Short-run elasticity (consumers delay purchase, trade forward-buys) differs from long-run. Competitor reactions change the effective elasticity — if competitors follow, relative price is unchanged and the volume effect is mostly a category effect (much smaller). In India, price points (₹5, ₹10) make increases discontinuous; a move from ₹10 to ₹12 is a 20% jump and elasticities from small increases do not apply.

Illustration

Detergent: \(Q_0\) = 225 lakh units over July–March, \(P_0\) = ₹80.50 net, contribution margin 38%, increase 4%, elasticity −0.6.

  • Volume impact = 225 × (−0.6) × 4% = −5.4 lakh units (−2.4%).
  • Revenue: \(R_0\) = ₹181.13 crore; \(R_1\) = 219.6 lakh × ₹83.72 = ₹183.85 crore (+₹2.72 crore).
  • Contribution per unit: ₹30.59 before; ₹33.81 after (price rises ₹3.22, variable cost unchanged).
  • Contribution: before 225 × 30.59 = ₹68.83 crore; after 219.6 × 33.81 = ₹74.25 crore (+₹5.42 crore).
  • Break-even volume change = −4% ÷ (38% + 4%) = −9.5%; the expected −2.4% is well inside, so the increase is profitable even if elasticity were nearly four times larger.

Practice Questions

Q1. Volume, revenue and contribution impact [L1]

A shampoo sells 120 lakh units a year at a net price of ₹140 with variable cost ₹77 per unit. A 5% increase is planned for the full year with elasticity −0.7. Compute the volume, revenue and contribution changes and the break-even volume change.

💡 Hint: Contribution margin as a fraction of price is needed for the break-even formula.

Q2. Estimating elasticity with LINEST [L2]

Describe how you would estimate a price elasticity in Excel from 36 months of volume, own price, competitor price and promotion-flag data. Specify the transformed columns, the LINEST formula and how you would interpret and sanity-check the coefficient.

Q3. Price point jump [L3]

A ₹10 MRP biscuit pack (list price ₹7.40) must absorb a 12% input cost increase. Options: move to ₹12 MRP with the same grammage (estimated elasticity −1.3 for this jump), or keep ₹10 and cut grammage by 10% (estimated volume loss 2%). Annual volume is 50 crore packs; variable cost before inflation is ₹5.20 per pack, of which ₹4.10 is grammage-linked. Compare the contribution impact of the two options.
