Key concept

Distribution-Led Volume: Outlets × Rate of Sale × Months

Concept: Distribution-led volume = Outlets reached × Rate of sale per outlet × Months

Statement

\[ V_{\text{dist}} = \sum_{m=1}^{12} O_m \times \text{ROS}_m \]

and in the simplified constant form

\[ V_{\text{dist}} = O \times \text{ROS} \times M \]

where \(O_m\) is the number of outlets reached (actively stocking and billed) in month \(m\), \(\text{ROS}_m\) the rate of sale in units per outlet per month, and \(M\) the number of months the outlets are active in the year. Incremental distribution-led volume uses only the new outlets and their ROS.

Explanation

Distribution is a primary growth driver in Indian FMCG because the outlet universe is vast (an estimated 1.2–1.3 crore retail outlets, mostly small kirana stores) and penetration in rural and smaller towns is incomplete. The formula converts a distribution plan — "add 40,000 direct-reach outlets in UP and Bihar" — into volume.

Key points:

  • Outlets reached should mean active outlets — billed at least once a month (or in each of the last three months) — not outlets billed once. Distinguish direct-reach outlets (served by the company's distributors' salesmen) from indirect (served via wholesalers).
  • ROS for new outlets is usually lower than for existing ones: new outlets are smaller or rural, and the brand is new to their shoppers. Use ROS from comparable recently added outlets, not the average.
  • Phasing: outlets are added over time; use a cumulative outlet curve and count only months when each tranche is active. Add pipeline fill (initial stocking) separately as primary volume.
  • Churn: some outlets drop out; budget net additions.
  • Cost to serve: more outlets need more salesmen or van routes, distributor margin support and display materials; include these costs so the decision is evaluated on contribution, not volume.
  • Overlap with market growth: distribution gains should not double-count the base volume growth already assumed from category growth.

The formula ties directly to numeric distribution: \(O = \text{ND} \times \text{Outlet universe}\).

Illustration

A soap brand plans to add 30,000 rural outlets: 10,000 active from July, a further 10,000 from October and 10,000 from January. ROS in new rural outlets is 18 units per month (existing average 32). Pipeline fill is 25 units per new outlet in the month added.

Tranche Outlets Active months in FY28 Volume (lakh units)
July 10,000 9 10,000 × 18 × 9 = 16.2
October 10,000 6 10.8
January 10,000 3 5.4
Secondary volume 32.4
Pipeline fill (30,000 × 25) 7.5
Primary volume 39.9

Using the existing ROS of 32 would have shown 57.6 lakh units of secondary volume — 78% higher — which is the classic overstatement in budget submissions. At an NSV of ₹26 per unit and contribution of 40%, secondary volume adds ₹8.42 crore NSV and ₹3.37 crore contribution; if the cost to serve (six additional salesmen at ₹3.6 lakh each per year plus ₹40 lakh of van costs) is about ₹0.62 crore, the expansion is clearly worthwhile.

Practice Questions

Q1. Phased distribution volume [L1]

A tea brand adds 8,000 outlets in May, 12,000 in August and 10,000 in November 2027, with ROS of 9 kg per outlet per month for new outlets. Compute FY 2027-28 incremental secondary volume.

💡 Hint: Count active months from the month of addition to March inclusive.

Q2. Net of churn and cost to serve [L2]

A plan adds 50,000 outlets evenly from April to September (about 8,333 per month) with monthly churn of 3% of newly added active outlets from the month after addition. ROS is 15 units; NSV ₹40 per unit; contribution 35%; cost to serve ₹180 per outlet per month. Estimate incremental contribution for the year and comment on whether churn changes the decision.

Q3. Reconciling ND targets with outlet counts [L2]

A brand's numeric distribution is 45% in an outlet universe of 9 lakh outlets in its target states. The AOP assumes ND rises to 52% by December with linear growth from April. Convert this into monthly outlet additions and incremental volume using ROS of 20 units per month for new outlets, and state the data you would request to validate the ND target.
