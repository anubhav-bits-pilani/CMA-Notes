Key concept

Capacity Quota: Productive Reps × Ramp × Full Productivity

Concept: Capacity quota = Σ (Productive reps per month × Ramp % × Full productivity per rep)

Statement

\[ C = \sum_{m=1}^{12} \sum_{r \in R} a_{r,m} \times \rho_{r,m} \times \frac{P_r}{12} \]

where \(C\) is the sales capacity for the year (₹ or units), \(R\) the set of rep positions, \(a_{r,m} \in \{0, 1\}\) indicates the position is filled and productive in month \(m\), \(\rho_{r,m}\) the ramp % (productivity relative to a fully ramped rep) based on tenure in month \(m\), and \(P_r\) the annual full productivity of a ramped rep in that territory type. Monthly seasonality can be added by replacing \(P_r/12\) with \(P_r \times I_m / 12\).

Explanation

Capacity quota answers: "Given the sales force we will actually have, what revenue can it deliver?" It is the bottom-up check on top-down quotas (s04) and on the AOP revenue itself.

Inputs and how to estimate them:

  • Productive reps per month — from the sales roster: existing reps (less expected exits), approved new hires from their start month, backfills after a vacancy lag. Reps on long leave or in training are not productive.
  • Ramp % — from historical cohort analysis: average productivity of reps in tenure months 1, 2, 3 … relative to tenured reps.
  • Full productivity — sales per fully ramped rep per year, by territory type (metro, urban, rural, MT key account). Use the median of tenured reps rather than the mean if a few star performers skew it.

Attrition handling: if positions have annual attrition \(\alpha\) and backfill lag \(L\) months, the expected productive months lost per position ≈ \(\alpha \times (L + \text{ramp shortfall months})\). Example: 18% attrition, 2-month vacancy and a ramp worth 2 lost months → about 0.72 months lost per position per year (6% of capacity).

Interpretation:

  • Capacity < AOP: the plan is under-resourced — hire earlier, improve productivity (routes, SFA tools, distributor coverage) or reduce the revenue plan.
  • Capacity > AOP with Σ quotas = AOP × (1 + buffer): consistent.
  • Capacity ≫ AOP: possible over-staffing or sandbagged productivity assumptions.

Illustration

Region with 30 tenured reps (full productivity ₹6 crore each per year = ₹50 lakh/month), 2 expected exits in September with backfills productive from December, and 4 new hires from July. Ramp: tenure months 1–3 = 40%, 4–6 = 75%, 7+ = 100%.

Component Rep-months × ramp Capacity (₹ crore)
28 reps all year: 28 × 12 336.0 168.0
2 exiting reps: April–September, 6 months each 12.0 6.0
2 backfills: December–February at 40%, March at 75% 2 × (3 × 0.40 + 0.75) = 3.9 1.95
4 new hires from July: Jul–Sep 40%, Oct–Dec 75%, Jan–Mar 100% 4 × (1.2 + 2.25 + 3.0) = 25.8 12.9
Total 377.7 188.85

If the AOP for the region is ₹200 crore, capacity is short by about ₹11 crore before any quota buffer — the conversation in catchball is about hiring earlier or productivity initiatives, not about raising every rep's quota.

Practice Questions

Q1. Capacity with hiring and exits [L2]

A team has 12 tenured reps (₹4.8 crore each per year). Two reps leave at the end of June; backfills join on 1 September. Three new hires join on 1 October. Ramp: months 1–2 = 30%, months 3–4 = 60%, month 5+ = 100%. Compute annual capacity and compare it with a regional AOP of ₹62 crore.

💡 Hint: Count productive months by ramp band for each cohort separately.

Q2. Estimating ramp from cohort data [L2]

Average monthly sales of three past hiring cohorts in tenure months 1–6 were ₹10, 18, 26, 32, 36 and 39 lakh; tenured reps average ₹40 lakh. Derive the ramp curve and explain two adjustments you would consider before using it.

Q3. Capacity vs quota reconciliation [L3]

For a national sales force, capacity is ₹1,140 crore, the AOP is ₹1,200 crore and Σ quotas with a 12% buffer are ₹1,344 crore. Write a one-page reconciliation note for the CFO explaining the gaps and proposing actions, including the effect on the incentive budget.
