Subtopic note

Sales Quota Setting: Top-Down, Capacity-Based and Territory Potential

Scope: Sales quota setting: top-down allocation vs capacity-based (ramped reps × productivity) vs territory-potential methods

Why it matters

Quotas translate the AOP revenue into individual commitments for regional managers, area sales managers and reps. They drive incentive cost, sales behaviour and — through sales force capacity — whether the revenue budget is achievable at all. FP&A usually co-owns quota setting with sales operations: FP&A ensures that quotas reconcile to the AOP and incentive budget; sales ops ensures they are fair and motivating. Three methods exist, and good practice triangulates them.

Core notes

Method 1: Top-down allocation

The company or region target is split to territories in proportion to a historical or strategic key:

\[ Q_i = T \times \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j} \]

where \(Q_i\) is territory \(i\)'s quota, \(T\) the parent target and \(w_i\) the weight — last year's sales, last year's share of growth, or a blend (e.g., 70% prior-year sales share + 30% potential share).

  • Pros: fast, reconciles automatically to the AOP, easy to explain.
  • Cons: perpetuates history (strong territories get harder targets every year — the "ratchet effect"); ignores capacity and potential; vacant or new territories are mishandled.

Method 2: Capacity-based (bottom-up)

Quotas reflect what the sales force can deliver given headcount, ramp and productivity (c04):

\[ \text{Capacity} = \sum_{m} \sum_{r} \text{Active}_{r,m} \times \text{Ramp}_{r,m} \times \frac{P}{12} \]

where \(\text{Active}_{r,m}\) is 1 if rep \(r\) is in seat in month \(m\), \(\text{Ramp}_{r,m}\) the productivity % given tenure, and \(P\) annual full productivity per rep (₹ or units). Productivity comes from historical sales per fully ramped rep, adjusted for territory type.

  • Pros: tests feasibility; integrates hiring plans and attrition; shows the revenue impact of hiring delays.
  • Cons: productivity averages hide territory differences; can become a "we need more people" argument.

Method 3: Territory potential

Quotas reflect market opportunity in each territory:

\[ \text{Potential}_i = \text{Market size}_i \times \text{Target share}_i \]

Market size from NielsenIQ or category data by state/town class, outlet universe (number of grocery outlets by town), population and income proxies; target share from current share plus planned gains. For B2B, potential is the addressable account base × expected wallet share.

  • Pros: fair across territories; directs effort to under-penetrated markets.
  • Cons: data-intensive; potential estimates are uncertain; may not reconcile to the AOP without scaling.

Triangulation in practice

  1. Compute the AOP-driven top-down quota per territory.
  2. Compute capacity per territory (reps in seat with ramp).
  3. Compute potential per territory.
  4. Compare: where top-down > capacity, the territory is under-resourced (hire, transfer reps, or reduce quota); where potential ≫ current sales, increase weight; where top-down > potential, the quota is unrealistic.
  5. Blend into a final quota, then apply over-assignment (s05) so Σ quotas exceed the AOP.

Illustration (₹ crore)

Territory Prior-year sales Top-down (AOP ₹300 crore, PY share) Reps (ramped FTE) × ₹6 crore Potential (market × target share) Blended quota (40/30/30)
North 110 122.2 19.0 × 6 = 114.0 140 125.1
West 90 100.0 17.5 × 6 = 105.0 98 100.9
South 70 77.8 13.0 × 6 = 78.0 92 82.1
Total 270 300.0 297.0 330 308.1

North's capacity (₹114 crore) is below its top-down quota and well below potential — a signal to add reps there. The blended total exceeds the AOP by about 2.7%; s05 decides whether this buffer is enough.

Quota measure and grain

  • Measure: FMCG GT reps are measured on secondary sales value or volume (what they influence) with distributor-level primary targets for area managers; MT key account managers on primary NSV; B2B on bookings or billings.
  • Grain: quota per rep per month or quarter, by category if mix matters (e.g., separate quota for focus brands).
  • Phasing: quotas follow seasonality (s06) — never flat — otherwise reps miss quotas in troughs and over-achieve in peaks, distorting incentive payouts.

Link to the incentive budget

Incentive cost = Σ reps' expected payout given the attainment distribution. If quotas are too easy, incentive cost exceeds budget while revenue only meets the AOP; if too hard, attrition rises. Model the incentive cost using the historical attainment distribution against the new quotas.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Ratchet effect: avoid using only last year's actuals; blend with potential or cap year-on-year increases for over-achievers to keep incentives.
  • Territory realignment: if territories are redrawn, recompute history on the new boundaries before allocation.
  • Key accounts spanning territories: allocate quota once (key account manager) and give territory reps "shadow" credit only if the plan is designed for it; double-counting inflates Σ quotas without adding revenue.
  • Vacant territories: assign quota to the area manager or a "house" account so revenue still has an owner; apply ramp when filled.
  • Large one-off deals (institutional tender): carve out from quota or treat separately to avoid windfall payouts.

Key takeaways

  • Top-down allocation reconciles to the AOP but perpetuates history and ignores capacity.
  • Capacity-based quotas sum ramped reps × productivity and test feasibility of the revenue plan.
  • Territory-potential quotas reflect market opportunity and fairness but need data and scaling.
  • Triangulate the three methods; differences reveal resourcing gaps and unrealistic targets.
  • Quotas must be on a measure the rep controls, phased with seasonality and linked to the incentive budget.
  • Handle ratchets, realignments, key accounts, vacancies and one-off deals explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Triangulating quotas [L2]

AOP revenue for a region is ₹180 crore. Territory data: A — PY sales ₹70 crore, 11 ramped FTE, potential ₹85 crore; B — PY ₹50 crore, 9 ramped FTE, potential ₹55 crore; C — PY ₹40 crore, 5 ramped FTE, potential ₹62 crore. Full productivity is ₹6.2 crore per FTE. Compute top-down, capacity and potential quotas, a 50/25/25 blended quota, and identify the resourcing action needed.

💡 Hint: Scale the potential method so its total can be compared with the AOP before blending.

Q2. Choosing a quota measure [L2]

For each role — GT sales rep, area sales manager, MT key account manager, e-commerce manager, institutional sales manager — recommend the quota measure (primary or secondary, value or volume, NSV or gross) and explain why.

Q3. Ratchet effect [L3]

A top-performing rep achieved 125% of quota in FY 2026-27. Under prior-year share allocation, his FY 2027-28 quota rises 32% versus a regional increase of 12%. Explain the behavioural risk, quantify a capped alternative (maximum increase of 1.5× the regional increase), and describe how the shortfall is reallocated.

Q4. Capacity shortfall [L2]

A capacity calculation shows the region can deliver ₹285 crore against an AOP of ₹310 crore. Hiring of 6 additional reps would take 3 months to fill with ramp 25/50/75/100% by quarter of tenure and full productivity ₹6 crore per year. Estimate the revenue each hire adds in FY 2027-28 if hired from July and present options to close the gap.
