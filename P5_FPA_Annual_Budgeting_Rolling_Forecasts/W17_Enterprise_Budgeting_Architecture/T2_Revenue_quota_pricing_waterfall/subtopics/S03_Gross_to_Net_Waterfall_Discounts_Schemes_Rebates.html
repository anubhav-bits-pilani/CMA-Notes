Subtopic note

Gross-to-Net Waterfall: Discounts, Schemes, Rebates, Returns, Margins

Scope: Gross-to-net (GTN) waterfall: trade discounts, schemes, cash discounts, rebates, returns, distributor margins

Why it matters

In Indian FMCG, the gap between gross sales at list price and net sales value (NSV) is typically 10–25% of gross. This "trade spend" is often the second-largest cost after materials, and under Ind AS 115 most of it is a reduction of revenue rather than an expense. A budget that ignores or misclassifies GTN overstates revenue and margin, misstates the A&P ratio and produces monthly variances when actual deductions hit. The GTN waterfall is also the control framework for trade spend: each layer has an owner, a rate, an accrual method and a settlement process.

Below NSV, some companies show "net net" measures (after freight or warehousing for MT) — that is the pocket price (c01).

Distributor margins — where they sit

There are two price-structure conventions:

  1. List price is the price to distributor: distributor margin is embedded in the gap between distributor's selling price to retailer and list price. It is not a deduction in the company's waterfall — the company never invoiced the higher amount (s02).
  2. List price is the distributor's selling price (or a common trade price): the distributor's margin is given as an on-invoice discount and appears as a waterfall layer.

Either is acceptable in the budget, but it must be consistent with how the ERP records invoices, or budget-vs-actual comparisons fail.

Budget mechanics

For each channel × customer class (and where material, SKU group):

\[ \text{NSV} = \text{Gross sales} \times \left(1 - d_{\text{trade}} - d_{\text{scheme}} - d_{\text{cash}} - d_{\text{rebate}} - d_{\text{customer}} - d_{\text{returns}}\right) \]

with each \(d\) a rate on gross (or on a defined base — cash discount on invoice value incl. GST is common commercially, so convert it to a rate on gross ex-GST in the model). Fixed-amount items (an annual listing fee of ₹1.2 crore with a chain) are budgeted as amounts by month, not rates.

GST (18%) is charged on the invoice value — the trade discount given on invoice reduces the GST base, while post-supply discounts reduce GST only if they meet Sec 15(3)(b) of the CGST Act conditions (agreed beforehand and linked to invoices, with ITC reversal by the recipient); otherwise GST is paid on the higher value and the credit note is a "financial" credit note. Budget this because it affects GST cash, not revenue.

Accrual and settlement

Off-invoice items are accrued monthly (Dr revenue deduction, Cr provision/accrued liability) and settled later via credit notes or payments. Budget the accrual in the P&L and the settlement in the cash budget with a lag (e.g., schemes settled 30–45 days after month-end, annual rebates settled after year-end). The accrued-scheme liability becomes a balance sheet line in the budgeted balance sheet.

Reconciling to history

Build the GTN rates from the last 24 months' actuals by channel, then adjust for planned changes (new scheme structure, change in cash discount policy, a new key-account agreement). Show the budgeted GTN % alongside actual GTN % for the last two years in the template; a sudden improvement needs an initiative and owner.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Free goods (buy 10 get 1 free) — the cost of free goods is COGS; revenue is allocated across all units delivered; in budgets, many companies model free goods as a volume uplift at zero price, which reduces average net price.
  • Display and visibility payments: if the retailer provides a distinct service at fair value (rare to demonstrate), the payment can be an expense; otherwise it reduces revenue. Check the accounting policy.
  • Distributor claims (damages, transport subsidies) — classify by substance: price concessions reduce revenue; reimbursement of distinct services may be expense.
  • Rebate constraint: if a target rebate is only payable at 110% of last year's volume and achievement is uncertain, the expected value method with the constraint determines the accrual; in the budget, accrue at the expected rate consistent with the budgeted volume.
  • Returns policy changes (e.g., stopping expiry returns in favour of a liquidation discount) move the cost between layers; keep the total trade spend view.

Key takeaways

  • GTN typically consumes 10–25% of gross sales in FMCG and must be budgeted layer by layer, channel by channel.
  • Most layers — trade discounts, schemes, cash discounts, rebates, customer payments, returns — reduce revenue under Ind AS 115.
  • Distributor margins are either embedded in the price structure or shown as on-invoice discounts; be consistent with the ERP.
  • Budget accruals in the P&L and settlements in cash with lags; the accrued trade spend is a balance sheet liability.
  • Post-supply discounts affect GST only if CGST Sec 15(3) conditions are met — a cash budget issue.
  • Reconcile budgeted GTN rates to two years of actuals and require initiatives for improvements.

Practice Questions

Q1. Building a channel waterfall [L2]

MT sales of soap for FY 2027-28: 150 lakh units at list price ₹30.50. On-invoice trade discount 6%, schemes 4%, QPS rebate 2% of gross payable annually in May 2028, listing fees ₹1.8 crore fixed for the year paid quarterly in advance, returns 1.2%. Build the annual waterfall to NSV and state the closing accrued rebate liability at 31 March 2028.

💡 Hint: The listing fee is a fixed amount, not a rate; spread its revenue reduction over the period it relates to.

Q2. GST on post-supply discounts [L2]

A distributor receives a 3% off-invoice scheme credit note on invoices of ₹50 lakh ex-GST (GST 18%). Explain the GST and cash budget treatment if (a) the scheme was agreed before supply and linked to invoices, and (b) it was announced after supply. Quantify the GST cash difference.

Q3. Classifying GTN items [L2]

Classify each of the following as revenue reduction or expense, citing Ind AS 115 reasoning: (a) ₹40 lakh paid to a hypermarket for end-cap display; (b) 2% cash discount for payment within 7 days; (c) free sample sachets given to consumers by the company's promoters; (d) a ₹25 lakh payment to an e-commerce platform for sponsored listings on its app; (e) expiry returns allowance.

Q4. GTN challenge in catchball [L3]

A BU budget shows GTN falling from 17.8% to 14.9% of gross, with the BU head citing "scheme rationalisation". Prepare an analysis plan and the catchball questions you would ask, and state what evidence would make you accept the improvement.
