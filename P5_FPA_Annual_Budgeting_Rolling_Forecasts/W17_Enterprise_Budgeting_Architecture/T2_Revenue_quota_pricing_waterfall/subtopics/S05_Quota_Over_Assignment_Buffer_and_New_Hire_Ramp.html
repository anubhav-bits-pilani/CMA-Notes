Quota Over-Assignment Buffer and New-Hire Ramp Curves

Scope: Quota over-assignment buffer (sum of rep quotas 105–120% of company target) and ramp curves for new hires

Why it matters

Quota attainment is never uniform: in a typical sales force, 50–65% of reps achieve quota, some exceed it and many fall short. Reps leave mid-year, territories go vacant, and new hires take months to become productive. If the sum of individual quotas equals the company target, the company's expected revenue is below target. Over-assignment (also called quota buffer or overlay) and ramp curves are the two mechanisms that reconcile individual quotas with the AOP. FP&A must quantify both, because they determine whether the revenue budget is supported and what the incentive plan will cost.

Core notes

Over-assignment

\[ \text{Over-assignment \%} = \frac{\sum_i Q_i}{T} - 1 \]

where \(Q_i\) is rep \(i\)'s quota and \(T\) the company (or region) target. Typical ranges are 5–20% (i.e., Σ quotas at 105–120% of target). The right buffer depends on:

  • Historical attainment — expected revenue ÷ Σ quotas. If, historically, the sales force delivered 90% of Σ quotas, the buffer must be at least \(1/0.90 - 1 = 11.1\%\).
  • Attrition and vacancy — reps who leave deliver part of their quota; vacant territories deliver less until filled.
  • New-hire share — the more new hires, the more ramp shortfall.
  • Level in the hierarchy — buffers are often layered: 5% from company to region, a further 5% from region to area, and 3–5% from area to rep, compounding to 13–16%.

Derivation of the required buffer:

\[ \sum_i Q_i \ge \frac{T}{\bar{a}} \]

where \(\bar{a}\) is the expected weighted attainment ratio (revenue delivered ÷ quota assigned) based on history and the planned sales force mix.

Illustration: region target ₹300 crore; historical attainment ratio 92% for tenured reps; 20% of quota sits with new hires whose ramp-adjusted delivery is 70% of a flat quota. Expected attainment = 0.8 × 92% + 0.2 × 70% = 87.6%. Required Σ quotas = 300 ÷ 0.876 = ₹342.5 crore — a buffer of 14.2%.

Trade-offs of a large buffer

  • Too small: the company misses the target even with normal attainment; management "re-cuts" quotas mid-year, damaging credibility.
  • Too large: quotas feel unattainable, morale and attrition worsen, and fewer reps hit accelerators; but if many reps do hit, incentive cost exceeds budget because payouts are on inflated quotas. Model incentive cost on the over-assigned quotas with the expected attainment distribution.
  • Uneven buffers: loading the buffer only onto high performers is the ratchet effect (s04); allocate it proportionally or by potential.

Ramp curves

A ramp curve gives the productivity of a new hire as a percentage of a fully ramped rep, by month or quarter of tenure. Typical FMCG GT ramp: 3–6 months; B2B enterprise sales: 6–12 months.

Tenure month 1 2 3 4 5 6 7+
Ramp % (FMCG GT) 20% 40% 60% 75% 90% 100% 100%
Ramp % (B2B, quarterly) Q1: 0–25% Q2: 50% Q3: 75% Q4: 100%

Build ramp curves from history (productivity of past cohorts by tenure month) and apply them to:

  • New hires from their start month.
  • Backfills after an exit, with a vacancy gap (time-to-hire) during which the territory delivers only a "house" baseline (e.g., 50–60% of normal through distributor momentum).
  • Transfers into new territories (a shorter ramp).

Ramp-adjusted quota and capacity

New hires' quotas are usually ramped too: a rep joining in September with a full-year quota of ₹6 crore gets a quota for the remaining seven months at ramp, not ₹3.5 crore (7/12) flat.

\[ Q_{\text{new}} = \sum_{m = s}^{12} \frac{P}{12} \times \text{Ramp}(m - s + 1) \]

where \(s\) is the start month index (April = 1), \(P\) full annual productivity and \(\text{Ramp}(k)\) the ramp % in tenure month \(k\).

Illustration: \(P\) = ₹6 crore (₹50 lakh per month); start September (\(s = 6\)); ramp 20/40/60/75/90/100/100%. Months Sep–Mar (7 months) quota = ₹50 lakh × (0.20 + 0.40 + 0.60 + 0.75 + 0.90 + 1.00 + 1.00) = ₹50 lakh × 4.85 = ₹2.425 crore, versus ₹3.5 crore on a flat pro-rata basis.

Excel implementation

A roster table of reps with start month, exit month and full productivity; a ramp lookup table by tenure month; a month grid:

=IF(AND(F$3>=[@Start_Month],F$3<=[@Exit_Month]),[@Full_Prod]/12*XLOOKUP(F$3-[@Start_Month]+1,tblRamp[Tenure],tblRamp[Ramp],1),0)

The if_not_found value of 1 treats tenure beyond the table as fully ramped; existing reps have a start month far in the past so they return 1. Sum the grid for capacity; compare with Σ quotas and the AOP.

Reconciliation pack

Present: AOP target → Σ assigned quotas (with buffer %) → expected delivery at historical attainment → capacity (ramped reps × productivity) → gap or surplus. This becomes evidence in catchball (Topic 1) and in the incentive cost budget (Topic 1, s08).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Seasonal hiring: hiring ahead of the festive season means reps are still ramping during peak months — consider hiring earlier or accepting a lower festive share in their quota.
  • Ramp for experienced lateral hires is shorter than for freshers; segment ramp curves by hire type.
  • Mid-year exits: the exiting rep's remaining quota must be reassigned (to the backfill on ramp and/or the area manager) or explicitly written off against the buffer.
  • Quota relief for leave, territory changes or product recalls should follow a written policy; ad hoc relief erodes the buffer.
  • Buffer at different levels compounds quickly; track the cumulative buffer from company to rep.

Key takeaways

  • Over-assignment makes Σ rep quotas exceed the company target (typically 105–120%) to absorb uneven attainment, attrition and vacancies.
  • Required buffer ≈ 1 ÷ expected attainment ratio − 1, based on history and new-hire mix.
  • Ramp curves give tenure-based productivity; apply them to new hires, backfills and transfers.
  • Ramp-adjusted quotas for new hires are lower than flat pro-rata quotas.
  • Model capacity and incentive cost on the over-assigned, ramped quotas.
  • Track buffers layered through the hierarchy and document quota relief rules.

Practice Questions

Q1. Computing the required buffer [L2]

A company target is ₹500 crore. Tenured reps carry 80% of quota with a historical attainment ratio of 94%; new hires carry 12% with ramp-adjusted attainment of 65%; vacant territories carry 8% with expected delivery of 55% of quota. Compute the expected attainment ratio and the Σ quota needed, and the buffer %.

💡 Hint: Weight each group's attainment by its share of quota.

Q2. Ramp-adjusted quota [L2]

A rep with full productivity of ₹7.2 crore per year joins on 1 November 2027. Ramp: month 1 = 25%, month 2 = 50%, month 3 = 75%, month 4 onwards = 100%. Compute the ramp-adjusted FY 2027-28 quota and compare it with a flat pro-rata quota.

Q3. Building a capacity grid in Excel [L3]

Design a capacity model for 60 reps with start months, exit months, ramp curve by hire type (fresher, lateral) and backfill rules (two-month vacancy; territory delivers 50% during vacancy). Specify the tables, formulas and the summary that reconciles capacity to the AOP and Σ quotas.

Q4. Buffer too high? [L3]

A sales head proposes Σ quotas at 125% of the AOP. Historical attainment ratio is 93%, and the incentive plan pays 2× accelerators above 100% attainment. Evaluate the proposal in terms of morale, incentive cost and revenue risk, and recommend a buffer with reasoning.
