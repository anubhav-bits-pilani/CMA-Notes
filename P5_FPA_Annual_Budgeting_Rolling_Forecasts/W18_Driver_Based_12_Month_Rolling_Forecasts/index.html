How this week fits

This is the second week of Phase 5 (FP&A, Annual Budgeting, Rolling Forecasts & Variance Storytelling). Week 17 built the static Annual Operating Plan (AOP): a driver sheet, phasing logic, functional budgets and a master budget rolling into a budgeted P&L, balance sheet and cash flow for FY26 (April 2025 to March 2026). A static budget is frozen the day the Board approves it. By September it is describing a world that no longer exists: demand has moved, input prices have moved, a competitor has launched. Week 18 turns that frozen plan into a living forecast in three moves:

  1. Timeline mechanics — a forecast window that rolls forward automatically when the books close, driven by one cutoff date.
  2. Actual + forecast blending — the Latest Estimate (LE), which combines closed actuals for the year-to-date (YTD) with a fresh forecast for the year-to-go (YTG) to answer "where will we land against the AOP?".
  3. Driver logic — forecasting revenue and cost from operational quantities (stores, footfall, leads, capacity, ARR) instead of extrapolating ledger balances.

Topics 4 and 5 (version/scenario governance and forecast accuracy) complete the week by making the forecast auditable over successive cycles and measurable after the fact.

Prerequisites. Week 9 Excel (dynamic arrays, LET, LAMBDA, date functions), Week 10 Power Query (appending ERP extracts into a long-format fact table), Week 11 Power BI/DAX time intelligence, and Week 17's budget workbook. If your Week 17 driver sheet is still hard-coded in places, fix that first; this week's exercise builds directly on it.

What comes next. Week 19 deepens variance analysis (price/volume/mix bridges against these forecasts), Week 20 packages the LE into the Monthly Business Review (MBR) pack and executive summary, and Week 21 uses the driver model for CVP and operating leverage analysis. Phase 4's 3-statement and DCF models reuse the same timeline and scenario mechanics.

Refresh before starting

Excel date functions and dynamic arrays

Excel date functions (EOMONTH, EDATE, SEQUENCE) and dynamic arrays

  • EOMONTH(start, months) returns the serial number of the last day of the month that is months away from start. EOMONTH("15-Sep-2025",0) gives 30-Sep-2025; with -1 it gives 31-Aug-2025; with +1 it gives 31-Oct-2025. Adding 1 to a month-end gives the first day of the next month.
  • EDATE(start, months) keeps the day-of-month (clamped to month-end), so EDATE("31-Jan-2026",1) returns 28-Feb-2026. Use EOMONTH for month-end keys and EDATE for anniversary-style dates (contract renewals, loan instalments).
  • SEQUENCE(rows, cols, start, step) spills an array. SEQUENCE(1,12) is a horizontal 1…12 that can be fed into EOMONTH to spill 12 month-end headers in one cell.
  • Spilled ranges are referenced with # (e.g. C4#). A spill collides (#SPILL!) if anything sits in its path — keep header rows clear.
  • Indian FY helpers: fiscal year of a date =YEAR(EOMONTH(d,-3))+1 gives the FY ending year (FY26 for any date April 2025–March 2026); fiscal month number =MOD(MONTH(d)-4,12)+1 (April = 1, March = 12).
=EOMONTH($B$1,SEQUENCE(1,12))
=LET(c,$B$1, fy,YEAR(EOMONTH(c,-3))+1, fy)
=MOD(MONTH(C$4)-4,12)+1

SUMIFS and XLOOKUP on long-format tables

SUMIFS/XLOOKUP on long-format tables and structured references

  • A long-format (tidy) table has one row per atomic fact: Version | Scenario | Entity | Account | Month | Amount. Convert it to an Excel Table (Ctrl+T) and name it, e.g. Act, so formulas refer to Act[Amt], Act[Month] and auto-expand as rows are appended.
  • SUMIFS(sum_range, crit_range1, crit1, …) aggregates any slice and returns 0 when nothing matches — safe for months with no postings.
  • XLOOKUP(lookup, lookup_array, return_array, [if_not_found], [match_mode], [search_mode]) returns a single match; for multi-criteria use a Boolean product: XLOOKUP(1,(Tbl[Acct]=$A5)*(Tbl[Month]=C$4),Tbl[Amt],0). Unlike SUMIFS, it returns only the first match — duplicates are silently ignored, which is a control risk.
  • Mixed references ($A5, C$4) let one formula fill an account × month grid.
  • Date criteria must match the stored value exactly: month keys must be true dates (serials) set to month-end, not text like "Apr-25".

Basic statistics refresher

Basic statistics: mean, standard deviation, regression, moving averages

  • Mean \( \bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i \); sample standard deviation \( s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}} \) — Excel AVERAGE, STDEV.S.
  • Coefficient of variation \( s / \bar{x} \) indicates how predictable a driver is — useful when selecting drivers.
  • Simple linear regression \( y = a + b x \): SLOPE, INTERCEPT, RSQ, FORECAST.LINEAR; multiple regression via LINEST or the Analysis ToolPak. Check \( R^2 \), the sign and plausibility of coefficients, and residual patterns.
  • Moving averages: a 3-month simple moving average smooths noise but lags turning points; a centred 12-month moving average removes seasonality and isolates trend. Exponential smoothing weights recent observations more heavily (FORECAST.ETS).

Cross-cutting concepts

One cutoff switch drives everything

One cutoff switch controls timeline headers, actual/forecast flags, YTD/YTG splits and LE totals

A forecasting workbook should have exactly one input that tells it "what is closed": the Current Actuals Cutoff Month, stored as a month-end date (e.g. 30-Sep-2025) in a named cell Cutoff. Every time-dependent element derives from it. Timeline headers for the rolling window are EOMONTH(Cutoff, SEQUENCE(1,12)); the fiscal-year headers are fixed April–March but carry a flag =--(Hdr<=Cutoff). That flag selects the data source per column (actuals database vs forecast calculation), splits the year into YTD (flag = 1) and YTG (flag = 0) using SUMPRODUCT or SUMIFS on the flag row, and therefore produces the LE total. Labels such as "6+6 LE FY26" are concatenated from the cutoff (=MOD(MONTH(Cutoff)-4,12)+1 & "+" & …). The test of a good design is mechanical: change the cutoff from 30-Sep to 31-Oct and every header, flag, subtotal, chart title and variance column moves with zero manual edits. Anything that has to be edited by hand is a defect — it will be forgotten in a close-week crunch.

Database-style storage

Database-style storage: Version × Scenario × Account × Entity × Month × Amount

Store numbers once, in a long-format fact table whose columns are the dimensions Version (Budget, Q1 Fcst, Q2 Fcst, LE Oct), Scenario (Base, Upside, Downside), Account, Entity (legal entity, BU, plant, region), Month (month-end date) and Amount. Reports are then views: a SUMIFS grid, a pivot table, or a Power BI matrix sliced by any dimension. This layout has three advantages over "one sheet per version": appending a new version is a paste (or a Power Query append) rather than a sheet copy with broken references; every version uses identical formulas, so comparisons are like-for-like; and the same table feeds Excel, Power BI and an EPM upload without reshaping. It mirrors how Anaplan modules, Oracle EPBCS cubes and Adaptive Planning store data, so the skill transfers directly to EPM tools. Keep a separate mapping table (GL account → reporting line, entity → BU) so a chart-of-accounts change is handled by editing a mapping row, not formulas.

Forecast as a decision tool

Forecast as a decision tool: accuracy, bias and speed matter more than precision

A forecast exists to trigger decisions — reallocate marketing spend, slow hiring, build inventory ahead of festive season, revise market guidance, draw a working-capital line. A forecast that is precise to the rupee but arrives on working day 12 is less useful than one that is directionally right on working day 4. Three properties matter. Accuracy: is the error small enough that the decision would not change (measured by WAPE/MAPE at the right lag)? Bias: is the error systematically in one direction (sandbagging revenue, padding costs)? Unbiased forecasts with moderate error are more useful than biased forecasts with small error, because bias compounds into wrong decisions every cycle. Speed: can the cycle be repeated monthly without heroic effort? This is why rolling forecasts use fewer lines than the budget, driver logic for material items and simple run-rates for the tail. Ask of every line: "would a different number here change a decision?" If not, simplify it.

Forecast-to-forecast reconciliation

Forecast-to-forecast reconciliation and narrative of changes each cycle

Every forecast cycle must explain how it differs from the previous one. The bridge runs from Prior LE to Current LE through: (a) actualisation — the month just closed replaced its forecast with actual, and the difference is a real variance; (b) driver changes — revised volumes, prices, conversion rates, FX, commodity costs for the YTG months; (c) new risks and opportunities crystallised or added; (d) timing shifts between months or into next year; (e) one-offs and reclassifications. Each bar needs an owner and a one-line narrative ("Q3 volume −₹4.2 crore: modern trade destocking ahead of GST rate change, per Sales Head"). Without this, leadership sees an LE that drifts every month without knowing whether the business is deteriorating or the forecasters are adjusting assumptions. It also exposes forecasters who leave YTG unchanged ("budget-plug") because no driver bar appears.

Required tabs

  1. Control — named Cutoff (data-validated month-ends), derived labels (e.g. "6+6 LE"), version selector, scenario selector.
  2. Drivers_Fcst — revised driver values by month for the YTG, with a column showing the AOP driver alongside.
  3. Fact — long-format table: Version, Scenario, Entity, Account, Month, Amount; loaded with Budget (AOP), Actuals, and at least two saved snapshots (Q1 Fcst 3+9 and Q2 Fcst 6+6).
  4. Rolling12 — next 12 months from the cutoff; headers via EOMONTH/SEQUENCE; flags; value formula switching between Fact actuals and driver-based forecast.
  5. LE_FY — April–March grid with Actual/Forecast flag row, YTD, YTG, LE, AOP, Gap to AOP, Attainment %, PY, required run-rate, seasonality-adjusted YTG, and an R&O overlay table with probability-weighted risk-adjusted LE.
  6. Bridge — Prior LE to Current LE bridge (actualisation, driver changes, R&O, timing).
  7. Accuracy — lag-1 and lag-3 MAPE, WAPE, bias % and tracking signal by BU, with conditional formatting for tolerance breaches.
  8. Checks — the check panel below.

Required checks

  • Flag row sums to the number of closed months implied by the cutoff.
  • YTD + YTG = LE for every line; LE grid total equals the sum of BUs.
  • Actuals pulled into the grid equal the Fact table total for closed months (difference = 0).
  • No hard-coded numbers in Rolling12 or LE_FY formulas (use ISFORMULA conditional formatting to highlight constants).
  • Prior versions unchanged: a checksum (sum of Amount by version) stored at save time equals the current sum.
  • Moving the cutoff from 30-Sep-2025 to 31-Oct-2025 changes headers, labels and totals without #REF!, #SPILL! or #N/A.

Deliverable

An .xlsx workbook plus a one-page PDF memo to the CFO: LE vs AOP for revenue and EBITDA, the three largest bridge items, top three risks/opportunities with probabilities, and forecast accuracy by BU with a comment on bias. No macros required; avoid OFFSET and INDIRECT.

Saturday assessment paper

Roadmap assessment: Saturday 3-Hour Practical Test: Convert a static FY26 budget into a 6+6 Latest Estimate (LE) model after ingesting 6 months of actuals and revised Q3/Q4 demand drivers.

Instructions. Time: 3 hours. Total: 100 marks. Open-book, Excel only (Microsoft 365). Build in one workbook; marks are awarded for correctness, structure (single cutoff, no hard-codes in calculation sheets), checks and the written commentary. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. FY26 = April 2025 to March 2026.

Company data — Kaveri Appliances Ltd (B2B and retail channels). FY26 AOP revenue ₹1,200 crore phased by month: Apr 6%, May 7%, Jun 7%, Jul 7%, Aug 8%, Sep 9%, Oct 12%, Nov 11%, Dec 8%, Jan 8%, Feb 8%, Mar 9%. AOP gross margin 32%, AOP fixed opex ₹18 crore per month, AOP EBITDA ₹168 crore. PY (FY25) revenue ₹1,080 crore.

Actuals April–September 2025 (revenue / gross margin %): Apr 70 / 31%, May 82 / 31%, Jun 80 / 30%, Jul 86 / 31%, Aug 92 / 30%, Sep 104 / 30%. Fixed opex actual: ₹18.6 crore per month.

Revised Q3/Q4 drivers: dealer orders are converted from B2B leads with a 2-month lag; leads Aug 4,800, Sep 5,600, Oct 5,200, Nov 4,600, Dec 4,400, Jan 4,500; lead-to-order conversion 18%; average order value ₹8.5 lakh. Retail channel revenue for Oct–Mar is 40% of total company revenue in every month (i.e. B2B = 60%). Copper cost increase reduces gross margin to 29% from November.

Q1. Ingest actuals and build the cutoff engine [15 marks]

Create a long-format Fact table containing the AOP (by month) and actuals for April–September. Create a named Cutoff cell set to 30-Sep-2025, FY month headers, an actual flag row and a "6+6 LE" label derived from the cutoff. Demonstrate that changing the cutoff to 31-Aug-2025 produces a "5+7" structure without edits.

💡 Hint: Derive closed-month count from the fiscal month number of the cutoff, not by counting cells.

Q2. Driver-based YTG revenue [20 marks]

Using the lag, conversion and order-value drivers, forecast B2B orders and revenue for October–March (show the lag mapping of each lead month to its order month explicitly), gross up to total revenue using the channel mix, and populate the YTG columns.

Q3. LE P&L and attainment [15 marks]

Build the 6+6 LE P&L to EBITDA. Compute attainment % and gap to AOP for revenue and EBITDA, and growth vs PY.

Q4. Run-rate analysis [15 marks]

Compute (a) the required monthly run-rate to hit AOP revenue, (b) the naive YTD-average run-rate projection, and (c) the seasonality-adjusted YTG using AOP phasing weights. Comment on which is the most credible and why the naive method misleads here.

Q5. Risk and opportunity overlay [10 marks]

Risks: large dealer insolvency ₹22 crore revenue at 30% probability; GST rate cut deferral delaying purchases ₹35 crore at 40%. Opportunities: government tender ₹40 crore at 25%; export order ₹15 crore at 60%. Assume 30% contribution margin on all items. Produce a risk-adjusted LE for revenue and EBITDA.

Q6. Forecast bridge [10 marks]

The Q1 Forecast (3+9) showed revenue LE of ₹1,215 crore. Build a bridge from Q1 Forecast LE to your 6+6 LE separating actualisation of Jul–Sep, Q3/Q4 driver changes and margin effects (for EBITDA). Use your own Q1 Forecast monthly assumptions where needed and state them.

Q7. Board memo [15 marks]

Write a 250-word memo to the Audit Committee covering LE vs AOP, the drivers of the gap, R&O, implications for the annual performance bonus accrual (bonus pool ₹9 crore at 100% attainment, zero below 90%, linear between) and whether the company's earlier public commentary of "double-digit revenue growth" remains supportable.

💡 Hint: Be explicit about which LE (base or risk-adjusted) drives the Ind AS 19 accrual and why.
