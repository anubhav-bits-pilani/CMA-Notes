Subtopic note

Version Comparisons and Forecast-to-Forecast Movement Bridges

Scope: Comparisons: Actual vs Budget vs Latest Forecast vs PY; forecast-to-forecast movement bridges

Why it matters

Senior management rarely asks "what is the forecast?" in isolation. They ask "how are we doing against Budget?", "how does this compare with last year?", and above all "what changed since the last forecast, and why?". The comparison grid and the forecast-to-forecast bridge are the two deliverables that answer those questions. They appear in every Monthly Business Review (Week 20), every Audit Committee pre-read and every analyst-call preparation pack. A bridge that does not reconcile, or that hides a ₹30 crore movement in "Others", destroys the credibility of the whole forecast process.

Core notes

The four comparison bases

Comparison Question answered Typical use Watch-outs
Actual vs Budget (AvB) Are we delivering what was committed? Monthly/YTD performance, incentive scorecards Budget may be stale by Q3; separate controllable vs external drivers
Actual vs Latest Forecast (AvF) Did the most recent view hold for the period just closed? Short-term accuracy, credibility of BUs Lag matters — compare with the forecast made before the month (Topic 5)
Latest Forecast vs Budget (LE vs AOP) Where will we land against target? Full-year attainment, Board, guidance Mixes actual and forecast months; show YTD and YTG separately
Actual/LE vs Prior Year (PY) Are we growing? External reporting narrative, analyst calls, SSSG PY must be restated for acquisitions, discontinued operations (Ind AS 105) and mapping changes

A standard layout shows, for each line: Actual (month and YTD), Budget, Latest Forecast, PY, and the variances in ₹ and %. With long-format storage (s02) each column is the same retrieval formula with a different version criterion — ACT, BUD, Q2F, and ACT with the month shifted back 12 months (EDATE(Hdr,-12) or EOMONTH(Hdr,-12)) for PY.

Variance sign convention

Define favourable/adverse rather than relying on raw signs: for revenue, Actual − Budget > 0 is favourable; for costs, Actual − Budget > 0 is adverse. Many teams compute variance as "impact on profit" so that positive is always favourable:

\[ \text{Var}_{\text{profit}} = s \times (\text{Actual} - \text{Comparator}), \quad s = +1 \text{ for income lines}, \ s = -1 \text{ for cost lines} \]

where \(s\) is a sign flag stored in the account master. Variance % should use the comparator as denominator and IFERROR or a guard for zero comparators.

Forecast-to-forecast (F2F) movement bridge

The F2F bridge explains how the full-year view moved from the prior forecast (e.g., Q1F 3+9) to the current forecast (Q2F 6+6). The standard decomposition (c03):

\[ \text{FY}_{\text{Q2F}} - \text{FY}_{\text{Q1F}} = \underbrace{(\text{A}_{\text{Jul-Sep}} - \text{F}^{\text{Q1F}}_{\text{Jul-Sep}})}_{\text{actualisation}} + \underbrace{\Delta \text{YTG from driver changes}}_{\text{e.g. volume, price, mix, cost rates}} + \underbrace{\Delta \text{R\&O}}_{\text{new risks/opportunities}} + \text{FX} + \text{one-offs/reclasses} \]

  1. Actualisation — for months that were forecast in the prior version but are now actual, the difference between actual and what was forecast. This is "what we already know happened".
  2. Driver changes — re-forecasting the remaining months with updated drivers: volume, price/realisation, mix, input cost rates, headcount, timing shifts. Each driver bar should be computed by flexing one driver at a time in a defined order (the order affects the split; document it).
  3. New risks and opportunities — discrete items not in the prior forecast (a new contract, a plant outage, a regulatory change such as a GST rate revision).
  4. FX and other normalisations — translation effects, remapping, scope changes.
  5. Residual / unexplained — must be small (a common tolerance is < 5% of the movement or below a fixed ₹ threshold) and explicitly labelled.

Worked illustration

Sahyadri Foods FY26 EBITDA (₹ crore): Q1F = 412.0; Q2F = 381.0; movement = −31.0.

Bridge bar ₹ crore Basis
Q1F full-year EBITDA 412.0 Locked Q1F snapshot
Actualisation Jul–Sep −14.0 Actual 96.0 vs Q1F forecast 110.0 for those months
H2 volume (festive demand revised) −22.0 6% lower H2 volume × contribution/tonne
Palm-oil price −11.7 9,000 t × (₹1,18,000 − ₹1,05,000)
New export order (70% weighted) +4.2 ₹6.0 crore × 0.70
FX (USD realisation) +2.6 Q2F rate ₹87.5 vs Q1F rate ₹86.0 on export revenue
Unexplained +9.9 Must be investigated before issue
Q2F full-year EBITDA 381.0

Here the residual of ₹9.9 crore (about 32% of the absolute movement) is far too large; on investigation it might be cost-saving actions in H2 (e.g., advertising deferral) that the BU entered without a comment. The bridge is not ready until that bar is named and quantified.

Presentation

  • A waterfall chart (Excel Insert → Waterfall, or Power BI) from prior forecast to current forecast, with actualisation shown first and the remaining bars ordered by size.
  • A short narrative of changes per bar: what, why, owner, whether it is permanent or timing.
  • The same bridge at revenue, gross margin, EBITDA and often free cash flow.
  • A companion LE vs Budget bridge for the Board, which explains the cumulative gap to AOP; the F2F bridge explains only this cycle's change.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Timing vs permanent. An order slipping from March to April moves FY revenue but not the 12-month rolling total. Label timing items separately; they matter for annual targets but not for underlying run-rate.
  • Order of decomposition. Volume-then-price gives a different split from price-then-volume when both change (the joint effect). State the order, or show the joint effect explicitly.
  • PY comparability. For PY comparisons after an acquisition, show reported and like-for-like (organic) figures. For discontinued operations under Ind AS 105, re-present PY.
  • Different FX and mappings. Normalise before comparing (topic pitfall): restate prior version to current mapping; show constant-currency variance and FX variance separately.
  • Comparing against an unlocked prior forecast. If the prior version was edited after lock, the bridge starts from a number the Board never saw. Always bridge from the locked snapshot.
  • Rolling horizon. For 12-month rolling forecasts the F2F bridge may be prepared on the overlapping months only (11 of 12), since the new version adds a month the old one did not contain.

Key takeaways

  • Four comparison bases answer different questions: AvB (delivery), AvF (forecast credibility), LE vs AOP (landing), vs PY (growth).
  • With long-format storage every comparison column is the same formula with a different version or shifted month.
  • Use a profit-impact sign convention so positive variance always means favourable.
  • F2F bridge order: actualisation → driver changes → new R&O → FX/normalisation → residual.
  • A large unexplained bar means the bridge is not finished; investigate before issuing.
  • Always bridge from the locked prior snapshot and separate timing from permanent changes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a comparison grid [L2]

Design a report for Revenue, COGS, Gross margin, Opex and EBITDA showing, for September 2025 and for April–September YTD: Actual, Budget, Q1 Forecast, PY Actual, and variances vs Budget and vs PY in ₹ and %. The fact table Fact holds Version (ACT, BUD, Q1F), Account, Month (month-end date) and Amount, and an account master holds a sign flag. Write the formulas for the PY column, the YTD Budget column and the favourable/adverse variance column.

💡 Hint: For YTD use SUMIFS with ">=" and "<=" date criteria built from the FY start and the selected month.

Q2. Revenue F2F bridge [L2]

Q1F FY26 revenue was ₹2,560 crore and Q2F is ₹2,498 crore. Jul–Sep actual revenue was ₹612 crore against a Q1F forecast of ₹640 crore for those months. For Oct–Mar, Q1F assumed 1,10,000 tonnes at ₹105/kg; Q2F assumes 1,06,000 tonnes at ₹106/kg. A new institutional contract adds ₹14 crore in Q4. Q1F used ₹86.0/USD and Q2F uses ₹87.5/USD on USD 40 million of H2 export revenue (included in the tonnes above). (a) Prepare the bridge (actualisation, volume, price, new contract, FX, residual), stating your decomposition order. (b) Comment on whether the FX effect is already embedded in the price bar.

Q3. Timing vs permanent changes [L2]

In the Q3F (9+3), a ₹45 crore project milestone billing moves from March 2026 to May 2026, and a ₹20 crore customer is lost permanently from January 2026. Explain how each affects (a) FY26 LE vs Budget, (b) the next-12-month rolling forecast total, and (c) the narrative to the Board.

Q4. Interview: the unexplained bar [L3]

You present a Q2F vs Q1F EBITDA bridge where "Other" is ₹19 crore of a ₹42 crore movement. The CFO asks why. Describe the investigative steps you would take using the long-format data, the change log and BU submissions, and how you would redesign the submission template so the problem does not recur.
