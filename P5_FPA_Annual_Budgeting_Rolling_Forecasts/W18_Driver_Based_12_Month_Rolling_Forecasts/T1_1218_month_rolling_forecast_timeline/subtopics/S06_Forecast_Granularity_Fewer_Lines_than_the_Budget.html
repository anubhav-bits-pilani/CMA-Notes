Forecast Granularity: Fewer Lines than the Budget

Scope: Granularity: forecast fewer lines (key drivers, material accounts) than the budget

Why it matters

The single biggest reason rolling forecasts fail in practice is that teams try to reforecast the AOP at AOP detail every month. The AOP may legitimately be built at cost-centre × GL level because it is a resource-allocation and accountability document. A forecast is an expectation used for decisions; its value comes from speed, honesty and attention to what moves the result. Choosing the right granularity — which lines are forecast explicitly from drivers, which are forecast by simple rules, and which are not forecast at all below a reporting line — determines whether a 4-working-day cycle is achievable.

Core notes

The principle

Forecast at the lowest level at which a decision is made or a driver is observable, and no lower. Typical outcome for a mid-sized Indian company:

Element AOP granularity Rolling forecast granularity
Revenue SKU × customer × month Category/channel × month from volume and price drivers
COGS BOM-level standard costs Gross margin % by category, adjusted for commodity indices
Employee cost Employee-level CTC Headcount × average CTC by function; increments as a step
Marketing Campaign-level Total by brand as % of revenue or fixed plan
Other opex 300 GL × 40 cost centres 10–15 reporting lines, run-rate with known step changes
Depreciation Asset-register based Run-rate plus capex additions × average rate
Finance cost Facility-level Average debt × average rate
Tax Computed Effective tax rate (e.g. 25.17% under Sec 115BAA) applied to PBT

Pareto selection

Rank accounts by their contribution to variance (not just to size) over the last 12–24 months. Usually 15–25 lines explain about 80% of absolute forecast variance. Those lines get driver-based forecasts with named owners; the long tail is forecast by rule:

  • Run-rate: average of last 3 actual months, adjusted for known one-offs.
  • Budget ratio: remaining AOP × YTD actual/YTD AOP ratio (acceptable for stable, immaterial lines only — this is exactly the "budget-plug" if applied to material lines).
  • % of revenue: for semi-variable lines like consumables or packaging.
  • Contractual schedule: rent, AMC fees, lease payments (Ind AS 116 depreciation and interest from the lease schedule).

Rolling up actuals to forecast lines

Actuals remain at GL detail in the ERP. The mapping table (s05) rolls them up to forecast lines so actual and forecast columns are like-for-like. If a report needs detail below the forecast line (e.g. by cost centre), allocate forecast lines down using YTD actual mix — and label the allocation clearly.

Granularity by time

Granularity applies to time too: months 1–6 monthly, months 7–18 quarterly (s01). Monthly phasing of outer quarters uses AOP seasonality weights.

Granularity and accuracy

Forecasting at higher aggregation is often more accurate, because independent errors partially offset (portfolio effect) and because aggregate drivers are more stable. The trade-off is diagnostic power: if total opex misses, you need actual detail to explain it. Hence: forecast aggregated, analyse actuals in detail.

Illustration

A cement company's AOP has 640 P&L lines. Analysis of FY25 forecast variances (₹ crore, absolute):

Group Lines Share of absolute variance
Volume by region (5) and realisation per tonne (5) 10 46%
Power and fuel (pet coke, coal price, kWh/tonne) 4 19%
Freight (rail/road mix, ₹ per tonne-km, lead distance) 4 14%
Raw material, employee cost, packing 5 8%
Remaining 617 lines 617 13%

The redesigned forecast models 23 lines from drivers and the rest as 8 rule-based reporting lines: 31 forecast lines in total, covering 87% of variance explicitly.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Statutory or covenant lines: some immaterial lines (e.g. CSR spend under Sec 135, at 2% of average net profit of the preceding three years) must be tracked separately because of compliance, even though they are small.
  • Lines that are small but volatile: FX gains/losses or ECL provisions can be small on average but swing sharply; give them their own line with a scenario rather than a run-rate.
  • Balance sheet and cash: for a cash forecast, working-capital days (DSO, DIO, DPO) by major segment replace account-level balance forecasts.
  • Political resistance: cost-centre owners may insist on line-level forecasts to protect budgets. Keep line-level tracking of actuals vs AOP in the MBR; it does not require line-level forecasting.
  • EPM templates: EPM tools make it easy to replicate AOP granularity; resist configuring forecast forms at the same level.
  • Revisit annually: materiality shifts (a new business line, a new commodity exposure) — refresh the Pareto analysis each AOP cycle.

Key takeaways

  • Forecast at the level where decisions are made or drivers are observed, not at AOP detail.
  • Use Pareto on historical variance to pick the 15–25 lines that deserve driver-based forecasts.
  • Forecast the tail by explicit rules: run-rate, % of revenue, contractual schedules, or (immaterial only) budget ratio.
  • Roll actuals up to forecast lines through the mapping table so comparisons are like-for-like.
  • Aggregated forecasts are faster and often more accurate; detailed actuals still serve diagnosis.
  • Keep compliance-sensitive and volatile small lines separate.

Practice Questions

Q1. Pareto analysis for line selection [L2]

From the following FY25 absolute forecast variances (₹ crore) — Revenue Domestic 42, Revenue Export 28, Raw material 31, Power 12, Freight 15, Employee 6, Marketing 9, Repairs 4, Rent 1, Travel 2, IT 2, Other 200 lines combined 18 — rank the lines, identify the set that explains at least 80% of variance, and propose a forecast method for each remaining line.

Q2. Rule choice for tail lines [L2]

For each line, choose and justify a forecast rule: (a) office rent under a 9-year lease with 15% escalation every 3 years, (b) printing and stationery, (c) packing material at 3.2% of revenue, (d) bank charges, (e) CSR expenditure, (f) FX gain/loss on USD payables.

Q3. Granularity debate in an interview [L3]

A Head of Sales insists the rolling forecast must be at SKU × distributor level (4,500 combinations) because "that's how we manage the business". Construct the argument for a coarser forecast, including the accuracy (portfolio) effect, cycle-time impact, and how SKU-level insight will still be available.

💡 Hint: Separate what demand planning needs for supply decisions from what FP&A needs for financial decisions.

Q4. Allocating forecast lines down [L2]

The forecast has "Other opex" of ₹12 crore per month for November–March. The MBR needs it by 6 cost centres. YTD actual (Apr–Oct) other opex by cost centre is: A 18, B 14, C 22, D 9, E 11, F 6 (₹ crore). Describe the allocation, write the Excel formula for one cost centre-month, and state the caveat you would print on the report.
