Key concept

First Forecast Month and SEQUENCE-Driven Month-End Headers

Concept: First forecast month = =EOMONTH(Cutoff,0)+1; month-end headers =EOMONTH(Cutoff,SEQUENCE(1,12)) for next 12 months

Statement

Given a named cell Cutoff holding the last closed month (any date within it, conventionally its month-end):

FirstFcst  =EOMONTH(Cutoff,0)+1
Headers    =EOMONTH(Cutoff,SEQUENCE(1,12))

Formally, for header column \( k \):

\[ H_k = \text{EOMONTH}(C, k), \quad k = 1, 2, \ldots, N \]

where \( C \) is the cutoff date, \( N \) is the horizon length (12 here), and \( H_k \) is the last calendar day of the \( k \)-th month after the cutoff month. The first forecast month starts on \( \text{EOMONTH}(C,0) + 1 \), the day after the cutoff month-end.

Explanation

EOMONTH(date, m) returns the month-end that is \( m \) months from date. With \( m = 0 \) it normalises any date in the cutoff month to its month-end (so a user typing 15-Sep-2025 still gets 30-Sep-2025); adding 1 gives the first day of the next month, which is the first forecast date. SEQUENCE(1,12) creates a horizontal array {1,2,…,12}; passing it as the months argument makes EOMONTH return 12 month-ends in one spilled row.

Why month-ends rather than first-of-month? Because the actuals table (and most ERP extracts converted in Power Query) is keyed on month-end, and EOMONTH gives month-ends natively. FirstFcst (a first-of-month date) is used for labels and for comparisons such as "is this transaction date in a forecast month?" — compare month-end headers to month-end keys, and dates to dates.

Conditions and practicalities:

  • Requires Excel 365/2021 for SEQUENCE and spilling; for older versions use the c02 pattern.
  • Apply number format mmm-yy; the underlying value remains a date serial.
  • The horizon can be parameterised: SEQUENCE(1,HorizonMonths).
  • A vertical timeline (months in rows, common for database-style outputs) uses SEQUENCE(12) instead.
  • Trailing headers for TTM: EOMONTH(Cutoff,SEQUENCE(1,12,-11)) gives the 12 months ending at the cutoff.

Illustration

Cutoff entered as 30-Sep-2025:

Item Formula result
EOMONTH(Cutoff,0) 30-Sep-2025
FirstFcst 01-Oct-2025
Header 1 (k = 1) 31-Oct-2025
Header 5 (k = 5) 28-Feb-2026
Header 12 (k = 12) 30-Sep-2026

If a user enters 17-Sep-2025, results are identical. Move the cutoff to 31-Oct-2025: headers become 30-Nov-2025 … 31-Oct-2026 and FirstFcst 01-Nov-2025, with no other edit. Combined with LET for readability:

=LET(c,EOMONTH(Cutoff,0), n,HorizonMonths, EOMONTH(c,SEQUENCE(1,n)))

Connections

This is the engine of s03 (dynamic headers) and feeds the flag in s04/c03 and the SUMIFS keys in s05. c02 gives the non-dynamic-array equivalent, c04 explains why the window length stays constant. It builds on Week 9 dynamic arrays. In Topic 2, the FY-grid version EOMONTH(FYStart,SEQUENCE(1,12,0)) produces April–March headers for the LE. In Phase 4 three-statement models the same pattern generates monthly or annual timelines from a model start date.

Practice Questions

Q1. Header values at a year-end cutoff [L1]

With Cutoff = 31-Mar-2026, state FirstFcst, the first, sixth and twelfth headers from EOMONTH(Cutoff,SEQUENCE(1,12)), and the fiscal year each falls in.

Q2. Vertical and trailing variants [L2]

Write formulas that (a) spill 18 forecast month-ends vertically down column A from Cutoff, (b) spill the 12 trailing month-ends ending at the cutoff across row 3, and (c) combine both into one row of 30 headers (12 trailing then 18 forward) using HSTACK.

💡 Hint: SEQUENCE accepts a start value; negative starts give trailing months.

Q3. Mismatch diagnosis [L2]

A colleague builds headers with =DATE(YEAR(Cutoff),MONTH(Cutoff)+SEQUENCE(1,12),1) and the actuals table is keyed on month-end dates. Explain what SUMIFS will return for each header and two ways to fix it.
