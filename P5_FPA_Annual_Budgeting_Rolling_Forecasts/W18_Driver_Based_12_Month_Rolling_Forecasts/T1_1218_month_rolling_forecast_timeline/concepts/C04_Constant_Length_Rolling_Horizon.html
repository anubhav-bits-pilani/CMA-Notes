Key concept

Constant-Length Rolling Horizon

Concept: Rolling forecast horizon stays constant length: each close drops one actual month and adds one forecast month

Statement

At every close the window shifts by one month while its length \( N \) is unchanged:

\[ W(C) = \{\, \text{EOMONTH}(C, k) : k = 1, \ldots, N \,\}, \qquad W(C') = W(C) \setminus \{H_1\} \cup \{\text{EOMONTH}(C, N+1)\} \]

where \( C \) is the current cutoff, \( C' = \text{EOMONTH}(C,1) \) the next cutoff after one more month closes, \( H_1 = \text{EOMONTH}(C,1) \) the month that has just become actual (and leaves the forward window), and \( \text{EOMONTH}(C, N+1) \) the new month appended at the far end. The number of forecast months is always \( N \).

Contrast with a static fiscal-year forecast, where the forecast months remaining equal \( 12 - m \) and shrink to zero, with \( m \) the number of closed months in the FY.

Explanation

The constant horizon is the defining property of a rolling forecast and the reason it exists: forward visibility never decays. In April and in February the organisation sees the same distance ahead. This changes behaviour — managers must think about the month 12 ahead every month, which forces a perpetual view of capacity, hiring and cash rather than an annual scramble during AOP season.

Mechanically, the month that "drops" is not deleted; it moves from the forward window into actuals. Two views coexist:

  1. Forward window (rolling 12/18): always \( N \) forecast months; the just-closed month leaves it.
  2. FY grid (LE view): fixed April–March columns; the just-closed month flips its flag from forecast to actual (Topic 2).

The newly added far month needs a forecast. Practical rules: seed it from the AOP-style driver logic (prior-year same month × growth), from the 18-month view if one exists, or from statistical baselines — not left blank, otherwise the NTM total falls by one month's value.

Before overwriting, the forecast for the dropped month must already be preserved in a snapshot, because that is what accuracy measurement (lag-n) compares with the actual.

Exceptions and nuances: hybrid windows (months then quarters) roll monthly in the near section and add a quarter only when a quarter boundary passes; 5-quarter windows add one quarter each quarter-end, not each month.

Illustration

12-month window, revenue (₹ crore):

Cutoff Window Months dropped / added NTM total
30-Sep-2025 Oct-25 to Sep-26 — 1,284
31-Oct-2025 Nov-25 to Oct-26 Drop Oct-25 (forecast was 128), add Oct-26 (new forecast 140) 1,284 − 128 + 140 + Δ revisions

If November–September forecasts are revised down by ₹10 crore in aggregate in the October cycle, the new NTM = 1,284 − 128 + 140 − 10 = 1,286. The October-25 forecast of 128 is compared with its actual (say 121) in the accuracy tracker using the snapshot saved at the September cycle.

Practice Questions

Q1. Window evolution [L1]

For an 18-month rolling window, list the window at cutoffs 31-Jan-2026, 28-Feb-2026 and 31-Mar-2026 and identify the month dropped and added at each step.

Q2. NTM movement reconciliation [L2]

NTM EBITDA at cutoff 31-Dec-2025 was ₹412 crore. In the January cycle: January's forecast EBITDA was ₹33 crore, the newly added January 2027 month is forecast at ₹37 crore, and revisions to February 2026–December 2026 total −₹14 crore. Compute the new NTM EBITDA and lay out the reconciliation in a table.

Q3. Seeding the new far month [L2]

Propose three methods for forecasting the newly added month 12 ahead (e.g. September 2026 at cutoff 30-Sep-2025) for a seasonal apparel retailer, state the data each needs, and recommend one with reasons.

💡 Hint: The prior-year same month already exists in actuals; think about growth and store-count adjustments.
