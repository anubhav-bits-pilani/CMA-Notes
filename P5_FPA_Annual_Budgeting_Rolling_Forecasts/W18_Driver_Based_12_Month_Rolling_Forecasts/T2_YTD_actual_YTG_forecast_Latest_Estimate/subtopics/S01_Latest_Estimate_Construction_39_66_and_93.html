Subtopic note

Latest Estimate Construction: 3+9, 6+6 and 9+3 Structures

Scope: Latest Estimate (LE) construction: 3+9, 6+6, 9+3 structures in Indian FY context

Why it matters

The LE is the most-quoted internal number in a company after the AOP itself. It feeds the Audit Committee and Board pack each quarter, management commentary on earnings calls, bonus and tax provisioning, covenant-headroom projections for lenders, and next-year AOP baselines. A badly constructed LE — wrong cutoff, budget plugs, mismatched account mapping between actual and forecast columns — produces a confident-looking but wrong full-year number. Constructing it correctly is a core FP&A deliverable and a standard assessment task (this week's Saturday test is a 6+6 conversion).

Core notes

Definition and naming

The LE for a fiscal year is the sum of actuals for closed months and forecast for open months:

\[ \text{LE}_{FY} = \sum_{t \in \text{closed}} A_t + \sum_{t \in \text{open}} F_t \]

where \( A_t \) is the actual for month \( t \) and \( F_t \) the current forecast. The "m + n" label gives months of actual (m) and forecast (n), with m + n = 12.

Structure Cutoff (Indian FY26) Actual months Forecast months Typical use
0+12 31-Mar-2025 none Apr-25 to Mar-26 The AOP itself (or a pre-year forecast)
3+9 30-Jun-2025 Apr–Jun (Q1) Jul–Mar Q1 Forecast; first reality check on AOP
6+6 30-Sep-2025 Apr–Sep (H1) Oct–Mar Q2 Forecast; H1 results; festive-season view
9+3 31-Dec-2025 Apr–Dec (9M) Jan–Mar Q3 Forecast; year-end landing, tax and bonus provisioning
11+1 28-Feb-2026 Apr–Feb Mar Final landing; year-end close planning

Monthly LEs (4+8, 5+7 …) are produced in companies with monthly cadence; the quarterly ones are formal versions submitted to the Board.

Indian FY specifics

  • Fiscal calendar: April–March under Sec 2(41) of the Companies Act, 2013 (financial year ending 31 March, with limited exceptions for subsidiaries of foreign companies with Tribunal approval). Indian subsidiaries of MNCs with a December group year-end often run two LEs: an Indian FY LE for statutory/tax purposes and a calendar-year LE for group reporting — the same database with two FY definitions.
  • Seasonality falls in the forecast half at 6+6: Navratri, Dussehra and Diwali (October–November), wedding season (November–February), and fiscal year-end bunching in March (B2B, government, capex suppliers). A 6+6 LE has the most seasonally important months still in forecast — the YTG must be driver-based, not extrapolated.
  • Statutory interactions: the 9+3 LE drives the advance-tax instalment due 15 March (cumulative 100% of tax liability; earlier instalments 15%, 45%, 75% by 15 June, 15 September and 15 December under Sec 211), year-end bonus and incentive accruals, and the CSR obligation (2% of average net profit of three preceding years).

Construction steps

  1. Set the cutoff (Topic 1) to the last hard-closed month; confirm the actuals load is complete and tied to the trial balance.
  2. FY grid April–March with flag row =--(Hdr<=Cutoff).
  3. Actual columns via SUMIFS on the actuals table, mapped to forecast lines.
  4. Forecast columns from drivers (Topic 3), with the YTG reflecting current assumptions, not AOP.
  5. Totals: YTD = SUMPRODUCT(Row,Flag), YTG = SUMPRODUCT(Row,1-Flag), LE = YTD + YTG.
  6. Comparatives: AOP, PY actual, Prior LE (from the version table).
  7. Variances: LE − AOP, LE − PY, LE − Prior LE; attainment % (s02).
  8. R&O overlay (s05) and risk-adjusted LE.
  9. Checks: actuals tie to TB; LE totals by BU sum to consolidated; no YTG cell equals AOP on material lines without justification; P&L sub-totals arithmetically consistent.
  10. Snapshot the LE as a version with timestamp and label (e.g. "FY26 Q2 Fcst 6+6").

Layout

Account Apr … Sep (Act) Oct … Mar (Fcst) YTD Act YTG Fcst LE AOP Gap Att % PY Prior LE

Show a "YTD AOP" column too, so YTD variance (actual vs plan to date) can be separated from YTG variance (forecast vs remaining plan).

Illustration

FY26 revenue (₹ crore). AOP ₹1,000. Cutoff 30-Sep-2025.

YTD (Apr–Sep) YTG (Oct–Mar) Full year
AOP 440 560 1,000
Actual / Forecast 418 548 LE 966
Variance −22 −12 −34

The LE gap of ₹34 crore splits into ₹22 crore already lost (YTD) and ₹12 crore expected YTG shortfall. Attainment = 966 ÷ 1,000 = 96.6%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Balance sheet and cash LE: P&L lines are additive, but balance sheet LE is the forecast closing balance at 31 March, not YTD + YTG; cash flow LE is YTD cash flows + YTG cash flows, and must reconcile to the balance sheet LE.
  • Non-additive KPIs: margins, ratios, ATV and headcount averages must be recomputed from LE components, never summed or averaged across months.
  • Restructuring or M&A mid-year: an acquisition closed in August means YTD includes only post-acquisition months; the LE must be comparable to an AOP that may not include it — show organic and inorganic separately.
  • Ind AS adjustments booked only at quarter-end (e.g. ECL, inventory write-downs, Ind AS 116 true-ups) make monthly actuals lumpy; the YTG forecast must include them at their expected timing.
  • Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105): present consistently between LE, AOP and PY.

Key takeaways

  • LE = closed-month actuals + open-month forecast; "m+n" labels the split of the April–March year.
  • 3+9, 6+6 and 9+3 align with Q1, H1 and 9M results and the quarterly Board cycle.
  • At 6+6, the festive peak and March bunching are still in forecast — YTG must be driver-based.
  • Build on the cutoff-flag engine, with AOP, PY and Prior LE comparatives and checks.
  • Split the LE gap into YTD variance and YTG variance.
  • Recompute ratios from components; treat balance sheet LE as closing balances.

Practice Questions

Q1. Structure mapping [L1]

State the LE label, closed months and forecast months at cutoffs 31-Jul-2025, 31-Dec-2025 and 28-Feb-2026 for an April–March FY, and identify which of these is used for the final advance-tax instalment computation.

Q2. Building a 9+3 LE [L2]

AOP FY26 EBITDA by quarter (₹ crore): Q1 40, Q2 45, Q3 60, Q4 55. Actual EBITDA: Q1 37, Q2 46, Q3 52. Forecast Q4 by month: Jan 17, Feb 17, Mar 21. Construct the 9+3 LE, split the gap into YTD and YTG variance, and compute attainment.

Q3. Dual FY LE for an MNC subsidiary [L3]

An Indian subsidiary of a German group reports to the parent on a January–December year and files statutory accounts April–March. Design a single database and LE engine that produces both a calendar-year 2025 LE and an FY26 (April–March) LE at the 30-Sep-2025 cutoff. Describe the date-table columns, the flag logic and how the two LEs share months.

💡 Hint: The same closed months and the same forecast months serve both; only the year-membership column differs.

Q4. Margin LE error [L2]

A reviewer finds the LE EBITDA margin computed as the average of 12 monthly margins. Monthly revenue (₹ crore) Apr–Mar: 60, 62, 58, 65, 70, 80, 120, 115, 85, 80, 78, 90 and monthly EBITDA margins 10%, 10%, 9%, 11%, 12%, 13%, 18%, 17%, 13%, 12%, 12%, 14%. Compute the reported (average) margin and the correct LE margin, and explain the direction of the difference.
