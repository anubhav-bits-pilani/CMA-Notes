Subtopic note

Communicating the LE to the Board: Guidance and Incentive Accruals

Scope: Communicating LE to the Board and implications for guidance and incentive accruals

Why it matters

The LE becomes consequential the moment it leaves FP&A. At the Board and Audit Committee it shapes decisions on capital allocation, dividend recommendations and whether to revise market guidance. In the books it drives the estimate of performance-bonus liabilities, sales commissions and other attainment-linked accruals under Ind AS 19, which the statutory auditor tests. For listed companies, an LE that diverges from public guidance creates disclosure obligations under SEBI LODR and insider-trading regulations. An FP&A professional must therefore be able to present the LE clearly, defend its assumptions and understand its accounting and regulatory knock-on effects.

Core notes

Board communication: structure

A Board-level LE presentation (one or two slides, with backup) typically follows:

  1. Headline: "FY26 6+6 LE: revenue ₹4,120 crore (97% of AOP, +11% vs PY); EBITDA ₹612 crore (94% of AOP); risk-adjusted EBITDA ₹598–630 crore range."
  2. Bridge AOP → LE (and Prior LE → LE): volume, price/mix, input costs, FX, opex, one-offs.
  3. YTD vs YTG split: how much of the gap is already lost vs addressable.
  4. Credibility evidence: required run-rate vs seasonal baseline; key driver assumptions (festive demand, commodity prices, FX).
  5. R&O: top five risks and opportunities with probabilities, owners and trigger dates.
  6. Gap-closure actions and their status.
  7. Implications: guidance, covenants, dividend, bonus pool, tax.

Principles: lead with the conclusion; show a range, not just a point; be consistent with what management has said publicly and with the bonus accrual; disclose changes from the last LE presented to the Board and why.

Implications for market guidance

Indian listed companies are not required to give guidance, but many do (directly or via management commentary on calls). The LE is the internal test of whether guidance remains supportable.

  • Within range: reaffirm, possibly narrowing the range as the year progresses.
  • Outside range, material: decide whether to revise. A revision, or information that materially changes expectations, is generally treated as material information disclosable to the stock exchanges under SEBI LODR Reg 30 (with the materiality policy and the quantitative thresholds of Reg 30(4)), and must be disseminated to all simultaneously — no selective briefing of analysts (Reg 46 website disclosure and call transcripts also apply).
  • UPSI: the LE, R&O register and Board pack are Unpublished Price Sensitive Information under the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 — maintain a structured digital database of persons with access, respect trading-window closures, and limit circulation.
  • Credibility: consistent, conservative guidance that is updated promptly builds credibility; repeatedly missing guidance damages valuation more than a lower initial guide.

Implications for incentive accruals (Ind AS 19)

Performance bonuses, sales incentives and variable pay paid within 12 months after the year-end are short-term employee benefits (Ind AS 19.9). Recognition requirements (Ind AS 19.19): the entity recognises the expected cost of profit-sharing and bonus payments when it has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of past events, and a reliable estimate can be made. A constructive obligation arises from past practice where the entity has no realistic alternative but to pay. A reliable estimate is possible when the plan has a formula, the amounts are determined before the financial statements are approved, or past practice gives clear evidence (Ind AS 19.22).

The accrual is measured on an undiscounted basis over the service period. In interim periods (Ind AS 34 quarterly results), the year-to-date accrual is based on the expected full-year attainment — i.e. the LE — applied to the payout curve:

\[ \text{Cum}_m = \text{Pool} \times f(\text{Att}_{LE}) \times \frac{m}{12}, \qquad \text{Charge}_m = \text{Cum}_m - \text{Cum}_{m'} \]

where \( \text{Cum}_m \) is the cumulative accrual after \( m \) closed months (assuming services are rendered evenly through the year), \( \text{Pool} \) the bonus pool at 100% payout, \( f(\cdot) \) the scheme's payout function of attainment, \( \text{Att}_{LE} \) the expected full-year attainment per the LE, and \( m' \) the month of the previous accrual. The period charge is the change in cumulative accrual — negative (a reversal) if the LE deteriorates.

Illustration. Pool at 100% attainment ₹10 crore; payout 0% below 90%, linear to 100% at 100%, 150% cap at 115%. At 6+6, LE EBITDA attainment 96% → payout \( (96 - 90) \div 10 = 60\% \) → expected bonus ₹6 crore → cumulative accrual for 6 months ₹3 crore. Suppose ₹2.5 crore was accrued by Q1 based on the 3+9 LE (100% attainment: ₹10 crore × 3/12). Q2 charge = 3.0 − 2.5 = ₹0.5 crore.

Practical points:

  • Statutory bonus under the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 (minimum 8.33% of wages for eligible employees) is separate from performance bonus and accrued irrespective of LE.
  • Base vs risk-adjusted LE: use the best estimate of attainment; if the risk-adjusted LE is management's best estimate, use it — and ensure the Board pack and the accrual use the same number.
  • Discretionary pools: if the Board can and does pay regardless of attainment, a constructive obligation may exist independent of the formula.
  • Audit: auditors will compare the attainment assumption with the LE in Board minutes and test reversals at year-end.
  • Tax: bonus is deductible under Sec 43B of the Income-tax Act only on actual payment (on or before the return due date for the year), creating a deferred tax asset if unpaid at year-end.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • LE optimism and accruals: an over-optimistic LE inflates the bonus accrual and understates profit; a sandbagged LE does the reverse — both distort interim results.
  • Multiple metrics: schemes with revenue, EBITDA and individual KPIs require a weighted attainment; model each separately.
  • Guidance vs internal LE: guidance is often set more conservatively than the LE; document the relationship so the Board understands the cushion.
  • Disclosure timing: a Board meeting that approves results and discusses a revised outlook should be followed by prompt exchange disclosure if guidance changes.
  • Sales commissions with clawbacks: under Ind AS 115, incremental costs of obtaining a contract may be capitalised — distinct from bonus accruals.

Key takeaways

  • Present the LE with a headline, bridges, YTD/YTG split, credibility evidence, R&O range and implications.
  • The LE is the internal test of public guidance; material revisions trigger SEBI LODR Reg 30 disclosure and UPSI controls.
  • Ind AS 19 requires bonus accruals when there is a legal or constructive obligation and a reliable estimate — measured from expected attainment per the LE.
  • Accrue cumulatively over the service period; the period charge is the change in cumulative accrual, including reversals.
  • Keep Board LE, guidance view and accrual assumptions consistent and documented.
  • Remember Sec 43B timing for tax deductibility of bonus.

Practice Questions

Q1. Bonus accrual journal sequence [L2]

Bonus pool ₹8 crore at 100% EBITDA attainment; 0% below 92%, linear to 100%, capped at 110% payout at 105% attainment. LE attainment: 3+9 = 101%, 6+6 = 97%, 9+3 = 93%, final = 91.5%. Compute the cumulative accrual at each quarter-end and the quarterly charge or reversal, and pass the journal entries.

💡 Hint: Compute the expected full-year bonus at each LE first, then the cumulative proportion.

Q2. Board slide draft [L2]

Draft the headline and bullet structure of a one-slide Board LE update from these facts (₹ crore): AOP revenue 3,000, LE 2,880, PY 2,610; AOP EBITDA 450, LE 405; Prior LE EBITDA 418; top risk: monsoon-related rural slowdown ₹20 crore EBITDA at 40%; top opportunity: price increase ₹12 crore at 70%.

Q3. Guidance revision decision [L3]

A listed company guided "EBITDA margin of 19–21%" for FY26. The 6+6 LE shows 17.8% with a risk-adjusted range of 17.2%–18.4%. The CEO prefers to wait for the Q3 results before saying anything. Advise the CFO on the regulatory, credibility and practical considerations, and recommend a course of action.

Q4. Accrual vs LE inconsistency [L3]

During the statutory audit of Q3 results, the auditor notes that the bonus accrual assumes 100% attainment while the Board pack 9+3 LE shows 94% attainment (payout 40%). Pool ₹15 crore. Quantify the misstatement in the 9-month accrual, discuss whether it is material for a company with 9M PBT of ₹180 crore, and identify the process control that failed.
