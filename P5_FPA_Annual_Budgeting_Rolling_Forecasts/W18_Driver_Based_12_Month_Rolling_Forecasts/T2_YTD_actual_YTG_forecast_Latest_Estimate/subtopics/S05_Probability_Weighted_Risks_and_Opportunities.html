Probability-Weighted Risks and Opportunities Overlay on the Base LE

Scope: Risks and opportunities (R&O) overlay on base LE, probability-weighted

Why it matters

A single-point LE hides the uncertainty that the CFO and Board most need to see. The base LE reflects the forecaster's central view on known drivers; but there are always discrete events that may or may not happen — a large tender, a customer insolvency, a price increase awaiting approval, a regulatory decision, a plant outage. Rather than burying them in the base (which encourages padding) or ignoring them (which produces surprises), FP&A teams maintain a separate R&O register and overlay it on the base LE with probabilities. The result is a range (base, risk-adjusted, downside, upside) that supports guidance decisions, bonus accruals and gap-closure planning.

Core notes

Base LE vs R&O items

  • Base LE: the forecast assuming known, more-likely-than-not outcomes on continuing drivers. Includes events judged highly probable (e.g. contracted price increases).
  • Risk: a discrete, identifiable event that would reduce the LE if it occurs, not included in the base.
  • Opportunity: a discrete event that would increase the LE if it occurs, not included in the base.

The boundary rule must be explicit and consistent. A common convention: include in base if probability ≥ 70–80%; list as R&O if between roughly 10% and 70–80%; ignore if remote (but monitor). Items must never be double-counted — in the base and in the register.

Risk-adjusted LE

\[ \text{LE}_{RA} = \text{LE}_{base} + \sum_{j} p_j O_j - \sum_{i} q_i R_i \]

where \( O_j \) is the P&L impact of opportunity \( j \) with probability \( p_j \), and \( R_i \) the impact of risk \( i \) with probability \( q_i \). Impacts must be measured at the same P&L level as the LE line — for EBITDA, a revenue item's impact is revenue × contribution margin, not the gross revenue.

Also report:

  • Downside case: base minus all risks above a threshold probability (or all risks), with no opportunities.
  • Upside case: base plus all opportunities above a threshold.

The R&O register

Field Purpose
ID, description Unambiguous event definition
Type (R/O), category Market, customer, operational, regulatory, FX/commodity
BU / owner Accountable manager
P&L line and impact (₹) Revenue and EBITDA impacts separately
Timing (month) Affects which month and whether it falls in this FY
Probability % With rationale; updated each cycle
Expected value Impact × probability
Trigger / status What would confirm or eliminate it; date of decision
Mitigation / action For risks; capture plan for opportunities
Date added / last updated Ageing and audit trail

Store it as an Excel Table so expected values and totals update automatically:

Sign        =IF([@Type]="R",-1,1)
EV_EBITDA   =[@Impact_EBITDA]*[@Prob]*[@Sign]
InFY        =--([@Month]<=FYEnd)
RA_LE       =LE_Base+SUMPRODUCT(RO[Impact_EBITDA],RO[Prob],RO[Sign],RO[InFY])

Probabilities: calibration and discipline

Probabilities are subjective. Improve them by:

  • Using defined bands (10%, 25%, 50%, 75%) rather than false precision (37%).
  • Requiring evidence for each band (e.g. 75% = "customer PO received, awaiting credit approval").
  • Back-testing: of items tagged 50% last year, did about half materialise? Persistent over-estimation of opportunities is optimism bias; persistent omission of risks is a governance issue.

Life cycle of an item

Items move each cycle: probability updated; crystallised items move into the base (or into actuals); eliminated items are removed. The LE bridge (Topic 4) must show "R&O moved into base" separately so the base does not appear to improve simply because an opportunity crystallised.

Illustration

Base 6+6 LE EBITDA ₹420 crore (AOP ₹450 crore). Register (EBITDA impact, ₹ crore):

Item Type Impact Prob. EV
Key distributor insolvency R 12 25% −3.0
Steel price spike Q4 R 18 50% −9.0
Price increase approval (Jan) O 15 60% +9.0
Government tender (Mar) O 20 30% +6.0
Net +3.0

Risk-adjusted LE = 420 + 15 − 12 = ₹423 crore. Downside (all risks, no opportunities) = 420 − 30 = 390; upside = 420 + 35 = 455. Attainment: base 93.3%, risk-adjusted 94.0%, range 86.7%–101.1%.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Correlated items: a demand shock may trigger both the distributor insolvency and lower volumes; simple summation of expected values assumes independence. For correlated items, define a combined scenario instead.
  • Binary vs expected value: a 30% chance of a ₹20 crore tender will never produce ₹6 crore; the expected value is a portfolio concept. With few large items, show the range, not just the EV.
  • Timing risk: an order may be certain but its timing (March vs April) uncertain — the risk to this FY is the timing slip; record it as a timing risk with the FY effect.
  • Accounting overlap: some risks are already accounting matters (e.g. a probable loss requiring a provision under Ind AS 37 or ECL under Ind AS 109). Once recognised in the books, they belong in actuals/base, not the register.
  • Padding the base: forecasters who include "contingency" in base costs and also list risks double-count. Ban unallocated contingencies in the base.
  • Disclosure: R&O registers for listed companies are UPSI; restrict distribution.

Key takeaways

  • Keep the base LE for likely outcomes on continuing drivers; list discrete uncertain events in an R&O register.
  • Risk-adjusted LE = base + Σ(probability × opportunity) − Σ(probability × risk), at the right P&L level.
  • Report base, risk-adjusted, downside and upside to show the range.
  • Use probability bands with evidence criteria and back-test calibration.
  • Move crystallised items into base and show that movement in the LE bridge.
  • Watch for correlation, timing-only risks, double-counting and items already provided for under Ind AS 37/109.

Practice Questions

Q1. Risk-adjusted LE computation [L1]

Base LE revenue ₹1,860 crore. Risks: ₹40 crore at 25%, ₹25 crore at 50%. Opportunities: ₹30 crore at 40%, ₹60 crore at 10%. Compute the risk-adjusted LE, downside and upside revenue.

Q2. EBITDA-level R&O with timing [L2]

Convert the following revenue R&O items to EBITDA impacts using a 35% contribution margin and include only items expected to fall in FY26 (April 2025–March 2026): (a) export order ₹45 crore, March 2026, 60%; (b) contract renewal at 4% lower price, annual revenue ₹120 crore, effective January 2026, 50%; (c) new channel launch ₹30 crore, May 2026, 80%; (d) customer default, receivable ₹8 crore (pure write-off, no margin effect), 30%. Compute the risk-adjusted EBITDA impact for FY26.

💡 Hint: For (b), only the months of the new price falling inside the FY matter.

Q3. Register design and governance [L2]

Design the Excel Table structure for an R&O register used by five BUs, the formulas for expected value and in-FY filtering, and three governance rules (boundary with base, update frequency, ageing) with how each is enforced in the workbook.

Q4. Calibration review [L3]

Last year's register had 40 items tagged 50%; 11 materialised. Of 25 opportunities tagged 75%, 9 materialised. Of risks, 6 of 20 tagged 25% materialised. Interpret the calibration by category, explain the likely behavioural causes, and recommend changes to the R&O process.
