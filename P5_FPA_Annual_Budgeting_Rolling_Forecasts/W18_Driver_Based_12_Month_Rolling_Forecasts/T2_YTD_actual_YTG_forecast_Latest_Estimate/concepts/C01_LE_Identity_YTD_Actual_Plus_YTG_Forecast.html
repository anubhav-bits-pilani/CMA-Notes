Key concept

LE Identity: YTD Actual Plus YTG Forecast

Concept: LE (Full year) = YTD Actual + YTG Forecast

Statement

\[ \text{LE}_{FY} = \text{YTD Actual} + \text{YTG Forecast} = \sum_{t=1}^{m} A_t + \sum_{t=m+1}^{12} F_t \]

where \( t \) indexes fiscal months (April = 1, March = 12), \( m \) is the number of closed months at the cutoff, \( A_t \) the actual for month \( t \), and \( F_t \) the current forecast for open month \( t \). In flag notation, with \( f_t = 1 \) for closed months and 0 otherwise, \( \text{LE} = \sum_t \left[ f_t A_t + (1-f_t) F_t \right] \).

Explanation

The identity is simple, but each term carries conditions:

  • YTD Actual must be hard-closed, tied to the trial balance and mapped to the same account structure as the forecast. Post-close adjustments to earlier months change YTD and must be visible in the LE bridge.
  • YTG Forecast must be a fresh view of the remaining months based on current drivers — not remaining AOP (the budget-plug pitfall) and not a straight-line extrapolation in a seasonal business.
  • Additivity: the identity holds for flow items (revenue, costs, cash flows). For stock items (balances, headcount at period-end) the LE is the forecast closing value at 31 March; for averages and ratios it must be recomputed from LE components (e.g. LE EBITDA margin = LE EBITDA ÷ LE revenue).
  • Consistency of basis: actuals and forecasts must use the same accounting policies, FX basis, intercompany eliminations and segment definitions. An actual booked under Ind AS 115 principal-vs-agent gross presentation and a forecast prepared net produce an LE that adds unlike items.

The identity also defines the two sources of LE change between cycles: (1) actualisation — a month moves from \( F_t \) to \( A_t \), and the difference \( A_t - F_t \) changes the LE; (2) revision — remaining \( F_t \) values change. Every LE bridge is a decomposition of these two effects (Topic 4, c03).

In Excel, with the flag row in C7:N7 and values in row 10: YTD =SUMPRODUCT(C10:N10,C7:N7), YTG =SUMPRODUCT(C10:N10,1-C7:N7), LE =YTD+YTG — with a check that LE equals SUM(C10:N10).

Illustration

FY26 gross profit (₹ crore), cutoff 31-Dec-2025 (9+3):

Apr–Dec (Actual) Jan Feb Mar LE
Gross profit 612 70 68 82 832

YTD = 612, YTG = 220, LE = 832. In January, actual gross profit is 64 (vs forecast 70) and February–March forecasts are revised to 66 and 85. New LE = 612 + 64 + 66 + 85 = 827. Change −5 = actualisation (64 − 70 = −6) + revisions (−2 + 3 = +1).

Practice Questions

Q1. LE from monthly data [L1]

Cutoff 30-Sep-2025. FY26 revenue actuals Apr–Sep (₹ crore): 44, 46, 45, 48, 51, 56. Forecast Oct–Mar: 72, 68, 55, 52, 50, 58. Compute YTD, YTG and LE.

Q2. Decomposing an LE change [L2]

Using Q1's data, October actual is ₹69 crore and November–March forecasts are revised to 70, 56, 53, 50, 60. Compute the new LE and decompose the change into actualisation and revisions.

Q3. Non-additive lines [L2]

At 6+6, average headcount YTD is 1,180 and forecast average for October–March is 1,260; closing headcount at 30 September is 1,210 and forecast closing at 31 March is 1,300; YTD revenue ₹640 crore and YTG ₹820 crore. Compute the LE revenue per employee correctly and explain why adding or averaging the closing headcounts would be wrong.

💡 Hint: Revenue per employee needs an average headcount over the full year.
