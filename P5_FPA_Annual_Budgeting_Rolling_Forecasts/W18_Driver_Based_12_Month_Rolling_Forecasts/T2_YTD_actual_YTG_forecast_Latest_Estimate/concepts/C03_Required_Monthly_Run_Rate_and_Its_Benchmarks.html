Key concept

Required Monthly Run-Rate and Its Benchmarks

Concept: Required monthly run-rate = (FY target − YTD actual) ÷ Remaining months; compare to YTD average and seasonality-adjusted run-rate

Statement

\[ RR_{req} = \frac{T - A_{YTD}}{n}, \qquad RR_{YTD} = \frac{A_{YTD}}{m}, \qquad RR_{seas} = \frac{1}{n} \times \frac{A_{YTD}}{W_{YTD}} \times W_{YTG} \]

where \( T \) is the full-year target, \( A_{YTD} \) the year-to-date actual, \( m \) closed months, \( n = 12 - m \) remaining months, \( W_{YTD} \) and \( W_{YTG} \) the sums of seasonality weights for closed and remaining months (\( W_{YTD} + W_{YTG} = 1 \)). The test ratios are \( RR_{req} \div RR_{YTD} \) and \( RR_{req} \div RR_{seas} \).

Explanation

The required run-rate states what the business must average for the rest of the year to hit the target. On its own it means nothing; it must be compared with what the business can plausibly deliver.

  • \( RR_{YTD} \) — the average delivered so far — is valid as a benchmark only in non-seasonal businesses. In a business with a festive H2, \( RR_{req} \) will look like a big stretch against \( RR_{YTD} \) even if the AOP is perfectly on track.
  • \( RR_{seas} \) — what normal seasonality would deliver given YTD performance — is the fairer benchmark. If \( RR_{req} \approx RR_{seas} \), the plan is on track; if \( RR_{req} > RR_{seas} \), the gap requires actions beyond normal seasonal lift.
  • The bottom-up YTG forecast's run-rate \( F_{YTG} \div n \) should be compared with both: a forecast that exceeds \( RR_{seas} \) significantly needs specific evidence (new stores, price rise, large orders).

Special cases: if \( T - A_{YTD} \le 0 \), the target is achieved; at \( n = 1 \), compare the single month with its prior-year equivalent and pipeline rather than with averages; for very early cutoffs (3+9), all benchmarks are noisy.

Excel:

=LET(n,12-ClosedMonths, req,(Target-YTD)/n, ytd,YTD/ClosedMonths,
     seas,YTD/YTD_w*(1-YTD_w)/n, HSTACK(req,req/ytd,req/seas))

Illustration

FY26 revenue target ₹960 crore; 6+6 cutoff; YTD ₹410 crore; H1 seasonality weights 42%.

  • \( RR_{req} = (960 - 410) \div 6 = 91.7 \)
  • \( RR_{YTD} = 410 \div 6 = 68.3 \) → ratio 1.34 (looks unreachable)
  • \( RR_{seas} = (410 \div 0.42 \times 0.58) \div 6 = 566.2 \div 6 = 94.4 \) → ratio 0.97 (on track)

The YTD comparison would trigger panic; the seasonal comparison shows the plan is achievable if H2 seasonality is normal. If the sales forecast YTG is ₹620 crore (run-rate 103), it exceeds the seasonal baseline by 9% and needs justification.

Practice Questions

Q1. Three run-rates [L1]

Target ₹1,500 crore, cutoff 31-Dec (9+3), YTD ₹1,080 crore, April–December weights 75%. Compute \( RR_{req} \), \( RR_{YTD} \), \( RR_{seas} \) and both ratios, and interpret.

Q2. Q4-heavy B2B business [L2]

A capital-goods supplier's weights are: Q1 15%, Q2 20%, Q3 25%, Q4 40%. Target ₹2,000 crore. At 9+3, YTD is ₹1,140 crore. Compute the three run-rates, explain why the YTD average is particularly misleading here, and state the Q4 revenue needed.

💡 Hint: Check what proportion of the target the YTD represents compared with the YTD weight.

Q3. Interview-style interpretation [L3]

In an interview you are told: "Required run-rate is ₹52 crore per month, we have averaged ₹44 crore, and the forecast says ₹55 crore." Explain, in a structured two-minute answer, what further information you need and how you would decide whether the forecast is credible.
