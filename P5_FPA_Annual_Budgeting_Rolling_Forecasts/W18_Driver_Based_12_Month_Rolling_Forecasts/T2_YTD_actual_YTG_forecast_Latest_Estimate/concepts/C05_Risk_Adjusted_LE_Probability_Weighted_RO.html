Key concept

Risk-Adjusted LE: Probability-Weighted R&O

Concept: Risk-adjusted LE = Base LE + Σ (Opportunity × probability) − Σ (Risk × probability)

Statement

\[ \text{LE}_{RA} = \text{LE}_{base} + \sum_{j=1}^{J} p_j \, O_j - \sum_{i=1}^{I} q_i \, R_i \]

where \( \text{LE}_{base} \) is the base Latest Estimate, \( O_j \) the P&L impact of opportunity \( j \) (positive amount) with probability \( p_j \), \( R_i \) the P&L impact of risk \( i \) (positive amount) with probability \( q_i \), and \( J \), \( I \) the numbers of opportunities and risks in the register that fall within the fiscal year.

Explanation

The base LE assumes continuing drivers and highly probable events; discrete uncertain events are held outside it in an R&O register (s05). The risk-adjusted LE is the expected value of the full-year outcome under the simplifying assumption that items are independent and their impacts additive.

Conditions:

  • No double-counting: an item is either in the base or in the register, never both. Contingency buffers in base costs are a hidden form of double-counting.
  • Correct P&L level: for an EBITDA LE, a revenue opportunity counts at its contribution, not its gross revenue; fixed-cost savings count in full.
  • In-period only: include only the portion of each item's impact that falls in the current FY. A timing slip of an order from March to April is a risk to this FY equal to its full in-year impact.
  • Independence: correlated items (a demand slowdown hitting several customers) should be grouped into a scenario; summing their expected values understates tail risk.
  • Expected value is not a likely outcome: with a few large binary items, the actual result will usually be near the base or near base ± a full item, not at the EV. Show downside (base − Σ R) and upside (base + Σ O) alongside.

Use: the risk-adjusted LE is often the best estimate for Board communication and for attainment-based accruals (Ind AS 19), provided the probabilities are calibrated; guidance is often anchored nearer the downside.

Excel, register as table RO with columns Impact, Prob, Sign (+1 opportunity, −1 risk) and InFY (1/0):

=LE_Base+SUMPRODUCT(RO[Impact],RO[Prob],RO[Sign],RO[InFY])

Illustration

Base LE PAT ₹210 crore. Register (PAT impacts after tax at 25.17%):

Item Type Impact Prob. EV
Arbitration award O 15 40% +6.0
Duty drawback rate cut R 8 60% −4.8
Key customer loss R 20 20% −4.0
Cost-saving programme (Q4) O 6 75% +4.5

Risk-adjusted LE = 210 + 10.5 − 8.8 = ₹211.7 crore. Downside = 210 − 28 = ₹182 crore; upside = 210 + 21 = ₹231 crore. The narrow EV change (+₹1.7 crore) hides a ₹49 crore range — the range is the message for the Board.

Practice Questions

Q1. Risk-adjusted EBITDA [L1]

Base LE EBITDA ₹530 crore. Opportunities: ₹24 crore at 50%, ₹10 crore at 80%. Risks: ₹30 crore at 30%, ₹16 crore at 50%, ₹5 crore at 90%. Compute the risk-adjusted LE, downside and upside.

Q2. Revenue-to-EBITDA conversion and timing [L2]

A ₹60 crore revenue opportunity (contribution margin 28%) has 50% probability; if won, 40% of the revenue falls in FY26 and the rest in FY27. A ₹12 crore fixed-cost saving has 70% probability, of which half falls in FY26. Compute the FY26 EBITDA expected value of both items and the FY27 carry-over.

Q3. Correlated risks [L3]

Three risks are listed: dealer destocking (₹18 crore, 40%), lower festive demand (₹25 crore, 40%), higher discounting (₹10 crore, 40%), all driven by weak rural demand. Explain why summing their expected values is misleading, construct a combined "rural slowdown" scenario, and show how you would present it to the Board.

💡 Hint: If the three events tend to happen together, think about the probability of the combined event rather than each separately.
