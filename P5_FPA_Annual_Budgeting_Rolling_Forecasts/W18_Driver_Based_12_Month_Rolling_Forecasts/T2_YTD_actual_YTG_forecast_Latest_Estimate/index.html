Roadmap topic: Actual + Forecast (Year-to-Go) Hybrid P&L: Blending YTD Actuals with remaining months' Forecast (e.g., 6+6 or 9+3 LE — Latest Estimate) to track full-year target attainment

Overview

The Latest Estimate (LE) — also called Outlook, Current Year Forecast or Full-Year Projection — answers the single most frequent question a CFO receives: "where will we land this year?". It is a hybrid P&L: closed months are actuals, open months are the current best forecast, and the total is the projected full-year result. In India the convention is to name it by the split of the April–March fiscal year: a 3+9 LE after the June close (Q1), 6+6 after September (H1), 9+3 after December (9M). These coincide with quarterly results under SEBI LODR Reg 33, with Audit Committee and Board meetings, and with the points at which management commentary to analysts is refreshed.

Where the rolling forecast (Topic 1) is about a constant forward horizon, the LE is anchored to the fiscal year because targets, bonuses, guidance, covenants and statutory results are all fiscal-year concepts. Mechanically, the LE is the FY slice of the same cutoff-driven engine: a flag row splits the April–March grid into YTD and YTG, and LE = YTD + YTG. The analytical content lies in what you do with it — compare it with the AOP and prior year to identify the gap; test whether the YTG is credible by comparing the required run-rate with what the business has been delivering, adjusted for seasonality; overlay probability-weighted risks and opportunities; and communicate it to the Board with implications for market guidance and for the bonus provision.

The learning sequence: construct the LE structures (s01, c01); compute attainment and gaps (s02, c02); test credibility through the required run-rate (s03, c03) and the seasonality-adjusted YTG (s04, c04); build the R&O overlay (s05, c05); and communicate (s06), including the guidance and Ind AS 19 accrual consequences. The pitfalls in this topic are behavioural as much as technical: linear extrapolation in seasonal businesses, budget-plugs, unreconciled LE movements, and calendar-year defaults in Power BI.

For target roles — FP&A analyst, business finance manager, investor relations support — the LE is the number you will present most often. Interviewers test whether you can explain an LE movement in two minutes and whether you can spot a YTG that is not credible.

Related concepts to study

Week 20 MBR pack and executive summary

→ Week 20: LE column in MBR pack and executive summary

In Week 20 the LE becomes a standard column set in the Monthly Business Review pack: Month (Actual, AOP, variance), YTD (Actual, AOP, PY, variance), and Full Year (LE, AOP, PY, Gap, Attainment %, Prior LE, movement). The executive summary opens with the LE headline ("FY26 LE EBITDA ₹312 crore, 94% of AOP, down ₹9 crore vs last month's LE"), followed by the bridge and top R&O. Designing your Week 18 LE with clean column structures, a stored Prior LE and an R&O table means Week 20 is formatting and storytelling, not rebuilding. Note that a month column of actuals and a full-year LE column answer different questions; MBR packs that mix them without labelling confuse readers.

Week 11 DAX time intelligence with March year-end

→ Week 11: DAX time intelligence (TOTALYTD, DATESYTD with "3/31" year-end) for YTD/YTG

In Power BI, YTD for an Indian FY requires the year-end argument: YTD Actual = TOTALYTD([Actual], 'Date'[Date], "3/31") or CALCULATE([Actual], DATESYTD('Date'[Date], "3/31")). Without it, DAX assumes 31 December and January–March figures restart the year. YTG is computed with a filter on the date table: YTG Fcst = CALCULATE([Forecast], 'Date'[IsActual] = FALSE(), 'Date'[FY] = SELECTEDVALUE('Date'[FY])); LE = YTD Actual + YTG Fcst. A marked date table with FY, fiscal month number (April = 1) and IsActual (driven by a cutoff parameter table) is essential. The "3/31" string is locale-sensitive in some configurations — test it, or use explicit FY columns.

Management guidance and expectation management

Management guidance and earnings expectation management for listed companies

Many NSE-listed companies give qualitative or quantitative guidance on earnings calls ("mid-teens revenue growth", "EBITDA margin 18–20%"). The LE is the internal basis for confirming, revising or withdrawing guidance. Under SEBI LODR Reg 30 and the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, guidance changes are material, price-sensitive information that must be disclosed simultaneously to all (exchange filing before or with the call) and handled under the UPSI framework — LE files are typically UPSI, and the trading window closes around results. Expectation management means updating the market early and consistently rather than surprising at results, and not giving guidance more precise than the LE's accuracy supports.

Bonus provisioning based on LE attainment

Bonus/incentive provisioning based on LE attainment (Ind AS 19 short-term benefits)

Under Ind AS 19, short-term employee benefits including performance bonuses are recognised as an expense and liability when the entity has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of past events and a reliable estimate can be made (Ind AS 19.19). A constructive obligation exists when the entity has a past practice of paying and no realistic alternative. During the year, the accrual is estimated from expected full-year attainment — i.e. the LE — applied to the bonus scheme's payout curve, and accrued over the service period. If the LE drops below the payout threshold, the accrual must be reversed. Auditors will test the attainment assumption against the LE presented to the Board, so the two must be consistent.

Common pitfalls

Linear extrapolation in seasonal businesses

Extrapolating YTD run-rate linearly in a seasonal business (e.g., Q3 festive peak)

The mistake: YTG = (YTD ÷ months closed) × months remaining. People do it because it is quick and looks neutral. In an Indian consumer business where October–November (Navratri, Dussehra, Diwali) carry 25–30% of annual sales, a 6+6 linear extrapolation from April–September understates the full year; for a business peaking in Q4 (e.g. B2B capital goods or government spend, which bunch in March) a 9+3 linear projection may overstate or understate depending on the pattern. Correct treatment: use seasonality-adjusted projections (c04) based on multi-year weights, or driver-based YTG. Detect it in review by comparing YTG monthly phasing with the prior-year shape; a flat YTG in a seasonal business is a red flag.

Budget-plug YTG

Letting YTG forecast simply equal remaining budget ("budget-plug") instead of a fresh view

The mistake: YTG months are set equal to AOP for those months. It happens because managers do not want to signal a miss, because forecasting takes effort, and because "we're still committed to budget". The LE then equals YTD actual + remaining AOP, so any YTD shortfall is the only gap shown and future risks are hidden until they actualise. Correct treatment: a fresh driver-based YTG with assumptions documented, AOP shown only as a comparison column. Detect it with a check: count YTG cells where forecast equals AOP exactly; review YTG vs AOP variance by line — zero variance on material lines with a YTD miss is implausible.

Unreconciled LE movements

Not reconciling LE movements month-on-month (what changed vs last LE and why)

The mistake: publishing a new LE without a bridge from the previous one. It happens under time pressure and when the prior LE was not stored as a snapshot. The consequence: leadership cannot tell whether the business changed or the forecast assumptions did, credibility erodes, and forecasters can quietly shift numbers. Correct treatment: store each LE as a version; bridge Prior LE → Current LE through actualisation of the closed month(s), YTG driver changes, R&O changes and timing; assign each bar an owner and narrative. Detect it by asking "what was last month's LE and why are we different?" — if the answer requires reconstruction, the process is broken.

Calendar-year DAX defaults

Using April–December YTD with calendar-year DAX defaults

The mistake: TOTALYTD([Revenue],'Date'[Date]) without the year-end argument, so YTD in January shows only January rather than April–January. It happens because DAX defaults to a 31 December year-end and many tutorials are US-centric; the report looks correct for April–December and breaks silently in Q4. Correct treatment: pass "3/31" to TOTALYTD/DATESYTD, use a date table with FY and fiscal-month columns, and sort fiscal months April–March. Detect it by testing the YTD measure at a February date against an Excel SUMIFS from 1 April, and by checking that Q4 visuals do not reset.

Practice Questions

Q1. 6+6 LE with credibility testing [L3]

Mahanadi Consumer Ltd (FMCG, FY April–March). FY26 AOP revenue ₹2,400 crore; FY25 actual ₹2,150 crore. FY25 monthly share of revenue (%): Apr 7, May 7, Jun 7, Jul 7.5, Aug 8, Sep 9, Oct 11, Nov 10.5, Dec 8, Jan 8, Feb 8, Mar 9. Actual revenue Apr–Sep FY26 (₹ crore): 160, 162, 158, 170, 182, 205. Sales team's submitted YTG: equal to AOP for each remaining month, using AOP phasing equal to FY25 shares. (a) Construct the base LE using the sales team's YTG and compute attainment and gap. (b) Compute the required run-rate, the YTD-average run-rate projection and the seasonality-adjusted YTG. (c) Explain why the sales team's YTG is a budget-plug and quantify the difference vs the seasonality-adjusted view. (d) Recommend the LE to present.

Q2. R&O overlay and bonus accrual [L3]

Base 9+3 LE EBITDA for FY26 is ₹186 crore vs AOP ₹200 crore. Risks: plant shutdown (EBITDA impact ₹9 crore, 40%), price-cut by competitor (₹6 crore, 50%). Opportunities: insurance claim settlement (₹5 crore, 70%), early export order (₹8 crore, 30%). The bonus scheme pays 0% below 90% EBITDA attainment, 100% at 100%, linearly between, capped at 120%; the target pool is ₹12 crore. YTD bonus accrual is ₹6.3 crore. (a) Compute the risk-adjusted LE. (b) Compute attainment on base and risk-adjusted LE and the implied full-year bonus. (c) Determine the Q4 accrual adjustment and pass the journal entry. (d) Discuss which LE you would use for the Ind AS 19 estimate and what the auditor will test.

Q3. LE movement bridge and DAX check [L2]

October LE revenue was ₹1,540 crore; November LE is ₹1,512 crore. November actual was ₹158 crore vs ₹171 crore forecast; December–March volume revisions −₹12 crore; price increase effective January +₹9 crore; one large order slipped from March into April (next FY) ₹12 crore. (a) Build the bridge and check it reconciles. (b) Write the DAX measures for YTD actual and YTG forecast that would produce the November LE in Power BI for an April–March year.

Q4. Guidance decision case [L3]

In its Q1 call, a listed specialty-chemicals company guided "revenue growth of 15–18% for FY26" over FY25 revenue of ₹3,200 crore. At the 6+6 stage, the base LE is ₹3,590 crore and risk-adjusted LE ₹3,540 crore; the R&O list includes a ₹120 crore anti-dumping duty decision expected in December. Assess whether guidance remains achievable, the disclosure considerations under SEBI LODR and PIT Regulations, and draft the recommendation to the Audit Committee.
