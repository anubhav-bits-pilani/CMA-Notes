Subtopic note

Cost Drivers: Freight, Commission, Payment Gateway and Cost per Hire

Scope: Cost drivers: freight per tonne, commission %, payment gateway %, cost per hire

Why it matters

Revenue drivers get most attention, but a forecast that projects costs as "last year + inflation" will miss margin movements caused by volume, mix and channel shifts. Cost drivers link each cost line to the activity that causes it, so that a revenue scenario automatically produces consistent costs. The four examples in this subtopic represent the main types: a volume × rate logistics cost (freight per tonne), a % of revenue selling cost (commission), a % of transaction value channel cost (payment gateway), and an activity-based people cost (cost per hire). Together they show how to model variable, semi-variable and step costs.

Core notes

Freight per tonne

\[ \text{Freight}_t = \text{Tonnes despatched}_t \times \text{Freight rate per tonne}_t \]

The rate itself is a function of lead distance (km), mode (rail vs road vs coastal), and diesel prices: \( \text{Rate per tonne} = \text{Lead km} \times \text{₹ per tonne-km} \). Indian cement, steel and FMCG companies track freight as ₹ per tonne and lead distance in investor presentations.

  • Drivers to forecast: tonnes (from sales volume), mix of plant-to-market routes, rail share, diesel price (freight contracts often have fuel-escalation clauses tied to diesel price changes).
  • Inbound vs outbound: inbound freight is part of inventory cost (Ind AS 2) and flows to COGS when goods are sold; outbound freight is a selling/distribution expense.
  • Illustration: 1,20,000 tonnes in November; average lead 280 km; road ₹4.10 per tonne-km (70% share), rail ₹2.60 per tonne-km (30%). Blended rate = 280 × (0.7 × 4.10 + 0.3 × 2.60) = 280 × 3.65 = ₹1,022 per tonne. Freight = ₹12.26 crore. A 5% diesel-linked escalation on road rates adds 280 × 0.7 × 4.10 × 5% ≈ ₹40 per tonne, about ₹0.48 crore.

Commission %

\[ \text{Commission}_t = \text{Eligible revenue}_t \times \text{Commission rate} \]

  • Applies to sales agents, distributors' margins treated as expense (where the distributor is an agent), marketplace commissions (e-commerce platforms charge category-wise commission plus fixed fees), and sales-team variable pay.
  • Tiered schemes: rates rising with attainment (e.g. 1% up to 100% of target, 1.5% above) require cumulative attainment logic — use the YTD/LE engine to apply tiers.
  • Accounting: commissions on obtaining a contract may be capitalised under Ind AS 115 as incremental costs of obtaining a contract if expected to be recovered (practical expedient: expense if amortisation period ≤ 1 year). Marketplace commissions on goods sold are selling expenses; for an agent (marketplace operator itself), revenue is the commission (net).
  • Illustration: marketplace sales ₹40 crore, average commission 14% plus ₹30 fixed fee per order on 2,50,000 orders → ₹5.60 crore + ₹0.75 crore = ₹6.35 crore.

Payment gateway %

\[ \text{PG cost}_t = \sum_{m} \text{Prepaid GMV}_{t,m} \times \text{MDR}_m \]

where \( m \) indexes payment methods. Gateway charges (merchant discount rate, MDR) differ by method: UPI is zero-MDR for most merchant transactions under current Government of India policy (subject to change), debit cards are lower, credit cards, international cards and EMI higher, and wallets vary; GST at 18% applies on the gateway fee (input tax credit generally available). Cash-on-delivery (COD) orders avoid gateway fees but incur COD handling charges from logistics partners and higher RTO (return-to-origin) rates.

  • Drivers: GMV, prepaid share, payment-method mix, negotiated rates.
  • Illustration: GMV ₹25 crore; prepaid 65%, of which UPI 60% (0%), cards 35% (1.8%), wallets/net banking 5% (1.5%). PG cost = 16.25 × (0.35 × 1.8% + 0.05 × 1.5%) = 16.25 × 0.705% = ₹11.46 lakh. A shift of 10 points from UPI to cards raises it by 16.25 × 0.10 × 1.8% = ₹2.93 lakh.

Cost per hire

\[ \text{Recruitment cost}_t = \text{Hires}_t \times \text{Cost per hire} \]

\[ \text{Hires}_t = \text{Net headcount change}_t + \text{Attrition}_t, \qquad \text{Attrition}_t = \text{Opening headcount}_t \times \frac{\text{Annual attrition \%}}{12} \]

Cost per hire includes agency fees (often 8.33% of annual CTC, i.e. one month's salary, or a flat fee), job-portal subscriptions, referral bonuses, assessment costs and onboarding. Hiring also drives a lag: new employees cost salary from joining date, with notice periods of 30–90 days in India delaying joining after offer.

  • Illustration: opening headcount 2,400; attrition 18% p.a.; planned net additions 30 per month → hires = 30 + 2,400 × 1.5% = 66 per month. Cost per hire ₹65,000 → ₹42.9 lakh per month. Reducing attrition to 15% cuts hires to 60 and saves ₹3.9 lakh per month in recruitment cost alone, plus productivity effects.

Building cost drivers in the model

Cost type Driver form Example
Variable volume Units × rate Freight per tonne, packing per unit
Variable value Revenue × % Commission, royalty, PG
Semi-variable Fixed + units × rate Power: fixed demand charge + kWh × tariff
Step-fixed Capacity blocks Supervisors per 25 workers, warehouses per region
Activity-based Activities × cost per activity Hires × cost per hire, orders × fulfilment cost
Fixed / contractual Schedule Rent, AMC, Ind AS 116 lease depreciation and interest

Link each driver to the revenue drivers (tonnes, orders, GMV, headcount) so scenarios flow through.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Non-linear contracts: volume-slab freight or commission tiers create kinks; model slabs explicitly (MIN/MAX or lookup tables).
  • Pass-through costs: some freight is recovered from customers (ex-works vs delivered pricing); net recoveries against freight or treat as revenue depending on Ind AS 115 principal/agent analysis.
  • Regulatory changes: MDR policies, GST rates on services and e-commerce TCS (Sec 52 of the CGST Act) affect channel costs and cash; flag as R&O.
  • Attrition seasonality: attrition spikes after bonus payouts and appraisal cycles (April–June in many Indian companies).
  • Hidden fixed elements: "variable" costs often have minimum commitments (minimum freight volumes, platform fees).

Key takeaways

  • Model each cost from its causal activity: tonnes, revenue, prepaid GMV, hires.
  • Freight = tonnes × (lead km × ₹ per tonne-km), with mode mix and diesel escalation.
  • Commission = eligible revenue × rate, with tiers driven by cumulative attainment; consider Ind AS 115 capitalisation.
  • Payment-gateway cost depends on prepaid share and payment-method mix (UPI vs cards); COD has its own costs.
  • Hires = net additions + attrition; recruitment cost = hires × cost per hire, with joining lags.
  • Classify costs as variable, semi-variable, step or fixed, and link drivers so scenarios flow through.

Practice Questions

Q1. Freight driver build [L1]

A tile manufacturer despatches 45,000 tonnes in December: 60% to markets with 350 km lead and 40% to markets with 620 km lead, road freight ₹3.90 per tonne-km. Compute December freight and freight per tonne.

Q2. Tiered commission with LE [L2]

Sales-agent commission is 2% of revenue up to the FY target of ₹500 crore and 3% on revenue above target, computed on cumulative FY revenue. YTD (Apr–Dec) revenue ₹390 crore; forecast Jan 38, Feb 40, Mar 52. Compute monthly commission for January–March and the FY total.

💡 Hint: Find the month in which cumulative revenue crosses ₹500 crore and split that month.

Q3. Payment-mix scenario [L2]

A D2C brand's monthly GMV is ₹18 crore; COD share 40% (COD handling ₹35 per order, 1,00,000 total orders evenly sized, RTO 22% on COD orders with reverse logistics ₹90 per RTO order); prepaid split UPI 55%, cards 40%, EMI 5% with MDR 0%, 1.9% and 2.5%. Compute total payment-related cost and the effect of a campaign that shifts COD share to 25% (to prepaid in the same mix).

Q4. Recruitment budget from attrition [L2]

Opening headcount 5,200 on 1 October; target headcount 5,500 at 31 March; annual attrition 21%; cost per hire ₹48,000 for lateral hires, with 30% of hires through agencies at an additional 8.33% of annual CTC of ₹7.2 lakh. Compute total hires and recruitment cost for October–March.
