Subtopic note

Statistical Support: Regression, Moving Averages, Smoothing, FORECAST.ETS

Scope: Statistical support: regression, moving averages, exponential smoothing, Excel FORECAST.ETS

Why it matters

Driver owners provide judgement; statistics provide a disciplined baseline and evidence for driver relationships. Regression tells you whether leads actually predict orders and with what lag; moving averages and exponential smoothing provide naive-to-intermediate baselines against which owner-submitted forecasts are tested (Forecast Value Added, Topic 5); FORECAST.ETS gives a seasonal statistical forecast in one Excel function. FP&A teams do not need data-science models for most lines, but they must use these tools competently and know their limits — and interviewers increasingly ask how statistical baselines fit into a driver-based process.

Core notes

Regression

Simple linear regression estimates a driver relationship \( y = a + b x + \varepsilon \), where \( y \) is the outcome (e.g. monthly orders), \( x \) the driver (e.g. leads lagged two months), \( a \) the intercept, \( b \) the slope and \( \varepsilon \) the error.

  • Excel: SLOPE(y,x), INTERCEPT(y,x), RSQ(y,x), FORECAST.LINEAR(x0,y,x), STEYX(y,x) (standard error of estimate). LINEST(y,x,TRUE,TRUE) returns coefficients with standard errors and statistics; multiple regression uses several x columns.
  • Interpretation checks: sign and size of coefficients are economically plausible; \( R^2 \) meaningful but not the only criterion; residuals show no pattern (plot them); t-statistics of coefficients (coefficient ÷ standard error) above about 2 in absolute value.
  • Lag selection: compute CORREL between y and x shifted by 0–6 months; choose the lag with the strongest, stable correlation (s03).
  • Pitfalls: spurious regression between trending series (both grow over time) — regress on changes or include a trend; multicollinearity between drivers; small samples (24 monthly points is minimal); structural breaks (COVID, GST introduction in July 2017).

Illustration. 24 months of data: orders vs leads two months earlier. SLOPE = 0.042, INTERCEPT = 6.5, RSQ = 0.71. Forecast for leads of 1,200: orders = 6.5 + 0.042 × 1,200 = 56.9. The intercept suggests a base of repeat orders independent of new leads — check that it is plausible.

Moving averages

  • Simple moving average (SMA): \( \text{SMA}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} y_{t-i} \). A 3-month SMA smooths noise; the forecast for next month is the latest SMA. It lags turning points by about \( (k-1)/2 \) periods.
  • Centred 12-month moving average: removes seasonality to reveal trend; used to derive seasonal indices (ratio-to-moving-average, Topic 2 s04).
  • Weighted moving average: heavier weight on recent months.
  • Excel: AVERAGE over a moving range built with INDEX (not OFFSET — c05 of Topic 1), or dynamic arrays with TAKE.

Exponential smoothing

Simple exponential smoothing (SES):

\[ S_t = \alpha y_t + (1 - \alpha) S_{t-1}, \qquad \hat{y}_{t+1} = S_t \]

where \( S_t \) is the smoothed level, \( y_t \) the actual, \( \alpha \in (0,1) \) the smoothing constant (higher = more responsive). Holt's method adds a trend component; Holt-Winters (triple exponential smoothing) adds seasonality — the basis of FORECAST.ETS.

Illustration (SES, \( \alpha = 0.3 \)). \( S_{Jun} = 100 \); July actual 112 → \( S_{Jul} = 0.3 \times 112 + 0.7 \times 100 = 103.6 \); August forecast 103.6. August actual 98 → \( S_{Aug} = 0.3 \times 98 + 0.7 \times 103.6 = 101.9 \).

Excel FORECAST.ETS family

=FORECAST.ETS(target_date, values, timeline, [seasonality], [data_completion], [aggregation])
=FORECAST.ETS.CONFINT(target_date, values, timeline, [confidence_level], [seasonality], ...)
=FORECAST.ETS.SEASONALITY(values, timeline)
=FORECAST.ETS.STAT(values, timeline, statistic_type, [seasonality], ...)
  • Implements the AAA version of exponential triple smoothing (additive error, trend, seasonality).
  • Timeline: numeric dates at a constant step (month-end dates from EOMONTH work; Excel tolerates month-length differences when dates are month-based). Up to 30% missing points can be filled (data_completion 1 = interpolate, 0 = zeros).
  • Seasonality: 1 or omitted = auto-detect; 0 = none; 12 = fixed annual cycle for monthly data. Prefer explicitly setting 12 when the business is known to have annual seasonality — auto-detection on short or noisy history can pick odd cycle lengths.
  • CONFINT returns the half-width of the confidence interval (default 95%) — useful for presenting a forecast range.
  • STAT returns α (level), β (trend), γ (seasonality) and error metrics (MASE, SMAPE, MAE, RMSE) for evaluating fit.
  • Forecast Sheet (Data → Forecast Sheet) builds an ETS forecast table and chart automatically.

Illustration. 36 months of monthly revenue (Apr-22 to Mar-25) in B5:B40 with month-end dates in A5:A40. Forecast for 31-Oct-2025:

=FORECAST.ETS(DATE(2025,10,31),$B$5:$B$40,$A$5:$A$40,12)
=FORECAST.ETS.CONFINT(DATE(2025,10,31),$B$5:$B$40,$A$5:$A$40,0.9,12)

The second returns the 90% half-width; present the forecast as point ± half-width.

Combining statistics with drivers and judgement

  1. Use statistics to estimate driver relationships (regression) and baselines for drivers themselves (ETS on footfall, leads).
  2. Let owners adjust the baseline for known events (launches, price changes, festivals shifting dates), recording each adjustment.
  3. Measure whether adjustments improve accuracy versus the statistical baseline (FVA, Topic 5).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Structural breaks: COVID-affected FY21, a demerger, a new channel — exclude or adjust history; ETS will otherwise propagate the break.
  • Short history: fewer than two seasonal cycles (24 months) makes seasonal estimation unreliable.
  • Moving festivals: ETS assumes fixed calendar seasonality; Diwali shifting between October and November produces errors — adjust with event flags or post-forecast reallocation.
  • Regression is correlation, not causation: a statistical relationship must also make business sense before being used as a driver.
  • Over-fitting: high in-sample \( R^2 \) with many variables may forecast poorly; hold out the last 6 months to test.
  • Explainability: stakeholders must understand the baseline; simple methods with explained adjustments beat opaque ones.

Key takeaways

  • Regression (SLOPE, INTERCEPT, RSQ, LINEST) quantifies driver relationships and lags; check plausibility and residuals.
  • Moving averages smooth noise and derive trend/seasonality but lag turning points.
  • Exponential smoothing weights recent data more; Holt-Winters adds trend and seasonality.
  • FORECAST.ETS (with seasonality 12 for monthly data) gives a seasonal baseline; CONFINT and STAT give ranges and fit statistics.
  • Use statistics as baselines and evidence, then layer documented judgement; test whether judgement adds value.
  • Watch structural breaks, short histories, moving festivals and over-fitting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Regression for a driver relationship [L2]

Monthly data for 18 months show orders (y) and leads two months earlier (x). Summary statistics: \( \bar{x} = 1{,}050 \), \( \bar{y} = 48 \), \( \sum (x - \bar{x})(y - \bar{y}) = 36{,}400 \), \( \sum (x - \bar{x})^2 = 9{,}10{,}000 \). Compute the slope and intercept, forecast orders for 1,300 leads, and list three checks you would run before using the relationship in the forecast.

Q2. SES vs 3-month SMA [L2]

Monthly actuals (₹ crore) Apr–Sep: 40, 44, 41, 47, 50, 46. Compute the 3-month SMA forecast for October and SES forecasts with \( \alpha = 0.2 \) and \( \alpha = 0.6 \) (initial level = April actual). Explain which method reacts faster and when that is desirable.

💡 Hint: Start SES with the level equal to April's value and update month by month.

Q3. FORECAST.ETS build [L2]

You have 42 months of month-end dates and footfall per store in A2:B43. Write formulas for (a) the forecast for each of the next 12 months, spilled from a header row built with EOMONTH, (b) the 95% confidence half-width, (c) the detected seasonality, and (d) the RMSE of the fit. Explain why you might override auto-detected seasonality.

Q4. Statistical baseline vs judgement [L3]

The statistical ETS baseline for H2 revenue is ₹612 crore with a 90% interval of ±₹45 crore. The sales team submits ₹560 crore citing weak dealer sentiment. Last year the team's H2 forecast was 9% below actual while the statistical baseline was 3% above. Discuss how you would reconcile the two, what evidence you would request, and which number would go into the LE.
