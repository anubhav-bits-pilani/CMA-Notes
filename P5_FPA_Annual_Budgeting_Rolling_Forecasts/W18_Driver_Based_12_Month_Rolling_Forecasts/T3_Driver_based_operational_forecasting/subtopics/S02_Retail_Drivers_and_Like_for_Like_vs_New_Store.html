Retail Drivers and Like-for-Like vs New-Store Growth

Scope: Retail: stores × footfall × conversion × average transaction value; like-for-like (LFL/SSSG) vs new-store growth

Why it matters

Organised retail in India — apparel (Trent, ABFRL), value retail (DMart, V-Mart), jewellery (Titan, Kalyan), electronics, QSR and beauty — is analysed by investors almost entirely through store-level drivers. Quarterly investor presentations report store count, area (lakh sq ft), revenue per sq ft, like-for-like or same-store sales growth (SSSG) and sometimes footfall and conversion. An FP&A analyst in retail must forecast these drivers, explain variances in their terms ("LFL +6% driven by ATV +4% and conversion +2%, footfall flat") and keep LFL and new-store effects separate, because the two have very different implications for valuation and capital allocation.

Core notes

The core retail identity

\[ \text{Revenue} = \text{Stores} \times \text{Days} \times \text{Footfall per store-day} \times \text{Conversion \%} \times \text{ATV} \]

  • Stores (or trading area): count of stores open and trading in the period; weighted by days open for openings/closures mid-month.
  • Days: trading days in the month (store closures for festivals or local restrictions reduce days).
  • Footfall: walk-ins counted by door counters; where unavailable, proxy from POS bills ÷ an assumed conversion, or from mall footfall data.
  • Conversion: bills ÷ footfall. Measures sales effectiveness, assortment availability and staffing.
  • ATV (average transaction value, basket size): net sales ÷ bills. Decomposes into units per transaction (UPT) × average selling price (ASP); ASP depends on MRP, mix and markdowns.

An alternative area-based identity used for space productivity: Revenue = Trading area (sq ft) × Revenue per sq ft per month. Both should reconcile.

Like-for-like (LFL) / same-store sales growth (SSSG)

LFL growth compares sales of the same stores across both periods, removing the effect of openings and closures:

\[ \text{LFL growth} = \frac{\text{Sales}_{t}^{LFL\ cohort}}{\text{Sales}_{t-1}^{LFL\ cohort}} - 1 \]

Defining the cohort: stores trading for at least 12 full months (some companies use 24 months, or "operational for the full comparable period") at the start of the comparison period, excluding stores closed, relocated or materially refurbished. The definition is company-specific — always disclose it.

Total growth decomposition:

\[ \text{Total growth} \approx \text{LFL contribution} + \text{New-store contribution} - \text{Closed-store effect} \]

where LFL contribution = LFL growth × (LFL base ÷ total PY sales).

New-store forecasting

New stores do not trade at mature productivity immediately. Model a ramp curve by months since opening (e.g. 55%, 65%, 75%, 85%, 90%, 95%, 100% from month 12 onwards, relative to a mature store of the same format and city tier). Build a cohort grid: rows = opening month cohorts, columns = forecast months, cells = stores in cohort × mature sales per store × ramp factor for months since opening. Pre-opening costs (rent-free fit-out period, staff training) and Ind AS 116 lease recognition (right-of-use asset and lease liability from commencement date) occur before revenue.

Forecast build in Excel

LFL revenue_t   =LFL_Stores*DAY(Hdr)*Footfall_t*Conv_t*ATV_t
NewStore_t      =SUMPRODUCT(Cohort_Stores,MatureSalesPerStore*Days_t,
                 XLOOKUP(MonthsSinceOpen_t,Ramp[Month],Ramp[Factor],1))

In practice the new-store formula is built in a cohort grid with DATEDIF or month-difference calculations for months since opening, (YEAR(Hdr)-YEAR(Open))*12+MONTH(Hdr)-MONTH(Open)+1.

Illustration

FY26 H2, apparel chain. LFL cohort: 160 stores. PY H2 LFL sales ₹640 crore. Forecast LFL drivers vs PY: footfall −2%, conversion +3% (relative), ATV +5%.

  • LFL growth = 0.98 × 1.03 × 1.05 − 1 = 5.99% → LFL sales ₹678.3 crore.
  • New stores: 20 opened during FY25 (now in months 7–18 of ramp) contribute ₹62 crore; 15 FY26 openings contribute ₹28 crore.
  • Closed stores (PY sales ₹18 crore) contribute nil.
  • PY H2 total = 640 + 18 (closed) + 34 (FY25 openings' PY H2 sales, partial ramp) = ₹692 crore.
  • Forecast H2 total = 678.3 + 62 + 28 = ₹768.3 crore → total growth 11.0%, of which LFL contributes 38.3 ÷ 692 = 5.5 points and new stores/closures the rest.

Reporting "11% growth" alone would overstate underlying demand by half.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Cohort definition changes (12 to 24 months) alter reported LFL; keep consistent definitions across periods.
  • Calendar shifts: a festival moving between months or a leap-year day distorts monthly LFL; compare festival-aligned periods.
  • Omni-channel: online orders fulfilled from stores (ship-from-store, BOPIS) — are they in store LFL? Decide and disclose.
  • Refurbished or relocated stores are usually excluded from LFL for the disruption period.
  • Cannibalisation: a new store near an existing one depresses the existing store's LFL; model a cannibalisation factor for dense cities.
  • Inflation: LFL in value includes price inflation; volume LFL (units or bills) shows real demand.
  • Franchise vs company-operated: for franchise stores, the company's revenue is royalties or wholesale, not store sales; keep separate trees.

Key takeaways

  • Retail revenue = Stores × Days × Footfall per store-day × Conversion × ATV; ATV = UPT × ASP.
  • LFL/SSSG uses a defined cohort of stores trading in both periods; disclose the definition.
  • Forecast LFL from driver changes and new stores via cohort ramp curves, separately.
  • Total growth = LFL contribution + new-store contribution − closures; report both components.
  • Watch calendar shifts, cannibalisation, omni-channel allocation and refurbishments.
  • Pre-opening costs and Ind AS 116 lease recognition precede new-store revenue.

Practice Questions

Q1. Driver-based monthly revenue [L1]

A jewellery chain has 48 stores, 31 trading days in October, footfall 140 per store-day, conversion 22% and ATV ₹68,000. Compute October revenue and the effect of a Dhanteras-driven 30% footfall uplift on 3 days.

Q2. LFL vs total growth [L2]

PY revenue ₹1,200 crore: LFL cohort ₹1,050 crore, stores closed during the year ₹30 crore, stores opened during PY ₹120 crore (partial year). Current-year LFL cohort (same stores) revenue ₹1,113 crore; PY openings now in full year ₹205 crore; current-year openings ₹70 crore. Compute total growth, LFL growth, and the contribution of each component in percentage points.

💡 Hint: PY openings are not in the LFL cohort until they have traded for the full comparable period.

Q3. New-store cohort grid [L2]

Openings: 5 stores in July 2025, 8 in October 2025, 6 in January 2026. Mature sales per store ₹1.1 crore per month; ramp 50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 85%, 90% for months 1–6, 95% for months 7–12. Build the cohort grid for July 2025 to March 2026 and compute FY26 new-store revenue.

Q4. Investor-presentation analysis [L3]

An NSE-listed value retailer reports for Q3: revenue +24% YoY, store count 410 vs 330, SSSG 4.5%, revenue per sq ft −3%. Explain what these figures together imply about underlying demand and new-store productivity, and list the three driver questions you would ask management on the earnings call.
