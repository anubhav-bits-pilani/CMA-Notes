Manufacturing Capacity Drivers and the SaaS ARR Waterfall

Scope: Manufacturing: capacity × utilisation × yield; SaaS/subscriptions: ARR waterfall (new, expansion, churn)

Why it matters

Manufacturing and subscription businesses sit at opposite ends of the driver spectrum. In manufacturing (cement, steel, chemicals, auto components, pharma formulations), supply-side physical constraints often bind: revenue cannot exceed what the plant can make and sell. Investors in Indian cement or steel track capacity (MTPA), utilisation and realisation per tonne every quarter. In SaaS and subscriptions (Indian SaaS exporters, OTT platforms, edtech, telecom, insurance-like recurring models), revenue is driven by the recurring base and its movements: new customers, upsell, downgrades and churn. Forecasting either with "last year + growth %" ignores the mechanism that actually generates revenue and produces forecasts that breach capacity or ignore churn.

Core notes

Manufacturing: capacity × utilisation × yield

\[ \text{Good output}_t = \text{Installed capacity}_t \times \text{Utilisation \%}_t \times \text{Yield \%}_t \]

  • Installed (rated) capacity: maximum output per period under design conditions, e.g. 5 MTPA cement = about 0.417 million tonnes per month. Changes with debottlenecking and new lines (from commercial production date, not capex approval).
  • Utilisation: actual production ÷ capacity. Driven by demand, planned shutdowns (annual maintenance), unplanned breakdowns, raw-material availability and power. Sustainable maximum is usually 85–95%.
  • Yield: good output ÷ gross output (or output ÷ input), reflecting scrap, rejects and process losses. In chemicals and pharma, yield on key raw material drives cost as much as volume.
  • OEE link: Overall Equipment Effectiveness = Availability × Performance × Quality; utilisation roughly corresponds to availability × performance and yield to quality.

Revenue = Sales volume × Realisation per unit, where sales volume = good output ± change in finished-goods inventory. Variable costs per unit (raw material per tonne via consumption norms, power and fuel per tonne, freight per tonne) multiply sales or production volume as appropriate.

Demand vs capacity: forecast volume = MIN(Demand forecast, Capacity × Max utilisation × Yield). When demand exceeds capacity, the forecast must show the constraint (and lost sales), not unconstrained demand.

Manufacturing illustration

Cement plant, 6 MTPA, Q3 FY26 (October–December, 92 days).

  • Quarterly capacity = 6 × 92 ÷ 365 = 1.512 million tonnes.
  • Utilisation 78% (monsoon over, construction season), yield (clinker/cement losses) 99%.
  • Output = 1.512 × 0.78 × 0.99 = 1.168 million tonnes.
  • Realisation ₹5,300 per tonne (net of GST and discounts) → revenue ₹619 crore.
  • A 5-point utilisation rise adds 0.075 million tonnes (₹40 crore revenue); at contribution of ₹1,600 per tonne, ₹12 crore EBITDA.

SaaS: the ARR waterfall

ARR (annual recurring revenue) = annualised value of active recurring contracts (MRR × 12). The waterfall:

\[ \text{ARR}_{close} = \text{ARR}_{open} + \text{New} + \text{Expansion} - \text{Contraction} - \text{Churn} \]

  • New: ARR from customers acquired in the period (drivers: pipeline × win rate × average new ACV, with sales-cycle lag).
  • Expansion: upsell, cross-sell, seat additions, price uplifts for existing customers.
  • Contraction: downgrades, seat reductions, discounts at renewal.
  • Churn: ARR of customers who leave entirely (logo churn).

Retention metrics:

\[ \text{NRR} = \frac{\text{ARR}_{open} + \text{Expansion} - \text{Contraction} - \text{Churn}}{\text{ARR}_{open}}, \qquad \text{GRR} = \frac{\text{ARR}_{open} - \text{Contraction} - \text{Churn}}{\text{ARR}_{open}} \]

measured on the opening cohort (new customers excluded). NRR above 100% means the existing base grows on its own; best-in-class B2B SaaS achieves 110–130%.

From ARR to revenue: recognised revenue in a month ≈ average MRR in the month (opening MRR + half the net movement), adjusted for implementation services and usage-based fees. Under Ind AS 115, subscription revenue is generally recognised over time as the service is provided, and contract liabilities (deferred revenue) arise from annual upfront billing — ARR is a management metric, not an Ind AS measure.

SaaS illustration

Opening ARR ₹180 crore (1 April). FY26 forecast: New ₹45 crore (phased by quarter 8, 10, 12, 15), Expansion ₹27 crore, Contraction ₹5 crore, Churn ₹13 crore.

  • Closing ARR = 180 + 45 + 27 − 5 − 13 = ₹234 crore (+30%).
  • NRR = (180 + 27 − 5 − 13) ÷ 180 = 105.0%; GRR = (180 − 5 − 13) ÷ 180 = 90.0%.
  • Forecast revenue ≈ average ARR over the year (not closing ARR), roughly (180 + 234) ÷ 2 = ₹207 crore if movements are spread evenly; back-loaded new bookings make it lower.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Capacity additions: use the commercial operation date and a ramp-up curve for utilisation of new lines; announcements are not capacity.
  • Planned shutdowns: annual maintenance (often in monsoon months for cement) reduces available days; model as capacity days lost, not lower utilisation.
  • Multi-product plants: capacity may be shared (a reactor making several chemicals); use machine-hours capacity and product mix with hours per unit.
  • Toll manufacturing / outsourcing can lift volume above own capacity at lower margin; model separately.
  • ARR definitions vary: whether one-time fees, usage fees or multi-year discounts are included; contracted-not-live ARR (CARR) vs live ARR. Define and disclose.
  • Churn timing: annual contracts churn at renewal dates; use a renewal schedule rather than a flat monthly churn rate.
  • FX in ARR: Indian SaaS exporters with USD contracts should forecast ARR in USD and translate, separating FX movement in the waterfall.

Key takeaways

  • Manufacturing output = installed capacity × utilisation × yield; sales volume = MIN(demand, constrained output) ± inventory change.
  • Use realisation per unit and per-unit variable cost norms to reach revenue and contribution.
  • SaaS: Closing ARR = Opening + New + Expansion − Contraction − Churn.
  • NRR and GRR measure the opening cohort's retention; NRR > 100% means the base grows by itself.
  • Revenue follows average ARR and Ind AS 115 recognition over time — not closing ARR.
  • Model shutdowns, new-line ramp-ups, renewal schedules and definitions explicitly.

Practice Questions

Q1. Plant output and constraint [L2]

A specialty-steel plant has capacity 60,000 tonnes per month, maximum sustainable utilisation 90% and yield 94%. Demand forecast for November is 54,000 tonnes and for December 48,000 tonnes. Realisation ₹72,000 per tonne. Compute sales volume and revenue for each month and quantify lost sales, assuming no opening finished-goods inventory.

Q2. ARR waterfall by quarter [L2]

Opening ARR ₹96 crore. Quarterly movements (New, Expansion, Contraction, Churn, ₹ crore): Q1 6, 3, 1, 2; Q2 7, 4, 1, 3; Q3 9, 4, 2, 2; Q4 11, 5, 1, 4. Build the quarterly waterfall, compute closing ARR, annual NRR and GRR, and estimate FY revenue assuming movements occur mid-quarter.

💡 Hint: Revenue in a quarter is approximately the average ARR for the quarter divided by 4.

Q3. Yield as a cost driver [L2]

A pharma API plant converts raw material at a yield of 68% (kg API per kg key starting material). Starting material costs ₹9,500 per kg. Output 12,000 kg API per month. Compute monthly raw-material cost and the annual saving from improving yield to 72%.

Q4. Choosing drivers for a hybrid business [L3]

An Indian industrial-IoT company sells sensors (hardware, one-time) and a monitoring subscription (₹18,000 per device per year). Design the driver model linking device shipments, installed base, subscription ARR, churn and revenue, and explain how you would forecast each driver and the timing of revenue recognition under Ind AS 115.
