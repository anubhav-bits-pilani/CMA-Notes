Key concept

Retail Revenue Identity: Stores × Footfall × Conversion × ATV × Days

Concept: Retail revenue = Stores × Footfall per store × Conversion % × ATV (basket size) × Days

Statement

\[ R_t = N_t \times F_t \times c_t \times \text{ATV}_t \times D_t \]

where \( R_t \) is net retail revenue for month \( t \); \( N_t \) the average number of trading stores (weighted by days open); \( F_t \) the average footfall per store per trading day; \( c_t \) the conversion rate (bills ÷ footfall); \( \text{ATV}_t \) the average transaction value (net sales ÷ bills, i.e. basket size); and \( D_t \) the trading days in the month. The product \( F_t \times c_t \) is bills per store-day; \( N_t \times D_t \) is store-days.

Explanation

The identity is exact by construction (each ratio's denominator cancels the next numerator), so it reconciles to actual revenue whenever its inputs are measured consistently. Its value lies in separating what drives revenue:

  • Stores × days — the network and its availability (expansion team, property, store operations).
  • Footfall — traffic: marketing, location, mall health, festivals, weather.
  • Conversion — sales effectiveness, staffing, stock availability (stock-outs lower conversion), pricing.
  • ATV — units per transaction × average selling price; driven by assortment, cross-selling, mix and markdowns.

Because it is multiplicative, percentage changes approximately add for small moves: \( \Delta R / R \approx \Delta N/N + \Delta F/F + \Delta c/c + \Delta \text{ATV}/\text{ATV} + \Delta D/D \). This underpins variance commentary ("revenue +8%: ATV +5%, conversion +2%, footfall +1%").

Conditions: use net-of-GST ATV if revenue is net (Ind AS 115 revenue excludes GST); weight stores opened mid-month by days traded; exclude online sales unless footfall is defined to include sessions; use LFL cohorts separately from new stores (s02), since new stores have different footfall and conversion during ramp-up.

Illustration

Electronics retailer, November 2025 (30 days), 120 stores.

Driver Value
Stores 120
Footfall per store-day 310
Conversion 11%
ATV (net of GST) ₹18,500
Days 30

Revenue = 120 × 310 × 0.11 × 18,500 × 30 = ₹227.1 crore. Bills = 120 × 310 × 0.11 × 30 = 1,22,760. If conversion rises to 12% (+9.1% relative) and ATV falls to ₹17,800 (−3.8%) due to a smartphone mix shift, revenue = 120 × 310 × 0.12 × 17,800 × 30 = ₹238.4 crore (+5.0%, roughly 9.1% − 3.8%, with the small difference being the interaction term).

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and decompose [L1]

A beauty retailer has 85 stores, 31 days, footfall 260 per store-day, conversion 24% and ATV ₹1,150. Compute revenue. Then compute revenue if footfall falls 6% and ATV rises 4%, and state the approximate and exact percentage change.

Q2. Reverse-engineering a target [L2]

A retailer must deliver ₹96 crore in March (31 days) from 140 stores with footfall 420 per store-day and ATV ₹1,700. Compute the conversion required and compare with the current 12.5%. If conversion can rise by at most 0.5 points, what ATV is required?

💡 Hint: Rearrange the identity to isolate the unknown driver.

Q3. Mid-month openings and closures [L2]

During December (31 days), a chain starts with 200 stores, opens 6 stores on 16 December and closes 2 on 11 December. Compute the day-weighted average store count and the revenue with footfall 350, conversion 15% and ATV ₹2,400, applying the same productivity to all stores, and explain why a separate new-store ramp would be more accurate.
