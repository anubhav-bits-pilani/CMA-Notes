Key concept

ARR Roll-Forward and Net Revenue Retention

Concept: Closing ARR = Opening ARR + New + Expansion − Contraction − Churn; NRR = (Opening + Expansion − Contraction − Churn) ÷ Opening

Statement

\[ \text{ARR}_{close} = \text{ARR}_{open} + N + E - C - X \]

\[ \text{NRR} = \frac{\text{ARR}_{open} + E - C - X}{\text{ARR}_{open}} \]

where \( \text{ARR}_{open} \) is annual recurring revenue at the start of the period; \( N \) ARR from new customers acquired in the period; \( E \) expansion (upsell, cross-sell, price uplift) from existing customers; \( C \) contraction (downgrades, seat reductions) from existing customers; \( X \) churned ARR from customers who left. Gross revenue retention is \( \text{GRR} = (\text{ARR}_{open} - C - X) \div \text{ARR}_{open} \), capped at 100%.

Explanation

The roll-forward is a balance-sheet-style reconciliation of a stock (ARR) through flows. It forces a forecast to state separately how much growth comes from acquiring customers (expensive: sales and marketing) and how much from the installed base (cheaper: customer success). NRR measures the base on its own: above 100%, the opening cohort grows even with zero new sales; below 100%, the business must sell new logos just to stand still.

Conditions:

  • Cohort basis: NRR uses only the opening cohort; new customers acquired during the period are excluded from both numerator and denominator (their expansion counts in the next period's NRR).
  • Period: usually trailing 12 months; quarterly NRRs are not comparable to annual ones without annualisation.
  • Definitions: whether ARR includes usage-based fees, one-time implementation (it should not), and contracted-not-yet-live amounts; whether churn is measured at contract end or notice date.
  • FX: for USD contracts reported in ₹, separate FX translation from operational movements, or compute NRR in contract currency.
  • ARR vs revenue: ARR is a run-rate metric; Ind AS 115 revenue for the year depends on the timing of movements (recognised over the service period). Forecast revenue ≈ sum of monthly MRR.

Forecast drivers: \( N \) = pipeline × win rate × average new ACV (with lags, c02); \( E \) = opening ARR × expansion rate (or seat growth × price); \( C \) and \( X \) = renewal schedule × probability of downgrade/churn, or opening ARR × monthly rates.

Illustration

FY26 for an Indian HR-tech SaaS company (₹ crore):

Movement Amount
Opening ARR (1-Apr-2025) 320
New 70
Expansion 48
Contraction (12)
Churn (26)
Closing ARR (31-Mar-2026) 400

NRR = (320 + 48 − 12 − 26) ÷ 320 = 330 ÷ 320 = 103.1%. GRR = (320 − 12 − 26) ÷ 320 = 88.1%. Growth 25%: 21.9 points from new logos, 3.1 points from the net base. A forecast improving churn from 26 to 18 lifts NRR to 105.6% and closing ARR to ₹408 crore.

Connections

Applied in s04 (SaaS ARR waterfall). New ARR is a lagged funnel (c02); churn and pipeline are leading indicators for revenue (c06). The ARR waterfall is structurally identical to the forecast-to-forecast bridge in Topic 4 and to a customer or headcount roll-forward. In valuation (Phase 4), NRR and growth drive SaaS revenue multiples and DCF revenue build-ups; Ind AS 115 governs the revenue recognised from ARR.

Practice Questions

Q1. Roll-forward and retention [L1]

Opening ARR ₹75 crore; new ₹18 crore; expansion ₹9 crore; contraction ₹2 crore; churn ₹6 crore. Compute closing ARR, NRR, GRR and the share of growth from new logos.

Q2. Forecasting churn from a renewal schedule [L2]

Of the opening ARR of ₹150 crore, contracts renewing in FY26 by quarter are: Q1 ₹30 crore, Q2 ₹45 crore, Q3 ₹35 crore, Q4 ₹40 crore. Expected churn at renewal 8% and contraction 3% of renewing ARR; expansion 12% of opening ARR spread evenly; new ARR ₹8 crore per quarter. Build the quarterly roll-forward and compute annual NRR.

💡 Hint: Churn and contraction apply only to the ARR that renews in each quarter.

Q3. ARR to revenue [L2]

Opening ARR ₹60 crore on 1 April. New ARR of ₹24 crore is signed evenly across the year (₹2 crore per month, live from the month after signing); no expansion or churn. Estimate FY recognised subscription revenue and explain why it is lower than both closing ARR and average of opening and closing ARR, if it is.
