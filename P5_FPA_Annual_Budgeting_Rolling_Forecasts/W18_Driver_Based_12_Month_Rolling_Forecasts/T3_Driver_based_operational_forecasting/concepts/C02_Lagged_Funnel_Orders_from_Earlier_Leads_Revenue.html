Key concept

Lagged Funnel: Orders from Earlier Leads, Revenue from AOV

Concept: Orders_t = Leads_(t−lag) × Lead-to-order conversion %; Revenue = Orders × AOV

Statement

\[ O_t = L_{t-k} \times c, \qquad R_t = O_t \times \text{AOV}_t \]

where \( O_t \) is the number of orders won in month \( t \), \( L_{t-k} \) the leads generated \( k \) months earlier, \( k \) the sales-cycle lag in months, \( c \) the lead-to-order conversion rate (product of stage conversions lead→MQL→SQL→order), \( \text{AOV}_t \) the average order value, and \( R_t \) the revenue (or bookings) attributable to month \( t \). With a distributed lag, \( O_t = c \sum_{k} s_k L_{t-k} \), where \( s_k \) is the share of a lead cohort's orders arriving after \( k \) months and \( \sum_k s_k = 1 \).

Explanation

The lag is the essential feature: leads are a leading indicator precisely because they precede orders by the sales cycle. Without the lag, a model would attribute this month's orders to this month's leads, so a marketing push in September would appear to raise September revenue, and a drop in leads would be noticed only when revenue fell.

Conditions and refinements:

  • Lag estimation: from CRM cohort data (time from lead creation to close) or from lagged correlation/regression; lags differ by segment (SME vs enterprise) and lead source.
  • Conversion stability: conversion changes with lead quality, pricing and sales capacity; forecast it by stage where data allow.
  • Order → revenue timing: under Ind AS 115, revenue is recognised when control transfers (delivery, installation, acceptance) or over time for services; add a delivery lag or use the order as a bookings metric and convert to revenue with a recognition profile.
  • Capacity of the sales team: conversion may fall if leads surge beyond sales capacity; cap SQLs at reps × deals per rep.
  • Early months: the formula needs leads from before the forecast window; the model must hold history columns (or the first months' orders come from the existing pipeline).

Excel, leads in row 10 with column offset by Lag:

=IFERROR(INDEX($C$10:$AD$10,XMATCH(F$4,$C$4:$AD$4)-Lag),0)*Conv*AOV

Illustration

Leads: June 1,800, July 2,000, August 1,600. Single lag 2 months; conversion 5%; AOV ₹2.4 lakh.

Month Leads used Orders Revenue (₹ crore)
August June 1,800 90 2.16
September July 2,000 100 2.40
October August 1,600 80 1.92

The August lead drop (−20% vs July) is visible in August but hits revenue in October — giving two months to respond (e.g. accelerate pipeline deals, adjust October inventory or reallocate marketing).

Practice Questions

Q1. Single-lag forecast [L1]

Leads: Sep 3,200, Oct 3,500, Nov 2,900, Dec 3,100. Lag 3 months, conversion 3.5%, AOV ₹1.6 lakh. Compute orders and revenue for each month that can be forecast from these leads.

Q2. Distributed lag and a lead shock [L2]

Leads are 2,500 per month through September; October leads drop to 2,000 and stay there. Conversion 4%; shares: 30% after 1 month, 50% after 2 months, 20% after 3 months; AOV ₹3 lakh. Compute monthly revenue from October to February and identify when revenue reaches its new steady state.

💡 Hint: Each month's orders draw on three earlier lead months with different weights.

Q3. Sales-capacity cap [L2]

Leads are forecast to double in Q3 due to a trade show. Each of 25 sales reps can handle at most 12 SQLs per month; lead→SQL is 15% and SQL→order 25%. With 3,000 leads per month in Q3 (vs 1,500 normal), compute SQLs, capped SQLs and orders, and explain how the cap should be represented in the model.
