Subtopic note

Accuracy Dashboards, Accountability and Tolerance Bands per BU

Scope: Accuracy dashboards, accountability and tolerance bands per BU

Why it matters

Metrics change behaviour only when they are visible, owned and acted on. An accuracy calculation buried in an FP&A workbook does nothing; a monthly dashboard reviewed by the CFO with each BU head, showing WAPE, bias and trend against an agreed tolerance band, changes how forecasts are prepared within two or three cycles. The design questions are practical: which metrics, at which lag and level, what tolerance is "good enough" for a volatile export BU versus a stable domestic one, who is accountable, and what happens when a BU is outside its band. Getting the design right also protects FP&A: it turns arguments about "your forecast was wrong" into a neutral, pre-agreed measurement framework.

Core notes

Dashboard content

A forecast-accuracy dashboard for an Indian mid-cap typically has four layers:

  1. Headline tiles — company bottom-up WAPE and bias % at the primary lag (e.g., lag-3) for revenue and EBITDA, current quarter and rolling 12 months, with arrow vs prior period.
  2. BU league table — for each BU: WAPE, bias %, tracking signal, FVA vs seasonal naive, RAG status against its tolerance band, trend sparkline.
  3. Diagnostics — accuracy by lag (lag-1, -3, -6), by account (revenue, gross margin, opex lines), forecast-triangle view for selected months, top 10 absolute misses with comments.
  4. Process metrics — submission timeliness, number of manual overrides, percentage of lines forecast by statistical method, cycle time.

Layout guidance: put direction (bias) next to size (WAPE) everywhere so they are never read separately; use a diverging colour scale for bias (blue = under-forecast, orange = over-forecast) and a sequential scale for WAPE; show rolling windows to dampen noise; and always label metric definitions, lag and level on the page.

Tolerance bands

A tolerance band is the range of WAPE and bias within which a BU's forecast is considered acceptable. Bands should reflect inherent forecastability, not one number for all:

BU type Example WAPE band (lag-3, monthly) Bias band
Stable, recurring Domestic FMCG staples, SaaS ARR ≤ 3% ±1.5%
Seasonal but predictable Festive-driven consumer durables ≤ 6% ±2.5%
Lumpy B2B / project Capital goods, EPC, exports to few customers ≤ 12% ±4%
New business / launch First 12 months of a new category ≤ 20% (monitor only) ±8%

Ways to set bands:

  • Statistical baseline: band = WAPE of the seasonal-naive or ETS forecast for that BU (the forecast must at least beat naive — FVA ≥ 0), possibly with an improvement target (e.g., naive WAPE × 0.8).
  • Historical percentile: band = the BU's own median WAPE over the last 8–12 quarters, tightened annually.
  • Decision tolerance: band = the error that would change a decision (e.g., ±₹20 crore revenue changes the production plan; ±2% EBITDA changes guidance language).
  • Coefficient of variation: BUs with higher \(\text{CV} = \sigma \div \mu\) of monthly revenue get wider bands.

Bias bands should be tighter relative to WAPE bands, because bias is controllable (it reflects behaviour) while some noise is not.

RAG logic

Typical rules, each BU each month on a rolling-6-month basis:

  • Green: WAPE within band and bias within band and \(\lvert \text{TS} \rvert \le 4\).
  • Amber: either WAPE or bias outside band, or \(\lvert \text{TS} \rvert\) between 3 and 4.
  • Red: both outside, or \(\lvert \text{TS} \rvert > 4\), or amber for three consecutive months.

In Excel:

=IF(OR(AND(W>WBand,ABS(B)>BBand),ABS(TS)>4),"Red",
  IF(OR(W>WBand,ABS(B)>BBand,ABS(TS)>3),"Amber","Green"))

where W is WAPE, B bias %, TS tracking signal, WBand and BBand the BU's bands (looked up from a band table with XLOOKUP). Conditional formatting colours the cell.

Accountability

  • Forecast owner: each BU head (and named account owners for key lines) owns the forecast and the accuracy result; FP&A owns the method and the measurement.
  • Review cadence: monthly MBR (Week 20) includes the accuracy page; quarterly deep-dive with each BU on red/amber items.
  • Required response: a red BU submits a root-cause note (information gap, process error, bias, external shock) and an action plan; repeated red triggers FP&A overlay on that BU's forecast.
  • Incentives: a modest weight (5–15% of variable pay) on forecast accuracy and bias within band, measured at a lag that cannot be gamed by late updates (s02). Pairs with decoupling incentives from self-set forecasts (s04).
  • Recognition: celebrate accuracy improvements, not just beats — otherwise sandbagging is implicitly rewarded.

Worked illustration

Q2 FY26, lag-3, rolling 6 months:

BU WAPE Band Bias % Band TS RAG
Staples 2.4% ≤ 3% −0.8% ±1.5% −1.2 Green
Durables 7.1% ≤ 6% +1.9% ±2.5% +2.1 Amber
Projects 10.5% ≤ 12% −5.6% ±4% +4.6 Red
Exports 13.8% ≤ 12% +6.2% ±4% −3.4 Red

Projects has acceptable WAPE but a persistent under-forecast (TS above +4 under the \(A - F\) convention) — likely sandbagging; Exports is both inaccurate and over-optimistic. Note that bias % (\(F - A\)) and TS (\(A - F\)) have opposite sign conventions — the dashboard footnote must say so.

Build options

  • Excel: long-format accuracy table (BU, Account, Month, Lag, Stage, Forecast, Actual, AbsErr), metric block per BU via SUMIFS, band table, RAG formula, sparklines, slicer on lag via data validation.
  • Power BI (Week 11): Fact table with Lag and Stage; measures for WAPE, bias, TS, FVA; a Band table related to BU; conditional formatting rules on the matrix; drill-through to the forecast triangle and miss commentary.
  • EPM: Anaplan or Adaptive dashboards using stored version snapshots; accuracy usually requires a separate module or data-hub export because native reports compare versions but rarely compute lag-n metrics.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • External shocks. A sudden GST rate change, export ban or monsoon failure can push every BU into red. Allow a documented "exceptional event" flag that removes the event's quantified effect from accountability metrics, but keep the raw metric visible.
  • Bands that never bind. If every BU is green every month, bands are too wide; tighten annually.
  • Gaming via late updates. If accountability uses lag-0 or lag-1 only, BUs simply update late. Fix the KPI lag (s02).
  • Small BUs. Percentage metrics are volatile for small BUs; use absolute ₹ thresholds or combine small BUs for accountability.
  • Cost lines. Apply bands to key cost lines too, otherwise cost padding goes unnoticed.
  • Over-engineering. A dashboard with twenty metrics is not read; keep the headline to WAPE, bias and RAG with diagnostics one click away.

Key takeaways

  • Dashboards must show size (WAPE) and direction (bias) side by side, by BU, at a fixed lag, with trend.
  • Tolerance bands should reflect each BU's forecastability — set from naive/statistical baselines, history, decision thresholds or volatility.
  • Bias bands are tighter than WAPE bands because bias is behavioural and controllable.
  • RAG rules combine WAPE, bias and tracking signal; red status requires a root-cause note and action plan.
  • Accountability: BU owns the forecast, FP&A owns measurement; a modest incentive weight on accuracy discourages sandbagging.

Practice Questions

Q1. Set tolerance bands [L2]

Four BUs have monthly revenue (FY25) with mean and standard deviation (₹ crore): Staples μ 210, σ 9; Durables μ 140, σ 28; Projects μ 75, σ 30; Digital μ 20, σ 6. Seasonal-naive lag-3 WAPE for FY25 was 3.8%, 9.5%, 18.0% and 22.0% respectively. (a) Compute the coefficient of variation for each BU. (b) Propose WAPE and bias tolerance bands using two methods and reconcile them. (c) Justify how you would treat Digital.

💡 Hint: A band at or slightly below the naive WAPE ensures the BU must at least beat the naive benchmark.

Q2. Apply RAG logic [L1]

Using the RAG rules in the notes, classify: (a) WAPE 4.2% (band 5%), bias −3.1% (band ±2%), TS +3.6; (b) WAPE 9.0% (band 8%), bias +1.0% (band ±3%), TS −1.5; (c) WAPE 2.1% (band 3%), bias +0.4% (band ±1.5%), TS +4.4. State the follow-up action required for each.

Q3. Dashboard specification [L3]

Specify a one-page Power BI forecast-accuracy dashboard for the CFO of an NSE-listed consumer company with six BUs: visuals, measures, slicers (lag, account, period), conditional-formatting rules, footnotes, and the drill-through page. Explain how the dashboard avoids each of the four pitfalls in this topic.

Q4. Accountability case [L3]

The Exports BU has been red for four consecutive months with bias % of +7% to +11%. The BU head attributes this to Red Sea shipping disruptions and USD/INR volatility. Describe how you would separate external-shock effects from forecasting behaviour, what changes you would propose to the BU's forecasting process, and whether its tolerance band should be widened.
