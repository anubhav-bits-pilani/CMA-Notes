Subtopic note

Measurement Lag: Lag-1, Lag-3 and Lag-6 Forecast Accuracy

Scope: Measurement lag: compare actuals to forecast made 1, 3 or 6 months earlier (lag-n accuracy)

Why it matters

In a rolling forecast process, every target month is forecast many times: in the Budget a year ahead, in each quarterly forecast and in each monthly LE until the month closes. The closer the forecast is to the month, the more accurate it will be — by the last week of the month most of the revenue is already invoiced. So "how accurate is our September forecast?" has no single answer; it depends on which September forecast. The decisions that matter — committing raw-material purchases, scheduling a plant, hiring sales staff, guiding the market on H2 — are made months in advance. Measuring accuracy at the lag that matches those decisions tells management whether the forecast is fit for purpose; measuring at lag-0 merely tells them that the accountants can add up the month.

Core notes

Definition of lag

For a target month \(m\) and a forecast version with cutoff (last closed actual month) \(c\), the lag is the number of months between the cutoff and the target:

\[ \text{Lag} = (\text{year}_m - \text{year}_c) \times 12 + (\text{month}_m - \text{month}_c) \]

so a forecast made after the June close for September has lag 3. Some organisations instead define lag as the number of months between submission and the target; the convention must be fixed and documented. Under the cutoff convention:

Version (cutoff) Target month Lag
LE after Aug close (31-Aug) Sep 1
Q1F 3+9 (30-Jun) Sep 3
Budget (prepared Feb, effectively cutoff 31-Mar of prior FY) Sep 6
Q2F 6+6 (30-Sep) Dec 3
Q2F 6+6 (30-Sep) Mar 6

In Excel, with cutoff and target month both stored as month-end dates:

=DATEDIF(Cutoff, Target, "m")
=(YEAR(Target)-YEAR(Cutoff))*12 + MONTH(Target)-MONTH(Cutoff)

The second form is more transparent; DATEDIF with two month-ends can misbehave when the earlier date is the 31st and the later month is shorter (e.g., 31-Aug to 30-Sep returns 0 full months), so prefer the arithmetic version.

Choosing the lag

Pick lags that match decision lead times:

  • Lag-1 — short-term cash and working-capital management, monthly guidance to the CFO, accrual estimates. Should be highly accurate; failures signal poor information flow from operations.
  • Lag-3 — quarterly decisions: production plans, procurement of imported inputs with 8–12 week lead times, quarterly results expectation management, A&P phasing. Often the primary KPI.
  • Lag-6 — half-year decisions: capacity, hiring, capex release, H2 guidance, Board and lender communication.
  • Budget accuracy (lag up to 12) — annual, informs how much to trust the AOP for incentive setting.

S&OP practice is to measure at the lag equal to the supply lead time; finance should align so that unit and revenue accuracy can be reconciled.

Data requirements

Lag-n accuracy is impossible unless every forecast version is locked and stored (Topic 4, s04 and c04). The long-format table needs, per row: Version, Cutoff date, Target month, BU, Account, Forecast amount. Actuals are joined by Target month, BU and Account. In a monthly rolling process this creates up to 12 (or 18) forecasts per target month — the "forecast waterfall" or "forecast triangle".

The forecast triangle

Laying out forecasts by version (rows) and target month (columns) shows convergence:

Version \ Target (₹ crore) Jul Aug Sep Oct
Budget (lag 4–7) 128 132 140 150
Q1F, cutoff Jun (lag 1–4) 118 122 131 146
LE Jul, cutoff Jul (lag 1–3) Actual 120 128 143
LE Aug, cutoff Aug (lag 1–2) Actual Actual 126 141
Actual 116 119 125 139

Reading down a column shows how the view of one month evolved; the diagonal at a fixed distance from "Actual" is the lag-n series. For September: lag-6 (Budget) error = 140 − 125 = 15 (over-forecast 12.0%); lag-3 (Q1F) = 6 (4.8%); lag-2 (LE Jul) = 3 (2.4%); lag-1 (LE Aug) = 1 (0.8%).

Worked illustration: lag-3 vs lag-1 WAPE

For Jul–Sep (₹ crore):

Month Actual Lag-3 forecast Lag-1 forecast
Jul 116 122 (LE with Apr cutoff) 118
Aug 119 124 120
Sep 125 131 126

\[ \text{WAPE}_{\text{lag-3}} = \frac{6 + 5 + 6}{360} = 4.7\%, \qquad \text{WAPE}_{\text{lag-1}} = \frac{2 + 1 + 1}{360} = 1.1\% \]

Reporting the lag-1 figure ("98.9% accurate") would hide that the forecast used for procurement was systematically 4–5% high — a bias that inflated imported raw-material inventory.

Reporting

  • Show accuracy by lag as a line chart (lag on the x-axis): a well-functioning process shows a smooth decline in error as lag shortens. A flat line means later forecasts add no information; a sudden drop at lag-1 means operations only reveal information at the last moment.
  • Fix the primary lag for KPI purposes (e.g., lag-3 for revenue, lag-1 for cash) and report others as diagnostics.
  • In Power BI, store Lag as a column and use it as a slicer (Week 11).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Quarterly-only forecasting. If forecasts are produced quarterly, available lags for a given month vary (Q1F gives lag-1 for July but lag-3 for September). Either measure each month at its "natural" lag and weight, or define lag buckets (1–3, 4–6).
  • Budget as a lag. The Budget is not a forecast in the same sense (it is a target with stretch), so its error mixes forecasting skill with target-setting policy. Report budget accuracy separately.
  • Restated actuals. If actuals are restated after a month closes (e.g., late credit notes posted in the next month), decide whether accuracy uses first-reported or final actuals and apply consistently.
  • Rolling 18-month horizons. Lags beyond 12 are available for some months; they are useful for capacity planning but noisy.
  • Calendar mismatch. Monthly LEs prepared mid-month (before close) have an ambiguous cutoff; define their cutoff as the last fully closed month.
  • Partial-month forecasts. A "lag-0" flash forecast for the current month should be excluded from accuracy KPIs or reported only as a close-process metric.

Key takeaways

  • Lag = months between the forecast's cutoff and the target month; define and document the convention.
  • Measure at lags matching decision lead times: lag-1 (cash), lag-3 (procurement, quarterly results), lag-6 (capacity, guidance).
  • Lag-0 or lag-1-only reporting inflates accuracy and hides the bias that hurt real decisions.
  • Lag-n accuracy needs every forecast version locked and stored with its cutoff date.
  • The forecast triangle shows how views of each month converged; error should fall smoothly as lag shortens.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute lags [L1]

For each pair, compute the lag under the cutoff convention: (a) Q1F (cutoff 30-Jun-2025), target 31-Dec-2025; (b) LE with cutoff 31-Oct-2025, target 31-Jan-2026; (c) Budget treated as cutoff 31-Mar-2025, target 30-Sep-2025; (d) Q3F (cutoff 31-Dec-2025), target 30-Jun-2026 in an 18-month rolling forecast. Write an Excel formula that computes the lag from two month-end dates without DATEDIF.

Q2. Forecast triangle analysis [L2]

Forecasts for December 2025 revenue (₹ crore) were: Budget 210; Q1F (cutoff Jun) 198; LE Sep (cutoff Sep) 205; LE Oct 207; LE Nov 202. Actual was 196. (a) Tabulate lag and percentage error, (Forecast − Actual) ÷ Actual, for each. (b) Comment on whether later forecasts added information. (c) Suggest a behavioural explanation for the movement between June and September.

💡 Hint: Look at whether the forecast moved towards or away from the eventual actual as the lag shortened.

Q3. Choosing the KPI lag [L2]

A pharmaceutical formulations company imports APIs with a 10-week lead time, sets quarterly A&P budgets and gives half-yearly guidance to analysts. Recommend the primary and secondary lags for revenue, gross margin and cash-flow accuracy KPIs, and justify each.

Q4. Data model for lag-n accuracy [L3]

Design the table structure (columns and keys) to support lag-1, lag-3 and lag-6 WAPE and bias by BU and account in Power BI, for a company producing monthly LEs from April 2025. Explain how actuals are joined, how the Lag column is derived, how versions with quarterly-only granularity are handled, and how you would prevent a later edit to a locked version from affecting the metrics.
