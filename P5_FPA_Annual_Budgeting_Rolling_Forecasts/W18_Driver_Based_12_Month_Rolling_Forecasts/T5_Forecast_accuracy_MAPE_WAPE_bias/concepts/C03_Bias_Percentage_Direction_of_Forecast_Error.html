Key concept

Bias Percentage: Direction of Forecast Error

Concept: Bias % = Σ (Forecast − Actual) ÷ Σ Actual; positive = over-forecast, negative = under-forecast (revenue sandbagging)

Statement

\[ \text{Bias \%} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} (F_t - A_t)}{\sum_{t=1}^{n} A_t} \times 100 \]

  • \(F_t, A_t\) — forecast and actual for observation \(t\) at a stated lag and grain
  • Positive bias: forecasts exceeded actuals in aggregate — over-forecast (over-optimism for revenue; padding for costs)
  • Negative bias: actuals exceeded forecasts — under-forecast (sandbagging for revenue; under-estimation for costs)
  • Signed errors are summed, so offsetting errors cancel — deliberately, since bias measures the net direction

Explanation

What bias captures. Accuracy metrics (MAD, MAPE, WAPE, RMSE) measure how far forecasts miss; bias measures whether they miss consistently in one direction. A forecast can be unbiased but inaccurate (large errors that offset) or biased but fairly accurate (small, consistent under-forecasts). The two dimensions require different fixes: noise calls for better information or methods; bias calls for recalibration and, frequently, changes in incentives and behaviour (s04).

Sign convention. The brief defines bias as \(F - A\), so positive means over-forecast. Many supply-chain tools define error as \(A - F\), giving the opposite sign, and the tracking signal (c04) uses \(A - F\). Every dashboard must state its convention; a mis-read sign turns sandbagging into optimism.

Interpreting by line type.

Line Negative bias Positive bias
Revenue, volume, margin Sandbagging / conservatism Over-optimism / hockey-stick
Costs, capex Under-estimation (optimism about savings) Padding / cost sandbagging
Cash collections Conservative Optimistic (liquidity risk)

Persistence matters. A single period's bias is weak evidence; rolling-window bias, sign tests and the tracking signal (c04) establish persistence. Typical tolerance bands are ±1.5–4% depending on BU volatility (s06).

Level. Because errors net, company-level bias can be near zero while BUs are strongly biased in opposite directions (s03). Compute bias by BU and by account, then aggregate.

Denominator. Using Σ Actual (not Σ |Actual|) is standard for positive quantities such as revenue. For lines that can be negative, report bias in ₹ (mean error) instead.

Illustration

North BU, six months at lag-3 (₹ crore):

Month Forecast Actual F − A
Apr 46 50 −4
May 48 52 −4
Jun 50 53 −3
Jul 49 54 −5
Aug 51 55 −4
Sep 53 57 −4
Total 297 321 −24

\[ \text{Bias \%} = \frac{-24}{321} \times 100 = -7.5\% \]

WAPE is also 24 ÷ 321 = 7.5% — every error has the same sign, so bias magnitude equals WAPE. This equality is a signature of pure bias with no offsetting noise: the BU's forecasts would be very accurate if simply scaled up by about 8%. Combined with a beat in 6 of 6 months, this is classic revenue sandbagging.

Excel: =(SUM(Fcst)-SUM(Act))/SUM(Act); DAX: Bias % := DIVIDE(SUM(Fact[Forecast]) - SUM(Fact[Actual]), SUM(Fact[Actual])).

Connections

Paired with WAPE (c02) on every dashboard (s06) to fix the "MAPE only" pitfall; the main statistic for detecting sandbagging and over-optimism (s04); its cumulative, noise-scaled cousin is the tracking signal (c04). Bias in human adjustment steps is the usual cause of negative FVA (s05, c06). Links to Topic 2's LE and Ind AS 19 bonus accruals, which are distorted by biased forecasts.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute and classify [L1]

For a cost centre, Apr–Sep FY26 forecasts (₹ lakh) were 95, 98, 100, 102, 104, 106 and actuals 90, 92, 95, 96, 97, 99. Compute bias % and WAPE, classify the behaviour, and suggest one control.

Q2. Bias vs accuracy matrix [L2]

Construct two six-month revenue series (actual and forecast, ₹ crore, actual average about ₹100 crore): one with bias near 0% but WAPE above 10%, and one with bias near −5% and WAPE near 5%. Explain what each pattern implies for the remedial action.

💡 Hint: Offsetting large errors produce low bias; same-sign small errors make bias magnitude approach WAPE.

Q3. Convention trap [L1]

A dashboard shows "Bias −6%" for the West BU. The FP&A system defines error as Actual − Forecast and bias as Σ error ÷ Σ Actual. Is West sandbagging or over-optimistic on revenue? Rewrite the figure under the brief's convention and draft the dashboard footnote that would avoid the confusion.
