Key concept

WAPE and Forecast Accuracy Percentage

Concept: WAPE = Σ |Actual − Forecast| ÷ Σ |Actual|; Forecast accuracy % = 1 − WAPE (floored at 0)

Statement

\[ \text{WAPE} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} \lvert A_t - F_t \rvert}{\sum_{t=1}^{n} \lvert A_t \rvert}, \qquad \text{Forecast accuracy \%} = \max\bigl(0,\ 1 - \text{WAPE}\bigr) \]

  • \(A_t, F_t\) — actual and forecast for observation \(t\) (period, BU, product or account), at a stated lag
  • the numerator is the total absolute error; the denominator is the total absolute actual
  • the floor at zero prevents negative "accuracy" when total absolute error exceeds total actuals

Equivalently, WAPE = MAD ÷ mean absolute actual, and WAPE is MAPE with each APE weighted by \(\lvert A_t \rvert \div \sum \lvert A_t \rvert\).

Explanation

Why weight by actuals? In finance, materiality is measured in rupees. A ₹10 crore miss on a ₹200 crore BU matters more than a ₹50 lakh miss on a ₹1 crore line, even though the latter's percentage error is larger. WAPE gives each observation influence in proportion to its size, so the metric tracks financial impact. It is defined whenever total actuals are non-zero, so individual zero-actual lines (new products, dormant cost centres) do not break it.

Accuracy %. Management prefers "we are 94% accurate" to "WAPE is 6%". The floor at zero matters in volatile lines: if total absolute error is 130% of actuals, accuracy would be −30%, which is meaningless; flooring at 0 shows "no accuracy". Report the unfloored WAPE in diagnostics.

Level and netting. WAPE's value depends on the level at which the absolute value is taken (s03). Taking absolutes at BU-month level and then summing gives a "bottom-up" WAPE that reflects BU decision quality; taking absolutes after summing to company totals gives a "top-down" WAPE that reflects consolidated forecast quality and is always lower or equal. Always state the grain, e.g., "WAPE (BU × month, lag-3)".

Limitations. WAPE has no direction — pair with bias % (c03). It is dominated by the largest lines, so a small BU's poor accuracy hardly moves company WAPE; show BU-level WAPE too. If computed on quarterly totals, it cannot see monthly phasing errors that net within the quarter.

Synonyms. In supply-chain literature WAPE is also called the MAD/Mean ratio or weighted MAPE (wMAPE); some tools define "forecast accuracy" as 1 − WAPE exactly as here.

Illustration

Using the c01 portfolio (₹ lakh): actuals 800, 400, 5, 0; forecasts 760, 420, 20, 3.

\[ \text{WAPE} = \frac{40 + 20 + 15 + 3}{800 + 400 + 5 + 0} = \frac{78}{1{,}205} = 6.5\%, \qquad \text{Accuracy} = 93.5\% \]

Compare MAPE of 103.3% on the same data. A second example: a new BU with actuals ₹4 crore, ₹6 crore and ₹5 crore and forecasts ₹12 crore, ₹14 crore and ₹10 crore gives WAPE = (8 + 8 + 5) ÷ 15 = 140%, so accuracy = max(0, 1 − 1.40) = 0%.

Excel and DAX:

WAPE      =SUM(ABS(Act-Fcst))/SUM(ABS(Act))
Accuracy  =MAX(0, 1-WAPE)

WAPE := DIVIDE( SUMX(Fact, ABS(Fact[Actual] - Fact[Forecast])), SUMX(Fact, ABS(Fact[Actual])) )

The DAX SUMX iterates at the fact-table grain, producing bottom-up WAPE at that grain.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute WAPE and accuracy [L1]

Four BUs, September 2025, lag-3 (₹ crore): A 120 F 112; B 85 F 95; C 40 F 38; D 8 F 15. Compute bottom-up WAPE, accuracy % and MAPE. Then compute top-down WAPE using company totals and explain the difference.

Q2. Floored accuracy [L2]

A start-up BU's quarterly actuals were ₹3 crore, ₹5 crore and ₹7 crore against forecasts of ₹9 crore, ₹10 crore and ₹8 crore. Compute WAPE and accuracy %. Explain why the floor exists and what the dashboard should display for this BU instead of, or alongside, accuracy %.

💡 Hint: Total absolute error exceeds total actuals here.

Q3. Weighting interpretation [L2]

Show algebraically that WAPE equals a weighted average of APEs with weights \(\lvert A_t \rvert \div \sum \lvert A_t \rvert\), and explain in two sentences to a BU head why company WAPE barely moved even though their small BU's APE doubled.
