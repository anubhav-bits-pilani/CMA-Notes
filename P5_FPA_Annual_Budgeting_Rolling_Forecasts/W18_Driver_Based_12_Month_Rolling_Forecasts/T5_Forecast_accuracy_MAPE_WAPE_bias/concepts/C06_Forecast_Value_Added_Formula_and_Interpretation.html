Key concept

Forecast Value Added Formula and Interpretation

Concept: FVA = Error of naive forecast − Error of submitted forecast (positive = process adds value)

Statement

\[ \text{FVA} = E_{\text{naive}} - E_{\text{submitted}} \]

  • \(E_{\text{naive}}\) — error metric (usually WAPE, sometimes MAPE or MAD) of a naive benchmark forecast: last period, seasonal naive, or a simple moving average
  • \(E_{\text{submitted}}\) — the same error metric, at the same lag, grain and period, for the forecast produced by the process (BU submission or final forecast)
  • FVA > 0: the process reduced error versus doing nothing sophisticated — it adds value
  • FVA < 0: the process is worse than the naive benchmark — it destroys value
  • Generalised step-wise: \(\text{FVA}_k = E_{k-1} - E_k\) for each stage \(k\) of the process (s05)

Explanation

The idea. Any forecast process consumes time and attention. The naive forecast is free. FVA asks whether all that effort produced something better than free. It reframes accuracy discussions from "is 7% WAPE good?" (no absolute answer) to "is 7% better than what we would get with no effort?" (a clear benchmark that already reflects how hard the series is to forecast). A volatile export BU with 12% WAPE may be adding a lot of value if seasonal naive gives 20%; a stable domestic BU with 4% WAPE may be destroying value if naive gives 3%.

Choosing the naive benchmark. For monthly financial data with seasonality (Indian festive season, monsoon, March year-end push), the seasonal naive \(F_t = A_{t-12}\) — ideally scaled by YTD growth — is the fair benchmark; last-period naive is too easy to beat in seasonal months and too hard in flat ones. For non-seasonal series (e.g., monthly rent, salaries), last period is appropriate.

Conditions for valid comparison. Same lag (a submission made at lag-1 must not be compared with a naive forecast "made" at lag-3), same actuals, same grain and the same set of observations. Measure over enough periods (6–12 months, many BUs/lines) to distinguish signal from noise.

Interpretation of negative FVA. Usual causes are bias (sandbagging or optimism, s04), anchoring to budget, adding noise by over-reacting to recent months, or poor information. Diagnose with bias % and the tracking signal before concluding that judgement should be removed — sometimes the fix is debiasing, not removal.

Expressing FVA. In percentage points of WAPE (absolute FVA) or as a relative improvement \(\text{FVA} \div E_{\text{naive}}\) (e.g., "the process removes 30% of naive error").

Illustration

Durables BU, monthly revenue at lag-3, rolling 12 months to September 2025:

Forecast Σ|A − F| (₹ crore) Σ A (₹ crore) WAPE
Seasonal naive (scaled) 118 1,640 7.2%
Statistical ETS 92 1,640 5.6%
BU submission 131 1,640 8.0%

\[ \text{FVA}_{\text{BU vs naive}} = 7.2\% - 8.0\% = -0.8 \text{ pp}, \qquad \text{FVA}_{\text{ETS vs naive}} = 7.2\% - 5.6\% = +1.6 \text{ pp} \]

The BU's elaborate bottom-up process is worse than a scaled seasonal-naive forecast and much worse than the statistical model. Its bias % over the period was −6.1%, pointing to sandbagging as the reason. The relative FVA of the statistical model is \(1.6 \div 7.2 = 22\%\) of naive error removed.

Excel, with WAPE values in named cells:

=WAPE_Naive - WAPE_Submitted
=IF(WAPE_Naive - WAPE_Submitted > 0, "Adds value", "Destroys value")

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute FVA [L1]

Over 12 months at lag-2, a BU's seasonal-naive WAPE is 9.4%, the statistical forecast's WAPE is 7.9%, and the BU submission's WAPE is 6.8%. Compute FVA for the submission versus naive, for the statistical forecast versus naive, and step-wise for the BU adjustment versus statistical. Express each also as a relative improvement.

Q2. Unfair comparison [L2]

A BU claims FVA of +4.5 pp, computed by comparing its LE submitted after the 20th of each month (effectively lag-0) with a seasonal-naive forecast at lag-3. Explain why the comparison is invalid and design a fair FVA test, specifying lag, benchmark, metric and evaluation window.

💡 Hint: Both forecasts must have been available at the same point before the target month.

Q3. Negative FVA response [L3]

Three of eight BUs show negative FVA versus seasonal naive over 12 months; two of them also show bias below −5%. Draft recommendations for the CFO covering process changes, incentive changes and whether to replace BU forecasts with statistical forecasts for some lines, with criteria for each decision.
