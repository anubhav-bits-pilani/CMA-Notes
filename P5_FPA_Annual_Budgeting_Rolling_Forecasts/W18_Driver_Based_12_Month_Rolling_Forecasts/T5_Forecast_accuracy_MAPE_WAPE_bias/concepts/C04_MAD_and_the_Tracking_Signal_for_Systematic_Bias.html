Key concept

MAD and the Tracking Signal for Systematic Bias

Concept: MAD = Σ |A − F| ÷ n; Tracking signal = Σ (A − F) ÷ MAD; |TS| > 4 signals systematic bias

Statement

\[ \text{MAD} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n} \lvert A_t - F_t \rvert, \qquad \text{TS} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} (A_t - F_t)}{\text{MAD}} \]

  • \(A_t, F_t\) — actual and forecast in period \(t\), at a fixed lag
  • \(n\) — number of periods in the evaluation window (cumulative from a start date, or rolling)
  • the numerator of TS is the running sum of forecast errors (RSFE), with error defined as \(A - F\)
  • decision rule: \(\lvert \text{TS} \rvert > 4\) indicates systematic bias (some organisations use ±3.75 or ±6); under this convention, TS > +4 means persistent under-forecast and TS < −4 persistent over-forecast

Explanation

MAD is the average absolute miss in rupees (or units). It is robust to small denominators, easy to explain, and serves as a scale for the typical size of error. It is not comparable across BUs of different size; for that use WAPE (c02, which equals MAD ÷ mean actual).

Tracking signal logic. If errors are random (unbiased), positive and negative errors offset and the RSFE wanders around zero; dividing by MAD expresses it in "typical error" units, so TS stays within a few units. If errors are persistently one-signed, the RSFE grows roughly linearly — after \(n\) same-sign errors of similar size, RSFE ≈ \(n \times \text{MAD}\), so TS ≈ \(n\). Hence TS crossing ±4 corresponds to roughly four periods' worth of accumulated one-directional error: strong evidence of bias rather than noise. The ±4 threshold originates in inventory-control practice, where (with MAD ≈ 0.8σ for normally distributed errors) it corresponds to a statistically unusual cumulative error.

Why TS adds to bias %. Bias % (c03) measures the size of the net error relative to volume; TS measures its persistence relative to noise. A BU with bias −2% but very low noise can have a high TS (reliably biased); a BU with bias −2% but large noise has a low TS (not yet conclusive).

Practical use.

  • Compute TS monthly per BU and key line on a cumulative-since-reset or rolling 12-month basis; plot it as a control chart with ±4 limits.
  • Reset the RSFE after a recalibration or process change so that old bias does not mask improvement.
  • Investigate any breach: is it an external shock (one large error) or a pattern (many same-sign errors)? A single huge error can also push TS outside limits — inspect the error series.

Sign convention. TS uses \(A - F\); bias % uses \(F - A\). A sandbagging revenue BU therefore shows negative bias % and positive TS. State both conventions on the dashboard (s06).

Illustration

West BU monthly revenue, lag-3 (₹ crore):

Month A F A − F RSFE |A − F| MAD (cum.) TS
Apr 82 80 2 2 2 2.00 1.0
May 85 81 4 6 4 3.00 2.0
Jun 79 82 −3 3 3 3.00 1.0
Jul 88 83 5 8 5 3.50 2.3
Aug 90 85 5 13 5 3.80 3.4
Sep 92 87 5 18 5 4.00 4.5

By September, TS = 18 ÷ 4.0 = 4.5 > 4: actuals have persistently exceeded forecasts, signalling revenue under-forecasting (possible sandbagging). Bias % for the half year = (498 − 516) ÷ 516 = −3.5%. The June miss in the other direction delayed but did not prevent the signal.

Excel (cumulative, row 6 onward, errors in column E, absolute errors in G):

RSFE  =SUM($E$6:E6)
MAD   =AVERAGE($G$6:G6)
TS    =IFERROR(F6/H6, 0)
Flag  =IF(ABS(I6)>4, "Bias alert", "")

Connections

MAD belongs to the s01 metric set and underpins RMSE comparisons (c05). TS is a key detector of sandbagging and over-optimism (s04) and a component of the RAG rules in s06. It requires a consistent lag (s02) and locked forecast versions (Topic 4, c04). Statistical forecasting (Topic 3, s07) uses TS to decide when an exponential-smoothing model needs re-fitting.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute MAD and TS [L1]

Monthly errors (A − F, ₹ lakh) for a cost centre are: −6, −4, −7, −3, −5, −6, −4, −5. Compute cumulative RSFE, MAD and TS after each month, identify the month in which the ±4 threshold is first crossed, and interpret the direction for a cost line.

💡 Hint: Negative A − F on a cost line means actual spend was below forecast.

Q2. Noise vs persistence [L2]

BU X has six monthly errors (A − F, ₹ crore) of +1, +1, +1, +1, +1, +1; BU Y has +12, −10, +9, −11, +8, −3. Compute bias in ₹, MAD and TS for each. Explain which BU has the bigger forecasting problem and which has the more systematic one.

Q3. Single-shock breach [L2]

A BU's TS jumps from 1.2 to 4.8 in one month after an unforecast plant shutdown created a ₹30 crore miss (MAD previously ₹4 crore). Explain why the TS breached, whether it indicates systematic bias, and how you would adjust the tracking (exceptional-event flag, reset, or rolling window).
