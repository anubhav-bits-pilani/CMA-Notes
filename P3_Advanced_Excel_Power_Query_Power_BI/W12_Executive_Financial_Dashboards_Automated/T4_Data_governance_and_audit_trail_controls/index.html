Overview

An executive finance pack is a reporting system, and like any system that produces numbers for the board it needs controls. This topic turns the auditor's instinct — "agree it to the TB, check completeness, check cut-off, check who can change it" — into measures, queries and Power BI Service settings that run on every refresh. The deliverable is a control panel page in the Week 12 Executive Finance Pack that proves, each time new files arrive, that the dashboard is arithmetically sound, complete, current and tied to the books; plus the access and change-control settings that keep it that way.

For the target roles this is a differentiator. FP&A and reporting interviews in India increasingly probe "how do you know your dashboard is right?" and "who can see BU-level salary data?". Listed companies are subject to internal financial controls (IFC) reporting under the Companies Act 2013 and CFO certification under SEBI LODR, and statutory auditors test IT general controls over systems that feed reported numbers. A candidate who can show Dr = Cr, BS and tie-out checks by month, an unmapped-ledger exception list, an IST refresh stamp, dynamic RLS and a Git-based change process is speaking the controller's language as well as the BI developer's.

The learning sequence runs from data to people. Start with arithmetic controls (s01: Dr = Cr, TB nets to zero, A = L + E), then agreement to the books (s02: GL-to-dashboard tie-out against ERP control totals), then completeness (s03: unmapped ledgers, row counts per file and branch, missing months) and freshness (s04: refresh time, latest posting date, list of source files). Bring these together on a control panel page (s05). Finally, govern who can see and change the product: access (s06: RLS, workspace roles, sensitivity labels, endorsement, lineage) and change control (s07: PBIP in Git, measure documentation, deployment pipelines). The concepts c01–c06 are the exact measures and M patterns you will type.

Related concepts to study

IFC and the Companies Act audit-trail requirement

→ Week 1–4: IFC (Companies Act s.134(5)(e), s.143(3)(i)) and the audit-trail requirement under Rule 3(1), Companies (Accounts) Rules

Section 134(5)(e) requires directors of listed companies to state that internal financial controls are laid down, adequate and operating effectively; s.143(3)(i) requires the auditor to report on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of IFC over financial reporting (guided by the ICAI Guidance Note on Audit of IFCoFR). The proviso to Rule 3(1) of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014, effective from 1 April 2023, requires accounting software with an audit trail (edit log) of each change that cannot be disabled, and Rule 11(g) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules requires the auditor to report on it. The BI layer is not "books of account", but controls in this topic — tie-outs, file lists, change logs — are the evidence that the reporting layer faithfully reflects the audited books, and they slot directly into an entity's IFC documentation (risk-control matrix).

Forensic red flags and data-integrity tests

→ Week 5–8: forensic red flags and data-integrity tests

The forensic tests from Weeks 5–8 — weekend and holiday postings, round-sum entries, entries posted by unusual users, manual journals just below approval thresholds, back-dated vouchers, Benford's-law digit distributions, duplicate invoice numbers — are natural extensions of the control page. Where s01–s04 ask "is the data complete and consistent?", forensic tests ask "is anything in it suspicious?". Implement them as additional exception tables (e.g. COUNTROWS(FILTER(GL, WEEKDAY(GL[PostingDate], 2) > 5 && GL[VoucherType] = "Journal"))) with drill-through to voucher detail. Keep them as WARN-level analytics for the controller, not hard pass/fail controls, and remember that the entry date versus voucher date distinction (useful for back-dating tests) needs both fields in the extract.

Lineage view and impact analysis

Power BI Service lineage view and impact analysis before changing shared models

Lineage view shows each workspace item and its upstream and downstream dependencies: SharePoint or gateway data sources, dataflows, semantic models, reports, dashboards and apps, including links into other workspaces. Impact analysis on a semantic model lists every dependent report and dashboard (with view counts) and lets you email their owners before a change. For a shared GL model feeding the Power BI pack and several Excel cube workbooks, run impact analysis before renaming measures, changing relationships, or altering RLS. Note that Excel workbooks connected via Analyze in Excel may not all appear; maintain a register of known Excel consumers too. This links to s06 (access) and s07 (change control).

Purview sensitivity labels and DLP

Microsoft Purview sensitivity labels and DLP policies

Sensitivity labels (configured in Microsoft Purview by the tenant's compliance admins) classify Power BI items as, for example, Internal, Confidential or Highly Confidential – UPSI, and the label with its encryption follows data exported to Excel, PowerPoint or PDF. Data loss prevention (DLP) policies for Power BI can detect sensitive information types (such as PAN or Aadhaar patterns) or specific labels in semantic models and raise policy tips or alerts; Microsoft 365 DLP on Exchange and SharePoint can then block emailing a labelled export outside the organisation. Availability depends on licensing (Microsoft 365 E5/Purview add-ons) and admin configuration. For quiet-period results data, labels plus DLP complement SEBI PIT obligations on handling UPSI.

Excel "All checks OK" flag

Excel equivalent: check cells rolled up into a single "All checks OK" flag

In Excel models and cube packs, each control is a check cell returning a difference (e.g. =ROUND(TotalDr-TotalCr,2)), and a summary cell rolls them up: =IF(SUMPRODUCT(--(ABS(Checks)>=1))=0,"All checks OK","CHECK FAILURES: "&SUMPRODUCT(--(ABS(Checks)>=1))), where Checks is a named range. Place the flag at the top of the cover sheet and in the print header, and use conditional formatting to colour it. With cube formulas, check cells can read the same model measures (=CUBEVALUE(conn,"[Measures].[BS Check]",Slicer_FY)), so Excel and Power BI report the same control results — a direct application of the single-measure-library principle of this week.

Common pitfalls

Controls only at grand-total level

Running controls only at grand-total level, which misses errors that offset across months or branches

The mistake is a single card "Dr = Cr: PASS" or "Revenue ties: PASS" evaluated for the full year and group. People do it because it is the simplest visual and because the annual figure is what they remember checking in Excel. But a voucher dated in the wrong month, or a branch file loaded into the wrong company, nets to zero at total while distorting monthly variance and BU results. The correct treatment is to evaluate each control by period and by entity (month × company or branch grids) and to count failing cells. In review, ask "show me this control for each month" — if the pack cannot, it is not controlled.

Missing or excessive tolerance

No defined tolerance (paise rounding), giving false FAILs, or a tolerance so wide it hides errors

Comparisons with = 0 fail on floating-point residues of ₹0.0000001 from CSV parsing or decimal-number columns, so users learn to ignore red; conversely, a tolerance of ₹10,000 "to stop the noise" hides real errors. The cause is not defining tolerance as a documented parameter of each control. Correct practice: type amounts as Fixed decimal (Currency.Type) in Power Query, use a tolerance of ₹1 (or zero for counts), store tolerances in the Controls table with an owner, and show the actual difference beside the status. Review by checking the largest "passing" difference and any tolerance above ₹1.

LocalNow stamps in UTC

DateTime.LocalNow() returns UTC in the Service, so refresh stamps are off unless converted to IST

Builders test in Desktop on an IST laptop, see the correct time, and publish. In the Service, refreshes run on UTC servers, so the stamp is 5 hours 30 minutes behind — an overnight 06:30 IST refresh shows as 01:00, which misleads anyone judging whether the morning's files were included. Use DateTimeZone.SwitchZone(DateTimeZone.UtcNow(), 5, 30) (c05) and label the stamp "IST". To detect the problem, compare the stamp after the first Service refresh with the refresh history in semantic model settings.

Unreviewed control page

A control page that is hidden or never reviewed, with no owner signing off on failures

A control page is often built for the demo, then hidden "so the CFO isn't confused", and nobody opens it again. Controls without review are not controls — auditors test operating effectiveness, which needs evidence of review. Make reviewing the page a defined close step with a named owner per control, record sign-off (SharePoint list loaded back into the model), surface an overall status card on the cover page, and alert owners on failures via data alerts and Power Automate. Detect this pitfall by asking for last month's sign-off evidence and how a FAIL was resolved.

Practice Questions

Q1. Designing the control layer for Project 3 [L3]

A ₹4,800 crore NSE-listed auto-components group has three companies and 11 branches. Monthly Tally (two companies) and SAP (one company) GL extracts land in a SharePoint folder; a CoA mapping is maintained in Excel; ERP P&L and TB reports for each company and month are exported to a /Controls/ folder at close.

(a) List every control you would build (accuracy, tie-out, completeness, freshness), with the measure or query name, the filter context it must use, its tolerance and its owner. (b) Sketch the control panel page layout, including summary card, control table, period × entity grids and exception lists. (c) Explain how the control results would appear in the Excel cube board pack.

Q2. Investigating a failed refresh validation [L2]

After dropping the April 2026 files and refreshing, the control page shows: Dr = Cr PASS for April; BS Check −₹1,26,40,000 at 30 April; Revenue tie-out FAIL for April by −₹1,26,40,000; Unmapped Count 2; Missing branch files 0; Latest GL posting date 30-Apr-2026; refresh stamp 01:12.

(a) Explain what each result tells you and propose the single most likely root cause linking the BS and revenue failures. (b) Identify the separate problem with the refresh stamp and its fix. (c) Describe the steps, in order, to resolve and re-validate, and what you would record in the sign-off log.

💡 Hint: Compare the unmapped amount with the two failing differences before looking elsewhere.

Q3. Governance for BU-wise access [L3]

The pack must be distributed to the CFO, two regional CFOs (three BUs each), eight BU heads (one BU each) and the group reporting team. BU heads must not see other BUs or the salary detail table. Quarterly results data is UPSI until board approval.

(a) Design the workspace structure, roles, App audiences and RLS (static or dynamic, with DAX). (b) Explain how you would hide the salary table and how RLS affects the control page for each audience. (c) Specify the sensitivity label, endorsement status and any DLP policy for the model, and the evidence of access review you would provide to the statutory auditor for IFC.

Q4. Change control for a remapping [L2]

The CFO requests that "Packing material" ledgers (₹3.8 crore YTD across 6 months) move from Other expenses to Cost of materials consumed, and that EBITDA be redefined to exclude ₹45 lakh of rental income currently in Other income.

(a) Describe the change process from request to Production using PBIP in Git and a deployment pipeline, including review artefacts. (b) State the effect of each change on gross margin, EBITDA and PBT for the six months, and what must be communicated about previously presented figures. (c) Identify which Excel cube workbooks or reports could break and how you would find them beforehand.

Q5. Interview case: defending a dashboard number [L2]

An interviewer points at an EBITDA card showing ₹612.4 crore for FY2025-26 and asks: "Prove to me this number is right and current." Structure a two-minute answer that walks from the card to the control page, the tie-out to ERP, the completeness checks, the refresh metadata and the change log, naming the specific measures you would show.
