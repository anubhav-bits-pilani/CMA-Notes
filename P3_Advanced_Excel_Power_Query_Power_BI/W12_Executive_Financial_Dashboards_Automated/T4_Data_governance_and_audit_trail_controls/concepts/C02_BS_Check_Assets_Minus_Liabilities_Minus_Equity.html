Key concept

BS Check: Assets Minus Liabilities Minus Equity Equals Zero

Concept: BS Check = [Total Assets] - [Total Liabilities] - [Total Equity], which should be 0 at every month-end

Statement

BS Check = [Total Assets] - [Total Liabilities] - [Total Equity]

\[ \text{BS Check}_t = A_t - L_t - E_t = 0 \quad \text{for every month-end } t \]

where \(A_t\), \(L_t\) and \(E_t\) are total assets, total liabilities and total equity at month-end \(t\), each expressed as a positive amount (assets as debit balances, liabilities and equity as credit balances with the sign reversed), and \(E_t\) includes profit for the period not yet closed to retained earnings.

Explanation

On a balanced TB, the BS identity fails only for presentation reasons: a ledger is unmapped or mapped to the wrong statement, signs are inconsistent, or current-period profit is missing from equity. That is why this check complements Dr = Cr — it tests the CoA mapping and the measure logic, not just the data.

Implementation points:

  • Closing balances are cumulative. [Total Assets] must sum all postings up to the selected month-end (including the opening-balance voucher), not just movements in the month: e.g. CALCULATE(SUM(GL[Amount]), CoA[Side] = "Asset", 'Date'[Date] <= MAX('Date'[Date])) with the Date filter replaced by that condition (use REMOVEFILTERS('Date') plus the condition, or DATESBETWEEN).
  • Profit for the period. Until the year-end closing entry, P&L ledgers hold the current year's result. Equity must include YTD profit: \(E_t = \text{Share capital} + \text{Other equity (opening)} + \text{YTD profit}_t\). After closing, do not add it again.
  • Sign handling. On a Dr-positive signed model, liabilities and equity sums are negative; negate them in their measures so the identity reads naturally. A sign mistake doubles the liability rather than cancelling it — an easy tell is a BS Check equal to twice total liabilities.
  • Every month-end, not just the selected one. Show the check in a matrix with month-ends on columns; a mapping error that appears only after a ledger's first posting in August would be invisible at April.
  • Ind AS presentation choices do not affect the identity: netting deferred tax assets and liabilities (Ind AS 12) or presenting contract assets and liabilities (Ind AS 115) move amounts between lines but must leave A = L + E. If a netting rule is applied in DAX to only one side, the check will expose it.
  • Tolerance. Use ₹1, as with c01. A non-zero check must never be "absorbed" into a suspense line on the BS page.

Illustration

Signed TB of a Gujarat specialty-chemicals company at 31 December 2025 (₹ crore, Dr positive):

Group Signed balance
Asset ledgers (mapped) 1,284.60
Liability ledgers −612.30
Equity ledgers (share capital + opening other equity) −548.90
P&L ledgers (Apr–Dec profit) −123.40
TB total 0.00

Total Assets = 1,284.60; Total Liabilities = 612.30; Total Equity = 548.90 + 123.40 = 672.30; BS Check = 1,284.60 − 612.30 − 672.30 = 0. Omitting the YTD profit gives a BS Check of ₹123.40 crore — exactly the period's profit, which is the diagnostic signature of a missing profit plug.

Practice Questions

Q1. Diagnosing a failing check [L2]

At 30 June 2025 the BS Check shows ₹47,80,000. Unmapped Count is 0, Dr = Cr passes, and YTD profit is ₹9.2 crore. List three possible causes and the measure or query you would use to test each.

💡 Hint: Look for ledgers whose CoA Statement attribute is inconsistent with their nature, and for sign conventions.

Q2. Year-end closing entry [L2]

On 31 March 2026 the accountant posts the closing entry transferring FY2025-26 profit of ₹212 crore from P&L ledgers to Retained earnings. Explain what happens to the BS Check for April 2026 if [Total Equity] always adds YTD profit of all P&L ledgers regardless of FY, and rewrite the profit component correctly.

Q3. Interview: why both checks? [L1]

Explain why a model needs both a TB-nets-to-zero check and a BS check, using one example of an error that only the BS check catches.
