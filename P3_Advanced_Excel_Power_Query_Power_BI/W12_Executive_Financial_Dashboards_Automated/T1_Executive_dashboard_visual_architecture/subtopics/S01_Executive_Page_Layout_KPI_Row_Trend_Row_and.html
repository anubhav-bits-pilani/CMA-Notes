Subtopic note

Executive Page Layout: KPI Row, Trend Row and Variance Row

Scope: Page layout: a top row of KPI cards (Revenue, EBITDA %, PAT, Cash, Net Debt), then a trend row, then a variance/detail row

Why it matters

A CFO opens the executive page for perhaps thirty seconds before a review meeting. In that time the page must answer "good month or bad month?", "is it a one-off or a trend?" and "where exactly is the problem?". The three-row layout maps one row to each of those questions. It is also the layout interviewers expect when they ask you to sketch a finance dashboard on a whiteboard, and it is the structure that survives export to PDF or PowerPoint for the board. Getting the layout right first prevents the most common failure of finance dashboards: a page full of individually correct visuals that together say nothing.

Core notes

The reading pattern

Executives scan a page in a Z or F pattern: top-left first, across, then down. The layout therefore places the most aggregated, most decision-relevant numbers top-left and the most granular content bottom-right. Each row answers a different question at a different level of detail:

Row Question answered Typical visuals Grain
1. KPI row Where are we, versus plan and last year? 5 cards with reference labels Selected period, single number
2. Trend row Is this a pattern or a blip? Line/column combo, Rolling 12M line Month over 12–24 months
3. Variance/detail row What drove it, and where? EBITDA waterfall, P&L matrix, diverging bars Line item / BU

Row 1: the five KPI cards

The five headline KPIs are chosen because together they cover growth, profitability, bottom line, liquidity and leverage:

  • Revenue — revenue from operations (Schedule III line), not including other income. Shown for the selected month or YTD, in ₹ crore.
  • EBITDA % — EBITDA divided by revenue from operations. A margin rather than an absolute amount, because the margin is what the CFO compares across months and to guidance. Define EBITDA once in the measure library (with or without other income) and print the definition in the tooltip or user guide.
  • PAT — profit for the period after tax, attributable to owners if the pack is consolidated with non-controlling interests.
  • Cash — closing cash and cash equivalents (Ind AS 7 definition) as at the period end. This is a semi-additive measure: never summed over months.
  • Net Debt — closing borrowings (non-current, current, and current maturities) less closing cash and cash equivalents; the treatment of other bank balances and liquid mutual funds is a stated policy.

Five is not magic but it is a sensible upper bound for a single row on a 16:9 canvas: each card gets about 230 pixels of width on a 1280-pixel canvas, which leaves room for the number, a comparison line and a small status indicator. If the business needs a sixth KPI (say, cash conversion cycle), it moves to Page 2 rather than squeezing the row.

Row 2: the trend row

The trend row puts the selected period into context. The usual pair is a monthly combo chart for the KPI that matters most (revenue or EBITDA: Actual columns, Budget line, PY line) and a Rolling 12M line that removes seasonality. The trend row must respect the same slicers as the KPI row but show more months: the measures use the selected month as the end point and look back (see s03). The x-axis is the fiscal month (Apr → Mar), sorted by 'Date'[FiscalMonthNo], never alphabetically.

Row 3: the variance/detail row

The bottom row explains the variance shown in the KPI row. A Budget-to-Actual EBITDA waterfall on the left shows which lines moved EBITDA; a compact P&L matrix (Actual, Budget, Var, Var %, PY) or diverging variance bars on the right shows the same story in tabular form. This row is where users right-click to drill through to the expense page or the GL detail page.

Grid, spacing and alignment

Build on a grid: for a 1280 × 720 canvas, use 16-pixel outer margins and 8- or 12-pixel gutters. A practical allocation is a 40-pixel title/status band, KPI row about 120 px high, trend row about 230 px and detail row about 290 px. Use Format > Align and Distribute horizontally on the selection, and lock objects (View > Lock objects) once placed. Slicers for FY, BU and Region sit either in the title band or in a narrow left rail — the same position on every page so sync looks natural.

Title and status band

Above row 1, a title using a dynamic measure ("Executive Overview – " & [Selected FY] & " · " & [Selected Month]), the slicers, and a status strip: control status (PASS/FAIL), last refresh time in IST, and data-as-of date. This keeps governance visible without a separate page visit.

Worked illustration

Sahyadri Chemicals, October 2025, YTD view:

Card Actual Reference label
Revenue ₹712.4 Cr vs Budget −3.1% · vs PY +8.4%
EBITDA % 15.9% vs Budget −0.8 pp · vs PY +0.6 pp
PAT ₹58.3 Cr vs Budget −₹6.1 Cr
Cash ₹84.6 Cr vs Mar-25 −₹12.0 Cr
Net Debt ₹226.9 Cr vs Budget +₹18.4 Cr (adverse)

The trend row shows revenue below budget only from August (monsoon-delayed agro sales), and the waterfall shows the EBITDA shortfall coming mostly from volume in Agro and power cost in Performance Polymers. The three rows tell one story.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Month vs YTD on cards. Mixing month cards and YTD trend confuses readers. Either add a bookmark toggle (Month/YTD) that switches all cards together, or show YTD on cards and month in the trend.
  • Balance KPIs and period selection. When a user selects a quarter, Cash and Net Debt must show the closing balance at quarter-end, while Revenue shows the quarter flow. Label them "as at" vs "for the period".
  • Consolidated vs standalone. If the model holds both, a slicer or page-level filter must make the entity explicit on the title band.
  • Negative KPIs. A loss-making month makes PAT negative; the card format must show a clear minus sign or brackets consistently with tables (s06).
  • Ratios at total level. EBITDA % across BUs must be recomputed from totals, never averaged across BU percentages.
  • Detail creeping into row 1. Resist adding a sparkline, a gauge and a table to each card; the KPI row is for numbers and comparisons only.

Key takeaways

  • Three rows answer three questions: where are we, is it a trend, what drove it.
  • Five KPI cards (Revenue, EBITDA %, PAT, Cash, Net Debt) cover growth, margin, profit, liquidity and leverage.
  • Balance KPIs (Cash, Net Debt) are closing positions; flow KPIs follow the period selection.
  • Use a pixel grid, consistent gutters and identical slicer positions across pages.
  • A status band with control status and IST refresh time belongs on the page, not hidden elsewhere.
  • Every row must be driven by the same slicers and the same measure library.

Practice Questions

Q1. Allocate the canvas [L1]

For a 1280 × 720 canvas with 16 px outer margins and 12 px gutters, compute the width of each of five equal KPI cards in row 1, and propose heights for the title band and three rows that sum to the available height.

Q2. Choosing the fifth KPI [L2]

A CFO of an NBFC-style equipment-finance subsidiary asks you to replace "Net Debt" on the KPI row with "Gross NPA %". Explain whether the three-row layout still works, what changes in the trend and variance rows, and what you would move to Page 2.

Q3. Card values for a quarter [L2]

Q3 FY2025-26 (Oct–Dec) data: revenue ₹102.1 Cr, ₹96.8 Cr and ₹110.4 Cr; EBITDA ₹16.0 Cr, ₹14.9 Cr, ₹18.6 Cr; closing cash ₹71.2 Cr, ₹66.5 Cr, ₹88.9 Cr; closing borrowings ₹298.0 Cr, ₹305.4 Cr, ₹301.7 Cr. State what each of the five cards should display when Q3 is selected (PAT not needed) and which DAX pattern each requires.

💡 Hint: Separate flow measures from snapshot measures before you add anything up.

Q4. Critique a layout [L3]

A draft executive page has: a gauge for revenue, a donut of expenses by type, a 40-row P&L table at the top, a map of regions and four cards with no comparisons. Write a review note to the analyst that restructures the page into the three-row pattern, specifying each visual, its measures and its comparison basis.
