Subtopic note

Waterfall Bridges: EBITDA, Revenue and Cash Flow

Scope: Waterfalls: Budget-to-Actual EBITDA bridge, PY-to-CY revenue bridge, cash flow bridge

Why it matters

"What drove the variance?" is the CFO's second question after "are we on budget?". A bridge (waterfall) answers it in one picture: start value, the drivers that moved it up or down, end value. Boards see three standard bridges: Budget-to-Actual EBITDA, PY-to-CY revenue (growth decomposition) and a cash flow bridge from opening to closing cash (or Net Debt). Analysts in FP&A spend a large part of month-end building these; in Power BI, a well-designed bridge is a measure plus a small steps table that refreshes automatically.

Core notes

Anatomy of a bridge

Every bridge has the identity:

\[ \text{End} = \text{Start} + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \Delta_i \]

where Start is the opening value (Budget EBITDA, PY revenue, opening cash), each \(\Delta_i\) is a driver contribution (positive = increase), and End is the closing value (Actual EBITDA, CY revenue, closing cash). If the steps do not sum exactly, the bridge is wrong — there must be no unexplained "other" except a deliberately labelled residual.

1. Budget-to-Actual EBITDA bridge

Start = Budget EBITDA, End = Actual EBITDA, steps = variance by P&L line, sign-adjusted so that a favourable variance increases EBITDA:

Step Variance (Actual − Budget), EBITDA impact
Revenue from operations Actual revenue − Budget revenue
Material cost (COGS) −(Actual COGS − Budget COGS)
Employee benefits −(Actual − Budget)
Power and fuel −(Actual − Budget)
Other expenses −(Actual − Budget)

With expenses stored as positive debits, a cost overrun is a negative EBITDA step. Using the Week 11 signed measure ([Actual Signed] with SignFactor), the step is simply [Actual Signed] - [Budget Signed] for each line. Later (Weeks 17–20), the revenue step splits into price, volume and mix; the material step into price and usage.

2. PY-to-CY revenue bridge

Start = PY revenue, End = CY revenue. Steps can be by BU/region (where growth came from) or by driver (volume, price, mix, FX, new products, acquisitions). The BU version is purely data-driven: step = CY − PY per BU. The driver version requires volume and price data (Weeks 17–20). For a listed company, an acquisitions step separates organic growth — important for investor commentary.

3. Cash flow bridge

Start = opening cash and cash equivalents (or opening Net Debt), End = closing. Steps follow the indirect-method logic of Ind AS 7:

Step Source
EBITDA P&L
Change in NWC (receivables, inventory, payables) Balance sheet movements, increase in assets negative
Taxes paid Cash flow / tax ledgers
Capex Additions to PPE and CWIP net of disposals
Interest paid Finance cost cash portion
Borrowings raised / repaid Movement in borrowings
Dividends paid Equity ledgers

A Net Debt bridge (opening Net Debt → closing Net Debt) is often preferred by CFOs because it is unaffected by moving cash between debt repayment and deposits; in that case debt movements are not steps, and free cash flow reduces Net Debt.

Implementation options in Power BI

There are two patterns (detailed in Concept c01):

  1. Native waterfall with Category and Breakdown. Category = a step field (e.g. FS line), Y-axis = variance measure. The visual adds an automatic Total bar. With Breakdown = a driver field and Category = scenario (Budget, Actual), the visual computes the bridge from Budget to Actual by breakdown items, and shows the top N drivers with the rest grouped as "Other".
  2. Disconnected bridge-step table + SWITCH measure. A small table Bridge Steps (StepNo, Step, Type) lists Start and each driver (the visual's own Total bar is End). One measure returns the right number for each step. This gives exact control over order, labels, subtotal points and residual lines.

The table pattern is usually right for board bridges because the order of steps is fixed by policy, not by magnitude.

Formatting

  • Sentiment colours: Increase green, Decrease red, Total dark grey/blue (Columns > Colors). For cost-oriented bridges, increase must still mean "favourable to EBITDA", which the sign-adjusted measure ensures.
  • Data labels on every bar in ₹ crore, one decimal.
  • Sort by the StepNo column (Sort axis by StepNo ascending), never by value, so the bridge reads Budget → drivers → Actual.
  • Category labels short ("Revenue", "Power", "Other opex"); full names in tooltips.

Worked illustration: Budget-to-Actual EBITDA, October 2025 YTD (₹ Cr)

Step Amount
Budget EBITDA 122.6
Revenue variance −22.8
Material cost saving (lower volume and better yields) +16.1
Employee cost overrun −1.4
Power and fuel overrun −4.2
Other expenses saving +2.9
Actual EBITDA 113.2

Check: \(122.6 - 22.8 + 16.1 - 1.4 - 4.2 + 2.9 = 113.2\). The Actual EBITDA bar must equal the Page 1 card and the P&L matrix; in the model this is enforced by a check measure [Bridge Check] = [Bridge End] - [EBITDA].

Note that the revenue step here is the full revenue variance (₹712.4 Cr vs ₹735.2 Cr), so the material step is the full material cost variance, part of which is simply lower volume. If the CFO prefers a contribution-based revenue step (revenue shortfall × budget contribution margin), the material step must then contain only price and usage variances. Mixing a contribution-based revenue step with a full material cost step double-counts the volume effect.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Steps that do not add up. If one P&L line is missing from the step table (for example, a new CoA FSLine "Exchange loss on operations"), the bridge silently breaks. Add a residual step computed as End − Start − sum of named steps and make the check measure fail if the residual exceeds tolerance.
  • Too many steps. More than 8–10 bars become unreadable; group small items into "Other" in the step table.
  • Period selection. Month bridge vs YTD bridge must be explicit; use the same Month/YTD toggle as the cards.
  • Negative start or end. A loss-making budget (negative EBITDA) shows the total bar below zero; the native visual handles it, but labels must show the minus sign.
  • Cash bridge mismatches. Non-cash items (Ind AS 116 lease additions, unrealised FX) should not appear as cash steps; reconcile the bridge to the cash flow statement.
  • Excel equivalent. For the board pack, the Excel native waterfall needs "Set as total" on Start and End points (Concept c06).

Key takeaways

  • A bridge must satisfy End = Start + sum of steps exactly; build a check measure.
  • Budget-to-Actual EBITDA steps are sign-adjusted variances so favourable means up.
  • PY-to-CY revenue bridges show growth by BU or by driver; cash bridges follow Ind AS 7 logic or Net Debt.
  • Prefer a disconnected step table with a SWITCH measure for fixed-order board bridges.
  • Sort by step number, colour by sentiment, and keep to fewer than ten steps.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build a revenue bridge [L1]

FY2024-25 revenue ₹1,128.0 Cr; FY2025-26 revenue by BU: Agro ₹512.3 Cr (PY ₹498.7 Cr), Pharma Intermediates ₹401.9 Cr (PY ₹352.0 Cr), Performance Polymers ₹325.8 Cr (PY ₹277.3 Cr). List the bridge steps and values and verify the end value.

Q2. Cash bridge from balances [L2]

Opening cash ₹96.6 Cr; EBITDA ₹204.6 Cr; receivables up ₹18.2 Cr; inventory down ₹6.4 Cr; payables up ₹9.0 Cr; taxes paid ₹38.5 Cr; capex ₹92.0 Cr; interest paid ₹27.9 Cr; net borrowings repaid ₹40.0 Cr; dividend paid ₹24.0 Cr. Lay out the bridge steps with signs and the closing cash, and state which steps would disappear in a Net Debt bridge.

Q3. Missing step [L2]

The October EBITDA bridge ends at ₹115.1 Cr while the EBITDA card shows ₹113.2 Cr. The step table has Revenue, Material, Employee, Power, Other expenses. Explain the most likely causes, and design a residual step and check measure that would have exposed the issue.

💡 Hint: Look for FS lines in CoA that are inside EBITDA but not in the step table.

Q4. Choose the bridge design [L3]

The CFO wants a Budget-to-Actual EBITDA bridge where the revenue step is further split into Agro, Pharma and Polymers, with steps always in the same order and a subtotal "Contribution" bar after material cost. Compare the native Category/Breakdown approach with a step-table approach and recommend one, with the table structure.
