Subtopic note

Trend Visuals: Actual vs Budget vs PY Combos and Rolling 12 Months

Scope: Trend visuals: line/column combos for Actual vs Budget vs PY, and rolling 12 months

Why it matters

A month's number on a card cannot tell whether a variance is a one-off or a drift. The trend row answers that. Finance teams use two standard trend views: a fiscal-month combo chart comparing Actual with Budget and Prior Year (PY), and a rolling 12-month (R12M or LTM) series that strips out seasonality. Both appear in every Indian listed company's internal MIS — LTM revenue and EBITDA are also the basis for leverage covenants (Net Debt / LTM EBITDA) and for valuation multiples in later weeks — so the trend row must be numerically exact, not just visually neat.

Core notes

The Actual vs Budget vs PY combo

Use the Line and clustered column chart (or Line and stacked column):

Well Field / measure
X-axis 'Date'[FiscalMonthName] sorted by 'Date'[FiscalMonthNo] (Apr = 1 … Mar = 12), or 'Date'[MonthEnd]
Column y-axis [Actual Revenue]
Line y-axis [Revenue Budget], [Revenue PY]

Design choices that carry meaning:

  • Columns for Actual, lines for comparatives. Actual is "what happened" (solid mass); Budget and PY are reference levels (lines). Budget dashed or a distinct grey, PY a lighter line. This is close to IBCS notation (s05): Actual solid, Plan outlined, PY grey.
  • Same axis. All three series are in ₹ crore and must share one y-axis. Turn off the secondary axis (Y-axis > Show secondary off, or Align zeros) — a secondary axis with different scale makes Budget look met when it is not.
  • Start y-axis at zero for columns. Truncated column axes exaggerate differences.
  • Future months. Budget exists for all twelve months; actuals only to the close month. Make the Actual measure return BLANK after [Last Actual Date] so columns stop and the budget line continues — the gap visually shows the remaining plan.

A guarded actual measure:

Actual Revenue Trend =
VAR LastActual = [Last Actual Date]
RETURN
    IF ( MIN ( 'Date'[Date] ) <= LastActual, [Revenue] )

PY by month uses SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR or DATEADD(..., -1, YEAR) over the fiscal Date table from Week 11.

Cumulative (YTD) variant

A cumulative chart (YTD Actual columns vs YTD Budget line) shows whether the year is recovering. Use the Week 11 TOTALYTD([Revenue], 'Date'[Date], "3/31") pattern; the line resets in April if the year-end string is correct.

Rolling 12 months

R12M at month m is the sum of the last 12 months ending at m:

\[ \text{R12M}_m = \sum_{k=m-11}^{m} \text{Revenue}_k \]

where \(m\) is the month on the x-axis. DAX:

Revenue R12M =
VAR EndDate = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    IF (
        EndDate <= [Last Actual Date],
        CALCULATE ( [Revenue], DATESINPERIOD ( 'Date'[Date], EndDate, -12, MONTH ) )
    )

Show R12M for the last 24 months so the user sees the slope. Two points matter:

  1. Window beyond the slicer. If the FY slicer filters the Date table to FY2025-26, a 24-month axis is impossible. Either use a disconnected or secondary date table for the axis, or apply the FY slicer only through "Edit interactions" (Topic 2) and let the R12M visual use a relative month filter (last 24 months up to the selected month).
  2. Completeness. R12M needs 12 actual months; for the first 11 months of the data, return BLANK or flag "partial" by counting months with data: CALCULATE(DISTINCTCOUNT('Date'[MonthEnd]), DATESINPERIOD(...), GL).

Showing a trailing window ending at the selected month

CFOs often want "the 12 months up to the selected month" on a combo chart. A common pattern uses a disconnected axis date table AxisDate and a measure that respects the selected month from the main Date slicer:

Revenue Trailing Axis =
VAR SelEnd   = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
VAR AxisEnd  = MAX ( AxisDate[Date] )
VAR InWindow = AxisEnd <= SelEnd && AxisEnd > EDATE ( SelEnd, -12 )
RETURN
    IF ( InWindow,
         CALCULATE ( [Revenue], REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ),
                     TREATAS ( VALUES ( AxisDate[Date] ), 'Date'[Date] ) ) )

Illustration

Sahyadri Chemicals revenue (₹ Cr): April 2024 to March 2025 totals ₹1,128.0 Cr. April 2025 revenue is ₹101.6 Cr versus ₹92.4 Cr in April 2024.

\[ \text{R12M}_{\text{Apr-25}} = 1{,}128.0 - 92.4 + 101.6 = 1{,}137.2 \text{ Cr} \]

The combo chart for FY2025-26 shows April–October columns, the budget line from April to March, and the PY line; the R12M line rises from ₹1,137.2 Cr to ₹1,192 Cr by October, confirming that the year-on-year growth is real even though monthly Actual is below Budget.

Formatting checklist

  • Data labels only on the last point or only on Actual columns; never on all three series.
  • Legend at top-left, or direct labels at line ends.
  • Gridlines light grey or off; one decimal ₹ crore on axis.
  • Tooltips show Actual, Budget, PY, Var and Var % (or a report-page tooltip, Topic 2).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Fiscal month sort. Sorting month names alphabetically (Apr, Aug, Dec…) is the most frequent trend bug; use Sort by column.
  • Leap year and 53-week calendars. Monthly R12M is unaffected; weekly R52W in retail requires a 4-4-5 calendar.
  • Acquisitions. R12M jumps when a subsidiary is acquired; show an organic R12M or annotate the step.
  • Budget phasing. If the annual budget is phased equally (1/12 per month), seasonal businesses will show false variances; use a phased budget.
  • PY restatement. If PY is restated (Ind AS 8 error correction or a common-control merger), the PY line must use restated data.
  • Ratio trends. EBITDA % R12M is R12M EBITDA divided by R12M revenue, not the average of 12 monthly margins.

Key takeaways

  • Actual as columns, Budget and PY as lines, all on one zero-based axis.
  • Blank actuals after the last close date so the remaining budget is visible.
  • R12M uses DATESINPERIOD(..., -12, MONTH) and needs 12 complete months.
  • A trailing window beyond the FY slicer needs a disconnected axis table or interaction control.
  • Sort fiscal months by FiscalMonthNo; compute ratio trends from rolled-up numerator and denominator.

Practice Questions

Q1. R12M by hand [L1]

Monthly revenue (₹ Cr) for Apr-2024 to Mar-2025 is 92.4, 88.1, 90.7, 94.2, 89.5, 93.0, 99.8, 96.4, 95.1, 92.6, 91.9, 104.3. April–June 2025 revenue is 101.6, 97.3 and 95.8. Compute R12M for June 2025 and the change versus R12M at March 2025.

Q2. Build the combo [L2]

Specify the fields, measures (with DAX) and formatting settings for a combo chart showing FY2025-26 monthly EBITDA: Actual columns to the last closed month, Budget dashed line for all months, and PY line, including how future months are handled.

Q3. Secondary axis trap [L2]

A draft chart shows Actual revenue columns on the primary axis (0–120) and Budget on a secondary axis (80–115). Explain why reviewers concluded "we are on budget" when Actual was 6% below Budget, and how to fix the visual.

Q4. 24-month R12M under an FY slicer [L3]

The CFO wants the R12M EBITDA margin line for the 24 months ending at the selected month, while the rest of the page is filtered to the selected FY. Design the model objects and DAX needed, and explain how the slicer interaction works.

💡 Hint: The axis cannot come from the Date table that the FY slicer filters.
