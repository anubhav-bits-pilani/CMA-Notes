Roadmap topic: Executive Dashboard Visual Architecture: Top-row KPI summary cards (Revenue, EBITDA %, PAT, Cash, Net Debt), trend visuals, and variance waterfall charts

Overview

Visual architecture is the discipline of deciding what goes where on an executive page and how each number is encoded so that a CFO reads the right message in seconds. It sits between the semantic model built in Week 11 and the interactivity of Topic 2: the model provides correct measures, and this topic turns them into a page that answers where are we, is it a trend, and what drove it. For FP&A and reporting roles in India, this is the visible part of the job — the monthly CFO pack, the board MIS and the investor-results deck all follow the same pattern of headline KPIs, trends and bridges.

The learning sequence starts with the page skeleton (s01): a KPI row of Revenue, EBITDA %, PAT, Cash and Net Debt; a trend row; and a variance/detail row. Next come the building blocks of each row: card visuals with reference labels against Budget and PY (s02), trend combos and rolling 12-month lines (s03), waterfall bridges for EBITDA, revenue and cash (s04), and variance visuals with favourable/adverse colour and IBCS notation (s05). Two cross-cutting presentation concerns follow: Indian number units (s06) and the theme, canvas, accessibility and mobile settings that make the pack consistent and usable (s07).

The key concepts are the reusable DAX and Excel mechanics behind those visuals: the two waterfall binding patterns (c01), the field-value colour measure (c02), crore measures and dynamic format strings (c03), the Net Debt snapshot measure (c04), margin variance in percentage points (c05) and Excel's native waterfall with "Set as total" for the board pack (c06). Together they ensure that the Power BI page and the Excel pack tell the same story in the same units, colours and signs.

Related concepts to study

Price/volume/mix variances feeding the bridge (Weeks 17–20)

→ Week 17–20: price/volume/mix variance analysis feeding the bridge steps

The Budget-to-Actual EBITDA bridge in this week uses one step per P&L line. In Weeks 17–20 the revenue step is decomposed into price, volume and mix variances (and FX for exporters), and the material step into price and usage variances — the same analysis CMA students know from standard costing, now computed from sales-register and BOM data. The bridge-step table pattern (c01) is built to accept these extra steps without redesign: add rows to the step table and branches to the SWITCH measure. The crucial link is that the sum of the sub-steps must equal the line variance shown this week, so the bridge still closes to Actual EBITDA.

Choosing CFO KPIs (Weeks 5–8)

→ Week 5–8: choosing CFO KPIs (ROCE, cash conversion cycle, interest cover)

The five headline cards are a default, not a rule. Weeks 5–8 covered ratio analysis: ROCE (EBIT / capital employed), cash conversion cycle (DSO + DIO − DPO), interest cover (EBIT or EBITDA / finance costs), Net Debt / EBITDA and asset turnover. A capital-intensive chemicals company might replace PAT with ROCE on the top row; a leveraged infrastructure company might show interest cover. The design rule from s01 still applies: keep five cards, each with a comparison, and move secondary KPIs to Page 2. Ratio KPIs must be computed from totals in the current filter context and labelled with their definition.

IBCS, Stephen Few and accessible palettes

IBCS and Stephen Few dashboard principles; accessible colour palettes

IBCS (International Business Communication Standards) prescribes semantic notation — actual solid, plan outlined, PY grey, forecast hatched — and the SUCCESS rules (say, unify, condense, check, express, simplify, structure). Stephen Few's dashboard work stresses a single screen, high data-ink ratio, avoiding gauges and 3D, using bullet graphs and sparklines, and placing the most important information top-left. Accessible palettes (for example colour-blind-safe blue/orange schemes) ensure fav/adv signals are not lost on readers with colour vision deficiency. These principles explain why the s01–s05 design choices are made and give you a vocabulary for critiquing a dashboard in an interview.

Power BI themes and .pbit templates

Power BI themes and .pbit templates for reusable packs

A theme JSON (s07) fixes colours, fonts and default visual formatting; a .pbit template saves report pages, theme, model structure, measures and parameters without data. Opening a .pbit prompts for parameter values (for example, folder path) and then loads data, producing a new PBIX. Finance teams use templates to roll out the same pack to several entities or BUs, or to start each new reporting project from a governed base with the measure library, Date table, control page and navigation already in place. With PBIP (Topic 5), the theme and report definition also become text files that can be version-controlled.

Common pitfalls

Too many KPIs or visuals, and cards with no comparison

Too many KPIs or visuals per page, and cards with no comparison (vs Budget/PY)

Builders add every available metric because it is easy, producing 12 cards and 15 visuals on one page. Each additional visual dilutes attention and slows rendering. Cards showing only an actual number ("₹712.4 Cr") force the reader to remember the budget. Correct treatment: five KPI cards, each with reference labels vs Budget and PY (s02); fewer than about eight visuals on the executive page; detail moved to Pages 2–3. Review test: for each visual, ask which CFO question it answers; for each card, check that a comparison and its direction are visible without hovering.

Waterfalls whose steps do not add up

Waterfalls without a proper breakdown table, so the steps do not add up to the closing value

This happens when the waterfall uses CoA[FSLine] directly and new FS lines appear, when only top-N breakdown items are shown without "Other", or when start and end are not included consistently. The visual then ends at a value different from Actual EBITDA. Correct treatment: a defined bridge-step table with a residual step and a check measure [Bridge Check] that must be zero (c01, s04). Review test: compare the waterfall end bar to the EBITDA card and the P&L matrix for several months and BUs; put the check on the control page.

Mixing lakhs and crores, or inconsistent signs

Mixing ₹ lakhs and crores, or using different signs on cards and tables

Different builders create different presentation measures, or a vendor table needs smaller units, and nobody standardises. Negative values appear as "−4.2" on a card, "(4.2)" in the matrix and "4.2" in red elsewhere. Readers misread by a factor of 100 or misjudge direction. Correct treatment: one unit per pack stated in the title band (s06), crore measures or a units calculation group (c03), and a single negative-number convention set in measure format strings. Review test: export all visuals' data for one month and compare units and signs line by line.

Pie or donut charts for variances or time series

Pie or donut charts for variances or time series

Pies are a default in many tools and look attractive, so they are used to show "expense mix by month" or even variances. A pie cannot show negative values (adverse variances disappear), angles are hard to compare, and time order is lost. Correct treatment: diverging bars for variances (s05), line/column combos for time (s03), and a sorted bar chart for composition at one point in time. Review test: any pie with more than three slices, any pie over time, and any pie containing a measure that can be negative should be replaced.

Practice Questions

Q1. Design the executive page end to end [L3]

Sahyadri Chemicals, November 2025 YTD (₹ Cr): Revenue Actual 815.0, Budget 840.0, PY 748.2; EBITDA Actual 130.4, Budget 140.3, PY 117.5; PAT Actual 66.1, Budget 72.0; closing cash 79.8 (budget 88.0); closing borrowings 305.4 (budget 290.0). (a) Compute the values and reference labels for all five KPI cards, including EBITDA % vs Budget in pp. (b) Sketch the three-row layout on a 1280 × 720 canvas with visual names, measures and sizes. (c) State the colours each reference label should show and why.

Q2. Bridge, colours and units together [L3]

November 2025 YTD line variances (Actual − Budget, ₹ Cr): Revenue −25.0; Material cost −14.6; Employee +1.9; Power and fuel +3.8; Freight +1.6; Other expenses −2.8. Budget EBITDA ₹140.3 Cr. (a) Build the bridge-step table and SWITCH measure, including a residual step. (b) Verify that the bridge closes to Actual EBITDA from Q1. (c) Write the [Var Colour] binding for a diverging bar chart of the same variances, and state the format string for ₹ crore data labels.

💡 Hint: Convert every line to a favourable-positive EBITDA impact before summing.

Q3. Trend row and Net Debt on Page 2 [L2]

Month-end Net Debt (₹ Cr) April–November 2025: 241.2, 238.6, 236.0, 233.9, 231.5, 229.0, 226.9, 225.6. Monthly revenue for the same months is available for both FY2024-25 and FY2025-26. (a) Specify the combo chart for revenue Actual vs Budget vs PY with future months handled correctly. (b) Write the Net Debt measure so that selecting Q2 shows the 30 September value. (c) Explain why a rolling 12-month revenue line cannot be drawn for 24 months when the FY slicer filters the Date table, and how you would solve it.

Q4. Review and remediate a draft pack [L3]

A draft CFO pack has: a donut of expenses by month; ten cards without comparisons; display units "Millions" on all visuals; red/green colours from Rules formatting on raw variances; no alt text; a 1280 × 2000 canvas; and an Excel board-pack bridge built with stacked columns. Write a prioritised review memo (at least eight findings) that references the correct technique for each fix, and define a theme JSON and template strategy to prevent recurrence.
