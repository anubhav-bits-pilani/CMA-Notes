Subtopic note

Distribution: Apps, Subscriptions, Exports and Teams Embedding

Scope: Distribution: Power BI Apps, email subscriptions, PDF/PowerPoint export, Teams embedding

Why it matters

The last step of Project 3 is getting the pack in front of the right people in the form they will actually use. A CFO may open the App on a phone at 08:30, the board wants a PDF in the meeting folder, BU heads live in Teams, and the Audit Committee needs a frozen snapshot of what was presented. Each channel has different security, licensing and freshness implications. Choosing them deliberately — and knowing that every export is a copy outside the model's controls — is part of governing a finance product.

Core notes

1. Power BI Apps

An App packages content from a workspace (reports, dashboards, Excel workbooks, links) for consumers, separate from the workspace where developers work.

  • Audiences: one App can have several audiences, each seeing a chosen subset of pages/reports — e.g. CXO (all pages), BU Heads (P&L and expense pages), Finance (including the control page). RLS still applies within each.
  • Navigation: sections, custom ordering, links to the Excel board pack and the user guide.
  • Update control: changes in the workspace reach users only when the App is updated (republished) — a natural release gate.
  • Access: grant to security groups; optionally install automatically for those users.
  • Licensing: consumers need Pro (or PPU for PPU workspaces) unless the workspace is on Premium P-SKU or Fabric F64 or larger capacity, where free-licence users can view.

Apps are the default distribution channel for the Executive Finance Pack.

2. Email subscriptions

Users can subscribe themselves (and, with permissions, others) to a report page or dashboard; Power BI emails a snapshot image with a link, optionally attaching a PDF or PowerPoint of the full report (attachment options depend on licence/capacity). Frequency: after data refresh, daily, weekly, monthly or hourly patterns.

  • "After data refresh" (once per day) ties delivery to the refresh — ideal for a morning flash.
  • Dynamic per-recipient subscriptions (Premium/Fabric) send each recipient a version filtered to their BU based on a mapping table, useful for BU flash reports.
  • RLS: subscriptions render according to the security context rules for the subscriber; when subscribing others under RLS, confirm what they will see before enabling.
  • Subscriptions carry data out of the controlled environment by email; sensitivity labels apply to attachments, and UPSI periods may require pausing them.

3. PDF and PowerPoint export

  • Export to PDF (File > Export) produces a static snapshot of report pages with current filters; include the refresh stamp and control status footer on every page so the PDF carries provenance (Topic 4 s04).
  • Export to PowerPoint either as static images or, in newer experiences, with the Power BI add-in for PowerPoint, which embeds live, interactive report pages in slides that refresh when the deck is opened by a user with access. For board decks that must be frozen, use the snapshot option.
  • Paginated reports (Power BI Report Builder) give pixel-perfect, multi-page outputs (e.g. a 40-page Schedule III annexure or ledger listing) and export to PDF/Excel; available on Pro for many scenarios in recent licensing, but check your tenant.
  • Tenant admins can disable export features; labels and DLP govern the exported files.

For the Audit Committee, archive the PDF actually circulated (with stamp) in the board-papers repository; that, not the live report, is the record of what was presented.

4. Teams embedding

  • Add a report as a tab in a Teams channel (Finance – Month-end, BU – South) so discussion sits next to the numbers.
  • Share links and chat about specific visuals; the Power BI app in Teams gives the same access as the Service.
  • Permissions are not granted by Teams membership: users still need access to the App/workspace and licences, and RLS applies. A common failure is adding a tab for a channel whose members lack report access.

5. Other channels

  • Mobile: the Power BI mobile app shows the mobile layout (Topic 1 s07); CFOs often consume KPIs this way.
  • Embedding in SharePoint pages (secure embed respects permissions).
  • Publish to web creates a public, unauthenticated link — never for finance data; admins should disable it.
  • Data alerts on dashboard tiles for KPI thresholds (e.g. Net Debt above ₹500 crore, checks failing above 0).

Choosing the channel

Audience Need Recommended channel
CFO Daily view, drill-through App (desktop and mobile), subscription after refresh
Board / Audit Committee Frozen pack for the meeting PDF export or paginated report, archived
BU heads Own BU, discussion App audience with RLS; Teams tab in BU channel; dynamic subscription
Finance team Controls, detail App Finance audience including control page; Excel cube pack
External auditors Evidence Exported, labelled PDFs and Excel tie-outs on request; no standing access

Illustration

A Chennai auto-ancillary company sets up: App "Executive Finance Pack" with three audiences; a subscription "after data refresh" for the CFO and MD to Page 1; a dynamic subscription to 9 BU heads for the expense page; a Teams tab in the "Month-end close" channel showing the control page; and a monthly PDF export of all three pages archived in the board portal with the stamp "Refreshed 06-May-2026 06:34 IST • GL to 30-Apr-2026 • All checks passed (12/12)".

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Quiet period / trading window. Before results, restrict App audiences and pause subscriptions to keep UPSI within designated persons (SEBI PIT Regulations).
  • Stale links. Bookmarked personal links to the workspace instead of the App show unreleased changes; train users to use the App.
  • Export limits. Exports have page and data-point limits; very large voucher tables should be exported via paginated reports or Analyze in Excel.
  • Mobile layout gaps. Pages without a mobile layout render as the desktop canvas on phones.
  • Licensing surprises. Sharing with a user lacking a licence fails silently from the sender's view; check licensing for each audience before go-live.

Key takeaways

  • Apps with audiences are the main distribution channel; they add a release gate and keep developers' workspaces separate.
  • Subscriptions "after data refresh" deliver the morning flash; dynamic subscriptions personalise by BU on Premium/Fabric.
  • PDF/PowerPoint exports are uncontrolled copies; stamp them and archive what the board saw.
  • Teams tabs put reports where discussion happens but do not grant access.
  • Never use Publish to web for finance data.
  • Match channel to audience, licence and confidentiality.

Practice Questions

Q1. App audience design [L1]

Design App audiences for a pack with four pages (Executive P&L, Balance sheet & working capital, Expense drill-down, Controls) for the CFO, eight BU heads, the finance team and the internal auditor. State which pages each audience sees and how RLS interacts.

Q2. Board pack freezing [L2]

The board meeting on 12 May 2026 reviews Q4 FY2025-26. The model will continue to change as audit adjustments are posted until 20 May. Describe how you would produce, stamp and archive the board version, and how you would explain any later differences.

💡 Hint: Think about what evidence proves which data state the board saw.

Q3. Teams tab not working [L2]

BU heads in the "South Region" Teams channel see "You don't have access to this report" on a newly added Power BI tab. List the likely causes and how you would resolve them without giving them workspace roles.

Q4. Channel strategy under constraints [L3]

The company has Power BI Pro licences only for 25 finance users; 60 managers have no licence. Management wants all 60 to receive a monthly BU expense summary. Compare at least three options (licensing, exports, capacity, Excel), with cost, control and freshness implications, and recommend one.
