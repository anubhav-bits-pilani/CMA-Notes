Key concept

Scheduled Refresh Limits: 8 per Day on Pro, 48 on Premium

Concept: Scheduled refresh limits: 8 per day on Pro, 48 per day on Premium/Fabric capacity

Statement

Workspace capacity Maximum scheduled refreshes per day Granularity of schedule Refresh time-out (per refresh)
Shared capacity (Power BI Pro licence) 8 30-minute slots 2 hours
Premium (P SKU), Premium Per User, Fabric capacity (F SKU) 48 30-minute slots 5 hours (Premium/Fabric)

The daily limit is per semantic model. Schedules are set in semantic model settings, with a time zone and failure notification recipients. These figures are Microsoft service limits as documented at the time of writing; they are licence-dependent and should be rechecked before committing to an SLA.

Explanation

Why the limits matter for finance packs:

  • Close-week cadence. During the first five working days of a month, adjustments arrive throughout the day. With 8 slots you might schedule 06:30, 09:30, 12:00, 14:30, 17:00 and 20:00 IST — six slots — leaving headroom. Outside close, one or two daily refreshes suffice.
  • Not real time. Even 48 refreshes (every 30 minutes) is batch. Intraday cash or sales monitoring needs DirectQuery, hybrid tables or Fabric Direct Lake, not more refreshes.
  • Time-outs. A refresh exceeding 2 hours on shared capacity fails. Large GLs need incremental refresh (c01) and model reduction long before hitting the limit.
  • Concurrency. Scheduled times are start requests; under load, a refresh may queue and start later. Do not schedule a dependent step (e.g. emailing the PDF) at a fixed time assuming completion — use "after data refresh" subscriptions or a Power Automate flow triggered on refresh completion.
  • Automatic deactivation. After repeated consecutive failures, the schedule is disabled; inactive models (no report views for about two months) have scheduled refresh paused. The owner gets an email — someone must act on it.
  • Other triggers. On-demand refreshes, REST API calls and Power Automate's "Refresh a dataset" action have their own rules; on Premium/Fabric, XMLA-endpoint refreshes allow partition-level and programmatic control. Check the current documentation for how these count against limits in your licence.
  • Dataflows have their own schedules and limits; a model depending on a dataflow should refresh after it.

Illustration

A Pro workspace, close week schedule (IST):

Slot Time Purpose
1 06:30 Overnight branch files
2 10:00 Morning provisions
3 13:00 Accruals and reclassifications
4 16:00 Audit-adjustment batch
5 19:00 Final for day; feeds next-morning subscription

Five of eight slots used. The GL refresh takes 18 minutes, well within the 2-hour time-out. If the CFO asks for "every 30 minutes on the last day of close", that needs 48 slots — i.e., Premium/PPU/Fabric — or a different architecture.

Connections

  • s04 (publishing and gateways): limits sit alongside credentials and gateway configuration in the refresh setup.
  • s05 and c01: incremental refresh shortens each refresh so more fit in a window and time-outs are avoided.
  • c04: Power Automate can trigger a refresh when a new file lands instead of polling on a fixed schedule.
  • Topic pitfall: ignoring refresh-failure emails leads to deactivated schedules.

Practice Questions

Q1. Designing a Pro schedule [L1]

Files arrive at 05:00, 11:30 and 16:00 IST on close days, and once at 05:00 on other days. Design scheduled refresh times within the Pro limit and explain the buffer you allow after each arrival.

Q2. When to upgrade [L2]

The sales director wants the revenue page refreshed every 30 minutes during the last three days of each quarter. The model takes 40 minutes to refresh. Evaluate whether moving to Premium Per User solves the request, and propose alternatives.

💡 Hint: Compare refresh duration with the requested interval before thinking about licence limits.

Q3. Schedule disabled [L2]

The CFO reports the pack has shown the same data for a week. Refresh history shows four consecutive failures followed by no attempts. Explain what happened, how to restore it, and what process change prevents recurrence.
