Key concept

Passing a Slicer Name as a CUBEVALUE Member Argument

Concept: =CUBEVALUE("ThisWorkbookDataModel", $B$1, $A5, C$4, Slicer_FY) passes a slicer name as a member argument

Statement

The formula, laid out one argument per line (the order of member arguments does not matter):

=CUBEVALUE("ThisWorkbookDataModel",
           $B$1,       ' measure member cell, fully anchored
           $A5,         ' row member cell, column A anchored
           C$4,         ' column member cell, row 4 anchored
           Slicer\_FY)    ' slicer passed by its formula name
  • B1 (absolute reference) holds =CUBEMEMBER("ThisWorkbookDataModel", "[Measures].[Actual]") — the measure.
  • A5 (column-anchored) holds a row member, e.g. an FS line built with CUBEMEMBER.
  • C4 (row-anchored) holds a column member, e.g. a month or a BU.
  • Slicer_FY is the slicer's Name to use in formulas; unquoted, it contributes the slicer's current selection as a set.

The value returned is

\[ \text{Measure}_{B1}\big(\text{row member}_{A5} \cap \text{column member}_{C4} \cap \text{selection}(\text{Slicer\_FY})\big) \]

Explanation

This is the workhorse formula of a cube-formula board pack: write it once in the top-left value cell and fill it right and down. The mixed references are what make the fill work — the measure stays fixed, each row picks its own FS line, each column its own month, and the slicer applies everywhere. It is exactly the shape that Convert to Formulas generates (s02), tidied with anchors.

Points to note:

  • Slicer semantics. No selection = All (every FY aggregated — usually wrong for flow measures, so add a warning); one item = that FY; multiple = aggregated over the selected FYs.
  • Same connection. The slicer must be built on the same connection as the formula; otherwise #N/A or #VALUE!.
  • One hierarchy per argument. If C4 is a FY member and the slicer is also on FY, the two conflict; the column members must come from a different hierarchy than the slicer (months, BUs, scenarios-as-measures).
  • Several slicers. Add Slicer_BusinessUnit, Slicer_Region as further arguments; each must be connected.
  • Measures as columns. Alternatively, C4 can hold measure members (Actual, Budget, PY) and B1 is omitted — then the column carries the measure. Do not supply two measures in one formula.
  • Named cells. Replacing the anchors with names (MsrActual, Conn) improves readability and avoids anchor mistakes when copying sections.

Illustration

Layout on the "P&L by month" sheet:

B C D E
1 Actual (measure member)
4 Apr May Jun
5 Revenue 101.6 97.3 95.8
6 Material cost 55.4 53.1 52.0

Cell C5 holds the concept formula (A5 = Revenue member, C4 = April member of 'Date'[FiscalMonth], B1 = Actual). With Slicer_FY on FY2025-26 the grid shows FY2025-26 months; selecting FY2024-25 changes every cell to the prior year (April 2024 revenue ₹92.4 Cr).

Practice Questions

Q1. Fill the grid [L1]

Describe the anchoring of each reference in the concept formula and what goes wrong if A5 is written fully relative and then the formula is filled right.

Q2. Slicer conflict [L2]

A sheet uses FY members in row 4 (FY2024-25, FY2025-26) and the concept formula with Slicer_FY. With FY2025-26 selected on the slicer, the FY2024-25 column shows unexpected values. Explain why and how to redesign the sheet.

💡 Hint: Two arguments are trying to set the same hierarchy.
