Subtopic note

COUNTIFS Data-Quality Tests on GL and TB Dumps

Scope: COUNTIFS for data-quality tests: duplicate voucher numbers, unmapped ledgers, postings outside the period

Why it matters

A rollup is only as good as the dump it sits on. Before any SUMIFS grid is trusted, an auditor or controller asks three questions of the raw data: are any vouchers duplicated, does every ledger have a mapping, and do all postings belong to the period being reported? COUNTIFS answers all three with formulas that re-run automatically every time a new dump is pasted. These are the same tests an internal auditor runs in IDEA or ACL, and the same "journal entry testing" procedures expected under SA 240 (fraud risk — unusual entries) and SA 500 (reliability of information produced by the entity); building them in Excel makes them part of every close rather than a once-a-year exercise.

Core notes

COUNTIFS mechanics

=COUNTIFS(criteria_range1, criteria1, [criteria_range2, criteria2], …)
  • Counts rows where all criteria are met (AND logic); up to 127 pairs.
  • Every criteria range must be the same size, or #VALUE!.
  • Criteria use the same string syntax as SUMIFS (">="&date, wildcards, "<>").
  • COUNTIF (single criterion) and COUNTIFS treat criteria that look like numbers as numbers: "0012" matches the number 12 and the text "12". This is a known trap with voucher numbers that have leading zeros.

Test 1 — Duplicate voucher numbers

Add a calculated column to the GL Table:

=COUNTIFS(GL[Voucher], [@Voucher], GL[VchType], [@VchType])>1

Voucher numbers in Tally restart per voucher type (Sales 1, Purchase 1, Journal 1…), so a duplicate test on number alone flags false positives. Key on number + type (and + FY if the dump spans years).

Variants:

Test Formula idea What it catches
Same voucher number, same type COUNTIFS(GL[Voucher],[@Voucher],GL[VchType],[@VchType])>1 Re-imported batches, manual renumbering
Same vendor, same invoice number COUNTIFS(AP[Vendor],[@Vendor],AP[InvNo],[@InvNo])>1 Duplicate purchase booking — classic fraud and GST ITC risk
Same vendor, amount and date three criteria pairs Double payment through two vouchers
Same voucher and amount COUNTIFS(GL[Voucher],[@Voucher],GL[Amount],[@Amount])>1 Duplicate line within a multi-line JV

Note that a multi-line voucher legitimately repeats its voucher number on every line (Dr and Cr legs). The duplicate test therefore belongs at the voucher header level (one row per voucher) or must include line-level fields such as Account and Amount.

A count over the whole column gives the dashboard figure:

=SUMPRODUCT(--(COUNTIFS(AP[Vendor],AP[Vendor],AP[InvNo],AP[InvNo])>1))

Passing the whole column as the criteria returns one count per row (an array); comparing to 1 and summing gives the number of rows involved in duplicates.

Test 2 — Unmapped ledgers

Every ledger in the TB must appear in the mapping table. In the TB Table:

=COUNTIFS(Map[Ledger], [@Ledger])=0

TRUE means the ledger has no mapping. Summarise with =COUNTIFS(TB[Unmapped], TRUE), and also test amount at risk: =SUMIFS(TB[Closing], TB[Unmapped], TRUE). A ₹0 closing ledger that is unmapped is a housekeeping issue; a ₹2 crore one is a misstatement in the draft financial statements.

The reverse test — mapping rows that no longer exist in the TB — catches renamed ledgers (Tally ledger names are the key, and a renaming breaks the link): =COUNTIFS(TB[Ledger], [@Ledger])=0 inside the mapping Table.

Test 3 — Postings outside the period

With period start and end in named cells PStart and PEnd:

=COUNTIFS(GL[Date], "<"&PStart) + COUNTIFS(GL[Date], ">"&PEnd)

Two COUNTIFS are added because COUNTIFS criteria are ANDed; "before start OR after end" needs two separate counts. Also count non-dates: =ROWS(GL[Date]) - COUNT(GL[Date]) gives the number of blank or text dates, which no date criterion will ever catch.

Related period tests:

  • Postings dated on Sundays or national holidays: helper column =WEEKDAY([@Date],2)=7 and count it.
  • Entries posted after close (needs a system "entered on" field): COUNTIFS(GL[EnteredOn], ">"&CloseDate, GL[Date], "<="&PEnd) — back-dated entries.
  • Round-sum journals: COUNTIFS(GL[VchType],"Journal",GL[Amount],">=1000000") combined with a MOD(Amount,100000)=0 helper.

A master check cell =IF(SUM(checks)=0,"OK","ERRORS") with conditional formatting sits at the top of the output sheet so no one reads a P&L built on failed data.

Worked illustration

A purchase register of 4,820 lines for Q1 FY26 contains vendor "Shree Packaging" invoice "SP/245" twice — once on 12-May for ₹4,72,000 and again on 19-May for ₹4,72,000 under a different voucher number. The vendor+invoice COUNTIFS flags both rows (count = 2); the dashboard shows 2 rows involved. Without this test the duplicate also inflates GST input credit claimed in GSTR-3B for May, which would fail reconciliation with GSTR-2B.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Leading zeros and long numbers. COUNTIFS coerces numeric-looking text, so "00123" and "123" count as the same voucher. Worse, criteria above 15 significant digits (bank reference numbers, 16-digit card numbers) are compared as numbers and truncated, so different references can falsely match. Use SUMPRODUCT(--(range=value)) or EXACT for exact text comparison of such keys.
  • Wildcard characters in data. An invoice number like INV*12 is treated as a wildcard pattern; escape with ~ (see criteria syntax).
  • Performance. A COUNTIFS calculated column over 2 lakh rows is O(n²) — 2 lakh × 2 lakh comparisons — and can take minutes. Sort and compare adjacent rows, or use Power Query Group By for large dumps.
  • Legitimate duplicates. Recurring rent invoices with the same number each month from small landlords; decide whether to add the month as a criterion rather than suppress the test.
  • Materiality. Pair every count with a SUMIFS of the amount so reviewers can prioritise.

Key takeaways

  • COUNTIFS gives re-runnable, formula-based journal-entry tests: duplicates, unmapped ledgers, out-of-period dates.
  • Key duplicate tests on the right combination (voucher + type, vendor + invoice), not on voucher number alone.
  • "Outside the period" needs two COUNTIFS added together, plus a separate count of non-date values.
  • COUNTIFS coerces numeric text and truncates beyond 15 digits — use SUMPRODUCT or EXACT for long text keys.
  • Always pair a count with the rupee amount involved, and surface all checks in one master check cell.

Practice Questions

Q1. Duplicate purchase invoice test [L1]

A Table PR (purchase register) has columns Date, VchNo, Vendor, GSTIN, InvNo, Taxable, GST. Write (a) a calculated-column formula flagging rows where the same GSTIN and invoice number appear more than once, and (b) a single-cell formula returning the total GST amount on flagged rows.

Q2. Out-of-period postings for a quarter [L2]

The Q2 FY26 GL dump should contain only postings dated 01-Jul-2025 to 30-Sep-2025, in named cells QStart and QEnd. Write formulas to count (a) postings before the quarter, (b) postings after the quarter, (c) rows whose Date is blank or text. Explain why a single COUNTIFS cannot do (a) and (b) together.

Q3. Bank reference false duplicates [L2]

A bank statement import has a 16-digit UTR column. Your COUNTIFS duplicate test flags 312 duplicates out of 9,400 rows, but manual inspection shows the UTRs are different. Explain the cause and write a replacement formula that tests duplicates correctly.

💡 Hint: Consider how COUNTIFS treats numeric-looking criteria and Excel's 15-digit precision.

Q4. Unmapped ledger impact [L2]

After pasting the FY26 TB (612 ledgers), the unmapped flag shows 9 ledgers. Their closing balances are: ₹0, ₹0, ₹1,250, ₹-48,000, ₹3,10,000, ₹-12,40,000, ₹85,60,000, ₹0, ₹-2,15,000. Design the check-cell block (count, net amount, absolute amount) and explain which ledgers you would resolve before releasing draft financial statements and why.

Q5. Journal-entry testing scope [L3]

The statutory auditor asks you to prepare formula-based JE tests on 1.8 lakh GL lines for FY26: duplicates, weekend postings, entries posted after the 5 April close date into FY26, round-amount journals above ₹10 lakh and postings by users outside Finance. Specify the columns you need in the export, the formula for each test, how you would avoid slow O(n²) COUNTIFS on this volume, and how you would present the results.
