Subtopic note

AVERAGEIFS, MAXIFS and MINIFS for AR/AP and Sales Analytics

Scope: AVERAGEIFS, MAXIFS, MINIFS for average ticket size, largest open vendor payment, oldest open invoice

Why it matters

Sums tell you how much; conditional averages and extremes tell you how it behaves. A CFO reviewing working capital asks "what is our average invoice value by channel?", "what is the single largest payment we still owe this vendor?", "how old is the oldest unpaid invoice from this customer?". These drive collection prioritisation, MSME 45-day compliance under Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act and the MSMED Act, credit-limit reviews and FP&A unit-economics KPIs (average order value, average ticket size). AVERAGEIFS, MAXIFS and MINIFS answer them directly from the sub-ledger with the same criteria grammar as SUMIFS.

Core notes

Signatures

=AVERAGEIFS(average_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, …)
=MAXIFS(max_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, …)
=MINIFS(min_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, …)

All three follow the SUMIFS pattern: the value range comes first, then up to 126/127 equal-sized criteria pairs combined with AND. MAXIFS and MINIFS arrived in Excel 2019 / Microsoft 365; in Excel 2016 they show #NAME?.

Behaviour AVERAGEIFS MAXIFS / MINIFS
No row meets criteria #DIV/0! 0
Text/blank in value range Ignored Ignored
Zero values in value range Included in the average Included (0 can be the min)
Typical wrapper IF(COUNTIFS(…)=0,"–",AVERAGEIFS(…)) IF(COUNTIFS(…)=0,"–",MAXIFS(…))

The "returns 0 when nothing matches" behaviour of MAXIFS/MINIFS is dangerous: for dates, 0 is 00-Jan-1900, and for amounts it is indistinguishable from a genuine zero. Always pair with a COUNTIFS test.

Average ticket size

Average invoice value by channel from a sales register SR:

=AVERAGEIFS(SR[Taxable], SR[Channel], $A5, SR[InvDate], ">="&FYStart, SR[InvDate], "<="&FYEnd, SR[VchType], "Sales")

Judgement points:

  • Exclude credit notes (negative values) or they drag the average down — the VchType criterion does this.
  • Decide taxable value vs invoice value (with GST) and say so in the KPI definition.
  • Average per invoice requires one row per invoice. A line-level sales register (one row per item) gives average line value instead. Aggregate to invoice level first (Power Query Group By, or UNIQUE plus SUMIFS) if needed.
  • Average of averages is wrong: the average ticket for two branches combined is total value ÷ total invoices, not the mean of the two branch averages.

Largest open vendor payment

From an AP open-items Table AP (Vendor, InvNo, InvDate, DueDate, Amount, Status):

=MAXIFS(AP[Amount], AP[Vendor], F2, AP[Status], "Open")

To return the invoice number behind the maximum, combine with a multi-criteria lookup:

=XLOOKUP(1, (AP[Vendor]=F2)*(AP[Status]="Open")*(AP[Amount]=G2), AP[InvNo], "None")

where G2 holds the MAXIFS result. If two open invoices share the maximum amount, XLOOKUP returns the first; FILTER returns all of them.

Oldest open invoice

Dates are serial numbers, so the oldest date is the minimum:

=MINIFS(AR[InvDate], AR[Customer], $A5, AR[Status], "Open")

Format the result as a date. Age in days at the reporting date: =ReportDate - MINIFS(…). Because an empty result is 0, the age of a customer with no open items would be about 46,000 days — wrap it:

=IF(COUNTIFS(AR[Customer],$A5,AR[Status],"Open")=0,
   "",
   ReportDate-MINIFS(AR[InvDate],AR[Customer],$A5,AR[Status],"Open"))

Use DueDate rather than InvDate when the question is "most overdue" rather than "oldest", and for micro and small enterprise vendors compare the oldest unpaid date with the 45-day limit (15 days where there is no written agreement) that drives the Section 43B(h) disallowance.

Worked illustration

AP open items for vendor "Kaveri Steels" (registered under Udyam as a small enterprise), report date 31-Mar-2026:

InvNo InvDate Amount (₹) Status
KS/101 05-Jan-2026 8,40,000 Open
KS/118 02-Feb-2026 12,75,000 Open
KS/122 20-Feb-2026 3,10,000 Paid
KS/131 10-Mar-2026 6,05,000 Open
  • MAXIFS (Open) = ₹12,75,000 (KS/122 is paid, so ignored).
  • MINIFS on InvDate (Open) = 05-Jan-2026; age at 31-Mar-2026 = 85 days — beyond 45 days. If Kaveri is a micro or small enterprise, the unpaid amount is disallowed for FY26 under Section 43B(h) and allowed only in the year of actual payment (the usual pay-before-return-due-date relief in Section 43B does not extend to clause (h)).
  • AVERAGEIFS on Amount (Open) = (8,40,000 + 12,75,000 + 6,05,000) ÷ 3 = ₹9,06,667.

Presentation

In an ageing or vendor dashboard, place COUNTIFS, SUMIFS, AVERAGEIFS, MAXIFS and MINIFS side by side for each vendor or customer row — count, total, average, largest, oldest — all keyed on the same criteria. Ensure the criteria are identical across columns (same status, same date cut-off), or the columns will not be mutually consistent.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Blank vs zero. A blank amount is ignored by AVERAGEIFS; a zero is included. Partial-payment rows with 0 balance can depress averages — filter on ">0".
  • Negative amounts in AP. If AP balances are stored as credits (negative), MAXIFS returns the smallest liability. Use MINIFS on negative data, or build an absolute-value column.
  • Weighted vs simple averages. AVERAGEIFS is a simple mean. Average days outstanding weighted by amount needs SUMPRODUCT (see the SUMPRODUCT topic).
  • Date criteria on text dates silently exclude rows; check COUNT vs ROWS.
  • Ties. MAXIFS gives the value, not the row; decide how ties are reported.

Key takeaways

  • AVERAGEIFS, MAXIFS and MINIFS share the SUMIFS signature with the value range first.
  • AVERAGEIFS returns #DIV/0! with no matches; MAXIFS/MINIFS return 0 — both need a COUNTIFS guard.
  • "Oldest" is MINIFS on a date column; "most overdue" uses DueDate.
  • Average ticket size needs one row per invoice and the exclusion of credit notes.
  • Pair MAXIFS with a multi-criteria XLOOKUP or FILTER to identify which invoice holds the extreme value.

Practice Questions

Q1. Customer dashboard row [L1]

A Table AR has Customer, InvNo, InvDate, DueDate, Amount, Status. For the customer in A6 and report date in RptDate, write formulas for count of open invoices, total open amount, average open invoice, largest open invoice, and oldest open due date — each returning a blank if the customer has no open items.

Q2. Average ticket size by channel [L2]

A D2C brand's line-level sales register (2.4 lakh lines, FY26) has InvNo, InvDate, Channel (Website, Marketplace, Retail), SKU, Qty, Taxable, VchType (Sales, Credit Note). Explain why AVERAGEIFS(SR[Taxable], SR[Channel], "Website") does not give average order value, and describe a formula-only approach (no PivotTable) to compute it correctly for each channel.

Q3. MSME payment compliance [L2]

Vendors are flagged MSME in a column AP[MSME] ("Y"/"N"). Report date is 31-Mar-2026. Write formulas that, for each MSME vendor, return the oldest open invoice date, its age in days, and the total open amount older than 45 days. State the assumption about the agreed credit period you are making.

💡 Hint: The amount older than 45 days is a SUMIFS with a date criterion built from the report date.

Q4. Unexpected 1900 dates [L1]

A colleague's vendor dashboard shows "00-01-1900" as the oldest open invoice for 14 vendors. Explain what happened and fix the formula.

Q5. Credit-sign AP data [L2]

An SAP AP extract stores open liabilities as negative numbers (e.g. –12,75,000). Explain what MAXIFS(AP[Amount], AP[Vendor], F2, AP[Status], "Open") returns on this data and write two alternative formulas that return the largest liability as a positive number.
