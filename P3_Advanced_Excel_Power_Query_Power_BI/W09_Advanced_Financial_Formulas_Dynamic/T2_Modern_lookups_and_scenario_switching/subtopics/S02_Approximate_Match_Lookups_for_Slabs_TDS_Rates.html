Approximate-Match Lookups for Slabs, TDS Rates, Tiers and Bands

Scope: Approximate match for tax slabs, TDS rates, commission tiers and depreciation rate bands

Why it matters

Large parts of Indian finance run on banded rules: income-tax slabs, TDS thresholds by section, sales commission tiers, credit-period bands, depreciation rules that depend on days in use, and interest-rate grids by tenure. Hard-coding these into nested IFs makes every Budget or policy change a formula-surgery exercise. The professional pattern is a small band table maintained as an input, plus an approximate-match lookup that finds the band a value falls into. Payroll models, TDS working papers, sales incentive calculators and tax provisions all rely on it, and getting the boundary right (is ₹12,00,000 in the lower or upper band?) is a classic test question.

Core notes

Band table design

A band table has one row per band, keyed on the band's lower bound ("From"), sorted ascending, starting at the lowest possible value (usually 0):

From Rate BaseTax (cumulative tax at From)
0 0% 0
4,00,000 5% 0
8,00,000 10% 20,000
12,00,000 15% 60,000
16,00,000 20% 1,20,000
20,00,000 25% 2,00,000
24,00,000 30% 3,00,000

These are the new-regime slabs under Section 115BAC for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), as amended by the Finance Act 2025 — date-sensitive; update the table, not the formula, when the Finance Act changes. BaseTax is the cumulative tax on all lower bands, computed in the table itself (=prior BaseTax + (this From − prior From) × prior Rate).

The lookup

=XLOOKUP(Income, Slab[From], Slab[Rate], , -1)

match_mode -1 returns an exact match or else the next smaller From. An income of ₹18,00,000 finds From = 16,00,000 → 20%. An income of exactly ₹16,00,000 finds the exact row → 20%, which means the From value belongs to the upper band. That matches statutory wording such as "₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000: 15%" closely enough for rupee incomes but not for fractional values — define the boundary convention explicitly.

Legacy equivalents: VLOOKUP(Income, SlabTable, 2, TRUE) or INDEX(Rate, MATCH(Income, From, 1)) — both require ascending sort, while XLOOKUP's linear search does not. LOOKUP(Income, From, Rate) also works on sorted data and is common in older models.

Marginal (progressive) tax vs single-rate bands

A slab rate is the marginal rate; the tax is not income × rate. Two correct approaches:

  1. Base-plus-marginal:
=LET(i, Income,
     f, XLOOKUP(i, Slab[From], Slab[From], , -1),
     b, XLOOKUP(i, Slab[From], Slab[BaseTax], , -1),
     r, XLOOKUP(i, Slab[From], Slab[Rate], , -1),
     b + (i - f) * r)
  1. Differential-rate SUMPRODUCT: add a column Diff = this rate − previous rate (0% on the first row, then 5% on each of the six later rows), then =SUMPRODUCT((Income>Slab[From]) * (Income-Slab[From]) * Slab[Diff]).

Worked illustration: taxable income ₹18,00,000. Lookup returns From 16,00,000, BaseTax 1,20,000, rate 20%. Tax = 1,20,000 + 2,00,000 × 20% = ₹1,60,000, plus 4% health and education cess = ₹1,66,400. Income × 20% (₹3,60,000) would be badly wrong.

TDS: threshold-then-rate logic

TDS is mostly a threshold rule, not a progressive slab: once the threshold is crossed, the rate applies to the whole amount (for most sections). A band table per section:

Section Nature From Rate
194J(b) Professional fees 0 0%
194J(b) Professional fees 50,000 10%
194H Commission 0 0%
194H Commission 20,000 2%

(Thresholds per Finance Act 2025, effective 1 April 2025; aggregate annual payments per payee are compared, and the rules differ by section — verify the current position before use.) The lookup combines the section key with an approximate match on amount:

=XLOOKUP(1, (TDS[Section]=[@Section]) * (TDS[From]<=[@AnnualPaid]), TDS[Rate], "Check", 0, -1)

The boolean array keeps only the section's rows at or below the aggregate paid; searching last-to-first (search_mode -1) on a From-ascending table returns the highest qualifying band. Alternatively, keep one table per section or a helper key.

Commission tiers: slab vs whole-amount

Achievement Rate
0 1.0%
25,00,000 1.5%
50,00,000 2.0%
  • Whole-amount (retro) tier: sales of ₹60,00,000 earn 2.0% on everything = ₹1,20,000 — a single approximate lookup times sales.
  • Incremental (slab) tier: 1.0% on 25 lakh + 1.5% on next 25 lakh + 2.0% on 10 lakh = 25,000 + 37,500 + 20,000 = ₹82,500 — use the base-plus-marginal pattern.

Read the incentive policy carefully: the formula type is a policy question, and retro tiers create a cliff effect (₹1 of extra sales can raise commission sharply), which affects sales behaviour near quarter-end.

Depreciation-rate bands

Band logic appears in depreciation in several places:

  • Income-tax half-rate rule (proviso to Section 32(1)): an asset put to use for less than 180 days in the year of acquisition gets 50% of the normal rate. Band table on days in use: From 0 → 50%, From 180 → 100% multiplier.
  • Capitalisation policy: items below a policy threshold (say ₹5,000) are expensed or fully depreciated in year one; above it, the normal Schedule II useful life applies.
  • Useful-life-to-rate tables for SLM/WDV rates by asset class, looked up with exact match on class, then approximate match on cost or life where policy bands exist.
=XLOOKUP(EOMONTH(FYStart,11)-[@PutToUse]+1, HalfRate[From], HalfRate[Multiplier], , -1) * [@BlockRate]

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Boundary convention. "Up to ₹4,00,000" means 4,00,000 itself is in the lower band; with a From-based -1 lookup, an exact 4,00,000 lands in the upper band. Because the upper band's rate applies only to income above From, the tax computed by base-plus-marginal is unaffected — but a single-rate threshold (TDS) at exactly the threshold may differ. Test boundary values explicitly.
  • Rebates and marginal relief. Under the new regime the Section 87A rebate makes tax nil up to ₹12,00,000 of taxable income (resident individuals), with marginal relief just above it; these are overlays on the slab calculation, not more slabs.
  • Below the first From (negative values, losses) returns #N/A; start the table at 0 or a large negative number.
  • Upper-bound ("Up to") tables use match_mode 1 (next larger) instead; mixing conventions in one model is a common error.
  • Unsorted tables are safe with XLOOKUP linear search but wrong with VLOOKUP TRUE, MATCH 1 and LOOKUP.

Key takeaways

  • Store bands as a From-ascending table and use XLOOKUP(value, From, Rate, , -1).
  • Progressive tax needs base-plus-marginal or differential SUMPRODUCT — never income × marginal rate.
  • TDS thresholds apply a single rate once crossed; combine a section key with the amount band.
  • Commission tiers can be whole-amount or incremental — decide from the policy, not the formula.
  • Test exact boundary values; define whether a From value belongs to the upper or lower band.
  • Rate tables are dated inputs (Finance Act changes) — update the table, never the formula.

Practice Questions

Q1. New-regime tax calculator [L2]

Build the FY 2025-26 new-regime slab table with From, Rate and BaseTax columns and write a single formula that returns tax before rebate and cess for taxable income in B2. List the test incomes you would use to validate the formula, including all slab boundaries.

💡 Hint: BaseTax for each row is the previous row's BaseTax plus the previous band's width times its rate.

Q2. Commission plan comparison [L2]

A sales head earns commission on quarterly sales: 0–₹40 lakh at 0.8%, ₹40–80 lakh at 1.2%, above ₹80 lakh at 1.6%. For quarterly sales of ₹36 lakh, ₹79.5 lakh and ₹81 lakh, set up formulas computing commission under (a) a whole-amount tier and (b) an incremental slab. Explain the behavioural risk each design creates near ₹80 lakh.

Q3. TDS band table for multiple sections [L2]

Design a single TDS table covering 194C (individual/HUF and others), 194H, 194I (plant and machinery; land and building) and 194J (technical; professional) with thresholds and rates, and write the lookup that returns the applicable rate given Section, Payee type and aggregate payment. State which values are date-sensitive.

Q4. Half-rate depreciation [L1]

A CNC machine (block rate 15% WDV for Income-tax) costing ₹48,00,000 is put to use on 22-Oct-2025. Write formulas that compute days in use in FY 2025-26 and the applicable rate using a band table, and identify what changes if it had been put to use on 30-Sep-2025.

Q5. Choosing the lookup convention [L1]

A credit-period policy says: invoices "up to ₹1,00,000: 30 days; up to ₹10,00,000: 45 days; above: 60 days". Build the band table in "Up to" form and write the XLOOKUP. Then rebuild it in "From" form with the corresponding match mode.
