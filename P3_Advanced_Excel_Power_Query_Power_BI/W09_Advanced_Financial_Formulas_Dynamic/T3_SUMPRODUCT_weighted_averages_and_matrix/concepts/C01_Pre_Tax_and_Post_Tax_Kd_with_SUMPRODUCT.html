Key concept

Pre-Tax and Post-Tax Kd with SUMPRODUCT

Concept: =SUMPRODUCT(Debt[Balance], Debt[Rate]) / SUM(Debt[Balance]) gives pre-tax Kd; post-tax Kd = Kd × (1 - t)

Statement

\[ K_d = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} B_i \, r_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} B_i}, \qquad K_d^{\text{post}} = K_d \times (1 - t) \]

where \(B_i\) is the balance of debt tranche \(i\) (or its target/market-value weight), \(r_i\) its pre-tax annual cost, \(n\) the number of tranches, and \(t\) the marginal income-tax rate at which interest is deductible. In Excel, SUMPRODUCT(Debt[Balance], Debt[Rate]) is the numerator (total annual interest at those balances) and SUM(Debt[Balance]) the denominator.

Explanation

Why weight by balance. Kd is the cost of a rupee of the company's debt as a whole. Each tranche contributes in proportion to its share of total debt, so the rate of a ₹250 crore NCD matters ten times more than that of a ₹25 crore ICD. A simple AVERAGE(Debt[Rate]) gives each tranche equal weight and is wrong whenever sizes differ.

Why (1 − t). Interest is deductible under Section 36(1)(iii) / Section 37 of the Income-tax Act, so each rupee of interest saves \(t\) rupees of tax, provided the company has taxable profits. The after-tax cost is what enters WACC.

Choosing t in India.

Regime Effective rate
Domestic company under Section 115BAA 25.168% (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess)
New manufacturing company under Section 115BAB (where still applicable) 17.16%
Old regime, total income above ₹10 crore 34.944%

Use the marginal rate expected over the forecast horizon. If the company has carried-forward losses or MAT credits and will not pay tax for some years, the tax shield is deferred — a constant (1 − t) overstates it; some practitioners use a lower effective rate or model the shield explicitly. Section 94B (interest limitation for interest paid to non-resident associated enterprises above 30% of EBITDA) can also cap deductibility.

Inputs for valuation. For a DCF, \(r_i\) should be current market or marginal borrowing costs (yields on the company's traded NCDs, rating-based spread over the G-sec curve, fresh quotes from lenders), all-in (including hedging cost for ECBs), and the weights should reflect target or market-value capital structure — not historic coupons on book balances.

Guard. =IF(SUM(Debt[Balance])=0, 0, SUMPRODUCT(Debt[Balance], Debt[Rate])/SUM(Debt[Balance])) avoids #DIV/0! in debt-free projection years.

Illustration

Tranche Balance (₹ crore) Rate
Term loan 150 9.10%
NCDs 200 8.05%
WCDL 50 9.75%
  • Numerator = 150 × 9.10% + 200 × 8.05% + 50 × 9.75% = 13.650 + 16.100 + 4.875 = ₹34.625 crore.
  • Pre-tax Kd = 34.625 ÷ 400 = 8.66%.
  • Post-tax Kd under 115BAA = 8.66% × (1 − 0.25168) = 6.48%.
  • Simple average rate = 8.97% — overstated because the largest tranche is the cheapest.

Practice Questions

Q1. Kd for a capital-structure table [L1]

A company has a ₹300 crore term loan at 9.35%, ₹150 crore NCDs at 8.20% and a ₹50 crore ECB with an all-in hedged cost of 8.90%. Write the Excel formulas for pre-tax and post-tax Kd (Section 115BAA), and the guarded version for a projection year with no debt.

Q2. Tax-shield judgement [L2]

A loss-making renewable-energy SPV expects no taxable income for the first four years of a 20-year DCF because of accelerated depreciation and brought-forward losses. Explain why applying Kd × (1 − 25.168%) from year 1 overstates value, and describe two ways to reflect the deferred tax shield in the model.

💡 Hint: Consider whether to adjust the discount rate or the cash flows.

Q3. Book vs market weights [L2]

The company's ₹200 crore of 7.25% NCDs issued in 2021 now trade at 94.5 (per 100 face value) with a yield to maturity of 8.65%. Explain which rate and which weight you would use for this tranche in a WACC calculation, and why the coupon and face value would be inappropriate.
