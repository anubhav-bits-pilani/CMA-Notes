Key concept

MMULT Dimensions: (1×n)·(n×m) Gives a 1×m Allocation

Concept: =MMULT(CostPool_row, Driver_matrix): (1×n)·(n×m) gives 1×m and spills in Microsoft 365

Statement

For a row vector \(\mathbf{p}\) of dimension \(1 \times n\) and a matrix \(\mathbf{D}\) of dimension \(n \times m\),

\[ \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{p}\,\mathbf{D}, \qquad a_j = \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \, D_{ij}, \quad j = 1, \dots, m \]

where \(p_i\) is the amount in cost pool \(i\), \(D_{ij}\) the share (or rate) of pool \(i\) assigned to receiver \(j\), and \(\mathbf{a}\) the \(1 \times m\) result. The inner dimensions (\(n\) columns of CostPool_row and \(n\) rows of Driver_matrix) must match; the result takes the outer dimensions (1 row, \(m\) columns). In Microsoft 365, entering the formula in one cell spills the 1 × m result to the right.

Explanation

Dimension rule. MMULT(array1, array2) requires COLUMNS(array1) = ROWS(array2); otherwise #VALUE!. The output has ROWS(array1) rows and COLUMNS(array2) columns. Check before building: =COLUMNS(CostPool_row)=ROWS(Driver_matrix).

Spill behaviour. In Microsoft 365 the result spills into m cells; reference it elsewhere with the spill operator (for example K5#). The spill area must be empty (#SPILL! otherwise) and cannot be inside an Excel Table. In Excel 2019 and earlier, select exactly 1 × m cells, type the formula and press Ctrl+Shift+Enter; the array formula can then only be edited as a block.

Numeric inputs only. Any blank, text or logical cell in either array makes MMULT return #VALUE! for the whole result. Fill empty driver cells with 0 and keep labels outside the referenced ranges.

Completeness property. If each row of Driver_matrix sums to 1 (shares), then SUM(result) = SUM(CostPool_row). This is the natural check cell. If the matrix holds rates instead (₹ per unit of driver), the property does not hold and the check becomes driver-volume based.

Orientation variants.

Inputs Formula Result shape
Pools as row (1×n), shares n×m MMULT(Pools, Shares) 1×m row
Pools as column (n×1), shares n×m MMULT(TRANSPOSE(Pools), Shares) 1×m row
Pools as column (n×1), shares n×m MMULT(TRANSPOSE(Shares), Pools) m×1 column
Monthly pools (12×n), shares n×m MMULT(PoolsByMonth, Shares) 12×m: every month allocated at once

The last row shows MMULT's power: one formula allocates all twelve months.

Illustration

Cost pools (₹ lakh) as a row: IT 18, HR 6, Admin 12 (n = 3). Receivers: Retail, Wholesale, Online, Exports (m = 4).

Pool \ BU Retail Wholesale Online Exports
IT 0.35 0.15 0.40 0.10
HR 0.50 0.20 0.15 0.15
Admin 0.40 0.25 0.20 0.15

Dimensions (1×3)·(3×4) → 1×4. Result:

  • Retail = 18×0.35 + 6×0.50 + 12×0.40 = 6.30 + 3.00 + 4.80 = ₹14.10 lakh
  • Wholesale = 2.70 + 1.20 + 3.00 = ₹6.90 lakh
  • Online = 7.20 + 0.90 + 2.40 = ₹10.50 lakh
  • Exports = 1.80 + 0.90 + 1.80 = ₹4.50 lakh

Total ₹36.00 lakh = 18 + 6 + 12 — complete. If the IT row's Exports cell were blank instead of 0.10, the whole output would show #VALUE!.

Practice Questions

Q1. Dimension check [L1]

State the result shape, or the error, for each: (a) MMULT of a 1×5 row and a 5×3 matrix; (b) a 5×1 column and a 5×3 matrix; (c) a 12×5 block and a 5×3 matrix; (d) TRANSPOSE of a 5×3 matrix multiplied by a 5×1 column.

Q2. Twelve months at once [L2]

Monthly cost pools for FY26 (12 months × 4 pools) are in C6:F17 and the share matrix (4 pools × 6 BUs) in I6:N9. Write one formula that produces the full 12 × 6 allocation, a check that each month's allocations equal that month's pools, and explain what happens in Excel 2019.

💡 Hint: Compare row totals of the result with row totals of the pools block.

Q3. Rates instead of shares [L2]

An ABC model has activity cost-driver rates (₹ per setup, ₹ per inspection, ₹ per order) as a 1×3 row and a 3×5 matrix of driver volumes by product. Explain what MMULT(Rates, Volumes) returns, why the completeness check differs from a share-matrix allocation, and how you would reconcile the result to total activity cost.
