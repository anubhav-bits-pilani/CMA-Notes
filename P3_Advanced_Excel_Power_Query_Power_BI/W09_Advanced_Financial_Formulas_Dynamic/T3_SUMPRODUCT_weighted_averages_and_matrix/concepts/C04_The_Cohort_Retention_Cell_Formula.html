Key concept

The Cohort Retention Cell Formula

Concept: Retention cell: =SUMPRODUCT((Cust[Cohort]=$A5)*(Cust[ActiveMonth]=EDATE($A5, C$4))) / $B5

Statement

For cohort month \(c\) (in column A of the current row), months since acquisition \(k\) (in row 4 of the current column) and cohort size \(n_c\) (in column B):

\[ R_{c,k} = \frac{1}{n_c} \sum_{i \in \text{Cust}} \mathbf{1}\left[\text{Cohort}_i = c\right] \times \mathbf{1}\left[\text{ActiveMonth}_i = \text{EDATE}(c, k)\right] \]

where each row \(i\) of Cust is one customer–month of activity, \(\text{Cohort}_i\) is that customer's acquisition month (month-start date), \(\text{ActiveMonth}_i\) the month-start date of the activity, and \(\mathbf{1}[\cdot]\) the indicator function. \(R_{c,k}\) is the share of the cohort active \(k\) months after acquisition.

Explanation

What each part does.

  • (Cust[Cohort]= cohort cell ) — 1 for rows belonging to this cohort.
  • EDATE( cohort cell , header cell ) — shifts the cohort month forward by the number of months in the header (0, 1, 2 …), producing the calendar month to test. EDATE keeps the day number, so cohorts and active months must both be stored as month-start dates (or both as month-end with EOMONTH logic).
  • (Cust[ActiveMonth]=…) — 1 for rows active in that calendar month.
  • The product counts customer-months meeting both; dividing by the cohort size gives the retention rate.
  • SUMPRODUCT is needed because the conditions are multiplied arrays; with a single product argument, the multiplication does the coercion.

Anchoring. The cohort column and cohort size column are column-absolute (A and B stay fixed while copying across), the header row is row-absolute (row 4 stays fixed while copying down). One formula fills the whole matrix.

Preconditions.

  1. Cust must be de-duplicated to one row per customer per active month — otherwise the numerator counts transactions and retention can exceed 100%.
  2. Cohort size in column B must be computed on the same basis (distinct customers acquired in that month).
  3. Dates must be true dates, consistently month-start.

Triangle and guard. Wrap with IF(EDATE(cohort, k) > LastMonth, "", …) so unobservable future cells are blank rather than 0% (a 0% would be read as total churn and would drag down averages). Guard n_c = 0 if any cohort can be empty.

Faster equivalent. COUNTIFS(Cust[Cohort], cohort, Cust[ActiveMonth], EDATE(cohort, k)) / size gives the same result with much better performance on large customer tables.

Illustration

Cohort Jul-25, size 400 (in B5), A5 = 01-Jul-2025:

Header k EDATE(A5, k) Customers active Retention
0 01-Jul-2025 400 100.0%
1 01-Aug-2025 268 67.0%
2 01-Sep-2025 222 55.5%
3 01-Oct-2025 206 51.5%

If the activity Table had not been de-duplicated and 30 customers placed two orders in August, the M1 numerator would be 298 and retention would show 74.5% — overstated by 7.5 points.

Connections

Practice Questions

Q1. Revenue retention version [L1]

Modify the concept formula to compute revenue retention, where Cust[Revenue] holds revenue for each customer-month and column B holds the cohort's M0 revenue. Add the triangle blanking for months after LastM.

Q2. Month-end dates problem [L2]

In a colleague's file, Cohort holds month-end dates (31-Jan-2026, 28-Feb-2026 …) while ActiveMonth holds month-start dates. Explain what the concept formula returns, and fix it without rebuilding the data.

💡 Hint: Consider what EDATE does to the day number, and which side should be converted.

Q3. Retention above 100% [L2]

A retention matrix shows 108% at M2 for the Nov-25 cohort. List three possible causes in the data or formula and the test you would run for each.
