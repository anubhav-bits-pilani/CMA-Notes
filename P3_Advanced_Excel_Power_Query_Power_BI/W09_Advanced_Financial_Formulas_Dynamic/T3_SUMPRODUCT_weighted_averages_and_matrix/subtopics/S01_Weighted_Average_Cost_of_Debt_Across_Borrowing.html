Subtopic note

Weighted Average Cost of Debt Across Borrowing Tranches

Scope: Weighted average cost of debt across tranches (balance × rate) and weighted average interest rate

Why it matters

Indian corporates rarely have one loan. A typical mid-cap balance sheet carries a rupee term loan from a public-sector bank, listed NCDs, an ECB, cash-credit or WCDL limits, commercial paper and perhaps promoter ICDs — each at a different rate. Three numbers depend on combining them correctly: the pre-tax cost of debt (Kd) in WACC and DCF work, the weighted average interest rate reported in MIS, rating-agency submissions and lender presentations, and the capitalisation rate for general borrowings under Ind AS 23. A simple average of rates is the classic mistake; the correct answer weights each rate by the amount of debt carrying it, which is exactly what SUMPRODUCT computes in one cell.

Core notes

The formula

\[ K_d = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} B_i \times r_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} B_i} \]

where \(B_i\) is the balance of tranche \(i\), \(r_i\) its annual interest rate, and \(n\) the number of tranches. The numerator is the annual interest the portfolio would cost at those balances; dividing by total debt gives the blended rate.

In Excel, with a Table Debt (Tranche, Lender, Balance, Rate):

=SUMPRODUCT(Debt[Balance], Debt[Rate]) / SUM(Debt[Balance])

SUMPRODUCT multiplies the two columns row by row and adds the products, so no helper column of "balance × rate" is needed. Post-tax cost of debt:

\[ K_d^{\text{post-tax}} = K_d \times (1 - t) \]

where \(t\) is the marginal tax rate at which interest is deductible — 25.168% for a company under Section 115BAA (22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess), 34.944% for a company under the old regime with income above ₹10 crore (30% + 12% surcharge + 4% cess).

Worked illustration

Tranche Balance (₹ crore) Rate Balance × Rate
Rupee term loan (SBI) 100 9.40% 9.400
Listed NCDs 250 7.95% 19.875
ECB (all-in hedged cost) 60 8.60% 5.160
Cash credit / WCDL 40 10.25% 4.100
Promoter ICD 10 12.00% 1.200
Total 460 39.735
  • Weighted pre-tax Kd = 39.735 ÷ 460 = 8.64%.
  • Simple average of the five rates = 48.20% ÷ 5 = 9.64% — overstated by 100 bps because the cheapest tranche (NCDs) is also the largest.
  • Post-tax Kd at 25.168% = 8.64% × 0.74832 = 6.46%.

What "rate" should mean

The weighting mechanics are trivial; the judgement is in the inputs.

Purpose Rate to use Balance to use
WACC / DCF (forward-looking) Marginal cost: current yield on the company's NCDs, fresh-borrowing quotes, or rating-based spread over G-sec Target or market-value debt mix
MIS "weighted average interest rate" Contractual coupon / current reset rate Closing or, better, average balance
Effective cost for the year Interest + amortised fees (Ind AS 109 effective interest rate) Average balance over the year
Ind AS 23 capitalisation rate for general borrowings Borrowing costs ÷ weighted average general borrowings outstanding during the period, excluding specific borrowings (Ind AS 23.14) Time-weighted outstanding

For the ECB, the relevant rate is the all-in rupee cost: SOFR plus spread, withholding tax gross-up where borne by the borrower, and hedging cost. Using the bare USD coupon understates Kd materially.

Weighted average interest rate from the P&L

An alternative, backward-looking measure is the effective rate actually paid:

\[ r_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\text{Finance cost on borrowings for the year}}{\text{Average borrowings}} \]

where average borrowings is ideally a monthly average, e.g. =AVERAGE(MonthEndDebt) across 13 month-ends (opening plus 12), not the simple mean of opening and closing. For a company that drew most of its debt in March, the opening/closing average badly overstates the debt that was actually outstanding during the year. Exclude non-borrowing finance costs (interest on lease liabilities under Ind AS 116, unwinding of discount on provisions, interest on income-tax) when the aim is the cost of borrowings — or include them consistently if lease liabilities are treated as debt in the WACC.

Building it in a model

  • Keep the debt schedule as a Table with one row per tranche and columns for opening balance, drawdowns, repayments, closing balance, average balance, rate and interest.
  • Compute interest per tranche as average balance × rate (or using a monthly schedule) and Kd as SUMPRODUCT of average balances and rates divided by total average balance.
  • For a projection, Kd can vary by year as the mix changes — compute it per column with SUMPRODUCT(AvgBal_col, Rate_col)/SUM(AvgBal_col).
  • Guard the division for years with no debt: =IF(SUM(w)=0, 0, SUMPRODUCT(w, x)/SUM(w)).

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Floating-rate tranches (MCLR- or repo-linked loans) — use the current reset rate for a point estimate and the forward view for projections.
  • Zero-coupon or deep-discount bonds — the coupon is zero; use the yield to maturity.
  • Undrawn limits — commitment fees are a cost but the balance is zero; include them in the effective rate, not in the weighting of drawn rates.
  • Subsidised or related-party debt (interest-free promoter loans) — Ind AS 109 measures them at fair value with an imputed rate; for WACC, consider whether the subsidy will continue.
  • Book vs market weights — for WACC, weights should reflect market values or a target structure; for MIS reporting, book balances are standard. Say which you are using.

Key takeaways

  • Kd = Σ(balance × rate) ÷ Σ balance; SUMPRODUCT does it in one cell without helper columns.
  • Post-tax Kd = Kd × (1 − t); under 115BAA, t = 25.168%.
  • A simple average of rates is wrong whenever tranche sizes differ.
  • Use marginal, all-in rates for WACC; contractual or effective rates on average balances for MIS and historical analysis.
  • Average balances (monthly) beat opening/closing averages when debt moves during the year.
  • Guard against zero total debt with an IF around the division.

Practice Questions

Q1. Blended Kd for a mid-cap [L1]

Debt at 31-Mar-2026: term loan ₹180 crore at 9.15%, NCDs ₹120 crore at 8.10%, CC ₹35 crore at 10.40%, CP ₹50 crore at 7.35%. Set up the Table and the SUMPRODUCT formula for pre-tax Kd, and the post-tax Kd for a company under Section 115BAA. Also compute the simple average rate and explain the direction of the difference.

Q2. Effective rate on average balances [L2]

A company's borrowings were ₹40 crore from April to December 2025 and ₹160 crore from January to March 2026 (a capex loan was drawn on 1 January). Finance cost on borrowings for FY26 was ₹4.62 crore. Compute the effective interest rate using (a) the average of opening and closing balances and (b) a 12-month average of month-end balances, and explain which is meaningful.

💡 Hint: Write out the twelve month-end balances and use AVERAGE before dividing.

Q3. ECB all-in cost [L2]

An ECB of USD 15 million carries interest at 3-month Term SOFR (assume 4.30%) + 1.85%. The company bears withholding tax gross-up of 5% on interest and pays a hedging premium of 2.4% a year. Estimate the all-in rupee cost to use in the Kd table and explain why the bare coupon would understate WACC.

Q4. Ind AS 23 capitalisation rate [L3]

During FY26 a company had general borrowings: ₹100 crore at 9% outstanding all year, and ₹60 crore at 10% drawn on 1 October 2025. It also has a specific project loan of ₹80 crore at 8.5%. Explain how you would compute the capitalisation rate for general borrowings in Excel (including time-weighting), which balances are excluded, and how the rate is applied to expenditure on a qualifying asset.

Q5. Model build [L3]

Build the specification for a five-year debt schedule tab (FY27–FY31) with five tranches, each with opening balance, drawdowns, repayments, closing balance, average balance, rate and interest. Write the formula for the yearly weighted average pre-tax and post-tax Kd, including the guard for years with no debt, and describe two checks that ensure the interest line in the P&L ties to the schedule.
