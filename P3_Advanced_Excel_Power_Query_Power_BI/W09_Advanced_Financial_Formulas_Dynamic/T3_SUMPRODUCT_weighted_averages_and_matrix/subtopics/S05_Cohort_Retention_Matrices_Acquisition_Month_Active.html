Subtopic note

Cohort Retention Matrices: Acquisition Month × Active Month

Scope: Cohort retention matrices: customers by acquisition month × active month

Why it matters

For subscription, SaaS, D2C, fintech and consumer-lending businesses, the cohort retention matrix is the single most informative customer-economics exhibit. It shows, for each group of customers acquired in the same month, what fraction remain active (or what fraction of their revenue remains) 1, 2, 3 … months later. Investors and FP&A teams use it to estimate lifetime value (LTV), payback on customer acquisition cost (CAC), churn trends and the quality of growth — a business growing revenue while recent cohorts decay faster is buying growth. Building it in Excel from a transaction log is a standard analyst test, and SUMPRODUCT or COUNTIFS fills the whole triangle from one formula.

Core notes

Data preparation

Start from a transaction or activity log (CustomerID, TxnDate, Amount). Build a customer-month activity Table Cust with one row per customer per month in which the customer was active:

Column Meaning Formula / source
CustomerID customer key from log
ActiveMonth month start of the activity =EOMONTH([@TxnDate],-1)+1, then de-duplicate customer–month pairs
Cohort month start of first activity (acquisition month) =MINIFS(Cust[ActiveMonth], Cust[CustomerID], [@CustomerID])
Revenue revenue in that month SUMIFS on the log

De-duplication matters: a customer ordering five times in May must appear once for May, or the matrix counts orders, not customers. Power Query Group By (CustomerID, Month) is the cleanest way; UNIQUE on the two columns works for moderate data.

Matrix layout

  • Rows: cohort months in A5:A16 (e.g. 01-Apr-2025 … 01-Mar-2026).
  • Column B: cohort size — customers acquired in that month.
  • Row 4: months since acquisition, 0, 1, 2 … 11 in C4:N4.
  • Body: retention percentage.

Cohort size (counting each customer once, at its acquisition month row):

=COUNTIFS(Cust[Cohort], $A5,
          Cust[ActiveMonth], $A5)

Retention cell:

\[ R_{c,k} = \frac{\#\{\text{customers in cohort } c \text{ active in month } c + k\}}{\#\{\text{customers in cohort } c\}} \]

where \(c\) is the cohort month and \(k\) the months since acquisition.

=SUMPRODUCT((Cust[Cohort]=$A5)
  * (Cust[ActiveMonth]=EDATE($A5,
  C$4))) / $B5

EDATE($A5, C$4) turns "months since acquisition" into the calendar month to test. The equivalent COUNTIFS (faster on large data):

=COUNTIFS(Cust[ActiveMonth], EDATE($A5,
  C$4), Cust[Cohort], $A5)
  / $B5

The triangle

Later cohorts have fewer observable months. Blank out cells beyond the last month of data (LastM):

=IF(EDATE($A5,
  C$4)>LastM, "", COUNTIFS(…)/$B5)

The result is the familiar upper-left triangle; conditional formatting with a colour scale makes decay patterns visible.

Worked illustration

Customers acquired (cohort size) and active counts:

Cohort Size M0 M1 M2 M3
Apr-25 200 200 140 118 104
May-25 260 260 169 135 —
Jun-25 310 310 186 — —

Retention: Apr-25 → 100%, 70.0%, 59.0%, 52.0%; May-25 → 100%, 65.0%, 51.9%; Jun-25 → 100%, 60.0%. Each newer cohort retains less at M1 — a warning sign even if total active customers are rising.

Weighted average retention by month-since-acquisition (for an LTV curve) must weight by cohort size, not average the percentages:

\[ \bar{R}_k = \frac{\sum_c n_c R_{c,k}}{\sum_c n_c} \]

over cohorts \(c\) with an observation at \(k\), where \(n_c\) is cohort size. In Excel: =SUMPRODUCT(Sizes, RetCol)/SUMIFS(Sizes, RetCol, "<>") — taking care that blank triangle cells are excluded from both numerator and denominator. For M1 above: (140 + 169 + 186) ÷ (200 + 260 + 310) = 495 ÷ 770 = 64.3%, versus a simple average of 65.0%.

Revenue retention

Replace the count with revenue to get net revenue retention (NRR) by cohort:

=SUMPRODUCT((Cust[Cohort]=$A5)
  * (Cust[ActiveMonth]=EDATE($A5,
  C$4)) * Cust[Revenue]) / $B5

with column B now holding M0 revenue of the cohort. NRR above 100% means expansion revenue from surviving customers exceeds churn — the SaaS benchmark metric.

Definitions to fix before building

  • Active — any transaction, a paid subscription, a login, a minimum spend? The definition changes the curve.
  • Reactivation — a customer inactive in M2 but active in M3 counts in M3 (this is "active in month", not "survived continuously"). A survival (unbroken) definition needs a different calculation.
  • Calendar vs fiscal — cohorts are usually calendar months; label FY boundaries for Indian reporting.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Partial current month — exclude the current incomplete month or retention will appear to collapse at the diagonal.
  • Acquisition date vs first purchase — sign-ups without purchase distort cohort sizes; define the cohort event clearly.
  • Small cohorts produce volatile percentages; show cohort sizes beside percentages.
  • Mergers of customer IDs (same person, two accounts) inflate churn; dedupe IDs first.
  • Performance — SUMPRODUCT across a 24 × 24 triangle on 5 lakh customer-months is 576 full scans; COUNTIFS or a PivotTable/Power Pivot is faster.

Key takeaways

  • Build a de-duplicated customer-month Table with Cohort = first active month.
  • Retention cell = customers in cohort active at cohort + k months ÷ cohort size, using EDATE on the cohort date.
  • Mixed references (cohort column locked, months-since row locked) let one formula fill the triangle.
  • Blank cells beyond the last data month; weight averages across cohorts by cohort size.
  • Revenue-based cohorts give NRR; agree the definition of "active" before building.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the cohort Table [L1]

From a D2C order log (OrderID, CustomerID, OrderDate, NetValue) for April 2025 to March 2026, describe the steps and formulas to create the customer-month Table with ActiveMonth, Cohort and Revenue columns, including the de-duplication step.

Q2. Retention triangle formula [L2]

Cohort months are in A6:A17, cohort sizes in B6:B17, months since acquisition 0–11 in C5:N5 and the last complete data month in LastM = 01-Feb-2026. Write the formula for C6 that fills the whole triangle with blanks beyond LastM, and explain each anchoring choice.

💡 Hint: The calendar month being tested is the cohort month shifted by the header value.

Q3. Weighted retention curve [L2]

M3 retention for five cohorts is 52%, 49%, 47%, 44% and 41%, with cohort sizes 200, 260, 310, 420 and 560. Compute the size-weighted M3 retention and the simple average, and explain which one you would use to forecast retention of a planned 600-customer cohort and why.

Q4. Reading the matrix [L3]

A fintech lender's cohort matrix shows M1 retention falling from 72% to 58% over six cohorts, while total monthly active users grew 40%. Marketing spend tripled in the same period. Write a short memo for the CFO interpreting the matrix, the implications for CAC payback and LTV, and the additional cuts (channel, product, ticket size) you would build next.

Q5. NRR vs logo retention [L2]

A B2B SaaS company's Apr-25 cohort had 40 customers and ₹18 lakh MRR at M0; at M12, 31 customers remain with ₹21.6 lakh MRR. Compute logo retention and net revenue retention, and explain how both can be "true" and what each tells an investor.
