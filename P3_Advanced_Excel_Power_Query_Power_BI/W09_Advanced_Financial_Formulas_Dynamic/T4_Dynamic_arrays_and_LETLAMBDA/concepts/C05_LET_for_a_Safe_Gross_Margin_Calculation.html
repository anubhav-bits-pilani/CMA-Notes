Key concept

LET for a Safe Gross Margin Calculation

Concept: =LET(rev, SUMIFS(...), cogs, SUMIFS(...), IF(rev=0, 0, (rev-cogs)/rev))

Statement

=LET(
   rev,  SUMIFS(GLm[Amount], GLm[FSLine], "Revenue from operations",
                GLm[MonthEnd], C$4, GLm[Dept], $B5),
   cogs, SUMIFS(GLm[Amount], GLm[FSLine], "Cost of materials consumed",
                GLm[MonthEnd], C$4, GLm[Dept], $B5),
   IF(rev = 0, 0, (rev - cogs) / rev))
  • rev, cogs — names bound to the results of two SUMIFS; each is evaluated once.
  • The last argument is the result: gross margin

\[ \text{GM} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } R = 0 \\ \dfrac{R - C}{R} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

where \(R\) is revenue and \(C\) is cost of goods sold for the department and month.

The row-locked C$4 holds the month-end header and the column-locked $B5 the department label, so the same formula copies across a department × month grid.

Explanation

Without LET, the margin formula needs the revenue SUMIFS three times (test, numerator, denominator). That is three full scans of the GL per cell, and three places to update if criteria change — the classic way a reviewer "fixes" one copy and leaves the others inconsistent. LET:

  • Computes once, reuses many times — the revenue SUMIFS runs once per cell, roughly halving calculation time on a large grid.
  • Documents the logic — names like rev and cogs read like a working paper.
  • Guards the division — IF(rev=0, 0, …) avoids #DIV/0! for departments with no sales (service departments, dormant months). Returning 0 is a presentation choice; some teams prefer "" (blank) or NA() so that charts skip the point. Choose deliberately: a 0% margin is a value that will be averaged; a blank is not.
  • Supports debugging — temporarily replace the final argument with rev or cogs to see the intermediate value.

Why not IFERROR((rev-cogs)/rev, 0)? IFERROR also hides #VALUE! or #REF! caused by broken references, so a genuine model error would show as a 0% margin. The explicit zero test only handles the expected condition — the same reasoning as preferring IFNA to IFERROR for lookups.

Sign conventions matter: if revenue is stored as a negative credit, rev must be negated (-SUMIFS(...)) before the margin is computed, or margins will appear above 100% or negative.

Illustration

Department "Retail", month 30-Jun-25:

Name Value (₹ lakh)
rev 420
cogs 273
result (420 − 273) ÷ 420 = 35.0%

Department "Corporate", same month: rev = 0 → result 0 (no #DIV/0!).

Department "Online", where revenue is mis-signed as −180 and cogs 117: result (−180 − 117) ÷ −180 = 165% — an impossible margin that signals the sign error. A check cell =COUNTIF(C5#,">1") counting margins above 100% makes such errors visible.

Practice Questions

Q1. Extend to EBITDA margin [L2]

Rewrite the formula to return EBITDA margin, where EBITDA = revenue − COGS − employee benefits − other expenses, each from a separate SUMIFS on FS line, with revenue stored as negative credits and expenses as positive debits.

Q2. Blank or zero? [L2]

The margin chart for 15 departments shows a sharp dip to 0% for three service departments every month. Explain the cause and propose two alternative return values for the zero-revenue case, stating the effect of each on an AVERAGE of margins and on a line chart.

Q3. IFERROR objection [L1]

A colleague proposes replacing the LET with =IFERROR((SUMIFS(...)-SUMIFS(...))/SUMIFS(...), 0). Give two reasons, one about performance and one about control, for rejecting this.
