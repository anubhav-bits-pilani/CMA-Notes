Subtopic note

Loan Amortisation with PMT, IPMT, PPMT, CUMIPMT and CUMPRINC

Scope: Loan amortisation with PMT, IPMT, PPMT, CUMIPMT, CUMPRINC; converting annual to monthly rates

Why it matters

Debt schedules sit in every 3-statement model, every project finance model and every treasury MIS. The P&L needs the interest charge for each period, the balance sheet needs the current and non-current split of borrowings (Schedule III requires current maturities of long-term borrowings to be shown within current borrowings), and the cash flow statement needs principal repayments under financing activities and interest under financing or operating depending on policy (Ind AS 7). Excel's annuity functions compute EMIs and their interest/principal split for any period without building the whole schedule, and the cumulative versions give a year's interest or a year's principal in one formula — ideal for annual models that rest on monthly-repaying loans. All of them depend on one discipline: the rate and the number of periods must use the same period length.

Core notes

The annuity family

All functions share the cash-flow sign convention: money received is positive, money paid is negative. If pv (the loan received) is positive, PMT returns a negative payment. Enter -Principal to get a positive EMI.

Function Signature Returns
PMT PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type]) Constant payment per period
IPMT IPMT(rate, per, nper, pv, [fv], [type]) Interest portion of payment number per
PPMT PPMT(rate, per, nper, pv, [fv], [type]) Principal portion of payment number per
CUMIPMT CUMIPMT(rate, nper, pv, start_period, end_period, type) Total interest between two payment numbers (negative)
CUMPRINC CUMPRINC(rate, nper, pv, start_period, end_period, type) Total principal between two payment numbers (negative)
  • rate — interest rate per period.
  • nper — total number of periods.
  • pv — principal at the start; fv — balance left at the end (0 for a fully amortising loan; a balloon is a non-zero fv).
  • type — 0 (default) payments at period-end (arrears); 1 at period-start (advance). CUMIPMT/CUMPRINC require type explicitly and require pv > 0.

For every period \(k\):

\[ \text{PMT} = \text{IPMT}_k + \text{PPMT}_k \]

and the EMI formula is

\[ \text{PMT} = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1+i)^{-n}} \]

where \(P\) is the principal, \(i\) the periodic rate and \(n\) the number of periods.

Converting annual to monthly rates

Which conversion applies depends on how the annual rate is quoted:

Quote Monthly rate Example: 8.5%
Nominal annual, compounded monthly (Indian bank EMI loans are quoted this way) = r/12 0.70833%
Effective annual rate (EAR) = (1 + r)^(1/12) - 1 0.68215%
Nominal, compounded quarterly, paid monthly = (1 + r/4)^(1/3) - 1 0.70337%

nper must also be in months: = Years * 12. EFFECT and NOMINAL convert between nominal and effective rates (topic-level related item).

Illustration: ₹40 lakh home loan

Inputs: Principal ₹40,00,000; 8.5% p.a. nominal monthly; 20 years; EMI in arrears.

=PMT(8.5%/12, 20*12, -4000000)                      EMI
=IPMT(8.5%/12, 1, 240, -4000000)                     interest in month 1
=PPMT(8.5%/12, 1, 240, -4000000)                     principal in month 1
=-CUMIPMT(8.5%/12, 240, 4000000, 1, 12, 0)           interest in year 1
=-CUMPRINC(8.5%/12, 240, 4000000, 1, 12, 0)          principal in year 1
Output Value (₹)
EMI 34,713
Month 1 interest 28,333 (= 40,00,000 × 0.70833%)
Month 1 principal 6,380
Year 1 interest 3,36,946
Year 1 principal 79,609
Balance after 12 months 39,20,391
Total interest over 20 years 43,31,103

Interest is about 82% of the EMI in month 1 — the front-loading that surprises borrowers and drives prepayment decisions. In an annual 3-statement model, year y's interest is -CUMIPMT(i, n, P, 12*(y-1)+1, 12*y, 0) and the current maturity at the end of year y is -CUMPRINC(i, n, P, 12*y+1, 12*(y+1), 0) — the principal due in the next 12 months, which Schedule III presents as current.

Building the full schedule

A row-per-period schedule (vertical spill using SEQUENCE, w9_t4) is still the reviewer's preferred evidence:

Column Formula idea
Period k =SEQUENCE(n)
Date =EOMONTH(FirstEMI, k-1)
Opening previous closing (first = Principal)
Interest =Opening * i
EMI =PMT(i, n, -P)
Principal =EMI - Interest
Closing =Opening - Principal

Checks: closing in period n equals 0 (to rounding); SUM(Interest) equals -CUMIPMT(i, n, P, 1, n, 0); IPMT(i,k,n,-P) + PPMT(i,k,n,-P) = PMT for a sample row. Indian banks round EMIs to the rupee and adjust the final instalment — model the rounding explicitly if reconciling to a bank statement.

Floating rates and resets

For repo-linked (EBLR) loans, the rate changes on reset dates. Excel's annuity functions assume a constant rate, so model each segment: on reset, recompute PMT on the outstanding balance and remaining tenor (EMI changes) — or, as many lenders do, keep the EMI and change the tenor, computed with NPER (topic-level related item). This is why a full schedule is necessary for floating-rate debt.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Mismatched periods. PMT(8.5%, 240, -P) uses an annual rate with monthly periods — the EMI becomes ₹3.4 lakh instead of ₹34,713. Always derive both rate and nper from the same frequency input.
  • Advance payments (type 1). Lease-style arrangements with payments at the start of each period: PMT with type 1 is lower than type 0 by a factor of (1 + i). IPMT for period 1 with type 1 is zero.
  • Moratorium periods. Interest-only or capitalised-interest moratoria (common in education and project loans) cannot be modelled with one PMT; split into phases.
  • Processing fees. Under Ind AS 109, upfront fees are included in the EIR; the coupon-based schedule above is not the accounting schedule. Compute EIR with IRR/XIRR on net proceeds and build the amortised-cost schedule separately.
  • Sign confusion. CUMIPMT requires positive pv and returns negatives; IPMT with positive pv returns negatives. Standardise: negate at the point of display, not inside calculation chains.
  • Bullet and balloon loans. Use fv for balloon amounts; bullets need no PMT at all — interest = balance × rate.

Key takeaways

  • PMT, IPMT, PPMT share the sign convention; enter the principal as negative to get positive outputs.
  • PPMT + IPMT = PMT in every period; CUMIPMT and CUMPRINC give totals over any period range.
  • Rate and nper must use the same period: nominal-monthly quotes use r/12; effective annual quotes use (1+r)^(1/12) − 1.
  • CUMPRINC for the next 12 months gives the current maturity required by Schedule III.
  • Floating-rate resets, moratoria and Ind AS 109 EIR need a full period-by-period schedule.

Practice Questions

Q1. EMI and first-year split [L1]

A ₹18 lakh car loan is taken at 9.75% p.a. (nominal, monthly compounding) for 5 years, EMIs in arrears. Write formulas for the EMI, interest and principal in month 13, and total interest in year 2.

Q2. Annual model from monthly loan [L2]

A ₹120 crore term loan is drawn on 1-Apr-2025 at 9.4% p.a. nominal, repayable in 96 equal monthly instalments from 30-Apr-2025. For an annual model (FY26–FY33), write the formulas for each year's interest expense, principal repaid, closing balance, and the current maturity of long-term borrowings at each year-end.

💡 Hint: Express each FY as a range of payment numbers and use CUMIPMT/CUMPRINC with type 0.

Q3. Effective vs nominal rate [L2]

A lender quotes an effective annual rate of 10.5% on a ₹2 crore loan repayable monthly over 7 years. Compute the monthly rate formula, the EMI formula, and explain the error in EMI if the analyst uses 10.5%/12 instead.

Q4. Rate reset [L3]

A ₹75 lakh home loan starts at 8.4% for 240 months. After 36 EMIs, the repo-linked rate rises to 9.15%. Design the formulas for (a) the outstanding balance after 36 EMIs, (b) the new EMI if tenor is unchanged, (c) the new remaining tenor if EMI is unchanged. Explain which option banks typically apply first and the risk it creates for the borrower.

Q5. Reconcile to the bank [L2]

Your modelled interest for FY26 on a term loan is ₹1,12,48,300; the bank's interest certificate shows ₹1,12,63,950. List five possible reasons for the difference relating to rate, day count, timing and rounding, and how you would test each.
