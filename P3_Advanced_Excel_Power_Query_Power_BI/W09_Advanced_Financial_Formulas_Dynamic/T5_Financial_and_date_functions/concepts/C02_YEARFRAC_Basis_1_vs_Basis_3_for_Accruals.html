Key concept

YEARFRAC Basis 1 vs Basis 3 for Accruals

Concept: =YEARFRAC(Start, End, 1) is actual/actual; basis 3 is actual/365, common in Indian bank accruals

Statement

=YEARFRAC(Start, End, 1)     actual/actual
=YEARFRAC(Start, End, 3)     actual/365 (fixed)

For a period of \(D\) actual days (End − Start):

\[ \text{YF}_{3} = \frac{D}{365} \qquad \text{YF}_{1} = \frac{D}{Y} \]

where \(Y\) is the year length Excel applies: 365 or 366 for periods within one year (366 when the period includes a 29 February), or the average length of the calendar years spanned for longer periods.

Explanation

Both bases count actual days in the numerator; they differ only in the denominator. Basis 3 always divides by 365, even in a leap year. Basis 1 adjusts for leap years.

Why basis 3 dominates Indian bank accruals. Rupee deposits and loans from Indian banks commonly compute interest on an actual/365 basis (banks' published interest-rate policies typically state a 365-day year; confirm for each lender, as conventions can differ for leap years), so a year-end accrual on an FD or a term loan should use basis 3 to agree with the bank's interest certificate. Many corporate treasury policies adopt actual/365 for all rupee instruments for consistency. Instruments that specify otherwise — money-market instruments linked to overseas benchmarks (actual/360), bonds with 30/360 coupons — must follow their term sheets.

When basis 1 is appropriate. Precise time apportionment where the "year" is a calendar year, such as pro-rating an annual fee over a leap year, or some bond markets' actual/actual conventions. Be aware that Excel's basis 1 algorithm is not identical to ISDA actual/actual for multi-year periods — when a contract specifies ISDA, split the period by calendar year.

Materiality. Outside leap years the two bases coincide for periods within one year. In a period containing 29 February, basis 1 gives a slightly lower fraction (÷366), so accruals are about 0.27% lower than basis 3. On a ₹1,000 crore borrowing book at 9%, a full year's difference is roughly ₹24.6 lakh — small but not trivial for a reconciliation.

Other details: YEARFRAC ignores the order of dates (returns a positive fraction even if Start > End), excludes the start day, and needs true dates (text gives #VALUE!).

Illustration

Fixed deposit of ₹10 crore at 7.4%, placed 1-Oct-2027, accrual to FY-end 31-Mar-2028 (182 days, includes 29-Feb-2028):

Basis Fraction Accrued interest (₹)
3 — actual/365 182/365 = 0.49863 36,89,863
1 — actual/actual 182/366 = 0.49727 36,79,781

Difference ₹10,082. If the bank certifies ₹36,89,863, the basis 1 schedule shows a reconciling difference that is purely methodological.

For 1-Oct-2025 to 31-Mar-2026 (182 days, no leap day), both bases give 0.49863.

Connections

Applied in s02 (all five bases), s05 and s06 (interest unwinding and accruals), and in Ind AS 109 EIR schedules from Weeks 1–4. Stub-period fractions in DCFs (Weeks 15–16) usually use actual/365 to match XNPV's 365-day convention (c04). In the close, accrual schedules feed the provision for interest accrued, and in audit, recomputation of interest uses the bank's stated basis.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute both bases [L1]

A ₹4 crore term loan at 9.6% has interest due on 31-Dec-2025 and the next on 31-Mar-2026. Write formulas for the 31-Mar-2026 interest using basis 3 and basis 1, and state whether they differ and why.

Q2. Reconcile to the bank certificate [L2]

The company's FY28 accrual schedule uses basis 1 for all 60 FDs (total ₹480 crore, average rate 7.2%), and the bank certificates use actual/365. The period from 1-Apr-2027 to 31-Mar-2028 contains 29-Feb-2028. Estimate the total reconciling difference for full-year deposits and explain which way the adjustment goes.

💡 Hint: For a full year the numerator is 366 days under both bases; only the denominator differs.

Q3. Choosing the basis [L2]

List the basis you would use, with a reason, for: (a) a rupee term loan from an Indian bank; (b) an ECB linked to SOFR; (c) pro-rating an annual software subscription billed for calendar year 2028; (d) a stub period in an XNPV-based DCF.
