Key concept

PMT, IPMT and PPMT: EMI and Its Interest/Principal Split

Concept: =PMT(rate/12, years*12, -Principal); =IPMT(rate/12, per, nper, pv); PPMT + IPMT = PMT for each period

Statement

=PMT(rate/12, years*12, -Principal)          monthly EMI (positive)
=IPMT(rate/12, per, nper, pv)                interest in payment number per
=PPMT(rate/12, per, nper, pv)                principal in payment number per

With periodic rate \(i\), number of periods \(n\) and principal \(P\):

\[ \text{PMT} = \frac{P\, i}{1-(1+i)^{-n}}, \qquad \text{IPMT}_k = i \cdot B_{k-1}, \qquad \text{PPMT}_k = \text{PMT} - \text{IPMT}_k \]

where \(B_{k-1}\) is the outstanding balance after \(k-1\) payments, so that for every period \(k\):

\[ \text{PPMT}_k + \text{IPMT}_k = \text{PMT} \]

Explanation

Period matching. rate/12 and years*12 convert an annual nominal rate and a tenor in years to monthly terms — both must change together. This is right for Indian bank EMI loans, which quote a nominal annual rate with monthly rests. If the annual rate is an effective rate, the monthly rate is \((1+r)^{1/12}-1\) instead.

Sign convention. Excel's financial functions treat cash received as positive and cash paid as negative. Entering -Principal for pv makes PMT positive (the borrower's perspective flipped for display). In the IPMT/PPMT signature in the concept, pv is shown without a sign: if you pass +Principal, IPMT and PPMT return negatives; pass -Principal (as with PMT) to get positives. Be consistent across the three so that PPMT + IPMT = PMT holds with the same sign.

Mechanics. The payment is constant; interest falls each period because it is charged on a declining balance, so the principal portion rises. Early EMIs are mostly interest — the reason prepayments early in a home loan save the most interest.

Optional arguments. fv (default 0) handles balloon payments; type 1 means payments at period start (advance), which lowers PMT by a factor of \(1+i\) and makes IPMT for period 1 zero.

Arrays. per can be an array (SEQUENCE(n)), so IPMT and PPMT spill an entire schedule in one formula each (w9_t4 s05).

Illustration

₹40 lakh home loan, 8.5% nominal annual, 20 years, monthly in arrears; i = 0.70833%, n = 240.

Formula Result (₹)
=PMT(8.5%/12, 20*12, -4000000) 34,713
=IPMT(8.5%/12, 1, 240, -4000000) 28,333
=PPMT(8.5%/12, 1, 240, -4000000) 6,380
Check: IPMT + PPMT − PMT for period 1 0

At period 120 (end of year 10), interest has fallen to about ₹19,936 and principal risen to about ₹14,777 — still the same EMI. The check =IPMT(i,k,n,-P) + PPMT(i,k,n,-P) - PMT(i,n,-P) should be zero for any k; put it in the model for a sample of periods or as a spilled check column.

Connections

Builds s05 (loan amortisation) and s06 (EMI schedules). CUMIPMT/CUMPRINC aggregate IPMT/PPMT over a period range for annual models and Schedule III current-maturity splits. RATE and NPER back-solve the rate or tenor (topic related item), and EFFECT/NOMINAL handle rate quotes. Debt schedules in the 3-statement model (Weeks 13–16) and the checklist item on loan calculations rest on this concept.

Practice Questions

Q1. Car loan [L1]

A ₹12 lakh car loan at 9.25% nominal for 4 years, EMIs in arrears. Write formulas for the EMI, the interest and principal in month 25, and a check cell proving the split.

Q2. Advance EMIs [L2]

A two-wheeler financier collects the first EMI on the disbursement date (advance EMI) on a ₹1.5 lakh loan at 14% for 24 months. Write PMT, IPMT and PPMT formulas with the correct type argument, and explain why IPMT for period 1 is zero.

💡 Hint: With type 1, no time has elapsed before the first payment.

Q3. Wrong sign, wrong period [L2]

A model has =PMT(9%, 60, 5000000) for a 5-year monthly loan of ₹50 lakh. Identify both errors, state the approximate magnitude of the EMI it shows, and write the corrected formula.
