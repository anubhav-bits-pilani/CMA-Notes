Key concept

Month-End, Month-Start and Same-Day Offsets with EOMONTH and EDATE

Concept: =EOMONTH(A2, 0) for month-end; =EOMONTH(A2, -1)+1 for month-start; =EDATE(A2, 3) for the same day three months later

Statement

=EOMONTH(A2, 0)        last day of A2's month
=EOMONTH(A2, -1) + 1   first day of A2's month
=EDATE(A2, 3)          same day-of-month, three months after A2

For a date \(d\) in month \(m\) of year \(y\):

\[ \text{EOMONTH}(d, k) = \text{last day of month } (m + k) \qquad \text{EDATE}(d, k) = \min\big(\text{DAY}(d),\ \text{days in month } (m+k)\big) \text{ of month } (m+k) \]

where \(k\) is an integer number of months (negative values move backwards; years roll over automatically).

Explanation

These three are the building blocks of all period logic in finance workbooks:

  • Month-end (EOMONTH(d, 0)) assigns a transaction to its accounting period and creates month-end headers. Every value in a month maps to the same month-end, regardless of day.
  • Month-start (EOMONTH(d, -1) + 1) is "the day after last month's end". It is more robust than DATE(YEAR(d), MONTH(d), 1) only in style — both are correct — but it pairs naturally with EOMONTH-based headers: the window for header H is EOMONTH(H, -1) + 1 to H.
  • Same-day offsets (EDATE(d, n)) give maturities, due dates on "n months" terms, review dates and prior-period comparatives (EDATE(d, -12)). When the target month is shorter, EDATE clips to the month-end: EDATE(31-Jan-2026, 1) = 28-Feb-2026.

Why not arithmetic like d + 30 or d + 90? Month lengths vary (28–31 days), so +30 drifts across months and +90 from 1-Nov lands on 30-Jan, not 1-Feb. A three-month FD booked on 1-Nov-2025 matures on 1-Feb-2026 (EDATE), not 30-Jan-2026 (+90) — a two-day difference in interest and in the cut-off of maturity proceeds.

Chaining is a subtle trap: EDATE(EDATE(31-Jan, 1), 1) = EDATE(28-Feb, 1) = 28-Mar, whereas EDATE(31-Jan, 2) = 31-Mar. Always offset from the original anchor, or use EOMONTH where a month-end convention applies.

Both functions return serial numbers (format as dates), accept arrays for the months argument (so they spill with SEQUENCE), and return #VALUE! if the date is text.

Illustration

A2 = 17-Aug-2025:

Formula Result
=EOMONTH(A2, 0) 31-Aug-2025
=EOMONTH(A2, -1) + 1 01-Aug-2025
=EDATE(A2, 3) 17-Nov-2025
=EOMONTH(A2, 6) 28-Feb-2026
=EDATE(A2, -12) 17-Aug-2024

A2 = 30-Nov-2025: =EDATE(A2, 3) = 28-Feb-2026 (clipped); =EDATE(A2, 6) = 30-May-2026 (not 31-May).

SUMIFS for the month containing A2:

=SUMIFS(GL[Amt], GL[Date], ">=" & (EOMONTH(A2, -1) + 1), GL[Date], "<=" & EOMONTH(A2, 0))

Connections

Core of s01 (period-end logic) and s08 (FY helpers), and the header pattern EOMONTH(Start, SEQUENCE(1, 12, 0)) from w9_t4 c04.
