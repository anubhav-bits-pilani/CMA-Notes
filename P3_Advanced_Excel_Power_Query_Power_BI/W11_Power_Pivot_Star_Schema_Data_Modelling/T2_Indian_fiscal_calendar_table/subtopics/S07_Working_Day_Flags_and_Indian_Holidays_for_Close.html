Working-Day Flags and Indian Holidays for Close Metrics

Scope: Working-day flags and Indian holidays for close-calendar metrics

Why it matters

The month-end close is managed in working days, not calendar days: "TB on WD2, MIS on WD4, board pack on WD7". Close-calendar dashboards track whether each task finished on its due working day, how many working days the close took, and how many journals were posted after WD3. Collections and payments analysis also needs working days — DSO-style metrics on bank working days, cheque-clearing timelines, and payment runs that skip bank holidays. In India, holidays differ by state (Maharashtra Day, Pongal, Onam, Durga Puja), banks close on second and fourth Saturdays, and many festival dates move every year, so a generic WEEKDAY test is not enough. A Date table with working-day flags driven by a maintained holiday list solves all of these at once.

Core notes

Holiday master

Maintain a small table (Excel/SharePoint list) loaded through Power Query:

HolidayDate HolidayName Scope State Applies To
26-Jan-2026 Republic Day National All Company, Bank
04-Mar-2026 Holi State Maharashtra Company, Bank
01-May-2026 Maharashtra Day State Maharashtra Company, Bank
15-Aug-2025 Independence Day National All Company, Bank
02-Oct-2025 Gandhi Jayanti National All Company, Bank
21-Oct-2025 Diwali (Lakshmi Puja) Company All Company

(Festival dates above are illustrative; take actual dates each year from the company's published holiday list and, for bank holidays, the RBI's state-wise list under the Negotiable Instruments Act.) Three national holidays (26 January, 15 August, 2 October) are fixed; most others vary with the lunar calendar.

Date table columns

Power Query (merge the holiday list on Date for the relevant scope, e.g. head office state):

AddDOW     = Table.AddColumn(Prev, "WeekdayNo", each Date.DayOfWeek([Date], Day.Monday) + 1, Int64.Type),
AddSatNo   = Table.AddColumn(AddDOW, "SaturdayOfMonth",
                 each if [WeekdayNo] = 6 then Number.IntegerDivide(Date.Day([Date]) - 1, 7) + 1 else null, Int64.Type),
MergeHol   = Table.NestedJoin(AddSatNo, {"Date"}, HolidaysHO, {"HolidayDate"}, "Hol", JoinKind.LeftOuter),
AddIsHol   = Table.AddColumn(MergeHol, "IsHoliday", each Table.RowCount([Hol]) > 0, type logical),
AddIsWD    = Table.AddColumn(AddIsHol, "IsWorkingDay",
                 each [WeekdayNo] <> 7 and not [IsHoliday]
                      and not ([WeekdayNo] = 6 and List.Contains({2, 4}, [SaturdayOfMonth])), type logical),
Cleaned    = Table.RemoveColumns(AddIsWD, {"Hol"})

This encodes a typical Indian office calendar: Sundays off, second and fourth Saturdays off, plus holidays. Adjust the weekend rule to the entity (many corporates have all Saturdays off; plants may work six days).

Then add, in DAX or Power Query:

  • WorkingDayNoInMonth: cumulative count of working days within the month, blank on non-working days. WD1 is the first working day of the month.
  • WorkingDaysInMonth: total working days in the month.
  • WorkingDayNoAfterMonthEnd: for close tracking, working days elapsed after the prior month-end (the "close day" of the prior month: 1 May = WD1 of the April close if 1 May is a working day; if 1 May is Maharashtra Day, 2 May is WD1).
WorkingDayNoInMonth =
IF (
    'Date'[IsWorkingDay],
    CALCULATE (
        COUNTROWS ( 'Date' ),
        ALLEXCEPT ( 'Date', 'Date'[MonthEnd] ),
        'Date'[Date] <= EARLIER ( 'Date'[Date] ),
        'Date'[IsWorkingDay] = TRUE ()
    )
)

In a calculated column, CALCULATE triggers context transition (the current row becomes a filter on every column of 'Date'); ALLEXCEPT('Date', 'Date'[MonthEnd]) removes those filters except the month, and the EARLIER comparison restores the "up to this date" condition. A VAR-based version (VAR d = 'Date'[Date]) is clearer and avoids EARLIER.

DAX also offers NETWORKDAYS(start, end, [weekend], [holidays]) (added in 2023) for ad hoc counts in measures, where holidays is a single-column table of dates and weekend is a code (1 = Sat–Sun; 11 = Sunday only). It cannot express "second and fourth Saturday" rules, so for Indian calendars the flag-based approach is more accurate.

Close-calendar metrics

With a Close fact (task, owner, due WD, completion timestamp) or GL posting timestamps:

Close Days Taken =
VAR MonthClosed = MAX ( 'Date'[MonthEnd] )
VAR SignOff     = CALCULATE ( MAX ( Close[SignOffDate] ), Close[Task] = "MIS release" )
RETURN
    CALCULATE (
        COUNTROWS ( 'Date' ),
        REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ),
        'Date'[Date] > MonthClosed,
        'Date'[Date] <= SignOff,
        'Date'[IsWorkingDay] = TRUE ()
    )

Late Journals (after WD3) =
CALCULATE (
    DISTINCTCOUNT ( GL[VoucherNo] ),
    GL[EntryWorkingDayAfterClose] > 3
)

(The second needs an entry-date column on GL mapped to a working-day number via the Date table — usually computed in Power Query by merging GL entry date to Date.)

Other uses: average daily sales per working day (\(\text{Revenue} / \text{WorkingDaysInMonth}\)) to normalise months with many holidays (October–November festival months); bank-working-day ageing of receipts; payroll attendance.

Illustration

April 2026, Mumbai head office, rule: Sundays and 2nd/4th Saturdays off, holidays per company list (assume 3-Apr Good Friday, 14-Apr Ambedkar Jayanti). Calendar days 30; Sundays 4 (5, 12, 19, 26); 2nd and 4th Saturdays 2 (11 and 25); holidays 2 → working days \(30 - 4 - 2 - 2 = 22\). If March 2026 MIS sign-off happened on 8 April, working days after 31 March up to 8 April: 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8 April (3 April holiday, 5 April Sunday) = 6 → close took WD6 against a WD4 target.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Multi-state entities. A company with plants in Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal has different holiday sets; keep IsWorkingDay per location in a separate Location × Date table, or flag only the head-office calendar used for close.
  • Holidays on weekends. A holiday on a Sunday does not reduce working days twice — the logic above handles it.
  • Declared holidays. Elections and sudden state holidays (bandh, weather) are added mid-year; refresh the list.
  • Bank vs company calendar. Payment run and NEFT/RTGS availability follow bank holidays (RTGS operates 24×7 since December 2020, but cheque clearing does not); keep separate flags.
  • Fiscal year-end. 31 March is a working day for banks for government transactions in many years per RBI instructions, even if a Sunday; annual closing on 1 April is a bank holiday for public dealing.

Key takeaways

  • Close calendars run in working days; build IsWorkingDay from weekend rules plus a maintained holiday list.
  • Indian rules: Sundays, second/fourth Saturdays (banks and many offices), national and state holidays.
  • Add WorkingDayNoInMonth and WorkingDaysInMonth for WD-based close metrics and per-working-day normalisation.
  • NETWORKDAYS handles simple weekends; flags handle Indian Saturday rules and state calendars.
  • Keep separate company and bank calendars where payments and close differ.
