Roadmap topic: Custom Indian Fiscal Calendar Table: Building a Date dimension supporting April–March fiscal years, quarters, and week offsets for time intelligence

Overview

The Date dimension is the most reused table in any finance model. Every YTD, prior-year, rolling-12-month, closing balance and close-calendar measure depends on it, and every matrix header — FY, quarter, month, week — comes from it. Indian companies report on an April–March year under Sec 2(41) of the Companies Act 2013, quarterly results under SEBI LODR Reg 33 end on 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March, and the Income-tax Act uses "previous year" and "assessment year" labels. Power BI's built-in Auto date/time assumes January–December. So the first deliverable of every Indian FP&A model is a custom fiscal calendar built, validated and governed once.

For FP&A roles, this topic is less about formulas — each column is a one-liner — and more about getting the invariants right: contiguous dates covering whole fiscal years and every fact date; fiscal month, quarter and week numbering anchored on 1 April; sort columns so April comes first; the table marked as the date table with Auto date/time off; and flags and offsets so dashboards roll forward with the close calendar instead of being edited by hand every month. A controller who has reconciled TBs will recognise the logic: the calendar is the period master, and IsClosedPeriod is the period-lock.

The learning sequence: decide where to build the table (DAX CALENDAR + ADDCOLUMNS vs Power Query List.Dates, s01) and what range it must cover (s02); add the columns — FY label, fiscal quarter and month, month name and month-end, fiscal week, period key (s03); fix sorting (s04); add offset columns for dynamic filters (s05); mark the table and switch off Auto date/time (s06); then add finance-specific attributes — working days and Indian holidays for close metrics (s07) and IsClosedPeriod / IsYTD flags for like-for-like comparisons (s08). The concepts are the exact formulas: the DAX table with FYEnd (c01), CALENDARAUTO(3) (c02), fiscal month and quarter (c03), fiscal week (c04), month offset (c05) and the Power Query List.Dates generator (c06).

Related concepts to study

Week 10: FY logic as M custom columns

→ Week 10: the same FY logic written as M custom columns

Every DAX column in this topic has an M equivalent you wrote last week: if Date.Month([Date]) >= 4 then Date.Year([Date]) + 1 else Date.Year([Date]) for FYEnd, Number.Mod(Date.Month([Date]) + 8, 12) + 1 for fiscal month, Date.EndOfMonth for month-end. Building the calendar in Power Query keeps all ETL in one place and allows a dataflow-based corporate calendar. The same logic was also used to stamp FY and period onto the GL and budget during cleansing; make sure both use identical rules, or the GL's FY column and the Date table's FY will disagree at the 31 March / 1 April boundary. Prefer relating facts to the Date table and dropping redundant FY columns from facts.

4-4-5 and retail calendars

4-4-5 and retail calendars, where built-in time intelligence fails and custom offset logic is needed

Retailers and some FMCG/consumer companies report in 4-4-5 (or 4-5-4, 5-4-4) periods: each quarter has two four-week months and one five-week month, so periods always end on the same weekday and contain whole weeks. A 53rd week is added every five or six years. DAX built-in functions (DATESYTD, SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, DATEADD) assume calendar months and break here. The solution is a Date table with explicit columns — FiscalYear, FiscalPeriodNo, FiscalWeekNo, and sequential offsets (PeriodSeq, WeekSeq) — and custom measures that filter on those integers, e.g. prior-year period = same FiscalPeriodNo with FiscalYear − 1. Indian statutory reporting remains April–March, so 4-4-5 is a management-reporting overlay, requiring reconciliation of period cut-offs to month-ends.

Rolling forecast windows and closed-period flags (Weeks 17–20)

→ Week 17–20: rolling forecast windows and closed-period flags

Rolling forecasts look a fixed number of months ahead (e.g. 12 or 18) from the latest closed month, regardless of fiscal year. The MonthOffsetClosed column built here defines that window: offsets +1 to +12 are the forecast horizon, 0 and below are actuals. IsClosedPeriod decides, month by month, whether a blended "outlook" measure uses actuals or forecast, and stops budget for open months from distorting YTD variance. Full-year outlook = YTD actual (closed months) + forecast for remaining months — evaluated per month with SUMX over MonthEnd so it totals correctly. These columns are therefore the hinge between this week's model and the FP&A planning weeks.

Calculation groups for time intelligence

Calculation groups that apply time intelligence without duplicating measures

Without calculation groups, every base measure (Revenue, Gross Profit, EBITDA, Opex) needs YTD, PY, PY YTD, YoY % and R12M variants — dozens of near-identical measures. A calculation group defines each time transformation once as a calculation item (e.g. YTD = CALCULATE(SELECTEDMEASURE(), DATESYTD('Date'[Date], "3/31"))) and applies it to whichever measure is in the visual. Calculation groups require a marked Date table with whole fiscal years and, in Power BI, force "discourage implicit measures". They can be created in Power BI Desktop's model explorer or Tabular Editor. Format strings can be dynamic per item (percentage for YoY %). Topic 4 and topic 6 use them.

Common pitfalls

Calendar not covering whole fiscal years

A calendar that does not cover whole fiscal years, so March YTD or prior-year measures return blanks

The mistake: building the Date table from MIN and MAX of GL dates, or from calendar-year bounds (1 January to 31 December), so the first or last fiscal year is truncated. It happens because "use the data range" sounds logical. Consequences: SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR and DATEADD return blank where shifted dates are missing; CLOSINGBALANCEYEAR with "3/31" is blank for a year that ends after the calendar does; budget and due dates fall into "(Blank)". Correct: round to 1 April and 31 March and include all fact date columns (s02). Detect: query MIN/MAX of 'Date'[Date] and check they are 1 April and 31 March; compare row count to the expected number of days.

Passing a fact date column to time intelligence

Passing GL[Date] instead of the Date table column to time-intelligence functions

The mistake: writing TOTALYTD([Actual], GL[PostingDate], "3/31") or SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(GL[PostingDate]). It happens because the fact date column is visible and seems equivalent. Time-intelligence functions then operate on the fact's own dates, which have gaps (holidays, Sundays) and do not filter Budget or TB; results are wrong or partial, and Budget YTD does not align. Correct: always pass 'Date'[Date] from the marked Date table, and hide fact date columns. Detect: search measure text (DAX query view or Tabular Editor) for time-intelligence functions whose first argument is a fact table column.

Month names sorted alphabetically or starting in January

Month names sorted alphabetically or starting in January in visuals

The mistake: leaving MonthName sorted by itself (Apr, Aug, Dec …) or sorted by calendar month number (Jan first). It happens because the default sort is alphabetical and calendar month number is the obvious helper. Correct: sort MonthName by FiscalMonthNo and MonthYear by PeriodKey (s04); set visual sort to the month axis ascending. Detect: open any matrix or slicer with months — April must be first; also confirm that measures removing month filters also remove the sort column.

Date keys with a time part

Date keys stored as datetime with a time part, so relationships fail to match

The mistake: GL posting dates loaded as datetime with times (e.g. 15-05-2025 14:32:10 from an ERP timestamp), while 'Date'[Date] is at midnight. The relationship matches only rows at exactly 00:00:00, so most GL rows fall into "(Blank)" and date-filtered measures drop them. It happens when source columns are timestamps or when Power Query types are left as datetime. Correct: convert to date in Power Query (DateTime.Date or change type to Date) on both sides; keep a separate time column only if needed. Detect: the RI test (topic 1 c05) on dates, a "(Blank)" member in a Date-based visual, or the Date column showing times in Data view.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build and validate the corporate calendar [L3]

An NSE-listed Mumbai FMCG company has GL from 1-Apr-2021, budget for FY2025-26 and FY2026-27 (MonthStart), and AP due dates up to 30-Sep-2027. It needs: FY label ("FY2025-26"), FYShort ("FY26"), fiscal quarter, fiscal month, month name, MonthYear, month-end, FY-anchored fiscal week, PeriodKey, MonthOffsetClosed, FYOffset, IsWorkingDay (Sundays and 2nd/4th Saturdays off plus a holiday list), IsClosedPeriod and IsYTDComparable (driven by a Close parameter table). Required: (i) state the date range and row count; (ii) choose DAX or Power Query and justify; (iii) write the formula for each column; (iv) list all Sort by Column settings; (v) list five validation queries you would run before publishing.

💡 Hint: Remember that the range must reach the end of the fiscal year containing the latest due date.

Q2. Debug a YTD report [L2]

A matrix shows FY2025-26 with months on rows and [YTD Actual] = TOTALYTD([Actual], GL[PostingDate], "3/31"), [PY YTD] = CALCULATE([YTD Actual], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR('Date'[Date])). Findings: months appear Apr, Aug, Dec …; PY YTD is blank for April–June; Auto date/time is on; Date table runs 1-Jul-2024 to 31-Dec-2025; GL[PostingDate] is datetime. Required: explain each symptom's cause, and list the fixes in the order you would apply them.

Q3. Close calendar and flags [L3]

The March 2026 close for a Chennai-headquartered company runs: TB on WD2, MIS on WD4, board pack on WD7. 1-Apr-2026 is a Wednesday; holidays: 3-Apr (Good Friday), 14-Apr (Tamil New Year / Ambedkar Jayanti). Weekend rule: Sundays and 2nd/4th Saturdays. Required: (i) list WD1–WD7 dates for the March close; (ii) describe the Close parameter table and how IsClosedPeriod changes on MIS sign-off; (iii) explain what a "YoY revenue growth" card shows on 6-Apr-2026 with and without IsYTDComparable if the card compares FY totals.

Q4. Offsets for a rolling dashboard [L2]

The last closed month-end is 31-Dec-2025. Design report filters (using offset columns only) for: last three closed months; same three months of FY2024-25; current fiscal YTD (closed months); and the next six months of forecast. For each, state the offset column and value range, and explain what changes after the January close.

Q5. 4-4-5 overlay [L3]

A retail subsidiary reports management results on a 4-4-5 calendar starting on the Monday nearest 1 April, while statutory accounts remain April–March. Required: design the extra Date-table columns, explain why DATESYTD and SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR cannot be used for the 4-4-5 view, write the prior-year-period measure using sequence or offset columns, and describe how you would reconcile the 4-4-5 year to the statutory FY revenue.
