Subtopic note

Opening Balances with OPENINGBALANCEMONTH and OPENINGBALANCEYEAR

Scope: Opening balances with OPENINGBALANCEMONTH/YEAR and the fiscal year-end argument

Why it matters

Every balance sheet schedule in an Ind AS financial statement and every 3-statement model is a roll-forward: opening balance + additions − deletions ± other movements = closing balance (PPE under Ind AS 16 reconciliation of carrying amount, provisions under Ind AS 37, borrowings in the Ind AS 7 net debt reconciliation, trade receivables in an ECL movement table). In a model, the opening balance of a period is the closing balance of the previous one. DAX provides a family of functions that evaluate a balance measure at period boundaries; used with the right year-end argument, they make roll-forward matrices a few lines of code.

Core notes

The function family

Function Evaluates the expression at
OPENINGBALANCEMONTH(expr, dates [, filter]) The day before the first date of the month in context (i.e., the previous month-end)
OPENINGBALANCEQUARTER(expr, dates [, filter]) The day before the first date of the quarter
OPENINGBALANCEYEAR(expr, dates [, filter] [, year_end]) The day before the first date of the year (with year-end "3/31": 31 March of the previous FY)
CLOSINGBALANCEMONTH(expr, dates [, filter]) The last date of the month in context
CLOSINGBALANCEQUARTER(expr, dates [, filter]) The last date of the quarter
CLOSINGBALANCEYEAR(expr, dates [, filter] [, year_end]) The last date of the year (31 March with "3/31")

They are shorthand for CALCULATE(expr, <boundary date>): OPENINGBALANCEYEAR(expr, dates, "3/31") behaves like CALCULATE(expr, PREVIOUSDAY(STARTOFYEAR(dates, "3/31"))), and CLOSINGBALANCEYEAR like CALCULATE(expr, ENDOFYEAR(dates, "3/31")). Microsoft's documentation describes the opening functions loosely as evaluating "at the first date"; behaviourally the value returned is the balance at the end of the previous period, which is what an opening balance means. Verify once in your model with a known TB.

Only the year variants take the year-end argument. Months are the same in both calendars, and fiscal quarters coincide with calendar quarters for an April–March year, so the month and quarter variants need nothing.

Which expression to pass

The functions do not compute balances; they move the date filter. The expression must already be a balance measure evaluated at a single date:

  • Snapshot fact: [Snapshot Bal] = SUM(TB[Balance]) — at a single month-end date it returns that month's TB balance. Opening for FY26 = TB at 31 March 2025.
  • Movement fact: [Cum Bal] (running total from inception, s04) — at a single date it returns the cumulative balance to that date. Opening for FY26 = cumulative to 31 March 2025.
Opening Bal FY   = OPENINGBALANCEYEAR  ( [Cum Bal], 'Date'[Date], "3/31" )
Opening Bal Qtr  = OPENINGBALANCEQUARTER ( [Cum Bal], 'Date'[Date] )
Opening Bal Mth  = OPENINGBALANCEMONTH ( [Cum Bal], 'Date'[Date] )
Closing Bal FY   = CLOSINGBALANCEYEAR  ( [Snapshot Bal], 'Date'[Date], "3/31" )
Movement         = CALCULATE ( SUM ( GL[Amount] ), GL[VoucherType] <> "Opening" )
Closing (roll)   = [Opening Bal FY] + CALCULATE ( [Movement], DATESYTD ( 'Date'[Date], "3/31" ) )

Closing (roll) must equal [Cum Bal] at the same date; that equality is the roll-forward control.

Roll-forward matrix

A schedule in the 3-statement style for borrowings, by quarter of FY26:

Line Measure
Opening balance OPENINGBALANCEQUARTER([Cum Bal Borrowings], 'Date'[Date])
Drawdowns CALCULATE(-[Movement], GL[TxnType] = "Drawdown")
Repayments CALCULATE(-[Movement], GL[TxnType] = "Repayment")
Ind AS 109 EIR unwinding CALCULATE(-[Movement], GL[TxnType] = "EIR")
Closing balance CLOSINGBALANCEQUARTER([Cum Bal Borrowings], 'Date'[Date])
Check Opening + movements − Closing = 0

(Borrowings are credits; the minus signs present them as positive amounts.)

Year-end argument and locale

As for DATESYTD, the year-end string is parsed in the model locale ("3/31" vs "31/3", see Topic 4 c06). Omitting it makes OPENINGBALANCEYEAR return the balance at 31 December of the previous calendar year — a value that looks plausible but corresponds to no reported date. Test: the FY26 opening must equal the audited 31 March 2025 balance in the FY25 annual report.

Date table coverage

The opening of the first FY in the model is evaluated on 31 March of the year before the calendar starts. If the Date table begins on 1 April 2023, OPENINGBALANCEYEAR for FY24 asks for 31 March 2023 — a date not in the table — and returns blank. Two consequences:

  1. Extend the Date table one year back (1 April 2022) if FY24 opening balances are needed in schedules.
  2. For roll-forwards, date the opening entries on 31 March 2023 (inside the extended calendar) rather than on 1 April 2023. Then the FY24 opening equals the audited balance and FY24 movements exclude the opening voucher. If they are dated 1 April 2023, FY24 opening = 0 and FY24 "movement" includes the whole opening balance.

Worked illustration

Trade payables (credit, shown positive, ₹ lakh). Opening entry 31 March 2025: 2,140. FY26 movements: purchases Q1 3,050, payments Q1 2,900; purchases Q2 3,300, payments Q2 3,410.

Q1 FY26 Q2 FY26 H1 / YTD Sep
Opening 2,140 2,290 2,140 (OPENINGBALANCEYEAR)
Purchases 3,050 3,300 6,350
Payments (2,900) (3,410) (6,310)
Closing 2,290 2,180 2,180

The Q2 opening (2,290) comes from OPENINGBALANCEQUARTER, i.e., the balance at 30 June. On the YTD column, the opening must come from OPENINGBALANCEYEAR — using OPENINGBALANCEQUARTER on a two-quarter selection would give the opening of the last quarter in context, not of the half-year.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Period selection width: the functions use the period containing the last visible date for closings and the corresponding start for openings; on a multi-month selection, OPENINGBALANCEMONTH returns the opening of the last month, not of the selection. Use the function matching the column's grain, or compute opening as [Cum Bal] at MIN('Date'[Date]) - 1.
  • CLOSINGBALANCE on incomplete periods:* CLOSINGBALANCEYEAR evaluates at 31 March 2026 for FY26 — blank on a snapshot fact before year-end, the cumulative-to-date value on a movement fact (because no postings exist after the last actuals date). Cap or use LASTNONBLANK.
  • Snapshot dated month-start: if snapshots are stamped on the first of the month, OPENINGBALANCEMONTH (previous month-end) hits no data. Re-date snapshots to month-end.
  • Reclassifications at year-end: current maturities of long-term borrowings are reclassified at 31 March under Schedule III; if posted as a GL entry, the FY opening of "current borrowings" includes it — the roll-forward must show a "reclassification" line.
  • Opening of P&L lines: for P&L accounts the opening concept is not meaningful except in a cumulative GL; restrict opening/closing measures to BS accounts.

Key takeaways

  • OPENINGBALANCE* returns the previous period-end value; CLOSINGBALANCE* the period-end value.
  • Only the year variants take a year-end argument; always pass "3/31" (locale-checked).
  • Pass a balance measure (snapshot sum or cumulative) — the functions only move the date.
  • Extend the calendar back a year and date opening entries on the prior 31 March for clean roll-forwards.
  • Prove Opening + Movements = Closing for every schedule and period.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write a PPE roll-forward [L2]

Using a movement GL with transaction types Addition, Disposal, Depreciation and Opening, write measures for a PPE roll-forward by quarter (opening, additions, disposals at carrying amount, depreciation, closing, check) for FY26, including the year-end argument where required.

Q2. Opening blank for the first year [L1]

The Date table runs 1 April 2023 – 31 March 2026 and opening entries are dated 1 April 2023. Explain what OPENINGBALANCEYEAR([Cum Bal], 'Date'[Date], "3/31") returns for FY24, FY25 and FY26 and what FY24 "additions" show, and propose the fix.

💡 Hint: Find the exact date at which the FY24 opening is evaluated and whether it exists in the calendar.

Q3. Roll-forward numbers [L2]

Provision for warranties (₹ lakh): balance at 31 March 2025 was 480. FY26 Q1: created 95, utilised 60; Q2: created 110, utilised 140, unwinding of discount 6; Q3: created 100, utilised 90, reversed unused 25. Prepare the quarterly and YTD roll-forward table your measures should produce, identifying which DAX function provides each opening figure.

Q4. Missing year-end argument [L2]

A model omits the year-end argument in OPENINGBALANCEYEAR. The FY26 opening shown for cash is ₹ 1,060 lakh while the audited 31 March 2025 cash balance is ₹ 1,325 lakh. Explain exactly which balance ₹ 1,060 lakh represents and how a reviewer would detect this without opening the DAX code.
