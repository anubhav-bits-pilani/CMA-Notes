Subtopic note

Additive, Semi-Additive and Non-Additive Financial Measures

Scope: Additive (P&L flows) vs semi-additive (balances) vs non-additive (ratios) measures

Why it matters

Every number in a finance model has an additivity class that decides how it may be aggregated. Revenue for a quarter is the sum of three months; receivables at a quarter-end are not the sum of three month-end balances; EBITDA margin for a quarter is neither a sum nor an average of monthly margins. A matrix in Power BI or a PivotTable on Power Pivot aggregates whatever the measure returns at every level — month, quarter, FY, total — so a measure written without considering additivity will produce absurd subtotals (₹ 13,800 lakh of "receivables" for a company whose TB shows ₹ 1,150 lakh). Classifying additivity is the first design decision for every measure, and it is the cross-cutting idea that decides which DAX pattern to use.

Core notes

The three classes

Class Definition Finance examples Aggregation over time Aggregation over other dimensions
Additive Can be summed across every dimension Revenue, COGS, employee cost, GST output tax, capex additions, cash receipts Sum Sum
Semi-additive Can be summed across some dimensions but not time Cash balance, trade receivables, inventory, borrowings, headcount at month-end, FD balance Take the value at a point (closing, opening) or average Sum (across accounts, branches, entities)
Non-additive Cannot be summed across any dimension Gross margin %, EBITDA %, DSO, ROCE, average selling price, distinct customer count, interest rate Recompute from components Recompute from components

The distinction is between flows (measured over a period: P&L items, cash-flow items, movements) and stocks (measured at a point in time: balance sheet items). Ratios combine flows and/or stocks and must always be recomputed at each level from their additive or semi-additive components.

Additive measures (P&L flows)

Actual  = SUM ( GL[Amount] )
Revenue = CALCULATE ( -[Actual], CoA[FSLine] = "Revenue from operations" )

A flow summed over months gives the quarter; summed over branches gives the company. Time intelligence (YTD, R12M, PY) applies directly. Even within the balance sheet, movements are additive: "receivables movement for Q3" = sum of monthly movements; "additions to PPE YTD" (from the fixed asset register or GL) = sum of monthly additions.

Semi-additive measures (balances)

A balance at 31 December is the result of everything posted since inception. Over the time dimension, the right aggregate is a point value:

  • Closing balance — value at the last date of the period (most common: balance sheet presentation, Schedule III figures "as at" the reporting date).
  • Opening balance — value at the last date of the previous period (roll-forwards, s05).
  • Average balance — mean of period-end values (for DSO, interest yield, ROCE denominators, s06).

Across accounts, branches and entities, balances are additive: receivables of Mumbai + Delhi = company receivables at the same date.

Closing Bal =
CALCULATE ( SUM ( TB[Balance] ), LASTDATE ( 'Date'[Date] ) )

At a month row, LASTDATE returns the month-end; at a quarter row, the quarter-end; at the FY row, 31 March — so the quarter shows the December balance, not the sum of three month-ends.

Non-additive measures (ratios)

EBITDA % = DIVIDE ( [EBITDA], [Revenue] )
DSO      = DIVIDE ( [Avg AR], [Revenue] ) * [Days in Period]

Each is computed from its components in the current filter context, so the quarter margin is quarter EBITDA ÷ quarter revenue. A calculated column holding a ratio, or a measure that averages row-level ratios, is wrong at every subtotal. Distinct counts (number of active customers) are also non-additive: a customer buying in April and May is one customer for the quarter.

Worked illustration

Trade receivables (month-end TB, ₹ lakh) and revenue for Q3 FY26:

Oct Nov Dec Q3 (wrong: sum) Q3 (right)
Revenue 1,200 1,050 1,350 3,600 3,600 (sum)
Closing AR 1,480 1,390 1,520 4,390 1,520 (closing at 31 Dec)
Avg AR (3 month-ends) — — — — 1,463
Gross margin % 32% 28% 35% 95% or 31.7% (avg) GP ÷ Revenue for Q3

Gross profit Oct 384, Nov 294, Dec 472.5; Q3 GP = 1,150.5, so Q3 margin = 1,150.5 ÷ 3,600 = 32.0% — neither the sum (95%) nor the simple average (31.7%) of monthly margins.

Implementing the classification in a model

  1. Add an attribute to the CoA (or the measure catalogue) such as CoA[Statement] = "PL" / "BS", or a measure-level property noted in the description, so each FS line's additivity is known.
  2. Keep one base measure per class: [Actual] for flows, [Closing Bal] for balances.
  3. Route a mixed financial-statement matrix through a dispatcher: P&L rows use [Actual] (with YTD views), BS rows use [Closing Bal].
FS Value =
IF (
    SELECTEDVALUE ( CoA[Statement] ) = "BS",
    [Closing Bal],
    [Actual]
)
  1. Write ratio measures only with DIVIDE on measures, never on columns.
  2. Test totals: the FY row of each balance must equal the 31 March TB; the FY row of each flow must equal the sum of months; each ratio at FY must equal FY numerator ÷ FY denominator.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Headcount is semi-additive (people at month-end); FTE-months (person-months) is additive; average headcount is semi-additive averaged. Know which one the HR cost-per-employee KPI needs.
  • Inventory valuation under Ind AS 2 is a balance; "consumption" (opening + purchases − closing) is a flow derived from two balances and one flow.
  • Accrual-based flows such as Ind AS 116 interest on lease liabilities are additive; the lease liability is semi-additive.
  • Mixed facts: a TB fact containing both P&L and BS accounts at month-end needs the P&L accounts' movement (or YTD balance), not their cumulative balance, if your source TB is YTD-cumulative for P&L (as most ERP TBs are).
  • "Average" is not always semi-additive: average invoice value (revenue ÷ invoice count) is non-additive.
  • Totals across time for a balance can still be useful for averages (sum of month-end balances ÷ 12), but never displayed as a balance.

Key takeaways

  • Flows are additive; balances are semi-additive (sum across accounts/entities, point-in-time across dates); ratios are non-additive.
  • Choose the measure pattern by class: SUM for flows, LASTDATE/LASTNONBLANK or cumulative patterns for balances, DIVIDE of components for ratios.
  • Quarter and FY rows are the acid test: balances must equal the period-end TB.
  • Keep one base measure per class and dispatch by statement in mixed matrices.
  • Distinct counts and averages are also non-additive.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the KPIs [L1]

Classify each of the following as additive, semi-additive or non-additive and state the correct aggregation for a quarter: (a) GST input tax credit availed; (b) balance in the electronic credit ledger; (c) inventory days; (d) number of active dealers; (e) term-loan principal outstanding; (f) interest expense on term loan; (g) month-end headcount; (h) salary cost per employee.

Q2. Fix the subtotals [L2]

A matrix with months on columns and FS lines on rows uses SUM(TB[Balance]) for every line. For FY25 it shows Cash ₹ 14,600 lakh, Borrowings −₹ 62,400 lakh and Revenue −₹ 18,900 lakh at the FY total, while the 31 March 2025 TB shows Cash ₹ 1,240 lakh and Borrowings −₹ 5,180 lakh, and revenue for FY25 is ₹ 18,900 lakh. Explain each number and design the measures (and any CoA column) needed to make the matrix correct.

💡 Hint: Separate the treatment for BS and P&L rows before thinking about signs.

Q3. Margin aggregation [L2]

Three branches report Q2 revenue (₹ crore) and EBITDA margin: Surat 40 at 22%, Nagpur 10 at 8%, Kochi 25 at 15%. Compute the company EBITDA % correctly and explain why a measure that averages branch margins gives a different answer.

Q4. Design a measure catalogue [L3]

You are building the standard measure catalogue for a group FP&A model covering P&L, balance sheet, cash flow and operational KPIs (headcount, store count, customers). Design the catalogue columns (name, additivity class, base pattern, time views allowed, format) and fill in ten representative rows, justifying the class chosen for each.
