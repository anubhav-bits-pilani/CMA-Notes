Subtopic note

Snapshot Facts vs Transaction Facts for Balances

Scope: Snapshot facts (month-end TB balances) vs transaction facts (GL movements plus opening balance)

Why it matters

There are two ways to get a balance sheet into a model. You can load the trial balance at each month-end — a periodic snapshot where each row already holds a closing balance — or you can load every GL movement plus an opening balance and let DAX accumulate them. The choice decides which DAX pattern computes the closing balance, how heavy the model is, whether users can drill from a balance to vouchers, and how easily the model ties to the audited TB. In practice most Indian mid-market FP&A models (Tally, SAP Business One, Oracle NetSuite, Zoho Books sources) use a hybrid, and you must be able to reason about both.

Core notes

Snapshot fact (periodic snapshot)

Grain: one row per account × entity (× cost centre, if needed) × period-end date, holding the closing balance.

Date AccountKey EntityKey Balance
31-Oct-2025 1100 (Trade receivables) IN01 1,480
30-Nov-2025 1100 IN01 1,390
31-Dec-2025 1100 IN01 1,520

Typical sources: month-end TB exports (Tally "Trial Balance" as on date, SAP FAGLFLEXT-style period balances), or a balance table generated in SQL/Power Query from the GL.

Closing Bal (snapshot) =
CALCULATE ( SUM ( TB[Balance] ), LASTDATE ( 'Date'[Date] ) )

Strengths:

  • Ties to the TB by construction — each month's rows are the TB.
  • Simple and fast — closing balance is a filter on one date; no running totals.
  • Handles accounts with no movement — the balance is present even if nothing was posted that month.

Weaknesses:

  • No drill-through to vouchers — a balance cannot be explained from the snapshot alone.
  • Storage grows with accounts × months, not with activity (acceptable for a TB of a few thousand accounts).
  • Restatements and late adjustments require reloading the affected snapshots (if November is reopened, every later month changes too).
  • Only period-end dates — a daily balance (cash position) needs a daily snapshot.

A subtle point for P&L accounts: many ERP TBs report P&L accounts as YTD cumulative balances. Snapshot P&L values are then "YTD to that month", and the monthly P&L is the difference between consecutive snapshots (with April equal to the April snapshot). Load P&L from the GL movement fact instead, or convert in Power Query.

Transaction fact (movements plus opening)

Grain: one row per voucher line (or per account-day if pre-aggregated), holding the signed movement. An opening balance row per account is added at the start of the model's history (for example dated 1 April 2023, voucher type "Opening"), carrying the closing balance of the last pre-model year.

Date VoucherNo AccountKey Amount VoucherType
01-Apr-2023 OB-0001 1100 1,210 Opening
05-Apr-2023 SI-0187 1100 118 Sales
12-Apr-2023 RC-0042 1100 −240 Receipt 
Closing Bal (movements) =
VAR d = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    CALCULATE ( SUM ( GL[Amount] ), REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ), 'Date'[Date] <= d )

Strengths:

  • Full drill-through from balance to vouchers; movement analysis (receipts, invoices) from the same fact.
  • Daily balances come free — any date can be an "as of" date.
  • Single source for P&L and BS.

Weaknesses:

  • Depends on the opening balance being complete and correct; any missing opening entry breaks every later balance.
  • Running totals are heavier to compute (fine for millions of rows in VertiPaq, but noticeable in large matrices).
  • P&L-to-reserves logic is needed: P&L accounts accumulate forever unless each year's profit is rolled into retained earnings (s04, topic related item).
  • Ties to the TB only if every adjustment (including audit adjustments posted outside the ledger) is in the GL.

Hybrid patterns

Pattern How When
Opening snapshot + movements One snapshot row per account at the model start; GL movements after Most common; small history in GL
Annual snapshots + movements Closing balance at each 31 March from the audited TB; movements within the year Audited balances are the anchor, GL detail for current year
Movements in model, snapshot generated in Power Query/SQL Cumulative sum by account-month pre-computed at refresh Large GL, heavy matrices; keep drill-through via relationship to GL

Reconciling the two

The control test is identical for both: for each month-end, the balance by account must equal the TB. Build a reconciliation table (account × month-end) with columns Model Balance, TB Balance and Difference; a correct model shows zero differences and a balance sheet that sums to zero (debits = credits) at every month-end.

\[ \text{Closing}_{t} = \text{Opening}_{\text{start}} + \sum_{d \le t} \text{Movement}_{d} \]

Worked illustration

Trade receivables, entity IN01 (₹ lakh). Opening at 1 April 2025: 1,210. Movements April–October: invoices 8,640, receipts −8,370. Closing at 31 October = 1,210 + 8,640 − 8,370 = 1,480. The snapshot TB at 31 October shows 1,480. If the opening entry had been dated 31 March 2025 (outside a Date table starting 1 April 2025), it would not join to the calendar and would sit in the Date table's blank member. Whether a cumulative measure then picks it up depends on how its date filter is written: a measure that filters DATESBETWEEN('Date'[Date], <calendar start>, d) drops it and shows 270, while other forms may include it. A model whose balances depend on that kind of accident is fragile — date opening entries on the first date of the calendar and test that no GL row falls outside the Date table.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Unposted audit adjustments: statutory auditors' adjustments sometimes live in a "Schedule III adjustments" workbook rather than the ledger. Load them as a separate adjustment fact (or GL voucher type) dated 31 March so both patterns include them.
  • Multi-currency balances: a snapshot in INR at closing rates (Ind AS 21 monetary items) cannot be recomputed from INR movements at transaction rates; revaluation entries must be in the GL.
  • Accounts re-mapped mid-year: with snapshots, history is as reported; with movements, the current CoA mapping restates history automatically. Decide which the user expects (Type 1 vs Type 2 dimension, Topic 1).
  • Intra-month cash: treasury wants daily balances; a month-end snapshot cannot serve that — use movements or a daily bank-balance snapshot from the bank statement feed.
  • Year-end closing entries: some ERPs post a closing entry transferring P&L to retained earnings; Tally does not. The movement pattern must know which applies.

Key takeaways

  • Snapshot facts hold closing balances at period-ends; transaction facts hold movements plus an opening entry.
  • Snapshots tie to the TB easily and are fast; movements allow drill-through and daily balances.
  • Opening entries must fall within the Date table and include every BS account.
  • ERP TB snapshots often hold YTD P&L values — do not treat them as monthly flows.
  • Whatever the pattern, reconcile model balances to the TB for every month-end.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choose the fact design [L2]

For each scenario, recommend a snapshot, movement or hybrid design and justify it: (a) a board pack showing quarterly balance sheets for five years; (b) a treasury dashboard of daily bank balances; (c) a receivables ageing analysis by invoice; (d) a group consolidation where subsidiaries send month-end TBs in Excel.

Q2. Missing opening entry [L2]

A movement-based model starts on 1 April 2024. Closing balances at 31 March 2024 were: Trade receivables ₹ 2,340 lakh, Inventory ₹ 3,110 lakh, Trade payables −₹ 1,960 lakh, Share capital −₹ 1,000 lakh, Retained earnings −₹ 2,490 lakh. Only receivables and payables were loaded as opening entries. Calculate the error in total assets and in total equity at 30 September 2024, and explain how the balance sheet check (sum of all BS accounts = 0) would reveal it.

💡 Hint: Which accounts' openings are missing, and on which side of the balance sheet do they sit?

Q3. YTD P&L in a TB snapshot [L2]

Your month-end TB snapshot for FY26 shows the Revenue account at −₹ 1,200 lakh (April), −₹ 2,250 lakh (May) and −₹ 3,600 lakh (June). Explain what these values represent, what the monthly revenue was, and write a Power Query or DAX approach that derives monthly flows from such a snapshot.

Q4. Reconciliation build [L3]

Design a reconciliation page that proves a movement-based model ties to monthly TB exports for FY25 and FY26. Specify the tables, measures, visual layout and tolerance, and list three likely causes of differences and how the page would point to each.
