Subtopic note

Cash, Net Working Capital and Net Debt Measures over Time

Scope: Cash, net working capital and Net Debt measures over time

Why it matters

Cash, net working capital (NWC) and net debt are the three balance-sheet numbers a CFO, lender or investor looks at first. They drive the cash-flow statement (changes in NWC are the bridge from EBITDA to operating cash flow), covenant tests (Net Debt / EBITDA), the 3-statement model's balance sheet schedules and the DCF's free cash flow. In a Power BI model they are composite semi-additive measures: sums of several FS lines' closing balances, with sign conventions and definitional choices that must be explicit and consistent across periods.

Core notes

Building blocks

Start with one closing-balance base measure (snapshot or cumulative, s02–s04) and filter it by FS line. Present balances with assets positive and liabilities positive by flipping credits:

Closing Bal = CALCULATE ( SUM ( TB[Balance] ), LASTDATE ( 'Date'[Date] ) )   -- Dr +, Cr −

Cash & CE        = CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] = "Cash and cash equivalents" )
Other Bank Bal   = CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] = "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" )
Trade Receivables= CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] = "Trade receivables" )
Inventories      = CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] = "Inventories" )
Trade Payables   = - CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] = "Trade payables" )
Borrowings       = - CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] IN { "Non-current borrowings", "Current borrowings" } )
Lease Liabilities= - CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[FSLine] = "Lease liabilities" )

FS line names follow Schedule III Division II. Under Schedule III (as amended in 2021), current maturities of long-term borrowings are presented within current borrowings, and lease liabilities are presented separately.

Cash

Ind AS 7 defines cash and cash equivalents as cash on hand, demand deposits and short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible to known amounts of cash and subject to insignificant risk of changes in value (deposits with original maturity of three months or less are the usual benchmark). Schedule III separately presents "Bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" (FDs of 3–12 months, earmarked balances, unpaid dividend accounts). Decide and document:

  • Cash for the cash-flow statement: Ind AS 7 C&CE, net of bank overdrafts repayable on demand that form an integral part of cash management (Ind AS 7.8).
  • Cash for net debt: often C&CE + other bank balances + liquid mutual fund investments, excluding earmarked or lien-marked balances.

Net working capital

Operating NWC excludes cash, debt and tax/dividend items so that it isolates the trading cycle:

NWC =
[Trade Receivables] + [Inventories] + [Other Operating Current Assets]
    - [Trade Payables] - [Other Operating Current Liabilities]

Define "other operating" lines through a CoA attribute (CoA[NWCClass] = "Operating CA" / "Operating CL" / "Excluded") rather than hard-coding FS lines in the measure:

NWC (by class) =
CALCULATE ( [Closing Bal], CoA[NWCClass] IN { "Operating CA", "Operating CL" } )

Because liabilities are credits (negative) in the GL, summing the raw signed balances of operating assets and liabilities gives NWC directly — no flips needed. That is the elegance of the sign convention: NWC = net debit balance of the operating working-capital accounts.

Change in NWC for the cash-flow bridge:

NWC Change = [NWC (by class)] - OPENINGBALANCEYEAR ( [NWC (by class)], 'Date'[Date], "3/31" )

An increase in NWC is a use of cash (negative in the indirect cash flow).

Net debt

Net Debt =
[Borrowings] + [Lease Liabilities] - [Cash & CE] - [Other Bank Bal] - [Liquid Investments]

Definitional choices to make explicit (they vary between companies, lenders and analysts):

Item Common treatment Note
Current maturities of long-term debt Include In current borrowings under Schedule III
Lease liabilities (Ind AS 116) Include for reported net debt; exclude for "pre-Ind AS 116" covenant tests Must match EBITDA definition (EBITDA after Ind AS 116 excludes rent)
Interest accrued but not due Often included in loan-agreement definitions Other financial liabilities
Liquid mutual funds Often deducted Ind AS 109 FVTPL investments
Lien-marked FDs / margin money Exclude from cash Not freely available
Preference shares classified as liabilities (Ind AS 32) Include Debt-like

Leverage measures combine semi-additive net debt with an additive LTM flow:

Net Debt / LTM EBITDA = DIVIDE ( [Net Debt], [R12M EBITDA] )

Over time

All three measures inherit their time behaviour from [Closing Bal]: month rows show month-ends, quarter rows the quarter-end, FY rows 31 March. For trend lines, plot them at month-end on a continuous axis. For averages (average NWC days, average net debt for interest cost), use AVERAGEX(VALUES('Date'[MonthEnd]), ...) as in s06. Cap at the last closed date so the FY26 row shows the latest closed balance (or blank), never a blank-induced "zero debt".

Worked illustration

Balances at 30 September 2025 (₹ crore, presentation signs): C&CE 42; other bank balances 18 (of which 6 margin money); liquid MFs 25; trade receivables 210; inventories 165; other operating CA 34; trade payables 140; other operating CL 48; non-current borrowings 260; current borrowings 95 (including current maturities 40); lease liabilities 36.

  • NWC = 210 + 165 + 34 − 140 − 48 = 221.
  • Net debt (incl. leases, excluding margin money from cash) = 260 + 95 + 36 − 42 − 12 − 25 = 312.
  • Net debt excluding leases = 276.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Overdrafts: a bank account with a credit balance at month-end is a borrowing, not negative cash. Map OD accounts to borrowings, or classify dynamically with a measure that tests the sign (rarely worth it — map them).
  • GST balances: electronic credit ledger (input tax credit) and GST payable are often netted in TB exports; for NWC they are operating items, but for statutory presentation they are separate assets and liabilities.
  • Capital creditors: payables for capex are not operating working capital; tag them separately or NWC change will hide capex timing in operating cash flow.
  • Customer advances and contract liabilities (Ind AS 115): operating current liabilities — include in NWC.
  • Window dressing: quarter-end balances can be managed (stretching payables in the last week of March); average NWC and average net debt reveal it.
  • Consolidation: inter-company receivables/payables must be eliminated before NWC, or both sides inflate NWC in entity-level sums.

Key takeaways

  • Cash, NWC and net debt are composites of closing-balance measures filtered by FS line or CoA attribute.
  • Use CoA attributes (NWCClass, NetDebtClass) rather than hard-coded lines; definitions change with lenders.
  • Raw signed balances make NWC a simple sum; presentation flips belong in the final measures only.
  • Make net debt choices (leases, liquid MFs, lien-marked deposits, interest accrued) explicit and match EBITDA.
  • Over time, these measures inherit semi-additive behaviour; average them for ratio denominators.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute NWC and net debt [L1]

Balances at 31 March 2026 (₹ crore): C&CE 28; FDs of 6 months 15; trade receivables 180; inventories 140; contract liabilities 22; trade payables 120; employee provisions (current) 9; capital creditors 14; borrowings (non-current 150, current 60); lease liabilities 25. Compute operating NWC and net debt with and without leases, stating each classification you made.

Q2. Design the CoA attributes [L2]

Design NWCClass and NetDebtClass columns for a CoA with 40 BS accounts of an Indian manufacturing company. List the allowed values, classify ten representative accounts (including GST ITC, TDS receivable, capital advances, unpaid dividend account and an overdraft), and write the NWC and Net Debt measures that use them.

💡 Hint: Some accounts belong in neither measure; give them an explicit "Excluded" value rather than leaving blanks.

Q3. NWC change in the cash-flow bridge [L2]

NWC was ₹ 196 crore at 31 March 2025 and ₹ 221 crore at 30 September 2025; EBITDA for H1 FY26 was ₹ 88 crore and tax paid ₹ 14 crore. Write the DAX for NWC Change YTD and compute operating cash flow for H1 using a simple EBITDA-based bridge, explaining the sign of the NWC term.

Q4. Covenant definition mismatch [L3]

A sanction letter defines Net Debt as total borrowings plus interest accrued less unencumbered cash and bank balances, and EBITDA as before Ind AS 116 adjustments (rent treated as an expense). Your dashboard's net debt includes lease liabilities and deducts liquid MFs, and its EBITDA is post-Ind AS 116. Explain how each mismatch moves the reported leverage ratio and redesign the measures so the dashboard shows both the reported and the covenant-basis ratio.
