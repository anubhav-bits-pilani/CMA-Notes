Roadmap topic: Semi-Additive Balance Sheet Measures: Computing closing cash, receivables, and debt balances across time using LASTDATE / MAX date filters

Overview

A balance is a stock, not a flow. Receivables of ₹ 15 crore at 31 October and ₹ 14 crore at 30 November do not add up to ₹ 29 crore of receivables for the two months. Yet every Power BI matrix and Power Pivot PivotTable aggregates a measure at every level of the axis — month, quarter, FY, grand total — so any balance sheet number written as a plain SUM will show nonsense at subtotals. Semi-additive measures solve this: they sum across accounts, branches and entities but take a point-in-time value (closing, opening) or an average across dates.

For FP&A and reporting roles this is the bridge from a P&L-only dashboard to full financial statements. A balance sheet page that ties to the TB, a cash and net debt trend, NWC and its change for the cash-flow bridge, working-capital days for the monthly review and leverage covenants for the treasury head all depend on correct closing, opening and average balance measures. They also expose data-quality issues — missing opening entries, P&L not rolled into reserves, snapshots dated on the wrong day — that an auditor would find anyway.

The learning sequence: classify additivity first (s01); choose the fact design — month-end TB snapshots or GL movements plus openings (s02); write the closing balance with LASTDATE, LASTNONBLANK or a MAX-date filter (s03, c01, c02) for snapshots, or a cumulative running total for movements (s04, c03); add opening balances and roll-forwards with the OPENINGBALANCE/CLOSINGBALANCE family and the fiscal year-end argument (s05, c04); compute average balances for ratios (s06, c05, c06); and finally build the composite cash, NWC and net debt measures (s07).

Related concepts to study

Weeks 1–4: roll-forwards and year-end carry-forward

→ Week 1–4: balance sheet roll-forwards and carrying opening balances forward at year-end close

Weeks 1–4 covered the accounting: the closing balance of every BS account at 31 March becomes the opening of 1 April; P&L accounts are closed to retained earnings (surplus in the statement of profit and loss under Schedule III); and every schedule — PPE, provisions, borrowings, equity — is presented as a roll-forward. The DAX measures here automate exactly those identities: OPENINGBALANCEYEAR returns the carried-forward balance, the cumulative measure plus the P&L roll reproduces the closing entries, and the roll-forward check (opening + movements = closing) is the same reconciliation a statutory auditor performs on the notes to accounts.

Weeks 5–8: working-capital ratios

→ Week 5–8: working capital ratios (DSO, DIO, DPO, cash conversion cycle)

Ratio analysis in Weeks 5–8 used published statements and usually two-point averages. The model can do better: month-end or 13-point averages from internal data, branch- and customer-level DSO, and trends over 24 months. The interpretation rules carry over — DSO against credit terms, DIO against production cycle, DPO against MSME payment limits (Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act requires payment to micro and small enterprises within the MSMED Act timelines for the expense to be deductible), and the cash conversion cycle as the working-capital funding need. Make the averaging convention match the analysis you are reconciling to.

Week 14: balance sheet schedules in the 3-statement model

→ Week 14: balance sheet schedules in the 3-statement model

In the Week 14 Excel model, every balance sheet line is a schedule: opening (= prior closing), drivers (DSO, DIO, DPO, capex, depreciation, drawdowns, repayments) and closing. The Power BI semi-additive measures provide the historical actuals that seed those schedules and the actual-vs-forecast comparison afterwards. Two conventions must agree between the BI model and the Excel model: whether working-capital days use average or closing balances, and how net debt is defined (leases, liquid investments). Mismatches show up as unexplained jumps in the first forecast year.

Rolling P&L into retained earnings in a cumulative GL model

Rolling P&L into retained earnings inside a cumulative GL model

In a movement-based model, P&L accounts never close: their cumulative balance grows since the first posting. For the balance sheet to balance, the presentation must add cumulative P&L (all prior FYs plus current YTD) to the retained earnings account balance, or split it into "retained earnings brought forward" and "profit for the period". If the ERP posts year-end closing entries (P&L to retained earnings on 31 March), include only current-FY P&L. Tally-sourced data typically has no closing entries — the prior years' profit sits in the "Profit & Loss A/c" ledger and must be mapped to retained earnings. The TB check (all accounts cumulative = 0) proves completeness.

Common pitfalls

Summing balances across months

Summing balances across months in totals or YTD (adding 12 month-end balances together)

Mistake: SUM(TB[Balance]) on a snapshot fact, or applying a YTD calculation item to balance measures. Why people make it: the measure is correct at month level, which is where it was tested. Correct treatment: use a semi-additive base (LASTDATE / LASTNONBLANK / MAX-date for snapshots, cumulative for movements), and exclude balances from additive time-intelligence items. Detect it: quarter and FY rows of balances are roughly 3× and 12× the month-end values; FY total does not equal the 31 March TB.

LASTDATE on future or unposted dates

LASTDATE pointing at a future or unposted date returns blank; use LASTNONBLANK or cap at the last actuals date

Mistake: relying on LASTDATE at quarter or FY rows of an incomplete year, or for the current month before close. Why: it works perfectly for closed periods, and the Date table always runs to 31 March. Correct treatment: use LASTNONBLANK with an inner CALCULATE, or compute an explicit as-of date MIN(MAX('Date'[Date]), [Last Closed Date]) and filter to it, showing the date in a tooltip. Detect it: FY26 balance sheet page is blank in November; the current quarter shows blanks while months show values.

Missing opening entries or P&L-to-reserves close

Cumulative measures that miss opening entries or the P&L-to-reserves close, so the balance sheet does not balance

Mistake: loading opening entries only for some accounts (debtors and creditors), dating them outside the Date table, or presenting BS accounts without rolling P&L into retained earnings. Why: migrations focus on control accounts; P&L accumulation is invisible in P&L views. Correct treatment: load a complete, balanced opening TB dated inside the calendar; add the P&L roll (or rely on posted closing entries, not both). Detect it: a TB check measure (all accounts cumulative) is non-zero; total assets ≠ equity + liabilities by exactly the cumulative profit or by the missing openings.

Practice Questions

Q1. Balance sheet page from a movement GL [L3]

An unlisted manufacturing company's GL (Tally export) starts on 1 April 2024. Audited balances at 31 March 2024 (₹ lakh): PPE 4,800 Dr; Inventories 1,950 Dr; Trade receivables 2,300 Dr; Cash 420 Dr; Share capital 1,500 Cr; Retained earnings (Profit & Loss A/c ledger) 3,170 Cr; Term loan 3,100 Cr; Trade payables 1,700 Cr. FY25 summarised movements: revenue 18,400 (credit); cost of materials 11,200; employee cost 2,300; other expenses 1,900; depreciation 520; interest 290; tax 560; capex 900; term-loan repayment 600; receivables rose by 380, inventories by 150, payables by 210 (assume all other items settled in cash). (a) Specify the opening entries and their date given a Date table starting 1 April 2023. (b) Write the Cum Bal, Retained Earnings (incl. P&L roll) and TB Check measures. (c) Compute the 31 March 2025 balance sheet and show that it balances. (d) Explain what the balance sheet would show if the P&L roll were omitted.

💡 Hint: Derive cash as the balancing movement after posting every other item, then check the TB sums to zero.

Q2. Snapshot model debugging [L2]

A model built on monthly TB snapshots shows, for FY26 (data to 30 November 2025): Cash ₹ 11,840 lakh at the FY row while the 30 November TB shows ₹ 1,310 lakh; the Q3 FY26 row is blank; and Net Debt at FY row is −₹ 9,600 lakh. The measures are Cash = SUM(TB[Balance]) filtered to cash accounts and Net Debt = [Borrowings] - [Cash]. Explain each symptom and rewrite the measures (closing balance with an explicit capped as-of date, net debt sign-consistent) so every row is correct.

Q3. Working-capital review pack [L3]

For a quarterly working-capital review, design measures for Avg AR, Avg Inventory, Avg Payables (month-end averages), DSO, DIO, DPO and cash conversion cycle, and NWC change versus the FY opening. Data for Q2 FY26 (91 days, ₹ crore): revenue 96, COGS 64; month-end AR 38, 41, 44 (opening 36); inventory 30, 33, 31 (opening 29); payables 22, 24, 27 (opening 21). Compute each metric under your chosen convention, state the convention, and describe the visual layout including how totals across branches are computed.

Q4. Covenant-basis net debt over time [L2]

A term loan covenant tests Net Debt / LTM EBITDA ≤ 3.0× at each quarter-end, defining net debt as borrowings plus interest accrued less unencumbered cash. Specify the CoA attributes and measures needed, explain how the measure behaves on month, quarter and FY rows (including the current incomplete FY), and list three reconciliation checks against the compliance certificate submitted to the bank.
