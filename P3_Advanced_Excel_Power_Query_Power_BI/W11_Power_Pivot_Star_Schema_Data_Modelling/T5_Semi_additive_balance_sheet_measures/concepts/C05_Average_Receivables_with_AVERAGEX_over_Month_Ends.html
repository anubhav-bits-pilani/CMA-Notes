Key concept

Average Receivables with AVERAGEX over Month-Ends

Concept: Avg AR = AVERAGEX(VALUES('Date'[MonthEnd]), [Closing AR])

Statement

Avg AR = AVERAGEX ( VALUES ( 'Date'[MonthEnd] ), [Closing AR] )

\[ \overline{AR}(P) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{m \in M(P)} AR_{\text{close}}(m) \]

where \(M(P)\) is the set of month-end dates visible in the current period \(P\), \(AR_{\text{close}}(m)\) is the closing trade receivables at month-end \(m\), and \(n\) is the number of month-ends in \(M(P)\) for which the closing balance is not blank.

Explanation

VALUES('Date'[MonthEnd]) returns the distinct month-end dates visible in the filter context: one at month grain, three at a quarter, twelve at a full FY. AVERAGEX iterates them; for each, [Closing AR] — being a measure — is evaluated with context transition, so the iterated month-end becomes the filter and the closing-balance logic (LASTDATE, LASTNONBLANK or cumulative) returns that month's balance. AVERAGEX then averages the results, skipping blanks but counting zeros.

Behaviour at each grain:

  • Month: a single point; the average equals the closing balance. If monthly DSO needs an average, use a two-point version (opening + closing ÷ 2).
  • Quarter: average of three month-ends (not including the opening). For a 4-point average add OPENINGBALANCEQUARTER.
  • FY: average of twelve month-ends. For a 13-point average add OPENINGBALANCEYEAR([Closing AR], 'Date'[Date], "3/31") and divide by 13.
  • Incomplete FY: on a snapshot fact, future month-ends return blank and are skipped — the average covers closed months only. On a cumulative fact, future month-ends repeat the latest balance and are not skipped; restrict VALUES('Date'[MonthEnd]) to closed months with a filter ('Date'[IsClosedPeriod] = TRUE()) or a cap.

The pattern generalises to any semi-additive average: average inventory (DIO), average payables (DPO), average capital employed (ROCE), average advances (NBFC yield), average headcount (cost per employee).

Illustration

Trade receivables (₹ lakh) at month-ends of Q3 FY26: October 1,480; November 1,390; December 1,520; opening (30 September) 1,450.

Measure Calculation Value
Closing AR (Q3) 31 December 1,520
Avg AR (3 month-ends) (1,480 + 1,390 + 1,520) / 3 1,463.3
4-point average (1,450 + 1,480 + 1,390 + 1,520) / 4 1,460.0
Two-point average (1,450 + 1,520) / 2 1,485.0

The differences are small here, but for a business that collects heavily at quarter-end (common in B2B with quarter-end sales pushes), the closing balance can be 30–40% below the average.

Practice Questions

Q1. Grain behaviour [L1]

For the month row December 2025, the Q3 FY26 row and the FY26 row (snapshots closed to 31 December 2025), state how many month-ends Avg AR averages and whether future months are included, for a snapshot fact and for a cumulative fact.

Q2. 13-point average measure [L2]

Write a measure for a 13-point average of trade receivables for a full FY, using OPENINGBALANCEYEAR and the AVERAGEX pattern, and compute it for: opening 1,200 and month-ends April–March of 1,260, 1,310, 1,180, 1,240, 1,300, 1,350, 1,480, 1,390, 1,520, 1,410, 1,370, 1,290 (₹ lakh).

💡 Hint: The opening is one extra point; the divisor counts all points actually used.

Q3. Zero versus blank [L2]

A newly opened branch has no receivables rows before September 2025 (blank) and a genuine zero balance at 31 December 2025 after collecting everything. Explain how AVERAGEX treats each month for the FY26 average, and whether the result is the figure a regional manager should be judged on.
