Key concept

Cumulative Balance with FILTER over ALL Dates

Concept: Cum Bal = CALCULATE(SUM(GL[Amount]), FILTER(ALL('Date'[Date]), 'Date'[Date] <= MAX('Date'[Date])))

Statement

Cum Bal =
CALCULATE (
    SUM ( GL[Amount] ),
    FILTER ( ALL ( 'Date'[Date] ), 'Date'[Date] <= MAX ( 'Date'[Date] ) )
)

\[ \text{CumBal}(P) = \sum_{r \in \text{GL} \,:\, \text{Date}_{r} \le \max(P)} \text{Amount}_{r} \]

where \(P\) is the set of visible dates and \(\text{Amount}_{r}\) is the signed movement (debit positive, credit negative) on GL row \(r\), including opening-balance entries. Other filters (account, entity, cost centre) remain.

Explanation

The measure is a running total from inception to the period end:

  • ALL('Date'[Date]) returns every date in the calendar, ignoring the current period filter.
  • FILTER iterates those dates; in its condition 'Date'[Date] is the iterated row's value, while MAX('Date'[Date]) is an aggregation evaluated in the filter context — which is still the outer cell's period, because a row context does not filter. So the condition keeps every date up to the period end.
  • CALCULATE applies that list to the date column. On a marked Date table, filters on other Date columns (month, FY) are removed as well, so the result is not clipped to the visible month.

The result is semi-additive by construction: month rows show month-end balances, quarter rows quarter-end, FY rows 31 March. It is correct only if:

  • Opening entries exist for every BS account and fall inside the Date table (date them on the last day before the model's first FY with an extended calendar, or on the calendar's first day).
  • All adjustments (audit entries, revaluations, eliminations) are in the GL fact.
  • P&L accounts are rolled into retained earnings for presentation (topic pitfall); on their own, P&L accounts accumulate since inception.
  • Future periods are capped: for months after the last posting the measure repeats the latest balance, which can be mistaken for an actual month-end.

A readable equivalent that avoids iterating with FILTER:

Cum Bal =
VAR asOf = MAX ( 'Date'[Date] )
RETURN
    CALCULATE ( SUM ( GL[Amount] ), REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Date' ), 'Date'[Date] <= asOf )

If the Date table is not marked and the relationship uses an integer key, ALL('Date'[Date]) leaves filters on 'Date'[MonthName] or 'Date'[FY] in place; the cumulative sum is then restricted to that month — a typical "cumulative equals monthly" bug. REMOVEFILTERS('Date') in the second form avoids that dependency.

Illustration

Cash account (₹ lakh). Opening entry 31 March 2025: 400. Net movements: April +120, May −80, June +45.

Row MAX('Date'[Date]) Cum Bal
Apr 2025 30 Apr 2025 520
May 2025 31 May 2025 440
Jun 2025 30 Jun 2025 485
Q1 FY26 30 Jun 2025 485
Jul 2025 (no postings yet) 31 Jul 2025 485 (cap needed)

Practice Questions

Q1. Why MAX sees the cell [L1]

Explain, in terms of row context and filter context, why MAX('Date'[Date]) inside the FILTER condition returns the period end of the cell rather than the date of the row being iterated. What would change if it were written as CALCULATE(MAX('Date'[Date]))?

💡 Hint: Context transition would turn the iterated row into a filter.

Q2. Cumulative equals monthly [L2]

In a model where GL relates to Date through DateKey (integer) and the Date table is not marked, a matrix with 'Date'[MonthName] and 'Date'[FY] on rows shows Cum Bal identical to the monthly movement. Explain why and give two fixes.

Q3. Compute cumulative balances [L1]

Borrowings (credit balances negative, ₹ crore): opening entry 31 March 2025 −250; drawdown 12 May 2025 −40; repayment 30 June 2025 +25; repayment 30 September 2025 +25. Compute Cum Bal for April, May, June, Q1, Q2 and H1 FY26 rows, and state how a presentation measure would display them.
