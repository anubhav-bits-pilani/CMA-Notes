Key concept

DSO Measure from Average Receivables and Days in Period

Concept: DSO = DIVIDE([Avg AR], [Revenue]) * [Days in Period]

Statement

DSO = DIVIDE ( [Avg AR], [Revenue] ) * [Days in Period]

\[ \text{DSO} = \frac{\overline{AR}}{R} \times D \]

where \(\overline{AR}\) is the average trade receivables over the period (semi-additive, c05), \(R\) is revenue for the same period (additive, presentation-signed positive), and \(D\) is the number of days in the period. A supporting measure:

Days in Period =
VAR lastDate = [Last Actual Date]
RETURN
    COUNTROWS ( FILTER ( VALUES ( 'Date'[Date] ), 'Date'[Date] <= lastDate ) )

Explanation

DSO (days sales outstanding) expresses receivables as the number of days of revenue they represent. The measure combines the three additivity classes: a semi-additive average balance, an additive flow and a count of days, producing a non-additive ratio that must be recomputed at every level — never summed or averaged across months or branches.

Design points:

  • Matching periods. Revenue and days must cover the same period. For a quarter row, revenue is the quarter's revenue and \(D\) = 92 (or 90/91). No annualisation is needed because both numerator scale and days are for the period. For an incomplete FY, Days in Period must be capped at the last actuals date, or the FY26 DSO in November uses 365 days of "time" against 7.5 months of revenue and is overstated.
  • Credit sales. DSO strictly relates to credit sales; for businesses with material cash or advance-paid sales (retail, D2C), use credit revenue or exclude cash channels.
  • GST. Receivables include GST (typically 18%) while revenue from operations excludes it (Ind AS 115 presents revenue net of GST). Gross up revenue by the effective GST rate, or accept and disclose that DSO is overstated by roughly that rate.
  • Gross vs net. Decide whether \(\overline{AR}\) is before or after the expected credit loss allowance (Ind AS 109); bankers often prefer gross.
  • Sign convention. With credits negative in the GL, [Revenue] must be the flipped (positive) measure; otherwise DSO is negative.
  • Countback method alternative. For volatile revenue, FP&A teams use countback DSO: exhaust closing receivables against the most recent months' revenue backwards until absorbed. More accurate for month-end reporting, but needs an iterative measure.

Companion ratios use the same template: DIO = average inventory ÷ COGS × days; DPO = average payables ÷ purchases (or COGS) × days; cash conversion cycle = DSO + DIO − DPO.

Illustration

Q3 FY26 (1 October – 31 December 2025, 92 days): revenue ₹ 3,600 lakh; month-end receivables 1,480, 1,390, 1,520 (average 1,463.3; closing 1,520). GST at 18% on all sales.

Variant Calculation DSO (days)
Average AR, revenue excl. GST 1,463.3 / 3,600 × 92 37.4
Closing AR, revenue excl. GST 1,520 / 3,600 × 92 38.8
Average AR, revenue grossed up for GST 1,463.3 / (3,600 × 1.18) × 92 31.7

A credit policy of 30 days looks breached by 7 days under the first variant and nearly met under the third — the definition must be agreed with whoever reads the dashboard.

Practice Questions

Q1. Compute DSO variants [L1]

H1 FY26 (183 days): revenue ₹ 52.0 crore; receivables at 31 March 2025 ₹ 13.5 crore and at month-ends April–September of 14.2, 15.0, 15.6, 14.8, 16.1, 15.3 crore. Compute DSO using closing AR, two-point average and month-end average, all without GST gross-up.

Q2. DSO for an incomplete FY [L2]

In November 2025 (actuals to 31 October), the FY26 DSO tile shows 92 days while monthly DSOs have been between 52 and 58 days. Explain the likely cause in the Days in Period or Avg AR measures, and rewrite them so the FY tile is consistent with the monthly figures.

💡 Hint: Check which of numerator, revenue and days include the months after October.

Q3. Branch league table [L2]

A sales director wants a branch league table showing DSO and a company total. Explain why the total row must not be the average of branch DSOs, and compute the company DSO for two branches: Pune (Avg AR ₹ 600 lakh, quarterly revenue ₹ 1,800 lakh) and Jaipur (Avg AR ₹ 300 lakh, revenue ₹ 450 lakh), using 91 days.
