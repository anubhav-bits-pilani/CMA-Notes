Key concept

Percentage within Statement with ALLEXCEPT

Concept: % within Statement = DIVIDE([Actual], CALCULATE([Actual], ALLEXCEPT(CoA, CoA[Statement])))

Statement

% within Statement =
DIVIDE (
    [Actual],
    CALCULATE ( [Actual], ALLEXCEPT ( CoA, CoA[Statement] ) )
)

ALLEXCEPT(CoA, CoA[Statement]) removes every filter on the CoA table except filters on CoA[Statement]. The denominator is therefore the total of the statement to which the current row belongs (Profit and Loss or Balance Sheet), within the other active filters (Date, Branch, CostCentre).

\[ \text{Share}_{row} = \frac{A(row)}{A(\text{Statement of the row})} \]

Explanation

In a matrix with Statement → FSLine → Account, the measure answers "what fraction of its statement is this line?". For a vertical (common-size) analysis you typically want lines as a % of a specific anchor — revenue for the P&L, total assets for the balance sheet — rather than the statement's net total, which brings a double-entry caveat:

  • Net totals can be tiny or zero. The P&L statement's net total is profit (income and expense netted). Each line as % of profit is rarely meaningful. The BS net total (assets + liabilities + equity with signs) is zero in a balanced TB — the measure returns blank. ALLEXCEPT by Statement is therefore most useful with sign-adjusted, one-sided groupings, e.g. Statement split into "Assets" and "Equity and liabilities", or with a measure that sums absolute values.
  • Common-size P&L uses revenue as the denominator instead: DIVIDE([Actual Signed], [Revenue]) (c01).

ALLEXCEPT behaviour to remember:

  • It removes filters from all other CoA columns, including slicer filters (e.g. AccountType = "Expense" on a slicer is removed from the denominator).
  • If CoA[Statement] is not filtered (grand total row), ALLEXCEPT keeps nothing — the denominator is the total of all accounts.
  • Use CALCULATE([Actual], REMOVEFILTERS(CoA), VALUES(CoA[Statement])) to keep exactly the visible statements — similar, but also respects a slicer on Statement through VALUES.
  • ALLEXCEPT with a column from another table (e.g. ALLEXCEPT(CoA, 'Date'[FY])) is legal only when that table is related as part of CoA's expanded table — usually not; keep the listed columns within the same table.

Illustration

Balance sheet at 31-Mar-2026 presented with a Statement column split into two "sides" and sign-adjusted amounts (₹ crore):

Statement (side) FSLine Amount % within Statement
Assets Property, plant and equipment 420 52.5%
Assets Trade receivables 180 22.5%
Assets Cash and cash equivalents 200 25.0%
Assets total 800 100%
Equity and liabilities Equity share capital and other equity 500 62.5%
Equity and liabilities Borrowings 220 27.5%
Equity and liabilities Trade payables 80 10.0%

With a single "Balance Sheet" statement and raw signs, the denominator would be \(800 - 800 = 0\) and every share blank.

Practice Questions

Q1. Predict blanks [L1]

In a model where CoA[Statement] has only "Profit and Loss" and "Balance Sheet" and amounts use raw signs, describe what % within Statement shows for the Balance Sheet rows and for the P&L rows, and why.

Q2. Common-size P&L [L2]

Write a measure that shows each P&L FS line as a percentage of Revenue from operations (sign-adjusted), works at account level as well, and blanks on Balance Sheet rows.

💡 Hint: Remove the CoA row filter from the denominator but anchor it to the revenue line.

Q3. Slicer interaction [L2]

A user slices CoA[AccountType] = "Expense". Explain what happens to the numerator and the ALLEXCEPT denominator, and rewrite the measure so the denominator respects the slicer.
