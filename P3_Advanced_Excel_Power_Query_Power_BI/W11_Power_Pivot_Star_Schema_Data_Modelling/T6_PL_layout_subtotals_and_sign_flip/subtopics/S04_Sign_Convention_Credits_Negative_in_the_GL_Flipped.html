Subtopic note

Sign Convention: Credits Negative in the GL, Flipped for Presentation

Scope: Sign convention: credits negative in the GL, with a presentation flip for revenue and income

Why it matters

Every ERP and every Tally export stores amounts either as signed values (debits positive, credits negative) or as separate Dr/Cr columns that Power Query combines into one signed column (Week 10). That convention is ideal for arithmetic — a trial balance sums to zero, NWC is the net debit of operating accounts — but nobody wants to read "Revenue −4,100". Presentation needs revenue and income shown positive, and either expenses shown negative (profit-signed) or positive with "Less:" semantics. Where and how the sign is flipped decides whether subtotals add up, whether Actual and Budget are comparable, and whether favourable/adverse logic works. Sign errors are the most common P&L model bug, and they are often invisible until a total is checked against the TB.

Core notes

Three sign layers

Layer Convention Purpose
Storage (GL fact) Debit +, Credit − (natural) TB balances to zero; one rule for every account
Computation (profit-signed) Income +, Expense − (multiply all P&L by −1) Subtotals are simple sums; Variance = Actual − Budget is favourable when positive
Display (optional) Income +, Expense + Matches printed statements that list expenses as positive numbers

The key design rule: flip once, in one place, and compute subtotals in the profit-signed layer. Display flips, if used, are applied last, row by row, and never feed back into subtotals.

Implementing the profit-signed layer

Put a SignFactor in the CoA: −1 for all P&L accounts (both income and expense), +1 for BS accounts if you want assets positive, or −1 for liabilities and equity in a BS layout.

Actual Signed = SUMX ( GL, GL[Amount] * RELATED ( CoA[SignFactor] ) )

Revenue (credit −4,100) × −1 = +4,100. Materials (debit +2,380) × −1 = −2,380. Gross Profit = 4,100 + (−2,380) = 1,720.

A faster equivalent that avoids iterating every GL row:

Actual Signed =
SUMX ( VALUES ( CoA[SignFactor] ), CoA[SignFactor] * [Actual] )

It iterates only the distinct sign values (two), computing [Actual] for each through context transition. For P&L-only models with a uniform −1, even simpler: Actual Signed = -[Actual] restricted to P&L accounts. The per-account SignFactor keeps the model general (BS layouts, contra accounts).

Display flip (expenses shown positive)

If the board pack prints "Cost of materials consumed 2,380" and computes "Gross Profit 1,720", apply a display sign on the layout line, not on the CoA:

PnL Display =
VAR t = SELECTEDVALUE ( Layout[Type] )
VAR displaySign = SELECTEDVALUE ( Layout[SignFactor], 1 )
VAR v =
    SWITCH (
        TRUE (),
        t = "Account",  [Actual Signed],
        t = "Subtotal", [Subtotal],
        t = "Ratio",    [Margin %]
    )
RETURN
    IF ( t = "Ratio", v, v * displaySign )

Layout[SignFactor] = −1 on expense lines, +1 on income and subtotals. The subtotal itself is still computed from profit-signed account values, so GP is correct whatever the display sign.

Where not to flip

  • Not in Power Query and DAX both. If Power Query already multiplies revenue by −1 ("make revenue positive") and DAX applies SignFactor = −1 again, revenue turns negative. Decide: storage stays natural; flips live in DAX (or, if you flip in Power Query, set every DAX SignFactor to +1 and document it).
  • Not differently for Actual and Budget. Budgets are often prepared in Excel with revenue positive and costs positive (a planner's view), whereas GL actuals are natural-signed. Normalise the budget to the GL's natural convention in Power Query (multiply revenue/income budget lines by −1 so they are credits) and then apply the same DAX SignFactor to both. Test: Actual Signed and Budget Signed for revenue are both positive.
  • Not in calculated columns on the fact unless needed for performance; a sign column in the fact duplicates CoA logic and drifts when an account is reclassified.

Contra and mixed-nature accounts

  • Sales returns, discounts, GST-inclusive revenue adjustments are debit-natured within Revenue: with a uniform −1 they correctly reduce revenue (debit 120 × −1 = −120).
  • Changes in inventories can be debit or credit: the same rule handles both.
  • FX gain/loss accounts flip between income and expense; map them to one line (e.g., Other income or Other expenses) and let the sign speak, or split by sign in Power Query if the statement requires gains and losses on separate lines.
  • Balance sheet: for a BS layout, a SignFactor of +1 on assets and −1 on liabilities/equity gives positive presentation; the BS check is then Assets − (Liabilities + Equity) = 0.

Worked illustration

Account GL natural SignFactor Profit-signed Display sign Displayed
Revenue from operations −4,100 −1 4,100 +1 4,100
Sales returns (within revenue) 120 −1 −120 +1 (part of revenue 3,980)
Cost of materials 2,380 −1 −2,380 −1 2,380
Gross Profit — — 1,600 +1 1,600

Revenue net of returns = 4,100 − 120 = 3,980; GP = 3,980 − 2,380 = 1,600.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Budget uploaded with mixed signs (some cost centres entering costs as negatives): normalise in Power Query using the CoA's natural-balance attribute (CoA[NormalBalance] = "Dr"/"Cr"): Amount = ABS(Input) × (if Dr then 1 else −1) — but only if inputs are never genuinely contra; otherwise enforce a template.
  • Signed variances on ratio rows: variances of margins are percentage points and are not sign-flipped.
  • Exports to Excel: users summing an exported column with display-flipped expenses and positive subtotals will double-count; label exports or export profit-signed values.
  • Visual-level "absolute value" tricks (e.g., custom format #,##0;#,##0 hiding minus signs) make negative revenue (credit notes exceeding sales in a month) look positive — avoid format strings that drop the sign.
  • Tally exports often show Dr/Cr in a separate column with positive amounts; the combine step in Power Query must use the Dr/Cr indicator, not the ledger group, to sign the amount.

Key takeaways

  • Store natural signs (Dr +, Cr −); compute in profit-signed form (all P&L × −1); display-flip last, per layout line.
  • Subtotals must be computed from profit-signed account values, never from display-flipped values.
  • Flip in exactly one place — never in both Power Query and DAX — and the same way for Actual and Budget.
  • SUMX(VALUES(CoA[SignFactor]), CoA[SignFactor] * [Actual]) is a fast alternative to iterating the GL.
  • Contra accounts (returns, discounts) are handled correctly by a uniform P&L flip.

Practice Questions

Q1. Apply the layers [L1]

GL natural amounts for October 2025 (₹ lakh): Revenue −3,600; Other income −90; Materials 2,050; Employee 520; Other expenses 410; D&A 140; Finance costs 75; Tax 120. Compute profit-signed values for each line and GP (revenue less materials), EBITDA, EBIT, PBT and PAT using the s01 layout, and state which lines would carry a display sign of −1 if expenses are printed positive.

Q2. Double flip [L2]

After a Power Query change, the dashboard shows Revenue −₹ 41 crore and EBITDA −₹ 49 crore while the TB shows revenue of ₹ 41 crore (credit) and an operating profit. Explain how a double flip produces these numbers, why EBITDA became more negative than revenue, and how you would make the model robust against this.

💡 Hint: Work through what happens to income and expense lines separately when only one of them is flipped twice.

Q3. Budget sign normalisation [L2]

The FY26 budget workbook has revenue and all costs as positive numbers. Write the Power Query (M) logic or DAX logic to normalise it to the GL's natural convention using CoA[NormalBalance], and describe the test that proves Actual and Budget are on the same basis.

Q4. FX gains and losses [L3]

Foreign-exchange differences post to one GL account that is a net gain in some months and a net loss in others. The company's policy (common Schedule III practice) presents a net gain for the period within Other income and a net loss within Other expenses. Design the mapping and DAX so that the YTD statement shows the net figure on the correct line, and explain why monthly and YTD presentations can place the account on different lines.
