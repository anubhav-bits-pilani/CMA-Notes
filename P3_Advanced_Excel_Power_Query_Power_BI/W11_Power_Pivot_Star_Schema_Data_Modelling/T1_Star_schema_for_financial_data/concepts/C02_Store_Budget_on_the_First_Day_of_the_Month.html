Key concept

Store Budget on the First Day of the Month

Concept: Simplest budget fix: store the budget on the first day of the month and relate it to 'Date'[Date] like actuals

Statement

Given a Budget fact at month grain (AccountKey × CostCentreKey × fiscal month), add a real date column

\[ \text{MonthStart} = \text{DATE}(\text{CalendarYear}, \text{CalendarMonth}, 1) \]

where CalendarYear and CalendarMonth are derived from the fiscal period (e.g. FY2025-26 month 10 = January 2026), and create a standard one-to-many, single-direction relationship 'Date'[Date] (1) → Budget[MonthStart] (*). Then:

Budget = SUM ( Budget[Amount] )

works with every Date attribute at month level or above (month, quarter, FY), exactly like [Actual].

Explanation

The Date dimension is at day grain and must have a unique key, so a month-grain fact needs a key that exists in 'Date'[Date]. Picking one canonical day per month — first day (MonthStart) or last day (MonthEnd) — gives each budget row a valid date inside its month. Filters on FiscalMonthNo, MonthName, FiscalQuarter, FY and IsYTD flow from Date to Budget through the relationship, and time intelligence (TOTALYTD with "3/31", SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR) works on Budget without any special code.

Why it is the "simplest" fix:

  • No TREATAS or virtual relationships; any analyst can read the model.
  • One conformed Date dimension filters GL and Budget identically.
  • YTD Budget = TOTALYTD([Budget], 'Date'[Date], "3/31") just works.

Conditions and caveats:

  • Day-level filters mislead. A filter on 1 April shows the whole April budget; any other April day shows blank. Hide budget below month level with IF(ISINSCOPE('Date'[Date]), BLANK(), [Budget]) or keep Date on visuals at month level.
  • Partial-month YTD. If IsYTD caps actuals at 15 March, budget for March is either fully in (MonthStart 1 March ≤ 15 March) or fully out (MonthEnd 31 March > 15 March), so choose MonthStart vs MonthEnd deliberately and document whether partial-month budget is pro-rated.
  • MonthStart vs MonthEnd. MonthEnd aligns with TB snapshots and LASTDATE logic; MonthStart is safer with as-of-date caps because the budget row appears as soon as the month begins. Either is fine if consistent.
  • Data type. Store as Date (not Date/Time with a time part, not text).

Illustration

Budget workbook (wide) for cost centre "PUN-PLANT", account 530200 Power & fuel, FY2025-26: Apr ₹42 lakh, May ₹45 lakh … Mar ₹40 lakh (annual ₹5.10 crore). After unpivoting in Power Query (Week 10) and adding MonthStart (1-Apr-2025, 1-May-2025 … 1-Mar-2026), the fact has 12 rows. In a matrix with FiscalQuarter on columns, Q1 shows the sum of April–June; FY2025-26 total shows ₹5.10 crore; [Actual] - [Budget] works in every cell. Compare the broken alternative: relating Budget[MonthName] "April" to a Date column that repeats "April" across five years — Power BI forces many-to-many and the April budget appears under every April in the calendar.

Practice Questions

Q1. Derive MonthStart [L1]

A budget table has columns FY ("FY2025-26") and FiscalMonthNo (1 = April … 12 = March). Write a Power Query custom column expression that returns the correct MonthStart date for every row.

💡 Hint: Fiscal months 10–12 fall in the second calendar year of the FY label.

Q2. Day-level guard [L2]

Users drill a matrix from month to day and see the full monthly budget on the 1st and blanks on other days. Write a measure that hides budget below month level, and explain whether ISINSCOPE or HASONEVALUE is the better test here.

Q3. Partial month YTD [L2]

Last actuals date is 15 March 2026. Full-year budget is ₹60 crore with March budget ₹6 crore. Explain what YTD Budget shows under MonthStart storage versus MonthEnd storage if YTD is capped at the last actuals date, and recommend how to present budget for a partial month in the MIS.
