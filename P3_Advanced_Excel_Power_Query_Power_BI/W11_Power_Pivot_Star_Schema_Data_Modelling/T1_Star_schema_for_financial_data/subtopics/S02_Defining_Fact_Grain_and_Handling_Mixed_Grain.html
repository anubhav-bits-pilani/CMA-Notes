Subtopic note

Defining Fact Grain and Handling Mixed Grain (Budget vs Actual)

Scope: Defining grain per fact (voucher line vs account-month) and handling mixed grain (monthly Budget vs daily Actual)

Why it matters

The grain of a fact — what exactly one row represents — determines which questions the table can answer and which dimensions it may legitimately relate to. Budget-versus-actual reporting is the textbook case of mixed grain: actuals are posted daily at voucher-line level, while the budget is set per account, cost centre and month. If the modeller ignores this, the budget either refuses to filter by date, or worse, repeats the monthly budget on every day of the month and inflates the total thirty-fold. Grain mistakes are also the root cause of most "the matrix total does not equal the sum of rows" complaints in MIS reviews.

Core notes

Declaring the grain

Kimball's rule: declare the grain before choosing columns. Write it as a sentence:

  • GL: "One row per voucher line, i.e. one debit or credit to one GL account on one posting date, for one cost centre and branch."
  • Budget: "One row per GL account × cost centre × fiscal month."
  • TB: "One row per GL account × month-end date (closing balance)."

Every column on the fact must be true at that grain. A GL row cannot carry "monthly budget" because budget is not defined at voucher-line level. A Budget row cannot carry VoucherNo.

Voucher-line grain vs account-month grain

Aspect Voucher-line grain (GL) Account-month grain (Budget, TB, summarised GL)
Row count Very high (lakhs to crores) Low (accounts × CCs × months)
Drill-through To voucher, narration, party None below month
Date relationship Daily 'Date'[Date] Month level only
Dimensions supported All, incl. party, voucher type Only those planned at that level
Typical use Audit trail, ageing, daily sales Board MIS, variance, forecasting

You can always aggregate a fine grain up to a coarser one (Power Query Group By account and month), but you cannot disaggregate a coarse grain down without inventing an allocation rule. Many FP&A teams keep the GL at voucher grain for drill-through and additionally load a summarised GL at account-month grain for speed in board packs.

The mixed-grain problem

The Date dimension is at day grain (one row per date). Budget is at month grain. Three ways to bridge them:

Option 1 — Store budget on the first (or last) day of the month (recommended for most models). Add MonthStart = #date(FY, month, 1) in Power Query and relate Budget[MonthStart] to 'Date'[Date] as a normal one-to-many relationship. Month, quarter and year filters work because each budget row sits on a real date inside the month. This is concept c02.

Option 2 — Virtual relationship with TREATAS. Keep Budget keyed on MonthEnd (or PeriodKey) with no physical relationship to Date, and transfer the filter in the measure:

Budget =
CALCULATE (
    SUM ( Budget[Amount] ),
    TREATAS ( VALUES ( 'Date'[MonthEnd] ), Budget[MonthEnd] )
)

Useful when you cannot touch the source, or when the budget arrives with PeriodKey 202504 and you want no physical relationship (concept c03).

Option 3 — A separate Month dimension. Build Month (one row per PeriodKey) related to both Date (one Month, many dates) and Budget. This snowflakes the Date dimension and is rarely worth it in Power BI; it is common in SSAS multidimensional cubes.

What not to do: relate Budget[MonthName] or Budget[PeriodKey] to a Date-table column that is not unique. That creates a many-to-many relationship (both sides repeat the key), and Power BI's many-to-many semantics silently produce totals that look plausible but are hard to reason about. See pitfall "Budget keyed on month text".

Showing budget only at valid levels

With Option 1, a user filtering a single day (say 15 May) sees blank budget, and a filter on 1 May shows the entire May budget. To stop misleading day-level budget figures, guard the measure:

Budget (Month+) =
IF (
    ISINSCOPE ( 'Date'[Date] ),
    BLANK (),
    [Budget]
)

Alternatively allocate monthly budget to days by working-day weights in Power Query if daily run-rate tracking is genuinely required (rare outside retail and treasury).

Mixed grain on other dimensions

Grain mismatch is not only about dates. If budget is set at FSLine level (e.g. "Employee benefits expense" for the whole company), it cannot relate to CoA[AccountKey]. Choices:

  • Map each budget line to a representative account (e.g. "Salaries – Budget") in CoA, so the key exists.
  • Relate Budget to a separate FSLine dimension and use TREATAS from CoA[FSLine] to Budget[FSLine].
  • Allocate budget down to accounts by prior-year mix (only if management accepts the allocation).

Similarly, budget by cost-centre group ("Sales – North") requires either a cost-centre-group key on CostCentre or TREATAS on the group attribute.

Numerical illustration

FY2025-26 opex budget for cost centre "MUM-ADM", account 520100 (Rent): ₹3,60,000 per month. If the budget is naively joined to every date in the month (a common error when people expand the budget to daily rows without dividing), April shows ₹\(30 \times 3{,}60{,}000 = 1{,}08{,}00{,}000\) instead of ₹3,60,000. With the MonthStart approach, April shows ₹3,60,000, Q1 shows ₹10,80,000, and FY shows ₹43,20,000 — all correct, because exactly twelve budget rows exist and each sits on one date.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Budget phasing changes. If the annual budget is phased in a later revision (RE1, RE2), add a Version column and keep grain "account × CC × month × version"; filter version in the measure or via a slicer.
  • Daily-grain budget for sales. A retail chain may set daily sales targets; then budget grain equals the Date grain and no bridging is needed.
  • TB at month-end vs GL at day. Relating TB[MonthEnd] to 'Date'[Date] works because month-end is a real date; but a filter on 1–15 March returns blank TB balances, which is correct for a snapshot fact.
  • 13th period / audit adjustments. Tally and SAP may post year-end adjustments in a special period. Map them to 31 March with an IsAdjustment flag rather than inventing a non-existent date.
  • Late-arriving facts. Backdated vouchers in a closed month change the grain-level row count but not the grain definition; your IsClosedPeriod logic must handle them (topic 2).

Key takeaways

  • Write the grain as a sentence before designing columns; every column must be true at that grain.
  • GL is at voucher-line grain; Budget and TB are at account-month grain.
  • Bridge month-grain budget to a day-grain Date table by storing it on MonthStart, or with TREATAS.
  • Never relate on a non-unique month text or period column — it forces many-to-many.
  • Guard day-level budget display with ISINSCOPE or an explicit allocation rule.
  • Grain mismatches on account or cost centre need representative keys, separate dimensions or TREATAS.

Practice Questions

Q1. State the grain [L1]

Write the grain statement for each of: (a) a SAP ACDOCA extract, (b) a sales-invoice line table from Tally with item, qty and rate, (c) a monthly headcount file by cost centre, (d) a quarterly capex budget by project.

Q2. Inflated budget diagnosis [L2]

A model shows FY2025-26 budget revenue of ₹1,824 crore against an approved budget of ₹60 crore. The budget table has 12 rows per account and cost centre, but someone joined it in Power Query to the Date table on MonthName before loading. Explain precisely how the inflation arose, estimate the multiplier you would expect, and describe the fix.

💡 Hint: Count how many dates share each month name across the calendar range.

Q3. Budget at FS-line level [L2]

The budget for FY2025-26 is given at FSLine × Branch × month (e.g. "Other expenses", "Pune", April 2025, ₹14.5 lakh). Actuals are at account × cost centre × branch × day. Design the model so that Actual vs Budget works by FSLine, Branch and month, and explain what the matrix shows when a user drills from FSLine to account.

Q4. Choose a bridging option [L3]

You inherit a Power BI file where Budget has PeriodKey (e.g. 202504) and no relationship to Date. The source budget workbook is owned by the planning team and you cannot change it, but you can add Power Query steps. Compare the MonthStart relationship and the TREATAS measure for this situation on maintainability, performance and user-understandability, and recommend one.

Q5. Model-build: daily run-rate [L3]

Sales leadership wants a daily "budget run-rate" line in a chart. Specify, in steps, how you would allocate the monthly sales budget to days using working-day weights from the Date table, including how you avoid double-counting when the monthly budget measure is also shown.
