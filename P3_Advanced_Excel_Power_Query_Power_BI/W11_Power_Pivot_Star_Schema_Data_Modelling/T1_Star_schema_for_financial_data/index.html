Roadmap topic: Dimensional Modelling (Star Schema): Designing Fact tables (GL Transactions, Budget) linked via 1-to-many relationships to Dimension tables (Chart of Accounts, Calendar, Cost Center)

Overview

Dimensional modelling is the discipline of shaping data so that analytical questions become simple filters and sums. In a finance context the facts are the GL transactions you posted, the budget you approved and the month-end trial balances you signed off; the dimensions are the Chart of Accounts, the fiscal calendar, cost centres and branches. A star schema places each fact at the centre, connected by one-to-many, single-direction relationships to small, descriptive dimension tables. Every DAX measure you write this week — Actual, Budget, Variance, YTD, closing balance, the formatted P&L — assumes this shape. When the shape is wrong, DAX becomes convoluted and numbers silently go wrong.

For the target roles this matters directly. FP&A analysts in Indian corporates and GCCs are expected to own the "semantic model" behind MIS dashboards: the controller asks why Budget does not respond to a branch slicer, the CFO asks why the P&L total does not equal the sum of lines, the auditor asks how unmapped ledgers are controlled. Each answer is a modelling answer. Your audit and TB-finalisation background is an advantage: a CoA mapping is a grouping schedule, referential integrity is completeness testing, and the "(Blank)" member is a suspense account nobody reconciled.

The learning sequence runs from design to hygiene. Start with what is a fact and what is a dimension (s01) and declaring the grain of each fact, including the monthly-budget-versus-daily-actual problem (s02). Then learn the three relationship properties — cardinality, direction, active status (s03) — and apply the active/inactive rule to role-playing dates (s04). Build the CoA hierarchy with sort and sign columns (s05), protect it with surrogate keys and an Unmapped member (s06), and finally recognise the anti-patterns — snowflakes and flat tables — in Power Pivot's Diagram View and Power BI's Model view (s07). The concepts give you the DAX tools that sit on top of the design: USERELATIONSHIP (c01), MonthStart budgets (c02), TREATAS (c03), CROSSFILTER (c04), the referential integrity test (c05) and the rule to hide fact keys (c06).

By the end you should be able to sketch, on a whiteboard, a model with GL, Budget and TB facts sharing CoA, Date, CostCentre and Branch, defend every relationship's cardinality and direction, and list the data-quality checks that prove it ties to the TB.

Related concepts to study

Kimball modelling: conformed dimensions and SCD types

Kimball modelling: conformed dimensions, and Type 1/Type 2 slowly changing dimensions for CoA remaps

Ralph Kimball's dimensional method underlies Power BI modelling. A conformed dimension has the same keys and attribute meanings across facts — CoA and Date shared by GL, Budget and TB — which is what lets Actual and Budget sit in one matrix. Slowly changing dimensions handle attribute changes: Type 1 overwrites (remap freight from Other expenses to Cost of materials and all history moves — good for comparability), Type 2 adds a new row with a new surrogate key and effective dates (history stays under the old mapping — good for "as reported" views). Finance teams usually want Type 1 for MIS restatement and keep a Type 2 or snapshot copy for audit trail of what was reported. Decide explicitly when the CoA is remapped mid-year.

OLTP normalisation vs analytical stars

Normalised OLTP schemas (your MySQL background) vs analytical star schemas

OLTP schemas (Tally's internal data, an ERP's MySQL or HANA tables) are normalised to third normal form to avoid update anomalies: ledgers reference groups, groups reference parent groups, vouchers reference voucher types. That suits fast inserts and integrity, not analysis. Analytical stars deliberately denormalise dimensions — repeating "Revenue from operations" on every revenue account row — to shorten filter paths, enable single-table hierarchies and compress well in columnar storage. Your SQL instincts about keys, joins and uniqueness transfer directly; your instinct to normalise does not. Write SQL views or Power Query merges that flatten lookup chains into one dimension.

Week 10 Power Query feeding the model

Last week's outputs are this week's tables: the cleaned GL query becomes the GL fact (typed, keys trimmed, AccountKey assigned), the unpivoted long budget becomes the Budget fact (with MonthStart added), and the CoA mapping with FS lines and sign factors becomes the CoA dimension. Power Query is where grain is fixed, surrogate keys and the Unmapped member are created, snowflakes are flattened, and data types are set so relationships match. Disable load on staging queries so only facts and dimensions reach the model. If a modelling problem can be fixed upstream in Power Query, fix it there rather than in DAX.

Budget and Forecast facts in Weeks 17–20

The FP&A weeks add more plan facts: annual budget, revised estimates (RE1, RE2), rolling forecasts. Each is a fact at month grain (account or FS line × cost centre × month × version) sharing the conformed CoA, Date and CostCentre dimensions. Variance analysis (price/volume/mix, favourable/adverse) then becomes measure arithmetic across facts. Design decisions made now — MonthStart keys, a Version column, budget at the same CoA granularity or via TREATAS — determine whether adding a forecast fact later is a one-hour job or a model rebuild. VertiPaq compression

VertiPaq compression: column cardinality, removing unused columns, integer keys

VertiPaq, the in-memory columnar engine in Power BI and Power Pivot, stores each column separately with dictionary and run-length encoding. Memory depends mainly on column cardinality (number of distinct values), not row count. Narration text or VoucherNo with lakhs of distinct values is expensive; a FY column with five values is nearly free. Practical rules: remove unused columns (every column costs memory even if hidden), use integer surrogate keys rather than long text keys, split datetime into date only, round amounts to two decimals, and keep descriptive text in small dimensions. VertiPaq Analyzer in DAX Studio shows size per column so you can target the worst offenders.

Common pitfalls

Bidirectional relationships everywhere

Bidirectional relationships everywhere, causing ambiguous paths and wrong totals

The mistake: setting every relationship's cross-filter direction to Both so that "everything filters everything", often because a slicer showed irrelevant members or a dimension count was wrong. With two facts sharing dimensions, this creates multiple paths between tables; Power BI deactivates relationships to resolve ambiguity, and totals for Budget or TB become wrong or repeat. Correct treatment: keep all fact–dimension relationships single direction; use CROSSFILTER(..., BOTH) inside the specific measure, or a visual-level filter on the slicer. Detect in review by scanning the Model view for double arrows and dashed lines you did not create, and by testing Budget totals by each dimension against the source.

Budget keyed on month text

Budget keyed on month text, forcing a many-to-many link to the Date table

The mistake: relating Budget[Month] ("Apr-25" or "April") or PeriodKey to a non-unique column in Date. Because both sides repeat, Power BI offers only many-to-many; April budget may then appear under every April in the calendar, or totals behave unpredictably. It happens because the budget workbook naturally has months as text headers. Correct treatment: convert to a real MonthStart date in Power Query and relate to 'Date'[Date] (c02), or use TREATAS on MonthEnd (c03). Detect by any : relationship in the model involving Date, and by comparing the model's FY budget total with the approved budget file.

Descriptions kept in the fact

Account names and descriptions kept in the fact table instead of the dimension

The mistake: leaving AccountName, LedgerGroup, CostCentreName or BranchCity on GL rows because the ERP extract had them. It bloats memory (text repeated on crores of rows), makes slicers on those columns filter only GL (Budget and TB ignore them), and creates conflicting descriptions when a ledger is renamed mid-year. Correct treatment: keep only keys and amounts in the fact; take descriptions from the dimension. Detect by scanning fact columns for text fields and by checking whether any visual slices by a fact column; VertiPaq Analyzer will show these as large columns.

Mismatched key types and trailing spaces

Key columns with mismatched types or trailing spaces, producing a "(Blank)" member that holds totals

The mistake: GL AccountCode loaded as text "400100 " (trailing space from a fixed-width export) or as a number while CoA has text; or dates with a time part. Power Query merges are exact and case-sensitive, so these rows fail to map, receive null keys and land in the virtual blank member, which shows "(Blank)" and quietly holds amounts. Correct treatment: set identical data types on both sides, apply Text.Trim, Text.Clean and consistent case before merging, convert datetimes to dates, and map residual misses to the Unmapped member (s06). Detect with the RI test (c05), a null-key count on the fact, and a "(Blank)" row check in the P&L matrix.

Practice Questions

Q1. Design the star for a listed manufacturer [L3]

A Pune-based NSE-listed auto-components manufacturer (FY ending 31 March) gives you: (a) SAP GL line items from 1-Apr-2021, 2.6 crore rows with PostingDate, DocDate, DueDate, GLAccount (text), CostCentre, ProfitCentre (branch), Amount; (b) FY2025-26 budget workbook by GL account × cost centre with months as columns Apr–Mar; (c) month-end TB for 60 months; (d) a CoA mapping sheet with 712 rows, of which 708 distinct GL accounts; (e) a cost-centre master with Department and Function in separate sheets. Required: (i) list the facts with their grain statements; (ii) list the dimensions with keys and attributes; (iii) specify each relationship's cardinality, direction and active status; (iv) describe how you handle the budget grain and the 4 duplicate mappings; (v) list the data-quality measures you would build.

Q2. Debug a model that does not tie [L3]

A Power BI MIS model shows FY2025-26 PAT of ₹48.6 crore against signed financial statements of ₹51.2 crore. You find: CoA–GL relationship set to Both; Budget related to Date on MonthName (many-to-many); GL[AccountCode] is text with some trailing spaces; there is a "(Blank)" row in the P&L matrix holding ₹2.6 crore of expense; a report-level slicer uses GL[PostingDate]. Required: explain how each finding could contribute to wrong or misleading figures, the order in which you would fix them, the DAX or Power Query used for each fix, and the tests that prove the final model ties to the TB.

💡 Hint: Separate issues that change Actual from those that only change Budget or slicer behaviour.

Q4. Budget at FS-line level with remapped CoA [L3]

Mid-year (October 2025), management moves "Freight outward" from Other expenses to a new MIS line "Distribution cost". The FY2025-26 budget was prepared at FS-line level under the old mapping (Other expenses budget ₹22 crore, including ₹6 crore freight). Required: (i) explain how Type 1 and Type 2 handling of the CoA change affects the Actual vs Budget report for H1 and H2; (ii) design the Budget fact and the DAX (TREATAS or physical relationship) so variance by FS line is meaningful; (iii) recommend a disclosure note for the MIS pack.

Q5. Size and performance review [L2]

VertiPaq Analyzer shows the model at 2.8 GB: GL[Narration] 1.1 GB, GL[AccountName] 420 MB, GL[PostingDateTime] (datetime) 380 MB, GL[VoucherNo] 300 MB, GL[Amount] 210 MB. Propose specific changes for each column, estimate which give the largest saving, and explain any reporting capability you would lose and how to preserve it (e.g. drill-through to narration).
