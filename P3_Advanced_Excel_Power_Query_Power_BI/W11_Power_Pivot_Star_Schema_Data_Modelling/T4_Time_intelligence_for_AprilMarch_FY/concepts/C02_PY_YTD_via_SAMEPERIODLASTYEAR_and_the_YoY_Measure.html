Key concept

PY YTD via SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR and the YoY % Measure

Concept: PY YTD = CALCULATE([YTD Actual], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR('Date'[Date])); YoY % = DIVIDE([YTD Actual] - [PY YTD], [PY YTD])

Statement

PY YTD = CALCULATE ( [YTD Actual], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR ( 'Date'[Date] ) )
YoY %  = DIVIDE ( [YTD Actual] - [PY YTD], [PY YTD] )

\[ \text{YoY \%} = \frac{\text{YTD}_{t} - \text{YTD}_{t-1}}{\text{YTD}_{t-1}} \]

where \(\text{YTD}_{t}\) is the fiscal YTD to the last visible date and \(\text{YTD}_{t-1}\) is the fiscal YTD to the same date one year earlier. DIVIDE returns BLANK when \(\text{YTD}_{t-1}\) is zero or blank.

Explanation

The PY YTD measure is a nested context modification. SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR first replaces the visible dates with the same dates one year earlier; then [YTD Actual] — itself a CALCULATE with DATESYTD — runs inside that shifted context, reads its last date and expands back to 1 April of the prior FY. Because the shift happens before the YTD expansion, the result is "YTD to the same point last year", not "last year's full YTD".

Order matters only in edge cases: CALCULATE([Actual], DATESYTD(SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR('Date'[Date]), "3/31")) gives the same result in standard month/quarter/year contexts. Both require the Date table to cover the prior year completely; for the first FY in the table PY YTD is blank, and DIVIDE correctly returns blank growth.

YoY % is a derived measure: it references [YTD Actual] and [PY YTD] rather than repeating their logic, so a fix to the YTD cap automatically flows through. Caveats:

  • Negative or near-zero bases. For EBIT or PAT that can be negative, use ABS([PY YTD]) in the denominator and consider showing "n.m." when the base is negative.
  • Partial periods. If the current year is capped at the last actuals date but PY YTD is not, YoY compares eight and a half months with nine. Cap both (s05).
  • Additivity. YoY % is a ratio: its total is computed from total YTD and PY YTD, never by adding row percentages. Branch-level YoY % does not sum to the company YoY %.
  • Variables for efficiency. VAR cy = [YTD Actual] VAR py = [PY YTD] RETURN DIVIDE(cy - py, py) evaluates each once.

Illustration

Revenue (₹ crore), December 2025 row:

Measure Window Value
[YTD Actual] 1 Apr 2025 – 31 Dec 2025 486.0
[PY YTD] 1 Apr 2024 – 31 Dec 2024 432.0
[YoY %] — (486.0 − 432.0) / 432.0 = 12.5%

At the branch level, Pune shows +40% (₹ 14 crore vs ₹ 10 crore) and Chennai −5% (₹ 95 crore vs ₹ 100 crore); the average of those two percentages (17.5%) bears no relation to their combined growth ((109 − 110) / 110 = −0.9%).

Practice Questions

Q1. Evaluate step by step [L1]

For a matrix cell showing Q2 FY26, list the date set after each step of evaluating [PY YTD] (outer context, after SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, after DATESYTD), with exact start and end dates.

Q2. Growth on a loss-making line [L2]

PAT YTD December FY25 was −₹ 12.0 crore and YTD December FY26 is −₹ 4.0 crore. Compute what YoY % as written in the concept returns, explain why it is misleading, and write an improved measure with a rule for when to show "n.m.".

💡 Hint: Check the sign of the denominator and what a "negative growth" label would imply to a reader.

Q3. Branch growth reconciliation [L2]

Three branches have YTD revenue (CY, PY) in ₹ crore of Delhi (60, 50), Kolkata (30, 40) and Hyderabad (45, 30). Compute each branch's YoY % and the company YoY %, and explain why the matrix total row must not be a sum or average of the branch percentages.
