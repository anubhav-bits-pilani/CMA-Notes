Roadmap topic: Time Intelligence in DAX: TOTALYTD (with '3/31' year-end parameter), SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, DATEADD, and MoM / YoY growth % measures

Overview

Time intelligence is the family of DAX patterns that move the date filter: to-date windows (YTD, QTD, MTD), shifts (prior year, prior month), rolling windows (R12M, T3M) and boundary values (opening and closing balances, covered in Topic 5). For an Indian finance team every one of these must respect the April–March fiscal year, line up with SEBI quarterly reporting and Ind AS comparatives, and behave sensibly while the current year is only partly posted. The functions are short; the judgement is in choosing the right one, feeding it the right date column, and capping it so that future months and partial periods do not distort growth numbers.

For FP&A and reporting roles this topic is where the model starts paying back the effort spent on the star schema and the fiscal calendar. YTD budget-vs-actual, YoY growth in the CFO's KPI strip, R12M revenue for valuation and covenant monitoring, and month-on-month run-rate reviews are all derived measures branching from a single [Actual] (and [Budget]) base. If the base measures and Date table are right, a dozen time measures — or one calculation group — deliver the entire comparative layer of an MIS pack.

The learning sequence is: first the prerequisites (s06) — a marked, contiguous, whole-FY Date table and filters that flow through it; then to-date windows with the fiscal year-end parameter (s01, c01, c06); then prior-period shifts and the differences between SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, DATEADD and PARALLELPERIOD (s02); then the growth measures built from them (s03, c02, c03); then rolling windows (s04, c04); then capping at the last actuals date so partial periods compare like-for-like (s05, c05); and finally calculation groups and field parameters that apply all of this without a measure explosion (s07).

Related concepts to study

Weeks 17–20: YTD budget vs actual and full-year forecast

→ Week 17–20: YTD budget vs actual, and full-year forecast = YTD actual + remaining budget

The budgeting and forecasting weeks reuse every measure here. YTD Budget is CALCULATE([Budget], DATESYTD('Date'[Date], "3/31")) cut at the same last actual date as YTD Actual, so the YTD variance is like-for-like. The latest estimate (LE) or full-year forecast is YTD actual for closed months plus budget (or the latest forecast version) for the remaining months: [YTD Actual (capped)] + CALCULATE([Budget], 'Date'[Date] > lastClosed, 'Date'[FYEnd] = fy). That formula only works if the cap from s05 is defined on closed periods rather than raw posting dates. Variance analysis then splits the full-year gap into "delivered YTD" and "still to deliver".

Weeks 5–8: YoY growth and trend analysis

→ Week 5–8: YoY growth and trend analysis

Financial statement analysis in Weeks 5–8 computes YoY growth, CAGR, common-size trends and margin changes from published statements. The DAX measures here automate the same calculations on internal data. The interpretation rules carry over: margins change in basis points, growth on a negative base is "n.m.", acquisitions require like-for-like splits, and seasonal businesses are judged on YoY rather than MoM. When you reconcile your DAX YoY % to a sell-side note's figure, differences usually come from period definitions (calendar vs fiscal, 9M vs LTM) or from exceptional items included in one and excluded in the other.

Calculation groups for YTD, PY and YoY variants

Calculation groups (model explorer or Tabular Editor) for YTD/PY/YoY variants

Calculation groups (s07) hold one DAX expression per time view using SELECTEDMEASURE(). They can be authored in Power BI Desktop's model explorer or, with more power and scripting, in Tabular Editor. The finance benefit is governance: the YTD cap and the year-end string live in one place, so a change is made once and tested once. The costs are that implicit measures stop working, items need format string expressions (YoY % must not inherit ₹ formatting), balance measures must be excluded from additive items, and precedence must be set when a second group (currency, scenario) exists.

Custom time intelligence for non-standard calendars

Custom time intelligence using FY number and fiscal month columns for non-standard calendars

Built-in functions assume a Gregorian calendar with a fixed year-end. Retailers on 4-4-5 or 52/53-week years, or groups with week-based periods, need custom logic: filter the Date table by fiscal-year and period-number columns directly. PY YTD becomes CALCULATE([Actual], REMOVEFILTERS('Date'), 'Date'[FYEnd] = fy - 1, 'Date'[FiscalPeriodNo] <= p), where fy and p are captured in variables. The same approach is locale-proof (c06) and transparent to auditors. Microsoft has also been previewing calendar-based time intelligence that lets the model define custom calendars for the built-in functions; check its status before depending on it.

Visual calculations PREVIOUS and RUNNINGSUM

Power BI visual calculations (PREVIOUS, RUNNINGSUM) inside matrices

Visual calculations are DAX expressions defined on a visual, operating on the visual's own rows and columns rather than on the model. RUNNINGSUM([Actual]) accumulates along the axis (a YTD-like running total if the axis starts in April), and PREVIOUS([Actual]) returns the prior row for MoM. They are quick for one-off analysis and avoid model changes, but they are bound to that visual's layout: if the axis is sorted differently, filtered, or starts in January, results change. They cannot be reused across visuals or in Excel. Use them for ad-hoc exploration; keep reported KPIs in model measures.

Common pitfalls

TOTALYTD without the year-end argument

TOTALYTD without the year-end argument gives calendar-year (Jan–Dec) YTD on Indian books

Mistake: TOTALYTD([Actual], 'Date'[Date]). Why it happens: tutorials are written for calendar-year companies, and the default produces believable numbers from April to December. Correct treatment: always pass the year end ("3/31" or the locale equivalent) or use custom fiscal logic. Detect it: the YTD resets in January rather than April; April YTD does not equal April's monthly value; March YTD does not equal the FY total.

PARALLELPERIOD with YEAR expands to calendar years

PARALLELPERIOD('Date'[Date], -1, YEAR) expands to whole calendar years, not April–March

Mistake: using PARALLELPERIOD(..., -1, YEAR) for "last year". Why: the name suggests "the parallel period last year". Correct treatment: use SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR/DATEADD(-1, YEAR) for like-for-like, PREVIOUSYEAR(..., "3/31") for the full prior FY, or PARALLELPERIOD(..., -12, MONTH) on full-FY selections. Detect it: the PY value for any month equals calendar-year revenue; PY for FY26 differs from published FY25 revenue.

Unmarked or gapped Date table

Time intelligence on an unmarked Date table or one with gaps returns wrong or blank results

Mistake: a Date table generated from distinct GL dates, or one not marked as a date table and related on an integer key. Why: it is quicker to build and looks fine in a simple monthly visual. Correct treatment: a generated, contiguous, whole-FY calendar, marked, with Auto date/time off (s06). Detect it: rows ≠ span of days; YTD equals the monthly value when month name is on rows; opening-balance or PY values blank on specific dates.

Partial current month against a full prior-year month

YoY compares a partial current month against a full prior-year month

Mistake: month-level YoY (or FY-level YoY) during the month compares 1–18 November with all of November last year. Why: SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR shifts the Date-table month, which is always complete. Correct treatment: cap the current period at the last actual date and cut the PY window at the equivalent date (s05), or show month YoY only for closed months. Detect it: YoY growth for the current month is sharply negative every month until close and "recovers" when the month completes.

Practice Questions

Q1. Build the comparative layer for an MIS pack [L3]

A listed FMCG company's model has GL (daily postings from 1 April 2023), Budget (monthly, stored on the first of each month), CoA and a Date table from 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2027. Actuals are closed to 31 October 2025 and posted to 14 November 2025. Revenue (₹ crore): YTD October FY26 = 1,284; YTD October FY25 = 1,175; October 2025 = 205; September 2025 = 188; FY25 full year = 2,060; YTD October FY26 budget = 1,310. (a) Write the measures Last Closed Date, YTD Actual, PY YTD, YoY %, PM Actual, MoM %, YTD Budget and YTD Variance %, capped at the closed date. (b) Compute the values each should show on the October 2025 row. (c) Explain what each would show on the FY26 total row and why. (d) Explain why the Date table must start on 1 April 2022 even though GL data starts in 2023.

💡 Hint: Treat the closed date, not the last posting date, as the MIS anchor, and remember which measures need the prior year to exist.

Q2. Debug a comparative page [L2]

A page shows, for December 2025, YTD Revenue ₹ 312 crore, PY YTD ₹ 540 crore and YoY −42%. The FY25 published revenue was ₹ 540 crore for the full year and ₹ 395 crore for April–December 2024; actuals are loaded to 31 December

  1. The PY measure is CALCULATE([YTD Actual], PARALLELPERIOD('Date'[Date], -1, YEAR)) and YTD Actual is

TOTALYTD([Actual], 'Date'[Date]). Identify every error, rewrite both measures, and compute the correct YoY %.

Q3. R12M EBITDA covenant with calculation groups [L3]

A borrower must maintain Net Debt / LTM EBITDA ≤ 3.0× at each quarter-end. Monthly EBITDA (₹ crore) for October 2024 to September 2025 is 14, 15, 16, 12, 13, 18, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17; net debt at 30 September 2025 is ₹ 505 crore. (a) Compute LTM EBITDA and leverage at September 2025. (b) Write a guarded R12M EBITDA measure. (c) Design a Time Calc calculation group with items Current, YTD, R12M and PY R12M, including how balance measures like Net Debt are excluded from the additive items. (d) Describe the visual and test evidence the treasury head would review.

Q4. Locale and template rollout [L2]

Explain what may break, list every function in this topic and Topic 5 that takes the year-end string, and design a hidden QA page (visuals and measures) that proves the fiscal logic before each monthly publish.

Q5. Visual calculations versus model measures [L2]
