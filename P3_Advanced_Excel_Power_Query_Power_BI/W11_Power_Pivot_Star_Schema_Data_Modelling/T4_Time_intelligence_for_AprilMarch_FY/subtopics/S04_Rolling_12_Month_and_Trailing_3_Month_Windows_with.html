Subtopic note

Rolling 12-Month and Trailing 3-Month Windows with DATESINPERIOD

Scope: Rolling 12-month and trailing 3-month windows with DATESINPERIOD

Why it matters

YTD numbers reset every April, which makes trend lines saw-toothed and hides momentum in Q1. Rolling windows fix that: Rolling 12-Month (R12M, also called LTM or TTM) revenue and EBITDA smooth seasonality, give an annualised run rate at any month-end, and are the basis for LTM multiples (EV/LTM EBITDA) in valuation, LTM covenant tests (Net Debt / LTM EBITDA) in bank sanction letters, and trailing-3-month (T3M) run rates in sales reviews. The Saturday assessment this week asks for an R12M Revenue measure explicitly.

Core notes

DATESINPERIOD

DATESINPERIOD ( <dates>, <start_date>, <number_of_intervals>, <interval> )
  • dates: the Date table date column.
  • start_date: the anchor date — usually MAX('Date'[Date]), the last visible date.
  • number_of_intervals: negative to look back (−12, −3), positive to look forward.
  • interval: DAY, MONTH, QUARTER or YEAR.

It returns the contiguous set of dates from the anchor back (or forward) by the given span. With anchor 30 November 2025 and −12 MONTH, the result is 1 December 2024 – 30 November 2025: twelve whole months ending on the anchor. Unlike DATESYTD, it does not reset at year boundaries.

The core measures

Rolling 12M =
CALCULATE (
    [Actual],
    DATESINPERIOD ( 'Date'[Date], MAX ( 'Date'[Date] ), -12, MONTH )
)

Trailing 3M =
CALCULATE (
    [Actual],
    DATESINPERIOD ( 'Date'[Date], MAX ( 'Date'[Date] ), -3, MONTH )
)

T3M Monthly Avg = DIVIDE ( [Trailing 3M], 3 )
T3M Annualised  = [Trailing 3M] * 4

MAX('Date'[Date]) is evaluated in the outer filter context before CALCULATE replaces the date filter — the filter arguments of CALCULATE are computed in the original context, so the anchor is the month-end of the row being displayed.

Rolling margins

Ratios over a rolling window are computed as ratio of rolling sums, never as averages of monthly ratios:

\[ \text{R12M EBITDA \%} = \frac{\sum_{m=t-11}^{t} \text{EBITDA}_{m}}{\sum_{m=t-11}^{t} \text{Revenue}_{m}} \]

R12M Revenue     = CALCULATE ( [Revenue], DATESINPERIOD ( 'Date'[Date], MAX ( 'Date'[Date] ), -12, MONTH ) )
R12M EBITDA      = CALCULATE ( [EBITDA],  DATESINPERIOD ( 'Date'[Date], MAX ( 'Date'[Date] ), -12, MONTH ) )
R12M EBITDA %    = DIVIDE ( [R12M EBITDA], [R12M Revenue] )

Guarding incomplete windows

Two failure modes:

  1. Not enough history. In the first eleven months of data, R12M is a partial sum and looks like a collapse relative to later months. Blank it when fewer than 12 months have data.
  2. Future months in the Date table. If the Date table runs to 31 March 2026 and actuals stop at 30 November 2025, an FY26 total row has anchor 31 March 2026: the window is April 2025 – March 2026, which contains only eight months of actuals. The value equals YTD, labelled as R12M — misleading.
Rolling 12M (guarded) =
VAR anchor =
    MIN ( MAX ( 'Date'[Date] ), [Last Actual Date] )
VAR window =
    DATESINPERIOD ( 'Date'[Date], anchor, -12, MONTH )
VAR monthsWithData =
    CALCULATE (
        COUNTROWS ( SUMMARIZE ( GL, 'Date'[MonthEnd] ) ),
        window
    )
RETURN
    IF ( monthsWithData = 12, CALCULATE ( [Actual], window ) )

SUMMARIZE(GL, 'Date'[MonthEnd]) is evaluated inside the window, so it lists only the month-ends that actually have GL rows. (Passing the GL table itself as a CALCULATE filter argument would not work: filter arguments are evaluated in the outer context, where GL is restricted to the current month.) MIN with two scalar arguments returns the earlier date. An alternative is a Date-table column MonthsSinceDataStart and a check that the anchor month is at least month 12.

Worked illustration

Monthly revenue (₹ crore) and the R12M at two month-ends:

Window Months included Sum
R12M at Nov 2025 Dec 2024 – Nov 2025 482.0
R12M at Oct 2025 Nov 2024 – Oct 2025 476.5

If November 2025 revenue is 41.0 and November 2024 revenue was 35.5, the R12M moved by 41.0 − 35.5 = 5.5: each month the window adds the new month and drops the same month a year earlier. That identity is a handy reconciliation:

\[ \text{R12M}_{t} = \text{R12M}_{t-1} + A_{t} - A_{t-12} \]

It also gives R12M from YTD figures, the way analysts compute LTM from published results:

\[ \text{LTM}_{\text{Dec FY26}} = \text{FY25 full year} - \text{YTD Dec FY25} + \text{YTD Dec FY26} \]

Other rolling variants

  • Rolling 12M average monthly: DIVIDE([Rolling 12M], 12).
  • Rolling average balance (semi-additive, see t5): AVERAGEX over the 12 month-ends in the window of [Closing Bal], not CALCULATE of a sum.
  • Rolling by days: DATESINPERIOD(..., -90, DAY) for a 90-day collections window.
  • Forward windows: DATESINPERIOD(..., +12, MONTH) from the forecast start for "next 12 months" pipeline.
  • DATESBETWEEN: explicit start and end, e.g., DATESBETWEEN('Date'[Date], EDATE(anchor, -12) + 1, anchor).

Visual design

A line chart of monthly Actual with R12M on a secondary axis (or R12M / 12 on the same axis) shows trend and seasonality together. In covenant monitoring, plot Net Debt / R12M EBITDA against the covenant threshold (say 3.0×) with month-ends on the axis.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Anchor at day level: on a daily axis the window is "the last 12 months to this day" — fine for flash reports, but the window then contains a partial first month.
  • Month-end arithmetic: a 28 February anchor with −12 MONTH returns 1 March – 28 February; DAX handles month ends, but test the 29 February 2024 case once.
  • Covenant definitions: bank sanction letters often define LTM EBITDA with add-backs (exceptional items, non-cash Ind AS 102 share-based payment, Ind AS 116 lease adjustments). The R12M measure must follow the contract definition, not the reported EBITDA line.
  • Acquired entities: pro-forma LTM includes the acquiree's pre-acquisition months — requires a separate pro-forma fact, not the GL.
  • Performance: DATESINPERIOD over a daily Date table is cheap; an R12M calculated in a matrix with hundreds of accounts × 36 months is still fine. Avoid FILTER(ALL('Date'), ...) with row-by-row comparisons where DATESINPERIOD suffices.

Key takeaways

  • DATESINPERIOD('Date'[Date], MAX('Date'[Date]), -12, MONTH) returns the 12 months ending on the visible month-end.
  • The anchor is evaluated in the outer context; cap it at the last actual date to avoid future-month windows.
  • Blank R12M until 12 months of data exist; check with a distinct count of month-ends with postings.
  • Rolling ratios are ratios of rolling sums.
  • \(\text{R12M}_{t} = \text{R12M}_{t-1} + A_{t} - A_{t-12}\) is the reconciliation check.

Practice Questions

Q1. Write R12M and T3M [L1]

Write DAX measures for R12M Revenue, R12M EBITDA, R12M EBITDA % and T3M Annualised Revenue, assuming base measures [Revenue] and [EBITDA] exist and the Date table is marked.

Q2. LTM from published results [L2]

A listed company reported FY25 revenue of ₹ 2,140 crore, 9M FY25 (April–December 2024) revenue of ₹ 1,560 crore and 9M FY26 revenue of ₹ 1,742 crore. Compute LTM December 2025 revenue and explain how your DAX R12M measure should be reconciled to this figure.

💡 Hint: LTM = last full year, minus the comparative stub, plus the current stub.

Q3. R12M that equals YTD [L2]

A user notices that the FY26 total row in a matrix shows R12M Revenue equal to YTD Revenue. Actuals are loaded to 31 October 2025 and the Date table runs to 31 March 2026. Explain the cause step by step and rewrite the measure to fix it.

Q4. Covenant dashboard [L3]

A term loan sanction letter requires Net Debt / LTM EBITDA ≤ 3.25× tested quarterly, where EBITDA excludes exceptional items and adds back Ind AS 102 share-based payment expense. Design the measures (including how you identify exceptional items and ESOP expense in the CoA), the guard for incomplete windows, and the visual that a treasury head would use to see headroom at each quarter-end of FY25 and FY26.

Q5. Rolling average monthly versus R12M ratio [L2]

Monthly EBITDA margins for the last three months were 18%, 12% and 21% on revenues of ₹ 20 crore, ₹ 50 crore and ₹ 30 crore. Compute the simple average of the margins and the T3M margin computed as a ratio of sums, and explain which one a T3M EBITDA % measure should return.
