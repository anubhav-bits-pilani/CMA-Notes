Key concept

RightAnti Join: Items in the Books but Not in the Bank

Concept: JoinKind.RightAnti returns items in the books but not in the bank (unpresented cheques, deposits in transit)

Statement

BookOnlyNested = Table.NestedJoin(Bank, {"Key"}, Book, {"Key"}, "Book", JoinKind.RightAnti),
BookOnly = Table.ExpandTableColumn(Table.SelectColumns(BookOnlyNested, {"Book"}), "Book",
    {"VchDate", "VchNo", "ChequeNo", "Narration", "AmtAligned"})

JoinKind.RightAnti returns the rows of the right table (Book) that have no matching key in the left table (Bank). In Table.NestedJoin, the output still has the left table's columns — all null for these rows — and the unmatched book row sits inside the nested Book column, which must be expanded.

Equivalent and often clearer:

BookOnly = Table.NestedJoin(Book, {"Key"}, Bank, {"Key"}, "Bank", JoinKind.LeftAnti)

Explanation

Book-only items are the entries recorded in the company's ledger that the bank has not yet processed, or that the books recorded wrongly. In a BRS they are mainly timing differences:

  • Cheques issued but not presented: the payment voucher is posted on issue; the bank debits only when the payee presents the cheque. Under CTS, cheques are valid for three months from the date; stale cheques beyond that should be reversed in the books (Dr Bank, Cr Party or a liability for stale cheques), not carried forever.
  • Deposits in transit (cheques deposited but not credited): receipts booked on deposit; the bank credits after clearing, often the next working day, so items around month-end are common.
  • Book errors: a payment posted to the wrong bank ledger, an amount keyed as ₹45,000 instead of ₹54,000, a duplicate voucher. These do not clear with time and need a correcting entry.

Ageing the book-only list is essential: items cleared in the first days of the next month confirm timing; items older than 30–90 days need investigation. Carry unmatched items forward each month and include them in the next month's matching so they clear when the bank finally processes them.

The null-left-columns behaviour of RightAnti is a frequent source of confusion ("the merge returned a table of nulls"). If you use RightAnti, select only the nested column and expand it, as in the statement. If you prefer each exception list to look like its source table, swap the inputs and use LeftAnti.

Illustration

Book (bank ledger) for June has 6 entries not found in the June bank statement:

VchDate Type Amount (₹) Cleared in July statement?
26-Jun Cheque 000512 issued 2,40,000 3-Jul
28-Jun Cheque 000515 issued 85,000 Not cleared
29-Jun Cheque deposited 1,60,000 1-Jul
30-Jun Cash deposited 75,000 1-Jul
14-Mar Cheque 000398 issued 32,000 Not cleared (stale after 14-Jun)
18-Jun NEFT to vendor 4,50,000 Never — posted to HDFC ledger but paid from ICICI

BRS at 30 June uses the first five as timing items: unpresented cheques ₹3,57,000 (including the stale ₹32,000, which should be reversed), deposits in transit ₹2,35,000. The NEFT of ₹4,50,000 is a book error: reverse it from the HDFC ledger and post it to the ICICI ledger.

Practice Questions

Q1. Fix the null table [L1]

A RightAnti merge of Bank (left) and Book (right) shows 47 rows with null in every column except a column "Book" showing "Table". Write the steps to turn this into a clean book-only list with VchDate, VchNo, ChequeNo and Amount.

Q2. Age the book-only list [L2]

Write M that adds DaysOutstanding (as at the parameter AsOfDate) and a Status column ("Timing — recent" up to 7 days, "Investigate" 8–90 days, "Stale cheque" over 90 days for cheque payments) to the book-only list.

💡 Hint: Duration.Days(AsOfDate - [VchDate]), and test the voucher type before applying the stale rule.

Q3. Stale cheque entry [L2]

A cheque of ₹68,000 issued to a vendor on 10-Dec-2025 remains unpresented at 31-Mar-2026. State whether it is stale under the three-month validity rule, pass the entry in the books, and explain how the RightAnti/LeftAnti list for April will change after the entry.
