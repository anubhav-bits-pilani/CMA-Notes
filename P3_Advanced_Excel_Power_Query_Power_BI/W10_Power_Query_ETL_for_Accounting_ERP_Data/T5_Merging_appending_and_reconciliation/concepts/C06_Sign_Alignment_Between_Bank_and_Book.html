Key concept

Sign Alignment Between Bank and Book

Concept: Sign alignment: bank credits equal book debits, so flip one side with Amount * -1 before matching

Statement

The bank statement is prepared from the bank's books, where the customer's account is a liability of the bank. The company's bank ledger is an asset in the company's books. Therefore the same transaction has opposite Dr/Cr labels:

Event Bank statement (bank's view) Company bank ledger
Customer receipt Credit (deposit) Debit
Vendor payment Debit (withdrawal) Credit

If each side's amount is computed as "Debit − Credit" in its own records, then

\[ A_{\text{bank}} = -A_{\text{book}} \]

where \(A_{\text{bank}} = \text{Debit}_{\text{bank}} - \text{Credit}_{\text{bank}}\) per the statement and \(A_{\text{book}} = \text{Debit}_{\text{book}} - \text{Credit}_{\text{book}}\) per the bank ledger. Matching requires multiplying one side by \(-1\):

BankAligned = Table.AddColumn(Bank, "AmtAligned", each -1 * (([Debit] ?? 0) - ([Credit] ?? 0)), type number)

Explanation

Alignment converts both sides to one convention — usually the company's view: positive = inflow (book debit, bank credit/deposit), negative = outflow (book credit, bank debit/withdrawal). Equivalent ways:

  • Compute bank amount directly as Deposit − Withdrawal (already in the company's view) and book amount as Debit − Credit. No flip is needed because the flip is built into the formula.
  • Compute both as Dr − Cr in their own records and multiply the bank side by \(-1\), as in the concept.

Whichever you choose, state it in a step name (AmtAligned_CompanyView) and apply it before keys, sequence numbers and anti-joins. Misalignment produces zero matches on amount (every receipt of ₹50,000 is +50,000 on one side and −50,000 on the other) — and, worse, a sign-insensitive workaround such as matching on Number.Abs pairs a ₹50,000 receipt with an unrelated ₹50,000 payment.

The same logic applies to other reconciliations: intercompany (A's receivable is a debit, B's payable is a credit — flip one side); customer statements vs AR ledger (the customer's books show the company as a creditor); vendor confirmations vs AP; and GSTR-2B credit notes (negative ITC) vs purchase register debit notes.

Use parentheses carefully with ??: ([Debit] ?? 0) - ([Credit] ?? 0). Without them, [Debit] ?? 0 - [Credit] ?? 0 parses as [Debit] ?? (0 - [Credit]) ?? 0 because ?? has lower precedence than subtraction.

Practice Questions

Q1. Align these amounts [L1]

Compute the aligned (company view) amount for: (a) bank statement withdrawal ₹4,72,000; (b) bank ledger credit ₹4,72,000; (c) bank statement deposit ₹19,800; (d) bank ledger debit ₹19,800. Identify which pairs should match.

Q2. Intercompany sign design [L2]

Entity A exports its TB with Dr positive and Cr negative; Entity B exports separate Debit and Credit columns. Write M for both sides that produces an aligned amount where A's receivable from B and B's payable to A appear with the same sign, and explain your convention.

💡 Hint: Decide first whose view is "positive", then derive each side's formula.

Q3. The Number.Abs shortcut [L2]

A colleague matches bank and book on Number.Abs(Amount) to avoid thinking about signs. Construct a small example (four transactions) where this produces a wrong match and an incorrect BRS, and explain why the error would not be detected by match-rate statistics.
