Subtopic note

Bank vs Book Matching on Amount, Reference and Date Window

Scope: Bank vs book matching on amount + reference (UTR/cheque no.) + date window

Why it matters

The join kind is easy; the matching key is where reconciliations succeed or fail. Bank statements and book ledgers describe the same transactions with different information: the bank knows the value date, UTR and its own narration; the books know the voucher date, party, invoice and the cheque number written. A key that is too strict (exact amount + exact date + reference) leaves genuine matches as exceptions; a key that is too loose (amount only) pairs unrelated ₹10,000 transactions. Practitioners therefore use tiered matching: strongest evidence first, progressively weaker rules for what remains, each tier removing its matches before the next.

Core notes

Evidence available on each side

Evidence Bank statement Books (Tally / SAP)
Amount Withdrawal or Deposit Debit or Credit to the bank ledger
Cheque number "Chq./Ref.No." column for cheques Cheque/instrument number on the payment voucher
UTR Embedded in narration for NEFT/RTGS/IMPS (e.g. "NEFT CR-HDFC0000123-ACME LTD-HDFCN52025040112345678") Often in voucher narration or a bank allocation field, if the accountant recorded it
Date Transaction date and value date Voucher date (may be the cheque date, before clearing)
Party Remitter/beneficiary name in narration Ledger/party name

UTR formats: NEFT UTRs are typically 16 characters beginning with the 4-letter bank code plus a letter (e.g. "HDFCN5..."); RTGS UTRs are typically 22 characters. Use these patterns when extracting from narration, but validate rather than assume, since formats vary by bank.

Tier 1: reference + amount

BankK = Table.AddColumn(Bank, "Key1", each [RefNorm] & "|" & Text.From([AmtAligned]), type text),
BookK = Table.AddColumn(Book, "Key1", each [RefNorm] & "|" & Text.From([AmtAligned]), type text),
T1    = Table.NestedJoin(BankK, {"Key1"}, BookK, {"Key1"}, "Bk", JoinKind.Inner)

(Merging on two key columns {"RefNorm","AmtAligned"} is equivalent; a concatenated key is convenient for later anti-joins and logging.) Exclude rows with null or blank references from Tier 1 so that null-equals-null does not create false matches.

Tier 2: amount + date window

For rows without a usable reference (cash deposits, some card settlements, direct debits), match on aligned amount, then accept pairs where the bank date falls within a window after the book date:

Rem1Bank = Table.NestedJoin(BankK, {"Key1"}, BookK, {"Key1"}, "x", JoinKind.LeftAnti),
Rem1Book = Table.NestedJoin(BookK, {"Key1"}, BankK, {"Key1"}, "x", JoinKind.LeftAnti),
Cand  = Table.NestedJoin(Table.RemoveColumns(Rem1Bank, {"x"}), {"AmtAligned"},
            Table.RemoveColumns(Rem1Book, {"x"}), {"AmtAligned"}, "Bk", JoinKind.Inner),
Exp   = Table.ExpandTableColumn(Cand, "Bk", {"VchNo", "VchDate"}, {"Bk.VchNo", "Bk.VchDate"}),
Lag   = Table.AddColumn(Exp, "LagDays", each Duration.Days([TxnDate] - [Bk.VchDate]), Int64.Type),
T2    = Table.SelectRows(Lag, each [LagDays] >= -1 and [LagDays] <= 5)

The window is a policy choice: cheques deposited typically clear in 1–3 working days under CTS; issued cheques can remain unpresented for weeks (validity is three months from the cheque date); a negative lag of a day may be allowed for book-date errors. Document the window and keep it as a parameter (WindowDays).

Tier 2 can produce many-to-many candidates (two ₹50,000 bank deposits, two ₹50,000 book receipts in the same week). Resolve by choosing the closest date per bank row and per book row, or by sequence numbering within the amount group (see s06), and flag ambiguous pairs for manual review rather than auto-matching.

Tier 3: reference only, amount difference

Rows sharing a reference but differing in amount (TDS deducted, bank charges netted, partial payment) are found by merging on RefNorm alone among the remaining items. Classify the difference: equal to a TDS rate on the invoice (e.g. 2% under Sec 194C, 10% under Sec 194J), within a small charges tolerance (e.g. under ₹1,000 with "CHG" or "COMM" in narration), or unexplained. These are "matched with difference" and need an entry (TDS receivable, bank charges) or follow-up.

Tier 4: residual exceptions

What remains after all tiers forms the bank-only and book-only lists. Each row gets a status column (Unmatched), and a suggested category by narration keywords: "CHG" → bank charges, "INT" → interest, "NACH"/"ECS" → mandate debit, "RTN"/"RETURN" → dishonour.

Matched register and audit trail

Append the tier outputs with a MatchTier column (1, 2, 3) so a reviewer can sample Tier 2 and Tier 3 matches, which carry more risk than Tier 1. Keep both sides' identifiers (bank row ID, book voucher number) on each matched row.

Worked illustration

April bank: 2,000 rows; books: 1,950 rows.

Tier Rule Bank rows matched Book rows matched
1 Ref + amount 1,640 1,640
2 Amount, lag −1 to +5 days 210 210
3 Ref only, amount difference ≤ policy 18 18
Residual — 132 bank-only 82 book-only

Check: \(1{,}640 + 210 + 18 + 132 = 2{,}000\) and \(1{,}640 + 210 + 18 + 82 = 1{,}950\). The Tier 3 differences total ₹1,84,200, of which ₹1,71,000 equals 10% TDS on three professional-fee receipts and ₹13,200 is unexplained.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • Value date vs transaction date. Use transaction (posting) date for matching windows; value date matters for interest and cash-pooling but can be earlier or later.
  • Bulk entries. A single bank credit for a card settlement or a payment gateway may cover 300 book receipts; match on aggregated amounts by settlement date (group book entries first), not line by line.
  • Salary and vendor bulk uploads. One bank debit, many book credits; same aggregation approach, keyed on the batch reference.
  • Reversals. A dishonoured cheque appears as a deposit and a reversal in the bank; both may sit in bank-only unless the books recorded the dishonour.
  • Cut-off. Items around 31 March need the next month's statement to clear; include early-April bank lines in the matching window, but report the March balance.

Key takeaways

  • Match in tiers: reference + amount, then amount + date window, then reference with amount difference, then residual exceptions.
  • Remove each tier's matches before the next, and record MatchTier for review.
  • Keep the date window as a documented parameter reflecting clearing practice.
  • Classify amount differences against TDS rates and charge tolerances rather than leaving them as exceptions.
  • Aggregate bulk and settlement entries before matching rather than forcing line-level pairing.

Practice Questions

Q1. Choose the tier [L1]

Assign each transaction to the tier most likely to match it: (a) RTGS receipt with UTR recorded in the voucher narration; (b) cash deposit of ₹2,40,000 at the branch; (c) customer NEFT of ₹4,41,000 against an invoice of ₹4,50,000 (2% TDS deducted); (d) cheque issued on 26 March, presented on 3 April.

Q2. Write the date-window step [L2]

Given a candidate table with columns BankRowID, TxnDate, AmtAligned, Bk.VchNo and Bk.VchDate, write M that keeps only pairs with lag between −1 and WindowDays, and then keeps only the closest book voucher for each BankRowID.

💡 Hint: Group by BankRowID and use Table.Min(_, "AbsLag") to keep one row per group.

Q3. TDS differences [L2]

Tier 3 finds these pairs with the same invoice reference: bank ₹98,000 vs book ₹1,00,000; bank ₹4,50,000 vs book ₹5,00,000; bank ₹1,99,410 vs book ₹2,00,000. Classify each difference (likely TDS section/rate, bank charges or unexplained) and state the entry required in the books.

Q4. Policy design [L3]

Draft a one-page matching policy for a company's six bank accounts: tiers, keys, date windows by instrument (cheque, NEFT/RTGS, cash, card settlement), tolerances, who reviews Tier 2/3 matches and how unresolved items older than 90 days are escalated.
