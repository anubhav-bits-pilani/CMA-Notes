Subtopic note

Other Reconciliations: GSTR-2B, AR Control and Intercompany

Scope: Other recons: GSTR-2B vs purchase register, AR sub-ledger vs GL control, intercompany balances

Why it matters

Once you can build a bank reconciliation in Power Query, the same pattern — normalise keys, align signs, merge, anti-join, classify differences — handles most other reconciliations in an Indian finance function. Three recur every month: the GSTR-2B vs purchase register reconciliation that determines how much input tax credit can be claimed in GSTR-3B; the AR (or AP) sub-ledger vs GL control reconciliation that supports the receivables figure in the balance sheet; and the intercompany reconciliation that must be clean before consolidation eliminations under Ind AS 110. Each has its own key design and its own consequences for errors.

Core notes

GSTR-2B vs purchase register

Context. Under Sec 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act (effective 1 January 2022), ITC is available only where the supplier has furnished the invoice in GSTR-1 and it is communicated in the recipient's GSTR-2B; Rule 36(4) aligns ITC to GSTR-2B. GSTR-2B is a static monthly statement. From October 2024 the Invoice Management System (IMS) lets recipients accept, reject or keep pending each inward document, and GSTR-2B reflects those actions — the rules here are date-sensitive, so check the current position when you build the recon.

Sources. GSTR-2B Excel download (B2B sheet with GSTIN of supplier, trade/legal name, invoice number, invoice date, invoice value, place of supply, taxable value, IGST, CGST, SGST, cess, ITC availability) and the purchase register from Tally or SAP (supplier GSTIN, bill number, bill date, taxable value, tax amounts).

Key design. Supplier GSTIN (15 characters, upper-case, trimmed) + normalised invoice number + (optionally) FY. Invoice numbers are the hard part: "INV/25-26/0042", "42", "INV-0042". Normalise by upper-casing, removing separators and leading zeros of the serial, and, where suppliers embed the FY, either strip it consistently on both sides or keep it on both.

Merge sequence.

  1. Exact merge on GSTIN + normalised invoice number. For matched pairs, compare taxable value and each tax head; difference within ₹1–₹10 (rounding) → "Matched"; above → "Value mismatch".
  2. For leftovers, merge on GSTIN + taxable value (or total tax) + invoice date within a few days → "Probable match — invoice number differs".
  3. Residual 2B-only → "In 2B, not in books" (purchase not booked, or booked under a different GSTIN or period).
  4. Residual books-only → "In books, not in 2B" (supplier has not filed or filed wrongly; ITC not eligible yet).

Consequences. Books-only items are ITC at risk: they cannot be claimed until reflected in 2B, and the time limit under Sec 16(4) (generally 30 November following the end of the FY) caps how long you can wait. 2B-only items may indicate unbooked purchases (cut-off), duplicates or invoices not belonging to the company. Rule 37A requires reversal of ITC where the supplier has not filed GSTR-3B by 30 September following the FY. The recon output feeds the GSTR-3B Table 4 ITC claim and vendor follow-up.

GSTKey = (gstin as nullable text, inv as nullable text) as nullable text =>
    if gstin = null or inv = null then null
    else Text.Upper(Text.Trim(gstin)) & "|" &
         Text.TrimStart(Text.Select(Text.Upper(inv), {"0".."9", "A".."Z"}), "0")

AR sub-ledger vs GL control

Context. The balance sheet shows trade receivables from the GL control account; the sub-ledger (party-wise balances) supports ageing, ECL and confirmations. In SAP, customer reconciliation accounts block direct postings, so differences usually come from timing (sub-ledger extract and GL extract run at different times) or from separate billing systems. In Tally, Sundry Debtors is a group total of party ledgers, so the "control" always equals the parties — but reconciliation is still needed against an external billing or CRM system, and against debtors posted to wrong groups.

Steps.

  1. Group the sub-ledger by customer code: closing balance per customer; total.
  2. Extract the GL control balance per account (and per profit centre/branch if relevant).
  3. Compare totals: difference = control − sum of sub-ledger.
  4. If different, merge the GL line items posted to the control account (FBL3N-type extract) against sub-ledger documents (FBL5N-type) on document number; anti-joins show GL-only postings (direct journal to control, FX revaluation at GL level, provisions mistakenly posted to control) and sub-ledger-only items (cut-off, documents posted to a different reconciliation account).
  5. Separately check credit balances in debtors (advances from customers), which Schedule III requires to be presented as other current liabilities rather than netted.

Intercompany balances

Context. Ind AS 110 requires intragroup balances and transactions to be eliminated in full on consolidation (Ind AS 110 B86). Elimination works only if both sides agree; differences must be investigated before consolidation, not plugged.

Key design. Build a counterparty pair key from each entity's perspective: for A's receivable from B, key = "A-B"; for B's payable to A, key = "A-B" as well (sorted pair or explicit direction). Align signs so A's receivable (debit, positive) is compared with B's payable (credit) flipped to positive. Convert foreign-currency balances at the closing rate used for consolidation, and compare in a single currency.

Differences typically arise from: goods or cash in transit (A has invoiced, B has not received); cut-off differences; FX rates (B books at transaction rate, A revalues at closing); disputes and unrecorded debit/credit notes; and mis-coded counterparties. At transaction level, merge on invoice number between the two entities' ledgers; at balance level, merge on the pair key and compute the difference.

Worked illustration

Intercompany at 31-Mar-2026: Entity A (India) receivable from Entity B: ₹2,85,40,000. Entity B (UAE) payable to A: AED 12,60,000. Closing rate ₹22.40 per AED, so \(12{,}60{,}000 \times 22.40 = 2{,}82{,}24{,}000\). Difference ₹3,16,000. A transaction-level merge shows one A invoice of ₹3,16,000 dated 30-Mar-2026 not in B's books (goods in transit). The consolidation adjustment recognises the in-transit item, after which the balances eliminate.

Edge cases & judgement calls

  • GSTIN errors. A supplier filing under the wrong recipient GSTIN (another state registration of the company) shows as 2B-only in one GSTIN and books-only in another; recon across all GSTINs together, keyed on supplier GSTIN + invoice.
  • Credit and debit notes. 2B reports them separately; match them to their own notes, not to invoices, and remember the sign.
  • Amendments. Amended invoices in 2B (B2BA) replace earlier values; process amendments before matching.
  • Unallocated receipts. In AR recon, on-account receipts sit in the sub-ledger without an invoice; they reconcile at balance level but distort ageing.
  • Intercompany plugs. Booking the difference to a suspense account to force elimination hides errors; disclose and resolve instead.

Key takeaways

  • The bank-recon pattern generalises: normalise keys, align signs, merge, anti-join, classify differences.
  • GSTR-2B recon keys on supplier GSTIN + normalised invoice number; books-only items are ITC at risk under Sec 16(2)(aa).
  • AR control recon compares GL control to grouped sub-ledger balances, then line-level anti-joins explain differences.
  • Intercompany recon uses a counterparty pair key, sign flip and closing-rate conversion before elimination under Ind AS 110.
  • Classify every difference (timing, value, missing, mis-coded) rather than leaving a single "unmatched" bucket.

Practice Questions

Q1. Classify the GST items [L1]

Classify each item into Matched, Value mismatch, In 2B not in books, or In books not in 2B, and state the ITC consequence: (a) invoice in both, taxable value ₹1,00,000 in books and ₹1,00,000 in 2B, IGST ₹18,000 both; (b) invoice in books, supplier filed GSTR-1 after the 2B cut-off; (c) invoice in 2B for ₹4,20,000 that the company never received; (d) invoice in both, taxable ₹2,50,000 in books vs ₹2,05,000 in 2B.

Q2. Write the 2B merge [L2]

Write M that creates the normalised key on both the 2B table and the purchase register, performs the exact merge, expands taxable value and IGST/CGST/SGST from the register, and adds a Status column with a ₹10 rounding tolerance on each tax head.

💡 Hint: Compute absolute differences per tax head and use List.Max of them in the Status condition.

Q3. AR control difference [L2]

At 30-Sep-2025 the GL control "Trade Receivables – Domestic" shows ₹18,46,72,300; the sum of customer balances from the sub-ledger is ₹18,39,15,800. A GL line-item extract shows a manual journal of ₹4,80,000 debited directly to the control account for a disputed claim, and an FX revaluation posting of ₹2,76,500 at GL level only. Determine whether these explain the difference, and describe the Power Query steps that would identify them automatically.

Q4. Intercompany design [L3]

A group has 7 entities in 4 currencies. Design the intercompany reconciliation pipeline: inputs per entity, pair-key logic, sign alignment, currency conversion, tolerance, outputs for the consolidation team and the monthly timeline for resolving differences before the close.
