Key concept

FY End Year and FY Label Formula

Concept: FY end year: Date.Year([Date]) + (if Date.Month([Date]) >= 4 then 1 else 0); label "FY" & Text.From(y-1) & "-" & Text.End(Text.From(y), 2)

Statement

For a date \(d\) with calendar year \(Y\) and calendar month \(m\), the Indian FY end year is

\[ y = Y + \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } m \geq 4 \\ 0 & \text{if } m \leq 3 \end{cases} \]

and the FY label is "FY" followed by \(y - 1\), a hyphen and the last two digits of \(y\):

FYEnd = Table.AddColumn(t, "FYEndYear", each Date.Year([Date]) + (if Date.Month([Date]) >= 4 then 1 else 0), Int64.Type),
FY    = Table.AddColumn(FYEnd, "FY", each
            let y = [FYEndYear] in "FY" & Text.From(y - 1) & "-" & Text.End(Text.From(y), 2), type text)
  • Date.Year, Date.Month — calendar year and month of the date.
  • The if expression returns 1 or 0 and must be in parentheses because it is inside an addition.
  • Text.From converts the number to text; Text.End(text, 2) takes the last two characters.

Explanation

The FY runs April to March, so April–December belong to the FY that ends in the next calendar year and January–March to the FY ending in the same year. Computing the end year first, as an integer, is the cleanest design: it is a sortable, joinable number, arithmetic is trivial (prior year = \(y - 1\)), and any label format can be derived from it.

The label format is a house convention. "FY2025-26" is common in Indian MIS and mirrors the Income-tax "previous year 2025-26" convention; annual reports often say "year ended 31 March 2026". Other variants: "FY26" (= "FY" & Text.End(Text.From(y), 2)), "FY 25-26", "2025-26". The label must match the budget and prior-year files exactly if it is used as a key or slicer value; otherwise appended Budget and Actual rows will not line up.

Conditions and checks:

  • [Date] must be a date or datetime (not text). Parse text dates with an explicit culture first.
  • Test boundary dates: 31-Mar and 1-Apr of the same calendar year must give different FYs.
  • Text concatenation with null returns null: if [Date] can be null, guard with if [Date] = null then null else ....

01-01-2026 2026 No 2026 FY2025-26

A common wrong version, "FY" & Text.From(Date.Year([Date])) & "-" & Text.End(Text.From(Date.Year([Date]) + 1), 2), labels January–March 2026 as "FY2026-27" — off by one for a quarter of every year.

Q2. Alternative labels [L2]

Starting from the FYEndYear column, write typed M columns for "FY26", "FY 25-26" and "2025-26", and a FYStartDate (1 April) and FYEndDate (31 March) column using #date.

Q3. Review the formula [L2]

A colleague's label column is "FY" & Text.From(Date.Year([Date]) - (if Date.Month([Date]) < 4 then 1 else 0)) & "-" & Text.From(Date.Year([Date]) + (if Date.Month([Date]) >= 4 then 1 else 0)). Evaluate it for 10-Jun-2025 and 10-Feb-2026, state whether it is correct in substance and in format, and recommend a cleaner implementation.
